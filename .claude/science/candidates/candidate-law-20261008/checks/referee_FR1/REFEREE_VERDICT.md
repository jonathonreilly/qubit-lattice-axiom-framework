9. **No gauge-field loophole exists inside the tested family.**  
   The fermion model has a global number \(U(1)\), but no dynamical gauge field or Gauss law; the magnet has an exchange-gradient Goldstone field. Assigning frozen sites an unscreened gauge charge would add a different vacuum class. It could give \(A_0\propto N/r\), but probe shifts would be \(q_sA_0\): charge-dependent electrostatics, with opposite signs and neutral species, not universal gravity. A gravity-like candidate requires a dynamical massless \(1/k^2\) mediator, additive source charge, and universal coupling to clock energy—phenomenologically a specially constrained scalar, or more naturally a massless spin-2/metric sector. None of those properties follows from freezing records.

**Strongest correct statement:** For the implemented half-filled staggered free-fermion model with removed sites and finite-range neighbour potentials, and for the specified exchange-only ordered-magnet boundary problem, compact frozen clusters do not generate the preregistered universal \(N/r\) clock field: the fermionic scalar responses decay substantially faster than \(1/r\), while the magnet’s legitimate \(1/r\) orientation texture is sourced by geometric capacitance rather than frozen-site number and is not itself a demonstrated universal excitation-energy shift. Thus FR1 is failed for these implementations. This supports “freezing alone is insufficient in these vacuum classes,” not a no-go theorem for every possible frozen-record vacuum; obtaining Newtonian behavior would require additional long-range mediator, additive-source, and universal-coupling structure.
