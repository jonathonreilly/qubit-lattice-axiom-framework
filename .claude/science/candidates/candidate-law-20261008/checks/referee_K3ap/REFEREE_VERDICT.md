4. **Linear versus quadratic graviton mass — linear is allowed generically, but neither law has been derived for this defect.**

   Writing \(T_R=T_\star+\epsilon\Delta T\), the graviton self-energy contains
   \[
   \langle T_RT_R\rangle
   =\langle T_\star T_\star\rangle
   +\epsilon\bigl(\langle T_\star\Delta T\rangle+\langle\Delta T\,T_\star\rangle\bigr)
   +\epsilon^2\langle\Delta T\Delta T\rangle .
   \]
   The Ward identity therefore permits a gauge-breaking term linear in a signed operator deformation unless a symmetry or orthogonality kills the cross term. Quadratic dependence applies to double-divergence spectral weights or models requiring two breaking insertions. But the measured mean \(|q_b-q_a+f|\) is neither a signed deformation parameter nor an operator norm, and no script computes the self-energy, contact terms, counterterms, or pole mass. Therefore the worker may say “linear is generically allowed,” but not \(m_g^2=C e^2\,{\rm defect}\) with nonzero \(C\). The \(10^{-51}\) and \(10^{-103}\) targets are both conditional assumptions, not outputs.

5. **Physical reading and the \(10^{15}\)-site bar.**

   Using the 2022 CODATA Planck length, \(1.616255\times10^{-35}\) m ([NIST](https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/pdf/all.pdf)), \(10^{17}\!-\!10^{19}\) sites means \(1.6\times10^{-18}\!-\!1.6\times10^{-16}\) m. No direct short-range gravity experiment reaches remotely near that scale; the torsion-balance test covers \(52\,\mu{\rm m}\) to 3 mm and constrains gravitational-strength Yukawa ranges above roughly \(38.6\,\mu{\rm m}\) ([Lee et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.11761)). Those experiments therefore do not exclude the conditional quadratic-scaling range.

   Conversely, \(10^{35}\) sites is about 1.6 m. An ordinary positive smearing kernel of that width would grossly distort laboratory gravity from micrometres through metre scales and would be strongly disfavoured by torsion-balance and Cavendish-type data. A formal exclusion still requires the actual smearing kernel; “range” alone is insufficient.

   The \(10^{15}\)-site bar, \(1.6\times10^{-20}\) m, is consequently far stricter than direct gravity-locality tests. It is legitimate only as a fixed theoretical/naturalness benchmark conditional on Planck spacing, an \(O(1)\) mass prefactor, and the assumed defect-to-mass law. It should not be reinterpreted as an experimental exclusion scale. The quoted \(1.3\times10^{-23}\) eV graviton-mass number is the published GWTC-3 bound ([LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA](https://dcc.ligo.org/LIGO-P2100275-v13/public)).
