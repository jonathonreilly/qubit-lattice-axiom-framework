### 4. Route 2 (rank 2): keep the event substrate quantum (not one frozen draw)

- **Premise dropped:** That matter propagates through one frozen classical realization of the sprinkling, i.e. records-as-events, so the event set is a fixed configuration that the matter sees. Here the event set stays in the possibility domain (unrecorded), in a Poincare-invariant quantum state.
- **Mechanism:** Elementary kinematic lemma (proved here; spot-checked over 4e5 random draws, with violations only in the k -> 0 tolerance limit; scratch out/kinematic_lemma.out).
- Take p^2 = p'^2 = m^2, p = p' + k, and k in the closed forward cone with k != 0.
- Then 2p.k = k^2. A null k gives a contradiction. A timelike k needs sqrt(k^2) >= 2m, and then p'.p <= -m^2 < 0, so p' is past-directed.
- So in a state that satisfies the spectrum condition, a stable on-shell massive particle cannot hand any 4-momentum to substrate excitations, at any order. Discreteness then enters only as a virtual self-energy (mass and field renormalization), not as decoherence.

Why the frozen draw is different: a quenched realization breaks momentum conservation, and the white Poisson structure factor has spacelike support. That gives scattering, and in Lorentz-invariant-in-law form the swerves (the M-B R3 swerve, the M-A/M-C noise).

This extends B1 section 2.10 (quenched vs annealed, massless probes; confirmed by the B referee at B_referee.md:85) to massive probes.
- **Must believe:** (1) Spacetime events are not records: they never lock a configuration-dependent value. B1's caveat 'records quench' must not apply to the substrate, only to sparse matter records (consistent with SUMMARY section 8: 'an event happening' is not 'a value being locked').
(2) There exists a quantum state of the event substrate with Poincare invariance plus the spectrum condition that also reproduces E1-prime's mean properties (one cone, shape-blindness). None is known: causal-set quantum dynamics is open. A classical Poisson process cannot be such a state's fluctuation spectrum, because its density correlations have spacelike Fourier support.
(3) Matter's passage does not itself record which configuration it saw. If the coupling is diagonal in the configuration basis and the branches are orthogonal, the probabilities revert to the quenched average E|A|^2. I showed this in the analysis; label it suggested.
- **First test:** A mechanism demonstration in 1+1 (analytic plus a small numeric check).
- A massive scalar coupled through g*phi^2*chi to (a) a quenched classical Gaussian chi with the same equal-time spectrum as (b) the vacuum of a free quantum chi.
- Compute the on-shell imaginary self-energy, i.e. the decay rate of the coherent packet amplitude, in both cases.
- Pre-register PASS: (a) gives a nonzero rate set by spacelike-q support, and (b) gives exactly zero below threshold.
- Then pre-register the decisive obligation separately: exhibit an event-substrate state with the spectrum condition (for example from quantum sequential growth or the quantum measure). This one is not cheap.
- **Cost:** 2-4 hours for the toy. The decisive obligation is open research with no estimate. The price is axiom-level and named only as a price: Record would not cover substrate events ('events happen' would be separate from 'records form'), on top of the existing Lattice replacement and supplied causal order.

### 6. Score matter by extinction, not by estimator variance (gain is not decoherence)

### 8. Exact price: who decides the sign (a non-locality or form-factor scale l_k)

- **Premise dropped:** That each mass insertion's sign and its link are decided by one event (Johnston's single-link re-emission). M-C's Lemma B says the only thing that matters is which events decide each sign.
- **Mechanism:** In the chain law M^2 = a b^2 rho, the factor a is the self-overlap of a single-link hop. A hop kernel decided by many events lowers that overlap; to do so Lorentz-invariantly it must be smooth on a scale l_k in |q^2|. C2, whose signs are each decided by about 1e150 events, is the confirmed coherent extreme, but it supplies the propagator by hand. For norm-preserving rules the same price appears as a form factor: Gamma = 1.6e-3 m (l/l_k)^4 (suggested, xm2). Smoothing leaves the long-wavelength Poisson power unchanged; only the large-|q^2| tail is cut. The bars then need l_k >= 1.9e6 l_P (electron trap, 1e-7/s), 1.2e6 l_P (UCN storage, 1e-3/s) and 6.6e5 l_P (Rb, 1/s), i.e. about 1-3e-29 m. That is ten orders of magnitude below the 1e-19 m locality bar. If this holds, the wall becomes a cheap decision (one non-locality scale) rather than a calculation.
- **Must believe:** A smeared recursive massive rule exists whose mean has no unstable complex zeros. Smearing the 4D BD operator does create them: Aslanbeigi-Saravani-Sorkin, cited by the E1 referee. Its noise gain also stays finite for source-driven fields. E1's EXACT L^2 statement gives infinite gain over an infinite past for interval-volume kernels in initial-value form. The source-driven restriction to the packet's worldtube is what must save it.
- **First test:** M-C's proposed M-A-prime (prior art; cite it), pre-registered in 1+1, where the exact 2D Volterra control exists. PASS: the rate scales as N_s^-p with p >= 0.4 over the range, and the mean stays within 5% of the KG kernel. FAIL: p < 0.1, or the mean grows faster than the noise falls. Otherwise INCONCLUSIVE.
- **Cost:** Half a day in 1+1; a 3+1 check of the l_k law takes about 2 days.
