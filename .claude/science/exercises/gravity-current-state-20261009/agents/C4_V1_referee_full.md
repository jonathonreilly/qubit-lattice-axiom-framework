## 2. Factorization and location of the anomaly

**The operator algebra is exact; its infinite-volume and sector claims need
qualification.**  For a common finite-volume regulator and the same density,

`sum_x x_j W p_i(x) W^dagger = W (sum_x x_j p_i(x)) W^dagger`.

Thus `K_lambda=W K_0 W^dagger`, exponentiation gives
`U_h(lambda)=W U_0 W^dagger`, and the displayed factorization follows exactly
(`V1_report.md:85-94`).  On infinite `Z^3`, first moments are unbounded and the
statement is a regulated/formal identity; the report gives no domain or
boundary-limit argument.

At `O(lambda)`, after choosing the `31` block diagonalization, the transformed
remainder is indeed `Y=lambda V_22`; consequently its anomaly is in a sector
with at least two transported quasiparticles in and out.  Likewise
`[S,H_2(h)]_{40}` is translation-covariant because both kernels carry the
ordinary momentum delta (`V1_report.md:91-102`).  At all orders, however, this
requires an exact quasi-local `W` that block-diagonalizes the vacuum and
one-particle spectral sectors; only `S_1` is constructed.  More importantly,
the label `>=2 in/>=2 out` is relative to the `W`- and `h`-dependent
quasiparticle decomposition.  It is not itself a basis-free definition of a
local operator split.

## 4. The seagull and the all-orders claim

self-confirming and the all-orders wording is too strong.**  If
`X=[S,H_2(h)]`, then for excited eigenstates of `H_2(h)`,
`X_{n0}=-E_n S_{n0}`.  Ordinary second-order perturbation theory therefore
gives

`Delta E_X^(2)=-sum_n E_n |S_{n0}|^2`,

while

`(1/2)<0|[S,[S,H_2(h)]]|0>=+sum_n E_n |S_{n0}|^2`.

This proves the cancellation, including its sign and factor.  The stored
Hessians reproduce at both masses (`v1_vacuum_seagull_m1.out:2-5`;
`v1_vacuum_seagull_m0.3.out:2-5`).  I also evaluated the seagull by a direct
three-independent-momentum sum at `L=4`, rather than the script's Laplace/FFT
route, and obtained the same E and T2 Hessians (`1.503664166e-5` and
`6.505210761e-7`).

The supplied script does not numerically check the negative term: after
computing the seagull at `v1_vacuum_seagull.py:41-47,59`, it defines the other
piece as its negative in the formatted output (`v1_vacuum_seagull.py:60-62`).
The cancellation is nevertheless secured by the spectral identity above, not

For an exact `W` and an exactly defined `Y_loc(h)` that annihilates the

## 5. Scattering price and low-momentum suppression

The kinematic low-momentum statement has a correct narrow core.  On a normal,

`sum sin(k)_in-sum sin(k)_out = -(1/6)(sum k^3_in-sum k^3_out)+O(k^5)`.

Thus the **absolute numerator** is `O((ka)^3)` for `f=sin(k)`, or
`O((ka)^(2R+1))` when a higher-order symbol matches `k` to that order.  Relative
to a momentum-sized leading quantity the suppression is `O((ka)^2)`, not
`O((ka)^3)`.  It is not uniform on the Brillouin zone or for umklapp; the
worker's own shell sample has median numerator about `1.8`

was computed, so the phenomenological suppression and the claim that the first
excited-state response starts only at `O(lambda^2) density^2` remain
unsupported (`V1_report.md:154-156`).  The `O(lambda epsilon)` amplitude defect
is already an observable in principle.
