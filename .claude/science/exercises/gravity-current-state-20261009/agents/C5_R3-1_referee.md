The worker's overall **INCONCLUSIVE** reading survives, as do the scalar torus theorem, the critical exponent (s_c=2), the forward-tube construction, the invariant-polynomial counts, the existence of direction dependence in the tube, and the finite resonance counts. Several stronger formulations do not survive: the Poincaré series converges as an orbit-average coefficient but does not define a convergent hopping operator; the quoted (1.3)--(1.4) Lorentz-violation exponent is not a stable asymptotic fit; a tube value at imaginary momentum is not yet a physical real-momentum dispersion; and exact ordinary momentum conservation does not hold for arbitrary multiparticle in-band processes.

## 1. Lemma A and Lemma M

- **Lemma A is confirmed.** Translation covariance makes an off-site scalar kernel a function of (d=y-x), and exact Γ_L covariance makes it constant on Γ_L-orbits. A finite orbit would give a finite-index subgroup fixing (d\ne0); Borel density would then make the connected Lorentz group fix (d), which it does not. This validates `R3-1_report.md:62-73`.

- **The scalar Lemma M is confirmed, and the torus action is ergodic.** For σ in (L^2(T^4)), invariance gives \(\widehat\sigma(\gamma d)=\widehat\sigma(d)\). Parseval and the infinitude of every nonzero character orbit force every nonzero Fourier coefficient to vanish. Thus σ is constant almost everywhere. This is exactly the group-action version of the character-orbit criterion; `R3-1_report.md:75-86` is complete without importing a separate Halmos theorem.

- **The vector/tensor conclusion is correct, but the printed proof needs replacement.** The assertions that its (M_r) is scalar on every *isotypic* block and that Jensen directly gives (M_r\ge I) are not justified when a (K)-type has multiplicity; sector equidistribution is also more machinery than needed (`R3-1_report.md:88-93`). A clean proof is: an equivariant (L^2) field (f:T^4\to W) has finite second moment (C=\int f f^*\), and equivariance makes (C) an invariant positive form on the essential span of (f). That span is therefore a finite-dimensional unitarizable subrepresentation. A finite-dimensional Lorentz representation has no nontrivial unitarizable subrepresentation of the noncompact connected simple factor, so only the trivial Lorentz subrepresentation remains; scalar ergodicity then makes that component constant. Hence regular off-site hopping is still excluded, while invariant on-site intertwiners may remain.

- **Spinors need one extra hypothesis.** One must specify a lift of Γ_L to the spin group (or work with an endomorphism/bilinear representation on which the central sign cancels). With that qualification the same second-moment argument applies. The unqualified phrase “spinor fields” at `R3-1_report.md:88` is not fully proved as written.

## 2. Proposition P and locality

- **The threshold is confirmed modulo the cited standard orbit-counting theorem.** A fixed nonzero timelike or spacelike orbit has \(N_{\mathcal O}(T)\sim c_{\mathcal O}T^2\); partial summation gives convergence of \(\sum_{d'\in\mathcal O}|d'|_E^{-s}\) exactly for (s>2), logarithmic divergence at (s=2), and power divergence below it. This supports `R3-1_report.md:95-109`.

- The numerical rerun is byte-for-byte identical to the supplied output. The last-window exponents are 1.9953--2.0016 and the (s=2) sums grow logarithmically (`referee_r31_counting_rerun.out:4-51`). The program counts an entire norm shell, however, not an individual Γ_L-orbit (`r31_counting.py:4-7`). A norm shell is a finite union of arithmetic orbits, so the computation is consistent with the theorem but is not by itself a numerical measurement of each orbit's exponent.

- `R3-1_report.md:106` is false as a general diagnostic: critical exponent (2) does not by itself prove finite covolume; infinite-covolume, co-amenable subgroups can have the ambient critical exponent. Cocompactness comes from the already-refereed arithmetic/Godement argument, not this fit.

- **The refutation of “power-law quasi-locality” is correct.** Once the orbit average is formed, its finite value is repeated at every member of an infinite orbit, so the resulting off-site kernel is in no finite ℓ^p space and has no Euclidean decay along that orbit (`R3-1_report.md:112-115`). The important correction is that Proposition P proves convergence of the *orbit-average coefficient*, not convergence of the hopping sum over (d), a bounded convolution operator, or a real-momentum Fourier symbol. The phrase “convergent Poincaré-series law” should therefore be replaced by “pointwise-defined invariant orbit weights.” The further proportionality across arbitrary different orbits claimed at `R3-1_report.md:113` is not established; exact \(|d^2|^{-s/2}\) scaling follows along integer dilations, while primitive-orbit constants can carry arithmetic dependence.

## 3. Proposition R, its converse, and the arrow

- **The direct convergence statement is correct.** For a past-timelike (d), (v=\operatorname{Im}p\in V_+) gives (v\cdot d\ge c_v|d|_E). A polynomially bounded invariant kernel is therefore dominated by a polynomial times (e^{-c_v|d|_E}), so its lattice Laplace/Fourier series is absolutely convergent and holomorphic in the open forward tube (`R3-1_report.md:117-122`).

- **The converse is correct for a nonzero orbit, but its scope and proof need sharpening.** For (v=0), terms on a nonzero orbit have constant nonzero modulus and fail the necessary term-to-zero test; they are not “unbounded” as stated at `R3-1_report.md:126`. For (v\ne0), sector-unboundedness of the arithmetic orbit gives exponential growth outside the appropriate timelike tube. A past-supported nonzero timelike orbit therefore has ordinary absolute convergence exactly in (T_+); spacelike support or support on both time orientations has none. Cocompactness alone does not justify the report's literal “coarsely dense” sentence on the de Sitter orbit (`R3-1_report.md:123-128`); the required replacement is the affine-symmetric-space sector-counting result.

- **“No time-symmetric law converges” is true only for the ordinary pointwise/unconditional lattice series.** A nonzero symmetric orbit kernel has no pointwise Fourier series anywhere, but it can still define a distribution, a regularized boundary value, or a bilinear form on a specially chosen test space. The explicit BD-count equation is consequently well-defined on exponentially past-decaying modes/in the tube, not established as dynamics on generic bounded real-time fields and not as a real-momentum S-matrix (`R3-1_report.md:128-134`).

- **Arrow without a preferred frame is correct in the precise group-theoretic sense.** Retardation selects a time orientation, but Γ_L fixes no nonzero vector by Zariski density. O1's averaging Lemma E is restricted to finite/compact groups and a proper convex cone (`C5_O1_report.md:155-163`; `C5_O1_referee.md:21`). Thus there is no invariant timelike vector/rest frame, although the law is time-oriented and time-reversal asymmetric (`R3-1_report.md:136`).

## 4. Invariant-polynomial counts and analyticity

- **The counts are confirmed.** The rerun reproduces dimensions (0,1,0,1,0,1) for homogeneous scalar polynomials of degrees 1--6 and two invariant quadratic forms on \(\operatorname{Sym}^2\) (`referee_r31_invariants_rerun.out:13-20`). Borel density supplies the all-degree theorem: Γ_L-invariant algebraic tensors are invariant under the connected Lorentz group. The computed counts concern one-momentum scalar polynomials; “Lorentz” here means the proper connected group, so parity-odd structures involving additional arguments are not excluded by this particular table.

- The two graviton forms are (h_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}) and (h^2). Calling this the “Fierz--Pauli count” is potentially misleading: there are two Lorentz-invariant mass structures, while Fierz--Pauli is the special ghost-avoiding relative combination. This corrects `R3-1_report.md:149-152` without changing the number.

- **The universal “no Taylor expansion at (p=0)” wording is too strong for the proof presented.** Lemma M excludes a nonconstant global (L^2) symbol. Companion Lemma A correctly shows that a nontrivial invariant periodic scalar symbol cannot be real-analytic on an entire invariant neighborhood \(\{|p^2|<r\}\) (`R3-2_report_unrefereed.md:183-197`). The Bragg cones do accumulate kinematically at zero (`R3-1_report.md:159-163`), but they are actual singularities only when the chosen continuum transform retains a light-cone singular term; the report itself labels that transform expansion and the moment-cancellation discussion “suggested” (`R3-1_report.md:164-166`). What is proved for all hopping kernels is absence of a global regular (L^2) symbol, not absence of every possible finite/asymptotic local expansion after signed multi-shell cancellations.

## 5. Per-realization direction dependence

- The requested rerun of `r31_lv_scaling.py` is byte-for-byte identical to `r31_lv_scaling.out`; it used 3,178,500 and 4,813,293 shell points and reproduced the quoted global slopes 1.302/1.385 (relative) and −0.879/−0.957 (absolute), with large truncation margins (`referee_r31_lv_scaling_rerun.out:2-33`). The exact Γ_L-related-direction check and the oblique-direction discrepancy are also present in the original computation (`r31_lv_orbitsum.out:14-19`). The effect is far above the estimated truncation tail.

- **The direction dependence is real continuous-Lorentz breaking of the fixed realization's tube symbol, not a coordinate or truncation artefact.** At fixed (p^2=-\kappa^2), a continuously Lorentz-invariant scalar transform would be independent of (u); the observed function is constant on Γ_L-images but not on the full hyperboloid. It is therefore accurately called an automorphic, per-realization effect.

- **The claimed scaling law is not established.** The data are strongly oscillatory. Merely dropping the two endpoints changes the (N=-3) relative slope from 1.302 to 0.938; restricting to \(\kappa\le0.1\) gives 2.281 and an *absolute* slope (+0.263). For (N=-7), reasonable windows give relative slopes from 0.607 to 2.245 (`referee_fit_sensitivity.out:1-12`). These runs show nonzero direction dependence over the sampled range, but neither an asymptotic \((\kappa\tau)^{1.3}\) law nor a proved nonshrinking absolute limit. The proposed automorphic spectral explanation remains suggested, as the worker itself partly acknowledges at `R3-1_report.md:189-194,301-304`.

- **Physical implication:** imaginary momentum is a legitimate analytic diagnostic but not an on-shell observable. It proves that this shell's holomorphic tube function is not continuously Lorentz invariant. It does not determine a real-energy dispersion, a cross section, a dimension-≤4 EFT coefficient, or a magnitude in physical units. Shell weights and the moment cancellations needed for a physical retarded operator can change or cancel the raw absolute divergence; the mixed multi-shell results at `R3-1_report.md:195-198` demonstrate precisely that unresolved dependence.

## 6. Bragg resonances and momentum

- The resonance rerun is byte-for-byte identical. On the (G^2=4\pi^2) reciprocal shell it reproduces the null counts through 1378 and 2774, the 240-vector rest-frame massive shell, and the boosted massive counts 19, 44, 75 (`referee_r31_bragg_resonance_rerun.out:1-5`). The algebra \((k-G)^2=k^2+G^2-2k\cdot G\) and the quoted shell locations are correct.

- The kinematic statement that boosted spacelike reciprocal vectors generate Bragg cones accumulating at the origin is correct. The reported counts are finite-cutoff, fixed-width \(|(k-G)^2|<1\) counts on one norm shell; they do not prove the residue or physical strength of a singularity, nor that an explicitly moment-cancelled kernel retains it. The conditional wording at `R3-1_report.md:215-220` is appropriate; the unconditional “singular cones” wording at `R3-1_report.md:18` is not.

- **Bloch/quasi-momentum conservation modulo (2\pi L_7^*) is confirmed, but exact in-band momentum conservation is not general.** The hard gap excludes a nonzero reciprocal transfer in a one-to-one process, and more generally whenever the *total* transfer is norm-bounded below the reciprocal gap. It does not exclude multiparticle Umklapp. For example, (N) future-null quanta of momentum \((\pi/N,\pi/N,0,0)\) can scatter to (N) with \((\pi/N,-\pi/N,0,0)\): energy is conserved and the total spatial transfer is \((0,2\pi,0,0)\in2\pi L_7^*\), while every individual energy can be arbitrarily small. Translation symmetry and a local \(\phi^4\) vertex do not forbid such (N\to N) processes at sufficiently high order. Thus “every real in-band process ... conserves exact 4-momentum, at every order” at `R3-1_report.md:226` is false; the earlier R2-2 referee only confirmed ideal one-to-one Bloch silence and explicitly left multiphoton/inelastic sectors open (`R2-2_referee.md:18-20`).

- On the hypotheses quoted in this packet, the regular-band boost-wall theorems do not apply to the non-(L^2), tube-only symbols, and they are not thereby refuted (`R3-1_report.md:230-238`). Their source reports are not included here, so this audit confirms only that logical non-implication, not every quoted hypothesis of those external theorems.

## Strongest correct statement

For the cocompact arithmetic lattice (L_7), every nonzero displacement has an infinite Γ_L-orbit, so a scalar Γ_L-invariant (L^2) torus symbol is constant almost everywhere and any nonzero orbit-constant hopping kernel is non-ℓ^p and non-decaying in Euclidean distance; the same regular-symbol conclusion extends to finite-dimensional Lorentz vector/tensor representations, with the stated spin-lift qualification. Each fixed nonzero timelike or spacelike orbit has (T^2) growth, so Euclidean-seed Poincaré coefficients converge exactly for (s>2), but this does not make the resulting hopping law convergent. Nonzero past-timelike kernels with polynomially bounded orbit weights have ordinary holomorphic symbols precisely in the forward tube, whereas nonzero time-symmetric or spacelike-orbit kernels have no ordinary pointwise Fourier series; distributional formulations remain open. All polynomial coefficients that exist are proper-Lorentz invariant, with scalar dimensions (0,1,0,1,0,1) through degree six and two symmetric-tensor mass structures. The computed shell transforms genuinely break continuous Lorentz invariance in their imaginary-momentum directional dependence, and Bragg cones accumulate kinematically, but neither a robust IR exponent nor a physical real-momentum Lorentz-violating observable has been established. Momentum is conserved modulo the reciprocal lattice; exact ordinary conservation is protected only when the total transfer lies inside the gap, not for arbitrary multiparticle in-band processes. Therefore the strongest campaign reading remains **INCONCLUSIVE**.
