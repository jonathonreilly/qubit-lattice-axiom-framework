# VERDICT: NOT CONFIRMED

The report contains a correct conditional momentum-balance lemma and a correct vacuum-localization lemma, and its central Oppenheim Table 1 reading is accurate.  Its headline result nevertheless does not survive: the compact-spacetime-support premise is not shown for a persistent collapse, the order-counting premise is narrower than a general quantized linearized graviton, C3 is not exhaustive, Lemma F does not prove its per-realization/rest-frame conclusion, and neither JKMS nor gravitational dressing supplies the completed models needed to label E1 and E4 `VIABLE` under the fixed readings.  The E5 emission estimate also confuses momentum spread with a change of total momentum.  Those are load-bearing, not cosmetic, corrections.

## Findings

1. **Lemma P: a sound conditional core, but not the claimed classification.**  If (i) the *total non-gravitational* stress is a globally defined distribution, (ii) its non-conservation/exchange has compact spacetime support (or sufficient spacetime decay), (iii) the relevant total four-momenta exist, and (iv) the other sector begins in its unique ground state and its final momentum expectation is finite, slab integration does give
   \[
   \Delta P_m^\nu+\Delta P_o^\nu=0,
   \qquad \Delta P_o\in\overline V_+,
   \qquad \Delta P_m\in-\overline V_+.
   \]
   Thus the algebra in `R3-4_report.md:133-138` is correct under its actual premises.  The unimodular step is also correct *if* \(\partial^\nu\Lambda\) itself has compact spacetime support: the complement of a compact set in \(\mathbb R^4\) is connected, so \(\Lambda\) has the same exterior constant before and after, and the integrated gradient vanishes (`R3-4_report.md:134-136`).  But a collapse-induced conditional state generally differs for its entire future; spatial localization of a body does not make \(\Delta T\) compact in spacetime.  The assertion that P2 “covers rGRWf flashes on localized bodies” is therefore unproved (`R3-4_report.md:125`).  For a flash-point ontology, one would still need a separately defined conserved stress/source bridge.

   The spectrum-condition step is valid for a normalized final state (or density operator) with finite first moment: spectral support in \(\overline V_+\) puts its four-momentum expectation there.  It is not by itself a statement about a separately interacting subsystem momentum.  The O(G) statement is standard for a *classically sourced* perturbation \(h=O(G)\), for which \(t_{\rm grav}\sim(\partial h)^2/G=O(G)\), but it is an extra scaling hypothesis, not a general fact about the quantum graviton: a finite-energy free graviton has canonically normalized amplitude \(h=O(\sqrt G)\) and energy \(O(G^0)\).  This limits `R3-4_report.md:127-138` to its declared P4 sector.

   C1 survives only in the bounded form already available from R2-3: a nonrelativistic real position-multiplier channel has strictly positive *mean* heating, so at least one positive-probability branch cannot coexist with P1-P4 (`R3-4_report.md:141-145`; `R2-3_referee.md:9`).  C3 does not follow as an exhaustive list.  It omits, among other failures of its own hypotheses, spacetime-nonlocal/temporally extended operations (P2), gravitational states with different power counting (P4), and retrocausal or two-boundary mechanisms that the report explicitly put out of scope (`R3-4_report.md:110-114`).  Hence “metric resets must be acausal re-solutions on a slice” and the E1/E4/E5/E2/E3 exhaustion are not proved (`R3-4_report.md:153-164`).

2. **C2 is confirmed, with domain and scope qualifications.**  For a bounded Kraus operator \(K\) in a bounded-region algebra, Reeh-Schlieder separation gives \(K\Omega=c\Omega\Rightarrow K=c1\).  Therefore a non-scalar \(K\) gives a non-vacuum branch.  If \(K\Omega\) is in the quadratic-form domain of \(H\), the spectrum condition plus uniqueness of the translation-invariant vacuum implies \(\langle H\rangle>0\); if it is outside that domain, its energy is infinite rather than a finite counterexample.  This confirms `R3-4_report.md:146-152` for nonzero vacuum branches.  It does not prove branchwise heating on arbitrary excited inputs (the report's own hot-state check has negative-energy branches, `r34_checks.out:5`) or establish the C3 classification.

3. **Lemma F is not proved as stated.**  A *Poincare*-invariant state with a well-defined stress one-point function has constant \(\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle=C\eta_{\mu\nu}\).  Lorentz invariance alone does not give that global form: position-dependent tensors can also be built from \(x_\mu x_\nu\).  More importantly, an invariant one-point function controls only an ensemble mean; it does not show that each realization cannot transfer four-momentum.  A Lorentz-invariant ensemble may have non-invariant, momentum-carrying realizations whose mean is proportional to \(\eta\).  The inference in `R3-4_report.md:169-174` therefore does not establish the advertised per-realization no-go.  If the realized stress is additionally Hawking-Ellis type I, it has a timelike eigenvector and hence a rest-frame direction (not necessarily a unique one under degeneracy).  Types II-IV are not excluded and need not possess a rest frame.  The strongest defensible statement is conditional: a non-degenerate type-I reservoir stress selects a timelike direction in each paying realization.

4. **E1/JKMS: the source attribution is right; the signalling attribution and `VIABLE` reading are too strong.**  JKMS explicitly uses \(G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}=8\pi G\,\omega(T^{\rm ren}_{ab})\), changes from \(\omega^-\) to the post-collapse \(\omega^+\), and re-solves the constraints in the next epoch.  The paper's tentative rule keeps the induced metric and transverse-traceless part of the extrinsic curvature continuous and jumps the other four components of the extrinsic curvature; calling it simply a “metric jump” is imprecise (`R3-4_report.md:182-186`; [JKMS, pp. 35-39](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.11671)).  JKMS also explicitly leaves both the generally covariant stochastic collapse law and the well-posedness results conjectural.

   The toy calculation is correct for an ideal projective, basis-dependent conditional-state source: the exact-field distributions \(\tfrac12(\delta_{g_L}+\delta_{g_R})\) and \(\delta_{g_{\rm mean}}\) have total-variation distance one (`R3-4_report.md:188-198`; `r34_checks.out:7-14`).  “O(1)” describes ideal statistical distinguishability, not the field amplitude, which the same calculation puts at \(6.7\times10^{-16}\,{\rm m\,s^{-2}}\).  It is not a theorem about the undeveloped JKMS objective-collapse law.  Bahrami et al. prove signalling for deterministic Schrodinger-Newton evolution plus the conventional collapse postulate and explicitly note that suitably stochastic collapse dynamics can avoid that argument ([Bahrami et al., secs. 4-5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.4370)).  E1 is therefore `OPEN`, not conditionally `VIABLE`, under `TESTS.md:2699-2702`.

5. **E2 literature check: the two principal claims are confirmed, but the open windows are bounded statements.**  The published Oppenheim et al. Table 1 really gives \(10^{-41}\ge D_2\ge10^{-9}\ {\rm kg^2\,s\,m^{-3}}\) for the continuous ultra-local nonrelativistic kernel: an empty 32-decade interval (`R3-4_report.md:225-235`; `r34_checks.out:28-31`; [Oppenheim et al., Table 1 and Eq. 44](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10696068/)).  That exclusion assumes the paper's ultra-local continuous model, polynomial/background-potential dependence and trade-off estimate; it is not an exclusion of all continuous CQ gravity.  The quoted 26-decade non-local and 24-decade discrete intervals are also in Table 1, but mean “not excluded by this squeeze,” not a complete relativistic viability demonstration.

   The correction to R2-3 is correct.  Oppenheim and Weller-Davies give a manifestly diffeomorphism-invariant CQ path-integral candidate for GR, beyond the Nordstrom construction (`R2-3_report.md:248-250`; `R3-4_report.md:240-245`; [Oppenheim-Weller-Davies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.07283)).  Their general path-integral form is proved completely positive under stated kernel conditions; the full GR application remains a candidate with further normalization, constraint, renormalization and phenomenology work.  The Newtonian Tilloy-Diosi and massive-flash statements are accurately bounded by their cited assumptions (`R3-4_report.md:236-237`), but none supplies set A's tested covariant quantum-graviton realization.  E2 as a set-A escape remains `OPEN`; its ultra-local continuous Newtonian subcase is `EXCLUDED`, while named Newtonian non-local/discrete subcases remain viable only within their tested windows.

6. **E3, E4 and E5 require material corrections.**

   - **E3.** Equilibrium Bohmian/Bell-type models with a fixed preferred foliation reproduce ordinary quantum predictions in their constructed domains, so that narrow route is `VIABLE` as an empirical escape, at the price of replacing A4's rare collapse flashes with non-back-reacting beables (`R3-4_report.md:262-287`; `C5_O5_synthesis.md:326-328`).  The stronger wave-function-determined-foliation version is programmatic: its own source presents candidate extractions and future work rather than a completed interacting QFT-plus-gravity model ([Durr et al., pp. 12-13](https://arxiv.org/abs/1307.1714)).  The claims that definite outcomes *require* a dense history and that there can be no nonlinear metric beable do not follow: correlated sparse histories are not excluded, and foliation-dependent Bohmian 3-metrics are still metric beables.  Those “prices” are suggestions, not necessities (`R3-4_report.md:288-292`).  Thus E3 is `VIABLE` only in the preferred-foliation constructed scope; the worker's covariant/state-determined strengthening is `OPEN`.  “Cheapest” has no registered cost functional and is not established.

   - **E4.** Donnelly-Giddings does confirm gauge-invariant gravitational dressings and spacelike noncommutativity at leading order \(G\) for the dressings studied (`R3-4_report.md:296-302`; [Donnelly-Giddings, sec. IV](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.07921)).  It does not construct a completely positive dressed Kraus collapse instrument, show that it has the advertised stochastic/covariant law, or prove that exactly one spacelike slice per flash is necessary.  That necessity rests solely on the non-exhaustive C3 (`R3-4_report.md:304-308`).  The numerical ADM heating rates are ensemble means, not fixed per-realization mass jumps; the report itself demonstrates branch-dependent energy changes (`r34_checks.out:1-5`).  E4 is `OPEN`, not `VIABLE`, under the fixed rule.

   - **E5.** The rerun reproduces the Earth-store density, dark-radiation flux, and local-dark-matter kinematic flux (`r34_checks.out:23-26,39-42`).  They are upper-resource estimates, not delivery/capture constructions, so `OPEN` is correct.  The “13 decades” statement applies only if the Earth-required *local* store density is imposed uniformly; the same script finds cosmic GRW heating accumulated over a Hubble time to be \(5.4\times10^{-27}\,{\rm J\,m^{-3}}\), about 17 decades *below* \(\rho_\Lambda c^2\), so it is not a general cosmological exclusion (`R3-4_report.md:338-341`; `r34_checks.out:23-26`).  The E5-prime number is wrong in principle: `r34_checks.py:121-130` substitutes the rms momentum **spread** \(\sqrt{\langle p^2\rangle}\) for the change in total momentum \(|\Delta\langle\mathbf P\rangle|\).  A real Gaussian localization of a real centered state can have \(\Delta\langle\mathbf P\rangle=0\) while raising its energy.  Lemma P therefore supplies no 2.4 eV per-flash lower bound, and the derived \(1.4\times10^{17}\) W Earth emission figure does not follow (`R3-4_report.md:342`; `r34_checks.out:33-37`).  For scale, the script's actual GRW mean localization energy is only \(4.99\times10^{-28}\) J \(=3.11\times10^{-9}\) eV per nucleon hit (`r34_checks.out:16-19`), though that mean is not itself a branchwise emission lower bound.

## Strongest correct statement

Under global linearized Einstein (or trace-free unimodular) dynamics, finite total momenta, a genuinely compact-in-spacetime exchange, and a compensating sector initially in its unique positive-energy ground state, slab integration implies \(\Delta P_m=-\Delta P_o\in-\overline V_+\); consequently the bounded nonrelativistic GRW position-multiplier class, whose ensemble energy gain is strictly positive, cannot satisfy all those premises, and any bounded non-scalar local Kraus operator acting nontrivially on the unique QFT vacuum produces a positive-energy branch.  These results do not classify all gravity/collapse couplings: the published 32-decade exclusion applies only to ultra-local continuous Newtonian CQ gravity, a covariant CQ-GR candidate exists but remains open, fixed-foliation equilibrium beables provide the only presently explicit empirically viable route surveyed here that preserves A5 (while changing set A's collapse reading), and JKMS resets, wave-function-selected foliations, dressed Kraus collapse laws, and reservoir models remain `OPEN`; no theorem here establishes that every viable compensator has a rest frame, that one slice per flash is necessary, or that E3 is uniquely cheapest.
