The spatial-spatial components force every $f^i=0$, because the three first derivatives of a point delta are linearly independent; after that, a time-spatial component forces $f^0=0$. The omitted $\dot f$ and $\dot X$ terms in $\partial_0J^i$ cause no gap because the spatial-spatial equations have already made $J^i=0$. This establishes the point-source obstruction in `R4-4_report.md:140-149`, subject to cancellation when several contributions have coincident support. It is enough for the literal point-particle C0 counterexample.

   The global claim in `R4-4_report.md:151-154` needs stronger falloff. Merely requiring that $\Lambda$ tend to one angularly independent constant does not make $\int\partial_i\Lambda\,d^3x$ vanish. For example, $\Lambda=C+a x_1/(1+r^2)^{3/2}$ tends to $C$, while its spherical boundary flux tends to $4\pi a/3$ (`referee_checks.py:19-22`).
