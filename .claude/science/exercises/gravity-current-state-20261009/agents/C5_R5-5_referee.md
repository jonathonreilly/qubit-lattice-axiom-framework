## 1. Theorem F, Corollary F′, and the reading of “frame averaged”

**Theorem F is exact but essentially the direct-integral superselection identity.**  For a normalized measurable section and decomposable `U = direct-integral(U_X)`, `O = direct-integral(O_X)`, the inner product is diagonal in `X`, giving exactly the formula at `R5-5_report.md:85-91`.  The same is true for compositions of decomposable instruments.  “Every measurable expectation” must therefore be read as “every expectation in the stipulated decomposable observable algebra,” not every observable allowed by quantum mechanics.  F3 is the load-bearing premise: it removes all cross-`X` matrix elements (`R5-5_report.md:66-68,93-100`).  The result is useful—it rules out obtaining a coherent operator average from this particular matter-on-a-fixed-realization algebra—but it is mathematically a superselection statement.

F2/F3 are one natural model of matter confined to a realization, but they are **not forced** by the words “only the frame-averaged law is physical.”  A group-averaged/relational theory, realization-changing dynamics, or an operational quantum-reference-frame algebra need not be decomposable.  Giacomini–Castro-Ruiz–Brukner treat a quantum-frame change as a unitary superposition of coordinate transformations with transformed observables, not as an assumed diagonal observable algebra ([arXiv:1712.07207](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.07207)); the worker correctly puts such dynamics outside its class at `R5-5_report.md:71-78`, but then cannot claim that its class is the unique reading of the FIXED premise.

**Corollary F′ is correct after two qualifications.**  In canonically identified/common matter fibres, for a *linear subspace* `K`, preservation of `|D(X,X′)| = 1` for every vector in `K` is equivalent to `U_X|K = exp(i theta_X) U_X0|K`.  Equality in Cauchy–Schwarz gives collinearity, and testing sums fixes one phase (`R5-5_report.md:107-110`).  For a single populated state it proves only collinearity of that one pair of output states; for a set not closed under superposition the “same phase for all populated states” step does not follow.  Also, the displayed reduced frame kernel and “frame decoherence equals matter dephasing” require the common-fibre, common-input, pure-state specialization already introduced at `R5-5_report.md:96-100`; they are not defined for arbitrary inequivalent fibres in Theorem F.

This settles a narrower question: an exact target **unitary channel on all states** cannot arise as a nontrivial random-unitary mixture, except when the branch unitaries agree up to phase.  It does not show that propagation decoheres the frame, and it does not exclude coincidences on a finite observed state/observable set.

## 2. Lemma P0

**Confirmed within F1, with scope narrowed.**  Under the geometric left action on `L²(P/(Gamma_L ⋉ L_7))`, translation shifts the torus coordinate and a dual-lattice Fourier component acquires the stated character; the `G* = 0`, `u`-independent orientation sector is therefore translation-trivial and has `P^mu = 0` (`R5-5_report.md:132-141`).  Since the Poincaré action is transitive, its invariant vectors are constants (finite invariant volume assumed).  Consequently the physical time-translation generator `P^0` has no gap between the invariant vector and bare orientation excitations in this representation.

The stronger prose needs correction.  Zero momentum makes a transition kinematically degenerate; it does not prove a nonzero interaction matrix element or decoherence (`R5-5_report.md:137-139`).  Nor does the argument exclude every Poincaré-covariant model with additional/dressed frame degrees of freedom and a different representation.  It excludes a gap generated by `P^0` **on F1’s geometric orientation sector**.  Adding the toy `H_F` as an external Schrödinger Hamiltonian indeed introduces an evolution not equal to the geometric Poincaré time translation (`R5-5_report.md:148-152`).

## 3. Lemma AE

**The finite-support lemma is confirmable, but the report should internalize rather than merely cite the unrefereed R5-2 step.**  For finite support, `D_j(s)` is entire.  The Mellin formula quoted from R5-2 is algebraically correct: direct integration at `s = 1.3`, `r = 1.7` agrees with its Gamma-product form to `1.1×10^-36` (`referee_checks.py:21-36`; `referee_checks.out:1-3`).  A contour shift yields the `k = 0` residues plus lower powers and a uniformly summable remainder.

The uniform-convergence estimate at `R5-5_report.md:165-169` has the right mechanism but suppresses an important line of proof.  For a fixed finite orbit set, `|A_j| = O(r_j)`, `||psi_j||_infinity = O(r_j)`, the leading transform contains `|Gamma(1/2 + i r_j/2)|/r_j`, and hence the pointwise leading summand is at most a polynomial times `exp(-pi r_j/4)`.  Weyl growth `N(R) = O(R^3)` then gives absolute uniform convergence.  The shifted-contour remainders require the same uniform-in-`j` Gamma bound; that is the load-bearing content imported from `R5-2_report_unrefereed.md:70-95`, not the numerical `10^-11` check.  It is straightforward for a finite Dirichlet polynomial, but it must be stated to make AE self-contained.

After grouping multiplicities, `E_r(u) = sum_{r_j=r} A_j psi_j(u)` is analytic.  R4-1’s refereed wavefront result guarantees at least one tempered eigenspace with `E_r` not identically zero for any finite-orbit kernel with a nonzero off-site weight (`C5_R4-1_referee_verdict.md:11`; `R5-5_report.md:170-174`).  Its nodal set has measure zero.  Outside that set the uniformly convergent leading series is a nonzero Bohr almost-periodic function; such a function cannot tend to zero at positive infinity, so the positive limsup follows.  Threshold and exceptional eigenspaces are finite; after grouping the highest nonzero growth rate they cannot remove the limsup.  The sentence that they “only increase the growth” is informal because cancellations can occur at isolated scales, but the conclusion remains valid.

Thus AE is sound for the corrected R4-1 class and Haar-a.e. orientation, modulo the explicit finite-support Mellin/remainder lemma and the already-refereed orbifold/torsion qualifications.  It proves an off-shell Gaussian-family limsup, not an on-shell propagation effect (`R5-5_report.md:178-180`).

## 4. Lemma C

**A narrow domain-support lemma is correct; the set-A conclusion is over-broad.**  If `Bbar` is literally defined by pointwise evaluation of an otherwise arbitrary field on a set `S`, and it must act at every `x in S`, then a nonzero shell term requires the field on `x - H_m^+` almost everywhere.  Iteration reaches the convolution support `H_m^+ + H_m^+`, whose interior is the future-timelike region with `-v^2 > 4m`.  Therefore no countable realization supplies all independent point values (`R5-5_report.md:224-228`).

At `R5-5_report.md:227`, the equality with `-v^2 >= 4m` is correct including the boundary, but that set is not open; only its strict interior is.  More importantly, the argument assumes literal independent point values.  It does not exclude interpolation/bandlimited reconstruction, an induced operator on discrete data, group-averaged relational observables, or other non-decomposable frame dynamics.  Hence “this particular hyperboloid integral needs a continuum field domain” is proved; “every physical implementation of the frame mean collapses to set A” is not.

## 5. Zero counts and instability

**The reported calculations reproduce and the three tested four-shell kernels are unstable under the stated plane-wave criterion.**  All worker scripts reproduce their saved stdout exactly.  An independent implementation gives keyhole windings 14, 4, and 16 at both 2,000 and 10,000 samples per segment (`referee_checks.py:81-114`; `referee_checks.out:5-18`). The two `m <= 96` smooth profiles independently have zero winding for `10^-5 < |z| < 10^4` and `10^-5 < |z| < 10^6` (`referee_checks.out:19-24`).  That is no-zero evidence only in those contours.  It neither proves global stability nor says anything about the infinite-support limits; the worker mostly acknowledges this at `R5-5_report.md:235-236,308-313`.

The scope correction is material: three examples do not prove that **all** four-shell mean kernels are unstable.  The correct statement is “the three tested four-shell choices have complex zeros (and hence unstable modes), whereas the two tested truncated smooth profiles have none in the checked cut-plane annuli” (`R5-5_report.md:230-235`; `scripts/r55_B_mean_law_stability.py:27-48`).

## 7. Reading and precise class

**FAIL should not rest on (i)+(ii) alone.**  The FIXED alternatives distinguish dynamical decoherence from preserved coherence (`TESTS.md:3000-3007`).  Theorem F plus AE show that, in an imposed superselection algebra, all allowed statistics are those of a classical mixture over a.e. bad off-shell branches.  They do not show that the reduced frame loses coherence, and a classical mixture of branch predictions is not automatically evolution by the averaged operator.  That is a valid no-mean-unitary result for a narrow model, but it is not the registered observed-scale-decoherence result.

The class should be stated as: normalized `L²` frame states on the geometric homogeneous-space representation; a measurable direct integral with fixed/decomposable controlled dynamics; the algebra of all allowed instruments restricted to decomposable operators; canonically common matter fibres and a linear observed subspace whenever reduced-frame overlaps or F′ are invoked; and R4-1’s fixed linear retarded scalar finite-orbit law with at least one nonzero off-site orbit.  Unitary quantum dynamics, the realization Hilbert space, F2/F3, Born weights, and an on-shell readout are downstream hypotheses, not consequences of the four minimal axioms (`MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-06-29.md:85-95,114-129`).

On the pre-registered ternary reading, the present evidence is **INCONCLUSIVE within the proposed physical model, while strongly excluding the exact frame-average-as-unitary mechanism in the narrower superselection class**.  A bounded FAIL would require an actual overlap/visibility calculation from a derived on-shell coupling for observed matter states (or a revised FIXED criterion that explicitly counts kinematic superselection as failure).
