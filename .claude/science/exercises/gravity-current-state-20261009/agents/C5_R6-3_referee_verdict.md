# VERDICT: CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTIONS

1. **Variance formula — confirmed.** Parseval, \(T^6\) shell scaling, timelike/spacelike stabilizer unfolding, and \(C_t^\ast\) are correct. Independent mod-\(p\) enumeration reproduced the stated densities for \(p=3,5,11,13\). The dual class-number-one claim needs the explicit involution \(A\mapsto7A^{-1}\); it is not automatic from “hence.” Also, \(0.1665\) is a numerical evaluation, not an exact coefficient. See `R6-3_report.md:113-179`, `scripts/r63_exact.py:122-156`, `referee_checks.out:7-11`, and the [Eskin–Rudnick–Sarnak mass formula](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/siegelmass.pdf).

2. **Theorem D — confirmed, with a wrong advertised constant.** Direct quadrature verifies the closed diamond transform. The bound \(J_D(M)=O((1+\log|M|)/M^2)\) and leading asymptotic \(16\pi^3\log M/M^2\) are correct. However, the report’s \(K\le1.7\times10^4\) is false for its own integrated envelope: the ratio reaches approximately \(2.0004\times10^4\) near \(M=165\). The fitted constant term \(B/M^2\) is numerical; rigorously it should be written \(O(M^{-2})\). See `R6-3_report.md:184-283`, `scripts/r63_proof_bounds.py:24-42`, and `referee_checks.out:1-3,12-14`.

3. **Past cone — \(O(T^3)\) is correct after repairing the proof, but the numerical evidence is partly defective.** At spacelike \(\omega=0\), the base and cone terms must be combined before estimating; their endpoint cancellation gives \(O(k^{-3})\). The timelike rest-frame \(L^2\) contribution gives \(J_P(-m)=O(m^{-3/2})\), hence variance \(O(T^3)\). The rare-frame mechanism—probability \(O(T^{-3})\) times counts \(O(T^3)\)—correctly explains both the mean and variance scale. But the production quadrature misses the increasingly narrow rest-frame peak above about \(M=10^8\), and its closed form is unstable near the origin. Thus \(0.4847\) is a convergent numerical estimate, not exact. See `R6-3_report.md:286-393`, `scripts/r63_formfactor.py:83-105`, `scripts/r63_Jtable.out:60-66`, and `referee_checks.out:6,15-16`.

4. **Counterexample — confirmed qualitatively, numerical coefficient uncertified.** The Lorentz-random affine \(\mathbb Z^4\) ensemble is stationary, Lorentz invariant, simple, and has finite local second moments. Its null reciprocal vectors are allowed by the literal gap and contribute \(c_0T^6\) with \(c_0>0\). Primitive null cusps have finite invariant density; multiples converge through \(\sum n^{-2}\). Heath-Brown’s nonsquare-discriminant quaternary estimate gives \(O(X^2)\), so the non-null part remains sub-volume. The quoted \(0.042T^6\) is numerically plausible—about \(0.0421T^6\)—but lacks certified truncation/error bounds. See `R6-3_report.md:396-486`, `scripts/r63_null_counterexample.py:218-262`, and [Heath-Brown’s original paper record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:bbd3c62f-f010-44b5-8be5-87e903fb0084).

5. **Lemma V′ — derived after two proof repairs.** Finite local second moments imply translation-bounded diffraction, which yields linear shell-weight growth. With the null cone included in the spectral gap, orbit decomposition plus the diamond and cone decay estimates gives sub-volume variance. The report’s Gaussian domination by \(N(A_1)\) is invalid because a Gaussian is not compactly supported; compact mollifiers or the standard \(L^2\) extension repair it. Orbit decomposition should also explicitly separate future/past timelike and null half-cones. Finally, the claim that every independent superposition adds a white spacelike component is false; thinning and Cox randomization do, arbitrary superposition does not. See `R6-3_report.md:489-596,631-640`.

6. **Cosmological consequence — arithmetic confirmed, interpretation properly conditional.** Recalculation gives approximately \(1.4113\times10^{30}\) for the cone and \(1.3660\times10^{59}\) for the diamond. Under H1, \(\Delta\Lambda\propto1/\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}N}\), so sub-volume count variance indeed produces *larger*, not smaller, \(\Lambda\) fluctuations. “Too large” is therefore the right reading under H1/H2/H4. The epoch exponents are correct for homothetic flat regions; comparison with \(H^2\sim T^{-2}\) assumes a power-law FRW epoch and is not universal in a \(\Lambda\)-dominated era. See `R6-3_report.md:599-696` and `referee_checks.out:17-19`.

7. **Readings — confirmed.** Count: **SUB-VOLUME**. Literal Lemma V(d): **REFUTED**. Null-inclusive V′: **DERIVED**, with the repairs above. Strict lensing silence: **OPEN**. See `TESTS.md:240-270` and `R6-3_report.md:699-725`.

**Strongest correct statement:** For the Lorentz-random \(L_7\) lattice, diamond counts have \(\operatorname{Var}N(D_T)=O(T^2\log T)=o(T^4)\), while past-cone counts have the rigorously repairable bound \(O(T^3)=o(T^4)\), with their \(T^3\) mean and variance dominated by rare near-rest frames. A reciprocal gap that excludes the null cone, together with finite local second moments, forces these sub-volume laws, but the original non-null-only gap does not: Lorentz-random affine \(\mathbb Z^4\) provides a finite-second-moment counterexample with a positive \(T^6\) null contribution. Under the imported H1/H2/H4 cosmological identification, these sub-volume laws make the predicted \(\Lambda\) fluctuations catastrophically larger than observed, but neither strict lensing silence nor a cosmological no-go theorem follows from the minimal axioms alone.
