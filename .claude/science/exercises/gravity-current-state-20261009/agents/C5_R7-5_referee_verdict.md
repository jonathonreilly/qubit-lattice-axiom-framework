  The independent random-point residual is `1.55e-15` (`scripts/r75_referee_checks.py:115-139`; `scripts/r75_referee_checks.out:5`). No operator domain, self-adjoint/semibounded completion, thermodynamic limit, or finite-site substitute is given.  `r75_oneloop.py:246-274` is a lattice quadrature for a self-energy, not a lattice Hamiltonian. - **Meaning of “every order”: narrow but correct by construction.**  In internal variables the mass-squared operator is declared independent of total `P`, and the Hamiltonian is then defined as `H=sqrt(M^2+P^2)` (`scripts/r75_bt_production.py:8-12,61-73`; `scripts/r75_bt3d.py:1-6,51-61`). That supports the narrow BT interpretation, not the report's use of BT as a second “box field theory” witness. ## 4. “Box field theory” and tuning

- The preregistration deliberately listed smooth box form factors and invariant-mass form factors (`TESTS.md:3377-3379`), so nonlocality at `1/Lambda_F` does **not by itself** disqualify a candidate.  A fully specified Hermitian, stable, finite-per-site, translation-invariant, super-polynomially quasi-local lattice Hamiltonian could meet the noun despite not being a local QFT.
- This packet does not provide that object.  INV is a formal continuum/Feynman self-energy identity sampled on a lattice dispersion; BT is a bounded-sector relativistic mass-operator model.  The report itself concedes that neither is a local QFT (`R7-5_report.md:263-264`).  More importantly, the missing stability, finite-site and operator-realization checks mean INV is not yet the explicit checked box field theory required by `TESTS.md:3383`.
- Choosing every vertex to depend only on invariant masses is not a cancellation of independently adjusted numbers, so it is not ordinary parameter fine-tuning.  It is, however, an imposed functional custodial condition—an infinite set of restrictions on lattice-allowed kernels—and the report derives no exact symmetry that enforces it, shows no closure under radiative corrections beyond the selected one-loop integrals, and admits two-loop INV is open (`R7-5_report.md:268-275`).  Polchinski's criticism is therefore relevant: small/zero breaking has been inserted in the interaction rather than obtained from the preferred-frame microscopic dynamics ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/1106.6346v2)).  Belenchia et al. do show that a separately Lorentz-invariant EFT cutoff below the LV scale can suppress percolation, but present it as a toy mechanism rather than a Standard-Model completion ([primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/1601.06700v2)).
