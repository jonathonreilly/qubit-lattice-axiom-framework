## 0. In plain words

1. **Two of the three wanted properties are easy.**
   - **The photon.** A lattice U(1) theory on Z³ × ℝ can have a photon whose dispersion is exactly ω = |k| inside a momentum box. The trick is R7-1's: build the photon's lattice difference operator from a smooth but non-analytic function.
   - **The Ward identity.** It can be exact at the same time. Charged matter couples through Peierls phases, so the lattice action is exactly gauge invariant.
   - **The law.** I built it (the "box QED" law, BQ). It uses one Dirac fermion per site (4 fermion modes) with R7-1/R7-5's box dispersion and a non-compact photon. Inside the box, every Feynman rule equals continuum QED: propagators, vertex, and no seagull term.
2. **The third property is impossible: the photon cannot be confined to the box.**
   - **Lemma C (proved).** In any lattice theory where the gauge transformations are generated by commuting, quasi-local charge densities, the electric charge is the same at every lattice momentum.
   - **So the photon reaches the zone edge.** A long-wavelength photon couples with full strength to every charged mode, including the zone-edge modes outside the box.
   - **So the vertex cannot be box-confined.** A vertex that switches off when a leg leaves the box violates the Ward identity.
   - **Invariant-mass form factors are also excluded for ordinary (on-site) charge densities.** The Ward identity fixes the soft pair-creation current at every momentum, so a compact invariant-mass form factor on it is impossible.
   - **This answers R7-1 §5.6 and R7-5 §6.** Gauge invariance ties the photon to the whole Brillouin zone, not to the box.
3. **The loops therefore leave the box, and the light cones split at full one-loop size.**

## 1. Hypotheses and classes (stated before any title)

**Class 𝒢 (for the no-go).**
- **(G1)** Z³ × ℝ, Hamiltonian. Matter is n fermion (CAR) modes per site. The free part is H₀ = Σ_k ψ_k† h(k) ψ_k with h continuous on T³ (ℓ¹ hopping, which includes R7-1's super-polynomial tails).
- **(G2)** The U(1) gauge transformations of matter are generated by bilinear densities ρ(x) = ψ† m_x ψ, with:
  - m_x = T_x m₀ T_x* (translation covariant);
  - an ℓ¹ kernel, so the momentum kernel μ(k′, k) of ρ̂(q) is continuous;
  - Σ_x ρ(x) = Q, and [H₀, Q] = 0;
  - [ρ(x), Q] = 0. This is implied by the mutual commutativity [ρ(x), ρ(y)] = 0 that first-class abelian Gauss constraints G(x) = (Dᵀ E)(x) − ρ(x) require.
- **(G3)** The scalar potential couples through ρ, so the A₀ vertex is Γ⁰(k+q, k) = μ(k+q, k). Couplings are gauge invariant, so the Ward–Takahashi (WT) identity holds.
- **𝒢₀ ⊂ 𝒢:** orbital on-site densities, μ ≡ q, a constant Hermitian matrix. This is the standard Peierls class, and BQ lies in it.

**Class BQ (for the computation): the explicit law of §3.** It uses non-compact U(1) links, Feynman gauge (with a ξ check) and one Dirac species.

**Excluded outside realizations, named before any title:**
- compact U(1), SU(N), and finite-dimensional (quantum-link or Z_N) link truncations;
- interacting or composite (non-bilinear) charge densities;
- charge densities with only power-law locality (discontinuous μ);
- non-orbital densities μ(k′, k) = W(k′) ĉ(k′ − k) W(k)† combined with non-minimal transverse vertices (§4.2(c));
- time-non-local actions;
- Euclidean Z⁴ (set B);
- chiral matter;
- two loops;
- gravity.

## 3. The law BQ (explicit; proved properties)

**Fermion.**
- One 4-component Dirac field per site, with separable axis-only hopping:
  h(k) = Σ_j [S(k_j) α_j + W(k_j) β] + mβ, where:
  - S = ρk + (1 − ρ) sin k;
  - W = r(1 − ρ)(1 − cos k);
  - ρ is a C^∞ Gevrey plateau: 1 on |k| ≤ b₁, 0 on |k| ≥ b₂ < π.
- In the box, h = α·k + βm exactly.
- The zone-edge doublers get Wilson mass m + 2r.
- Parity ψ(x) → βψ(−x) is a symmetry (S odd, W even).

**Photon.**
- Non-compact links A^std with forward-difference gauge shifts.
- The physical field is A_j = G_j A^std_j. Here G_j is a 1D convolution along axis j with smooth positive periodic symbol g(q) = (q/2)/sin(q/2) on the photon plateau [c₁, c₂], tending to 1 outside.
- D_j = ∇_j G_j has |D̂_j(q)|² = d(q_j)², with d = ρ_γ q + (1 − ρ_γ)·2 sin(q/2).
- The Hamiltonian is H_γ = ½ Σ |G_j⁻¹ Π_j|² + ¼ Σ |G_j G_l F^std_jl|². This is gauge invariant, quasi-local (super-polynomial tails) and bounded below.
- The transverse photon has ω² = Σ_j d(q_j)², which equals |q|² **exactly** for |q_j| ≤ c₁ (proved; the eigenvalues of |D̂|²δ − D̂D̂* are |D̂|² twice and 0).

**Coupling.**
- Peierls phases of A^std along the straight axis path of each hop. This is exactly gauge invariant, and Gauss's law is Σ_j ∇_jᵀ Π_j = ρ with on-site ρ = ψ†ψ, so BQ ∈ 𝒢₀.
- Vertex: V_j(k+q, k) = ie[h_j(k_j) − h_j(k_j+q_j)]/D̂_j(q).
- Seagull: [h_j(p+q) + h_j(p−q) − 2h_j(p)]/d(q_j)².
- In the box (k_j, k_j+q_j inside the fermion plateau and q_j inside the photon plateau): V_j = −eα_j e^{−iq_j/2} and the seagull is 0 (proved, by divided differences of a linear function). The phase is the link-midpoint position of A_j. It cancels in every Feynman-gauge loop.
- **So every Feynman rule equals continuum Feynman-gauge QED when all lines are in the box.**

**Exact Ward identity (proved).** Σ_j D̂_j V_j(k+q, k) = −ie[h(k+q) − h(k)]. With A₀ (vertex ie, D̂₀ = iω) this gives the lattice WT identity Σ_μ D̂_μ V_μ = −ie[G⁻¹(k+q) − G⁻¹(k)].

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Lemma C and Theorem B (no box-confined gauge vertex) — proved

**Lemma C (charge rigidity).**
- **Claim.** In 𝒢, every nonzero eigenvalue λ of the charge symbol q(k) = μ(k, k) has the same multiplicity at every k ∈ T³. In particular, the charge-λ subspace has constant rank over the whole Brillouin zone.
- **Proof.**
  1. [ρ̂(q), Q] = 0 gives the intertwining relation μ(k, k−q) q(k−q) = q(k) μ(k, k−q) for all k, q. So μ(a, c) maps the ν-eigenspace of q(c) into the ν-eigenspace of q(a).
  2. Fix a and a nonzero eigenvalue λ of q(a). Take a small circle Γ around λ excluding the rest of spec q(a).
  3. For c near a, by continuity of q, the eigenvalues of q(c) inside Γ have total multiplicity mult_a(λ). Their spectral projector P_Γ(c) is close to P_λ(a).
  4. By continuity of μ, ‖μ(a, c) − q(a)‖ is small. Since q(a) = λ on range P_λ(a), μ(a, c) is injective on range P_Γ(c).
  5. By step 1, μ(a, c) sends the ν-eigenspace of q(c) into the ν-eigenspace of q(a), which is zero for ν ≠ λ inside Γ. Injectivity therefore forces q(c) to have only the eigenvalue λ inside Γ, with the same multiplicity.
  6. So nonzero eigenvalues and their multiplicities are locally constant. They are constant because T³ is connected. ∎
- **Illustration (checked, `r85_lemmaC_illustration.out`).**
  - A smooth box-only charge (m_x = |w_x⟩⟨w_x|, Σ_x m_x = χ_box(k)) has ‖[m₀, m_y]‖/‖m₀‖² = 0.50.
  - The on-site control and a phase-dressed (gauge-equivalent) density commute to 2e-17. Both have constant symbol 1.

**Theorem B (no box-confined vertex).**
- **Claim.** In 𝒢 with Q ≠ 0, let B ⊊ T³ be a box whose complement has nonempty interior. Then no gauge coupling has a vertex that vanishes whenever a fermion leg lies outside B.
- **Proof.**
  1. By (G3), Γ⁰(k+q, k) = μ(k+q, k), which tends to q(k) as q → 0 (continuity).
  2. By Lemma C, q(k) has a nonzero eigenvalue at every k, including k ∉ B.
  3. So for k ∉ B and small q, Γ⁰ ≠ 0 on the charged subspace. ∎
- **Plain meaning.** A soft photon sees the full charge of every charged mode, including the zone-edge modes. Those modes cannot be relativistic on the whole torus (C3 `TESTS.md:1213`; the R7-1 anomaly of −1 per mode sits there). So gauge loops always reach the non-Lorentz region. Box confinement of a gauge vertex is impossible, not merely unbuilt.

### 4.2 Invariant-mass-structured couplings

- **(a) Minimal coupling in 𝒢₀: compact INV pair vertices are impossible — proved.**
  1. With μ ≡ q, i[H₀, ρ̂(q)] has kernel q·(h(k+q) − h(k)). Continuity in q and the continuity equation fix the full zero-transfer current: ĵ_j(k, k) = q ∂_j h(k).
  2. Its interband (pair-creation) elements are ⟨c|∂_j h|v⟩ = (E_v − E_c)⟨c|∂_j v⟩. For BQ's h these are nonzero wherever ∂_j n ≠ 0 (n = h/E). That holds on open sets outside the box; the positive integrand ½|∂_y n|²/E of P_E is a direct check.
  3. R7-5's INV structure needs F(M²) = 0 on every pair with a leg outside the box (Lemma INV-k, arranged by a doubler-free extension with E(k) ≥ E_edge outside). The Ward identity needs F = 1 at the zero-transfer pairs (k, k), M = 2E(k), for every such k outside the box. The two requirements contradict each other.
  4. So R7-5's INV structure cannot be imposed on the WT-fixed part of a minimal gauge coupling.
- **(b) General 𝒢 — partial.**
  - Theorem B still holds.
  - Densities μ(k′, k) = W(k′) ĉ(k′ − k) W(k)†, with W a smooth unitary band frame, commute and keep charge rigidity.
  - Choosing W = band frame outside the box switches off soft pair creation there (suggested; requires a trivial band bundle).
  - But the in/out division is then a one-body, frame-fixed function W(k). That is R7-5's class FF (frame-fixed product windows), whose refereed bounded FAIL gave XI_FF ≈ 11 for Yukawa. For QED this is suggested, not computed.
  - Whether a general commuting quasi-local μ can be non-factorizable (an invariant-mass function) is **not proved either way**.
- **(c) Transverse non-minimal vertices — open.**
  - Gauge-invariant Pauli-type couplings (bilinears times F_μν) are unconstrained by WT. They can carry any kernel, including INV form factors.
  - A construction in which (b)'s frame rotation removes all minimal pair creation and an INV Pauli vertex supplies it is not excluded here, and not built.
  - Even then, the A₀ (Coulomb) vertex stays pinned to the charge on all modes (Theorem B).
  - Reading: any such construction re-imposes Lorentz invariance by choice of kernel. It is the R7-5 referee's "imposed functional custodial condition", now with gauge constraints added.
