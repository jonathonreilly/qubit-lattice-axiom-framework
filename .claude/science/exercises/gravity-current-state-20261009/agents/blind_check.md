# Blind check (Claude Opus 5.5; fresh context; no files; given only the first-draft plain version)

**1. Goal.** The authors want gravity to come out of their model's own rules (a fixed grid of sites) instead of being added as an extra ingredient. They don't say why. My guess is that producing gravity would explain it, while adding it only makes room for it.

**2. Failures.**
- Qubit-like parts: the waves are slower than light (fails feature 1), or they have an extra sideways wobble (fails feature 3).
- Continuous parts: the waves gain mass (fails feature 2) because the vacuum resists shear. Only a number set by hand cancels it.
- Records as carrier: there is no pull at long distances (see 5).

**3. Coverage.** The evidence covers small waves, weak interactions and only the models they built. It does not cover strong gravity, large waves, strong interactions, models they haven't tried, or grids that aren't fixed. These are failed attempts, not a proof that it can't be done.

**4. Success.** A grid model whose gravity is massless, has one pattern and pulls on everything equally, with no number set by hand. Or naming which starting assumption has to be dropped.

**5. Unclear phrases (my reading).**
- "checked": verified by experiment, or a checklist they apply? I read verified.
- "fixed grid": a grid that doesn't move or bend. But then how does space "stretch" on it?
- "one pattern": never described. I read a single allowed squeeze/stretch shape.
- "write Einstein's equations… on the grid": put in by hand, not derived.
- "fails one feature": exactly one, at least one, or the same one? The qubit route fails two, so I read "at least one."
- "qubit-like" vs. "continuous parts": on/off switches vs. dials.
- "records," "carrier": never defined. "Records" is the hardest to follow.
- "interacting vacuum": the grid's resting state, with parts affecting each other.
- "waves pick up a mass": not explained (I assume it makes them slower and short-range). Why resisting shear causes it is also not explained.
- "no long-range pull": which feature does it fail? Possibly "pulls on everything."
- "weak interactions": could be misread as the weak nuclear force. I read "parts influence each other weakly."
- "shown": proved, or seen in examples? I read seen in examples.
- The success list leaves out "light speed" and "long-range." I assume "massless" covers them.
- "the premise that must go": the premises are never listed.

**6. Push.** Yes:
- The closing crystal/liquid line points to an escape: a liquid-like vacuum that doesn't resist shear, giving massless waves with nothing set by hand. "Analogy only" disclaims it, but it is the last thing you read.
- The continuous route reads as the near miss, off by one number.
- "Fixed grid" together with "the premise that must go" hints that the fixed grid is what to drop.
- Counting "naming the premise" as getting past it lets a dead end count as success.
