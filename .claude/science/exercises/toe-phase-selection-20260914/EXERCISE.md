# Ground-sector selection and the actual collective phase

Personal exercise, 2026-09-14. This packet is a research plan, not an audit or
a claim that the framework produces a theory of everything. Source baseline:
main `5deabeb698a27c2c3f68c5df685af2521ef15307`; prior campaign
`652ea36706f7df14f153f2d40ee900662fe73706`. Execution is personal throughout.

The four framework axioms leave the admissibility rule unspecified beyond its
stated structure. The three approved primitives do not select a Hamiltonian,
quantum vacuum, typical Record configuration, coupling, or phase. Constructing
one candidate with desirable behavior would be an existence result under its
added premises. A failure of that candidate would not force an axiom update.
An update becomes a live scientific conclusion only if a necessary target is
incompatible with the entire permitted class, or a specific indispensable
missing input is isolated with the alternative routes still exposed.

The last campaign constructed a local three-state clock Hamiltonian whose
hard temporal-penalty limit has an integer-neutral sector equivalent to a
spin-one quantum-link model. It did not prove that this sector contains the
global ground state at a volume-independent spatial charge penalty. Nor did
it prove the collective phase inside that sector, or its stability at finite
penalty. These are distinct obligations. The available loop/filter criterion
still ends at unproved ground-state expectation estimates.

First target: for explicitly declared finite cubic cell complexes, compare the
bottom energies of all allowed integer-divergence sectors of the hard model,
with couplings fixed independently of volume. Seek either a sector-selection
theorem with useful uniform bounds or a concrete counterexample to the claimed
selection mechanism. Then determine what changes for finite temporal penalty.

Second target: use a successful selection result, or a corrected physical
sector, to attack a ground-state estimate that distinguishes a Coulomb phase
from a gapped or ordered phase. A finite gap fit, an invariant sector, a local
generator, a positive twist susceptibility, and a Gaussian surrogate do not
constitute this result. Neither does a conditional theorem whose terminal
premise is equivalent to the original phase question.

Boundary cases include zero kinetic coupling, large electric stiffness, hard
versus finite charge penalties, open and periodic topology, frozen versus
mobile configuration components, integer versus half-integer link spin, and
the order of volume, time-step, and penalty limits. The finite-clock physical
space imposes divergence zero modulo three; it does not impose integer zero.

Progress must change an actual obligation: a proof with checked hypotheses,
a falsifying configuration, a corrected limiting generator, or a quantitative
estimate applicable to the specified ground state. At each substantial result
or exhausted mechanism, reassess the route portfolio and continue until
2026-09-15 01:30:44 UTC. No idle duration filling or automatic axiom changes.
