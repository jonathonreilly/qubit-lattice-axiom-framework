## 2. The time-mesh route and the independent cutoff route

The author works on the full finite tensor B(H_X), then its quasi-local
completion, rather than assuming that finite sums of elementary products
are norm dense in all bounded finite-region operators. All-A occupancy is
built into local effective site factors; intermediate F operators are
compressed finite local compositions. There is no infinite P projection.
The diagonal electric sum has the correct multiplication domain and core.
The remaining Hamiltonian and jump terms are bounded at each finite graph,
so the finite trace-class dynamics is normal CPTP and mild for general
densities. Coercivity in every electric direction is not assumed.

The commuting electric terms conjugate a local operator only through terms
meeting its original support. The exact support enlargement is one fixed
neighborhood, not an iteration through the newly added cells. The pair and
jump incidence diameters 12 and 8 agree with the independent control. The
author bound

    ell_0 = 4|delta| z^4 + 8 kappa (z-1)^2

is a valid conservative cb bound. The PRE used a maximum of the same two
upper bounds, since it indexed commutators and dissipators separately.
That harmless difference is not an error. Grouping the two resolved sign
dissipators by edge still obeys the author's bound. Norm bounds on elementary
operators and UCP contractivity survive arbitrary spectator factors, so no
finite-dimensional Hilbert-space premise is hidden in the locality estimate.

The new obligation in the source is its time-mesh approximation. For every
trace-class rho, strong-star continuity of bounded h(t),L(t) gives trace-norm
continuity of L(t)_*rho, first for rank-one rho and then by finite-rank
approximation. The exact finite-volume trajectory is trace-norm continuous,
hence compact on a compact time interval. Uniform boundedness and a finite
net on that compact set make left-endpoint time sampling converge uniformly
there. The nonautonomous integral equation then bounds the evolution error
by an integral of a discrepancy tending to zero. This is strong convergence
of the trace-class propagators, not convergence of the generators in
operator norm.

At each mesh, the frozen-coefficient factors are CPTP. The support-path
recursion applies on each constant interval with the same range, overlap
and cb bound. Equivalently, convex interpolation of the local GKLS maps
between time nodes gives norm-continuous generators with the same bounds;
one need not interpolate jump amplitudes and falsely identify that with
convex interpolation of their dissipators. The resulting local norm estimates
pass to the strong trace-class/ultraweak dual limit by lower semicontinuity,
also at finite matrix amplifications. Thus the author does not assume the
norm-continuity hypothesis that the cited NVZ theorem explicitly requires.

The PRE obtained the same spatial estimate by a different construction:
cap each commuting D_e by min(D_e,M), prove the bounded locality bound with
constants independent of M, and remove M at fixed volume on trace class.
That bounded-electric-cutoff proof remains independently attributable.
Neither route supplies norm time continuity for the full bounded rotor
algebra, and neither exchanges the microscopic, spin and volume limits.

## 3. Boundary, normality, and physical-state scope

The boundary comparison concerns the actual induced-graph target. Its
incomplete stars and pairs differ from a complete-star truncation only in a
fixed boundary strip. Electric dressing enlarges that strip by a fixed
amount. Each source in Duhamel annihilates the identity and has a uniformly
bounded local cb norm; it need not itself be a GKLS generator. The two
propagators used around it remain UCP. Summation over polynomially growing
distance shells gives the claimed vanishing tail.

The norm estimate is uniform over a fixed local observable unit ball. This
is sufficient both for normality of the limiting restricted functionals
and for passage of the semigroup law by approximation of T_s(A) by local
observables. Local Gauss zero projections are bounded local observables.
The shifts preserve charge and field together; every local term commutes
with the relevant spectral projectors. Those projections are therefore
fixed by finite-volume maps and by the limit. These are correctly local
constraint claims, not an assertion about one global infinite-volume
projection or density matrix.

The source restricts boundary perturbations to uniformly bounded local cb
density and range. Arbitrarily large or bulk-reaching boundary driving is
not included. The PRE separately gave explicit qualifications for more
general exhaustions and commuting electric boundary prescriptions, periodic
boxes, initial marginal convergence and normal maps in locally normal
representations. Those extensions and the global-folium counterexample
remain in the independent REPORT; they are not silently inserted into the
author's stated theorem. In particular, dynamical boundary independence
does not identify physical state sequences selected by different boundary
flux conditions.

## 4. New phase control and strengthened time counterexample

I rebuilt the new external-field example without the author builder, using
all links oriented A to B rather than the author's positive-coordinate
orientation. On a 6 by 6 induced box, put circulation m around

    (1,0) -> (2,0) -> (2,1) -> (1,1) -> (1,0).

Choose a=(0,0), marked newborn endpoint b=(1,0), and old-record destination
c=(0,1). All original star link fields at a are zero for every m. Before
birth the electric diagonal is 4m^2. Afterwards the occupied B site b
deactivates the two adjacent external cycle-edge contributions. The newly
shifted birth and transfer edges also terminate at occupied B sites. The
remaining electric contribution is exactly 2m^2, for **both** newborn
orientations. The interaction-picture matrix element therefore acquires
exp(-2 i K t m^2). The original and final words obey Gauss exactly.

This establishes a real need for the added electric shell: an operator
supported on the original star alone could not have this dependence on
outside input fields. The two affected external electric links are in
the one-shell enlargement; nothing requires iterating that enlargement.
This agrees with the author control and its interpretation.

The author also strengthens the PRE's purely electric norm-time example to
arbitrary fixed bounded local Hamiltonian/birth perturbations. That stronger
claim is sound. For a fixed final time t, hold A_t=alpha_t^D(A) constant in
the following integration variable. With V the interaction-picture dual,

    (V(0,t)-I) A_t = int_0^t V(0,s) L_I(s) A_t ds.      (1)

Only the uniformly finite number of dressed terms meeting supp(A_t)
contribute to L_I(s)A_t. Contractivity, or the time-mesh version of (1)
followed by the ultraweak limit, gives

    ||T_t(A)-alpha_t^D(A)|| <= C_A t,                  (2)

where C_A depends on the local support and fixed coefficients, not volume
or electric amplitude. No extensive total perturbation norm appears.
Consequently the Wilson-loop times t_m=pi/[K(8m+4)] satisfy

    ||T_(t_m)(W)-W|| >= 2-C_W t_m -> 2.               (3)

This is an analytic reconstruction of the author's stronger claim, not a
large-m finite-matrix extrapolation. It is not retroactively attributed to
the earlier PRE, whose explicit countercontrol set delta=kappa=0.

I also independently reconstructed the indexed support overlaps in two
selected author boxes: 41 for d=2, side 4 and 300 for d=3, side 4. The
numbers agree. The other finite overlap counts were authenticated and

## 5. Instrument and density checks

For fixed initial all-A-plus/B-empty matter, a resolved edge mark has
z-1 destinations. Distinct destinations have distinct final occupied B
patterns, and each field translation is unitary. Thus the initial-sector
operator Gram is (z-1)I on the entire physical field Hilbert space, not
merely the diagonal value at zero field. The two newborn signs have
orthogonal final A-charge ranges, so their cross Gram vanishes and the
coherent edge Gram is 2(z-1)I.

The new control checks these off-diagonal field identities on four distinct
normalizable circulation words, m=-2,0,1,3. It gets 3I and 6I for the
resolved/coherent Grams and a zero plus-minus cross Gram. The all-field
identity follows from the preceding orthogonality/unitarity argument;
the finite matrix corroborates normalization and interference. Together
with the unchanged PRE controls, this confirms the initial local B-site
derivatives 48 kappa in d=2 and 120 kappa in d=3, and the corresponding
expected record-density derivatives 24 kappa and 60 kappa.

These are initial rates, not a finite-time exponential local waiting law
or a closed density equation. The coherent channel is the unnormalized
sum of the two charge amplitudes with the same sqrt(kappa), as stipulated.
Discarding a resolved mark gives a different recycling state from the
coherent channel. The author explicitly keeps that distinction.

The detector sentence can be implemented with a prescribed finite-outcome
or saturated classical register, or with a finite number of countable
registers using bounded shifts for exact unbounded counts. A finite-dimensional
register cannot store an arbitrarily large exact count. A recorded-jump
extension must preserve the original channel when the register is discarded,
rather than adding a duplicate birth channel. The source makes no unlimited
finite-resource or global first-event claim, so these normal instrument
conditions do not change its local initial diagnostic. No new assertion
about an autonomous detector implementation is reviewed here.

physical Wilson-loop norm counterexample persists. Locally normal
