## 2. Static spectral theorem and domains

The author's shrinking-window proof is the same valid mechanism found in
the PRE: bounded strong convergence of
`H6,S/eta=H2,S+(delta/eta)H4,S` gives weak spectral-measure convergence;
a subsequence of rescaled moving centers and continuous majorants exclude
concentration when the limiting measure has no atom. The common norm bound
handles centers escaping to infinity. No fixed chemical-energy center,
absolute-continuity density, gap or convergence rate is assumed.

The finite-net extension is correct on norm-compact input families and
families approaching them uniformly. It does not imply uniformity over
the full unit ball or arbitrary unrelated spin-dependent states. The PRE's
normalized shrinking-cell counterexample remains a separately derived
illustration of this limit. On the ring, the author's fixed-center version
only needs zero atom at -4 on the complement; the complete ring spectrum
also supplies the PRE's stronger arbitrary-center statement on its continuous
subspace. On the cube the whole physical N6 space has no point spectrum.

The common embedding consists of complete physical spin boxes in the rotor
Hilbert space, with whole-box zero extensions. Those boxes increase strongly
to the identity. Fixed-state exterior tails vanish, so the zero extension's
spurious exterior eigenvectors cannot invalidate the statewise assertions.
Uniform bounds apply to the complete spin space, not a fixed small electric
cutoff. The result does not concern H2 extended to unrelated particle-number
or P-perp sectors.

The purely Hamiltonian step is also justified. The candidate explicitly
repeats the prepared proof with zero loss, retaining the same unbounded
electric correction, weighted estimates, crossing cutoff and four residual
terms. None of those estimates divides by kappa or requires strict
dissipation; at kappa=0 the exact propagators are unitary contractions.
Negative times are controlled by the same absolute weighted/Duhamel bounds.
The limiting h_F is self-adjoint on D(f²), with quadratic diagonal and bounded
finite-range H4 part. Finite-support flat vectors are a core, and unitarity
extends convergence to arbitrary normalizable vectors without a moment
assumption. Thus the source proof legitimately supplies this variant rather
than silently identifying a dissipative semigroup with Hamiltonian calculus.

The independent PRE uses a different valid route: restore the uniformly
bounded loss through a Dyson expansion, using `Gamma6 F=4 kappa F`, then
deduce negative-time convergence by unitarity and compact vector orbits.
This route remains independently attributable; it is not retroactively
inserted into the author's proof.

Fourier integration and uniform C0 approximation establish the flat
functional calculus. On the complement, the shrinking-window lemma and
vanishing C0 tails give zero. The candidate correctly identifies the limit
as a degenerate calculus `g(h_F)F`, not the calculus of a self-adjoint
operator with identity F on the entire original Hilbert space.

Tightness on F plus sublinear-window rejection on its complement proves
strong convergence of the expanding centered sharp projector. Neither a
field-moment bound nor a speed of window expansion is needed. Its additional

has no eigenvalues at the endpoints, continuous upper/lower approximants
give the sharp projector limit. This does not include endpoint atoms. The
PRE's alternating-center counterexample and fixed-window caution remain

## 4. Reference-error repair reconstructed

The first successful author run reported only the omitted-boundary residual
of a numerical finite resolvent as a reference norm-error bound, approximately
`1.214e-153`. That alone does not include interior numerical solve error or
rounding. The archived qualification correctly identifies the problem. The
new analytic truncation argument is valid for the stated reference seed:

1. Split h_F=D+V, where D is the real electric/H4 diagonal. Exact compact
   path algebra gives off-diagonal row and column absolute sums at most
   `4 delta=14/5` for delta=7/10. Thus `||V||<=14/5`.
2. For z=+/-16i, `||(D-z)^(-1)||<=1/16`, so the Neumann ratio is at most
   `r=7/40<1` independently of the unbounded diagonal.
3. The reference compact-frame seed has f=1 and the cutoff is |f|<=24.
   Each off-diagonal application changes f by at most one; diagonal
   resolvents do not change it. At least n0=24 applications are needed
   before a full-space term can differ from the compressed term.
4. For each resolvent sign, the full-minus-compressed error is bounded by
   `(2/16) r^24/(1-r)`, using seed norm at most one. Combining
   the two resolvents for `g(x)=16²/(x²+16²)` gives

   `2 r^24/(1-r)`.

The comparison independently derives the step bound and off-diagonal norm
from the frozen path implementation, then verifies the exact rational value

This is a bound on the difference between exact infinite and exact truncated

The time reference has a separate interaction-picture Dyson bound. With
