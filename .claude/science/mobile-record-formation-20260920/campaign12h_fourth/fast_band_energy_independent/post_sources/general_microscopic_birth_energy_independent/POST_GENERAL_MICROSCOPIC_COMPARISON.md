## 2. Convention and scope reconciliation

Author edges are oriented A to B. The frozen PRE engine orients each edge from
the lower-numbered endpoint to the higher-numbered endpoint. Reversing an edge
negates its electric coordinate and reverses its link-shift sign. Matter charges,
physical Gauss words, norms, and Hamiltonian moments are invariant under that
basis relabeling. On the cube square 0-1-3-2-0 the author's fields
`E01=n,E31=-n,E32=n,E02=-n` therefore become exactly the PRE fields
`E01=n,E13=n,E23=-n,E02=-n`. The controls compare signs at the A endpoint,
not signs attached indiscriminately to the lower-numbered endpoint.

There is also a variable collision. Author `u=n^2/C` tends to `alpha^2`.
The PRE's macroscopic-flux parameter `u=1-alpha^2` is its complement. In this
POST discussion write `t=n^2/C` for the author variable. The PRE expression
`4+36u^2+32u^3` becomes

    72 - 168t + 132t^2 - 32t^3

at the limiting relation u=1-t. The apparent difference in those polynomials
is purely notational.

The released note canonically rotates P against its whole complement. The PRE
also resolves higher W clusters when interpreting rare high-energy components.
Their P columns use the same positive-overlap canonical dressing. For the
leading results compared here, the first Pi1 rotated-jump coefficient is the
same. The direct P/Q rotation does leave order-epsilon couplings between Pi1
and Pi2. The author does not assume these couplings vanish; the order counting
below shows why they first contribute to the relevant numerator at order six.
Higher finite coefficients must not be compared by silently identifying those
two choices of basis in Q.

The PRE principally fixed the original lambda=0 compensation. The released
note additionally treats the already supplied electric family, changing both
H and its canonical initial preparation with lambda. This is not the earlier
fixed-star-preparation convention. The original resolved j+ and j- marks, or
the original coherent mark j++j-, are retained without filtering or a new
normalization of the coherent mark.

## 3. Independent reconstruction of the uniform operator statement

Let h=W+epsilon T+epsilon^2 C_lambda, T=-(F+F*), and P=1_(W=0).
The supplied compensation preserves W and has

    P C_lambda P = M + Q,
    M=P F*F P,       Q=[(1-lambda)D+lambda E2]/C,
    C=S(S+1),       0<=lambda<=1.

These are supplied definitions/premises, not deductions selecting a physical
compensation. On a fixed graph with m edges and A-degrees d_a, crude bounds
suffice: ||F_a||<=d_a, ||T||<=2m, and

    ||C_lambda|| <= sum_a d_a^2 + 4m.

Indeed each normalized integer-spin link shift has norm at most one; each
D_a,S and D_a,infinity has norm at most d_a; the occupancy gates have norm one.
Each active E(E-q)/C lies in [0,1], and E^2/C<=1, so the added electric diagonal
has norm at most 2m. Resolved jumps have norm at most one and coherent jumps at
most sqrt(2). None of these bounds scales with spin-space dimension. A smaller
uniform epsilon can be chosen so the perturbation norm is below 1/4 and the
canonical projector difference is below one. The contour |z|=1/2 then gives a
uniform Neumann resolvent bound; the projector and direct rotation have uniform
analytic Taylor remainders. This supplies the needed operator-norm control,
not just vectorwise perturbation at each separately fixed S.

Write U for the canonical direct rotation. Independently expanding the low
column gives

    U P = P + epsilon F P
              + epsilon^2(F^2 P/2 - M/2) + O(epsilon^3).

The denominator two is the W=2 energy denominator. Compensation has no
inter-W block, so it cannot introduce an order-two Pi1 term. Since j annihilates
P and lowers W by one,

    P U* j U P = epsilon B + O(epsilon^3),  B=P j F P,
    Pi1 U* j U P = epsilon^2 R + O(epsilon^4),
    R=j F^2 P/2 - F B.

For a mark centered at a, F_a^2=0. For c different from a, the outward F_c
commutes with j and with F_a on the relevant words: if two operators try to
create on the same B site, both orderings vanish by the hard-core rule. Finite
spin boundary blocking does not alter this argument. Terms in jF^2P without
F_a vanish because a remains occupied. The two orderings containing F_a cancel
the remote-center part of FB. Hence, as an operator on the whole P space,

    R = -F_a B.

This applies also with B sites occupied and with negative A charges, subject
to the original legal Gauss sector. It is not only a calculation on Omega_n.
The new K(2,4) control checks all its physical P columns exactly.

Let hbar=U*hU, Qbig=I-P, and J=(-1)^W. One has

    P hbar Qbig = 0,
    P hbar P = epsilon^2 Q + O(epsilon^4),
    Pi1 hbar Pi1 = Pi1 + O(epsilon^2).

The next high blocks of jbar P have grades and orders Pi2:epsilon^3,
Pi3:epsilon^4, etc. The surviving Pi1-to-Pi2 block of hbar starts at epsilon;
its possible gain contribution therefore starts at epsilon^2 * epsilon *
epsilon^3 = epsilon^6. The low-high blocks vanish exactly. Covariance
`h(-epsilon)=J h(epsilon) J` and `jbar(-epsilon)=-J jbar(epsilon) J` makes the
P-compressed gain, loss, and jump norm even in epsilon. Thus their operator
expansions are

    P jbar* hbar jbar P
        = epsilon^4(B* Q B + R*R) + O(epsilon^6),
    P jbar* hbar^2 jbar P
        = epsilon^4 R*R + O(epsilon^6),
    P jbar* jbar P = epsilon^2 B*B + O(epsilon^4).

For hbar^2, the low block begins at epsilon^4 and the Pi1 diagonal again begins
at one. This establishes the second formula independently of any density limit.
For arbitrary normalized psi in P, b=||Bpsi||^2>=b_*>0, r=||Rpsi||^2,
and q=<Bpsi,Q Bpsi>, division by the physical jump norm proves

    <H>_j = delta epsilon^-2 (q+r)/b + O(delta),
    Var_j(H) = delta^2 epsilon^-6 r/b + O(delta^2 epsilon^-4).

The bounded q term cannot be omitted for flux of order S. The square of the
mean is only of the indicated subleading order in the variance. No statement
with a uniform normalized denominator is justified when b approaches zero.

For the loss term, exact P/Q block diagonalization gives

    P {jbar*jbar,hbar} P/2
        = epsilon^4 {B*B,Q}/2 + O(epsilon^6).

Summing and restoring the microscopic factor kappa delta epsilon^-6 yields

    P U* L_micro*(H) U P
      = kappa delta epsilon^-2
          [ D_B*(Q) + sum_j R_j*R_j ] + O(kappa delta).

This is an operator-norm remainder on all P, uniform in S, lambda, and normalized
input. The Hamiltonian contribution to L*(H) vanishes identically. No replacement
of the anticommutator by an eigenvalue is used. In particular a superposition
of different Q eigenvalues is covered. Under epsilon^2 C=delta/K this becomes

    d<H>/dt|0 = kappa K <D_B*(D_lambda)>
                 + kappa K C sum_j ||R_j psi||^2 + O(kappa delta).

This reconstruction agrees with author equations (3)-(7). It also specifies
why an order-five remainder, obtained by ignoring parity, would have been too
weak for the claimed bounded physical remainder.

## 4. Finite-spin cube formulas and electric family

The new polynomial control does not insert the author drift into its inputs.
It enumerates each old transported destination, marked edge and sign, updates
its physical charge/flux word with the frozen PRE primitives, evaluates D and
E2 on that word, and only then simplifies the resulting polynomial. If an
A-to-B edge initially has field e, write

    m(e)=1-e(e-1)/C,      p(e)=1-e(e+1)/C.

For an old destination o and marked edge j, the two B-output probabilities are
m(E_o)p(E_j) and m(E_o)m(E_j). For R after the plus mark, two orderings of the
remaining old/final destinations yield the same physical output, hence a factor
four for each unordered pair. After the minus mark the negative final charge
locates the last destination, so ordered pairs are orthogonal. Explicitly,

    r_(j,+)=4 p(E_j) sum_(o<t; o,t!=j) m(E_o)m(E_t),
    r_(j,-)=m(E_j) sum_(o!=t; o,t!=j) m(E_o)p(E_t).

The coherent plus/minus R supports are disjoint for the fixed marked edge.
These expressions derive the interference factors rather than infer them from
effective rates. All boundary-vanishing factors are retained.

For the cube square word, put t=n^2/C, v=n/C, a=1-t-v, b=1-t+v.
At each affected A center, the outgoing weight squares are (b,a,1).
Both instruments give the independently simplified sums

    sum ||B_j Omega_n||^2 = 48 - 32t + 8t^2,
    sum ||R_j Omega_n||^2 = 72 - 72t + 36t^2 + 12t/C,
    <D_B*(D)>  = -32n^2(3-3t+t^2) - 16n^2/C,
    <D_B*(E2)> = 96 - 128t + 48t^2.

Initial D and E2 equal 4n^2. In particular the drift includes the loss of that
initial energy; the positive output-energy gain by itself is not the full
energy derivative. The independent algebra gives exactly the released finite
identities, including the terms suppressed by one additional power of C.

For the selected resolved (01,+) mark, direct paths give

    b_selected=a(1+a),    r_selected=4a^2,
    <D>_B=2n^2/(1+a),
    <E2>_B=4n^2+2+2n(2a+1)/(1+a).

At n=S its leading b vanishes, so no division is used there. For n/S->alpha,
|alpha|<1, the fast contribution tends to 4(1-alpha^2)/(2-alpha^2), the slow
D/C contribution to 2alpha^2/(2-alpha^2), and the slow E2/C contribution to
4alpha^2. Their combination therefore gives

    epsilon^2 <H>_(01,+)/delta
       -> 2 + lambda * 2alpha^2(3-2alpha^2)/(2-alpha^2),
    epsilon^6 Var_(01,+)(H)/delta^2
       -> 4(1-alpha^2)/(2-alpha^2).

The initial scaled mean is 4alpha^2 for every lambda in this preparation
family. Thus at lambda=0 the selected conditional mean can drop even though
the summed cube initial power is positive. These are distinct balances.

Restoring both independently computed drifts gives

    [d<H>/dt|0]/(kappa K C)
      = F_lambda(t) + [-4t+lambda(96-112t+48t^2)]/C + O(1/C),

    F_lambda(t)=72-72t+36t^2
                   -32(1-lambda)t(3-3t+t^2).

The displayed inverse-C term belongs to the leading compressed operator;
the uncomputed microscopic remainder is also O(1/C). It is therefore not the
full next coefficient. Independent expansion verifies

    F_0'(t)=-24(t-1)(4t-7),       F_0(1)=4.

On [0,1] the derivative is nonpositive, and increasing lambda adds a nonnegative
quantity. The stated lower bound F_lambda>=4 is correct for this cube/single-
square input family, including the all-mark boundary limit. At fixed n the
leading coefficient is 72 for every lambda. The finite-spin polynomial itself
is uniform up to the boundary; the conditional divided formula is not.

## 7. Limits, failed attempts, and disposition

No scientific formula discrepancy was found in the released leading claims.
No author edits are required by this comparison. There are no remaining
uncomputed steps needed for the stated leading moment/power conclusions under
their premises. The finite subleading coefficients outside the PRE's explicitly
computed lambda=0 cases remain uncomputed; the released inverse-C display does
not pretend otherwise.

The claims remain conditional on the supplied Hamiltonian, compensation family,
original marks, and canonical preparation. They concern actual first-event
moments and the full instantaneous initial generator. They do not establish
finite-time cube accumulation, a bath or heat interpretation, a reservoir supply
construction, event-time statistics, full-instrument realization, a volume or
continuum limit, a selected lambda, or empirical identification. Positive power
for the specified cube family is not positive power for arbitrary P input.
The ring and K(2,4) counterexamples narrow scope without supplying a universal
negative theorem.

At fixed field, rare high-band probability of order epsilon^2 at microscopic
energy of order epsilon^-4 produces a divergent mean of order epsilon^-2 and
variance of order epsilon^-6 when R is nonzero. Trace convergence of normalized
densities controls fixed bounded observables; it does not determine these
moments of growing operators. Moving n/S inputs also do not supply a single
fixed normalizable rotor input sequence to which a fixed-input density theorem
can simply be applied. The microscopic and effective-target variances therefore
have different observables and limit hypotheses.
