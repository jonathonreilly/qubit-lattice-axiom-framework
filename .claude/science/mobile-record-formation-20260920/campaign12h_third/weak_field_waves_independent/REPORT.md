# Independent finite-volume weak-field wave-packet reconstruction

2026-09-22. This report was derived before reading any new author weak-field
argument, runner, result, seal, checkpoint, or registry. It is a bounded
mathematical check, not a publication or formal audit determination.

**Result.** There is a controlled compact-rotor approximation to transverse
harmonic wave packets on every fixed finite periodic cubic box. A sufficient
choice is

$$
 h=\sqrt{K/J}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \omega_0=\sqrt{KJ}>0\ \hbox{fixed},\qquad
 K=\omega_0h,\quad J=\omega_0/h.
 \tag{1}
$$

The prepared states are localized compact-space lifts of fixed harmonic
Schwartz packets, in the sector of zero global electric flux. Their norm
error at fixed time is bounded explicitly below by $O_{V,\psi,T}(h)$.
The two transverse mode frequencies are

$$
 \omega(k)=2\sqrt{KJ}\,
       \sqrt{4\sum_{i=1}^3\sin^2(k_i/2)}.
 \tag{2}
$$

Gauss's law alone does not remove three physical harmonic modes. The
additional global-flux sector selection is explicit. The small ratio $K/J$
alone also does not imply the same statement on fixed physical time
intervals along every coupling path. A compact cosine countercontrol below
shows an order-one phase discrepancy when $K$ is held fixed and $J$ grows.

## 1. Exact compact space and the harmonic-sector choice

Let the periodic sides be $L_1,L_2,L_3$, each even and at least six, to retain
the preceding record-model geometry. Write $V=L_1L_2L_3$. There are $3V$
oriented positive-axis links and $3V$ elementary plaquettes, one orientation
per coordinate pair. Give real link cochains their Euclidean inner product.
Let $D$ be the vertex-to-link gradient and $C$ the link-to-plaquette curl:

$$
 (D\phi)_{x,i}=\phi_{x+e_i}-\phi_x,\qquad
 (Cq)_{x,ij}=q_{x,i}+q_{x+e_i,j}-q_{x+e_j,i}-q_{x,j}.
 \tag{3}
$$

The link-angle Hilbert space is $L^2((\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z)^{3V})$,
with $E_e=-i\partial_{q_e}$. The physical zero-charge space is invariant
under vertex gauge translations $q\mapsto q+D\phi$, equivalently spanned
by the integer electric configurations with $D^TE=0$. The Hamiltonian

$$
 H=K\sum_e E_e^2-J\sum_p(W_p+W_p^\dagger),\qquad
 W_p=e^{i(Cq)_p},
 \tag{4}
$$

preserves that space.

There are also three conserved global electric fluxes. On a divergence-free
integer configuration, the flux through any plane normal to $i$ is an
integer $F_i$ independent of the plane, and
$\sum_{x}E_{x,i}=L_iF_i$. Choosing $F_1=F_2=F_3=0$ is equivalent to
invariance under all constant link-angle translations in each direction.
These are physical global degrees of freedom, not additional vertex gauges.

Every flat $U(1)$ link connection is a vertex gauge times a constant
connection: transport along a spanning tree, use plaquette flatness for
path independence, and retain its three fundamental Wilson holonomies.
Taking an $L_i$-th root of each holonomy supplies the constant representative.
Thus the group of flat connections is connected and is generated by vertex
gauges and these three constant directions. The sector just selected is
exactly the space of functions invariant under this full flat group.

Define the real coexact space and its compact lattice by

$$
 \mathcal T=(\ker C)^\perp=\operatorname{range}C^T,\qquad
 r=\dim\mathcal T=2(V-1),\qquad
 \Lambda=2\pi P_{\mathcal T}\mathbb Z^{3V}.
 \tag{5}
$$

The flat-connection quotient is the compact flat torus
$\mathcal Q=\mathcal T/\Lambda$. It is not obtained by arbitrarily setting
some link angles to zero. The preceding flat-connection characterization
shows that two transverse lifts represent the same quotient point precisely
when their difference lies in $\Lambda$. Its character lattice consists of
integer, divergence-free electric cochains with all $F_i=0$.

The curl is injective on $\mathcal T$. Moreover $C\lambda$ is a nonzero
$2\pi$ integer vector for every nonzero $\lambda\in\Lambda$. Since
$\|C\|\le\sqrt{12}$, one has

$$
 \|\lambda\|\ge\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{12}}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt3}.
 \tag{6}
$$

This also directly proves discreteness of the projected lattice. It spans
$\mathcal T$ and hence has full rank there. On $\mathcal Q$ the kinetic
operator is exactly $-\Delta_{\mathcal T}$, as can be checked on its
integer electric characters. We use the flat Lebesgue volume on a
fundamental cell; replacing it by probability Haar measure changes only
the overall unitary normalization.

The function $\sum_p(1-\cos(Cq)_p)$ has one zero on this quotient. Without
the harmonic-sector selection it would have a three-dimensional flat
set even after imposing Gauss. Consequently a positive transverse
Gaussian cannot be normalized by merely taking the determinant of the
curl square on the whole Gauss space.

## 2. A legitimate compact initial packet

Put $B=C^TC|_{\mathcal T}$, which is strictly positive at fixed volume.
After removing the scalar $-2J(3V)$, the exact compact Hamiltonian is

$$
 \mathsf H_h=H+6JV
 =\omega_0\left[-h\Delta_q+
          \frac2h\sum_p(1-\cos(Cq)_p)\right].
 \tag{7}
$$

The noncompact harmonic comparison on $L^2(\mathcal T)$ is

$$
 H_0=-\Delta_x+x^TBx,\qquad
 \psi_t=e^{-i\omega_0tH_0}\psi,
 \qquad \|\psi\|_2=1.
 \tag{8}
$$

Take a fixed Schwartz packet $\psi$. Examples include a finite Hermite
superposition, a coherent displacement of the positive harmonic Gaussian,
or a finite superposition of such displacements. All weighted norms needed
below are finite on every fixed time interval. No compact ground-state
identification is assumed.

Choose

$$
 R=\frac{\pi}{4\sqrt3},\qquad r_0=R/2,\qquad a=R-r_0.
 $$

Let $\chi(q)$ be radial, equal to one on $\|q\|\le r_0$, zero on
$\|q\|\ge R$, and on the intervening annulus set

$$
 \chi=1-10u^3+15u^4-6u^5,\qquad
 u=(\|q\|-r_0)/a.
 $$

This is a $C^2$ cutoff, with

$$
 M_1=\|\nabla\chi\|_\infty\le\frac{15}{8a},\qquad
 M_\Delta=\|\Delta\chi\|_\infty
 \le \frac{10}{\sqrt3\,a^2}+\frac{(r-1)M_1}{r_0}.
 \tag{9}
$$

The lattice bound (6) makes the support ball injective in the quotient.
Define a periodic function by

$$
 (I_h\psi)([q])=
 h^{-r/4}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
       \chi(q+\lambda)\psi((q+\lambda)/\sqrt h).
 \tag{10}
$$

At most one summand is nonzero at any point. Thus this is an actual
compact-space state, with exact norm

$$
 \|I_h\psi\|^2
 =\int_{\mathcal T}\chi(\sqrt h\,x)^2|\psi(x)|^2\,dx.
 \tag{11}
$$

It lies in the domain of the compact kinetic operator. Its lift to the
original link space is exactly gauge invariant and in the specified
global-flux sector. In particular it is uniform along the removed
holonomy directions; it is not a delta-function preparation of a holonomy.
The prepared normalized rotor state is
$\Phi_h(0)=I_h\psi/\|I_h\psi\|$.

## 3. Quantitative finite-time estimate

For a fixed $T$ define

$$
 M_4(T)=\sup_{0\le t\le T}\||x|^4\psi_t\|_2,\qquad
 M_{31}(T)=\sup_{0\le t\le T}\||x|^3\nabla\psi_t\|_2.
 \tag{12}
$$

The first quantity uses the eighth position moment. Its subscript denotes
the power multiplying the wavefunction, not a fourth probability moment.
Assume $h^4M_4(T)^2/r_0^8\le1/4$. Equation (11) and Markov's inequality
then give $n_t=\|I_h\psi_t\|\ge\sqrt3/2$ for the whole interval.

The following explicit residual estimate holds:

$$
 \|(\mathsf H_h I_h-I_h\omega_0H_0)\psi_t\|
 \le
 \omega_0\left[
  12hM_4(T)
  +\frac{2h^2M_1M_{31}(T)}{r_0^3}
  +\frac{h^3M_\Delta M_4(T)}{r_0^4}
 \right]=:\mathcal R_h(T).
 \tag{13}
$$

Here is the calculation, including the chart boundary. The exact global
Taylor inequality

$$
 |2(1-\cos u)-u^2|\le |u|^4/12
 $$

gives, after $q=\sqrt h\,x$, a potential residual at most

$$
 \frac{\omega_0h}{12}
 \left\|\sum_p(Cx)_p^4\psi_t\right\|
 \le\frac{\omega_0h\|C\|^4}{12}M_4(T)
 \le12\omega_0hM_4(T).
 $$

The last estimate uses
$\sum_p(Cx)_p^4\le(\sum_p(Cx)_p^2)^2$; no plaquette factor is silently
dropped. Differentiating the cutoff creates the two terms
$-\omega_0h(\Delta\chi)\psi$ and
$-2\omega_0\sqrt h\,\nabla\chi\cdot\nabla\psi$ after scaling.
Their supports obey $|x|\ge r_0/\sqrt h$. The tail bounds

$$
 \|1_{\{|x|\ge r_0/\sqrt h\}}\psi_t\|
       \le h^2M_4(T)/r_0^4,\qquad
 \|1_{\{|x|\ge r_0/\sqrt h\}}\nabla\psi_t\|
       \le h^{3/2}M_{31}(T)/r_0^3
 $$

give exactly the second and third terms of (13). Periodizing introduces
no additional derivative terms because the cutoff and its first two
derivatives match zero at the outer boundary.

The compact Hamiltonian is self-adjoint on its usual torus Laplacian
domain, since its potential is bounded for every fixed $h$. The positive
quadratic $H_0$ is self-adjoint with the oscillator core. Differentiating
$e^{-i(t-s)\mathsf H_h}I_h\psi_s$ on this core and integrating therefore gives

$$
 \|e^{-it\mathsf H_h}I_h\psi-I_h\psi_t\|
 \le t\,\mathcal R_h(T).
 \tag{14}
$$

For the normalized comparison packet
$\Psi_h(t)=I_h\psi_t/n_t$, the norm inequality
$|n_t-n_0|\le t\mathcal R_h(T)$ yields

$$
 \sup_{0\le t\le T}
 \|e^{-it\mathsf H_h}\Phi_h(0)-\Psi_h(t)\|
 \le\frac{4T}{\sqrt3}\mathcal R_h(T).
 \tag{15}
$$

Thus the trace distance of the two pure densities, and every bounded
observable difference divided by its operator norm, are at most twice
the right side of (15). This is the stated fixed-volume, finite-time
$O(h)$ approximation. Finite Hermite packets and fixed coherent packets
have bounded versions of (12) even uniformly in time, but the bound
still grows with elapsed time through (14); no unrestricted long-time
claim is made.

The constants are not uniform in spatial volume. Both $r$ in the cutoff
Laplacian and the packet moments depend on the box. The smallest transverse
eigenvalue also tends to zero on growing boxes. The proof keeps all of
these dependences instead of turning a finite-volume estimate into a
thermodynamic theorem.

## 4. Transverse harmonic dynamics and bounded readouts

Use Fourier transforms at edge and face centers. For
$k_i=2\pi m_i/L_i$, put
$\widehat k_i=2\sin(k_i/2)$. Direct substitution into (3) gives

$$
 \widehat{Cq}_{ij}(k)
   =i(\widehat k_i\widehat q_j-\widehat k_j\widehat q_i),\qquad
 B(k)=|\widehat k|^2I-\widehat k\widehat k^T.
 \tag{16}
$$

For nonzero lattice momentum the Gauss direction is longitudinal and
the other two directions have eigenvalue
$\lambda(k)=|\widehat k|^2$. The three $k=0$ harmonic directions were
removed by the global-flux sector choice, not by a positive magnetic mass.
Counting all real coordinates gives $r=2(V-1)$.

In any real transverse normal coordinate with eigenvalue $\lambda>0$,

$$
 H_0=p^2+\lambda x^2
      =2\sqrt\lambda\,(a^\dagger a+1/2),\qquad
 a=(\lambda^{1/4}x+i\lambda^{-1/4}p)/\sqrt2.
 \tag{17}
$$

The harmonic Gaussian is
$(\det B^{1/2}/\pi^r)^{1/4}
\exp(-x^TB^{1/2}x/2)$. A coherent packet has centers

$$
 x_c(t)=\cos(2\omega_0B^{1/2}t)x_c(0)
       +B^{-1/2}\sin(2\omega_0B^{1/2}t)p_c(0),
 $$
$$
 p_c(t)=\cos(2\omega_0B^{1/2}t)p_c(0)
       -B^{1/2}\sin(2\omega_0B^{1/2}t)x_c(0).
 \tag{18}
$$

Consequently $\ddot x_c+4KJ\,B x_c=0$, with (2). These are the two
transverse lattice-photon oscillator polarizations within this prepared
harmonic limit. The compact packet approximates their quantum state,
not merely their frozen linearized equations.

For fixed coherent parameters the original angle amplitude is $O(\sqrt h)$
and the electric amplitude is $O(h^{-1/2})$. The natural canonical
variables are $x=q/\sqrt h$ and $p=\sqrt h E$. This is not a claim about
fixed nonzero angle-amplitude classical waves at arbitrarily small $h$.

The norm statement already controls arbitrary bounded compact observables.
It can also be expressed as convergence of scaled Weyl readouts. On a
fundamental cell choose a phase multiplier equal to
$e^{iv\cdot q/(2\sqrt h)}$ in the ball of radius $2R$, with any unit-modulus
measurable continuation outside, and combine it on both sides of the exact
torus translation $q\mapsto q+\sqrt h\,u$. For fixed $u,v$, its action on
$I_h\psi$ agrees with the Euclidean Weyl operator
$e^{ivx/2}e^{u\cdot\nabla}e^{ivx/2}$ up to cutoff tails of order $h^2$
on the packet class. To see this, for sufficiently small $h$ all relevant
translations stay in the chart unless
$|x|\ge r_0/(2\sqrt h)$; apply the same fourth-power tail bound.
Therefore finite-mode bounded Weyl characteristic functions converge.
Finite products at finitely many times follow by applying the same
residual argument to the finite list of displaced Schwartz packets and
telescoping. No assertion about arbitrary unbounded observables is needed.

## 5. A subsequent continuum limit, with its order stated

For a fixed physical side length $\ell$, take cubic lattice spacing
$a=\ell/N$ and set $\omega_{0,N}=c/(2a)$ with fixed $c>0$.
For each finite $N$, first take the weak-packet limit $h\to0$ above,
with $K_N=\omega_{0,N}h$ and $J_N=\omega_{0,N}/h$.
Only after that comparison take $N\to\infty$, retaining a fixed finite
set of nonzero integer Fourier indices and finite times.

The harmonic frequencies and transverse projectors satisfy

$$
 \omega_N(m)=\frac c a
       \sqrt{4\sum_i\sin^2(\pi m_i/N)}
       \longrightarrow c\,|2\pi m/\ell|,
 $$
$$
 I-\frac{\widehat k\widehat k^T}{|\widehat k|^2}
       \longrightarrow I-\frac{mm^T}{|m|^2}.
 \tag{19}
$$

For each fixed nonzero index the errors are $O(a^2)$. Choose matching
polarization frames for those finitely many modes, specify their state
in oscillator coordinates, and put the remaining oscillators in the
harmonic Gaussian. Their finite-mode dynamics and bounded characteristic
functions then have the massless two-polarization limit (19).
Real sine/cosine coordinates or a conjugate-compatible complex Fourier
description give the same oscillator count.

This is an ordered finite-mode continuum statement on a fixed physical
box. It is not uniform control of all ultraviolet modes, an
infinite-volume phase theorem, a deconfinement proof, or construction of
all continuum field observables. An arbitrary simultaneous choice
$h=h_N$ requires bounds on the $N$-dependence of (12) and (15); no such
choice is inferred silently.

The previously checked large-spin record theorem can be composed only
with its own hypotheses. One sufficient order is: fixed box and fixed
$h>0$, then $S\to\infty$ in that theorem; next $h\to0$; finally the
finite-mode limit (19). The compact $C^2$ packet has finite electric
fourth moments. At fixed box and $h$, Fourier truncation to $|E_e|\le S$
preserves Gauss and global flux and converges to the packet; its normalized
fourth moments remain bounded for sufficiently large $S$.
The required hopping parameter satisfies
$\epsilon_S^2S(S+1)=1/(2h^2)$, so even the elementary small-hopping
condition requires $hS\to\infty$ in a simultaneous limit.
The preceding theorem's constants also depend on $h$ through couplings
and moments. There is no unsupported universal joint power law here.
Formation can remain enabled at every finite record parameter, with the
already checked decreasing rate; this does not establish nonzero limiting
production density.

## 6. Time normalization countercontrol

The factor $\omega_0$ in (13) matters. Along $K$ fixed and $J\to\infty$,
one has $\omega_0h=K$, so the leading bound is not small on a fixed
physical time interval. This is not merely a defect of an estimate.

A single cyclic square in its Gauss sector has one compact coordinate
$\theta$ and the exact Hamiltonian
$4K m^2-2J\cos\theta$, $m=-i\partial_\theta$.
At $K=h,J=1/h$, after adding $2/h$ and scaling $\theta=\sqrt h\,x$,
its local operator is

$$
 H_h^{\rm sq}=H_0^{\rm sq}+hV_1+O(h^2),\qquad
 H_0^{\rm sq}=-4\partial_x^2+x^2,\quad V_1=-x^4/12.
 \tag{20}
$$

The first two harmonic energies are $2,6$. Their diagonal first
corrections are $-1/4,-5/4$. Exact first vector corrections are

$$
 \eta_0=\frac{\sqrt2}{16}|2\rangle+
                    \frac{\sqrt6}{96}|4\rangle,\qquad
 \eta_1=\frac{5\sqrt6}{48}|3\rangle+
                    \frac{\sqrt{30}}{96}|5\rangle.
 \tag{21}
$$

They solve
$(H_0-E_n)\eta_n=-(V_1-\langle n|V_1|n\rangle)|n\rangle$.
Cutting off and compactly periodizing
$|n\rangle+h\eta_n$ produces quasimodes with norm residual $O(h^2)$:
the global sixth-order cosine remainder is bounded by
$h^2|x|^6/360$, and the finite-Hermite cutoff tails are exponentially
small. These are genuine prepared-state phase estimates; they do not
rely on identifying eigenvectors from a numerical fit.

For completeness, the first few exact eigenvalues have the same labeling.
A partition into $|\theta|\lesssim\delta$ and its complement, with
$\delta=h^{1/4}$, has kinetic localization error $O(h/\delta^2)$.
Inside, the potential bounds the oscillator potential up to
$1-O(\delta^2)$; outside, it is bounded below by a constant times
$\delta^2/h\to\infty$. Min-max comparison and cutoff oscillator trial
states give convergence of each fixed low eigenvalue to $4n+2$.
Their limiting separation is four. The above quasimode residuals
therefore identify the two simple low eigenvalues through $O(h^2)$.

Now keep $K=1,J=h^{-2}$, multiplying (20) by $h^{-1}$.
The first relative energy is $4/h-1+O(h)$.
At the fixed physical time $t=\pi/2$ and along $h=1/n$, the uncorrected
harmonic relative phase is one, while the exact prepared relative phase
tends to $i$. A superposition of the two packets thus has a nonvanishing
state and bounded-readout discrepancy. The same conclusion follows
directly from the corrected-quasimode evolution, whose residual after
this rescaling is only $O(h)$.
This one-coordinate countercontrol explains why the normalization and
time in (1) are part of the claim; it is not asserted as a separate
classification of all three-dimensional coupling paths.
