## Incidence and physical sector

The forward-difference curl satisfies the two exact chain identities. Its
complex Fourier Gram matrix is `lambda I-d d^*`, with
`lambda=4 sum_j sin^2(k_j/2)`. This gives two nonzero singular values
sqrt(lambda) at each nonzero momentum, and only one zero momentum even on
even tori. The real transverse space has dimension 2V-2; the kernel consists
of V-1 gradient directions plus three harmonic edge directions. The face
image has both zero magnetic divergence and zero harmonic magnetic flux.

The correct electric equation uses C^T. With E=-P and B=CQ, the cross bracket
is iC, the equations are `E_dot=C^T B`, `B_dot=-C E`, and the positive energy
is conserved. An exact finite countercontrol gives energy derivative +1
when C is wrongly substituted for C^T. The source uses the correct adjoint.

The proper-rotation action is a cochain action, including basepoint shifts
for reversed edges and for negative face tangential directions. Together
with the physical Cartesian-triplet sign, it gives the stated covariance
of the supplied edge-block architecture. It is not a rotation claim about
the original site primitive.

Quantizing the real orthogonal complement of ker C selects zero electric
charge and harmonic electric flux. Its positive oscillator Hamiltonian has
2V-2 canonical modes and a normalizable ground state at fixed coarse volume.
The unreduced gauge coordinates are retained as free coordinates before
the observable-sector limit. The source does not impose a nonnormalizable
exact momentum-zero vector in that unreduced Hilbert space.

## Changing state and bounded field convergence

The link-block lowering formula is the exact spin-K/2 representation on
K actual singlet/triplet pairs. The common-Fock cutoff sector is reducing.
Its finite commutator is `1-2N_e/K`, so electric Gauss generally fails to
commute with the finite Hamiltonian. Magnetic divergence and harmonic
magnetic flux remain exact readout identities.

The previously checked weighted Hamiltonian estimate requires fixed real
coefficients, not a symmetric curl. It remains valid for C^T C. Writing
M=3V, one sufficient constant is

    ||(H_K-H)psi|| <= [(17M/2)(2+3sqrt(2))]/K
                      * ||(N_tot+1)^2 psi||.

The gauge-squeezed state has Q_Z scale epsilon^-1 and P_Z scale epsilon.
Canonical flows and fixed Weyl words give the weighted bound
`||(N_tot+1)^2 word psi_epsilon|| <= A epsilon^-4`.
Occupation projection and normalization cost at most
`A K^-2 epsilon^-4`. Telescoping with canonical intermediate vectors gives
Hamiltonian errors `A K^-1 epsilon^-4` and linear-Weyl errors
`A K^-1 epsilon^-3`. No unproved bound on finite-Hamiltonian evolved number
moments is needed.

Only electric gauge momenta occur in the field algebra. Their commuting
factor in a canonical field word is exactly
`exp[-epsilon^2 ||sum_j a_(j,Z)||^2/4]`. Choosing epsilon=K^-1/6 balances
the Hamiltonian and gauge-phase errors, yielding the stated O(K^-1/3)
finite-word error; normalized projection contributes O(K^-4/3).
The constants fix volume, state, word and time interval. This is not a
uniform thermodynamic/continuum assertion.

## Why the addendum controls Gauss mean square

The spin-component norm is exactly sqrt(K/2) for each Q_K and P_K, so
`||H_K|| <= (17M/4)K` is a sufficient bound. Multiplying it by the normalized
projection error makes that contribution to initial energy O(K^-1/3).
The weighted Hamiltonian difference and canonical gauge energy have the
same order. Thus the finite initial energy approaches the unshifted target
`E_T=<psi_T,H_T psi_T>` to O(delta), delta=K^-1/3. Both evolutions conserve
their respective energies. This step uses target-energy convergence,
not merely an upper bound on energy.

For a single Heisenberg Weyl expectation, evolved-state errors cost O(delta)
independently of its real test z. Weyl Duhamel and sixth moments of the
translated canonical state give the uniform polynomial bound

    |chi_K(z,t)-chi_T(z,t)| <= A delta(1+||z||)^4.

The two field families E_(T,K)(t) and B_K(t) separately have commuting
components. They therefore have separate ordinary joint spectral measures;
joint commutativity between E and B is neither needed nor claimed. Gaussian
Fourier smoothing of
`F_a(X)=(1-exp(-a||X||^2))/a`, together with the canonical fourth moment,
gives a finite quadratic-moment lower bound with error `A(a+delta/a)`.

Adding the transverse electric and magnetic lower bounds, and subtracting
them from the conserved total energy, leaves at most
`A(delta+a+delta/a)` for `<||E_(Z,K)||^2>`. Taking a=sqrt(delta) proves the
uniform fixed-time O(K^-1/6) bound, also for electric divergence after the
fixed d_0^T map. The harmonic electric components are included. The
sufficient root-mean-square rate is K^-1/12.

This extra argument is necessary: a variable equal to R with probability
R^-2 and zero otherwise has characteristic functions converging uniformly
to one while its second moment stays one. Thus bounded characteristic
convergence alone would not prove the addendum. The supplied energy and
transverse lower-bound argument avoids this counterexample.
