## Result, in one paragraph
Give every empty site an exponential clock whose rate depends covariantly on the recorded set and its values. The next site is the one whose clock rings first, with probability `λ_x/Σλ`, and the finished law is the jump chain's; value-blind laws give a mixture over orders that depends on the law only through the induced distribution of recorded-set multisets (R1). On `2×3` at `(3,1,2)` the uniform, seeded and attracting laws give three different laws at distances `0.0291`, `0.0203`, `0.0258` from the static law — the first equal to the census note's Theorem 3 rational — and on the cube the all-`+x` probabilities differ (R2). On every tree window the seeded law equals the static law exactly, because every record after the first has exactly one recorded neighbour (R3). On the plaquette no covariant rate law whatsoever — value-dependent ones included — gives the static law at any non-constant rule: the first site is uniform, the only surviving value-dependent decision is the probability `p_type` of jumping across the square, which depends only on the type of the first recorded value, and for patterns whose two diagonals have the same pair type `μ/Π K = (1/4) Σ_s G(p_{type(s)}, d)` with `G(p,d) = (1−p)d/6 + p d²/36` strictly increasing in `d = 1/K_2`; the patterns `(+x,−x,+x,−x)` and `(+x,+x,−x,−x)` have equal type multisets and `d_same = 72/13 ≠ d_anti = 72/11` (R4; `(+x,−x,+y,+y)` with `d_orth = 6` covers `p = q`). Hence the static plaquette law is not a convex combination of the order laws (R5), and the value-dependent parallel-growth law even leaves their linear span (R6).
