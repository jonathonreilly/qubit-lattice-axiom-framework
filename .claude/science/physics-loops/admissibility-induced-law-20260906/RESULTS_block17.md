## Result, in one paragraph
The weight matrix has eigenvalues `Z_1`, `p − q` (three) and `p + q − 2r` (two) and is invariant under the full cube group, transitive on the six values (T1). On every torus of even side the static law is reflection positive through planes containing sites, for every positive weight matrix — the shared plane makes `E[F·F∘θ]` a sum of squares — so no definiteness of the coupling is needed (T2). The chessboard estimate follows by the iterated quadratic-form inequality (T3), and the disseminated event "every bond of one direction disagrees" has probability at most `(6m/p)^N`, `m = max(q, r)` (T4). In two dimensions, planar cut–cycle duality and an explicit count of enclosing dual cycles (`(n/2)3^{n−1}`) give, for `ε = 6m/p ≤ 1/36`, i.e. `p ≥ 216 m`, `μ_L(v_0 ≠ v_x) ≤ 5/24 + 45/256 = 295/768 < 1/2` for all `x` within a quarter of a box of side at least `20` (T5). Every limit point of the torus laws is a translation-invariant Gibbs state with that long-range order; a unique Gibbs state would be extremal, hence tail-trivial, hence short-range correlated with `μ(v_0 = v_x) → 1/6`: contradiction; so the static law on `Z²` has at least two Gibbs states for `p ≥ 216 m`, and the symmetry orbit of a non-symmetric extremal state has between two and six members (T6). In three dimensions the same chain restricted to a coordinate plane through the origin — two differing records are separated by bad bonds of the plane they share, so the planar count applies while the chessboard estimate and the disseminated bound are the three-dimensional ones — gives at least two Gibbs states for the same threshold `p ≥ 216 m`, with no surface-connectivity lemma (T7). The thresholds are existence constants, not optimal.
