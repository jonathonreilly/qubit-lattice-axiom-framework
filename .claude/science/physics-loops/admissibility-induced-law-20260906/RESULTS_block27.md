## Result, in one paragraph
The sphere kernel's total-variation sensitivity to its predecessor sum is at most `1/(2√3)` per unit: the density's log-derivative is `s − A(κ)û`, the directional second moment is `A/κ + (1 − 3A/κ − A²)(û·d)²`, and the coefficient of the angle is non-positive because `A(κ)/κ` is decreasing — a series comparison of `2 sinh² κ` with `(κ/2) sinh 2κ + κ²` whose `κ²` terms cancel and whose higher coefficients are `(2^{2m}/(2m)!)(m/2 − 1) ≥ 0` (T1). Coupling two runs from different initial planes site by site with a near-optimal coupling of the two kernels, the per-site expected chordal distance obeys `D_{t+1} ≤ √3β D_t`; for `β < 1/√3` this gives exactly one invariant law, invariant under all rotations, reached from every initial plane within `2(√3β)^t` per site (T2), and the aligned plane's magnetization obeys `m_t ≤ (√3β)^t` (T3; on one site exactly `A(3β)^t ≤ β^t`). The route cannot reach beyond `β = 1` because the kernel's mean alone moves by a third of a small change (T4); its exact total-variation version would stop at `2/3` (the sensitivity is `1/4` at `V = 0` and numerically never larger). Block 26's runs lose the memory at every coupling tried, so `1/√3` is the route's constant, not a threshold. The four-way table of explicit weak-coupling uniqueness regions is complete.
