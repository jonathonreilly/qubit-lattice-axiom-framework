   139	## 2. Load-bearing step (A) — additivity on effects from (M1)–(M3)
   140	
   141	The complement law and partial additivity follow from the parent's exact
   142	hypotheses:
   143	
   144	```text
   145	two-outcome POVM {E, 𝟙 − E}:  m(E) + m(𝟙 − E) = 1            (complement law)
   146	three-outcome POVM {E_1, E_2, 𝟙 − E_1 − E_2} (valid when E_1 + E_2 ≤ 𝟙):
   147	                              m(E_1) + m(E_2) + m(𝟙 − E_1 − E_2) = 1.
   148	```
   149	
   150	Subtracting, and using the complement law on `E_1 + E_2`
   151	(`m(𝟙 − (E_1+E_2)) = 1 − m(E_1+E_2)`):
   152	
   153	```text
   154	m(E_1 + E_2) = m(E_1) + m(E_2)        whenever E_1 + E_2 ≤ 𝟙.   (partial additivity)
   155	```
   156	
   157	This is the effect-algebra additivity law. The runner reproves the complement law
   158	and partial additivity exactly on a rational qubit instance (`Part 0`) and
   159	numerically over 2000 admissible effect pairs on `M_2` (`Part 3`, residuals
   160	`~10⁻¹⁶`).
   161	
   162	## 3. Load-bearing steps (B),(C) — linearity and the real-linear extension
   163	
   164	**(B) Homogeneity.** Iterating partial additivity gives `m(nE) = n m(E)` whenever
   165	`nE ≤ 𝟙` (integer `n`), hence `m((p/q)E) = (p/q) m(E)` for rationals
   166	`p/q ∈ [0,1]` with `(p/q)E` an effect. The bound `0 ≤ m ≤ 1` makes `t ↦ m(tE)`
   167	monotone and bounded on `[0,1]`, so the rational homogeneity extends to all real
   168	`t ∈ [0,1]`: `m(tE) = t m(E)` (the standard Cauchy-on-a-bounded-domain upgrade).
   169	The runner reproves rational homogeneity exactly (`Part 0`, `q = 1/2, 1/3, 3/7`)
   170	and real homogeneity numerically over 1000 seeds (`Part 4`, residuals `~10⁻¹⁶`).
   171	
   172	**(C) Real-linear extension to `Herm(M_2)`.** `Herm(M_2)` is the **real** 4-dim
   173	space spanned by `{𝟙, σ_x, σ_y, σ_z}`. Any Hermitian `H` can be written
   174	`H = c_0 𝟙 + c · σ`; choosing a real `α > 0` and `β` so that `αH + β𝟙` lies in
   175	the effect cone, (A)+(B) determine `m(αH + β𝟙) = α F(H) + β m(𝟙)`, which defines
   176	a single real-linear functional `F` on `Herm(M_2)` agreeing with `m` on effects.
   177	`F` is fixed by its four values `F(𝟙), F(σ_x), F(σ_y), F(σ_z)`; equivalently,
   178	`F(σ_a) = 2 m(P_a^+) − 1` since `σ_a = 2P_a^+ − 𝟙` and `F(𝟙) = m(𝟙) = 1`. The
   179	runner evaluates `F` on the basis using only effect-cone values of `m`
   180	(`reconstruct_sigma_from_effect_functional`, used throughout).
   181	
   182	## 4. Load-bearing steps (D),(E) — Riesz/trace form and that `σ` is a state
   183	
   184	**(D) Riesz / trace form.** On the real Hilbert space `(Herm(M_2), ⟨·,·⟩)` with
   185	`⟨X,Y⟩ = Tr(XY)`, every real-linear functional `F` is represented by a unique
   186	`σ ∈ Herm(M_2)` via `F(H) = Tr(σ H) = ⟨σ, H⟩`. Expanding `σ` in the orthogonal
   187	basis (`Tr(B_a B_b) = 2 δ_{ab}` on `M_2`):
   188	
   189	```text
   190	σ = (1/2) Σ_a F(B_a) B_a
   191	  = ½[ F(𝟙) 𝟙 + Σ_{a} (2 m(P_a^+) − 1) σ_a ]
   192	  = ½[ 𝟙 + Σ_{a∈{x,y,z}} (2 m(P_a^+) − 1) σ_a ].
   193	```
   194	
   195	The runner reproves the basis orthogonality `Tr(B_a B_b) = d δ_{ab}` on `M_2` and
   196	`M_4` (`Part 7`), and reconstructs `σ` from `m` **blind** to any pre-chosen
   197	operator, verifying `σ_reconstructed = σ_true` exactly on a rational qubit
   198	instance (`Part 0`) and numerically over 400 random qubit measures (`Part 1`,
   199	residuals `~10⁻¹⁶`). Uniqueness is immediate from the orthogonal expansion.
   200	
   201	**(E) `σ` is a density matrix.** Normalization (M2) gives
   202	`Tr σ = ⟨σ, 𝟙⟩ = F(𝟙) = m(𝟙) = 1`. Nonnegativity: for any unit `|ψ⟩`, the rank-1
   203	projector `P_ψ = |ψ⟩⟨ψ|` is an effect, and `m(P_ψ) = Tr(σ P_ψ) = ⟨ψ|σ|ψ⟩ ≥ 0`
   204	by (M1)–(M3)-nonnegativity (`m ≥ 0`); since this holds for all `|ψ⟩`, `σ ≥ 0`.
   205	The runner confirms `m(P_ψ) ≥ 0` on random rank-1 projectors and that the
   206	reconstructed `σ` has nonnegative spectrum (`Part 5`), and that the induced
   207	measure satisfies (M1)–(M3) on random POVMs with `n = 2,3,4` outcomes
   208	(`Part 2`).
   209	
   210	Combining (A)–(E): `m(E) = Tr(σ · E)` on `E(M_2)` with `σ` a unique density
   211	matrix. This is the Busch / CFMR effect-Gleason representation, reproven on the
   212	qubit from primitives — the load-bearing direction the parent imported by name.

    96	## Step 1 — Why Gleason's projection-lattice theorem fails at dim 2
    97	
    98	At `dim H_Λ = 2` (single qubit, `|Λ| = 1`), the projection lattice
    99	`P(H_Λ)` has a small structure: the only orthogonal pairs are
   100	`(|ψ⟩⟨ψ|, |ψ^⊥⟩⟨ψ^⊥|)` for unit vectors `|ψ⟩`. The orthonormal-basis
   101	constraint `m(|ψ⟩⟨ψ|) + m(|ψ^⊥⟩⟨ψ^⊥|) = 1` permits non-unique
   102	extensions — frame functions on `S(ℂ²)` are not forced to be
   103	quadratic.

   106	bases come in continuous families, and the frame function's values

   108	argument. At dim 2, the rotation freedom is insufficient and the
   109	quadratic extension can fail.

   112	to the POVM-effect-algebra extension.
