   119	**T1 (effect horn: sitewise forcing at every finite dimension, no bridge
   120	input).** Assume E1-E2 on a finite region of dimension `d ≥ 2`. Then there is
   121	a unique density matrix `σ` with `w(E) = Tr(σE)` for every effect `E`. The
   122	proof is the five-step finite argument, dimension-generic:
   123	
   124	- **(A) Partial additivity.** For effects with `E_1 + E_2 ≤ 1`, apply E2 to
   125	  the menus `{E_1, E_2, 1 − E_1 − E_2}` and `{E_1 + E_2, 1 − E_1 − E_2}`:
   126	  subtracting, `w(E_1 + E_2) = w(E_1) + w(E_2)`. The two-outcome menu
   127	  `{E, 1 − E}` gives the complement law `w(E) + w(1 − E) = 1`.
   128	- **(B) Homogeneity.** Iterating (A) on the `(r+1)`-outcome menu
   129	  `{E/r, ..., E/r, 1 − E}` gives `w(E/r) = w(E)/r` and then `w(qE) = q w(E)`
   130	  for rational `q ∈ [0,1]`. Monotonicity follows from (A): if `E ≤ F` then
   131	  `F − E` is an effect and `w(F) = w(E) + w(F − E) ≥ w(E)`. For real
   132	  `t ∈ [0,1]` and rationals `q_1 < t < q_2`, the operator inequalities
   133	  `q_1 E ≤ tE ≤ q_2 E` then squeeze `w(tE)` between `q_1 w(E)` and
   134	  `q_2 w(E)`, so `w(tE) = t w(E)`.
   135	- **(C) Linear extension.** `Herm(M_d)` is a real vector space of dimension
   136	  `d^2` spanned by effects (the normalized identity-plus-generator elements
   137	  `(1 + B_k)/2` and `1` itself). Additivity plus homogeneity on the positive
   138	  cone extend `w` to a well-defined real-linear functional `F` on
   139	  `Herm(M_d)`: scale any positive `A` into the effect interval, set
   140	  `F(A) = s·w(A/s)` for `s` dominating the top eigenvalue (well-defined by
   141	  homogeneity), and define `F` on differences; the standard two-sided
   142	  cancellation argument shows representation-independence.
   143	- **(D) Trace representation.** The trace pairing `⟨X,Y⟩ = Tr(XY)` is a
   144	  nondegenerate real inner product on `Herm(M_d)`, so `F(H) = Tr(σH)` for a
   145	  unique Hermitian `σ`, reconstructed linearly from the `d^2` basis values of

   682	## Paper-Facing Prose Voice
   683	
   684	The target voice for paper-facing prose, curated methodology prose, repo
   685	instructions, and new synthesis is Feynman-like in the useful sense:
   686	plain, physical, concrete, curious, and hard to fool. Do not imitate
   687	quirks or make the prose performative. Use the attitude.
   688	
   689	Do not rewrite raw captures or historical dated notes just to polish the
   690	voice; those files are evidence of the work as it happened.
   691	
   692	### The rule
   693	
   694	Explain what we are doing as if the reader is smart, skeptical, and busy.
   695	
   696	Say the simple physical question first. Then say what was checked. Then say
   697	what did not close.
   698	
   699	### Use this voice
   700	
   701	- Start from the physical question, not from the institutional framing.
   702	- Prefer short sentences when the idea is hard.
   703	- Explain why a result is surprising or useful in ordinary language before listing the machinery.
   704	- Put equations after the idea they express.
   705	- Say "we find", "we check", "we keep", and "we do not claim" when those are the honest verbs.
   706	- Treat negative results as information.
   707	- Keep the reader close to the object: lattice, operator, runner, eigenvalue, projector, source, bridge.
   708	
   709	### Avoid this voice
   710	
   711	- "broad exact backbone"
   712	- "methodological innovation"
   713	- "transformative framework"
   714	- "groundbreaking result"
   715	- "paradigm"
   716	- "comprehensive package" unless the sentence immediately says what is inside
   717	- "already closed" without naming the exact surface
   718	- any sentence whose main job is to sound important
   719	
   720	### Decoration-pruning tone
   721	
   722	When pruning algebraic decoration, the tone is "claim-surface management",
   723	not "criticism of the algebra itself". The identities are real; what's
   724	being pruned is the count of separately-retained rows. A package that
   725	presents 25 separate retained theorems where 3 distinct claims exist is
   726	harder to review, harder to falsify, and harder to take seriously as an
   727	external reader. Pruning improves signal density of the publication
   728	package. Use that framing in prose.
   729

   731	
   732	For each paragraph, ask:
   733	
   734	1. What is the question?
   735	2. What is the object being calculated?
   736	3. What is the support surface?
   737	4. What is still open?

   739	If the paragraph does not answer at least one of these, cut it or move

    72	
    73	Landed science must use domain-explicit names. Do not introduce a bare

    80
