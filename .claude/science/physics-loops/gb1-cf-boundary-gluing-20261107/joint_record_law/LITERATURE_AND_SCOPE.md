## Primary literature

Gleason's theorem, arXiv:quant-ph/9909073v3, 28 May 2003, later PRL 91, 120403:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9909073 . Read pp.1-2 through the positive-cone
extension and the discussion of the larger effect domain. His P1-P3 assume
a probability assignment on all effects, not merely projective menus. The
finite-dimensional proof is written and checked in RESULT; no infinite normal-
state theorem is needed. Caves, Fuchs, Manne and Renes,

localizable quantum operations, arXiv:quant-ph/0102043v2:

and the early section-II causal-operation discussion. It distinguishes

## Route choice and research value

The effect-calculus route provides one structural law over a continuous
measurement family; it avoids a four-setting table but exposes its stronger
probability assumptions. Local quantum instruments provide an explicit
no-message realization within that additional model. A literal atomic pair
append tests the current record ontology and complete neighborhood support.
The sequential extension is tested on its first nontrivial intermediate state,
where the conditional collision is decisive. The polynomial joint-law control
keeps the same record premises and demonstrates that no table and no-signalling
alone do not select the quantum answer. These are compared routes, not votes.

The result closes this finite conditional construction and diagnoses the
remaining physical identification. It does not promote existing standard
identities as new mathematics or establish a native asynchronous Bell process.
Evidence that would move the next decision is a concrete formation mechanism
with its full conditioning semantics, not another fit to the four correlations.
