# Minimal matter coupling and radiative discriminator

Frozen before numerical controls, October12,2026. Direct parent authorship.
Deadline is the parent campaign deadline; no worker or separate clock.

Observation motivating this block: binary-pulsar timing resolves orbital
period decay together with other timing parameters. Weisberg & Huang2016,
arXiv1606.02744 and DOI10.3847/0004-637X/829/1/55, provide a primary dataset
and a relativistic interpretation. Their inferred masses and published
corrected observed/GR ratio are not model-independent raw measurements.
No published ratio or mass is a calibration or validation input here.

Target: extend the checked constant-coefficient vector action by universal
nonrelativistic source coupling, then derive its leading slow-source radiation
and conservative circular equatorial mechanics under explicit assumptions.
Use the source multipoles and exact time-harmonic density integral as separate
checks. The static susceptibility cannot by itself validate the dynamics.

New supplied premises: matter has ordinary positive inertial masses and
kinetic energy; scalar physical potential is e.u; density is conserved mass;
coefficients and e remain constant; source size and velocity are small enough
for the multipole expansion; weak damping permits a conservative orbit for
the leading power. Smooth finite bodies regulate self-fields. Point masses
are used only as the exterior/multipole limit, not finite-self-energy states.
For a self-consistent circular example the orbital plane is perpendicular
to e. No relativistic clock, photon, strong-body or metric bridge is supplied.

Required proof: source and matter energy exchange cancel; characterize the
leading far-zone propagator including why its middle term is subradiative;
show which conserved multipoles remove monopole/dipole radiation at the
stated approximation; obtain positive angular flux for both modes; compare
with independently integrated exact source harmonics at decreasing orbital
speed; derive the static circular frequency and vertical stability.

The GR quadrupole power may be used only as an explicitly supplied mathematical
comparator for the same trajectory, inertial masses and static calibration.
Identifying either wave speed with light or matching one ratio is a new
calibration assumption, not an independent observational test. No binary
pulsar exclusion, fit, new-theory adoption or TOE is claimed by this block.
