> In the supplied common matter/rotor model, each original formation event reduces
> a particular aggregate field-response strength by the same amount.

The body correctly distinguishes the unconditional expected-count budget from the normalized history formula, whose stated hypothesis is definite initial number. For clarity, the difference is already visible from the existing identities. With \(F=48K(2nI-N)\) and a selected jump \(B_j\),
\[
B_j^*F B_j=B_j^*B_j(F-96K).

After normalization, the pre-jump term is weighted by \(B_j^*B_j\); it need not equal the unconditional prior expectation of F. This does not alter the exact ensemble relation \(f(t)+96K\,\mathbb EC(t)=f(0)\) or the fixed-number history statement.

> In the supplied common matter/rotor model, the expected original formation count
> fixes the change of a particular aggregate field-response strength.
