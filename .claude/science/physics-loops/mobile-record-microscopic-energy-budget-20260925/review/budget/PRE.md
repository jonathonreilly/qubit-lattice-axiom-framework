## 2. Extending the capped estimate without assuming a capped trajectory

Choose the single fixed cap

    M_* = e_*+Delta.

For sufficiently large S, write `r_S=c_(M_*) epsilon_S>=0`. If `E_S(rho)<=M_*`, (1) applies. If `E_S(rho)>M_*`, then

    a-b[E_S(rho)-e_*]-r_S < 0 <= gamma_S(rho).

Consequently the same bound holds for **every** microscopic density at that S:

    Gamma_S + b(H_S-e_* I) >= (a-r_S) I.                 (3)

This is an operator inequality because the equivalent expectation bound holds for all normalized vectors in a finite-dimensional space. The proof does not incorrectly apply the capped transfer estimate to high-energy states. It uses only positivity of Gamma there.

Define

    Theta_S = e_* + (a-r_S)/b,
    d_S = Theta_S-e_S.

Then

    E_S(rho) >= Theta_S - gamma_S(rho)/b,
    Gamma_S+b(H_S-e_S I) >= b d_S I,
    Theta_S -> e_*+Delta,       d_S -> Delta>0.           (4)

The error in Theta_S is O(epsilon_S) with an unspecified, imported constant. The error in d_S includes `e_S-e_*`; the supplied infimum convergence alone does not give an O(epsilon_S) rate for that difference.

Equation (3) is independent of time. It can therefore be evaluated on any actual state rho_S(t), even if its energy later becomes very large. This supplies a uniform algebraic time budget at a fixed sufficiently large S. It does not supply uniform convergence of microscopic trajectories to a rotor semigroup, or uniformity in g, volume or a laboratory scale. The source's absence of a uniform long-time dynamical approximation is respected.

## 3. Exact finite-time budgets

Let rho_S(t) be the actual full finite-spin GKLS evolution of any initial density. The adjoint dissipator and (2) give exactly

    d/dt Tr(N rho_S(t)) = 2 gamma_S(t),
    int_0^T gamma_S(t) dt
       = [Tr(N rho_S(T))-Tr(N rho_S(0))]/2
       <= q_S := [2n-Tr(N rho_S(0))]/2 <= n/2.           (5)

This is an identity of unconditioned expectations. It holds for arbitrary initial coherences. At a definite N sector, the related trajectory count bound is a finite integer bound; it is not needed for the mean-energy argument.

Integrating (4) and then (5) yields, for every T>0 at each sufficiently large S,

    (1/T) int_0^T E_S(t)dt
       >= Theta_S
          -[Tr(N rho_S(T))-Tr(N rho_S(0))]/(2 b T)
       >= Theta_S-q_S/(b T).                            (6)

The actual realized expected count gives the sharper middle expression. The bound is not an assumed relation between the energy of each mark and a photon energy. Counts enter only through the integrated positive intensity and the imported affine energy/activity inequality.

For a ground-space initial density, `E_S(0)=e_S`, so

    (1/T) int_0^T [E_S(t)-E_S(0)]dt >= d_S-q_S/(b T),
    sup_(0<=t<=T) [E_S(t)-E_S(0)] >= d_S-q_S/(b T).       (7)

The pointwise excess is always nonnegative by the variational minimum. The lower bound in (7) can be replaced by its maximum with zero. These are an average and a peak bound, not an endpoint bound at T or a monotonicity assertion.

For example, uniformly over all ground-space input families and at any fixed T,

    liminf_(S->infinity) (1/T) int_0^T [E_S(t)-e_S]dt
       >= max(0, 4 delta n - 5 delta n/(8 kappa T)).      (8)

Here `q_S<=n/2` was used; a known smaller number capacity improves the coefficient. The right side is positive when `T>5/(32 kappa)`. At `T=5/(16 kappa)` it is at least `2 delta n`. These are conditional sufficient statements in the supplied model's time and energy units. Neither c_(M_*) nor a numerical finite-spin threshold is supplied by this reconstruction.

More generally, if a trajectory remained below `e_S+u` for all `0<=t<=T`, with `u<d_S`, (4) would force `gamma_S(t)>=b(d_S-u)`. Therefore

    T <= q_S/[b(d_S-u)].                                (9)

This is a bound on how long a specified energy ceiling can hold. It neither estimates an instantaneous heating rate nor states when the first excursion occurs. It applies to any initial state that meets that ceiling, not only a ground input.

Because (6) holds for every T with the same r_S, it also justifies time-average bounds for joint sequences `S->infinity`, `T_S->infinity` at this fixed graph and fixed couplings:

    liminf_S (1/T_S) int_0^(T_S) [E_S(t)-e_S]dt >= Delta. (10)

No uniform-in-time microscopic-to-rotor approximation is being used in (10). The finite-capacity remainder is at most `n/(2bT_S)`. This argument does not justify a corresponding pointwise endpoint assertion at arbitrary jointly chosen times t_S; that would require further uniform relaxation information.

## 4. Long time at each fixed finite spin

For a fixed S the Hilbert space and GKLS generator are finite dimensional. Thus gamma_S(t) is differentiable and uniformly continuous on t>=0: its derivative is bounded by the finite norm of the generator applied to Gamma_S. It is nonnegative and integrable by (5). It follows that

    gamma_S(t) -> 0 as t->infinity.                      (11)

For completeness, if a positive lower-sized spike persisted along a sequence of late times, uniform continuity would supply intervals of fixed positive width and area, contradicting integrability. The derivative bound can grow with S; this is a fixed-spin argument. It does not contradict the formation parent's careful absence of a pointwise infinite-volume rotor rate theorem.

Equations (4) and (11) already imply

    liminf_(t->infinity) E_S(t) >= Theta_S.               (12)

In fact the ordinary energy has a limit at each fixed S. Here is a direct finite-dimensional justification, rather than an assumption of convergence of the density. The bounded quantum semigroup has generator eigenvalues with nonpositive real parts, and any eigenvalues on the imaginary axis are semisimple; an imaginary-axis Jordan block would make its powers grow polynomially in time, contradicting boundedness. Each scalar expectation is consequently a finite sum of decaying exponential-polynomial terms and undamped Fourier terms. Since gamma_S is integrable, all its undamped coefficients vanish: multiplying by each conjugate Fourier phase and time averaging isolates that coefficient, while the absolute integral divided by T tends to zero. Hence gamma_S decays exponentially times a finite polynomial, with constants depending on S.

Put `A_S=H_S-e_S I>=0`, `M_S=||A_S||`. The Hamiltonian part changes no expectation of H_S. Cauchy--Schwarz gives for the dissipator

    |dE_S/dt|
       <= M_S gamma_S(t)
          + M_S sqrt(||Gamma_S|| gamma_S(t)).            (13)

Indeed the positive jump-output term is bounded by M_S gamma_S. The anticommutator term is the real part of `Tr(rho A_S Gamma_S)`, whose absolute value is at most `sqrt(Tr rho A_S^2) sqrt(Tr rho Gamma_S^2)`. This is bounded by the second term in (13). The fixed-spin exponential-polynomial decay makes both terms integrable. Thus

    E_(infinity,S) := lim_(t->infinity) E_S(t)
       exists and is at least Theta_S.                  (14)

This does not require the density itself to converge. A dark invariant subspace may retain Hamiltonian coherences and oscillating observables while its H_S expectation stays constant. Finite-dimensional Cesaro limits are stationary and have the same limiting energy; they need not be unique across preparations.

Combining (14) with P37b gives

    liminf_(S->infinity) [E_(infinity,S)-e_S] >= Delta.   (15)

For ground inputs this is also a lower bound on the asymptotic net ordinary system-energy gain. The order in (15) is long time at each finite spin, then the resource limit. Equation (10) gives an additional joint statement only for time averages. Neither (11) nor (14) has a proved spin-uniform relaxation time. No pointwise limit at a chosen rotor or infinite-volume state is inferred.

Stationary densities are a special case: (5) makes them dark in mean, and positivity gives `L_(mu,S) rho^(1/2)=0` for every original channel. Then (4) yields `E_S>=Theta_S`. Darkness at one instant alone is not stationarity; the formation parent's companion argument already distinguishes those statements.

## 5. Ground degeneracy, initial power and what is not monotone

Let rho_0 be any density supported in the full microscopic ground eigenspace at a sufficiently large S. Degeneracy, mixtures and coherences in that space are allowed. From (4), every such density has activity at least `b d_S>0`. Nevertheless activity and initial energy power are different observables. Direct differentiation gives

    dE_S/dt at 0
       = sum_mu Tr[(H_S-e_S I) L_(mu,S) rho_0 L_(mu,S)*]
       = sum_mu ||(H_S-e_S I)^(1/2)
                       L_(mu,S) rho_0^(1/2)||_HS^2 >= 0. (16)

It is strictly positive exactly when at least one actual jump output has weight outside the ground eigenspace. A positive activity lower bound alone does not ensure that property: an original mark could initially connect degenerate ground sectors with N differing by two. The no-event anticommutator cannot be discarded when examining later derivatives or the actual conditional state.

There is still strict energy increase for sufficiently small positive time for each particular finite-S ground density. The function `E_S(t)-e_S` is nonnegative and real analytic. If it vanished on any initial open time interval, analyticity would make it vanish for all time. Then the state would stay in the ground space, activity would remain at least b d_S, and (5) would be violated. Thus a first nonzero Taylor coefficient exists and is positive; the excess is strictly positive on some `0<t<t_0(S,rho_0)`. This gives no resource-uniform t_0, leading power or coefficient. It does not classify possible isolated later times with a zero excess.

Two exact abstract examples distinguish invalid stronger inferences. They are not the original torus, not a reconstruction of P37, and not numerical evidence for native coefficients. They obey the algebraic number balance and an affine inequality of exactly the same form, so they test only inferences from those ingredients.

Take three orthonormal states |0>,|1>,|2>, `N=diag(m,m+2,m+4)`, and two jumps `sqrt(a)|1><0|`, `sqrt(a)|2><1|`. Set `Delta=a/b`, and start in |0>. The populations, with x=at, are

    p0=exp(-x),   p1=x exp(-x),   p2=1-(1+x)exp(-x).

First choose `H=diag(0,0,Delta)`. Then `Gamma+bH=a I`, every ground density has activity a, and

    E(t)=Delta[1-(1+x)exp(-x)].                          (17)

The initial derivative is zero although the energy becomes positive quadratically. Ground degeneracy invalidates a proposed universal positive linear heating coefficient.

Instead choose `H=diag(0,2Delta,Delta)`. Now `Gamma+bH=diag(a,3a,a)>=aI`, but

    E(t)=Delta[1+(x-1)exp(-x)],
    E'(t)=a Delta(2-x)exp(-x).                          (18)

The energy rises from its nondegenerate ground and later decreases, approaching Delta from above. Thus number monotonicity and the imported affine bound do not imply monotone ordinary energy. Both examples keep `[H,N]=0` and +2 jumps. They can be assigned number values within the fixed capacity for m+4<=2n. No energy law or new mark is substituted into the actual native conclusion.

Coherences do not invalidate the positive bounds above. H_S and Gamma_S commute with N, and the generator is covariant under number phases. Expectations of H_S, N and Gamma_S are insensitive to dephasing between different N sectors. Within an N sector, coherent amplitudes remain in the original jump maps and in every quadratic form. No incoherent replacement of the stipulated coherent instrument is used. No classification of the finite-spin ground degeneracies, number sectors, normal rotor ground states or preparation accessibility is claimed.

## 6. An unexcited environment and explicit energy conservation

The following is a separate implementation test, with its own exact assumptions. It is not contained in the phenomenological GKLS equation.

If the spectral infimum is not attained, the phrase “exactly unexcited normal state” needs an additional definition; this argument does not silently supply one. Assume the implementation unitary U(t) strongly commutes with

    H_bare = H_S tensor I + I tensor H_E.                (19)

Equivalent conservation of its finite mean is sufficient for the expectation argument. A merely formal unbounded-operator commutator without domain control is not the premise. Start the system in a ground density. A product input suffices, but correlations confined to the joint ground subspace do not change the result. Initially the nonnegative excitation operator

    (H_S-e_S I) tensor I + I tensor(H_E-e_E I)

has expectation zero. Energy conservation and positivity keep that expectation zero. Each nonnegative marginal term therefore has zero expectation at every time, and the system remains supported in its ground space with `E_system(t)=e_S`.

Ground degeneracy permits nontrivial rotations, mixing and records within this subspace; it does not force the system density to stay fixed. This is why ground preservation, not uniqueness or immediate stationarity, is the correct first conclusion. The original full GKLS law cannot preserve that energy for all time, by (5) and the positive ground activity bound. More sharply, its analyticity argument in Section 5 excludes exact agreement on any initial interval of positive length for each sufficiently large finite S and ground input. This is a statement about an exact time-dependent realization. It does not classify equality of a single isolated endpoint channel.

For an environment with initial bare excitation energy R>=0, the same accounting instead gives `E_system(t)-e_S<=R` at all times, in the absence of work and other energy stores. If R<d_S, (9) limits an exact realization over a whole interval to

    T <= q_S/[b(d_S-R)].                                (20)

A realization for arbitrarily late times must supply at least d_S of initial bare energy above the joint bare ground for that input; asymptotically this necessary amount has liminf at least `4 delta n`. This is a conditional ordinary-energy requirement for the explicitly assumed implementation. It is not an optimal resource construction, a sufficiency claim, or a laboratory energy calibration. ## 7. Interaction energy, work and heat cannot be hidden in “total energy”

Conservation of an interacting Hamiltonian

    H_tot=H_S+H_E+V

does not by itself mean conservation of the bare sum (19). When the separate expectations exist, exact total-energy conservation gives

    Delta E_system = -Delta E_environment -Delta <V>.    (21)

With an initially bare-ground environment, its bare mean cannot decrease below e_E. A system-energy gain can then be paid by a decrease of interaction energy. Calling the environment unexcited does not remove that store or establish that the joint preparation is a ground state of H_tot. With time-dependent driving or switching, an additional work term enters the ledger. If individual terms lack defined finite expectations, the separate balance (21) itself requires form/domain justification.

An exact two-qubit example demonstrates the distinction without an appeal to an unspecified reservoir. It is only an abstract implementation counterexample, not the original marked law. Set

    H_S=omega |1><1|,       H_E=omega |1><1|,
    V=g(|11><00|+|00><11|),       omega,g>0.

Start in |00>, with both bare subsystems unexcited, and evolve with the time-independent H_tot. Put `Omega=sqrt(omega^2+g^2)`. Exact diagonalization of the {|00>,|11>} block gives

    p_11(t)=(g^2/Omega^2) sin^2(Omega t),
    E_system(t)=E_environment(t)=omega p_11(t),
    <V>_t=-2 omega p_11(t),       <H_tot>_t=0.            (22)

Total energy is conserved while both bare energies rise. The initial product is not the joint ground: the block's lower eigenvalue is `omega-Omega<0`. An additive constant shift changes no energy difference or conclusion. This rules out using bare environmental emptiness plus unspecified total-energy conservation as a substitute for (19).

The supplied GKLS law identifies ordinary system energy and the original record rates; it does not specify a bath, a work store, an interaction-energy convention or a detector Hamiltonian. Its system-energy gain alone is therefore not a derived heat, work or photon-absorption energy. No correspondence between its energetic minimum and the observed vacuum is established. Driven states, excited environments, interaction-energy preparation, constrained preparations, alternative physical identifications and laws with recycling are not excluded by the bounded result.

Finally, this is an exact energy-accounting obstruction under explicit implementation assumptions. It is not automatically a no-approximation theorem in trace norm as S grows: `||H_S||` grows, and small trace errors need not control energy means. An approximation must specify energy accuracy, the operational process norm and its resource/time order before a quantitative implementation conclusion is transferred. No such additional assumption is adopted here.
