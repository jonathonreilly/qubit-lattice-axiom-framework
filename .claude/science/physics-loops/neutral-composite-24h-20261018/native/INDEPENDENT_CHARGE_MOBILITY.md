## Exact charge rule

The three plane families commute on an all-even periodic torus: unlike Pauli matrices contribute one minus sign per site in each plane intersection, whose length is even. For a Pauli string, the draft's charges count precisely its anticommutations with each plane generator:

 cx(x)=number of Y/Z sites in that x plane mod 2;
 cy(y)=number of X/Z sites in that y plane mod 2;
 cz(z)=number of X/Y sites in that z plane mod 2.

If rho commutes with the symmetry group, conjugating <P(t)Q> by any generator multiplies it by the product of the corresponding two charge signs, while leaving the expectation unchanged. A mismatch therefore forces it to vanish. The same proof applies separately to both terms of the retarded commutator. Time evolution preserves charges because H commutes with every generator. Finite conjugation averages decompose general local operators into their character components without enlarging support. This does not require perturbation theory or a ground state.

For a fixed nonzero charge pattern on a cyclic axis of length L, invariance under a nonzero shift d would contain complete orbits of the subgroup generated by d. If g=gcd(d,L), a nontrivial orbit has equally spaced points with spacing g and shortest containing arc length L-g>=L/2. Thus no nonempty pattern contained in an arc of width strictly less than L/2 can be invariant under that shift. This proves the finite-torus restriction in the draft. Equality at L/2 is excluded for a reason: two opposite sites provide a counterexample there. In the infinite-line limit a nonempty finite set has no nonzero translation symmetry.

A single definite-charge template therefore has its own translated response confined to dx=0 for nonzero cx, and likewise for y,z. Z is charged under x and y planes and can have translated correlations only along z; X only along x; Y only along y. The transverse x-separated ZZ dipole has two x charges while its y charges cancel, and is allowed within a yz plane. These are kinematic allowances, not guarantees of transport or nonzero response.

For an operator containing several charge components, different components can match after a displacement. Their allowed sets can be offset planes or lines rather than the planes through zero; for a bounded template these offsets remain bounded by its support. A neutral component must be treated separately using the prior neutral-response theorem. The draft's finite-width qualification is appropriate. Arbitrary coherent superpositions of different ground characters are not necessarily invariant states and cannot silently be covered by this charge-selection argument.

## Local Z within the isolated band

On the coefficient ground manifold, column configurations are s_xy=a_x b_y and are independent of z. Multiplication by Z_xyz therefore acts as the character function a_x b_y on the regular permutation basis. Fourier transformation on the row/column flip quotient maps it to a toggle of the x-plane and y-plane character labels. Flipping two character bits preserves the single allowed-character product constraint. Its coefficient is exactly one; there is no missing phase or normalization factor. It commutes with the z-plane generators.

To compare with the exact band, identify the two bands by the near-identity spectral intertwiner and undo the symmetry-preserving normal-form unitary. The local dressing changes Z by O(lambda) in operator norm uniformly in volume at fixed order; the band-projector/intertwiner discrepancy contributes O(lambda^29) in the prescribed boxes. Thus the exact local-Z band compression has the stated leading toggle with O(lambda) error after this identification. This is an order-one band matrix element, not an order-one static susceptibility: unknown tiny energy denominators prevent dividing the error by a splitting or inferring a finite limiting susceptibility. The band may split or remain degenerate.

## Pair-column tunneling does not act directly within P+

For transverse dimensions greater than two, flipping only two columns does not have the form a_x b_y. From a plaquette-positive pattern an adjacent pair creates four bad transverse plaquettes; a diagonal pair creates six, and sufficiently separated columns create eight. The sign count is independent of the initial gauge pattern. Therefore

 P_+ T_pair P_+=0.

The familiar order-Lz amplitude for simultaneously flipping two neighboring columns connects the aligned-column manifold to plaquette-defective configurations. It is not a leading matrix element inside the actual plaquette-positive ground manifold. Small transverse tubes can be exceptional: on a width-two array a pair can comprise a full row, so that result must not be carried over to the large square torus.

A nontrivial row/column gauge-pattern flip changes at least min(Lx,Ly) entire columns. Since each native transverse operation flips two physical spins, a coefficient word realizing an off-diagonal transition between distinct gauge configurations requires at least Lz min(Lx,Ly)/2 operations. This is only a necessary finite-word count, not a formula for a splitting or a convergent high-order estimate. Sequences through plaquette defects require a separate treatment with their small energy denominators. At fixed m and L growing like lambda^-4, none of these wrapping transitions occur in B_m; its gauge ground manifold remains exactly degenerate.

The exact subsystem-symmetry restrictions are established prior structure, not a new universal force exclusion. Neutral composites, noninvariant states, changed templates, nonlocal dressing and other regimes are outside a single charged-template mobility statement. No all-force or thermodynamic no-go follows.
