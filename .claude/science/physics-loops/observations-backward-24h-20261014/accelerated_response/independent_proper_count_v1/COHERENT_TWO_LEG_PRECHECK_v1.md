# Independent ideal coherent two-leg extension precheck

Frozen by `/root/localized_history_check` after the one-way review and before
opening any new multi-leg author source. Root remains author. The earlier one-way
precheck/results/report are immutable and are not relabeled as two-leg controls.
Use c=1, a known constant phase multiplier k>0, one shared station clock, zero
turnaround proper delay and no independent spacecraft oscillator or link phase.
The propagation and supplied conformal metric assumptions are the one-way ones.

## Exact composition

Write r for station reception, m for the common spacecraft receive/re-emit event,
and e for station emission. The station path is x_G and spacecraft path x_S.
Distinct null equations are

    r-m=|x_G(r)-x_S(m)|,
    m-e=|x_S(m)-x_G(e)|.

Let n_d point from x_S(m) to x_G(r), and n_u from x_G(e) to x_S(m). Define

    D_dr=1-n_d.v_G(r), D_dm=1-n_d.v_S(m), d=D_dr/D_dm,
    D_um=1-n_u.v_S(m), D_ue=1-n_u.v_G(e), u=D_um/D_ue.

All denominators are positive under the supplied uniform subluminal margins,
and the two light-time solutions must exist uniquely with nonzero ranges.
Then dm/dr=d and de/dm=u, so de/dr=u d. A single station state and clock must be
evaluated at e and r; they are not independent station copies.

Ideal instantaneous phase coherence means Phi_out,S(m)=k Phi_in,S(m)+constant.
The incoming phase is Phi_G(e), and the constant turnaround phase cancels from
finite counts. Hence

    Phi_received(r)=k Phi_G(e)+constant,
    N=k nu_G [tau_G(e_b)-tau_G(e_a)]

at fixed receiving station proper-clock labels T_a,T_b. The instantaneous
received/source ratio is

    nu_received/(k nu_G)=K_2=f_G(e) u d/f_G(r).

One may equivalently multiply the two proper-frequency transfer factors:
[f_G(e) u/f_S(m)] [f_S(m) d/f_G(r)]. The spacecraft proper lapse cancels exactly
at the same turnaround event. Treating the spacecraft as a second constant
proper-frequency source is a different model and would lose this cancellation.

## First variations and endpoint rows

As in the one-way precheck, xi_A is fixed-coordinate path variation and chi_A is
fixed-coordinate proper-clock variation. At each fixed receiver proper endpoint,

    delta r=-chi_G(r)/f_G(r),
    g_d=n_d.(xi_S(m)-xi_G(r))/D_dm,
    delta m=d delta r+g_d,
    g_u=n_u.(xi_G(e)-xi_S(m))/D_ue,
    delta e=u delta m+g_u.

Therefore

    delta N=k nu_G Delta S_2,
    S_2=chi_G(e)-K_2 chi_G(r)+f_G(e) [u g_d+g_u].

Its endpoint coefficients on the combined station/spacecraft state are

    station at e: position +f_G(e) n_u/D_ue, clock +1;
    spacecraft at m: position f_G(e)[u n_d/D_dm-n_u/D_ue], clock 0;
    station at r: position -f_G(e) u n_d/D_dm, clock -K_2.

The spacecraft position terms from both legs add at the same event. There is no
direct velocity atom. Count endpoints give the difference of these phase rows,
with division by the fixed proper span if normalized. A perturbation of k, a
nonconstant turnaround phase, proper delay or independent oscillator would add
terms outside this model.

## Direct clock cancellation and surviving force

The spacecraft clock is absent from the exact coherent phase mapping, not merely
small to first order. Its direct proper-lapse perturbation thus vanishes even
when f_S varies. Spacecraft trajectory changes still affect both null legs and
usually survive with nonzero coefficient. For a stationary station at x=0,
stationary reference spacecraft x=L>0 and unperturbed station clock,

    delta e=-2 xi_S(r-L),
    delta N=-2 k nu_G A_b [xi_S(r_b-L)-xi_S(r_a-L)].

A time-varying displacement, such as one generated by acceleration, changes the
count; a constant range displacement only changes the phase offset in this
static reference. Clock cancellation is not force cancellation.

For the supplied augmented orbit/clock system in which clocks are passive
integrals and do not feed the external orbital dynamics, the absence of every
spacecraft clock endpoint/source row implies lambda_clock,S=0. Hence its direct
field-clock weight c_S=alpha f_S lambda_clock,S vanishes, while its force weight
W_S=alpha lambda_velocity,S need not vanish. If an external controller or thrust
schedule depends on the spacecraft proper clock, an indirect dynamical clock
response can reappear and must be modeled. Endpoint cancellation alone would
not prove lambda_clock,S=0 in that more general coupled system.

With bounded piecewise continuous linear coefficients, position/clock atoms and
L2 distributed readouts of the allowed form, lambda_velocity has no atom jumps.
It remains continuous piecewise H1 on both bodies. The full initial-state
contrast plus terminal zero supplies zero outer traces. Thus the same force-
weight extension regularity follows for finite coherent counts. The spacecraft
clock component is zero only under the passive-clock premise above. An arbitrary
velocity readout or proper turnaround delay changes this regularity/row problem.

## Shared-clock and constant-background cautions

An additive station clock shift appears at both station events. Its finite count
response is -k nu_G kappa_G (K_2,b-K_2,a), rather than two independent clock
nuisance columns. Constant K_2 makes it vanish; changing geometry need not. The
spacecraft clock anchor does not enter this ideal mapping. Initial position and
velocity variations remain until an actual initial-state contrast removes them.

Static paths with any time-independent spatial conformal profile have constant
station lapse at both emission and reception. The coherent count is k nu_G
Delta T, even if the spacecraft lapse differs. In contrast, a homogeneous
A=A_b exp(epsilon b t), with static range L, gives

    N_epsilon=k nu_G exp(-2 epsilon b L) Delta T.

The direct shared-station clock response is then nonzero. Constant-background
cancellation must still be checked for the declared fixed-coordinate path family;
for varying Doppler it need not follow solely from coherence. A derived constant
response column remains the appropriate conditional test, not a chosen absolute
background or a presumed universal cancellation.

   N_epsilon=k nu_G exp(-2 epsilon b L) Delta T.
