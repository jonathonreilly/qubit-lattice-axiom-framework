# Receiver calibration supplier: focused primary-source check

Reviewer: `/root/localized_history_check`. Scope: documentation and metadata definitions, with elementary mathematical consequences. Root retains scientific authorship. No author proof/code, raw I/Q samples, new empirical sample, spectrum, fit, or track-specific calibration value was examined or computed. No formal audit or publication verdict is supplied.

## Answer and exact boundary

The checked 2001 RSR specification and day-169 label do **not supply a measured covariance shape for the proposed validation error, an isotropic/white proper Gaussian or spherically symmetric law for that error, or independence of its first and second 500 samples**. They describe receiver processing, settings, and a few measured monitoring quantities. FIR filtering plus equality of nominal sample rate and bandwidth does not mathematically imply any of those statistical properties.

This finding is limited to the exact sources and read intervals below. It is not a claim that suitable noise/calibration observations are absent from the full Cassini archive. In particular, the 2018 user guide explicitly identifies possible receiver noise-temperature/SNR monitor products and a procedure for estimating free-space SNR from I/Q. Those are useful supplier leads; neither a matching product nor a measured value for this 2002 interval was obtained in this check. A conditional analysis can posit a stated noise law; that law has not thereby been derived from these receiver documents.

## Documented facts

1. **Filtering and gain settings.** DSN 820-013, 0159-Science, 28 February 2001, §2.3, printed pp.2-2–2-6 (PDF 12–16), describes analog filtering and digitization, DDC FIR coefficient banks, separate I/Q filter sets, NCO mixing, then a decimating FIR and truncation. The final subchannel bandwidth/sample rate are selectable. Its gain is set at the beginning of a pass using bandwidth and an expected signal/noise ratio. Neither a covariance matrix nor the actual FIR coefficient array is specified in these read sections. Time-delay compensation in §2.5, printed p.2-8 (PDF 18), aligns samples and tuning polynomials; alignment is not a statement of statistical independence. [Primary 2001 specification](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/radiosciencedocs/urn-nasa-pds-radiosci_documentation/DSN_0159-SCIENCE/dsn_0159-science.2001-02-28.pdf).

2. **Expected FGAIN versus measurements.** Printed p.3-13 (PDF 35), byte 22, identifies FGAIN Px/No in dB-Hz as an expected ratio used to compute filter-gain settings. Byte 23 continues onto p.3-14 and is an expected IF bandwidth used for the same purpose. The registered day 169 X label confirms this: column 33, lines 504–522, describes an expected signal-power/one-Hz-noise-power ratio and says the gain is chosen to avoid clipping; column 34, lines 524–537, describes the expected IF bandwidth. A setting intended to avoid clipping is not an observed absence of clipping. Column 44, lines 661–674, gives operator-selected sample rate/bandwidth in kilosamples/s; it does not define a noise PSD or effective noise bandwidth. [Registered X label](https://atmos.nmsu.edu/pdsd/archive/data/co-ss-rss-1-sce1-v10/cors_0024/sce1_169/rsr/c32easc2002_169_1335x54x25d.lbl).

3. **There are measured ADC monitor fields.** The same 2001 specification, printed p.3-14 (PDF 36), bytes 26–35, identifies RMS and peak amplitude of the DIG ADC stream and their measurement time. Label columns 37/38, lines 570–594, specify about 10,000 8-bit ADC samples; columns 39–41 give their time. Column 36 is digitizer attenuation in half-dB steps. These are genuine measurement definitions, not merely expected values. They concern the upstream broadband ADC, however, and do not by themselves separate spacecraft carrier power from noise, supply the subchannel noise PSD/covariance, identify an I/Q pseudo-covariance, or determine the covariance of a downstream frequency/phase error. No numeric values of these fields were read here. Column 43 counts hardware errors; a zero count would not certify a Gaussian or independent noise process.

4. **Quantization convention.** Printed p.2-6 (PDF 16) permits 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16 bits per component and describes truncation. Printed p.3-20 (PDF 42), and Cassini SIS §1.5.2.1 lines 423–444, specify signed-code correction `2*k+1`. The resulting code alphabet is symmetric around zero and excludes zero. This is a statement about the representation; it is not a measured symmetry of probabilities and is much weaker than rotational symmetry of a whole error vector. No independent uniform quantization-error model is given in these sections. The SIS §1.3 reference[8], lines 305–306, explicitly cites the 28-February-2001 specification. [Cassini SIS](https://atmos.nmsu.edu/pdsd/archive/data/co-ss-rss-1-sce1-v10/cors_0024/document/casrssis.txt).

5. **Positive calibration leads in the guide.** Cassini Radio Science User's Guide (30 September 2018), §2.5.2.4 p.18, says some data sets include edited DSN monitor streams; potential parameters include receiver system noise temperature and carrier signal/noise ratio. The accompanying labels define actual content. Section 3.3.4.2 p.49 describes free-space power calibration from appropriate unobscured periods and identifies antenna pointing/elevation, atmosphere, receiver gain and thermal fluctuations as possible contributions to amplitude variation. Section 3.3.4.6 pp.53–54 describes additive thermal noise, coherent averaging and estimation of free-space one-second/one-Hz SNR from recorded I/Q; it also supplies typical station-class values as alternatives. None of these paragraphs supplies a measured day 169 validation covariance or validates substitution of a typical value for the present channel/epoch. These are later ring/occultation analysis examples, not a direct measurement of this track. [Cassini guide](https://pds.nasa.gov/data/pds4/misc/document_cassini/Cassini_Radio_Science_Users_Guide_30Sep2018.pdf), DOI 10.17189/1522471.

6. **The guide does discuss independence—within a specific processing example.** Section 3.2.1.1.1, pp.33–35, discusses a 2009 Titan occultation. Page 34 describes an averaging uncertainty reduction, and a sliding 512-point FFT reusing 448 points; it calls every eighth resulting output independent. Page 35 explicitly identifies correlations caused by sliding windows and describes independent estimates at 0.256 s versus displayed estimates at 0.032 s. This positive wording must not be suppressed. It is an operational statement for that analysis example; the section supplies no measured 2002 transfer coefficients, boundary covariance, conditional validation-error law or certificate for two contiguous 500-sample blocks in the present procedure. It is also a warning that densely displayed estimates are not automatically independent. The terms independent receiver/feed in §2.3.2 p.12 describe hardware/operation, not a joint probability law.

7. **Later JPL RSR processing is not a statistical certificate.** Border and Paik, JPL IPN 42-177 article 177C (15 May 2009), §V pp.9–10, writes a complex tone plus additive noise model, describes Fourier frequency estimation and local phase fitting, and uses a sample rate equal to the 2 kHz bandwidth for its experiments. It does not specify Gaussianity, properness, whiteness, a covariance matrix or independence of arbitrary blocks in that passage. The experiments were in 2007/2008 (§IV p.8). Section VI p.11 reports slowly changing differences of order 20 cm between two range methods and expressly cannot establish which is more accurate because position/plasma uncertainty remains. Section III pp.3–4 discusses expected performance, not a measured error distribution for 2002. [JPL177C](https://ipnpr.jpl.nasa.gov/progress_report/42-177/177C.pdf).

## Mathematical inferences, not additional receiver facts

For a zero-mean discrete input `x_j` and a fixed FIR/decimation stage,

`y_n = sum_k h_k x_(nD-k)`.

If one separately assumes white input with `E[x_j conjugate(x_l)] = sigma^2 delta_(jl)`, then

`C_nm = sigma^2 sum_k h_k conjugate(h_(k+(m-n)D))`.

Thus output whiteness requires the relevant shifted impulse-response inner products to vanish. The length of the retained sample vector, a nominal bandwidth, or a label saying sample rate equals bandwidth does not impose those equations. Equivalently, stationary output correlations are Fourier coefficients of its alias-adjusted power spectrum; a flat appropriate spectrum gives zero nonzero-lag covariance, whereas a bandwidth label alone does not give that spectrum. Actual coefficient banks, their decimation schedule and upstream statistics matter. The displayed formula is an explanatory single-stage model, not a claim to have reconstructed the RSR filter.

An exact elementary counterexample suffices: let independent, unit-variance, proper complex Gaussian variables be `xi_j`, and set

`y_n = (xi_n + xi_(n-1))/sqrt(2)`.

Every output has the same variance and the joint output is proper Gaussian, but `E[y_n conjugate(y_(n+1))] = 1/2`. Blocks `y_0,...,y_499` and `y_500,...,y_999` have a nonzero cross-covariance between `y_499` and `y_500` because both involve `xi_499`. They are dependent. This is a logical FIR counterexample, not an empirical RSR assertion; no fit or simulated observational evidence is needed. Giving a nominal rate/bandwidth does not fix the FIR overlap inner product and therefore cannot rule it out. The rate/bandwidth issue can also be made literal: a stationary proper complex Gaussian process with PSD `(1+cos(2*pi*f/f_s))/f_s` on `[-f_s/2,f_s/2]` has spectral-support bandwidth `f_s`; sampling at `f_s` gives variance 1 and adjacent covariance 1/2. Its spectrum is not flat despite matching support width and sample rate. Here bandwidth means full two-sided support of a complex baseband signal, not an asserted RSR filter convention.

Four distinct properties must be kept separate:

- White covariance is a second-moment property. Proper centered complex noise additionally requires vanishing pseudo-covariance `E[e e^T]`. Equal I/Q variances alone establish neither full temporal whiteness nor all pseudo-covariances.
- Identity covariance does not imply spherical direction: independent symmetric signs in real dimension at least 2 have identity covariance but a finite set of directions, not a rotation-invariant direction law. Similarly proper complex noise need not be circular/unitarily invariant.
- Joint Gaussianity with suitable isotropic covariance gives a spherical real representation (or proper complex isotropy). Independence of blocks then follows from vanishing cross-covariance, with the necessary pseudo-covariance conditions in a complex formulation. Without Gaussianity or another specified joint law, uncorrelated blocks need not be independent.
- For fixed true signal, fixed ancillary inputs and finite stored word length, a deterministic estimator applied to a finite quantized record takes only finitely many possible values. Its error therefore cannot have an exactly continuous nondegenerate Gaussian distribution under that conditioning. A Gaussian approximation can be useful, but requires stated approximation conditions. Subtracting a truncation offset does not create independent additive uniform noise or a continuous Gaussian law. Clipping, signal-dependent quantization and nonlinear phase/frequency extraction may change the error law further.

Even exact raw-I/Q Gaussianity would not automatically make a fitted phase/frequency validation-error vector spherical. The estimator, fitted mean/nuisance parameters, shared references, windows, weighting, outlier rules and any calibration estimation all enter its distribution. A normalization by one scalar C/N0 supplies scale, not a full shape or a proof of independence. A known correct whitening transform can remove second-moment anisotropy; it cannot alone supply a spherical law for a non-Gaussian vector. Estimating the transform on the same validation samples adds another dependency.

## What an actual supplier would need to add

These are proposed physical/statistical premises and calibration tasks, not claims that they have been completed:

1. Pin the actual 2002 receiver/configuration and effective transfer response: FIR coefficients/phase/decimation, gain/attenuation and quantizer transfer, bit depth/full scale, clipping behavior, and relevant clocks/electronics. A nominal bandwidth needs an effective noise bandwidth/transfer calibration before use as an integrated noise scale.
2. Identify matching monitor products or independent calibration intervals/injected signals that distinguish carrier, broadband noise and interference, with calibrated gain/system temperature/C/N0 as needed and their uncertainties. Upstream ADC RMS can constrain total loading; it is not a substitute for that separation.
3. Determine or justify the lag covariance, I/Q cross- and pseudo-covariance, cross-channel/common-reference terms, drift and stationarity on the relevant interval. For independence of blocks, account for filter memory and any shared fitting/calibration variables. Disjoint underlying independent input sets plus a sufficiently large known guard interval is one possible route; mere disjoint output indices is not.
4. Propagate those inputs through the **actual validation-error construction**, using separately frozen calibration/development/test roles. If an exact or approximate spherical direction law is needed, state its model and conditioning and assess its approximation error, rather than treating covariance alone as proof. Finite calibration data can test consistency and bound selected departures under stated models; they do not prove exact Gaussianity or independence without assumptions.

No supplier has been selected or a measured physical noise/error budget manufactured here. Conversely, this check does not rule out obtaining one from appropriate archived monitor/calibration products and a supported receiver model.
