# Focused RSR phase and timestamp documentation check, v1

The documentation supplies a time-tagged NCO phase model as well as a sky-frequency formula. It does **not by itself establish the complete signed sky phase from `I+jQ` at every raw sample**. A specific conflict must be preserved: the day-169 label evaluates its phase polynomial at the millisecond midpoint, whereas the original 2001 specification evaluates NCO phase at the millisecond start and only NCO frequency at the midpoint. This review resolves neither version against the actual receiver implementation.

Scope: documentation only. No raw samples, binary headers, other empirical products, author proof/code, or observed signal were opened. The earlier instrument-premise report remains frozen and separate. This is a focused source check, not a formal audit or a claim that phase reconstruction is impossible.

## What the sources positively specify

- The registered day-169 label, columns 45–46 (lines 675–737), gives `Fsky = RFtoIF_LO + DDC_LO − NCO_Freq + Fresid`. The LO fields are in MHz; the frequency polynomial and residual are in Hz. Columns 61–63 (lines 924–979) give the quadratic frequency polynomial at `x=(msec+0.5)/1000`.
- Column 64 (lines 981–995) supplies accumulated whole turns at the beginning of the second. Columns 65–68 (lines 997–1075) supply a cubic sub-channel phase polynomial in cycles, to be added to that accumulation. The label explicitly uses `x=(msec+0.5)/1000` in those phase descriptions as well. This is a model of sub-channel phase, not a direct measurement of sky phase.
- The exact original [0159-Science specification dated 28 February 2001](https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/radiosciencedocs/urn-nasa-pds-radiosci_documentation/DSN_0159-SCIENCE/dsn_0159-science.2001-02-28.pdf), §2.4, printed pp. 2-7–2-8 / PDF pp. 17–18, says the phase polynomial is obtained by integration of the frequency polynomial and its endpoint initializes the next second, making the modeled phase continuous across seconds. It describes phase and frequency register updates at each millisecond. Its phase equation uses `msec/1000`; its frequency equation uses `(msec+0.5)/1000`. Both pages were visually checked, not accepted from extraction alone. The corresponding phase/frequency/accumulation fields are described at printed p. 3-17 / PDF p. 39.
- The same specification §2.5, printed p. 2-8 / PDF p. 18, explicitly assigns the SFDU year/day/seconds tag to its first sample at the DIG ADC, and assigns later sample times by adding the sample period. It describes pipeline-delay compensation in the DDC, VDP and real-time buffer handling. Thus it would be wrong to state that these documents provide no sample-time mapping. The one-second polynomial interval and sample-time tag must both be handled. Its stated 100 ns timing accuracy is a design-level statement, not a deterministic verified error certificate for these observations.
- The label column 72 (lines 1116–1156), the Cassini SIS §1.5.2.1 (lines 418–444), and original specification printed p. 3-20 / PDF p. 42 establish Q in the high 16 bits, I in the low 16 bits, packed-sample order from lower to higher bits, and signed two's-complement unpacking with truncation correction `2*k+1`. The unsigned word container does not make the individual sample values unsigned.

## What remains a conditional phase bridge

**Complex sign.** In the complete applicable signal-path and analysis sections (§§2.3–2.6 of the 2001 specification), I and Q are named and spectral analysis is required to obtain the residual frequency. Those sections do not explicitly define `z=I+iQ`, give the sine/cosine mixer convention, or state that `d arg(z)/(2π dt)=+Fresid` rather than its negative. The packed-word order and the sky-frequency equation do not independently select the sign of that mathematical phase convention. This is a source-bounded gap, not an assertion that no other instrument document or calibrated sign test can select it.

**NCO timing.** The original specification describes a phase loaded at each millisecond boundary and a frequency computed at the midpoint. The label instead places the phase evaluation at the midpoint. They must not be silently conflated. For a nonconstant frequency, moving the phase argument by 0.5 ms can introduce a time-dependent phase change; it need not be just an additive phase origin. A hypothetical ideal register implementation, with exact loads and frequency held within each millisecond, would have a piecewise phase `P(mΔ)+F((m+1/2)Δ)*(t−mΔ)`, plus accumulated turns, where `Δ=0.001 s`. That is an explicit implementation inference, not a statement that the cubic `P(t)` is the exact oscillator phase at all intermediate sample times. Register quantization and the realized convention were not checked here.

**Sky phase.** If phase derivatives share the stated signed-frequency convention and common time coordinate, integrating the documented frequency relation yields the conditional increment identity

`ΔΦsky = ∫(RFtoIF_LO + DDC_LO) dt − ΔΦNCO + ΔΦresid`,

with phases in cycles and frequencies converted to Hz. This is an inference from the frequency identity. Identifying `ΔΦresid` with an unwrapped argument of the stored I/Q series still requires the sign, cycle continuity, sample timing and estimator conventions. The NCO accumulation and polynomial alone are not `Φsky`. LO-frequency metadata also do not furnish all physical LO phase origins, phase fluctuations, or electronics transfer phases. Constant origins can drop out of suitable increments; that does not establish their absence from arbitrary cross-channel phase combinations.

The supported next step is therefore to carry the documented NCO model, the original start-phase/midpoint-frequency convention, and the conflicting label convention explicitly through any exposed-development test. A selected sign or convention from such a test is a calibrated premise until independently checked. No assumption about an exact X/Ka uplink ratio, spacecraft scalar response, or gravity observable follows from this metadata review.
