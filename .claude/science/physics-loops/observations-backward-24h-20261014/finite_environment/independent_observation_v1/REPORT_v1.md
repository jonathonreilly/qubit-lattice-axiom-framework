## Calibrated inputs that remain

For an areal-radius static metric `-exp(2n)dt^2+exp(2l)dr^2+r^2 dOmega^2`, physical Ricci curvature depends on `n''`, `n'`, `l'`, `l` and the calibrated radius. Clocks constrain lapse ratios; radial ranging gives optical path integrals; lensing constrains optical combinations and source/observer geometry. Combining ideal complete data can identify more than any probe alone. Finite noisy samples need a specified inverse model or derivative-error enclosure before they become a curvature bound. Small metric-amplitude errors do not bound second derivatives. These are requirements for the proposed inference, not a theorem that curvature can never be measured.

The candidate application still needs:

2. Data coverage for the actual interior/exterior domain and uncertainty bounds on the required metric derivatives; non-geodesic motion, source positions, plasma/medium effects and timing/shape responses where relevant.
3. Scalar boundary or ambient matching information and regularity, including any surfaces, horizons or noncompact limits. Scalar flux/charge or force is not supplied by an amplitude bound alone.
4. The candidate action and parameters, including H_v in matching physical units. The bare Jordan curvature coefficient is not automatically the measured Newton constant.
5. For a realized body, consistency with the metric and matter equations, supporting stresses/pressure and appropriate initial/environmental evolution. The scalar equation on an imposed metric is only a necessary part of that problem.
