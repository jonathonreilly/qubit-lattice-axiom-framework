## Exact results and qualifications

1. For N>=1 and a known unit-modulus sampled template, completing the Hermitian square gives `||z-cu||²=E+N|c-u*z/N|²`. Hence a supplied nonnegative deterministic B yields the exact frequency-feasibility condition E<=B² and a disk of feasible complex amplitudes at each frequency. The statement permits c=0. If a nonzero carrier is separately required, the boundary B²=||z||² and u*z=0 has only c=0 and must be treated accordingly. N=1, coincident times and exact aliases can prevent unique frequency identification even at zero discrepancy. Unknown frequency without a search domain is not silently restricted to an FFT band.

2. At the true frequency, E is the squared norm of the component of error perpendicular to the template. The unobserved parallel component is absorbed into fitted amplitude. Thus residual is not a deterministic upper noise bound. The exact witness z=(1,1), times (0,1), has a zero-residual DC fit yet can come from the nonzero f=1/2 carrier (1,-1) with additive error (0,2). A fitted B=0 would falsely exclude that possible truth. With no supplied restriction on error, every frequency and chosen complex amplitude is explainable. Finite voltage does not bound noise when a large carrier may cancel a large error.

3. Across all arbitrary-phase constant-modulus vectors, the exact center-distance minimum is S-(sum r)²/N. Exact upper radius integers C_n=ceil(sqrt(x_n²+y_n²)) give the valid weaker lower bound max(0,S-C²/N). For closed unit-halfwidth complex cells, center error squared is at most 2N, so NS-C²>2N² is a valid exclusion. The inequality must be strict. Floors in place of ceilings are invalid: identical centers 3+5i have a perfect constant waveform but floor radii falsely trigger the exclusion. The boundary centers 1+i and 3+3i admit the shared corner 2+2i with residual squared exactly 2N.

4. Closed-cell radius bounds have exact squared endpoints `max(|x|-1,0)²+max(|y|-1,0)²` and `(|x|+1)²+(|y|+1)²`. Their common-amplitude overlap condition is necessary and sufficient for arbitrary independently chosen phases. It is only necessary for a tone: the phase progression remains another constraint. At fixed f, exact noiseless tone feasibility is intersection of the rotated cells `conj(u_f,n) Q_n` in the complex amplitude plane. With a supplied discrepancy ball B, it is equivalently `min_c sum dist(cu_f,n,Q_n)²<=B²`. This does not select an unmeasured subcell realization as true.

5. The exact distance from the product closed cells to the arbitrary-phase common-modulus class is the one-dimensional convex minimum `min_a>=0 sum dist(a,[ell_n,h_n])²`. For centers 1+i and 9+9i this minimum is 36, giving a necessary discrepancy norm of at least 6 in the assumed code units. This is a lower discrepancy requirement; it supplies no upper physical noise bound. If real cells are half-open, successful closed-cell feasibility may occur only at a boundary that the real convention excludes, while strict closed-cell exclusions remain safe.

6. A shifted time origin requires defining the rephased correlation `A_tau(f)=exp(2pi i f tau) A(f)`. Then the complex Lipschitz bound uses `L_tau=2pi sum |t_n-tau||z_n|`. The same L_tau always bounds magnitude differences of the original A. It cannot bound differences of the uncentered complex A without that rephasing: one sample at time 1 is a direct counterexample when tau=1. A weighted median minimizes this L1 time-origin bound; an ordinary mean is valid but not generally minimal.

7. A grid with known covering radius delta on an explicitly specified domain K gives `sup_K |A|<=min(sum r, max_grid |A|+L_tau delta)`. A finite grid is not a finite-radius cover of the whole real axis. Its values and upper constants must be exact or outward-enclosed to obtain a certificate. Floating FFT output is not itself an interval certificate. The synthetic pair z=(1,-1), times (0,1), has zero amplitude on grid {0,1} and amplitude 2 at the missed frequency 1/2; the Lipschitz enclosure correctly retains it.
