# Independent finite-pair measurement scope

A smooth weighted two-distance light-cone covariance functional is a possible observational target. Finite pair sampling can estimate such a functional in expectation under explicit sampling, response and calibration hypotheses. Neither smooth weighting alone nor a mean selection-function correction establishes those hypotheses. This is an independent supplier assessment, frozen before reading any new parent `unequal_window` theory or code; it does not validate that forthcoming construction.

The important distinction is between a correlation of the underlying fields and a correlation conditional on galaxies being selected at the two positions. An inverse **pair** density weight cancels the sampling measure only when the selected-pair mark moment equals the desired field correlation, or when a justified correction supplies that equality. Even knowing the unconditional pair density does not generally remove selection that correlates with the signal. Magnification, source clustering, tracer bias, and selection through noisy distance/shape quantities must therefore be included in the observation model. Random catalogues can encode an assumed angular/radial selection function; they do not by themselves measure every field-selection correlation.

The independent derivation in `PAIR_FUNCTIONAL_DERIVATION_v1.md` states two legitimate routes: a random catalogue design with calibrated joint pair intensity and admissible mark moments, and a fixed finite population with known joint inclusion probabilities. The latter is unbiased for that finite population's sum, not automatically a continuum integral. A nonrandom fixed grid supports a discrete quadrature functional; equality to a prescribed continuum functional needs a controlled quadrature error, an admitted finite signal space, or exact window representation. A narrow smooth bump invisible at finitely many fixed sample points shows the need for such a condition, without ruling out controlled quadrature or random sampling.

The primary sources give the following bounded support:

- [Schneider et al., astro-ph/0206182v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0206182v1), §§2–4 and the opening of §5, PDF pp. 3–6, Eqs. 1–26: finite weighted ellipticity-pair estimators, shape-noise assumptions, and covariance terms involving shared objects and shear four-point functions. Their mean calculation conditions on positions/weights and random intrinsic orientations; their displayed covariance closure adds Gaussian shear. For a finite bin I retain the exact weighted window rather than identify it with the bin centre. Gaussianity is not needed for the mean identity below.
- [Schmidt et al., 0904.4703v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0904.4703v3), §§I–II and Appendix A, PDF pp. 1–3 and 7–9, Eqs. 1–10 and A1–A18: galaxy-pair normalization stays inside the expectation, and observed density contains intrinsic clustering plus magnification/size selection. The resulting selected shear correlation contains mixed higher moments. Their reduced-shear and lensing-bias expansion illustrates a correctable, sample-dependent effect, not an impossibility theorem or a correction value admitted here.
- [Wang et al., 2108.08036v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08036v1), §§1–3 and the opening of §4, PDF pp. 1–5: radial-velocity pair geometry, finite-bin estimators and distance-dependent weights. The paper explicitly trades increased measurement error against effective-volume/cosmic-variance effects. Its CF3 application uses a chosen Hubble normalization and redshift-based positions. Those choices are not a supplied two-distance covariance for this scalar theory; no fitted parameter, mock result or catalogue was imported.
- [Davis and Scrimgeour, 1405.0105v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0105v2), §§1–6, PDF pp. 1–5, especially Eqs. 3–9, 12 and 16: peculiar velocities require a calibrated distance/redshift map and reference frame. The cosmological and peculiar redshift factors multiply. The discussion expressly notes differing emission times on the past light cone and selection/zero-point complications. I import neither their illustrative cosmology nor numerical survey corrections.

The unchanged Taylor2001v1, Heavens2003v2 and Wang2018v1 readings from M15 retain their exact original scope: Born/shear conventions and angular nulls; source-distance/mask/noise mixing; and radial pair geometry/calibration. Their hashes and original reading scopes are reused, not expanded by citation alone. Rotated-text extraction warnings are preserved; six relevant pages were checked visually.

For an actual estimate, shape responses, intrinsic alignments, velocity zero points, cross-channel/systematic noise, mean subtraction, pair overlap, distance uncertainty, redshift-space displacement and any approximation to physical velocity must be calibrated or bounded. Dropping self-pairs removes only the corresponding diagonal noise term under its stated assumptions. A pairwise variance is not automatically proportional to the inverse number of pairs: shared objects and cosmic fields correlate them. A supplied probability/error model remains necessary for a finite-catalogue rejection threshold.

A smooth unequal-time target can keep both emission times and avoid inferring a pointwise equal-time diagonal or a common growth law. It still needs its desired kernel to match the calibrated observation windows, or a stated discrepancy bound. The inspected papers do not supply the particular joint pair-inclusion law, mark-bias correction, redshift response and joint uncertainty model needed for an arbitrary new kernel. This is a statement about the inspected sources and proposed admission, not all present or possible observations.
