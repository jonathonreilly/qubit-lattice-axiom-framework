# Cross-environment action compatibility from a calibrated force port

Exploratory derivation by /root, 2026-10-16. Conditional classical mathematics,
not a derived physical action, quantum mechanism or gravity identification.
The aim is a necessary relation between three independently extracted observable
features, without choosing the hidden mass, coupling function or ringdown state.
No measured candidate dataset has been opened for this block.

## Observation question and prior art

Suppose a calibrated coordinate can be held at several environments x. Can its
small imposed-displacement force response and its nonlinear force ringdown be
generated by one reciprocal quadratic hidden-mode action across those x values?
The preceding sampled inverse supplied common hidden dynamics. Here the hidden
inertia, stiffness, kinetic cross coupling and damping can all vary with x.
Frequency changes alone do not determine an interaction force: the necessary
force calibration and mode normalization cannot be supplied by an analogy.

The connection between oscillator frequency changes and generalized forces is
established prior art. Aspelmeyer, Kippenberg and Marquardt, arXiv:1303.0733v1,
Sec. III.A-B, explicitly relate radiation force to cavity frequency pull within
an adopted Hamiltonian (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.0733). Perkins and Bellan,
Phys. Plasmas 22, 022108 (2015), discuss classical Hellmann-Feynman orbit averages
(https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/jgh6r-1as74). The latter abstract
was inspected; no uninspected proof from it is imported. Neither citation
validates this candidate or establishes novelty of the identity below. The
campaign contribution being investigated is an operational compatibility test
with hidden normalization eliminated; a general novelty claim is not made.

## Declared candidate and force convention

Let x be the calibrated imposed coordinate and w one hidden real coordinate.
On an open x interval assume sufficiently smooth real functions in

    T = M(x) xdot²/2 + k(x) xdot wdot + tau(x) wdot²/2,
    V = U(x) + B(x) w + C(x) w²/2,
    D = d(x) xdot²/2 + nu(x) xdot wdot + gamma(x) wdot²/2.

D is a Rayleigh dissipation function. The equations are Euler-Lagrange for
T-V plus partial derivatives of D with respect to velocities. The external
force R is the force *required to impose x*, conjugate to x in work R dx.
There is no external force on w during the ringdown or linear-response probe.
Assume tau>0, C>0, M tau-k²>0, gamma>0 and d gamma-nu²>=0.
Set Gamma=gamma/tau, O=C/tau. Restrict to O>Gamma²/4 and define

    lambda = -Gamma/2 + i sqrt(O-Gamma²/4), omega=Im(lambda)>0.

The kinetic and Rayleigh reciprocity and the quadratic dependence on w are
candidate premises. Functions of w in the kinetic metric or dissipation,
multiple hidden modes, stochastic forcing, nonlocal kernels and nonlinear
hidden restoring forces are outside this exact statement. No claim that this
class is fundamental or uniquely selected by the observations is made.

## Exact clamped force

At fixed x the hidden equilibrium is w0=-B/C. With z=w-w0,

    tau zddot + gamma zdot + C z = 0,
    z(t) = c exp(lambda t) + c* exp(lambda* t).

Every real finite initial position/velocity is represented by a complex c.
Neither c nor a distribution over it is chosen. A nonzero excitation is needed.
Primes below mean x derivatives at the fixed clamp. Direct variation gives

    R = R0 + G z + k zddot + nu zdot + C' z²/2 - tau' zdot²/2,     (1)
    R0 = U' + B' w0 + C' w0²/2,       G = B' + C' w0.

The negative inertia-gradient term in (1) is essential. At xdot=0,
d/dt(partial T/partial xdot)=k wddot whereas partial T/partial x
contains tau' wdot²/2. Derivatives k' and M' multiply vanished xdot
terms. The reciprocal damping cross term nu zdot remains at a clamp.

Define the force coefficients by the unique exponential expansion

    R(t)=R0 + f exp(lambda t)+f* exp(lambda* t)
         + h exp(-Gamma t)
         + g exp(2 lambda t)+g* exp(2 lambda* t).                 (2)

All six real basis functions are independent on any open time interval when
Gamma>0 and omega>0. With N(s)=G+nu s+k s²,

    f = N(lambda)c,
    h = (C'-tau'|lambda|²)|c|² = tau O' |c|²,
    g = (C'-tau'lambda²)c²/2.                                   (3)

If real cosine/sine amplitudes are a_cos,a_sin, the convention is
f=(a_cos-i a_sin)/2. Omitting this factor creates a factor-four error.
As Gamma tends to zero, h merges with the static offset and becomes badly
conditioned. Gamma=0 is excluded, not resolved by fitting a free constant.

## Independent small-signal response at rest

At the same x and w0, impose an infinitesimal displacement xi. For the hidden
perturbation eta, linearization of both reciprocal equations gives

    tau(s²+Gamma s+O) eta = -N(s) xi,
    delta R = P(s) xi + N(s) eta,
    P(s)=M s²+d s+V_xx(x,w0).

Thus the measured dynamic stiffness, *force divided by displacement*, is

    Z_x(s) = delta R/xi = P(s)-N(s)²/[tau(s²+Gamma s+O)].          (4)

It is not the usual displacement/force susceptibility. Confusing the two
swaps poles and zeros. The linear probe must characterize a rest equilibrium
or otherwise establish the same linear operator independently; arbitrary
ringdown preparation is removed only from (2)-(3), not from this calibration
obligation. The pole and residue are fitted observable features under a model
class and finite-band uncertainty, not exact deductions from finitely many
noisy frequencies. No analytic continuation for free is assumed.

At the simple observable pole lambda, suppose N(lambda)!=0. The residue rho is

    rho = -N(lambda)²/[tau(2 lambda+Gamma)],
    Kspec = |(2 lambda+Gamma)rho| = |N(lambda)|²/tau > 0.           (5)

An unknown polynomial P drops out of this residue. Reciprocal kinetic and
damping cross terms need not be set to zero: their common N cancels below.

## Observable common-action condition

Provided f!=0, (3)-(5) imply

    Q(x) := Kspec(x) h(x) / |f(x)|² = d O(x)/dx.                 (6)

The hidden amplitude/phase, hidden inertia and its gradient, potential
coupling, kinetic cross coupling and reciprocal damping cross coupling have
been eliminated. Their numerical values are unnecessary for this necessary
test. Their existence and structural placement in the candidate remain
premises. In particular, (6) does not reconstruct tau, B, C or U individually.
An arbitrary U(x) affects offsets and the analytic part P, not this identity.

Changing the time origin multiplies h and |f|² by the same exp(-Gamma t0),
so Q is unchanged. Constant hidden rescalings and shifts change c and the
action coefficients but leave (6) unchanged. For constant observed affine
scales X=g_x x+o_x, Y=g_R R+o_R with g_x*g_R>0, the measured quantities obey
Kspec_new=|g_R/g_x|Kspec, h_new=g_R h, f_new=g_R f and
Q_new=Q/g_x=dO/dX. Negative work orientation must be corrected by calibration;
the modulus in (5) cannot determine it. A common time-scale change transforms
both sides consistently; environment-dependent clock errors do not cancel.

No pointwise derivative need be estimated if the environment scan is available:

    O(b)-O(a) = integral_a^b Q(x) dx.                            (7)

Only finite points require a separate interpolation certificate. For true
Lipschitz Q with known constant L_Q, interval widths h_i and midpoints m_i,

    |integral Q - sum h_i Qhat(m_i)|
      <= sum h_i e_Qi + L_Q sum h_i²/4.                         (8)

Endpoint errors in O add; coordinate, clock and quadrature calibration errors
must also be included. L_Q is an added certificate, not inferred from finite
samples. With no such certificate a smooth between-sample spike remains free.

## Deterministic extraction-error budget

Let true |lambda|<=Lmax, |Gamma|<=Gmax, |rho|<=Pmax and error radii
e_lambda,e_Gamma,e_rho. By product and modulus triangle inequalities,

    e_K <= (2 Lmax+Gmax)e_rho + Pmax(2e_lambda+e_Gamma)
           +(2e_lambda+e_Gamma)e_rho.                           (9)

Let |f| in [Fmin,Fmax], |h|<=Hmax, Kspec<=Kmax, with Fmin>0.
For complex f error e_f and real h error e_h, define

    e_D=2 Fmax e_f+e_f²,   Dmin=Fmin²,
    e_N=Kmax e_h+Hmax e_K+e_K e_h.

If e_D<Dmin, then

    |Qhat-Q| <= e_N/(Dmin-e_D)
                +Kmax Hmax e_D/[Dmin(Dmin-e_D)].                (10)

These bounds accept correlated errors. They require valid *true-quantity*
bounds. They do not manufacture pole, residue, mode-isolation, finite-band,
actuator-transfer or ringdown-fit error certificates. Coefficient fits with
ill-conditioned bases may make this route unusable, which is a real limit.

## Rejection controls and non-uniqueness

The incorrect shortcut h=C'|c|² drops tau' and generally fails even for a
reciprocal action. Dropping k or nu from only one response equation likewise
breaks the necessary shared N. These are algebraic mistakes, not discoveries
of nonreciprocity.

As a separate candidate, add an output-only force epsilon(x) z² without
changing hidden dynamics or the linearized operator. It preserves the same
small-signal poles/residue and f, but shifts h by 2 epsilon |c|². Its observed
Q shifts by 2 epsilon/tau. Thus (6) can reject that alternative when the
calibration/error margin resolves the shift. A nonphysical readout distortion
can create the same effect, so a failed test does not by itself identify its
cause. No claim that every nonreciprocal model violates (6) is made.

An output-only cubic term leaves these selected exponent coefficients and the
linear response unchanged, while introducing additional decays/harmonics.
Checking the full six-function closure catches it; checking only (6) does not.
More general alternatives can still match finite observations. Passing a
finite compatibility test is not a unique action derivation.
