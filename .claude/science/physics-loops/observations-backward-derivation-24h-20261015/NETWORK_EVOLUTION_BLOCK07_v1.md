## The missing physical identification

Blocks02 and04 construct real transport and exclusive outcome records. Their
quadratic quantity is a conserved mathematical flux. They do not establish
that, in each individual trial, a fractional flux p at a port is an actual
energy p*epsilon delivered there, or that writing a click transfers epsilon
of that same energy into a resolved sink. Test that optional identification
explicitly, rather than letting it become an unnoticed premise.

Real detector records require an apparatus model. For example the NIST/LANL
overview [Rosenberg et al.](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=32485)
describes biased avalanche multiplication and external quench/reset circuitry,
and distinguishes energy-resolving sensors. A digital avalanche pulse is not
itself a measurement of an isolated photon-sized energy transfer. Only the
operating-principle passages of that two-page source are used here; no device
numbers or efficiencies are inserted in the candidate.

## Explicit finite-reservoir extension

Adopt the following assumptions ONLY for this test:

- Each trial delivers a total energy epsilon>0, with actual incoming energies
  p_t*epsilon and (1-p_t)*epsilon at the two ports.
- Exactly one event per trial transfers epsilon to an accounted local sink.
  Let C_t=1 denote the event at port0, C_t=0 at port1.
- Between those deliveries and transfers, there is no other energy exchange
  with either local reservoir. Each stored energy is nonnegative and the sum
  of stored energies is B*epsilon, with B finite and fixed.
- Events are resolved at trial boundaries. The assumptions include the
  timing, the sink identification and absence of unmeasured exchange.

In units of epsilon, local conservation requires

    e_0(t)=e_0(0)+sum_(j=1)^t (p_j-C_j),
    e_1(t)=B-e_0(t),        0<=e_0(t)<=B.

Thus for every prefix

    e_0(0)-B <= sum_j(C_j-p_j) <= e_0(0).

This is a consequence of the declared physical bridge, independent of the
internal update law or how much informational memory that law possesses.
It does not follow merely from the existence of binary records.

For p_t=1/2, B an integer >=1, and e_0(0)=B/2, define

    S_t=sum_(j=1)^t (2*C_j-1).

The allowed records satisfy |S_t|<=B for all t. The iid fair-coin target assigns
positive probability to a violation once t>B. No finite such buffer can
produce that target exactly for all finite times. For a fixed initial central
energy, N0=sum C_j lies in an interval of width B, so Var(N0)<=B^2/4 by the
elementary bounded-range variance inequality. The iid value N/4 eventually
exceeds it. This is a failure of the closed finite fractional-energy extension,
not of all deterministic network models or of energy conservation in quantum
physics. A quantum output probability is not asserted to be energy physically
split on every trial.

## Exact finite-horizon statistical cost

Let P_N be the iid fair-coin distribution on N records, and A_N the allowed
prefix-path event max_(t<=N)|S_t|<=B. Every law Q_N satisfying the finite-buffer
contract has support in A_N. Therefore

    TV(Q_N,P_N) >= 1-P_N(A_N).

There is a causal record rule attaining this bound simultaneously at every N:
use a fair decision while |S|<B; at S=B force the next increment to -1; at S=-B
force it to +1. This is a reflecting walk. Its choices may be generated by
the uniform deterministic reservoir of block04, using its explicit deterministic
endpoint extension from block05v2 at a forced choice. No new random seed per
trial is required under that supplied initial ensemble.

Proof of optimality: every allowed path has Q_N probability 2^(-N+k), where k
counts steps which depart a boundary and hence were forced. This is at least
its P_N probability 2^(-N). On disallowed paths Q_N is zero. Summing positive
and negative parts of Q_N-P_N gives exactly 1-P_N(A_N). The rule does not need
future knowledge or an N-dependent conditioning policy. This is a mathematical
best approximation among all distributions with that allowed support, not a
derivation of a real detector's control law or microscopic energy Hamiltonian.

The shared reservoir state and complementary energy bookkeeping would still
need a physical preparation/coordination mechanism. A causal central record
algorithm is not by itself a spatially local implementation of two separated
detectors. In particular an arbitrary control p_t must be available through
the stipulated local incoming data, with the required common trial alignment.

Compute the target survival probability with a substochastic matrix K on
states s=-B,...,B: K_(s,s+1)=K_(s,s-1)=1/2 when the destination is in range;
the missing boundary probability is absorption. Then

    P_N(A_N)=e_center^T K^N 1.

K is symmetric, with eigenvalues cos[r*pi/(2B+2)], r=1,...,2B+1. Expanding in
the elementary sine eigenvectors, or simply bounding the Euclidean operator
norm, gives

    P_N(A_N) <= min(1, sqrt(2B+1)*cos[pi/(2B+2)]^N).

Consequently the minimal TV tends to one for every fixed finite B. The exact
recursion is used for finite examples; the norm bound is not represented as
tight. At B=1 the exact survival is 2^(-floor(N/2)).

## Observable correlations under this energy feedback

For the reflecting chain, the invariant distribution gives mass 1/(4B) to
each endpoint and 1/(2B) to each of the 2B-1 interior states. It is normalized
and satisfies detailed balance. The chain has period two; invariance does NOT
mean convergence to it from every fixed initial state.

In that invariant ensemble, each increment X_t=S_t-S_(t-1) has zero mean and
unit square. A nonzero conditional mean of X_(t+1) occurs only when S_t is an
endpoint. On either endpoint, X_t*X_(t+1)=-1. Hence

    E[X_t X_(t+1)] = -1/(2B),
    Corr(C_t,C_(t+1)) = -1/(2B).

The count mean can remain correct while local storage forces serial structure.
This formula is specific to the reflecting candidate and its invariant ensemble;
the earlier finite-horizon support bound applies to every closed-buffer law.
The phase-history and context-correlation tests from block05 are independent
ways to examine intermediate relations, with different supplied assumptions.

## Open environments change the obligation

If the detector can exchange cumulative energy W_0(t) with its environment,
then instead

    E_0(t)-E_0(0)
      = epsilon*sum_(j=1)^t(p_j-C_j)+W_0(t).

The closed-buffer bound no longer applies without a bound on that exchange.
For example, actively holding the buffer energy constant requires

    W_0(t)=epsilon*sum_(j=1)^t(C_j-p_j).

Under iid balanced outputs this has mean zero and variance epsilon^2*t/4.
Under an analogous fixed-energy contract for the other detector, W_1=-W_0.
These are accounting requirements, not a demonstrated available energy source,
not a signal sent between detectors, and not a fit to avalanche pulse energies.
Unlimited or unmeasured environmental exchange cannot be silently counted as
a derived mechanism. Conversely a powered detector must not be rejected by
a theorem whose no-exchange assumption it does not satisfy.

The next physical route is to derive the joint field, detector and environmental
energy transfer and its relation to measured pulses or calorimetric deposits.
Then both record statistics and energy flow constrain the same mechanism.
This directly concerns the user's proposal that records form through the
neighborhood: the neighborhood must carry actual resources and dynamics,
not only supply an unconstrained label that makes the desired count occur.
No scalar action, detector coupling, energy scale or initial-state selector
has been retired. All numerical examples in the paired runner are synthetic.
