# From photon and tracer records to the inverse's ideal inputs

Root's next scientific target after the joint-potential calculation. These
are proposed derivations and falsifiers; this plan is not a passed physical
measurement bridge. No new observational array has been opened.

The useful progress is now conditional: the scalar coupling, scalar speed and
fixed-bare-density curvature can be reconstructed from sufficiently rich
ideal potential histories inside the supplied action class. The remaining
high-value step is to derive the map from actual records to those histories,
or reformulate the inverse in terms of records that are accessible. Repeating
more ideal algebra or selecting smooth potential curves will not perform it.

## First derivation and discriminant

Start with sky positions, shapes, redshifts, distances and repeat-time tracer
positions. Keep photon propagation, clocks, source distance calibration,
intrinsic shape/velocity distributions, survey response and noise explicit.
Use a conditional flat FLRW scalar-perturbation metric, and derive the Born
lensing kernel for P=(PsiJ+PhiJ)/2 without using a GR Poisson conversion to
mass. Fix the sign of the lensing potential by its angular deflection convention.

The known inverse to a flat-distance Volterra lensing kernel is prior art:
Taylor2001 equations3-4. Check it by substitution with the time argument
written explicitly along the past cone. Determine which angular modes and
boundary functions image distortion measures; do not replace a missing
monopole/dipole or finite-mask boundary with an unexplained zero. A conditional
continuous lensing potential is already an inferred quantity, not a raw image.

Then compare the derivative along source distance on a single cone with the
matter-time derivative at fixed spatial coordinate needed by the scalar
inverse. Test a smooth perturbation vanishing on that cone but with nonzero
normal derivative. Such a test is a measurement-operator example only unless
the perturbation also satisfies the same complete scalar/dust action; do not
claim it is a physical no-go or a second compatible universe by construction.

As a constructive alternative, derive the information supplied by repeated
observer-time lensing and velocity maps at registered comoving distances.
For a fixed source distance, observer conformal-time differentiation should
recover the local conformal-time derivative of the cone field under the Born
and fixed-coordinate conditions. Explicitly compute the overlap of source
emission intervals before treating repeated cones as a common-time spatial
Fourier history. Limited observing duration can leave no common-time volume;
do not quietly use a chosen growth function or primordial spectrum to fill it.

Finally derive the tracer acceleration equation and compare inferred PsiJ
with lensing P. The earlier exact matter Euler equation is Z'=-PsiJ, but a
redshift velocity product is not automatically Z at fixed time. Retain source
and observer motion, coordinate conventions, endpoint and line-integral terms.
Decide whether a direct cone-based weak/integrated residual avoids high time
derivatives more usefully than reconstructing both potentials pointwise.
