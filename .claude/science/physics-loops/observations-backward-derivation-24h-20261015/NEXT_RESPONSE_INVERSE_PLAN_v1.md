# Next route: eliminate supplied response functions jointly

Proposed by root after the matter-clock/response support block. This is a
research contract and hand derivation to challenge, not an executed theorem,
empirical measurement or retained claim. It should be checked or rejected
before opening another dataset or reusing it as a load-bearing result.

Observation entry: positions, redshifts, velocities and shapes can constrain
time-dependent matter-metric potentials only through explicit survey,
projection, tracer, noise and clock models. First consider ideal registered
potential functions as conditional inputs and determine precisely which
theoretical inputs they could eliminate. This does not declare those ideal
functions directly observed. Keep the canonical conformal/dust action as an
explicit class premise, and compare with known scalar-tensor reconstruction
prior art before a novelty or milestone decision.

For common-background nonzero Fourier modes define

    P=(PsiJ+PhiJ)/2, J=(PsiJ-PhiJ)/2,
    J=b w, w=delta(phi)/M, ell=h-bx, L=b'/b.

On a b!=0 chart the exact source-eliminated equation suggests

    J''+kJ^2 J = -a J' - c J + d P' + e kJ^2 P + f P,
    a=3ell-2L,
    c=d_eff+L^2-L'-3ell L,
    d=6b^2 ell+4bx,
    e=2b^2,
    f=6b^2 ell^2+2b x'+6bhx-5b^2x^2.

Five shared response coefficients would therefore be conditionally
identifiable at an epoch if the actual mode rows [-J',-J,P',kJ^2P,P]
have rank five. That rank is a hypothesis to test on full forward solutions,
not something to assume from the number of modes. Algebraic source examples
that violate other perturbation equations do not establish this rank.

If that inversion is possible, B=e/2=b^2>0 and q=bx might follow from

    q=(d-6B h)/(4-6B),

away from the critical B=2/3. The derivative identity
a=3(h-q)-B'/B and f then provide compatibility checks, while c could
determine the fixed-bare-density kappa with the clock/force corrections
retained. Do not divide at B=0 or B=2/3; investigate those cases separately.
Do not silently choose one field orientation, initial mode or scalar velocity.
