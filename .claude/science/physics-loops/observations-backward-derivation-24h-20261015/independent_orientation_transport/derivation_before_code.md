# Independent proof reconstruction before author-code inspection

Let U=(I+BA)/sqrt(2(1+c)). From A^2=B^2=I and AB+BA=2cI, U†U=I and UA=BU. Its scalar Pauli component is sqrt((1+c)/2)>0; its vector part is -i(a cross b).sigma/sqrt(2(1+c)). Norm-one quaternion form gives determinant one. Uba=Uab†, covariance is immediate, and Uaa=I.

For any other permitted T, K=U†T satisfies KA=AK, thus K=k+P_a+ + k-P_a-. Its two coefficients can be extracted continuously as Tr(P_a± K). Under joint proper rotation, both P_a± and K conjugate; traces are invariant and the oriented plus label remains plus. Every ordered pair with a fixed dot product lies on one SO(3) orbit. Reversal is on the same orbit: for a!=b rotate by pi about (a+b)/||a+b||, exchanging a and b; for a=b use identity. The excluded antipodal case is exactly where this bisector construction fails. Therefore k±(b,a)=k±(a,b), without interchanging the signs. Then Kba=Uab Kab Uab†. Substituting Tba=Uab†Kba and Tab†=Kab†Uab† yields Kab=Kab†, so each coefficient is ±1. Continuity can be checked along a=z,b=(sqrt(1-c^2),0,c), c in (-1,1], which includes the diagonal endpoint. Diagonal normalization forces both constant signs to +1. This proof genuinely uses phase-sensitive reversal, full rotation covariance, pair-only dependence, continuity and diagonal identity. It does not assume det T=1.

At (a,-a), every axial rotation stabilizes the ordered pair. Covariance forces T to commute with all exp(-itA/2), hence with A. It cannot simultaneously conjugate A to -A. This is a stabilizer obstruction, not merely a denominator singularity.

For three vectors, tau changes sign on reversal with n fixed. The twist exp(-i*k*tau*A/2) commutes with A, and U transports A to B. Thus Tba=U† exp(+i*k*tau*B/2)=exp(+i*k*tau*A/2)U†=Tab†. Diagonal tau vanishes; unitary, continuity and joint covariance survive. Therefore pair-only uniqueness does not extend to a neighborhood with a third independently transforming vector. Kappa remains arbitrary, and allowing kappa(c) would already enlarge this family.

On z->x->y->z, the three shortest rotors are (I-iY)/sqrt2, (I-iZ)/sqrt2, (I-iX)/sqrt2. Their ordered product is (I-iZ)/sqrt2, diagonal exp(-i*pi/4),exp(+i*pi/4). For n=z, only tau(x,y,z)=1. Inserting the middle exp(-ikX/2) and transporting it across U(z,x) puts exp(-ikZ/2) on the right of the loop, yielding the stated phases. Inverse order of exact edge inverses cancels the product for fixed contexts.

Multiplying U by exp(i*chi(c)) does not change its conjugation channel. On reversal, both forward and reversed scalar phases have the same sign, whereas the inverse requires the opposite sign. Any continuous chi with chi(1)=0 supplies channel-level ambiguity; for chi(c)=1-c, c=0 makes the matrix reversal fail. This is an unobservable global phase for one isolated channel, but becomes a relative path phase only after supplying coherent composition and readout.

F(b)F(a)† on the chart excludes -z, transports A to B, is exactly reciprocal, and telescopes to identity on every chart loop. It is not fully pair-only SO(3)-covariant. A direct invariant witness: ordered pairs (z,x) and (x,y) both have dot product zero, yet their flat-transport traces are sqrt2 and 1, respectively. Conjugation covariance would require equal traces. Thus the fixed reference axis is a genuine imported choice, even though formulas have only a,b as variable arguments.
