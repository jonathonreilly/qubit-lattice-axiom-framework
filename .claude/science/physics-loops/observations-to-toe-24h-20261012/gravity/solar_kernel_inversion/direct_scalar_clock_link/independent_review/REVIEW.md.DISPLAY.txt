## Canonical normalization, spectrum and static response

The upstream scalar term is

    -c³/(8pi G_*) integral d⁴x K_AB eta^(mu nu)
       partial_mu phi^A partial_nu phi^B,

with x^0=ct. Substituting d⁴x=c dt d³x and partial_0=partial_t/c gives exactly the displayed physical-time action c²/(8pi G_*) times [phi_dot K phi_dot-c² grad(phi) K grad(phi)]. Its canonical momentum is c² K phi_dot/(4pi G_*).

A transparent normalization is

    q = c K^(1/2) phi / sqrt(4pi G_*).

Then q has the conventional action (1/2) integral [q_dot²-c²(grad q)²], and each Fourier oscillator has frequency ck. The matter scalar is

    zeta = sqrt(4pi G_*)/c * alpha^T K^(-1/2) q.

The squared coefficient is 4pi G_* a/c², with a=alpha^T K^-1 alpha. The vacuum two-point coefficient is therefore 4pi G_* hbar a/c² multiplying the standard 1/(2ck) Fourier measure. This also shows why extra positive uncoupled scalar directions do not supply another adjustable noise amplitude in this channel. The a=0 limit has no coupled scalar noise.

The angular measure, the 2pi frequency delta function and its 1/c Jacobian give

    S_0^+(omega) = 2 G_* hbar a omega/c⁵
                   * exp[-(ell omega/c)²] theta(omega).

The sign of frequency matches the convention integral dt exp(i omega t) C(t), with vacuum C(t) proportional to exp(-ickt). There is no missing two-sided-spectrum factor or factor of c. S^+ has units of time because zeta is dimensionless.

The same normalized action coupled through H_I=E alpha.phi gives the static equation

    Delta phi = (4pi G_*/c⁴) K^-1 alpha E delta³(x).

Its solution yields zeta=-G_* a E/(c⁴r). Minimizing field energy plus the source interaction, or taking half the on-shell total source-potential sum, gives one cross interaction -G_* a E1 E2/(c⁴r); counting both source terms without the field energy would double it incorrectly. Setting E=mc² reproduces G_*a. The prior weak map G_N=G_*(1+a) therefore gives G_*a=G_N f_scalar.

For the stated normalized Gaussian, the Fourier profile is exp(-ell²k²/2), so a covariance or two-source interaction contains exp(-ell²k²). An independent positive real-space convolution gives

    average 1/|X1-X2|
       = 1/(sqrt(pi) ell) integral_0^1 exp[-d²s²/4] ds
       = erf(d/2)/L,  d=L/ell.

The coincident limit is 1/(sqrt(pi)ell), not an infinite point self interaction. This verifies the width convention and static erf factor. It is a supplied profile, not an inferred atom or a constructed material body.

## Logical source, common coupling and oscillator influence

Universal proper-time coupling gives the supplied clock splitting hbar Omega exp(zeta). At first order its branch difference is hbar Omega zeta. For the logical pair, the branch difference is hbar Omega(zeta_1-zeta_2), so H_I,L=(hbar Omega/2)Z_L B with B=zeta_1-zeta_2. There is no extra factor of two from the two-clock logical basis.

The protocol's identical spectra/profiles and symmetric stationary setting support the common coupling proportional to zeta_1+zeta_2. Its commutator with the difference is

    C11(t,s)-C12(t,s)+C21(t,s)-C22(t,s)=0,

using equal self commutators and reciprocal cross commutators. Equivalently, with centres chosen at +/-L/2, each real cosine/sine Fourier-mode pair puts the common source entirely in its cosine coordinate and the difference entirely in its sine coordinate. Those two coordinates commute at all times. Thus the common coupling supplies no omitted relative phase in this symmetric model.

More generally, a common linear force can change a deterministic relative phase through the commutator, but it does not change Gaussian visibility. That distinction matters: dropping a phase is not itself a calculation of decoherence. The parent confines the exact phase cancellation to the symmetric setup and explicitly supplies phase compensation/readout.

## Complete positivity, classical degeneracy and remaining physical imports

For a factorized initial state, conditional environment unitaries give the dephasing factors as a Gram kernel. In the present Gaussian construction the finite-time Weyl displacements are well defined, so positivity is not inferred from a selection of sampled exponents. Equivalently for the logical qubit, chi>=0 gives a valid dephasing channel. This does not imply Markovianity or complete-positive divisibility in time, neither of which is claimed.

The symmetric covariance is also a positive real classical covariance: for a real test history its quadratic form is the expectation of a squared Hermitian smeared field. A real Gaussian random field with that covariance gives the same commuting longitudinal phase visibility. In the symmetric pair the common-source phase cancels; for a single reference a known deterministic phase can be treated separately. These data therefore do not distinguish quantum vacuum noise from an appropriately chosen classical Gaussian field.

Canonical quantization and the chosen vacuum, not the static force coefficient alone, fix the noise covariance. Thermal/squeezed states, different preparation correlations, dressing, other fields or nonuniversal couplings need not share this result. Finite-time source-driven Gaussian displacements are included in the linear influence calculation; nonlinear field/source dynamics and a full gravitating apparatus are not.

The whole Gaussian-profile free-field integral is an explicit idealization. Because the Fourier profile has noncompact support, a cutoff effective action cannot inherit the exact formulas without a remainder estimate. Likewise the all-time bounds apply to the specified linear scalar channel, not uniformly to the full exponential clock interaction or a nonlinear gravitational theory. Actual atomic profiles, supports, tensor fluctuations, source formation, causal controls and readout are still unsupplied. The source statements maintain these distinctions.
