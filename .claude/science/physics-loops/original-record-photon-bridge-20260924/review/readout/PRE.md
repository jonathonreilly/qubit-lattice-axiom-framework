## 7. A finite narrow-band propagation bound

This part concerns the harmonic one-particle reference and its contribution to (26). It is not an assumption about the field after the first record. For a nonzero lattice momentum,

\[
\lambda(k)=4\sum_{\mu=1}^3\sin^2(k_\mu/2),\qquad
\omega(k)=\frac{c}{a_{\rm lat}}\sqrt{\lambda(k)}.
\]

Choose lifts of a finite band to a convex ball of radius \(\Delta\) about \(k_0\), away from every reciprocal zero, and let \(s_{\min}=\inf\sqrt{\lambda(k)}>0\) on that ball. Direct differentiation gives

\[
v_0=\nabla_k\omega(k_0)=\frac{c}{a_{\rm lat}}
\frac{\sin k_0}{\sqrt{\lambda(k_0)}},
\]

\[
(\nabla^2\omega)_{\mu\nu}
=\frac{c}{a_{\rm lat}}\left[
\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}\cos k_\mu}{\sqrt\lambda}
-\frac{\sin k_\mu\sin k_\nu}{\lambda^{3/2}}\right],
\qquad \|\nabla^2\omega\|\le M:=\frac{2c}{a_{\rm lat}s_{\min}}.
\tag{32}
\]

The last bound uses \(\sum_\mu\sin^2k_\mu\le\lambda(k)\). For normalized mode coefficients \(\xi_{k,\alpha}\) supported in the band, let \(\xi_t^{\rm lin}\) use the affine phase
\(e^{-it[\omega(k_0)+v_0\cdot(k-k_0)]}\) instead of \(e^{-it\omega(k)}\). Taylor's integral remainder and \(|e^{ix}-e^{iy}|\le|x-y|\) imply

\[
\|\xi_t-\xi_t^{\rm lin}\|
\le\frac{|t|M}{2}
\left(\sum_{k,\alpha}|\xi_{k,\alpha}|^2|k-k_0|^4\right)^{1/2}
\le\frac{|t|M\Delta^2}{2}=:\epsilon_{\rm band}.
\tag{33}
\]

The affine phase is precisely a common carrier phase times translation by \(v_0t\) of the finite trigonometric interpolation of the lattice packet. Fractional lattice translations here refer to that interpolation, not to a new sharp site observable. Its physical group velocity is \(a_{\rm lat}v_0\). The local response satisfies

\[
|F_p(t)-F_p^{\rm lin}(t)|\le\sqrt{v_p}\,\epsilon_{\rm band},\qquad
\big||F_p(t)|^2-|F_p^{\rm lin}(t)|^2\big|
\le2v_p\epsilon_{\rm band}.
\tag{34}
\]

Thus replacing the leading boundary signal in (26) by the translated profile incurs at most
\(4\kappa^2m_im_je^{-\gamma t}g^2v_p\epsilon_{\rm band}\), in addition to (26)'s remainder. This is a finite-band estimate. It does not make an arbitrary packet rigid or justify inserting times growing with \(g^{-1}\) into (19). At fixed finite volume the allowed momenta are discrete; arbitrarily narrowing a nontrivial band can require a new volume limit with new constants. At small nonzero carrier momentum the physical velocity tends to \(c\) in the carrier direction, with \(c\) still a supplied parameter.
