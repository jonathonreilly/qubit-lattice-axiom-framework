1. Setup, mode normalization and the volume-independent covariance range

Write N=L^3 and Omega_max=2 sqrt(3). Let C be the real link-start curl, and let u_mu be real orthonormal positive eigenmodes of C^T C, with eigenvalues w_mu^2. In momentum space C(k)^* C(k)=omega(k)^2 I-d(k)d(k)^*, so each nonzero k has two positive modes with frequency omega(k). The zero momentum gives no positive modes. Thus there are exactly 2(N-1) positive real oscillators; the complex Fourier basis is a unitary change of this real basis, not an additional set of oscillators.

For v_pmu=(Cu_mu)_p/sqrt(2w_mu), direct multiplication gives
  sum_p v_pmu v_pnu = (w_mu/2) delta_mu,nu.                 (1)
The covariance c_p=sum_mu v_pmu^2 is independent of p by lattice translation and cubic symmetry. Its trace is
  sum_p c_p = sum_mu w_mu/2 = sum_(k!=0) omega(k),
so c_p=c_L. The full grid, including its zero term, has mean omega^2=6 because the mean of each cos(k_i) is zero. Since omega<=sqrt(12),
  mean omega >= mean omega^2/sqrt(12)=sqrt(3),
  mean omega <= sqrt(mean omega^2)=sqrt(6).
Consequently 1/sqrt(3)<=c_L<=sqrt(2/3). No Riemann-sum limit is used.

For a normalized one-particle eigenvector f of frequency w, define a_f^dagger=sum_mu f_mu a_mu^dagger and a_f=sum_mu conjugate(f_mu)a_mu. Let z_p=sum_mu v_pmu f_mu. Then
  sum_p |z_p|^2=w/2.                                      (2)
This follows from (1), including when f is a complex linear combination in a degenerate frequency space.

2. Denominators, total momentum and conjugation

The reused independent result has the exact interface
  omega(p)+omega(q)-omega(p+q)
    >= omega(p)omega(q)[omega(p)+omega(q)]/(8d)
for all torus momenta, with equality only when at least one frequency is zero. It applies to wrapped momenta without choosing a small-momentum branch.

For n>=3 nonzero momenta, order their frequencies as alpha>=beta>=gamma>=... . Pair the two largest and use ordinary subadditivity for the remaining momenta. If Delta_n=sum_j omega(q_j)-omega(sum_j q_j), this gives
  Delta_n >= alpha beta(alpha+beta)/(8d)>0,
  product_j omega(q_j)/Delta_n
    <= 8d gamma/(alpha+beta) product_(j>=4) omega(q_j)
    <= 4d (2sqrt(d))^(n-3).                               (3)
The reference uses d=3, so the last bound is 12 Omega_max^(n-3).

At fixed L the number of positive oscillators is finite and their minimum frequency is positive. There are finitely many occupation states below any fixed energy. The odd total-momentum-k sector, after deleting the entire two-dimensional one-particle space, has n>=3. Equation (3) excludes an eigenvalue at or below w in this sector. High occupation energies tend to infinity, so the restricted inverse (H0-w)^(-1) is bounded and positive at each fixed L. This argument does not produce a volume-uniform operator norm for that inverse. The bounds below control specific vectors instead.

Translation invariance puts v0 and v_g at total momentum zero. Creating the external photon sends their spectator vectors to momentum k. The connected vectors are also at momentum k. Thus every occurrence of the minus resolvent acts on the permitted odd, momentum-k subspace. The plus inverse has no threshold problem and is positive on the relevant sectors.

Let J be coefficient conjugation in the real occupation basis. It is antiunitary, commutes with H0, fixes v0 and v_g, and sends external momentum k to -k. It conjugates the polarization coordinates as well. The exchange calculation initially produces a vector at -k. Its plus-resolvent quadratic form equals the plus-resolvent quadratic form of the connected vector at k by J-invariance and the reality of a self-adjoint quadratic form. One must not identify those vectors without J or silently use the minus denominator at -k.

At a self-inverse zone-boundary momentum, J remains an antiunitary operation within the two-dimensional polarization space. The Fourier basis still contains exactly two modes there. No doubling or halving is introduced. A change of polarization frame at k is unitary, so the resulting two-by-two forms transform by unitary conjugation.

3. Full order-g^4 coefficient: direct terms and spectator cancellation

The elementary vacuum generating function exp(tB)=exp(c t^2/2):exp(tB): yields
  B^4=:B^4:+6c:B^2:+3c^2,
  B^6=:B^6:+15c:B^4:+45c^2:B^2:+15c^3.
Between one-particle states only the degree-two normal-ordered term and the scalar can contribute. Using (2),
  P1(V4-<V4>_0)P1=-(c/4)H0,
  P1(V6-<V6>_0)P1=(c^2/16)H0.                            (4)
Here V4=-sum_p B_p^4/24, V6=sum_p B_p^6/720, and c=c_L.

Put b_p^dagger=sum_mu v_pmu a_mu^dagger and use normalized f. Directly applying the normal-ordered polynomials to vacuum gives
  v0=-(c/4)sum_p (b_p^dagger)^2 Omega
       -(1/24)sum_p (b_p^dagger)^4 Omega,
  C_f=-(1/6)sum_p z_p (b_p^dagger)^3 Omega,
  s_f=a_f^dagger v0,
  Q V4 f=s_f+C_f.                                        (5)
For example [V4,a_f^dagger]=-(1/6)sum_p z_p B_p^3; the one-particle component of this commutator is -(c/4)w f and is deleted by Q. The four-creation part of v0 creates the entire spectator five-particle sector in s_f. It has not been omitted.

Since [H0,a_f^dagger]=w a_f^dagger,
  (H0-w)^(-1) a_f^dagger v0=a_f^dagger H0^(-1)v0.
The spectral shift and the CCR then give
  <s_f,R_k s_f>-<v0,H0^(-1)v0>
    =<a_f v0,(H0+w)^(-1)a_f v0>.                         (6)
Indeed a_f a_f^dagger=1+a_f^dagger a_f and
  a_f H0^(-1)=(H0+w)^(-1)a_f
on the excited sectors in question. This cancels the extensive vacuum term algebraically before taking any estimate.

Annihilating one photon in v0 gives
  a_f v0=-(c w/4)Jf+J C_f.                               (7)
The first summand is one-particle and the second is three-particle. They are orthogonal, including for complex f. On the first summand the plus denominator is 2w. Applying the conjugation argument of section 2 to the second therefore yields
  <s_f,R_k s_f>-<v0,H0^(-1)v0>
    =c^2 w/32+K_+(f),
  K_+(f)=<C_f,(H0+w)^(-1)C_f>.                           (8)
The finite term c^2w/32 and K_+ are the Bose-exchange remainder; they must not be removed along with the extensive vacuum term.

The interference can be calculated independently of (8). Only the two-particle component of H0^(-1)v0 can pair with a_f C_f:
  (H0^(-1)v0)_2=-(c/16)sum_mu (a_mu^dagger)^2 Omega,
  a_f C_f=-(1/2)sum_p |z_p|^2 (b_p^dagger)^2 Omega.
The elementary normalized-oscillator contraction is
  <Omega,(sum_mu a_mu^2)(b_p^dagger)^2 Omega>=2c.
It follows that
  <s_f,R_k C_f>=c^2 w/32.                                (9)
There are two interference copies in <s_f+C_f,R_k(s_f+C_f)>.

Substituting (4), (8), (9) and
  K_-(f)=<C_f,(H0-w)^(-1)C_f>
into the specified vacuum-subtracted perturbation formula gives
  <f,D4(k)f>
    =c^2w/16-[c^2w/32+K_+(f)+2c^2w/32+K_-(f)]
    =-c^2w/32-K_-(f)-K_+(f).                             (10)
Because this holds for every complex f in the external two-dimensional space, polarization gives the complete Hermitian matrix identity T1. It is not merely an identity on a chosen pair of real diagonal elements.

This is the full coefficient for the specified finite Gaussian Rayleigh-Schrodinger formula: the degenerate first-order compression is scalar, and Q removes the whole one-particle eigenspace in the invariant momentum/parity sector. It does not establish an analytic eigenbranch for the compact Hamiltonian or an error bound for truncating an interacting expansion.

4. Bose multiplicities, Fourier convention and T2

In a Fourier basis, B_p=sum_alpha[v_palpha a_alpha+conjugate(v_palpha)a_alpha^dagger]. For an external f at k, z_(x,o)=exp(ik.x)b_(o,f)/sqrt(2Nw). Thus the n-creation tensor is
  T_(alpha_1,...,alpha_n;f)
     =sum_p z_p product_j conjugate(v_(p,alpha_j)),
and it vanishes unless sum_j q_j=k modulo reciprocal lattice periods. The minus denominator is sum_j omega(q_j)-w. The plus denominator, after the real-conjugation exchange identification, is sum_j omega(q_j)+w on this same tensor.

A symmetric ordered creation tensor t has squared norm
  ||sum_(ordered alpha) t_alpha a_alpha1^dagger...a_alphan^dagger Omega||^2
       =n! sum_(ordered alpha) |t_alpha|^2.                (11)
This follows by commuting the n annihilators across the creators and summing all n! pairings. Repeated modes do not require an additional correction. For example, a tuple with three identical modes gives norm 3!|t|^2; a multiset with two equal modes has three ordered copies and norm 3^2*2!|t|^2=3!*3|t|^2. These checks distinguish ordered-tensor notation from a sum over occupation multisets.

For the quartic connected coefficient -1/6, equation (11) gives
  K_+/-(f)=(1/6)sum_(ordered matched triples)
           |T|^2/[sum_j omega(q_j) +/- w].               (12)
For arbitrary n, the curl identity ||b_(.,f)||_2=w and ||b_(.,alpha)||_2=omega(q) gives, by the elementary finite-dimensional Holder bound and the lattice Fourier sum,
  |T_n|^2 <= N^(1-n) w product_j omega(q_j)/2^(n+1).       (13)
There are at most N^(n-1) ordered momentum choices and 2^n polarization choices. This deliberately overcounts choices involving a forbidden zero mode; it does not omit any legal tuple. It also applies at self-inverse momenta.

For n=3, (3), (12), (13) give
  K_- <= (1/6)*(w/(16N^2))*(8N^2)*12 = w.
For the plus denominator, AM-GM and omega_j<=Omega_max imply
  product_j omega_j/(sum_j omega_j+w)
     <= product_j omega_j/(sum_j omega_j)
     <= Omega_max^(n-1)/n.
At n=3 this is 4, hence K_+<=w/3. Both K forms are positive. Therefore
  -[1/48+1+1/3]w I <= D4 <= -[1/96]w I,
which is exactly T2. These are analytic uniform bounds, independent of the numerical boxes checked below.

5. Full cosine: domains and the exact cancellation identity

Fix a finite L and g>0. With y=g^2c, r=exp(-y/2), t=(r-1)/2, vacuum normal ordering of the EXACT cosine gives the coefficients
  W_g-<W_g>_0 = t sum_p :B_p^2:
           +sum_(even m>=4) A_m sum_p :B_p^m:,
  A_m=r(-1)^(m/2+1)g^(m-2)/m!.
No truncation in m is used here. On vacuum,
  ||(b_p^dagger)^m Omega||=sqrt(m! c^m).
Thus the norm-series majorant is a constant times (g sqrt(c))^m/sqrt(m!), which converges for every finite g, even after any fixed polynomial number/energy weight. There are finitely many p at fixed L. The same holds for the one-photon and connected vectors and their creation/annihilation images. These estimates justify the normal-order identities, resolvent forms and limits on these vectors; they do not assert a globally convergent operator Taylor expansion of W_g on arbitrary Fock vectors.

The exact compression is P1(W_g-<W_g>_0)P1=tH0. Write
  v_g=t sum_p (b_p^dagger)^2 Omega
         +sum_(even m>=4) A_m sum_p (b_p^dagger)^m Omega,
  C_(g,f)=sum_(odd n>=3) a_n sum_p z_p (b_p^dagger)^n Omega,
  a_n=r(-1)^((n-1)/2)g^(n-1)/n!.
The exponent differs from the report's version by two, so the signs coincide. The identities
  QW_g f=a_f^dagger v_g+C_(g,f),
  a_f v_g=t w Jf+J C_(g,f)
then follow either from the convergent series or from
  [W_g,a_f^dagger]=sum_p z_p[sin(gB_p)/g-B_p].
Repeating the exact spectator cancellation yields
  S_g(f)=t^2w/2+K_(g,-)(f)+K_(g,+)(f)+2 Re X_g(f),
  X_g(f)=<H0^(-1)v_g,a_f C_(g,f)>.                        (14)
This is an exact identity for the two-insertion reference functional, including all spectator terms.

6. Interference sign and normalization

Define the real covariance
  G_tau(p,q)=sum_mu v_pmu v_qmu exp(-tau w_mu).
Then |G_tau|<=c. Applying (1) before integrating proves the useful exact identity
  sum_p integral_0^infinity G_tau(p,q)^2 d tau
    =sum_mu v_qmu^2*(w_mu/2)/(2w_mu)=c/4.                (15)
For even m>=2 let
  J_m(f)=sum_(p,q)|z_q|^2 integral_0^infinity G_tau(p,q)^m d tau.
Since G_tau is real and m even,
  J_2=cw/8,  0<=J_m<=c^(m-1)w/8.                        (16)
In particular this step would not be justified by treating a generally complex Fourier covariance as though every even power were positive; the real basis is essential to this version of the sign proof.

For m particles the resolvent contraction is m! integral G_tau^m. The degree-m coefficient of a_f C_(g,f) is
  (m+1)a_(m+1)=r(-1)^(m/2)g^m/m!.
For m=2, multiplying by t and 2! gives
  X_2=-t r g^2 J_2=-t r y w/8>=0.
For each even m>=4, multiplying the last coefficient by A_m and m! gives
  X_m=-r^2 g^(2m-2)J_m/m!<=0.                            (17)
The opposite signs are not a discarded correction. Equation (16) supplies an absolutely convergent majorant and gives
  -r^2 w[cosh(y)-1-y^2/2]/(8y)
       <= X_high=sum_(even m>=4)X_m <=0.                 (18)
For complex f all the dependence in J_m is |z_q|^2, so the same sign statements apply to every polarization. At fixed L, the g^4 limit of X_2 is c^2w/32; the higher interference starts at g^6. This independently checks the factor and sign in (9).

7. Entire connected series and the all-g reference bounds

Particle-number sectors are orthogonal. Squaring the n-creation coefficient and applying (11) leaves r^2g^(2n-2)/n! times the ordered tensor sum. Combining (3), (13), and the mode count gives
  K_(g,-)(f) <= w F_-,
  F_-=6r^2g^4 sum_(odd n>=3) x^(n-3)/n!
      =6r^2g^4[sinh(x)-x]/x^3,
where x=Omega_max g^2. The plus-denominator estimate of section 4 gives
  K_(g,+)(f) <= w F_+,
  F_+=(r^2/2)sum_(odd n>=3) x^(n-1)/(n n!).              (19)
Both bounds are entire series in finite g after filling removable zero values. The powers of N cancel before the n sum. Positivity permits summing the sector bounds; the factorial majorants justify absolute convergence.

Let
  A0=t^2/2-t r y/4,
  H=r^2[cosh(y)-1-y^2/2]/(4y).
Equations (14), (18), (19) give
  (A0-H)w <= S_g(f) <= (A0+F_-+F_+)w.                   (20)
This establishes F7 for all finite positive g without a small-g series truncation. At g=0 the limits are zero. Since all previous steps hold for every normalized complex f, these are matrix inequalities.
