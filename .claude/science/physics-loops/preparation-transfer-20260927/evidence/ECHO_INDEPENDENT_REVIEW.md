## Independent source proof of a rate-normalization discrepancy

The same pinned Fig S5 notebook has a concrete factor-of-two difference between its T1 and Echo model coefficients:

1. Cell13 `qutrit_decay_function` evolves populations with gamma_10, gamma_21, gamma_20 exactly as ordinary downward rates. There is no factor two in its ODE.
2. Cell27 defines each T1 list as `1e6/gamma` (microseconds). Cell29 obtains trimmed means of those T1 lists, converts them back to seconds, and supplies them as fixed T1 arguments to the Echo model. This is a source averaging prescription, not our calibration, and is not imported here.
3. Cell7 converts time and T1 to microseconds, then calls QuTiP mesolve with collapse operators `sqrt(2/T1_10)*|0><1|`, `sqrt(2/T1_21)*|1><2|`, `sqrt(2/T1_20)*|0><2|`. Under the canonical dissipator D[C]rho=C rho C†−{C†C,rho}/2, C=sqrt(2/T)|j><i| gives du_i/dt=−(2/T)u_i and corresponding +2/T gain in j. Thus these are 2/T population rates, not 1/T. Cell7 uses each interval t/2 twice: those halves partition total time and do not cancel the rate factor. The imported three_level_basis uses normalized states; its explicit outer products introduce no compensating coefficient.
4. Krause2024.txt lines3418–3440 gives S1–S3 with ordinary Gamma rates and identifies T1=Gamma^-1, agreeing with the T1 ODE convention. Lines3517–3524 describe fixing rates from relaxation for the Echo calculation but do not explain a factor two.

This establishes the numerical coefficient discrepancy in the archived analysis source; it does **not** establish whether the authors intended a convention, made a modeling error, or compensated an acquisition timing detail not available in the archive. The analysis notebook is not an executed schedule. No new doubled-rate curve was generated and no agreement-based resolution was attempted. Preserve this source-normalization ambiguity alongside the conditional failure rather than silently adopting source model coefficients or inventing a corrected physical delay.
