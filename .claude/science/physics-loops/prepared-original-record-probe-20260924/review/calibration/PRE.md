# Blind PRE: calibration of the original resolved record observable

**Result.** The specified pair of original formation marks measures a plaquette cosine on the initial field. For every fixed finite-Hermite preparation, its leading vacuum-subtracted response is the excess **real plaquette quadrature second moment**. This equals twice the positive-frequency intensity for number-invariant preparations, with the readout's particular mode weights. It also contains anomalous pair moments for general preparations. Explicit vacuum/two-quantum superpositions show that no fixed affine calibration of this one mean reproduces an ideal absorption intensity on the whole preparation class. Raw single and pair events are already different from ideal absorption events. Spatial or time averaging yields useful conditional mean identities, with important survival-weight and limit qualifications.

This is an independent reconstruction from the permitted sources, using the exact resolved boundary effect as an explicit provisional dependency. It does not recheck its path enumeration, establish an optical detector, judge an unreleased author argument, or assert a framework-wide obstruction. ## 1. Fixed model, state preparation and exact observable

Fix an even cubic torus with each side at least six, V vertices, its all-A-plus/all-B-empty matter word, physical Gauss constraint and initial zero electric winding. The original recorded marks are resolved edge/sign marks. Use exactly the compensated common rotor generator from the local-compensation and local-pair notes, with

\[
 \tau_*=a/c,\qquad K=g^2/(2\tau_*),\qquad
 \delta=1/(4\tau_*g^2),\qquad \kappa>0.
\]

The graph, a,c,kappa and the observation horizon are fixed when g tends to zero. The plaquette and two marks have the permitted source's geometry: first birth edge from a=(0,0,0) to b=(-1,0,0), second from d=(1,1,0) to f=(2,1,0); their unresolved outward hops share c=(1,0,0), e=(0,1,0), giving plaquette a,c,d,e. Either resolved sign is fixed. Translations/rotations of this legal geometry supply the corresponding plaquette probes.

To avoid conflating two D's, write \(D_c=(C^TC)|_{\mathcal S}>0\) for the curl square on the parent's transverse quotient, and \(D_{\rm mat}\) for the original matter-dependent electric operator. Set

\[
 \Omega=D_c^{1/2},\quad \Omega_\alpha>0,\quad
 \omega_\alpha=\Omega_\alpha/\tau_*,\quad
 H_h=\tau_*^{-1}\sum_\alpha\Omega_\alpha(a_\alpha^\dagger a_\alpha+1/2).
\]

All oscillator calculations below use this finite number of real transverse modes, not gauge or harmonic zero directions. Let \(\mathcal F\) be a fixed finite span of Hermite eigenvectors, independent of g. For a normalized vector psi in this span, the physical preparation is exactly the parent's normalized smooth cutoff packet \(\widehat I_g\psi\), evolved by the actual initial-sector Hamiltonian. Finite mixtures mean declared mixtures of these preparations. Bounds below are uniform over unit vectors/densities in a fixed such span. In particular the preparation's excitation cutoff cannot grow with g under this statement.

For a density rho on \(\mathcal F\), one can also explicitly prepare
\(I_g\rho I_g^\dagger/\operatorname{Tr}(I_g\rho I_g^\dagger)\).
On the fixed span, \(I_g^\dagger I_g=I+O(e^{-d/g^2})\) in matrix norm, so this convention and any specified mixture of normalized parent packets have the same leading result. Exact preparation conventions are not silently identified at finite g.

The permitted provisional effect is

\[
 B_j^\dagger B_j=5I,\qquad
 (B_lB_j)^\dagger(B_lB_j)=23I+W_p+W_p^\dagger.                 \tag{1}
\]

No coherent-edge multiplicity is extended to positive lag. No matter dephasing or field-only postbirth dynamics is introduced. With \(\lambda=30\kappa V\), the actual first-mark density is exactly \(5\kappa e^{-\lambda s}\), independent of the initial field. If \(\rho_g(s)\) is its normalized prebirth unitary orbit, the boundary of the density for j first at s, then l next at s+u with no intervening event, is

\[
 f_{\rho,g}(s,0+)=\kappa^2e^{-\lambda s}
              [23+2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_g(s)W_p)].       \tag{2}
\]

It is a right-lag density, not the probability of exactly simultaneous events. Later history is unrestricted. Equation (2) follows from strong continuity of the full no-event semigroup and bounded jumps, and requires no Hamiltonian derivative. Its normalized effect lies between 21 and 25 for every normalizable initial field. Given the first mark, the selected immediate second intensity lies between \(21\kappa/5\) and \(5\kappa\).

## 2. General leading prepared-state response, with a resolvable remainder

Choose real eigenvectors e_alpha of \(D_c\), and let c_p be the plaquette row. Define

\[
 d_{p\alpha}=\frac{c_p\cdot e_\alpha}{\sqrt{2\Omega_\alpha}},\quad
 b_p(s)=\sum_\alpha d_{p\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha s}a_\alpha,
 \quad Q_p(s)=b_p(s)+b_p(s)^\dagger,
 \quad v_p=\sum_\alpha d_{p\alpha}^2.                         \tag{3}
\]

Here Q_p is the harmonic plaquette coordinate, and \([b_p,b_p^\dagger]=v_p I\). No identification of b_p with a physical electric-field detector coupling has yet been made.

For every fixed T and fixed preparation span,

\[
 \frac{f_{\rho,g}(s,0+)}{\kappa^2e^{-\lambda s}}
       =25-g^2\operatorname{Tr}(\rho Q_p(s)^2)+O_T(g^4),
              \qquad 0\le s\le T.                            \tag{4}
\]

This includes preparations with a nonzero first quadrature moment, coherences between different particle numbers, and finite mixtures. Centered parity is not required for (4).

**Proof.** The allowed weak-field theorem gives, after a scalar phase alignment, actual vector = normalized reference + error, with norm error O_T(g^2) plus a Gaussian cutoff tail. The estimate is uniform on the unit sphere of the fixed finite span: the residual is linear before normalization and all needed polynomial moments are uniformly bounded there. Let \(O_p=W_p+W_p^\dagger-2I\). Its norm is at most 4, while on the reference chart

\[
 \|O_p\,\widehat I_g\psi_s\|
  \le g^2\| (c_p\cdot x)^2\psi_s\|+O(e^{-d/g^2})=O_T(g^2).
\]

Expanding its expectation gives a cross error bounded by
\(2\|e\|\|O_p\,\mathrm{reference}\|\) and an error-square bounded by \(4\|e\|^2\), hence O_T(g^4). The identity term cancels by exact normalization. On the reference,
\(2\cos(g c_p\cdot x)=2-g^2(c_p\cdot x)^2+O(g^4|c_p\cdot x|^4)\)
in expectation. Fixed finite-Hermite moments and cutoff tails prove (4). Convexity gives the density version. No unbounded moment of the actual vector error is inferred from its norm. This proof uses the intrinsic Hamiltonian packet theorem, not the older slow-formation schedule as a live-record theorem.

Subtract the actually prepared vacuum response, divide by the specified scale, and define

\[
 \mathcal R_{p,\rho}(s)
 :=\lim_{g\to0}\frac{f_{0,g}(s,0+)-f_{\rho,g}(s,0+)}
                         {\kappa^2e^{-\lambda s}g^2}
 =\operatorname{Tr}(\rho Q_p(s)^2)-v_p.                       \tag{5}
\]

The error in this normalized expression is O_T(g^2). The elementary oscillator identity is

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal R_{p,\rho}(s)
       =2\operatorname{Tr}(\rho b_p^\dagger b_p)
        +2\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}(\rho b_p^2).}     \tag{6}
\]

Subtracting v_p is normal ordering of the real quadrature square: \(Q_p^2-v_p I=:Q_p^2:\). It does **not** remove its two-annihilator/two-creator terms. Thus the issue is not merely a choice between normal and symmetric notation; selecting the absorption combination \(b_p^\dagger b_p\) is an additional operation or state restriction.

## 3. Which state families admit a mean calibration?

Write N=\(\sum a_\alpha^\dagger a_\alpha\). If \([\rho,N]=0\), all \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho a_\alpha a_\beta)\) vanish, and (6) is exactly twice the matched positive-frequency intensity. This includes arbitrary fixed-number states, multimode coherences within a fixed number, and number-invariant finite mixtures. The condition \([\rho,N]=0\) is sufficient rather than necessary; vanishing relevant anomalous moments is enough.

For n quanta in one normalized wave-packet mode xi, the coefficient is
\(2n|\sum d_{p\alpha}e^{-i\omega_\alpha s}\xi_\alpha|^2\).
For a number-invariant density it is the corresponding quadratic form of its one-body density matrix. A given plaquette or selected geometry may be dark. A superposition of vacuum and one quantum can also have zero anomalous moment, despite not commuting with N. Consequently number coherence alone is not a sufficient diagnosis of failed calibration.

There is, however, an exact finite-Hermite obstruction to a universal affine mean calibration. Excite only one real mode with nonzero plaquette weight d, leave other modes in vacuum, and evaluate at s=0. Let

\[
 |\psi_\pm\rangle=(\sqrt2|0\rangle\pm|2\rangle)/\sqrt3.
\]

Both have \(\langle a^\dagger a\rangle=2/3\), but
\(\langle a^2\rangle=\pm2/3\). Their excess quadrature responses are respectively
\(\mathcal R_-=0\) and \(\mathcal R_+=8d^2/3\), while the matched absorption intensities are both \(2d^2/3>0\). The vacuum also has zero leading excess response and zero intensity. Therefore no fixed affine function of this one leading mean can equal the intensity on the whole allowed state class. The equally elementary state

\[
 |\psi_{\rm neg}\rangle=(2|0\rangle-|2\rangle)/\sqrt5
\]

has intensity \(2d^2/5\) but
\(\mathcal R=4d^2(1-\sqrt2)/5<0\). Its original pair probability has an excess over the prepared vacuum at leading order, rather than the deficit familiar from number states.

Even on the number-invariant class, matching a mean is not matching a count law. The states \(|1\rangle\langle1|\) and
\((|0\rangle\langle0|+|2\rangle\langle2|)/2\) have the same \(\langle N\rangle=1\) and same leading record coefficient, but their \(\langle N(N-1)\rangle\) are 0 and 1. They need not agree in higher-order cosine terms, which (4)'s error does not resolve. This example concerns the leading mean, not an impossibility of tomography or nonlinear processing of a richer observable family.

Nontrivial untruncated coherent/squeezed families and thermal densities generally lie outside a fixed finite-Hermite span. Fixed finite truncations are within this theorem; an untruncated or cutoff-growing family needs additional tail and uniformity estimates. The finite examples above already settle the stated general-calibration question without importing those extensions.

## 4. Spatial summation and time averaging

The exact finite-mode identity
\(\sum_p d_{p\alpha}d_{p\beta}=\Omega_\alpha\delta_{\alpha\beta}/2\)
follows from \(C^TC=D_c\). Therefore

\[
 \sum_p\mathcal R_{p,\rho}(s)
 =\sum_\alpha\Omega_\alpha\langle N_\alpha\rangle
  +\operatorname{Re}\sum_\alpha\Omega_\alpha
            e^{-2i\omega_\alpha s}\langle a_\alpha^2\rangle.    \tag{7}
\]

Spatial summation removes cross-mode terms in this real eigenbasis but not every anomalous term. The one-mode negative example survives the full sum. For number-invariant states, (7) equals \(\tau_*\langle H_h-E_{\rm vac}\rangle\). It is energy-weighted occupation, not an unweighted photon number. If occupations lie in a band \(\Omega_-\le\Omega_\alpha\le\Omega_+\), its mean lies between \(\Omega_-\langle N\rangle\) and \(\Omega_+\langle N\rangle\). A scalar number calibration is exact on a monochromatic eigenspace, or approximate in a declared narrow band. Summing means of the different legal mark-pair probes is not itself a theorem about an unrestricted total count.

For local averaging, let \(m_{\alpha\beta}=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho a_\alpha a_\beta)\),
\(\nu_{\alpha\beta}=\omega_\alpha+\omega_\beta>0\), and
\(F_T(\nu)=(1-e^{-i\nu T})/(i\nu T)\). The unweighted harmonic average obeys

\[
 \overline{\mathcal R}_{p,T}-2\overline{\langle b_p^\dagger b_p\rangle}_T
 =2\operatorname{Re}\sum_{\alpha\beta}
       d_{p\alpha}d_{p\beta}m_{\alpha\beta}F_T(\nu_{\alpha\beta}),           \tag{8}
\]

whose absolute value is bounded by
\(2\sum|d_{p\alpha}d_{p\beta}m_{\alpha\beta}|
 \min\{1,2/(\nu_{\alpha\beta}T)\}\).
At fixed finite graph this tends to zero. Starting the interval at s0 adds only phase factors to these coefficients. Applying (4) requires first taking the weak-field limit at each fixed T; the allowed parent supplies no error uniform for T growing with g. Taking T to infinity afterward is a statement about the harmonic coefficient, not a proved long-time physical detector.

There is a further distinction for the **actual** first-event statistic. Its natural weight is \(e^{-\lambda s}\). Normalizing that weight over [0,T] replaces F_T by

\[
 F_{\lambda,T}(\nu)=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+i\nu}
             \frac{1-e^{-(\lambda+i\nu)T}}{1-e^{-\lambda T}}.
                                                                    \tag{9}
\]

For fixed positive lambda its limit is \(\lambda/(\lambda+i\nu)\), generally nonzero. Merely extending the raw first-event observation interval therefore does not eliminate the anomalous terms. Dividing out survival at the level of inferred densities, reweighting recorded trials, or assuming \(\lambda\ll\omega_{\min}\) would be further calibration/protocol hypotheses. Reweighting can amplify sampling noise and is not a free change to the physical event law. Number-invariant preparations do not require this averaging remedy.

## 5. A conservative positive-bin version and its domains

For completeness the boundary coefficient can be realized as a limit of positive-bin probabilities using only the permitted Hamiltonian and jumps. The following conservative estimate applies to the entire finite-Hermite class; it makes no claim of optimal lag scaling. It reuses the elementary graph-norm method employed in earlier checker work, restated here from the allowed Hamiltonian rather than imported as a new author result.

On the initial quotient write \(H_g=KQ_E+V_g\), \(Q_E=-\Delta\),
\(V_g=(\tau_*g^2)^{-1}\sum_p(1-\cos(c_p\cdot A))\ge0\).
The parent residual estimate gives \(\|H_g\phi_g(0)\|\le C/\tau_*\).
Self-adjoint evolution preserves this norm and its domain. Integration by parts on smooth packets gives

\[
 \|H_g\phi\|^2=K^2\|Q_E\phi\|^2+\|V_g\phi\|^2
 +2K\int V_g|\nabla\phi|^2-K\int(\Delta V_g)|\phi|^2.
\]

Since \(K\|\Delta V_g\|_\infty\le C/\tau_*^2\), graph-core approximation yields
\(\|Q_E\phi_g(s)\|\le Cg^{-2}\),
\(\|\nabla\phi_g(s)\|\le Cg^{-1}\).
On all link angles, B_j is a finite smooth matter-matrix multiplier preserving Gauss. In each matter word \(D_{\rm mat}\) is a diagonal polynomial in electric momenta of degree at most two, with fixed coefficients. The product rule and the initial quotient's pullback to the full torus imply
\(B_j\phi_g(s)\in D(D_{\rm mat})\) and
\(\|D_{\rm mat}B_j\phi_g(s)\|\le Cg^{-2}\).
No postbirth winding restriction is required. Finite physical electric words and approximation in the full electric Sobolev graph norm justify this domain statement, including unconfined directions of \(D_{\rm mat}\).

The full no-event generator is
\(A_g=-i(KD_{\rm mat}+\delta H4)-\kappa\Lambda/2\), with the original bounded H4 and **all** channel losses. Thus
\(\|A_g B_j\phi_g(s)\|\le C/(\tau_*g^2)\).
Its contraction semigroup, on these domain vectors, satisfies
\(\|e^{uA_g}\eta-\eta\|\le u\|A_g\eta\|\).
For the bounded effect \(M=B_l^\dagger B_l\), this bounds the difference of squared-effect expectations by \(Cu/(\tau_*g^2)\). The same estimate extends to the declared mixtures.

Let \(P_{\rho,g}(I,b)\) be the probability of precisely the specified first-two-mark event, first time in a fixed interval I, positive next lag at most b, and no intervening event. Then

\[
 \frac{P_{0,g}(I,b)-P_{\rho,g}(I,b)}{\kappa^2 b g^2}
 =\int_I e^{-\lambda s}\mathcal R_{p,\rho}(s)\,ds
       +O_T\!\left(g^2+\frac{b}{\tau_*g^4}\right).             \tag{10}
\]

Consequently \(b=o(\tau_*g^4)\) is one sufficient positive-bin realization for arbitrary states in the fixed span. This estimate does not apply a bare microscopic derivative or replace the full postbirth generator. It is intentionally not an extrapolation of a stronger lag bound proved for special centered state families: general finite-Hermite superpositions need not have even reference probability densities. Transfer of shrinking-bin coefficients to a microscopic labeled process requires the appropriate comparison and order of limits; it is not supplied by unconditioned density convergence alone. No such new microscopic theorem is asserted here.

## 6. What comparison with absorption photodetection actually says

The primary comparison is [Glauber, Rev. Mod. Phys. 78, 1267 (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.78.1267), equations 10–16 and 18–20, and the ordering discussion on p. 1277. Under his idealized small, frequency-independent absorption detector assumptions, the lowering field annihilates vacuum; the single response is a density expectation of \(E^-E^+\), and the pair response uses \(E^-E^-E^+E^+\). Their order follows from absorption amplitudes and summing final states. The paper explicitly distinguishes other measurement orderings. These are the comparator's assumptions, not a conclusion that every possible detector must be this idealization.

Here the first formation has nonzero effect 5I even on the prepared vacuum. The selected pair has effect at least 21I even on vacuum or a one-quantum packet. Under an identification of the Hermite quanta with photons, these raw specified events cannot literally be the ideal single and pair absorption events: the corresponding ideal vacuum response, and ideal two-annihilator response on at most one quantum, are zero. This is an operator/statistic mismatch for the stated geometry and preparation, not an assertion about all possible detector constructions from the model.

For a generous mean comparison one may **choose** an ideal detector with lowering kernel proportional to b_p(s). Even then (6) agrees with its mean only under the stated moment restriction or controlled averaging. A laboratory electric-dipole detector is not automatically such a detector: the record weights are plaquette/curl, direction and polarization dependent. Its spatial profile, frequency response, mode normalization and coupling constant must be identified separately. A calibrated signed deficit is not a positive absorption event; matching a restricted mean supplies neither the measurement backaction nor the normal-ordered higher counting moments. Vacuum subtraction alone does not correct this distinction.

## 7. Empirical benchmark: what Brouri et al. measured

[Brouri et al., quant-ph/0007032v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0007032v1) report continuous excitation of an N-V center, confocal collection, a 50/50 splitter and two avalanche photodiodes, with timing electronics and filters against optical cross-talk. Their raw histogram is normalized as \(C_N=c/(N_1N_2wT)\). They independently estimate the signal fraction \(\rho_s=S/(S+B)\), check a flat off-center background, and use an uncorrelated-background assumption to obtain
\(g^{(2)}=[C_N-(1-\rho_s^2)]/\rho_s^2\).
The corrected zero-delay dip is therefore not a claim of zero raw coincidences. At their listed fraction 0.34, corrected zero corresponds to raw normalized 0.8844. These are finite-time, detector-specific fluorescence measurements, not a statistic conditioned on the first two events of a finite system with the prescribed all-A initial pattern.

This benchmark sets measurement obligations; it is not data to fit. The record's common pair background has not been proved to be a separately emitted, independent Poisson optical background. Brouri's subtraction formula cannot simply be applied to it. No source/detector mapping or matched experiment is given, so disagreement of the two differently defined statistics is not an experimental falsification of the supplied framework. Physical pumping, collection, reset/replenishment, response, timing and background models remain necessary.
