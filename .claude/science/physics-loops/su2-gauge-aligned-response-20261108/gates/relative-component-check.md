### Independent reconstruction of the decisive perturbation step

Treat the finitely many background values of one factor as a parameter p of size O(epsilon). Put

    D_p(q) = h_p(q)-h_p(I)-h_0(q)+h_0(I).

Here h_0(I)=0. Global Lipschitz dependence on p gives |D_p(q)|<=C epsilon uniformly for every compact q. In a fixed identity cap, the derivative at I is O(epsilon), and the x-Hessian of D_p(exp(ix)) is O(epsilon): integrate a mixed third derivative along the small background-parameter segment, entirely where |M|>=1/2. Taylor's formula therefore leaves an O(epsilon sum |x_e|^2) remainder after its linear term.

The global function b(q)=Im(q) satisfies b(exp(ix))=x+O(|x|^3). Replacing the local linear term by the derivative paired with b(q) preserves that remainder bound. Outside the fixed cap, at least one chord-square is at least a fixed delta^2. The already established global bound and the bounded proxy then give the same inequality after increasing the constant. This also covers q=-I, where the proxy is zero but the chord-square is four, and factors with M=0.

Summing these factor inequalities is legitimate because the proxy is the same function of a given coordinate in every factor. The summed derivative is the derivative of the full energy difference at q=I and is exactly zero. Hence the relative error is quadratic in chord distance globally, despite possible nonsmoothness of the mean norms at zero. Combining it with the independently checked global compact lower bound proves (2). The stationary construction and this cancellation use the same leading energy; adding radial-factor derivatives to only one side would invalidate the cancellation.

## Symmetry scope addendum

The supervisor's simultaneous-conjugation observation is correct for the full law. At terminal V=I, simultaneous U_l -> g U_l g* preserves probability Haar, the Wilson action, all normalized kernel factors, and the fixed terminal field; each path mean transforms as M -> g M g*. At I a terminal tangent is purely imaginary, so the insertion is

    A_c(v) = kappa u v dot Im(M_1,c).

The vector Im(M_1,c) transforms in the adjoint SO(3) representation, which has no nonzero invariant vector. If F and G are invariant under simultaneous conjugation, every scalar-invariant weighted expectation with one such vector insertion vanishes. In particular kappa_3(F,G,A_c(v))=0. This argument can be made on the full original chain and does not discard an orbit covariance.

The rooted forest transforms covariantly under simultaneous conjugation, and d(a_e,I) is conjugation invariant. Hence the chord-defined component events are invariant too. For invariant F,G, their two-source/one-boundary coefficient kappa_4(1_A,F,G,A_c) vanishes, including under the invariant real-source tilts. The general bounds still apply to bounded quotient tests lacking this extra global invariance; the vanishing is a scope constraint for singlet applications, not a defect in the estimates.

For distinct terminal links c and d, with sources independent of V,

    D_c D_d Cov(F,G) = kappa_4(F,G,A_c,A_d),

because the raw score A_c has no dependence on V_d. Conjugation allows an invariant rank-two color tensor proportional to delta_ab and therefore does not force this response to vanish. A same-link second derivative also has a derivative-of-score term and cannot be replaced by that fourth cumulant alone. For an event probability with two source derivatives and two distinct boundary derivatives, the corresponding expression has the event indicator as a fifth cumulant argument. Nonidentity backgrounds are another possible setting for nonzero one-boundary response; no bound at those backgrounds is supplied by the new component theorem.
