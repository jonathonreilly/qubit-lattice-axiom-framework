## 1. Definitions inferred by the distiller

Use LW1's law and notation. In particular \(S\in\{1/2,1,3/2,\ldots\}\), \(a_o=\varepsilon+\mu/2>0\), and \(\operatorname{Ad}V(z)=VzV^*\). Fix an even cell \(A=b+\{0,1\}^3\). Let \(\mathcal A_A=\mathfrak A_A^m\otimes\mathfrak L_A\), where the factors are the eight-mode CAR algebra and the twelve internal-link matrix algebras. Let \(\mathcal O_A\) be the algebra generated by all modes outside \(A\) and all links not internal to \(A\). Then \(\mathcal A_A\) and \(\mathcal O_A\) generate the quasi-local algebra. Matter factors across the cut are graded; links commute with matter.

A **frame** is a *-automorphism \(\varphi\) of the quasi-local algebra that maps \(\mathcal A_A\) onto itself and fixes \(\mathcal O_A\) pointwise. It need not preserve number or parity by assumption, and may entangle matter with internal links. A **register change** is \(\operatorname{Ad}L\), with \(L\) a unitary on finitely many links. It is **split at \(A\)** if \(L=L^{\rm in}\otimes L^{\rm out}\), supported on internal and non-internal links respectively. Link-by-link changes are split. A cube-local change for an odd cube is split, since that cube has no internal link of \(A\).

For each \(x\in A\), let \(O_x\) be the odd cube containing it. The **odd-gate hypothesis** requires, for each \(x\), a split \(L_x\) and a real \(a_x\ne0\) such that
\[
 (\varphi\circ\operatorname{Ad}L_x)(e^{ia_xH_{O_x}})=e^{ia_xH_{O_x}}.
\]
This is weaker than invariance of all cube gates with changes on their own links. The law uses \(a_x=a_o\). The wider reading **R-M** permits an outside matter-and-link automorphism as compensation. **R-E** permits a register unitary entangling internal and outside links. **R-T** asks only for invariance of the base tick.

Let \(\gamma_e=\operatorname{Ad}M_e^*\), \(\gamma_o=\operatorname{Ad}M_o^*\), \(\gamma_E=\operatorname{Ad}W_E^*\), and \(\alpha=\gamma_e\gamma_o\gamma_E\); compositions act right to left. At infinite volume these mean LW1 E1's automorphisms, not infinite unitary elements. **Hypothesis T** requires a split register change with \(\psi=\varphi\circ\operatorname{Ad}L\) and \(\psi\alpha=\alpha\psi\). On a finite even torus of side at least four this is equivalent to \(\psi(U_0)=cU_0\), \(|c|=1\), since the centre of the full matrix algebra is scalar.

For \(\lambda_x=e^{i\theta_x}\), put \(D_\lambda=\prod_xe^{-i\theta_xn_x}\), so \(\operatorname{Ad}D_\lambda(c_x)=\lambda_xc_x\). A mode image is asserted only when the frame preserves the mode algebra and acts linearly on its annihilators.

For a finite set \(F\) of sites, \(z\) has \(F\)-charge \(k\) if \(\operatorname{Ad}e^{i\theta N_F}(z)=e^{ik\theta}z\) for all real \(\theta\). Thus \(c_y\) has \(\{y\}\)-charge \(-1\) and \(c_y^\dagger\) has \(+1\). (referee closure, B5B_REFEREE.md)

## 2. Local algebra and gate lemmas

**Lemma 2.1 (cube geometry).** Each \(O_x\) meets \(A\) exactly at \(x\); the eight \(O_x\) are distinct and have no link internal to \(A\).

*Proof.* The odd tiling partitions the sites. In each coordinate an even interval \(\{2k,2k+1\}\) and an odd interval \(\{2j+1,2j+2\}\) intersect in at most one point. This proves the site assertions. An internal link has both endpoints in \(A\), so it cannot lie in an odd cube meeting \(A\) at one point. ∎

**Lemma 2.2 (local algebra).** A frame preserves the parity of \(\mathcal A_A\). Its restriction is \(\operatorname{Ad}V\) for a homogeneous unitary \(V\in\mathcal A_A\), unique up to phase. Conversely each homogeneous \(V\) extends to such a frame by its graded action on \(\mathcal A_A\) and identity on \(\mathcal O_A\). For disjoint finite matter-and-link algebras \(\mathcal C_1,\mathcal C_2\), if \(a_j,a,a'\in\mathcal C_1\), \(b_j,b,b'\in\mathcal C_2\), and the \(b_j\) are independent and all have the same parity, \(\sum_j a_jb_j=0\) implies \(a_j=0\). In particular, \(ab=a'b'\ne0\), with \(b,b'\) of the same parity, implies \(a'=\kappa a\), \(b'=\kappa^{-1}b\) for a nonzero scalar \(\kappa\). Every automorphism of a full matrix algebra is inner, and the commutant of \(M_n\otimes1\) in \(M_n\otimes M_k\) is \(1\otimes M_k\).

*Proof.* A local even or odd element respectively commutes or anticommutes with an outside \(c_y,c_y^*\). Since a frame fixes both, their CAR separate the two parity components of its image. For a *-automorphism \(\psi\) of \(M_n\), let \(f_{ij}=\psi(e_{ij})\). The projection \(f_{11}\) satisfies \(f_{11}M_nf_{11}=\psi(e_{11}M_ne_{11})=\mathbb C f_{11}\); a rank-\(r\) projection has corner algebra \(M_r\), so \(f_{11}\) has rank one. Choose a unit vector \(w\) in its range and set \(Ve_i=f_{i1}w\). Then \(\langle Ve_i,Ve_j\rangle=\langle w,f_{1i}f_{j1}w\rangle=\delta_{ij}\), so \(V\) is unitary. Moreover \(Ve_{ij}V^*(Ve_k)=\delta_{jk}f_{i1}w=f_{ij}f_{k1}w=f_{ij}(Ve_k)\), so \(Ve_{ij}V^*=f_{ij}\) and \(\psi=\operatorname{Ad}V\). (referee closure, B5B_REFEREE.md) Uniqueness follows from the scalar commutant. Parity preservation makes \(P_AVP_A\) a scalar multiple of \(V\); applying parity twice makes that scalar \(\pm1\). For even \(V\), \(\operatorname{Ad}V\) fixes \(\mathcal O_A\). For odd \(V\), let \(\theta_{\rm out}\) send outside modes to their negatives and fix the modes of \(A\) and all links. Then \(\operatorname{Ad}V\circ\theta_{\rm out}\) acts as \(\operatorname{Ad}V\) on \(\mathcal A_A\) and fixes \(\mathcal O_A\), since \(V\) anticommutes with outside modes. (referee closure, B5B_REFEREE.md)

For the splitting statements, order the modes of \(\mathcal C_1\) first in a Jordan–Wigner representation. An element \(b\) of parity \(p\) in \(\mathcal C_2\) becomes \(P_1^p\otimes\widehat b\), with an injective linear hat map. Thus \(ab=(aP_1^p)\otimes\widehat b\). Ordinary tensor independence and uniqueness of nonzero simple tensors give the claims. The commutant of \(M_n\otimes1\) in \(M_n\otimes M_k\) is \(1\otimes M_k\), by expansion in matrix units. ∎

**Lemma 2.3 (gate amplitudes).** For a cube \(C\), edge \(\{x,y\}\), nonzero real \(a\), and \(G=e^{iaH_C}\), there are one-particle states with nonzero \(\langle y|G|x\rangle\). Moreover
\[
 (1-n_x)G^*c_yG n_x\ne0,\qquad n_xGn_xG^*(1-n_x)\ne0.
\]
In particular both off-diagonal \(n_x\)-blocks of \(G\) are nonzero.

*Proof.* Put each unused link incident to a chosen path at the extremal weight that blocks hopping away from the path. Put each path link one step inside the extremum that its first hop moves toward: \(m=S-1\) when the path leaves through the link's source, \(m=-S+1\) when it leaves through the link's target, so the next hop has magnitude \(\sqrt{2S}\). The cube has no triangles; a two-edge path has no additional edge between its endpoints. The one-particle states on a path are therefore an invariant block, with signed real hopping magnitude \(\beta=\sqrt{2S/3}\). For the path \(x,y\), the transition amplitude is \(i\sigma\sin(a\beta)\). For \(x,y,z\), it is \(i\sigma_1\beta\sin(a\omega)/\omega\), where \(\omega=\sqrt2\beta\). Both cannot vanish for \(a\ne0\), by irrationality of \(\sqrt2\). The block matrices are symmetric, giving both directions.

For the first displayed inequality, apply \(c_yG\) to the initial \(|x\rangle\) in a block with nonzero transition. It leaves a nonzero empty-matter state, which \(G^*\) fixes. Write \(g_{vx}=\langle v|G|x\rangle\) for states \(v\) of the block. For the second, the relevant block matrix element of \(Gn_xG^*\) is \(g_{yx}\overline{g_{xx}}\). In the two-site block it vanishes only if \(\sin(2a\beta)=0\); in the three-site block \(g_{xx}=(1+\cos(a\omega))/2\), so it vanishes only if \(\sin(a\omega)=0\). Both equations cannot hold for nonzero \(a\), since \(\omega/(2\beta)=1/\sqrt2\) is irrational. Take the adjoint for the displayed orientation. ∎

**Lemma 2.4 (one-mode expansion).** For an algebra \(\mathcal C\) of finitely many other modes and links, each element \(z\) of the algebra generated by \(c_x\) and \(\mathcal C\) has a unique expansion
\[
 z=(1-n_x)P+n_xQ+c_x^\dagger X+c_xY,\qquad P,Q,X,Y\in\mathcal C.
\]
Moreover \((1-n_x)zn_x=c_xY\) and \(n_xz(1-n_x)=c_x^\dagger X\). If \(z\) is even or odd, \(P,Q\) have its parity and \(X,Y\) the opposite parity. If \(z\) has total charge \(k\) for \(x\) and a set \(F\) of other modes, the respective \(F\)-charges are \(k,k,k-1,k+1\).

*Proof.* Order \(x\) first in a Jordan–Wigner representation. The four terms occupy the four distinct \(2\times2\) matrix blocks, with an invertible parity factor on each coefficient. This gives existence and uniqueness. The even projection \(n_x\) commutes with \(\mathcal C\), and \(n_xc_x=0\), \(c_xn_x=c_x\), \(c_x^\dagger n_x=0\), \(n_xc_x^\dagger=c_x^\dagger\); projecting the expansion on either side gives the two block identities. (referee closure, B5B_REFEREE.md) Parity and number conjugations preserve each block and yield the stated charges. ∎

## 3. Split register changes

**Theorem 3.1 (B5b; odd-gate invariance).** Under the odd-gate hypothesis,
\[
 \varphi(c_x)=\lambda_xc_x,\qquad
 \varphi|_{\mathcal A_A}=\operatorname{Ad}(D_\lambda W)
\]
for phases \(\lambda_x\) and a unitary \(W\) on the internal links. Conversely every tuple in \(U(1)^8\) occurs with cube-local compensations for the base gates. The gauge unitary fixes every base gate and every plaquette monomial, with compensation on each object’s own links. Thus every continuous compact group of such frames has a closed abelian mode image contained in \(U(1)^8\); the image of the full compatible set is \(U(1)^8\).

*Proof.* Fix \(x\), put \(G=e^{ia_xH_{O_x}}\), and let \(N_{\rm out}\) count the other seven modes of \(O_x\). The frame and register change fix its spectral projections. Number conservation and Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4 show that the component of \(G\) lowering \(N_{\rm out}\) by one is the nonzero block \(n_xG(1-n_x)=c_x^\dagger X\), where \(X\) is odd and supported outside \(A\). Invariance gives
\[
 \varphi(c_x^\dagger)\operatorname{Ad}L_x^{\rm out}(X)=c_x^\dagger X.
\]
The internal part of \(L_x\) commutes with \(X\); the frame fixes its outside factor. Lemma 2.2 gives \(\varphi(c_x^\dagger)=\overline{\lambda_x}c_x^\dagger\), with \(|\lambda_x|=1\) by CAR. Repeat for every corner. The remaining automorphism \(\operatorname{Ad}D_\lambda^*\circ\varphi\) fixes the full matter matrix algebra. It restricts to an inner automorphism of its internal-link commutant by Lemma 2.2, yielding \(W\).

For the converse, extend \(\theta\) by zero outside \(A\). The product \(\prod_{x\in A}e^{-i\theta_x\mathcal G_x}\) factors into \(D_\lambda\), internal-link exponentials, and outside incident-link exponentials. Take the first two as the frame and, for each gate, the incident factors on its own links as its compensation. The gauge unitary \(\prod_{x\in A}e^{-i\theta_x\mathcal G_x}\) commutes with every base gate and every plaquette monomial (B4). Its factors on a gate's own links compensate that gate, and are cube-local; its factors on a plaquette's own links compensate that plaquette monomial. The electric gates commute with these link exponentials. ∎

**Corollary 3.2 (one site).** A frame on one mode satisfying the analogous split odd-gate hypothesis is a phase. The particle-hole branch of LW1 B3 is excluded without assuming number preservation.

*Proof.* Apply the first paragraph of Theorem 3.1 with the one-mode algebra in place of \(\mathcal A_A\). ∎

Generator invariance implies gate invariance. The converse follows when \(t\mapsto e^{iat}\) is injective on the finite spectrum of \(H_C\), by polynomial spectral calculus. It need not follow at spectral collisions. Theorem 3.1 uses the gate directly, so it holds for every nonzero angle, including any collisions.

**Theorem 3.3 (B5b-T; whole base tick).** Under Hypothesis T, \(\varphi|_{\mathcal A_A}=\operatorname{Ad}(D_\lambda W)\). Every \(\lambda\in U(1)^8\) occurs. This holds for all real \(a_e,\tau_E\), all allowed \(S\), and \(a_o\ne0\).

*Proof.* Absorb \(L^{\rm in}\) into \(\varphi\); restore it at the end as part of \(W\). Write the remaining outside-link unitary as \(L\). Set
\[
 \psi'=\gamma_E\psi\gamma_E^{-1},\quad
 \chi=\gamma_e^{-1}\psi\gamma_e,\quad
 \chi=\gamma_o\psi'\gamma_o^{-1}. \tag{3.1}
\]
The last identity follows from \(\psi\alpha=\alpha\psi\). Both \(\gamma_e\) and \(\gamma_E\) preserve \(\mathcal A_A\) and \(\mathcal O_A\): the even cube gate on \(A\) and its internal electric gates lie inside, while all other even cube gates and electric gates lie outside and are even. Consequently \(\psi'=\varphi'\operatorname{Ad}L'\), with the frame \(\varphi'=\gamma_E\varphi\gamma_E^{-1}\) and outside-link unitary \(L'=W_E^*LW_E\). Likewise \(\chi=\varphi_1\operatorname{Ad}R\), with the frame \(\varphi_1=\gamma_e^{-1}\varphi\gamma_e\) and even outside unitary \(R=M_eLM_e^*\). Here \(R\) can act on outside matter: the even-layer conjugation of an outside link uses only even cubes other than \(A\).

Fix \(x\in A\), a neighbour \(y\) of \(x\) in \(O_x\), and \(G=e^{ia_oH_{O_x}}\). Since \(\psi'(c_y)=c_y\), equation (3.1) makes \(\chi\) fix \(Z=G^*c_yG\). Expand \(Z\) as in Lemma 2.4, with \(F=O_x\setminus\{x\}\). Its total charge is \(-1\); hence \(P,Q,X,Y\) have \(F\)-charges \(-1,-1,-2,0\). The coefficient \(Y\) is nonzero by Lemma 2.3. The outside factor of \(\chi\) may change matter, but preserves parity. In a Jordan–Wigner order with \(A\) first, apply an outside linear functional that is one on \(\widehat Y\) and zero on the charge \(-1\) and \(-2\) spaces. Taking the odd cell component of \(\chi(Z)=Z\) shows that \(c_x\) lies in the span of \(d=\varphi_1(c_x)\) and \(d^\dagger\). Taking adjoints and using CAR gives \(d=uc_x+vc_x^\dagger\), with \(uv=0\) and \(|u|^2+|v|^2=1\). Thus \(\varphi_1(n_x)\) is \(n_x\) or \(1-n_x\).

Now put \(Z'=Gn_xG^*\). From (3.1), \(\psi'(Z')\) is \(Z'\) or \(1-Z'\). Its Lemma 2.4 coefficient \(X'\) of \(c_x^\dagger\) has \(F\)-charge \(-1\), is odd, and is nonzero by Lemma 2.3. Project the identity onto \(F\)-charge \(-1\). Since \(L'\) acts only on links, this gives
\[
 \varphi'(c_x^\dagger)\operatorname{Ad}L'(X')=\pm c_x^\dagger X'.
\]
Lemma 2.2 and CAR give \(\varphi'(c_x)=\lambda_xc_x\). Repeat for all corners and use the commutant argument in Theorem 3.1. Conjugating back through the internal electric layer changes only \(W\), since that layer commutes with \(D_\lambda\). The converse follows from the gauge transformations in Theorem 3.1, which fix each layer. ∎

**Proposition 3.4 (odd half-step).** If a split register change makes a frame commute with \(\gamma_o\gamma_E\), the same phase conclusion holds. Conversely all phase tuples occur. An even-half-step invariant frame can act on matter beyond site phases.

*Proof.* Absorb the internal register factor. The remaining composite fixes \(c_y\), hence fixes \(Z=G^*c_yG\) as above. Its outside part is a link-only change, which preserves \(F\)-charge. The charge-zero component of \(Z\) is \(c_xY\), with \(Y\ne0\). Splitting \(\varphi(c_x)\operatorname{Ad}L(Y)=c_xY\) gives a phase. Theorem 3.1 supplies the converse. For the last assertion, the even cube gate \(G_A\) implements a frame and commutes with its own even layer. Lemma 2.3 shows it does not preserve \(n_x\), so it cannot send every \(c_x\) to a site phase. ∎

## 4. Wider compensation readings

**Theorem 4.1 (R-M).** Suppose each odd gate admits a compensating outside matter-and-link automorphism, and the even gate of \(A\), at a nonzero angle, admits a register change on internal links. Require each outside compensation \(\kappa_x\) to preserve parity, so that \(\varphi\) on \(\mathcal A_A\) and \(\kappa_x\) on \(\mathcal O_A\) together define an automorphism of the quasi-local algebra. (referee closure, B5B_REFEREE.md) Then either \(\varphi(c_x)=\lambda_xc_x\) for every corner or \(\varphi(c_x)=\lambda_xs_xc_x^\dagger\) for every corner, where \(s_x=(-1)^{x_1+x_2+x_3}\). Both occur. Their mode transformations form \(U(1)^8\rtimes\mathbb Z_2\), with the nontrivial element inverting the phases.

*Proof.* Fix an odd gate \(G\). Expand it as in Lemma 2.4. Its diagonal coefficients have outside charge zero, and its two off-diagonal coefficients have charges \(-1,+1\) and are nonzero by Lemma 2.3. The outside compensation and frame preserve parity: their joint automorphism fixes the CAR relations across the cut. Apply outside functionals selecting the two nonzero charge components in the invariance identity, then take the odd cell component. As in the first part of Theorem 3.3, \(\varphi(c_x)\) lies in the span of \(c_x,c_x^\dagger\), so CAR permits only a phase or a particle-hole map.

Let \(F\) be the set of flipped corners. The even gate conserves \(N_A\), and invariance makes it also conserve \(N_{A\setminus F}-N_F\). Thus it conserves \(N_F\). If \(F\) is nonempty and proper, a cube edge crosses its boundary; Lemma 2.3 gives a one-particle transition across that edge, contradicting conservation. Hence \(F\) is empty or all of \(A\).

For the second branch, apply \(c_x\mapsto s_xc_x^\dagger\) on all modes of a cube and \(R=e^{-i\pi S^x}\) on each of its links. Since adjacent signs multiply to \(-1\), \(RJR^*=J^\dagger\), and CAR swaps each dressed hop with its adjoint, every cube Hamiltonian is fixed. Use this on \(A\) and separately on each \(O_x\), with outside matter included in the odd-gate compensation. The phase branch follows from B4. Conjugation by particle-hole reverses the phases, giving the semidirect product. ∎

R-M is a wider mathematical reading: its compensation changes neighbouring matter, not only registers. Its discrete non-abelian extension does not mix distinct modes. It does not establish a non-abelian Lie algebra of mode mixing.

**Theorem 4.2 (B5b-E; R-E for odd gates).** Without the split condition, odd-gate compatibility is equivalent to
\[
 \varphi|_{\mathcal A_A}=\operatorname{Ad}\!\left(W_0\prod_{x\in A}e^{-in_xh_x}\right),
\]
where \(W_0\) is an internal-link unitary and the \(h_x\) are pairwise commuting internal-link Hermitian operators. Such a frame fixes every \(n_x\) and sends \(c_x\) to \(c_xW_0e^{ih_x}W_0^*\).

*Proof.* As in Theorem 3.1, invariance fixes the nonzero inflow \(c_x^\dagger X\). Expand \(X=\sum_jm_jk_j\) in independent odd outside-matter elements and link coefficients. The frame and every register change fix the \(m_j\), so Lemma 2.2 gives \(\psi(c_x^\dagger k_j)=c_x^\dagger k_j\). Choose a nonzero \(k_j\). Expanding its image under the register unitary in independent outside-link coefficients shows \(\varphi^{-1}(c_x^\dagger)=c_x^\dagger v_x\) for an internal-link operator \(v_x\). CAR make \(v_x\) unitary. Thus \(\varphi^{-1}(c_x)=c_xu_x\), with unitary \(u_x\). CAR between different corners make the \(u_x\) commute.

Let \(\varphi^{-1}=\operatorname{Ad}V\) on \(\mathcal A_A\). It fixes every \(n_x\), so \(V=\sum_\sigma P_\sigma V_\sigma\). Comparing the block of \(Vc_xV^*=c_xu_x\) from occupation \(\sigma\) to \(\sigma-e_x\) gives \(V_{\sigma-e_x}V_\sigma^*=u_x\). Hence \(V_\sigma=(\prod_{x:\sigma_x=1}u_x^*)V_0\). Simultaneously diagonalize the commuting unitaries and write \(u_x=e^{-ih_x}\). This yields the displayed form with \(W_0=V_0^*\).

Conversely, write \(\mathcal G_x=n_x+E_x^A+E_x^o\), where \(E_x^o\) is the signed sum of the three odd-cube incident electric fields. Set \(L_x=e^{-ih_xE_x^o}\). It may entangle internal and outside links. The combined implementer is
\[
 W_0\prod_{z\ne x}e^{-in_zh_z}\,e^{-ih_x(n_x+E_x^o)}.
\]
All \(h_z,W_0,n_z\) for \(z\ne x\) commute with the odd gate, and B4 shows the gate commutes with \(n_x+E_x^o\), because its links exclude \(E_x^A\). Thus it fixes the gate. ∎

For example, take \(h_x=\theta E_m\) on an internal link \(m\), all other \(h_z=0\), and \(W_0=1\). If \(\theta\notin2\pi\mathbb Z\), the factor \(e^{i\theta E_m}\) is non-scalar. This closes B5b-E for the odd gates. The frame does not preserve the matter algebra, so it has no mode image as defined above. The set of R-E compatible frames is not closed under composition (RC5), so it is not a group.

**Proposition RC5.** Let \(x\ne x'\) be corners of \(A\) and \(m\) an internal link. Put \(\varphi_1=\operatorname{Ad}e^{-i\theta n_xE_m}\) and \(\varphi_2=\operatorname{Ad}e^{-i\theta' n_{x'}S^x_m}\), with \(S^x_m=(J_m+J_m^\dagger)/2\) and \(\theta=\theta'=\pi/2\). Each \(\varphi_j\) is R-E compatible for the odd gates. The composite \(\varphi_1\circ\varphi_2\) is not. So the set of R-E compatible frames is not closed under composition, and it is not a group.

*Proof.* Each \(\varphi_j\) has the displayed form of Theorem 4.2, with \(W_0=1\) and one nonzero operator: \(h_x=\theta E_m\) for \(\varphi_1\) and \(h_{x'}=\theta'S^x_m\) for \(\varphi_2\). By its converse, each is R-E compatible. The composite \(\varphi=\varphi_1\circ\varphi_2\) is a frame on \(A\). Theorem 4.2 gives \(\operatorname{Ad}e^{in_xh}(c_x)=c_xe^{-ih}\), so \(\varphi_1^{-1}(c_x)=c_xe^{-i\theta E_m}\). The unitary \(e^{i\theta'n_{x'}S^x_m}=(1-n_{x'})+n_{x'}e^{i\theta'S^x_m}\) is even, so \(\varphi_2^{-1}\) fixes \(c_x\). Hence
\[
\varphi^{-1}(c_x)=\varphi_2^{-1}\big(c_xe^{-i\theta E_m}\big)=c_x\big[(1-n_{x'})A+n_{x'}B\big],\qquad
A=e^{-i\theta E_m},\quad B=e^{i\theta'S^x_m}e^{-i\theta E_m}e^{-i\theta'S^x_m}.
\]
Suppose \(\varphi\) were R-E compatible. The first paragraph of the proof of Theorem 4.2 gives \(\varphi^{-1}(c_x)=c_xu\) with \(u\) an internal-link operator. Then \(c_x(1-n_{x'})\otimes(A-u)+c_xn_{x'}\otimes(B-u)=0\) in \(\mathfrak A^m_A\otimes\mathfrak L_A\). The matter elements \(c_x(1-n_{x'})\) and \(c_xn_{x'}\) are nonzero and linearly independent, so \(A=u=B\). But \(A\ne B\). The rotation identity \(e^{i\theta'S^x}S^ze^{-i\theta'S^x}=S^z\cos\theta'+S^y\sin\theta'\) gives \(B=e^{-i(\pi/2)S^y}\) and \(A=e^{-i(\pi/2)S^z}\), where \(S^z=E_m\). The operator \(A\) is diagonal in the basis \(|m\rangle\). The operator \(B\) is not: it maps \(S^z\) to \(\pm S^x\) under conjugation, while a diagonal operator commutes with \(S^z\). This holds for every \(S\). ∎ (referee closure, B5B_REFEREE.md)

**Lemma 4.3 (internal register commutant).** If an internal-link operator \(O\) commutes with \(H_A\), it is scalar.

*Proof.* Distinct ordered hopping operators \(c_y^\dagger c_x\) are linearly independent on the one-particle space. Therefore \([O,H_A]=0\) implies \([O,J_\ell]=[O,J_\ell^\dagger]=0\) on every internal link. Their commutator gives \(E_\ell\); its simple spectrum and the nonzero ladder coefficients generate the full link matrix algebra. Its commutant on all internal links is scalar. ∎

Lemma 4.3 explains why the R-E odd-gate construction does not directly fix the even generator. It does not rule out compensation at the even gate.
