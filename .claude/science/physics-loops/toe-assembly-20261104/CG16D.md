## 2. Flux identities

**Theorem 1 (relative-label invariance).** Quasi-equivalent representations have the same defined `χ_sp`. A direct sum of representations with the same defined value has that value.

*Proof.* The elements `s_ΛΦ_Λ` are uniformly bounded. The direct-sum result follows by convergence on finite-support vectors. Normal isomorphisms between the generated von Neumann algebras preserve bounded strong convergence; equivalently use the amplification argument of `RCS1.md` R2(a), already proved there for the same kind of net. □

**Theorem 2 (finite-region Gauss identity).** For GR registers,
\[
\prod_{x\in Λ}z_x=(-1)^{|Λ|}\left(\prod_{ℓ\in L_{int}(Λ)}d_ℓ\right)Φ_Λ.
\]
All factors commute and square to one. If every link is link-pure, `Φ_Λ=σ_Λ∏_{x∈Λ}z_x`, where `σ_Λ=(-1)^{|Λ|}∏_{int}d_ℓ∈{±1}`. It is then central in `𝔄^G` and fixed by every tick in `𝒰`.

*Proof.* The matter contribution to `z_x` is `exp(2πi(n_{x,1}−1/2))=−1`, so `z_x=−∏_{ℓ∋x}ε_{ℓ,x}`. Multiply over `Λ`: internal links supply both end factors, crossing links one. End actions commute. The `z_x` are central in `𝔄^G` and tick-fixed because they are functions of gauge generators; scalar `d_ℓ` gives the corollary. □

**Theorem 3 (so(20) registers).** For any `Spin(20)` register, `ε_{ℓ,x}=ε_{ℓ,y}=ρ_ℓ(−1_{Spin(20)})`, so `d_ℓ=1`.

*Proof.* In the Clifford realization a `2π` rotation in either end plane equals the same kernel element `−1` of `Spin(20)→SO(20)`. Apply `ρ_ℓ`. This is already proved in `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md`, Lemma K0(iii); the draft is not a frozen authority. The same fact is recorded in frozen `LW1_5.md` line 169: “For an `so(20)` link the two `ε`'s coincide.” □

**Theorem 4 (`L5-O[S_+]`).** Every `ε_{ℓ,x}=−1`, every `z_x=−1`, and every `Φ_Λ=1`. Every state is flux-sharp and every representation has `χ_sp=+1` relative to it. In every factor representation the local centre implementer has value `±i`; ratios between two factor values are `±1`.

*Proof.* The kernel element `−1` acts as `−1` on `S_+`. Theorem 2 gives `z_x=−(−1)^6=−1`. Since `6|Λ|=2|L_int(Λ)|+|∂Λ|`, the number of crossing links is even, so `Φ_Λ=1`. The relative-label statement follows. The factor statement follows from centrality and `c_x²=z_x=−1`, as already recorded in `RCS1.md` Theorem 1 and `LW1_5.md` Σ. □

Thus the flux-defined target sector does not exist on `S_+`, irrespective of string energy or dynamics. This does not decide a different identification of the CG1 representation.

## 3. Static labels on link-pure registers

Fix a flux-sharp background `ω₀`. Set `κ₀(x)=ω₀(z_x)∈{±1}`, `u_x=κ₀(x)z_x`, and `Z_Λ=∏_{x∈Λ}u_x=s_ΛΦ_Λ`. For `Λ={x}` there are no internal links, so Theorem 2 gives `Φ_{{x}}=−z_x`, and flux-sharpness gives `κ₀(x)∈{±1}`. As `z_x` is a self-adjoint unitary, `ω₀((z_x−κ₀(x))^2)=0`, so `π₀(z_x)Ω₀=κ₀(x)Ω₀` in the GNS space of `ω₀`. Hence `ω₀(∏_{x∈Λ}z_x)=∏_{x∈Λ}κ₀(x)` and `s_Λ=σ_Λ∏_{x∈Λ}κ₀(x)`. With `Φ_Λ=σ_Λ∏_{x∈Λ}z_x` this gives `s_ΛΦ_Λ=∏_{x∈Λ}κ₀(x)z_x=Z_Λ` (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md). For a configuration `κ`, let `D(κ)={x:κ(x)≠κ₀(x)}` and let `H_κ` be the joint eigenspace of all `π(z_x)` with eigenvalues `κ(x)`.

**Theorem 5 (support).** On link-pure registers, `χ_sp(π:ω₀)=−1` exactly when `H` is the orthogonal direct sum of the `H_κ` with `D(κ)` finite and odd.

*Proof.* For the forward direction suppose `π(Z_Λ)→−I` strongly. For a unit vector `v` and `δ>0`, choose finite `Λ₀` so `‖(π(Z_Λ)+I)v‖<δ` whenever `Λ⊇Λ₀`. For finite `F` disjoint from `Λ₀`, and every `F'⊆F`, the identity `Z_{F'}=Z_{Λ₀∪F'}Z_{Λ₀}` gives `‖(I−π(Z_{F'}))v‖<2δ`. Averaging over `F'` shows that `E_F=∏_{x∈F}(I+π(u_x))/2` obeys `‖(I−E_F)v‖²<2δ`. The decreasing limit over finite `F` outside `Λ₀` projects onto vectors with background labels outside `Λ₀`. As `δ→0`, these projections span `H`. Split each such subspace into the joint eigenspaces on the finite set `Λ₀`. Thus only finite differences occur. An even difference gives eventual eigenvalue `+1`, hence its subspace vanishes. Conversely, on each finite odd difference `Z_Λ` is eventually `−I`; boundedness extends convergence from finite sums to all of `H`. □

**Theorem 6 (local creation and flat bands).** Under the same hypotheses:

(i) If a state of `𝔄` extends `ω₀`, any nonzero state produced from its GNS vector by a local or quasi-local `B∈𝔄` generates an `𝔄^G` representation with `χ_sp=+1`.

(ii) If `ω₀` is also even-translation invariant, every band in any separable covariant representation with `χ_sp=−1` satisfies `V=1` on a neighbourhood of each node. Hence every `N_j=0`.

(iii) The same neighbourhood-flatness conclusion holds in a separable covariant representation spanned by joint centre eigenvectors whose configurations differ on a finite nonempty set from some cell-periodic configuration.

*Proof.* (i) For local `B`, choose `S` containing its matter support and both ends of each supported link. `∏_{x∈S}z_x=σ_SΦ_S` commutes with `B`, since the boundary links do not meet its support. For `x∉S`, the site factor of `z_x` is the scalar `−1` and its end factors act on links at `x`; none is in the support of `B`, since every supported link has both ends in `S` (`LW1_5.md` Theorem SC(b)). Thus for every `Λ⊇S`, `Z_Λ` commutes with `B` (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md) and fixes the background vector. It also commutes with `𝔄^G`, so it fixes the cyclic subspace. Norm approximation extends this to quasi-local `B`, with strong convergence on the cyclic subspace. This recovers the pairing obstruction already in `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` (K5) and frozen `LW1_5.md` line 171 (F and K) and extends the local static-label result of `LW1_5.md` Theorem SC to the stated sector representation.

(ii) Theorem 5 gives an orthogonal sum over finite odd defects. The tick preserves each configuration; translations shift it by `2β`. The least point `p` of each finite defect obeys `p(β·κ)=p(κ)+2β`. Apply `RCS1.md` Theorem 5 with `q_j=p_j/2` and Lemma N below. (iii) A cell-periodic configuration equal to `κ` outside a finite set is unique: two distinct periodic configurations differ on infinitely many translated sites. Its nonempty defect has the same translation rule. Apply the same theorem. □

**Lemma N (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md) (unrefereed).** Let `(H,π,T,Ũ)` be a separable covariant representation with `H=⊕_{c∈C}H_c`, `ŨH_c=H_c`, `T_βH_c=H_{β·c}`, and real functions `q_1,q_2,q_3` on `C` with `q_j(β·c)=q_j(c)+β_j`. *Proof.* Step 1 (boosted bands; the proofs of `RCS1.md` Theorem 5 and Lemma 5.1). Fix `j`. Put `B_t=Σ_c e^{itq_j(c)}Π_c`. It is unitary and commutes with `Ũ`, since each `Π_c` does. Step 2 (no variation along `e_j` anywhere in `B`). Put `h=i log V` on `B`. It is `C¹` and Hermitian, with spectrum in `(−π/2,π/2)`, and `V=e^{−ih}`. Suppose `∂_j tr(h^p)≠0` at some `K₁∈B`, for some `1≤p≤d`. By continuity it is nonzero on a ball `B₁∋K₁`. The number of distinct eigenvalues of `h` is lower semicontinuous, so it is constant on a smaller ball `B₂⊂B₁`. There the distinct eigenvalues `λ_1<…<λ_r` have constant multiplicities `m_i`, and they and their Riesz projections `P_i` are `C¹`. Since `tr(h^p)=Σ_im_iλ_i^p`, some `∂_jλ_i` is nonzero at a point of `B₂`. Choose a closed ball `B₃` and `t_*>0` with `B₃+[0,t_*]e_j⊂B₂` and `∂_jλ_i≠0` on that set. Then `t↦λ_i(K+te_j)` is strictly monotone on `[0,t_*]` for each `K∈B₃`. Let `X_t` be `G_t` restricted to `L²(B₃; ran P_i(·+te_j))`. It is a nonzero isometry with `T_βX_t=X_tM_β` and `ŨX_t=X_tM_{w_t}`, where `w_t(K)=e^{−iλ_i(K+te_j)}` is scalar. `RCS1.md` Lemma 5.1's proof gives, for such `X`, "`P(Γ)X=XM_{1_Γ(K,w(K))}` for every Borel `Γ`", with `P` the joint spectral measure of `(T_{e_1},T_{e_2},T_{e_3},Ũ)`. So the ranges of `X_t`, `t∈[0,t_*]`, are nonzero and pairwise orthogonal. Uncountably many such subspaces contradict separability. Hence `∂_j tr(h^p)=0` on all of `B`.

Step 3. Step 2 holds for `j=1,2,3`. So each `tr(h^p)`, `1≤p≤d`, is constant on the convex set `B` and equals `tr(h(K₀)^p)=0`. By Newton's identities the characteristic polynomial of `h(K)` is `λ^d`. A Hermitian matrix with only the eigenvalue `0` is `0`. So `h(K)=0` and `V(K)=1`. □

**Proposition C-NN (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md) (unrefereed).** Read NN-a with `H_ℓ=Q⊗Q`. For every real `θ`, the automorphism of `W_K(θ)=∏_ye^{iθh_y}` is a composition of `64L` NN-a layers, where `L` is the length of one Lemma U word for `e^{iθh_0}`. *Proof.* (1) The six links of the star of `y` form a connected set `L′`. The set `S′` of sites shared by two of them is `{y}`. `e^{iθh_y}` is a unitary on `⊗_{ℓ∋y}H_ℓ`; it is the identity on the far-end factors and on all matter. Lemma U writes `e^{iθh_y}⊗1` as a finite word of unitaries in the algebras `𝒢_{y,ℓ}`, `ℓ∋y`. Fix one word for `y=0` and use its translate at every `y`. This is valid because `h_y` is the translate of `h_0`: the register and `K` are the same at every site.
(2) Each letter is an NN-a gate for its link `ℓ=(y,y′)`. It is a unitary in `𝒜_y^+⊗B(H_ℓ)`, so it is supported within `F_y` and `H_ℓ`. It commutes with `P_y`, being even at `y`, and with `P_{y′}`, an even element at another site; links commute with matter. So `[g,P_yP_{y′}]=0`.
(3) The word at `y` uses edge triples whose sites lie within distance 1 of `y`. If `y−y′∈4Z³∖{0}`, then `‖y−y′‖_∞≥4`, so the two words use disjoint site sets and hence disjoint edge triples. So for each class `c∈Z³/4Z³` the `k`-th letters of the words at all `y∈c` form one NN-a layer, invariant under `4Z³`. All words have length `L`.
(4) The gates `e^{iθh_y}` commute pairwise (Theorem 8(iii)). So `W_K(θ)=∏_c∏_{y∈c}e^{iθh_y}` for any order of the 64 classes, and each class product is the parallel run of its words, in `L` layers. On each local observable only finitely many gates act, so the operator identities give the identity of automorphisms (`LW1.md` E1, as in the proof of `GA1_5.md` Proposition C). The count `64L` does not depend on the volume. □

This is the scheduling of `GA1_5.md` Proposition C(ii) with `k=2`. Status: `GA1_5.md` line 11 lists Lemma U and Proposition C among "Referee closures, unrefereed". C-NN inherits that status.

**What C-NN does not give.** The letters are not gauge invariant; NN-a allows this. The complete layer fixes every local gauge generator, since each `e^{iθh_y}` is gauge invariant. Cell covariance of the complete update is Lemma CV below. C-NN covers the transfer layer only. **Lemma CV (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md) (unrefereed).** Let `g∈𝒢` have linear part `R` with `Re_i=σ_ie_{π(i)}`, `σ_i=±1`. Put `J(g)={π(i):σ_i=−1}` and `s_g(x)=(−1)^{Σ_{j∈J(g)}x_j}`. Define the D-reg lift `α̂_g` as the lift of `LW1_5.md` line 264 with two changes: on a reversed link the bare swap replaces the Spin(20) swap, and the matter sign `d_g(gx)` is multiplied by `s_g(gx)`. Then the D-reg base tick at `τ_E=0`, with or without the transfer layer appended, commutes with every `α̂_g`, and `α̂_g` maps Gauss generators to Gauss generators.

*Proof.* `g` reverses exactly the links of the directions `i` with `σ_i=−1`. Their images are all the links of the directions in `J(g)`. For a link `(x,x+e_j)`, `s_g(x)s_g(x+e_j)=−1` if `j∈J(g)` and `+1` otherwise. So the extra signs give exactly one factor `−1` on the image of each reversed hop, and no factor elsewhere. Each hop then maps to its image hop with the same sign as on `S_+`, and the cube layers map as in the proof at `LW1_5.md` line 264. At `τ_E=0` there is no electric layer. The matter signs cancel in `M^{cd}_x`, and the bare lift maps `L^{cd}_{ℓ,z}` to `L^{cd}_{gℓ,gz}`; so Gauss generators go to Gauss generators. The transfer layer acts on links only. Matter signs commute with it, and the bare lift maps it to itself. □

The proof ports `LW1_5.md` line 264 (frozen) for the base tick. The only new steps are the sign computation and `s_g`. It needs `τ_E=0`: `LW1_5.md` line 92 says "A more general register and an invariant electric operator require their own covariance check." With C-NN, the transfer layer on D-reg meets NN-a's decomposition, evenness, gauge and covariance conditions, read with `H_ℓ=Q⊗Q`, provided the base tick's own gates meet NN-a. That proviso is not checked here.

**Theorem 9 (singlets and strings on the wider register).** At every site choose exactly one incident end in `H`, all others in `|0⟩`, and couple that end and the matter spin module to a local singlet. There are two singlets, one per matter parity. For a finite site set `Y`, let `𝒮_Y` be the span, in the space of the matter at `Y` and the `Y`-end factors of the links at `Y`, of the product vectors with exactly one `H`-end per site, singlet-coupled to that site's matter, all other ends `|0⟩`. Each `h_y`, `y∈Y`, maps `𝒮_Y` into itself; so does each hop on a link with both ends in `Y`, tensored with the far ends. On `𝒮_Y`, `z_y=+1` for `y∈Y`. The D1 state and string states are product states whose local vectors lie in such spaces (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md). The end choices are arrows; `d_ℓ=−1` exactly when one end of `ℓ` has an arrow. A columnar matching, with a fixed eigenvector of definite pair parity `P_xP_y` of each paired-link hop and electric time `τ_E=0`, gives a pure locally gauge-invariant D1 state for the base tick. A half-column shift gives a weak-* string-limit state with a single unmatched link and `χ_sp=−1` relative to that state.

*Proof.* The spin modules decompose as `16⊕16̄`; `16⊗16̄` and its reverse each have one singlet, while equal chiralities have none. One spinor end gives `z_x=−(−1)=+1`. The transfer is the identity on the moved spinor and preserves the singlet; the hop vanishes unless both ends of its link are spinors, and otherwise preserves the two local singlet spaces by gauge invariance. An eigenvector of definite pair parity exists because the hop commutes with `P_xP_y`; its local state is even, since odd operators take it to the orthogonal opposite-parity space. The product is therefore of even pure states on disjoint finite blocks (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md). On a columnar matching, occupied links are pairwise disjoint. A direction-1 link belongs to an even-tiling cube exactly when its base coordinate `x_1` is even (`LW1_5.md` line 216), so every two-arrow link lies in an even cube (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md). The even-cube gate factors into commuting occupied-link gates; odd-cube hops vanish. A product of their eigenvectors is pure, stationary and invariant under even translations and local gauge transformations, hence D1. A finite shifted segment has two unmatched links; its local state is obtained by a local map from the matching state. A half-infinite shift leaves one unmatched link and is the local weak-* limit. For any finite `Λ` containing it and all the support of a fixed local observable, the relative flux is `−1` on the limiting vector and commutes with that observable, since the boundary lies outside its support. Local gauge-invariant elements are dense on D-reg: approximate any invariant `A` by local `A_n`, then average `A_n` over the product of gauge groups at the finitely many sites meeting its support; Haar averaging is contractive, fixes `A`, and yields local invariants converging to `A` (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md). This proves strong convergence to `−I` on a dense GNS domain and hence `χ_sp=−1`. □

The transfer layer also breaks matchings. Neither an update that preserves the D1 state while giving the defect a nonzero band generator nor finite string energy is proved. The proposed D-reg specification has the draft's four-test result `F,P,P,F`: it is a closed register and does not itself decide a phase, but was chosen for the target and its independent end classes lack a working-set reason. It is recorded, not proposed for adoption. The wider family row c13 in `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` records failures 1, 2 and 4; it does not by itself prove that this particular register fails test 2.

**Observation (referee closure, CG16D_REFEREE.md) (unrefereed).** Take link-pure registers. Let `ω_per` be a flux-sharp state of `𝔄^G` whose configuration `κ_per` is cell-periodic. Let `ω₁` be a flux-sharp state whose configuration `κ₁` differs from `κ_per` exactly on a finite set `F₀` of odd size. Then the GNS representation `π_per` of `ω_per` has `χ_sp(π_per:ω₁)=−1`.

*Proof.* The `z_x` are central in `𝔄^G` and sharp in `ω_per`, so `π_per(z_x)=κ_per(x)I` on its cyclic space. Relative to `ω₁`, `Z_Λ=∏_{x∈Λ}κ₁(x)z_x` acts there as `∏_{x∈Λ}κ₁(x)κ_per(x)=(−1)^{|F₀∩Λ|}`. This is `−1` once `Λ⊇F₀`. □

Example 1 gives an instance: `ω₁` is its one-defect string state and `ω_per` its background `ω₀`. So "spinor class relative to `ω₁`" includes the periodic sector itself, with every band that a covariant representation of that sector may carry. Theorem 6(iii)'s words "a finite nonempty set" exclude exactly this configuration. The label measures the background's own defect as well as the sector's. For the target this does no harm. The observation shows only that relative class over a non-periodic background is not a position label; it says nothing about bands of `ω₀`.
