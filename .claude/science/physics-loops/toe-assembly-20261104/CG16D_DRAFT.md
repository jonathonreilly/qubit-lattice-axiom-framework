## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**Decided by proof on `L5-O[S_+]` and on `L5-U`: no.** (i) On `L5-O[S_+]` no representation of `𝔄^G` differs from any other in spinor class (Theorem A). Every link end carries the same fixed spinor class, so the spinor flux through every closed surface is the number `+1` in every state. The literal reading "centre eigenvalue ±i" holds at every site in every factor sector, the vacuum included (`RCS1.md` Theorem 1), so it names the site content, not an excitation. This holds for every update whose automorphism fixes the local gauge generators (LZ1's class, NN-a, any plaquette class GA1-P-5) and every state. (ii) For every `so(20)` register of `L5-O`, and every register that is link-pure in the sense of `LW1_5.md` inferred definition 7, the spinor flux through a closed surface `∂Λ` equals `±∏_{z∈Λ}z_z` (Lemmas 1-2). So a sector of spinor class relative to a flux-sharp background is carried by finite odd sets of flipped local centre labels (Lemma S). Such a sector is never made from the background by a local operator, and some are made by strings (Example B.1). In every covariant representation built from such sectors, every band is flat along every axis at every node, so every leading generator is zero (Theorem B). This holds whatever the string energy. **The failing step is propagation, not confinement.** The named step: link-purity makes the spinor flux a central label that the tick fixes (Theorem SC(a)) and translations shift (`RCS1.md` Theorem 5). (iii) On `L5-U` the analogue does not exist: no gauge transformation of the law acts as `−1 ∈ Spin(10)`, so every sector has relative spinor flux `+1` (Proposition C). The odd-sector question that remains there is RCS-P. (iv) **Sharpness.** Proposition D shows the step is where the law enters. On a Definition-GR register that is not link-pure, a gauge-invariant local gate moves spinor flux between links without changing any central label. A gauge-invariant fermion-odd local operator turns a vector-class fermion into two link spinors (`16⊗16 ⊃ 10`). That register also has a pure D1 state of exact local Gauss singlets, relative to which a single spinor-class defect is a monomer of a matching, made by a string. Such registers lie outside both laws. The four-test row on file for registers outside `so(20)` (c13) fails tests 1, 2 and 4; the single register used here (D-reg, section 5) fails tests 1 and 4. They are recorded, not proposed. Whether a law with such registers has a deconfined, propagating spinor-class sector is not decided here.

## 1. Setting and definitions

### 1.1 Registers, centre elements, updates

Use the quasi-local field algebra `𝔄`, its gauge-invariant subalgebra `𝔄^G`, the cell translations `τ_β` (by `2β`) and the conventions of `RCS1.md` definitions 1-6 and `LZ1.md` section 1.

**Registers (Definition GR of `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md`, line 49).** Each link `ℓ=(x,y)` carries a finite-dimensional Hilbert space `R_ℓ` with two commuting unitary representations `ρ_{ℓ,x}`, `ρ_{ℓ,y}` of `Spin(10)`, with Hermitian generators `L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}`, `L^{cd}_{ℓ,y}`. Links are ordinary tensor factors that commute with matter. The Gauss generators are `𝒢^{cd}_x=M^{cd}_x+Σ_{ℓ∋x}L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}`. The registers of `L5-O[R]` are the case of one representation `ρ_ℓ` of `Spin(20)` on `R_ℓ`, with `L^{cd}_{ℓ,y}=T_{cd}` and `L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}=T_{10+c,10+d}` (`LW1_5.md` section 4, Q); call these `so(20)` registers. `L5-O[S_+]` is the case `R_ℓ=S_+` on every link.

**Centre elements.** As in `LW1_5.md` line 169, put
\[
z_x=e^{2\pi i\mathcal G^{12}_x},\qquad \epsilon_{\ell,x}=e^{2\pi iL^{12}_{\ell,x}},\qquad d_\ell=\epsilon_{\ell,x}\epsilon_{\ell,y}\quad(\ell=(x,y)).
\]
A register is **link-pure** if `d_ℓ` is a number (`LW1_5.md` inferred definition 7). For a finite site set `Λ`, let `L_int(Λ)`, `∂Λ` and `in(ℓ)` be as in `RCS1.md` definition 2, and define the **spinor flux** through `∂Λ`:
\[
\Phi_\Lambda=\prod_{\ell\in\partial\Lambda}\epsilon_{\ell,\mathrm{in}(\ell)} .
\]
`ε_{ℓ,x}` is `ρ_{ℓ,x}` of the central element `−1∈Spin(10)`. So it is a self-adjoint unitary that commutes with both end actions on `R_ℓ` (the end actions commute). Hence `ε_{ℓ,x}`, `d_ℓ` and `Φ_Λ` lie in `𝔄^G`.

**Updates.** The update class `𝒰` is the set of tick automorphisms `α` of `𝔄` (in `LW1.md` E1 form) that fix every local gauge generator and commute with every `τ_β`, for a translation-invariant register assignment. Every update of LZ1's class is in `𝒰`: each of its gates commutes with every local gauge transformation (`LZ1.md` line 34), and its layers are closed under the even translations. Every update admitted by NN-a fixes every local gauge generator by the text of NN-a ("Its automorphism `α` fixes every local gauge generator"). Any plaquette class GA1-P-5 enters only through such gates. For `α∈𝒰`, `α(z_x)=z_x` and `τ_β(z_x)=z_{x+2β}`: `z_x` is a function of `𝒢^{12}_x` (`LW1_5.md` Theorem SC(a); `RCS1.md` Theorem 1).

### 1.2 The relative spinor label

The centre of `Spin(10)` is `Z4`, generated by `ω` with `ω^2=−1`. A representation is of spinor class exactly when `−1=ω^2` acts as `−1`, that is, when `ω` acts with eigenvalues `±i` (`LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` line 72: "`O` is spinor class at `x` exactly when `Ad z_x(O) = −O`"). So "centre eigenvalue ±i" is decided by the action of `−1`. The spinor flux `Φ_Λ` is the action of `−1` on the inner ends of the links that cross `∂Λ`. By Lemma 1 below it measures the spinor class of everything inside `Λ`, as a Gauss law does.

**Definition SF (flux-sharp background).** A state `ω_0` of `𝔄^G` is *flux-sharp* if `ω_0(Φ_Λ)=s_Λ∈{±1}` for every finite `Λ`. Then `ω_0((Φ_Λ−s_Λ)^2)=0`.

**Definition χ_sp.** For a representation `π` of `𝔄^G`, write `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=s` if `s_Λπ(Φ_Λ)→s·1` strongly along the net of finite site sets ordered by inclusion, with `s∈{±1}`. Call `π` **of spinor class relative to `ω_0`** if `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=−1`.

This is `RCS1.md` R2's `χ_tot`, with matter parity `P_Λ` replaced by the spinor flux `Φ_Λ`.

**Lemma 0 (invariance; port of `RCS1.md` R2(a)).** If `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=s` and `σ` is quasi-equivalent to `π`, then `χ_sp(σ:ω_0)=s`. If every `σ_i` has `χ_sp(σ_i:ω_0)=s`, so does `⊕_iσ_i`.

*Proof.* R2(a)'s proof uses only that each `ε_Λπ(P_Λ)` is the image of a norm-one element of `𝔄^G`. The same holds for `s_Λπ(Φ_Λ)`. So the proof applies word for word: strong convergence of a uniformly bounded net on a dense set gives it everywhere, and Zorn's lemma with definition 3 of `RCS1.md` reduces quasi-equivalence to direct sums of unitarily equivalent pieces. ∎

**The other words of the question.** *Admissible update:* any `α∈𝒰`. *D1 state:* `LZ1.md` line 40. *Propagates:* there is a band in a covariant representation `(H,π,T,Ũ)` (`RCS1.md` definition 5) with a node `K_0` and some `N_j=∂_j(i log V)(K_0)≠0`. *In that sector:* `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=−1`; by Lemma 0 this holds on every subrepresentation. *Created by a string, not a local operator:* section 3, Theorem B(ii), shows that a vector state made from a flux-sharp `ω_0` by a local element of `𝔄` never has `χ_sp=−1` on link-pure registers; so on those registers every spinor-class sector is a non-local limit, such as a string limit of `RCS1.md` section 5. *Finite string energy:* no definition is needed. Theorems A and B hold for every sector of spinor class, deconfined or not, so this file adopts none.

## 2. The spinor flux is a Gauss law; on `L5-O[S_+]` it is constant

**Lemma 1 (Gauss identity for the spinor flux).** For every Definition-GR register assignment and every finite site set `Λ`,
\[
\prod_{x\in\Lambda}z_x=(-1)^{|\Lambda|}\Big(\prod_{\ell\in L_{\rm int}(\Lambda)}d_\ell\Big)\,\Phi_\Lambda .
\]
All factors commute and square to `1`.

*Proof.* The summands of `𝒢^{12}_x` are `M^{12}_x` and the `L^{12}_{ℓ,x}`, one for each of the six links at `x`. They act on different tensor factors (the matter at `x`, and six different links), so they commute and the exponential factorizes: `z_x=e^{2πiM^{12}_x}∏_{ℓ∋x}ε_{ℓ,x}`. Since `M^{12}_x=n_{x,1}−1/2` (`LW1_5.md` section 1), `e^{2πiM^{12}_x}=e^{−iπ}e^{2πin_{x,1}}=−1`. So `z_x=−∏_{ℓ∋x}ε_{ℓ,x}`, as `LW1_5.md` line 169 states. Take the product over `x∈Λ`. A link with both ends in `Λ` contributes `ε_{ℓ,x}ε_{ℓ,y}=d_ℓ`. A link with one end in `Λ` contributes `ε_{ℓ,in(ℓ)}`. Other links contribute nothing. Factors on different links commute. On one link, `ε_{ℓ,x}` and `ε_{ℓ,y}` commute because the end actions commute. Each `ε` squares to `ρ(1)=1`, since `(−1)^2=1` in `Spin(10)`. ∎

**Corollary 1.1.** If every register is link-pure, then `Φ_Λ=σ_Λ∏_{x∈Λ}z_x` with the number `σ_Λ=(−1)^{|Λ|}∏_{ℓ∈L_int(Λ)}d_ℓ∈{±1}`. Then `Φ_Λ` is central in `𝔄^G`, and `α(Φ_Λ)=Φ_Λ` for every `α∈𝒰`.

*Proof.* Lemma 1, `d_ℓ^2=1`, and section 1.1: each `z_x` is central in `𝔄^G` (it is a function of `𝒢^{12}_x`, which commutes with every gauge-invariant element, and it is itself gauge invariant) and is fixed by every `α∈𝒰`. ∎

**Lemma 2 (every `so(20)` register is link-pure; port of `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` Lemma K0(iii)).** On an `so(20)` register `(R,ρ)`, `ε_{ℓ,x}=ε_{ℓ,y}=ρ(−1_{Spin(20)})`. Hence `d_ℓ=1`.

*Proof.* In `Cl(20)`, `T_{11,12}=(i/2)η_{11}η_{12}` and `(η_{11}η_{12})^2=−1`. So `e^{2πiT_{11,12}}=e^{−πη_{11}η_{12}}=\cos π−η_{11}η_{12}\sin π=−1`. This is the element `−1` of `Spin(20)`, the nontrivial element of the kernel of `Spin(20)→SO(20)`. `ρ` is a representation of `Spin(20)` and `L^{12}_{ℓ,x}=dρ(T_{11,12})`, so `ε_{ℓ,x}=e^{2πi\,dρ(T_{11,12})}=ρ(e^{2πiT_{11,12}})=ρ(−1)`. The same computation with `T_{12}` gives `ε_{ℓ,y}=ρ(−1)`. Then `d_ℓ=ρ(−1)^2=ρ(1)=1`. ∎

So on every `so(20)` register, Corollary 1.1 holds with `σ_Λ=(−1)^{|Λ|}`: the spinor flux through `∂Λ` is `(−1)^{|Λ|}∏_{x∈Λ}z_x`. This is the native `Z2` analogue, for the spinor class, of `RCS1.md` Theorem 2's parity/flux identity.

**Theorem A (`L5-O[S_+]`: no sector differs in spinor class).** Let every link carry the half-spin representation `S_+` of `Spin(20)`. Then, with no hypothesis on the update or the state:

(a) `ε_{ℓ,x}=ε_{ℓ,y}=−1` on every link, `z_x=−1` at every site, and `Φ_Λ=1` for every finite `Λ`, as elements of `𝔄`.

(b) Every state of `𝔄^G` is flux-sharp with `s_Λ=1`. For every representation `π` of `𝔄^G` and every state `ω_0`, `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=+1`. No representation of `𝔄^G` is of spinor class relative to any state.

(c) In every factor representation `π` of `𝔄^G`, the centre implementer `c_x=ω_x` has scalar value `κ_π(x)∈{±i}` at every site. For two factor representations, `κ_π(x)/κ_{π'}(x)∈{±1}` at every site.

*Proof.* (a) By Lemma 2, `ε=ρ_{S_+}(−1_{Spin(20)})`. The element `−1` acts as `−1` on the spin module of `Cl(20)`, of which `S_+` is a chiral half; so `ε=−1`. Then `z_x=−(−1)^6=−1` (as in `LW1_5.md` line 204, Σ). Counting link ends, `6|Λ|=2|L_int(Λ)|+|∂Λ|`, so `|∂Λ|` is even and `Φ_Λ=(−1)^{|∂Λ|}=1`. (b) `π(Φ_Λ)=1` and `s_Λ=ω_0(Φ_Λ)=1` for every `π` and `ω_0`. (c) `RCS1.md` Theorem 1 gives `κ_π(x)^2=−1` on `L5-O[S_+]`. A ratio of two square roots of `−1` is `±1`. ∎

**What Theorem A settles.** On `L5-O[S_+]` the spinor flux is a constant of the algebra. Relative to any background, every representation of `𝔄^G` is tensor class: localized or not, made by a local operator or by a string, factor or not, deconfined or not. Any odd sector that `χ_tot` labels on `L5-O[S_+]` (`RCS1.md` R2) is therefore of vector class (centre ratio `−1`), never of spinor class. The question of the brief is answered **no** on `L5-O[S_+]` before deconfinement or propagation arises. Part (c) is the literal reading of "centre eigenvalue ±i": it holds at every site of every sector, the vacuum included. It describes the site content of every state (the escape E1 of `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` line 139), not an excitation. #10045 found the same structure in its model, where `Spin(10)` is global and only the centre `Z4` is gauged: "`z_v = -i (-1)^F_v`, so `z_v^2 = -1` is a c-number" (consolidation note line 1204). Theorem A is that statement for the gauged law, carried from local operators to all sectors.

## 3. Link-pure registers: spinor class sits in static site labels

Throughout this section every register is link-pure and the register assignment is translation invariant. By Lemma 2 this includes `L5-O[R]` for every `so(20)` register `R`, for example `S_+⊕C^{20}` (`LW1_5.md` line 175). Fix a flux-sharp state `ω_0` of `𝔄^G`. For `Λ={x}` there are no internal links and `Φ_{\{x\}}=−z_x` (Lemma 1), so `ω_0` has sharp values `κ_0(x):=ω_0(z_x)∈{±1}`. Put
\[
u_x=\kappa_0(x)\,z_x,\qquad Z_\Lambda=\prod_{x\in\Lambda}u_x .
\]
By Corollary 1.1, `s_ΛΦ_Λ=Z_Λ`: indeed `Φ_Λ=σ_Λ∏_{x∈Λ}z_x` and `s_Λ=ω_0(Φ_Λ)=σ_Λ∏_{x∈Λ}κ_0(x)`. The `u_x` are commuting self-adjoint unitaries, central in `𝔄^G`. For a configuration `κ:Z^3→{±1}` let `D(κ)={x:κ(x)≠κ_0(x)}`, and let `C^{odd}(κ_0)` be the set of `κ` with `D(κ)` finite and of odd size. It is countable.

**Lemma S (support).** Let `π` be a representation of `𝔄^G` on `H`, and for each `κ` let `H_κ={v∈H:π(z_x)v=κ(x)v` for all `x}`. Then `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=−1` if and only if
\[
H=\bigoplus_{\kappa\in C^{odd}(\kappa_0)}H_\kappa .
\]

*Proof.* "If": for `v` in a finite sum of the `H_κ`, `π(Z_Λ)v=−v` once `Λ` contains every `D(κ)` involved, because `π(Z_Λ)` acts on `H_κ` as `(−1)^{|D(κ)∩Λ|}`. Such `v` are dense and `‖π(Z_Λ)‖=1`, so `π(Z_Λ)→−1` strongly.

"Only if". *Step 1 (a character average).* For finite `F`, put `E_F=∏_{x∈F}(1+π(u_x))/2`, the projection onto `{v:π(z_x)v=κ_0(x)v` for `x∈F}`. Expanding the product,
\[
E_F=2^{-|F|}\sum_{F'\subseteq F}\pi(Z_{F'}),\qquad\text{so}\qquad \tfrac12(1-E_F)=2^{-|F|}\sum_{F'\subseteq F}\tfrac12\big(1-\pi(Z_{F'})\big).
\]
*Step 2.* Fix a unit vector `v` and `δ>0`. Choose a finite `Λ_0` with `‖(π(Z_Λ)+1)v‖<δ` for every finite `Λ⊇Λ_0`. Let `F` be finite and disjoint from `Λ_0`. Since `Z_{Λ_0}^2=1`, `Z_F=Z_{Λ_0∪F}Z_{Λ_0}`, and
\[
\|\pi(Z_F)v-v\|\le\|\pi(Z_{\Lambda_0\cup F})(\pi(Z_{\Lambda_0})v+v)\|+\|\pi(Z_{\Lambda_0\cup F})v+v\|<2\delta .
\]
The same bound holds for every `F'⊆F`. By Step 1, `‖(1−E_F)v‖^2=⟨v,(1−E_F)v⟩≤\max_{F'⊆F}‖(1−π(Z_{F'}))v‖<2δ`. The projections `E_F` decrease as `F` grows; let `Q_{Λ_0}` be their strong limit over finite `F⊆Z^3∖Λ_0`. It is the projection onto `{w:π(z_x)w=κ_0(x)w` for all `x∉Λ_0}`, and `‖(1−Q_{Λ_0})v‖^2≤2δ`.

*Step 3.* The `Q_{Λ_0}` increase with `Λ_0`, and by Step 2 their strong limit is `1`. On `Q_{Λ_0}H` the finitely many commuting involutions `π(u_x)`, `x∈Λ_0`, split the space into joint eigenspaces. Each lies in some `H_κ` with `D(κ)⊆Λ_0`. So `H` is the closed span of the `H_κ` with `D(κ)` finite; they are pairwise orthogonal, being joint eigenspaces for distinct eigenvalues.

*Step 4.* If `D(κ)` is finite of even size and `v∈H_κ`, then `π(Z_Λ)v=v` for every `Λ⊇D(κ)`, while `π(Z_Λ)v→−v`. So `v=0`. ∎

**Theorem B (static spinor class on link-pure registers).** Let `α∈𝒰`, let `ω_0` be a flux-sharp state of `𝔄^G`, and keep the notation above.

(i) Every representation of spinor class relative to `ω_0` is carried by finite, odd-size sets of flipped centre labels (Lemma S).

(ii) *No local or quasi-local operator makes one.* Let `ω̂_0` be a state of `𝔄` restricting to `ω_0`, with GNS data `(Ĥ,π̂,Ω̂)`. Let `B∈𝔄` (local or quasi-local) with `π̂(B)Ω̂≠0`. Then the representation of `𝔄^G` on the closure of `π̂(𝔄^G)π̂(B)Ω̂` has `χ_sp=+1`.

(iii) *Every band is flat.* Let `ω_0` also be invariant under every `τ_β`. Let `(H,π,T,Ũ)` be a covariant representation (`RCS1.md` definition 5) for `α` with `χ_sp(π:ω_0)=−1`. Then every band is flat along `e_1`, `e_2` and `e_3` at each node: `V(K_0+se_j)=1` for small real `s`. So `N_1=N_2=N_3=0`, and there is no Clifford cone.

(iv) *General form.* Let `(H,π,T,Ũ)` be a covariant representation for `α`, and suppose `H` is the closed span of joint eigenvectors of the `π(z_x)` whose configurations each differ from a cell-periodic configuration on a finite nonempty set. Then every band is flat along each axis at each node.

*Proof.* (ii) Since `Z_Λ` is a self-adjoint unitary with `ω̂_0(Z_Λ)=1`, `ω̂_0((Z_Λ−1)^2)=0`, so `π̂(Z_Λ)Ω̂=Ω̂`. Let `S` be a finite site set containing the matter sites of `B` and both ends of every link in its support. By `LW1_5.md` Theorem SC(b), `B=Σ_{T⊆S}B_T`, with `Ad z_x(B_T)=−B_T` for `x∈T` and `Ad z_x(B_T)=B_T` for `x∉T`. Each `B_T` is supported where `B` is: in the average that defines it, the factors `ε` of the `z_x` on links outside the support of `B` commute with `B` and square to `1`, so they cancel. By Corollary 1.1, `∏_{x∈S}z_x=σ_SΦ_S`, and `Φ_S` acts only on links crossing `∂S`, which are outside the support of `B`. So `Ad(∏_{x∈S}z_x)` fixes `B_T`, while it also multiplies `B_T` by `(−1)^{|T|}`. Hence `B_T=0` when `|T|` is odd (this is `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` (K5), rebuilt from Lemma 1). For `Λ⊇S`, `Z_ΛB_T=(−1)^{|T|}B_TZ_Λ=B_TZ_Λ`, so `π̂(Z_Λ)π̂(B)Ω̂=π̂(B)π̂(Z_Λ)Ω̂=π̂(B)Ω̂`. For `A∈𝔄^G`, centrality of `Z_Λ` gives `π̂(Z_Λ)π̂(A)π̂(B)Ω̂=π̂(A)π̂(B)Ω̂`. So `π̂(Z_Λ)=1` on the cyclic subspace for all `Λ⊇S`, and `χ_sp=+1`. For quasi-local `B`, take local `B_n→B` in norm with sets `S_n`. For `Λ⊇S_n`, `‖π̂(Z_Λ)π̂(B)Ω̂−π̂(B)Ω̂‖≤2‖B−B_n‖`, so `π̂(Z_Λ)π̂(B)Ω̂→π̂(B)Ω̂` along the net; centrality carries this to the cyclic subspace, and uniform boundedness gives `χ_sp=+1`.

(iii) Invariance gives `κ_0(x+2β)=ω_0(τ_β(z_x))=κ_0(x)`: `κ_0` is cell-periodic. By Lemma S, `H=⊕_{κ∈C^{odd}(κ_0)}H_κ`. Since `Ũπ(z_x)Ũ^*=π(α(z_x))=π(z_x)`, `ŨH_κ=H_κ`. Since `T_βπ(z_x)T_β^*=π(z_{x+2β})`, for `v∈H_κ` we get `π(z_{x+2β})T_βv=κ(x)T_βv`; so `T_βH_κ=H_{β·κ}` with `(β·κ)(y)=κ(y−2β)`. Periodicity of `κ_0` gives `D(β·κ)=D(κ)+2β`, so `β·κ∈C^{odd}(κ_0)`. Each `D(κ)` is nonempty. Let `p(κ)` be its lexicographically least point; then `p(β·κ)=p(κ)+2β`. For `j=1,2,3` put `q_j(κ)=p(κ)_j/2`, so `q_j(β·κ)=q_j(κ)+e_j·β`. `RCS1.md` Theorem 5 with `n=e_j` gives flatness along `e_j` at every node and excludes a Clifford cone; Lemma 5.1 there gives `N_j=Σ_k(e_j)_kN_k=0`.

(iv) A cell-periodic configuration that agrees with `κ` off a finite set is unique: two cell-periodic configurations that agree off a finite set agree everywhere, since every `x` has translates `x+2β` outside any finite set. Call it `κ_per(κ)`, and let `p(κ)` be the least point of the finite nonempty set where `κ` and `κ_per(κ)` differ. Since `κ_per(β·κ)=κ_per(κ)`, `p(β·κ)=p(κ)+2β`. The configurations involved form a countable set (finitely many cell-periodic ones, times finite sets). Group the joint eigenvectors by configuration and argue as in (iii). ∎

**Example B.1 (spinor-class sectors made by strings exist).** Take `L5-O[S_+⊕C^{20}]`. By Lemma 2 and the action of `−1∈Spin(20)` (as `−1` on spinors and `+1` on vectors), `ε=(−1)⊕(+1)` on every link. Let `Φ_0` be a product state of `𝔄` that puts every link in one fixed unit vector of `S_+` and every site in one fixed even matter vector. For `r≥1`, let `Φ_{x,r}` differ from `Φ_0` only on the links `ℓ_i=(x+(i−1)e_1,x+ie_1)`, `1≤i≤r`, each in one fixed unit vector of `C^{20}`; let `Φ_x` do so for all `i≥1`. In each product state every `ε` is sharp, so every `z_y=−∏_{ℓ∋y}ε_{ℓ,y}` is sharp. In `Φ_0`, `z_y=−1` everywhere. In `Φ_{x,r}`, `z` flips exactly at `x` and `x+re_1` (one vector link each); interior ray sites have two vector links and keep `z=−1`. In `Φ_x`, `z` flips exactly at `x`. ## 4. `L5-U`: the analogue does not exist

`LW1_5.md` Theorem U (line 143): "For \(L5\)-U, every \(\mathcal G_x^T\) commutes with every hop and electric gate. Its generator-level site frame group is exactly \(U(5)\)". The local gauge transformations at `x` are `Γ_x(u)=e^{i𝒢^T_x}` for `u=e^{iT}∈U(5)`. They act by second quantization on the matter at `x` and on the five rishon modes at `x`'s end of each incident link. So `u↦Γ_x(u)` is a representation of `U(5)` itself.

**Proposition C.** Let `G̃⊂Spin(10)` be the preimage of `U(5)⊂SO(10)` under the covering map `p:Spin(10)→SO(10)`. Its centre contains the centre `{±1,±ω}` of `Spin(10)`.

(a) `p(−1)=I` and `p(±ω)=−I`, as elements of `U(5)`.

(b) So every action of the law's gauge group, pulled back to `G̃`, sends `−1` to the identity: on the matter, on every link register and on `𝔄`. Its local implementer is `1`, which has value `1` in every representation of `𝔄^G`. The elements `±ω` act as `Γ_x(−I)`, whose adjoint action is `Ad c_x` with `c_x=e^{iπQ_x}` (`RCS1.md` definition 2).

(c) Hence on `L5-U[m]`, for every `m`, every update, every state and every pair of representations of `𝔄^G`: the action of `−1∈Spin(10)` gives relative value `+1` everywhere, and the relative value of the `ω`-implementer `c_x` lies in `{±1}`. No representation of `𝔄^G` is of spinor class relative to another, in the sense of section 1 or in the sense "relative centre eigenvalue `±i`".

*Proof.* (a) `−1∈Spin(10)` is the nontrivial element of `ker p`, so `p(−1)=I`. `ω` acts on the Majorana modes by `−1` (`LW1_5.md` line 154, step 2: "`R(ω)=−I`"), so `p(ω)=−I_{10}`, which is `−I_5` in `U(5)`; then `p(−ω)=p(−1)p(ω)=−I` as well. (b) The law's gauge transformations at `x` form the representation `Γ_x` of `U(5)`. Pulled back along `p|_{G̃}`, `−1` acts as `Γ_x(I)=1`, and `±ω` act as `Γ_x(−I)`. On the matter, `Γ_x(−I)=(−1)^{N_x}`; on a rishon end, `(−1)^{p_{ℓ,x}}`. Their product over the site and its six ends is `e^{iπQ_x}=c_x`. ## 5. Where the step bites: registers that are not link-pure

Theorem B uses the law in one place: link-purity (Corollary 1.1), which Lemma 2 supplies for every `so(20)` register. Theorem SC's own class is wider: "finite link registers with commuting endpoint \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) actions" (`LW1_5.md` line 187). This section shows that link-purity cannot be dropped from Theorem B: outside it, spinor flux moves without any central label changing.

**The register.** Let `H≅C^{32}` carry the spin representation of `Spin(10)`, with Clifford generators `Γ_a` and generators `(i/2)Γ_cΓ_d`, and put `Q=C|0⟩⊕H`, with `Spin(10)` trivial on `C|0⟩`. On each link `ℓ=(x,y)` put `R_ℓ=Q^{ℓ,x}⊗Q^{ℓ,y}`, with `ρ_{ℓ,x}` acting on the first factor and `ρ_{ℓ,y}` on the second. The end actions commute, so this is a Definition-GR register. It carries a `(10,10)` tensor operator for `L5-O`'s Majorana hop, `U^{ab}=Γ^{x}_b⊗Γ^{y}_a` on `H⊗H` and `0` on the other three summands, by the computation in `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` Proposition E(ii). It is not the restriction of a `Spin(20)` representation: by Lemma 2 such a restriction has `ε_{ℓ,x}=ε_{ℓ,y}`, while here `ε_{ℓ,x}=1_C⊕(−1_H)` on the first factor only.

**Proposition D.** On this register:

(a) `d_ℓ` is not a number: it is `+1` on `C⊗C` and `H⊗H` and `−1` on `C⊗H` and `H⊗C`. The register is not link-pure.

(b) *A gauge-invariant transfer.* Fix a site `y` and two links `ℓ_1≠ℓ_2` at `y`, and write `Q_i=Q^{ℓ_i,y}`. Let `(h_k)` be an orthonormal basis of `H` and
\[
K_{\ell_1\ell_2}=\sum_{k=1}^{32}|h_k\rangle\langle0|\otimes|0\rangle\langle h_k|\qquad\text{on }Q_1\otimes Q_2 .
\]
`K` does not depend on the basis. It commutes with every Gauss generator `𝒢^{cd}_z`, so `K`, `K^*` and `h_y=Σ_{ℓ≠ℓ'∋y}K_{ℓℓ'}` lie in `𝔄^G`; `h_y` is Hermitian since `K_{ℓℓ'}^*=K_{ℓ'ℓ}`. `K` commutes with every `z_z`. It anticommutes with `ε_{ℓ_i,y}` and with `d_{ℓ_i}`, for `i=1,2`.

(c) *The gate layer is admissible.* The gates `e^{iθh_y}`, one per site, act on pairwise disjoint tensor factors (the `y`-end factors of the six links at `y`), so they commute. The family is translation invariant and covariant under the proper cubic rotations about each site, and each gate is gauge invariant with finite support. So appending this layer to any update of `𝒰` gives an update of `𝒰`; with the Majorana hop on this register the result has the form of LZ1's class. A rotation that reverses a link swaps its two end factors, which the register `Q⊗Q` allows.

(d) *The spinor flux is not a central label.* Let `ℓ_1` have both ends in `Λ` and let `ℓ_2` cross `∂Λ` with inner end `y`. Then `KΦ_Λ=−Φ_ΛK`. So `Φ_Λ` is not central in `𝔄^G`, the gate of (c) changes it, and no position label of the kind used in Theorem B(iii) is conserved.

(e) *A gauge-invariant fermion-odd local operator.* There is a nonzero equivariant linear map `B^*:C^{10}→H⊗H` (the vector `10` occurs in `16⊗16`). Put `w_a=B^*e_a`, `X_a=|w_a⟩⟨0,0|` on `Q_1⊗Q_2`, and
\[
O_y=\sum_{a=1}^{10}\gamma_{y,a}X_a .
\]
Then `O_y` is local, gauge invariant, nonzero, and fermion odd. So `RCS1.md` Theorem 4's conclusion ("Every `A∈𝔄^G` is matter-even"), proved there for the two laws' registers, fails on this register.

(f) *An invariant sector of local singlets, and a D1 state in it.* Let `𝒮` be the closed span of the product vectors in which, at every site `y`, exactly one of the six `y`-end factors lies in `H`, the matter at `y` and that end are in a `Spin(10)`-invariant vector of `(H_+⊕H_−)⊗H`, and every other end is `|0⟩`. That invariant space is two-dimensional, one vector for each matter parity. Every vector of `𝒮` is fixed by every local gauge transformation, and `z_y=+1` on `𝒮` at every site. `𝒮` is invariant under each `h_y` of (b) and under the Majorana hop with the `U^{ab}` above. In `𝒮` the end classes form an *arrow configuration*: each site points to one of its links. `d_ℓ=−1` exactly on links that carry one arrow. Relative to a *matching* (every arrow returned by the other end of its link), a configuration has spinor flux `−1` through `∂Λ` exactly when an odd number of links inside `Λ` carry one arrow: a spinor-class defect is a monomer of the matching. Finally, for the base tick of `L5-O` built on this register as in `LW1_5.md` section 2, with `τ_E=0`, the *columnar* state is D1: every link `(x,x+e_1)` with `x_1` even carries two arrows, and each such pair is in one fixed eigenvector of its gate.

*Proof.* (a) `ε_{ℓ,x}` is `ρ_{ℓ,x}(−1)`, which is `1` on `C|0⟩` and `−1` on the spin module `H`; likewise at `y`. Multiply. (b) The element `Σ_k|h_k⟩⊗⟨h_k|` is the canonical element of `H⊗H^*` for the identity map, so it does not depend on the orthonormal basis. For `g∈Spin(10)`, `ρ(g)` fixes `|0⟩` and is unitary, so
\[
(\rho(g)\otimes\rho(g))K(\rho(g)\otimes\rho(g))^{-1}=\sum_k|gh_k\rangle\langle0|\otimes|0\rangle\langle gh_k|=K,
\]
because `(gh_k)` is again an orthonormal basis. Differentiating, `K` commutes with `L^{cd}_{ℓ_1,y}+L^{cd}_{ℓ_2,y}`. It acts on no other factor of `𝒢^{cd}_y`, and on no factor of `𝒢^{cd}_z` for `z≠y`; so it commutes with every Gauss generator. Being gauge invariant, it commutes with every `z_z` (section 1.1). `K` maps the summand `C⊗H` of `Q_1⊗Q_2` onto `H⊗C` and vanishes on the other summands. So it changes `ε_{ℓ_1,y}` from `+1` to `−1` and `ε_{ℓ_2,y}` from `−1` to `+1`, and leaves the far-end factors alone. Since `K` vanishes off that summand, `ε_{ℓ_i,y}K=−Kε_{ℓ_i,y}` as operators, and the same holds for `d_{ℓ_i}=ε_{ℓ_i,y}ε_{ℓ_i,{\rm far}}`, whose far factor commutes with `K`. (c) Each gate acts only on the six `y`-end factors at its own site, and different sites have different end factors; so the gates commute. A rotation about `y` permutes the six links at `y` and maps `h_y` to itself, because the sum runs over all ordered pairs. Translation invariance is by construction. (d) `Φ_Λ` contains the factor `ε_{ℓ_2,y}`, which `K` flips. It does not contain `ε_{ℓ_1,y}`, since `ℓ_1` is internal. So `KΦ_Λ=−Φ_ΛK`. (e) Let `C` be invertible with `CΓ_aC^{-1}=Γ_a^T` (it exists because the `Γ_a^T` satisfy the same Clifford relations; `LW1_5.md` line 214 uses the same `C`). Then `C\,g\,C^{-1}=(g^{-1})^T` for `g` in the spin group, so `β_a(s,t)=s^TCΓ_at` obeys `β_a(gs,gt)=Σ_bR(g)_{ab}β_b(s,t)`, where `gΓ_ag^{-1}=Σ_bR(g)_{ba}Γ_b`. Since `CΓ_a` is invertible, `β_a≠0`. The linear map `B:H⊗H→C^{10}`, `s⊗t↦Σ_aβ_a(s,t)e_a`, therefore satisfies `B(g⊗g)=R(g)B` and is nonzero. Taking adjoints, with `R(g)` real orthogonal and `g` unitary, gives `(g⊗g)B^*=B^*R(g)`. So `(g⊗g)w_a=Σ_bR(g)_{ba}w_b`, and, as `ρ(g)|0⟩=|0⟩`, `Ad(ρ(g)⊗ρ(g))X_a=Σ_bR(g)_{ba}X_b`. On matter, `gγ_{y,a}g^{-1}=Σ_bR(g)_{ba}γ_{y,b}` (`LW1_5.md` line 153, step 1). So a gauge transformation at `y` sends `O_y` to `Σ_{b,c}(Σ_aR_{ba}R_{ca})γ_{y,b}X_c=O_y`. `O_y` acts on no factor of another site's Gauss generator. It is fermion odd because each `γ_{y,a}` is and link operators are even. It is nonzero because the `γ_{y,a}` are linearly independent and act on a factor different from the `X_a`, not all of which vanish. (f) The matter space `H_+⊕H_−` and `H` are each `16⊕\overline{16}`. Under the diagonal `Spin(10)`, `16⊗\overline{16}` and `\overline{16}⊗16` each contain one invariant and `16⊗16`, `\overline{16}⊗\overline{16}` contain none (the consolidation note, line 1204, #10045(F): "16 x 16 has no singlet, 16 x 16bar has one"). So the invariant space is two-dimensional. A vector of `𝒮` is invariant under each site's gauge group, which acts only on that site's matter and ends. `z_y=−∏_{ℓ∋y}ε_{ℓ,y}=−(−1)^1=+1`. `K_{ℓℓ'}` restricted to `H_{ℓ'}→H_ℓ` is the identity map, which intertwines the two end actions, so it carries the invariant vector with end `ℓ'` to the invariant vector with end `ℓ`; it kills every vector of `𝒮` whose `H`-end at `y` is not `ℓ'`. So `h_y𝒮⊆𝒮`. `U^{ab}` vanishes unless both ends of `ℓ` are in `H`, that is, unless `ℓ` carries two arrows. On such a link the hop preserves the end classes and commutes with `Spin(10)_x×Spin(10)_y`, so it preserves the tensor product of the two two-dimensional invariant spaces. The arrow statements follow from (a): `d_ℓ=−1` exactly when one end of `ℓ` is in `H`. With `z≡+1`, Lemma 1 gives `Φ_Λ=(−1)^{|Λ|}∏_{ℓ∈L_int(Λ)}d_ℓ`; in a matching every `d_ℓ=+1`. For the columnar state: the two-arrow links `(x,x+e_1)`, `x_1` even, lie in the even cubes, and they are pairwise disjoint. Every hop preserves the arrow configuration (it acts inside `H⊗H` or vanishes). So on the subspace of `𝒮` with the columnar configuration, the hops on links with fewer than two arrows vanish, and an even cube's generator is the sum of the hops on its four two-arrow links. These act on disjoint sites and commute, so the gate is a product of commuting unitaries, one per two-arrow link, each acting on that link's four-dimensional invariant space. Pick one eigenvector of that unitary and use it on every two-arrow link. In an odd cube every hop annihilates the state, since no link inside an odd cube carries two arrows. So the tick multiplies each two-arrow factor by a phase and fixes the rest; the state is stationary. It is invariant under every `τ_β`, and it is pure and locally gauge invariant. ∎
