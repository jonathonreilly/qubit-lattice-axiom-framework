## 2. Site count and the centre

**Theorem 1 (encoding; draft Lemma 3.1).** For finite `Λ`, the graded space of configurations with occupied subset `S⊆Λ` and content `F` at each occupied point is canonically `⊕_S ⊗̂_{x∈S}F_x ≅ ⊗̂_{x∈Λ}V_x`, equivariantly for the frames.

*Proof.* Expand the graded tensor product of the direct sums. Empty factors are even and contribute no signs. Frame actions agree on each summand. □

This is an encoding, not a uniqueness theorem for all possible changing-site models. Interstitials require a larger register. A unitary can empty a point while transferring information to its other support factors. The two axiom readings are R-site and R-point above.

**Theorem 2 (centre algebra; draft Lemma 3.2 and Corollary 3.3).** Put `z_x=exp(2πi M_x^{12})` and `ω_x=exp(iπ∑_j M_x^{2j−1,2j})`. On `V`,
\[
 z_x=(-1)^{o_x},\qquad ω_x=|∅⟩⟨∅|-i(-1)^{N_x}o_x,\qquad \prod_{x∈Λ}ω_x=(-i)^{N_s}(-1)^F.
\]
Thus `Ad(∏ω_x)A=(−i)^{Δn}θ(A)A`. On `V⁻`, `ω_x=|∅⟩⟨∅|+io_x` and `z_x=P_x`; hence `θ(A)=(−1)^{Δn}`. Filling or emptying one point is spinor class for either content parity. Under SC-min both even and odd such operators exist; SC-odd selects a fermionic half-spin site.

*Proof.* On `F`, `M^{2j−1,2j}=n_j−1/2`. Thus the five factors give `(−i)^5(−1)^N=−i(−1)^N`, and the `2π` rotation gives `−1`. Both act as 1 on `|∅⟩`. Multiply the commuting factors and compare source and target eigenspaces. Under SC-odd the content parity is odd, giving the last identities. □

## 3. Gauge links and D1 bands

**Theorem 5 (flat odd nodes; draft Theorem 4.2).** In setting G assume either (a) SC-odd, Spin(10), ω-pure registers and local centres `ω_x`, or (b) U(5), either point register, link operators preserving total rishon number, and centres `exp(iπQ_x)`. For any D1 state, the GNS space decomposes into finite-support centre-pattern eigenspaces. Its odd sector lies in the nontrivial-pattern subspace. On that subspace, translations and the tick take the form `L²(T³;K)`, `M_β`, and `1⊗W`. Every finite-fibre odd band is identically 1 on a connected `C¹` neighbourhood of a node.

*Proof.* Local centre actions commute, have finite order, commute with the complete tick and transform covariantly under even translations. Average a local operator over the finite centre group on a set containing its matter support and both ends of its links. This splits it into finitely many centre eigenoperators, trivial outside that set. Apply them to the D1 cyclic vector and use density and orthogonality.

In (a), the product of centre actions over that set acts on a homogeneous matter component as `i^{Δn}` and trivially on links by ω-purity. Odd components have odd `Δn`, hence nontrivial pattern. Under SC-odd, `θ=(∏_{z∈Λ}Ad ω_z)²` on operators localized in `Λ`; a D1 state is therefore `θ`-invariant, and `U_θ=(∏κ_z)²` on `H(κ)`. In (b), the product is matter parity: both link ends contribute and total rishon number is preserved. A finite nonempty pattern has no nonzero translation stabilizer. Choose orbit representatives; translations identify the nontrivial-pattern space with `ℓ²(Z³;K)`, while the tick acts identically on every translate. Fourier transform gives `1⊗W`. For a finite-fibre band, its fibre eigenvalues lie almost everywhere in the countable point spectrum of `W`. The `C¹` traces of powers of the band matrix are constant on a connected neighbourhood by the countable-range/Fubini argument in `LZ1.md` Theorem 2.2. At a node all eigenvalues equal 1, so unitarity gives the result. □

This extends the already proved pattern and flat-node results in `LZ1.md` Theorems 2.1–2.2; the supplied D1 state need not be VC1. It excludes an odd propagating Clifford node only in the proposed finite-fibre R-loc reading. It does not rule out dressed sectors or prove that every propagating 16 must use R-CS^str. **GAP:** `1⊗W` need not equal 1. Only its eigenvalue-1 part is tick-fixed and belongs to the LZ1-SC static class; flat nodes at eigenvalue 1 are fixed on their node neighbourhood.

## 4. Free walks and chirality

Let `V:T³→U(d)` be smooth and set `W₃[V]=(24π²)⁻¹∫tr(V⁻¹dV)³`. At a quasienergy-zero Weyl point `K₀`, assume `dim ker(V(K₀)−1)=2`, a smooth local logarithm `H=i log V`, and a smooth rank-two spectral frame with `H=a1+b·σ`, `a(K₀)=b(K₀)=0`. Put `N_{ij}=∂_j b_i(K₀)`. Require `N` invertible and `|∇a·n|<|Nn|` for every unit `n`. The chirality is `χ=sign det N`. These assumptions isolate a non-over-tilted node. At quasienergy `μ`, replace `V` by `e^{iμ}V`.

**Theorem 7 (draft Theorem F).** If `Z₀={K:1∈spec V(K)}` is finite, there are local indices with `∑ ind(K₀)=W₃[V]`; at a Weyl point `ind(K₀)=−χ(K₀)`. Hence, if all points of `Z₀` are Weyl points, `∑χ=−W₃[V]`. The same holds at `μ`, provided its corresponding node set is finite and has the stated type.

*Proof.* The form `ω₃=(24π²)⁻¹tr(g⁻¹dg)³` is closed: the Maurer–Cartan identity gives `d tr θ³=−tr θ⁴=0`, the last equality by graded cyclicity. On `C={U:1∉spec U}`, the logarithm `L` with eigenvalues in `(0,2π)` defines `Φ(t,U)=exp(itL(U))`. The homotopy formula gives a two-form `β` with `dβ=ω₃|_C`. Delete small balls around `Z₀` and apply Stokes. The limits of the individual negative boundary integrals exist because `V*ω₃` is bounded on shrinking shells; their sum is `W₃`.

At a Weyl point the tilt condition makes the two local energies positive and negative on a small surrounding sphere. The logarithm `L(V)` tends, in `C¹` on that sphere, to `2πΠ` on the doublet, where `Π=(1+\widehat{Nn}·σ)/2`; the complementary block becomes independent of the sphere. For `g=1+(e^{2πit}−1)Π`, direct multiplication gives
\[
\operatorname{tr}(g^{-1}dg)^3=-3|1-e^{2πit}|^2(2π i\,dt)\operatorname{tr}(ΠdΠdΠ).
\]
Its integral is `−c₁(Π)` with `c₁=(i/2π)∫tr(ΠdΠdΠ)`. The degree of `n↦\widehat{Nn}` is `χ`, and `c₁(Π)=−χ`; the boundary integral is therefore `χ` and its negative is `−χ`. A scalar phase does not change `V⁻¹dV`. □

The draft reports `check_F.py` numerical checks of the sign and normalization for two specific maps, yielding `W₃=0, ∑χ=0` and `W₃=−1, ∑χ=1`; those calculations were not rerun here and are not proofs. The draft relates this formula to Bessho–Sato, arXiv:2006.04204, Eq. (15); this pass did not independently inspect that paper. Nonzero winding leaves a Floquet loophole, so Theorem 7 alone does not establish doubling.

*Proof.* Each gate fixes its local empty vector up to `λ_g` and preserves the local one-site subspace. The normalized induced action is `λ_g⁻¹g` on that subspace and identity outside the gate. (2) *A path.* In each block choose a Hermitian logarithm with spectrum in `(−π,π]`: `L_k=e^{iA_k}`, `‖A_k‖≤π`. Put `V(t)=e^{itA_m}⋯e^{itA_1}`, so `V(0)=1` and `V(1)=V`. Put `θ_a=V^*δ_aV`, `η=V^*V'` and `Ω=ε_{abc}θ_aθ_bθ_c`, summed. Every `V(t)` has propagation at most `mD`. On each block, `‖[B_a−c,e^{itA_{k}}]‖≤D` for a suitable constant `c`. So `θ_a`, `η` and `δ_aη` are bounded uniformly in `t`, with propagation at most `2mD`.

(3) *Variation identity.* `(V^*)'=−ηV^*` gives `θ_a'=δ_aη+[θ_a,η]`. Also `δ_a(V^*)=−V^*(δ_aV)V^*` gives `δ_aθ_b−δ_bθ_a=−[θ_a,θ_b]`. Antisymmetrizing, `ε_{abc}(δ_cθ_a)θ_b=−Ω` and `ε_{abc}θ_a(δ_cθ_b)=Ω`, so `ε_{abc}δ_c(θ_aθ_b)=0`. Substitute `θ'`. Collect the `[θ,η]` terms into `[Ω,η]`. Move each `δη` factor to the right by commutators and relabel cyclically. This gives the exact identity
\[
Ω'=[Ω,η]+ε_{abc}\big([δ_aη,θ_bθ_c]+[θ_aδ_bη,θ_c]\big)+3ε_{abc}δ_c(θ_aθ_bη).
\]
The referee reports `[check]` `check_rcw.py`: on random 24-dimensional matrices with diagonal commuting `B_a` and `V(t)=e^{itA}e^{itB}`, the two sides agree to `3·10^{-7}` (finite differences), against entries of size 34.

(4) *Box traces.* Put `f_L(t)=L^{-3}Tr(1_LΩ(V(t)))`. This is a finite sum of smooth matrix entries. By (1) and (3), `|f_L'(t)|≤C/L`, with `C` independent of `t` and `L`. Since `f_L(0)=0`, `|f_L(1)|≤C/L`.

(5) *The periodic end point.* `V` commutes with translations, and translations shift `B_a` by constants. So `Ω(V)` commutes with translations, and `f_L(1)=τ(Ω(V))` for every `L`, where `τ(Y)=Tr(1_{cell 0}Y)`. Letting `L→∞` gives `τ(Ω(V))=0`.

(6) *Bloch form.* Fourier transform in `β` takes `V` to the trigonometric polynomial `V(K)` and `δ_a` to `±i∂_{K_a}`. It also gives `τ(Y)=(2π)^{-3}∫_{T³}tr Y(K)d³K`. Hence `τ(Ω(V))=(±i)³(2π)^{-3}·24π²·W₃[V]`. The factor is nonzero, so `W₃[V]=0`. □

**Use.** The one-site walk of Theorem 8 is a product of layers `⊕_g u_g`, with blocks `E₁(supp g)` in at most two adjacent cells. Each `u_g` preserves content parity, and under gatewise symmetry it preserves the isotypic multiplicity spaces. So Lemma RC-W applies to `V`, to each `V_σ` and, when gates commute with `G`, to each `V_ρ`. Theorem 9 therefore holds without the draft distillation’s added periodic-layer hypothesis. The proof uses finite matrices, Fourier series of trigonometric polynomials and one-variable calculus, so it is Lean-formalizable. It follows the standard argument that the odd Chern character varies by exact terms. The trace per unit volume replaces the integral, as in the noncommutative index theory of Bellissard and of Prodan and Schulz-Baldes. This is credited from memory and is not load-bearing.

**Scope of the repair.** The draft's homotopy presupposes periodic layers, which NN-a does not require. RC-W repairs that gap: in setting F under NN-a and condition E, draft Theorems H and 4.5 hold. At any quasienergy whose node set for `W_σ` is finite and consists of Weyl points, the net chirality is zero; in particular it cannot consist of a single Weyl node. ## 5. Records, zoom and downstream costs

**Theorem 10 (actual values; draft Lemma 5.1 and Proposition 5.2).** Under proposed PS-3g with Spin(10), the fixed point algebra on `V` is spanned by the projectors onto `∅`, `S_+`, `S_-`; on `V⁻` it is spanned by the empty and occupied projectors. Given the remaining TM1 hypotheses, `TM1.md` Theorems 1–5 apply to a covariant finite-spectrum even observable whose spectral projectors are these projectors.

*Proof.* `C`, `S_+`, `S_-` are pairwise inequivalent irreducibles, so Schur's lemma gives the fixed algebras. The projectors are even and covariant under lifts preserving the empty state and half-spin sectors. TM1's arguments use the stated finite observable, its disagreement projections, stationarity, covariance and their other explicit hypotheses; the proof is already in `TM1.md` Theorems 1–5. □

**Theorem 11 (record permanence; correction to draft Proposition 5.3).** In a history measure obeying L-point, if a record at `x` forms at `t` with random value `R_x`, then on the event of that record `ξ_{2n}(x)=R_x` for all `n≥t`, almost surely. If the event has probability 1 and `R_x=v` almost surely, the Born marginal obeys `ω'∘α^n(Π_x(v))=1` for all `n≥t`. TM1 Theorem 1 says formation at a fixed site and tick has probability zero or one. Whenever the record forms, the later marginal is the distribution of `R_x`, not necessarily a point mass.

*Proof.* The first statement is L-point. Apply the Born marginal and take probabilities. □

**GAP:** the draft treats “`x` records value `v`” as an unconditional almost-sure event. A record may have a random Born value. For example, a stationary identity tick on a perfectly correlated mixture of all-empty and all-occupied configurations can record at every point while the one-point occupation remains non-sharp. Taking `O=1` in this example refutes the draft corollary’s word “sharp”. Persistence still holds sample by sample. Under TM1 case (E), for a local excitation `ω'=ω(O*·O)/ω(O*O)`, a tick-1 disagreement witness `α(D)` commuting with `O` has zero expectation in `ω'` if it has zero expectation in `ω`: Cauchy–Schwarz and positivity imply `ω(O*O α(D))=0`. Finite range makes this true outside a bounded neighbourhood of `supp O`. Thus those points record at tick 1 and their *realized* values persist under L-point. To infer a bounded defect relative to a background history one needs a specified coupling or sharp background values; the draft does not provide one. L-site would require a rule identifying and transporting records with sites; the point-register law does not specify one. Named site labels are one possible additional mechanism, not a proved necessity. These are costs to TM1-i, not a resolution of it.

**RC-T (referee closure, CG16S_REFEREE.md) — awaiting one check.** Let `ω` be stationary, symmetric as in TM1 Theorem 4, and in case (E), with a tick of finite range `r`. Let `O` be local with `ω(O^*O)>0`, and put `ω′=ω(O^*·O)/ω(O^*O)`. Let `B` be the set of points within `r+1` of `supp O`. Then: (i) every `x∉B` lies in `F_1(ω′)`, so `t_x=1`; (ii) every history measure with the Born marginals of `ω′∘α^n` that obeys L-point has `ξ_{2n}(x)=ξ_2(x)` for all `n≥1` and all `x∉B`, almost surely; (iii) the law of `ξ_2(x)` is the Born law of `ω′∘α` at `x`, which need not be a point mass.

*Proof.* (i) Case (E) and stationarity give a pair `y,z` with `ω(α(D))=0` for `D=D_xy+D_xz`. `D` is a sum of two projections, so `0≤D` and `α(D)²≤2α(D)`. (ii) L-point at each point, and a countable intersection of sure events. (iii) Born marginals; the random-content example above with `O=1`. □

**Theorem 12 (coarse centres; corrected draft Proposition 5.4). RC-Z (referee closure, CG16S_REFEREE.md) — awaiting one check.** Under SC-odd with Spin(10), let a unital *-homomorphism `Φ` satisfy (G1) for `S_u`, with `|S_u|≥3`. Fine link registers are finite, with commuting endpoint actions. Then `cμ₄⊆spec ω_u^{(1)}` for some `c∈U(1)`.

*Proof.* `Φ(ω_u^{(1)})=∏_{x∈S_u}ω_x=M⊗Λ`, where `M=∏_xω_x^{mat}=i^{N_s(S_u)}` acts on the matter of `S_u`. `Λ` is the product of all `ω` end factors at points of `S_u`, on the finite tensor product of incident link registers. These factors commute, being on distinct links or commuting end actions of one link. The finite-dimensional unitary `Λ` has an eigenvalue `c`; `N_s(S_u)` takes values `0,1,2,3`, so `spec(M⊗Λ)⊇cμ₄`. Since `spec Φ(a)⊆spec a`, the result follows. Neither internal nor boundary link-purity is needed. □

**Scope.** Under (G1) with `|S_u|≥3`, the incident coarse end factors of `ω` at `u` are not all scalar: otherwise the coarse SC-odd point has spectrum only a scalar multiple of `{1,i}`.
