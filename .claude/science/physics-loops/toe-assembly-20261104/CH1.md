## 1. Definitions inferred by the distiller

1. A **periodic free update** is a one-body unitary commuting with translations by a finite-index sublattice, with a continuous Bloch matrix \(U(k)\). No assertion about an interacting, dynamical gauge field follows by replacing its quantum links with c-numbers.
2. Quasienergy \(w\) means eigenvalue \(e^{-iw}\). The cuts are \(0,\pi\). Away from both cuts, the sea projector \(P\) selects \(-\pi<w<0\). A PS-7r sea additionally requires a gap at \(\pi\). We call a cut-\(\pi\) crossing a light Floquet pocket only in the modulo-\(2\pi\) sense. Its physical interpretation needs a continuum bridge.
3. A **pocket decomposition** is a finite collection of disjoint closed balls containing the cut sets, with neither cut on a boundary. Orient each sphere as the boundary of its ball. Its filled-bundle charge is \(c=(i/2\pi)\int\mathrm{tr}(P\,dP\wedge dP)\). A free spectrum is **vector-like in this pocket sense** when these charges sum to zero in each preserved charge or weight block. This does not assert a mass term, pairing of fields, or an interacting spectrum.
4. A **regular Weyl node at a cut** has an isolated two-dimensional eigenspace there and local Hamiltonian \(h(q)=t(q)I+(Mq)\cdot\sigma+O(|q|^2)\), \(\det M\ne0\), with \(|t(q)|<|Mq|\) for every nonzero \(q\). Its chirality \(\chi\) is the lower-band Chern number on a small sphere. The strict inequality ensures one eigenvalue lies on each side of the cut. Other bands must remain separated from that cut in the ball.
5. A **charge sector** or torus **weight sector** is an invariant block of the one-body update and of the relevant projector. A claim per sector requires that preservation, not merely exact symmetry of a different interacting update.
6. A local operator has definite total charge \(q\) when its conjugation by the product of local gauge transformations over a region containing its full support is \(e^{iq\theta}\). Fermion parity refers to the matter CAR grading. Claims about such operators do not classify arbitrary nonlocal dressings, charged superselection sectors, or all excitations of a state.
7. A representation is **carried at** \(y\) if local gauge transformations at \(y\) act on its finite-dimensional operator space in that representation and transformations at every other site fix it. R-F asks that family operators be fermion odd. R-B asks for its individual components under a subgroup \(H\). R-M asks for a full exact-group multiplet whose restriction is the family, or family plus conjugate. These are conditional readings, not established properties of the infrared.
8. **IR** is the drafts' hypothesis of quasi-local odd operators with large-separation Weyl correlations and no other long-range connected correlations. Charged correlators require a gauge-covariant dressing or a charge-sector prescription. Massless gauge correlations also require the clause about other correlations to be refined. IR is not constructed here.
9. \(G_\infty\) denotes a proposed operational long-distance gauge group. Exact microscopic local gauge invariance is not literally broken by a gauge-invariant state. A definition in terms of relational operators, screening and spectral channels, and existence of the corresponding state, are open.

## 2. Law-independent free-update theorems

Set \(W_1\) to the winding of \(\det U\), and in three dimensions set \(W_3=(24\pi^2)^{-1}\int\mathrm{tr}(U^{-1}dU)^3\). Orientations fix one universal sign in F4.

**Theorem F1.** A finite product of layers of disjoint finite-block one-body unitaries is null-homotopic as a Bloch map if every layer is periodic under a finite-index sublattice. Its determinant is constant in momentum; all \(W_1\) and \(W_3\) vanish. The blocks may contain any finite number of modes.

*Proof.* Intersect the layer periods. For each layer choose one block per translation orbit. Their union is a fundamental domain. In its Bloch basis the layer is a constant unitary \(C\); in a common basis it is \(\Delta(k)C\Delta(k)^{-1}\), with diagonal phase matrix \(\Delta\). Join each \(C\) to the identity inside its unitary group and multiply the resulting layer paths. The determinant of each layer is \(\det C\). Homotopy invariance gives the winding statements. Finite periodic families of commuting, overlapping finite-range gates can be colored into disjoint periodic layers. \(\square\)

**Theorem F2.** If continuous \(U:T^d\to U(n)\) omits a fixed eigenvalue at all momenta, it is null-homotopic. Thus a PS-7r free sea has vanishing \(W_1,W_3\). Conversely \(W_3\ne0\) forces every quasienergy into the spectrum somewhere.

*Proof.* A logarithm with its branch cut at the omitted point defines continuous Hermitian \(H=i\log U\). The path \(e^{-itH}\) joins the identity to \(U\). The converse is immediate. \(\square\)

**Theorem F3.** For \(C^1\) \(U:T^3\to U(n)\) and a pocket decomposition, \(\sum_j c_j=0\). The same holds in each preserved charge or torus-weight block. In one dimension, if the cut set is finite, the jumps \(\mathrm{rank}\,P(k_j+0)-\mathrm{rank}\,P(k_j-0)\) sum to zero in each block.

*Proof.* On the complement of the pocket interiors, \(P\) is a smooth-enough Riesz projection. Differentiating \(P^2=P\) makes \(dP\) off-diagonal relative to \(P\), so \(d\mathrm{tr}(P\,dP\wedge dP)=\mathrm{tr}(dP)^3=0\). Stokes gives zero total boundary integral, with the boundary orientation opposite to each ball's. For \(C^1\) maps use smooth approximation on this compact cut-free region. Apply the argument to each block. In one dimension the rank is constant on each complementary interval; its jumps telescope around the circle. \(\square\)

**Lemma F3a and Corollary F3b.** At a regular Weyl node, \(c=\chi\) at \(0\) and \(c=-\chi\) at \(\pi\). If every cut point is such a node, \(\sum_{0}\chi=\sum_{\pi}\chi\); if \(\pi\) is gapped, the former sum is zero.

*Proof.* The two-band spectral projector and the filled projector of all other bands extend across the ball, so their boundary Chern numbers vanish. At \(0\) the sea fills the lower Weyl band. At \(\pi\), after reduction modulo \(2\pi\), it fills the upper band. The two band Chern numbers sum to zero. Apply F3. \(\square\)

**GAP F3a/F4 — tilt.** The draft's definition allows arbitrary \(t\), but its proof assumes the two eigenvalues straddle the cut. For \(h(q)=2q_3 I+q\cdot\sigma\), both eigenvalues are positive on the north axis and negative on the south axis; the zero set is a cone, not an isolated Weyl pocket. The regular-node condition above repairs the proof. Critical tilt is not resolved by that argument.

**Theorem F4.** For \(C^3\) \(U:T^3\to U(n)\), if all points at a chosen cut are finitely many regular Weyl nodes, then \(W_3=\sigma\sum_j\chi_j\), with one convention-dependent sign \(\sigma\), independent of the cut and of \(U\).

*Proof.* Remove small balls around the nodes. On their complement choose the logarithm cut at the selected eigenvalue and use \(e^{-isH}\) to contract \(U\). The winding 3-form is closed, so Stokes expresses the winding as the limit of integrals over each boundary sphere times \([0,1]\). The smooth complementary spectral block contributes zero in the shrinking limit. For the node, the lower projection tends to \(P_-(n)=(1-\widehat{Mn}\cdot\sigma)/2\), and the boundary homotopy tends, up to a scalar phase, to \(1+(e^{-2\pi is}-1)P_-(n)\). Its SU(2) part is the suspension of \(n\mapsto\widehat{Mn}\). Its degree is \(\operatorname{sgn}\det M\), up to orientation; the lower-band Chern number has the same determinant sign up to one fixed convention. The normalized SU(2) winding-degree identity gives the stated universal sign. \(\square\)

**Theorem K1 (endpoint).** Suppose a \(C^1\) periodic projector \(P_l\) defines a light bundle and the light generator preserves each stated charge block. Suppose either a Hermitian light Hamiltonian has a pocket decomposition at zero, or a unitary light update has a pocket decomposition and a \(\pi\) gap. Then its zero-cut pocket charges sum to zero in each block. A nonzero net Weyl chirality in a region requires compensating pocket charge outside it.

*Proof.* Extend the Hamiltonian to the full trivial bundle as \(H_l+c(1-P_l)\), with \(c>\|H_l\|\). For a unitary extend it as \(U_l\oplus e^{-i\pi/2}(1-P_l)\). These additions are cut-gapped and preserve the blocks. Apply the Stokes proof of F3 to the negative-energy projector, or F3 with the \(\pi\) gap. \(\square\)

**Limits.** F1 requires periodic layers, not merely a periodic product. F3 requires pockets; it does not cover arbitrary Fermi surfaces or nonsmooth updates without further work. K1 proves compensation only at a quasi-free endpoint. It does not prove that a particular interacting PS-8 gate releases partners. F4's degree theorem uses the credited normalization above. A nonzero winding and the one-dimensional shift do not evade F3's pocket accounting. In a Majorana/Nambu description the complexified map obeys F1–F4 as a matrix map; counting independent physical degrees and anomalies needs the particle-hole identification. Opposite momenta have opposite total pocket charge under particle-hole symmetry: conjugation reverses the Berry curvature and the occupied complement reverses it again, while \(k\mapsto-k\) reverses the sphere orientation. Thus the total Nambu sum alone is redundant; a preserved weight block can still constrain it.

## 3. One-mode law \(L_{\rm cube}\)

The site CAR modes are \(c_x\). The fixed law has elementary matter charge \(1\), finite quantum links, and spatial group \(2\mathbb Z^3\rtimes O_{c_0}\), which acts transitively. The following parity claims concern local operators under the global product of local gauge transformations.

**Lemma S1.** For a local homogeneous operator of total charge \(Q\), its matter parity is \((-1)^Q\). Hence an odd local field has odd charge and a neutral local field is even. The same holds if each site's elementary charge is \(\pm q\), after division by nonzero \(q\).

*Proof.* Expand in CAR monomials times link operators. The global gauge action on a finite region containing both endpoints cancels on links. Each matter creator or annihilator changes charge by \(\pm q\) and parity by one. Every monomial of a definite charge has the same parity. \(\square\)

The 1+1D 3-4-5-0 set cannot be represented entirely by such odd local fields with this exact charge: charges 4 and 0 violate S1. Site transitivity alone is not a proof of a universal charge normalization for arbitrary link representations; the fixed law supplies the uniform charge used here.

**Theorem S2.** For a family including \(\nu^c\), let \(X=aY+b(B-L)\), with \(Y(Q)=1/6\). All six multiplet charges are odd multiples of \(q\) exactly when \(a=2\alpha q\), \(b=\beta q\), \(\alpha\) is even, \(\beta\) odd, and \(\alpha+\beta\equiv0\pmod3\).

*Proof.* In units of \(q\), the charges on \((Q,u^c,d^c,L,e^c,\nu^c)\) are \(((\alpha+\beta)/3,-(4\alpha+\beta)/3,(2\alpha-\beta)/3,-\alpha-\beta,2\alpha+\beta,\beta)\). Oddness of the last three forces \(\beta\) odd and \(\alpha\) an even integer; the first enforces divisibility by 3. These conditions make each remaining numerator odd and divisible by 3. \(\square\)

Hypercharge and electric charge alone fail the local parity test. \(B-L\), in units with \((\alpha,\beta)=(0,3)\), and \(X=5(B-L)-4Y\), with \((-2,5)\), pass. \(X\) has charges \(10\cdot1,5\cdot(-3),1\cdot5\), whose linear and cubic sums vanish. This does not rule out emergent gauge factors or nonlocal charged sectors. LW1 proves abelian site-local one-body frame actions; its cell-frame B5(b) remains open in the frozen text. The stronger B5B draft has its own hypotheses. Thus CH1's exact Standard Model group is not obtained from the site-local \(U(1)\), but the draft's unconditional failure claim for every possible reading is not established.

**Lemma C2 (odd clusters).** For an odd finite set \(S\), let \(C_S=\prod_{x\in S}c_x\) in fixed order. Then \(C_S^2=0\), \(C_S^\dagger C_S=\prod_Sn_x\), and \(C_SC_S^\dagger=\prod_S(1-n_x)\). On the empty-or-full subspace, these are CAR modes; disjoint odd clusters anticommute. Their charge is \(-|S|\). Equal-size cluster hopping dressed by oppositely transforming link paths is gauge invariant and preserves the product of empty-or-full subspaces.

*Proof.* Move the CAR factors together. The two products are the full and empty projectors; exchanging disjoint odd products produces a minus sign. The path phases cancel those of the source and target, and the hop swaps full and empty clusters or annihilates the state. The adjoint has the reverse action. \(\square\)

**GAP C2 (free embedding).** The draft infers that every free cluster walk embeds exactly with finite quantum links. The invariant-subspace proof does not show this: finite link raising operators are not unitary and can annihilate boundary-flux states. The dressed gate remains gauge invariant, but its matter action depends on the link state. Nearest-neighbor factorization is another separate input.

**Lemma C3.** A partition of \(\mathbb Z^3\) into finite equal-size clusters, invariant under \(2\mathbb Z^3\rtimes O_{c_0}\), has cluster size dividing 8. In the 1D analogue the size divides 2. In particular an odd cluster has size 1.

*Proof.* The spatial group acts transitively on clusters. The even-translation subgroup acts freely on them: a finite cluster cannot be fixed by a nonzero translation. In the finite quotient by even translations, each cluster orbit contains exactly \(s\) of the 8 site classes. Transitivity makes all such orbits equivalent, so their number is \(8/s\), an integer. Replace 8 by 2 in one dimension. \(\square\)

**K2.** A set of elementary charge-1 Weyl fields with net chirality \(\chi\) has cubic and mixed gravitational coefficients \(\chi\). *Proof.* Both sums are \(\sum_i\chi_i\), since \(q_i=1\). \(\square\)

At \(\mu=0\), the draft imports two Weyl fields of each chirality from LW1's free cube spectrum; at positive \(\mu\) that free zero-cut node is gapped. K1 excludes an unbalanced retained elementary set at a qualifying quasi-free endpoint. K2 makes such a set anomalous. The interacting exclusion needs AM; it is not a theorem of this part. The null-vector and small-charge searches in pass 1 are guides, not proofs of a symmetric gap.

**Lemma S1-5.** On \(L5\text{-U}\), and \(L5\text{-O}\) with the stated irreducible registers, a homogeneous local operator has integer total \(U(1)_N\) charge \(q\) and matter parity \((-1)^q\).

*Proof.* On matter the generator is \(N\) on U and \(N-5/2\) on O. The product of local rotations by \(\pi\) on a region containing both ends of all supported links acts as matter parity; link transformations cancel or act trivially by the stated register-center identity. *Proof.* Apply C2 to the selected modes. A representation of ten CAR modes is a representation of \(M_{2^{10}}(\mathbb C)\), so any nonzero invariant space has dimension at least 1024. \(\square\)

**Lemma V (free anomaly ledger).** Under F3's pocket hypotheses, \(\sum_jc_j f(w_j)=0\) for any function \(f\) of a preserved torus weight. *Proof.* Group the sum by weight and apply F3. For torus element \(X\), choose \(f(w)=w(X)^3\) or \(w(X)\). **Theorem O-M (conditional operator obstruction).** For the law's tensor-class local operator algebra, any full \(Spin(10)\) multiplet whose restriction to a Case I \(H\) contains \((3,2)\) has dimension at least 40. In Case II it contains no weak doublet. Thus no such multiplet restricts to just \(F_t\) or \(F_t\oplus F_t^*\).

*Proof.* Tensor-class torus weights are integer 5-vectors, and the \(D_5\) Weyl group permutes coordinates and flips any even number of signs. A \((3,2)\) weight has color coordinates \((k+1,k,k)\) up to permutation and weak-coordinate difference \(\pm1\); it has at least two nonzero coordinates. If at most four are nonzero, the signed-permutation orbit has at least \(\binom5r2^r\ge40\) weights. If all five are nonzero with unequal magnitudes, it has at least \(5\cdot16=80\); equal integer magnitudes cannot give color differences of 1. Each distinct weight needs a dimension. In Case II the weak \(SU(2)\) central element maps to the nontrivial kernel element of \(Spin(10)\to SO(10)\), which acts trivially in tensor class but as minus one on a doublet. \(\square\)

### 4.2 Anomaly and endpoint ledger

**Lemma O-A.** For every finite-dimensional representation of \(Spin(10)\), \(\operatorname{tr}X=\operatorname{tr}X^3=0\) for \(X\in\mathfrak{so}(10)\).

*Proof.* Simplicity makes every linear trace vanish. On a five-dimensional Cartan, the cubic trace is invariant under even sign changes. Each cubic monomial has an odd-power coordinate and at least one unused coordinate; flipping those two changes its sign. Averaging kills every cubic monomial. Conjugacy to the Cartan completes the proof. \(\square\)

For the free U law, retaining a net \(\chi\) elementary Weyl \(5\)'s gives \(SU(5)^3=\chi\), mixed \(U(1)_N SU(5)^2=\chi/2\), cubic \(U(1)_N^3=5\chi\), and gravitational \(U(1)_N=5\chi\), using \(T(5)=1/2\). The corresponding O carrier is the real 10, admits an invariant Majorana bilinear, and has no perturbative \(Spin(10)\) anomaly by O-A. Its restriction to the standard subgroup is \((3,1)\oplus(\bar3,1)\oplus(1,2)\oplus(1,2)\), so it contains no \(Q=(3,2)\). The elementary U \(5\) also contains no \(Q\). These representation facts do not establish an interacting gap. K1 requires a preserved weight sector and a qualifying endpoint.

**Theorem U-A (one-family \(U(5)\) arithmetic).** Suppose the family is a \(10_a\oplus\bar5_b\oplus1_c\) of \(U(5)\), with a common chirality and \(T(5)=1/2\). Its pure \(SU(5)^3\) anomaly vanishes. Its gravitational, cubic and mixed \(U(1)_N\) coefficients are
\[
10a+5b+c,\qquad 10a^3+5b^3+c^3,\qquad (3a+b)/2.
\]
They all vanish exactly on \((a,b,c)=u(1,-3,5)\). For the one-site carriers they are \((-40,-400,-5)\).
