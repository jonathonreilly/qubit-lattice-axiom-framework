## 2. Exact consequences of local invariance

**Theorem 1 (global action and implementers; draft Lemma G).** Define, for local `A`, `κ_g(A)=∏_{x∈S(A)}a_{x,g}(A)`. This extends to a norm-continuous `Spin(10)` action on `𝔄`. It commutes with `α` and with cell lifts that permute the local gauge actions. Every gauge-invariant state is `κ`-invariant. Moreover `κ_{−1}=id` and `κ_ϖ=Θ`. Its GNS space has a unique strongly continuous representation `U(g)π(A)Ω=π(κ_g(A))Ω`, with `U(g)Ω=Ω` and `U(−1)=1`. If the state is stationary, the tick implementer and its spectral projections commute with `U`. The analogous statement holds for a state-preserving cell lift.

*Proof.* Local actions commute, preserve support, and act trivially outside gauge support; hence the finite product is independent of enlargement. It is an isometric automorphism, and local continuity extends by uniform approximation. The same finite-support calculation proves commutation with the tick and cell lifts. Local invariance implies global invariance. The two central identities are the source inputs in Definition 3. The GNS formula preserves norms because `ω∘κ_g=ω`; the inverse is obtained from `g⁻¹`. It is strongly continuous on the dense cyclic domain, hence everywhere. The tick and cell implementers are constructed identically and commute on that domain. Functional calculus gives commutation with spectral projections. ∎

**Theorem 2 (averaging; draft Theorem E).** A gauge-invariant `ω` is even and equals `ω∘E`. For a covariant multiplet `κ_g(A_i)=∑_jρ(g)_{ji}A_j`, its expectation vector obeys `v=ρ(g)^T v`; it vanishes if `ρ` has no trivial summand. If `∫a_{x,g}(A)dg=0` and `C` is fixed by every `a_{x,g}`, then `ω(AC)=ω(CA)=0`. Thus fields carried in nontrivial local representations at different sites have zero two-point functions at any tick separation. Every odd `A` has `E(A)=0`; for local odd `A` and local `C` of disjoint gauge support, `ω(AC)=ω(CA)=0`.

*Proof.* Theorem 1 gives evenness; Haar averaging gives `ω=ω∘E`. Invariance gives the displayed vector equation. The Haar projection onto invariant vectors has the same rank as its transpose, so absence of a trivial summand forces `v=0`. Averaging the local action at `x` proves the product identities. Tick covariance preserves invariance at `x` of a field initially carried at `y≠x`. Since `E∘Θ=E`, oddness gives `E(A)=0`; continuity covers quasi-local `A`. Factorization of `E` for disjoint gauge support proves the last assertion. ∎

**Theorem 3 (tail bound; draft Corollary IR).** For odd quasi-local `ψ,φ` centred at `x,y`, `|x−y|∞>2r` implies
\[
|ω(ψ^*φ)|\le δ_{ψ,x}(r)\|φ\|+(\|ψ\|+δ_{ψ,x}(r))δ_{φ,y}(r).
\]
For a translate `φ=τ_tψ`, this is at most `3‖ψ‖δ_{ψ,x}(r)`. Local odd fields have zero correlation once their gauge supports are disjoint.

*Proof.* Approximate both fields locally and take their odd parts; the parity projection cannot increase the approximation error. The local approximants have disjoint gauge supports, so their correlation vanishes by Theorem 2. Expand the difference and take the infimum. For translates use `δ≤‖ψ‖`. ∎

Under the additional stated premise that a chosen equal-time free Weyl correlator has a nonzero `r⁻³` leading behaviour along an unbounded sequence, the bound excludes agreement for a translate of a field with tail `o(r⁻³)`. The draft credits this Weyl asymptotic from memory; it is not derived here. The same algebraic bound holds on `L5-U` and `L_cube` if their cited regional parity and averaging identities hold. It does not exclude slowly dressed fields or gauge-invariant composite correlations. Consequently the short-tail formulation of IR used by CH1-5O-i, CH1-5U-i, AM and AM-5 needs care; the general obligations remain open.

## 3. Tensor-class and spectral statements

**Theorem 4 (colour exotic; draft Lemma X).** A finite-dimensional tensor-class `Spin(10)` representation whose Case I restriction contains `(3,2)_y` also contains an `H`-type whose colour representation is neither `1`, `3` nor `3̄`.

*Proof.* Tensor-class torus weights have integer coordinates `n∈Z⁵`, and the D5 Weyl group acts by permutations and an even number of sign changes. A weight restricting to `(3,2)` has colour coordinates `{k+1,k,k}` or their negatives and has `n₄−n₅=±1`. Thus at least two coordinates are nonzero. If one coordinate is zero, move two nonzero entries and the zero into the first three positions and use the zero to flip signs independently. If none is zero, use paired flips to make one of the first two entries positive and the other negative. In either case an orbit weight has colour spread at least two. The colour weights of `1`, `3`, and `3̄` have spread at most one, and restriction preserves the indicated colour weight. Hence a component is exotic. ∎

Pass 1 reports these scratch checks: all 432 qualifying weights with coordinates bounded by 3 have an orbit weight of colour spread at least 2; the smallest orbit has 40 elements; the 16 spinor weights have spread at most 1; and exterior powers Λ¹ through Λ⁵ have respectively 0, 6, 30, 66 and 84 exotic weights, with Λ¹ having no `(3,2)` weight and Λ²–Λ⁵ having both. These reported checks are not premises or independent proof. Their raw output was not available for this distillation. The proof covers arbitrary finite-dimensional tensor-class representations, not spinor-class `16`s.

**Theorem 5 (spectral selection; draft Theorem L).** For gauge-invariant `ω` and bounded `F` commuting with `U`, the set of `H`-types of quasi-local fields with nonzero `F`-weight is the restriction of a tensor-class `Spin(10)` representation. If it contains `(3,2)_y`, it contains a colour exotic. The partner can be local and odd if the original field is odd; if the original field is local, the partner can be chosen in its global gauge orbit span and with the same gauge support.

*Proof.* The compact-group character projection `P_τ=d_τ∫_H overline{χ_τ(h)}κ_h dh` maps local operators to local operators of type `τ`, preserving gauge support and parity. Apply it to local approximants of a quasi-local field. Nonzero `F`-weight persists for a sufficiently close approximant.

For a local `A`, its orbit span `V` is finite dimensional. The map `T(B)=Fπ(B)Ω` intertwines `κ` with `U`; its image `M` is a finite-dimensional unitary tensor-class representation. Complete reducibility and intertwining show that a nonzero `(3,2)` image occurs in an irreducible summand. Theorem 4 gives an exotic component in that summand. Its preimage in the corresponding `H`-isotypic part of `V` is the required field. Centrality of `ϖ` preserves parity throughout the orbit. Taking the direct sum of all such images gives exactly the asserted set of types; the converse follows by taking preimages. ∎

**Theorem 6 (finite-orbit correlations; draft Corollary L.2).** Let `V` be a finite-dimensional `κ`-invariant space and let `γ_j,γ'_j` commute with `κ`. If a `(3,2)` field in `V` has `ω(γ_j(A)^*γ'_j(A))` not exponentially decaying in `j`, some exotic-type field in `V` has the same property. This also holds within the odd subspace when the field is odd.

*Proof.* Haar-average an inner product on `V`. The forms `b_j(A,B)=ω(γ_j(A)^*γ'_j(B))` are invariant. Schur's lemma makes each form `1⊗b_{j,λ}` on each isotypic summand `λ⊗M_λ` and zero between inequivalent summands. Expand the field in an orthonormal basis of its `(3,2)` components. Its correlation is a finite sum of diagonal multiplicity forms; one fails exponential decay. Theorem 4 supplies an exotic unit vector in the same `λ`; pairing it with the same multiplicity vector gives the same scalar correlation. Parity is a central scalar on each irreducible summand. ∎

**Theorem 7 (uniform correlation bound; draft Corollary L.4).** Suppose `ω` is invariant under each `γ_j=α^{n_j}τ_{t_j}` and these automorphisms commute with `κ`. If fixed `C,μ>0` satisfy
\[
|ω(φ^*γ_j(φ))|\le C\|π(φ)Ω\|^2e^{-μj}
\]
for every odd quasi-local field of exotic `H`-type, the same bound holds for every odd `(3,2)`-type quasi-local field `ψ`.

*Proof.* Decompose the GNS representation as the Hilbert sum `⊕_λ λ⊗𝓜_λ`. Each `γ_j` implementer commutes with `U` and acts as `1⊗K_{j,λ}`. Decompose `π(ψ)Ω` into `(3,2)` vectors `∑_k e_k⊗m_k`. For every nonzero summand Theorem 4 gives an exotic unit vector `e_X` in the same `λ`. Compact-group matrix-coefficient projections, followed by the `H`-isotypic projection, produce an odd quasi-local field whose GNS vector is `e_X⊗m_k`. Apply the assumed bound and sum the absolute values. Orthogonality gives the norm on the right. ∎

**Conditional consequence (draft Corollary L.3).** If “light” means nonzero weight in every nonempty quasienergy window `0<|w|<m` and “gapped” means zero weight in one such window for every odd non-family field, Theorem 5 contradicts exactly `F_t` light content when the family contains `(3,2)`. For correlation definitions the contradiction holds for finite gauge orbits by Theorem 6, and for arbitrary quasi-local fields under Theorem 7's common constants. Local short-tailed free-Weyl fields are already excluded under Theorem 3's asymptotic premise. A nonuniform gap for infinite-orbit fields is not covered. A zero-quasienergy eigenvector is also excluded by the chosen punctured-window definition; another spectral definition requires a separate check.

**GAP: identification of CH1's light spectrum.** Exact local invariance does not by itself identify the effective charged spectrum with these GNS vectors or make R-A the intended reading of CH1-5O-i. Hence the draft's “refuted as worded,” “R-M forced,” and unconditional CH1(a) failure are proved only under the stated R-A definitions. They are not a refutation of CH1-5O-i in R-E. Under R-A no spinor-class vector appears because `U(−1)=1`. The analogous compact-group argument on `L5-U` yields `U(5)` multiplets; CG1K's `10⊕5̄⊕1` family decomposition shows this particular tensor-class exotic obstruction does not follow there. It does not construct the spectrum.

## 4. Centre, finite registers, and lifts

**Theorem 8 (centre and parity; draft Proposition N).** (i) `𝔄^G⊂𝔄^even`. An odd field times an even field cannot be gauge invariant unless the product is zero. (ii) `Z` fixes every vector in any finite-dimensional `κ`-invariant even operator space, and in its sums, tensor products and duals. (iii) For a Case I embedding with even `a=α`, `±ϖ∉H`; a bosonic-data stabilizer containing `H` therefore contains the strictly larger `HZ`. (iv) The corresponding parity statements hold for `L5-U` under its cited identity with central `−I₅`.

*Proof.* An invariant operator is fixed by `κ_ϖ=Θ`, proving (i), including for quasi-local operators. Theorem 1 makes both `ϖ` and `−1` trivial on the even space, proving (ii). CG1K Theorem O-I states that even `α` is equivalent to `−I₁₀` not lying in the image of `H` in `SO(10)`; both `±ϖ` project to it. This proves (iii). For (iv), use the source's identity that regional `−I₅` induces parity and the centrality of `−I₅`. ∎

**GAP: effective parity bridge.** The draft infers that an effective theory has no odd invariant observable if every effective invariant observable has a counterpart in the law. Existence of a counterpart alone does not preserve parity: an ungraded correspondence can map an odd effective element to an even element. The corrected conditional assertion requires a parity-preserving observable identification, including a faithful nonzero image for the odd observable. The Standard Model with a total-singlet `ν^c` has an odd invariant `ν^c`; `ε_{ab}Φ^aL^b` is another odd invariant. A parity-preserving faithful match to `𝔄^G` is therefore impossible. This is a conditional comparison obstruction, not a proof that every effective description has gauged parity.

The draft also observes that no Standard Model gauge element acts as `−1` on `L` and `+1` on the Higgs doublet. Indeed an element with these scalar actions has, on the doublets, phases `(-1)^e e^{-iθ/2}` and `(-1)^e e^{iθ/2}`; their product is `1`, whereas the desired product is `−1`. This uses the stated hypercharges and standard representation normalization. The centre result alone does not determine EW1's effective group.

**Theorem 9 (finite-register commuting multiplets; draft Proposition Q).** Let connected compact `K` act continuously by automorphisms on a finite-dimensional C*-algebra. A nontrivial irreducible covariant multiplet cannot have all its entries and their adjoints mutually commuting.

*Proof.* Otherwise its generated C*-algebra is finite-dimensional and commutative. The action permutes its finitely many minimal projections. Continuity and connectedness make each permutation constant, hence every projection and generator is fixed. This contradicts nontriviality. ∎

The theorem applies to finite-region algebras when the chosen local or global action preserves the region. It does not rule out a classical effective field, noncommuting quantum Higgs variables, or a commuting limit. If `X_{i,a}` belong to mutually commuting region algebras, then `‖[N⁻¹∑_iX_{i,a},N⁻¹∑_iX_{i,b}]‖≤N⁻¹ max_i‖[X_{i,a},X_{i,b}]‖`. This follows by expanding the commutator.

**Theorem 10 (a proper stabilizer containing the centre; draft Attack A2).** In the standard Case I embedding with `Y=i diag(−2,−2,−2,3,3)/6`, consider `v₁=Y` in the adjoint and `v₂=f⊗f` with `f=f₁∧⋯∧f₅`, `f_j=e_{2j−1}−ie_{2j}`, in `Λ⁵(C¹⁰)⊗Λ⁵(C¹⁰)`. Their joint stabilizer is closed, contains `H` and `Z`, and has Lie algebra `su(3)⊕su(2)⊕u(1)` embedded as the trace-zero subalgebra of `u(3)⊕u(2)`.

*Proof.* The unequal absolute values `1/3` and `1/2` split the real representation into its colour and weak blocks. Commuting with `Y` gives `u(3)⊕u(2)`, of dimension 13. On the complex volume form `f`, `U(5)` acts by a determinant character or its inverse. Fixing `f⊗f` imposes zero infinitesimal trace, leaving dimension 12 and the stated Lie algebra. The trace-zero block scalar direction is proportional to `Y`, so the connected SM image fixes both vectors. The element `−I₁₀` acts by `+1` on `Λ²` and on `Λ⁵⊗Λ⁵`; its lifts and the kernel `±1` therefore fix both. The stabilizer is closed by continuity. ∎

This supplies a proper closed stabilizer containing `HZ`; its identity component is `H` up to the usual global-kernel convention, and the quotient by that component is finite. The proof does **not** establish that the full stabilizer equals `HZ` or that the law realizes this order. Thus the centre alone cannot prove that every proper subgroup containing `H` is impossible. Pass 1 reports floating-point dimension checks; they were not rerun.

**Theorem 11 (dark-witness loop; draft Attack A4).** For the original `L5-O[S_+]` dark product state `Φ₀` of the cited `VC1_5_DRAFT.md` construction and its gauge average `ω₀=Φ₀∘E`, a gauge-invariant vector loop that uses every link in its path exactly once has expectation zero.

*Proof.* Gauge invariance gives `ω₀(W_C)=Φ₀(W_C)`. The product state factorizes over distinct links. Each loop summand has one factor `U_ℓ^{ab}=iη^y_aη^x_b` on each link. Acting on the link vacuum it changes one occupation at each end, so its vacuum expectation vanishes. Every summand therefore has zero expectation. ∎

For `D_ω(W)=ω(W*W)+ω(WW*)−2ω(W)−2ω(W*)+2`, positivity gives `D_{ω₀}(cW_C)≥2` for any scalar `c`. Thus the draft's SC-loop criterion `D<η` fails for `η<2` for these loops. **GAP: comparison.** A zero loop expectation does not alone prove that every weak-coupling comparison class has a uniformly nonzero corresponding expectation, nor does it identify a tolerance. That needs a pinned normalization, class and quantitative bound. It also does not establish that `ω₀` matches an infinite-coupling theory for all comparison observables.

**Theorem 12 (two lifts, conditional on the original tick; draft Proposition P).** Assume the cited source's dark product state `Φ₀` and its gauge average `ω₀` are stationary for the original tick. They are lifts of the same invariant state. `ω₀` is invariant under all local `Spin(10)` actions. `Φ₀` is invariant under the standard local `SU(5)` and not under all local `Spin(10)`. Each `Φ₀∘a_{x,g}` is another stationary lift, with its local stabilizer at `x` conjugated by `g` and its other local stabilizers unchanged.

*Proof.* `E` fixes invariant operators and satisfies `E∘a_{x,g}=E`; hence both states agree on `𝔄^G` and have the same gauge average. The source supplies stationarity and gauge invariance of `ω₀`. Traceless number-conserving `su(5)` Gauss generators annihilate the local matter and rishon vacua, proving `SU(5)` invariance. Full local invariance would force each matter occupation to have expectation `1/2`, whereas the product vacuum has expectation zero. Tick covariance makes each transformed state stationary; the composition law gives the conjugated stabilizer, and different-site gauge maps commute. ∎

**GAP: added gates.** A gauge-invariant extra gate need not preserve `Φ₀` or `ω₀`. The source's stationary-witness argument is therefore limited to its original tick, or to added gates separately shown to preserve the states. The calculation proves a proper stabilizer containing `SU(5)`, not that it is exactly `SU(5)`. It demonstrates lift dependence; it does not by itself identify a physical breaking pattern.
