## 2. Theorem L is idle on gauge-invariant data

**Proposition V (the exactness results exclude no gauge-invariant data).** Fix an `L5-O` update. Let `𝒮^G` be the set of states `φ` of `𝔄^G` with `φ∘α = φ` (and `φ∘β_s = φ` for every cell lift, when cell symmetry is asked).
(i) `φ ↦ φ∘E` is a bijection from `𝒮^G` onto the set of stationary (cell-symmetric) gauge-invariant states of `𝔄`. Its inverse is restriction.
(ii) Every state in its image satisfies the conclusions of Lemma G, Theorem E, Theorem L, Corollaries L.2, L.4 and IR of `CH1_5O_DRAFT.md`.
(iii) Let `𝒫` be any property of a state that depends only on its restriction to `𝔄^G`. A stationary (cell-symmetric) gauge-invariant state with `𝒫` exists if and only if one exists that has `𝒫` and satisfies every conclusion in (ii). Clauses (a) and (b) of CH1-5O-E, in each reading of section 1.3, are such properties. So Theorem L and Lemma X neither imply nor exclude them.
(iv) For `O ∈ 𝔄^G` and every `g`, `U(g)π(O)Ω = π(O)Ω`. So the only `H`-type that Theorem L(iv) can give a vector `π(O)Ω` with `O` gauge invariant is `(1,1)_0`.

*Proof.* (i) `VC1_5.md` line 35: "Restriction gives an affine bijection from gauge-invariant states on `A` to states on `A^{gauge}`, with inverse `φ↦φ∘E`. The map `E` commutes with `α` and the cell lifts." The tick maps `𝔄^G` into itself, because it commutes with every local gauge map (`CH1_5O_DRAFT.md` Lemma G(ii)). For `φ ∈ 𝒮^G`, `φ∘E∘α = φ∘α∘E = φ∘E`; the second step uses `E(𝔄) ⊆ 𝔄^G` and `φ∘α = φ` there. The cell lifts are the same. Conversely, the restriction of a stationary gauge-invariant state is `α`-invariant on `𝔄^G`. (ii) The hypotheses of those results are an `L5-O` update and a gauge-invariant state, stationary or cell symmetric where the statement says so. (iii) follows from (i) and (ii). The products of observables of `Λ_r` and their adjoints lie in `𝔄^G`, so (a) and (b) are statements about the values of `ω` on `𝔄^G`. (iv) For local `O ∈ 𝔄^G`, `κ_g(O)` is a finite product of local gauge maps applied to `O`, so it is `O`; continuity of `κ_g` gives the rest. Then `U(g)π(O)Ω = π(κ_g(O))Ω = π(O)Ω` (Lemma G(v)). ∎

So the route "Theorem L forces the comparison data to contain colour exotics at the same scale" has no content. If Theorem L implied that conclusion, the conclusion would hold for every stationary gauge-invariant state, because Theorem L does (Proposition V(ii)). By (iii) it would then be a statement about all stationary states of `𝔄^G`, to be proved without Theorem L. Theorem L reads the `H`-types of gauge-variant fields in a fixed frame of `Spin(10)`. Comparison data never contain such a field. In reading R-E′ they are pure-gauge loops and contain no fermion field at all. The definition of a colour-exotic type (`CH1_5O_DRAFT.md` line 67) also counts the gluon octet `(8,1)_0`, which every `G_∞ ⊇ H` carries as a light field; that is no obstruction.

*The same in the comparison model.* Let `ν` be a frozen gauge-Higgs measure (section 3). Let `f_1, …, f_d` be bounded functions of `(U, φ)` that the gauge maps at one site `x` carry by a representation `ρ` with no invariant vector. Then `E_ν(f_i) = Σ_jρ(h_x)_{ji}E_ν(f_j)` for every `h_x`, and averaging over `h_x` gives `E_ν(f) = 0`, as in the proof of Theorem E(E2). So `ν` obeys the exactness results. Yet its gauge-invariant data are those of a `K`-gauge measure with `K` proper (Theorem C). Exactness of the gauge group and a match to a proper subgroup coexist in one model.

## 3. Frozen complementarity

### 3.1 Stabilizers and induced representations

**Lemma S (every closed subgroup is a stabilizer).** Let `G` be a compact Lie group and `K ⊆ G` closed. There are a finite-dimensional unitary representation `(W, σ)` of `G` and `v ∈ W` with `{g : σ(g)v = v} = K`.

*Proof.* Let `G` act on `C(G)` by right translation, `(R_hf)(x) = f(xh)`. Haar invariance makes `R_h` unitary for the `L^2` inner product. (1) Fix `g ∉ K`. `G/K` is compact Hausdorff and `gK ≠ K`, so Urysohn's lemma gives a continuous `f̄` on `G/K` with `f̄(gK) = 1` and `f̄(K) = 0`. Put `f(x) = f̄(xK)`. Then `f(xk) = f(x)` for `k ∈ K`, `f(g) = 1` and `f(1) = 0`. By the Peter-Weyl theorem (cited) there is a finite sum `p` of matrix coefficients of finite-dimensional representations with `‖f − p‖_∞ < 1/3`. Put `p_K(x) = ∫_Kp(xk)dk`. Since `f(xk) = f(x)`, `‖f − p_K‖_∞ < 1/3`. The matrix coefficients of one finite-dimensional representation span a finite-dimensional space that every `R_h` maps to itself, so `p_K` lies in a finite-dimensional `R`-invariant space `V_g`. `R_kp_K = p_K` for `k ∈ K`, and `(R_gp_K)(1) = p_K(g) > 2/3 > 1/3 > p_K(1)`, so `R_gp_K ≠ p_K`. (2) The finite intersections of the stabilizers of these `p_K` (one for each `g ∉ K`) are closed subgroups containing `K`. Closed subgroups of a compact Lie group satisfy the descending chain condition (cited: a strictly smaller closed subgroup has smaller dimension, or the same identity component and fewer components). So the family has a minimal member `K_0 = Stab(p_1) ∩ ⋯ ∩ Stab(p_n)`. For `g ∉ K`, the intersection of `K_0` with the stabilizer of the `p_K` made for `g` is in the family and inside `K_0`, so it equals `K_0`; hence `g ∉ K_0`. So `K_0 ⊆ K ⊆ K_0`. Put `W = V_1 ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ V_n`, `σ = R`, `v = (p_1, …, p_n)`. ∎

**Lemma S′ (every representation of `K` sits in one of `G`).** Let `(W_1, σ_1)` be a finite-dimensional unitary representation of a closed `K ⊆ G`. There are a finite-dimensional unitary representation `(W_2, σ_2)` of `G` and a `K`-equivariant isometry `j: W_1 → W_2`.

*Proof.* Let `𝒱 = {f ∈ C(G; W_1) : f(xk) = σ_1(k)^{-1}f(x) for k ∈ K}`, with `G` acting by `(λ_hf)(x) = f(h^{-1}x)`. Evaluation `ev(f) = f(1)` obeys `ev(λ_kf) = f(k^{-1}) = σ_1(k)f(1)`, so it is `K`-equivariant. It is onto: for `w ∈ W_1` and continuous `χ ≥ 0` on `G`, `f_{χ,w}(x) = ∫_Kχ(xk)σ_1(k)w\,dk` lies in `𝒱` (substitute `k → k'^{-1}k`), and `f_{χ,w}(1) = ∫_Kχ(k)σ_1(k)w\,dk` tends to `w` when `χ|_K` concentrates at `1` with `∫_Kχ = 1`; a subspace of a finite-dimensional space that comes arbitrarily close to every vector is the whole space. The `λ`-finite elements are dense in `𝒱`: approximate `f` uniformly by `f'` with coordinates in finite sums of matrix coefficients (Peter-Weyl, cited), and apply `P f'(x) = ∫_Kσ_1(k)f'(xk)dk`; `P` maps into `𝒱`, fixes `𝒱`, does not increase the sup norm, and commutes with every `λ_h`, so `Pf'` is `λ`-finite and close to `f`. So `ev` is onto from the `λ`-finite elements. Choose `λ`-finite `f_1, …, f_m` whose values at `1` span `W_1`, let `W_2` be the span of their `λ`-orbits (finite dimensional) with a `G`-invariant inner product, and `σ_2 = λ`. Then `ev: W_2 → W_1` is a `K`-equivariant surjection, `ev^*` is a `K`-equivariant injection, and `j = ev^*(ev\,ev^*)^{-1/2}` is a `K`-equivariant isometry. ∎

### 3.2 The theorem

**Theorem C (frozen complementarity).** Let `G = Spin(10)` (any compact Lie group will do), `K ⊆ G` closed, and `(W, σ)`, `v` as in Lemma S. Let `μ` be a `K`-gauge measure on `K^L` and `m` the Haar probability measure of `G`. Define `Ψ: K^L × G^S → G^L` by `Ψ(u, g)_ℓ = g_xu_ℓg_y^{-1}`, the measure `μ̃ = Ψ_*(μ ⊗ m^{⊗S})`, and `ν` the law of `(Ψ(u, g), (σ(g_x)v)_x)` under `μ ⊗ m^{⊗S}`.
(i) `μ̃` is a `G`-gauge measure. `ν` is a gauge-Higgs measure frozen at `v`, and `μ̃` is its link marginal.
(ii) For every bounded Borel `G`-gauge-invariant `F` on `G^L`, `E_μ̃(F) = E_μ(F|_{K^L})`. For every bounded Borel gauge-invariant `F` on `G^L × W^S`, `E_ν(F) = E_μ(F(·, v̄))`, where `v̄` is the constant field `v`.
(iii) Let `𝔉_v` be the frozen set `{(U, φ) : φ_x ∈ σ(G)v and σ(U_ℓ)φ_y = φ_x for all x and ℓ = (x, y)}`. For every continuous `K`-gauge-invariant `f` on `K^L` there is exactly one gauge-invariant function `F` on `𝔉_v` with `F(u, v̄) = f(u)` for `u ∈ K^L`, and it is continuous.

*Proof.* (i) For `h ∈ G^S`, `h·Ψ(u, g) = Ψ(u, hg)` with `(hg)_x = h_xg_x`, and the product Haar measure is invariant under `g ↦ hg`. So `μ̃` and `ν` are invariant. `ν` is frozen: `σ(g_x)v ∈ σ(G)v`, and `σ(Ψ(u, g)_ℓ)σ(g_y)v = σ(g_x)σ(u_ℓ)v = σ(g_x)v`, because `u_ℓ ∈ K = Stab(v)`. The link marginal of `ν` is the law of `Ψ(u, g)`. (ii) `Ψ(u, g) = g·u` is the `G`-gauge action of `g` on `u ∈ K^L ⊆ G^L`, and `(Ψ(u, g), σ(g)v̄) = g·(u, v̄)`. A gauge-invariant `F` takes the same value at both points, so `E_μ̃(F) = ∫∫F(g·u)\,dμ(u)\,dm(g) = E_μ(F|_{K^L})`, and likewise for `ν`. (iii) Let `𝒢 = {(U, g) ∈ G^L × G^S : g^{-1}·U ∈ K^L}`, a closed subset of a compact space, and `π(U, g) = (U, (σ(g_x)v)_x)`. If `(U, φ) ∈ 𝔉_v` and `φ_x = σ(g_x)v`, then `σ(g_x^{-1}U_ℓg_y)v = σ(g_x)^{-1}σ(U_ℓ)φ_y = σ(g_x)^{-1}φ_x = v`, so `(U, g) ∈ 𝒢`: `π` maps `𝒢` onto `𝔉_v`. Put `F(π(U, g)) = f(g^{-1}·U)`. Two preimages of one point differ by `g ↦ gk` with `k ∈ K^S`, because `σ(g_x)v = σ(g'_x)v` exactly when `g_x^{-1}g'_x ∈ K`. Then `(gk)^{-1}·U = k^{-1}·(g^{-1}·U)`, and `K`-gauge invariance of `f` makes `F` well defined. `h·(U, φ) = π(h·U, hg)` and `(hg)^{-1}·(h·U) = g^{-1}·U`, so `F` is gauge invariant. At `(u, v̄)` take `g = 1`: `F(u, v̄) = f(u)`. Every point of `𝔉_v` is gauge equivalent to some `(u, v̄)`, so `F` is unique. `(U, g) ↦ f(g^{-1}·U)` is continuous on `𝒢`. `π` is a continuous surjection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space, so it is closed and hence a quotient map, and `F` is continuous. ∎

This is the FMS orbit construction in the form needed here. `LIT_HIGGS.md` line 67 records the FMS lemma: it "extends an equivariant function on that orbit to an equivariant polynomial restricted to the orbit". Here the extension runs along gauge orbits, needs no polynomial and no section of `G → G/K`, and comes with the equality of expectations. For a Wilson-loop function the extension is the same trace formula on `G`-valued links (section 1.2).

**Corollary C.1 (the plain reading refutes CH1-5O-E).** Take reading R-Esym. Suppose (a) holds with `μ ∈ 𝒞(G_∞)`, whose Higgs fields `η` take values in a representation `(W_1, σ_1)` of `G_∞`. ### 3.3 How large a proper subgroup can be

**Lemma B (proper subgroups that contain `H` are small).** Let `H` be the group of a Case I embedding with `b ≠ 0` (for instance `α` even). Every closed `K` with `H ⊆ K ⊊ Spin(10)` has `dim K ≤ 33`.

*Proof.* *Pieces.* Write `5 = 3_a ⊕ 2_b`, subscripts the `ψ(Y)`-charges. Then `so(10) ⊗ C = (5 ⊗ 5bar) ⊕ Λ^2(5) ⊕ Λ^2(5bar)`, with `5 ⊗ 5bar = (3 ⊗ 3bar)_0 ⊕ (3 ⊗ 2bar)_{a−b} ⊕ (2 ⊗ 3bar)_{b−a} ⊕ (2 ⊗ 2bar)_0` and `Λ^2(5) = (3bar)_{2a} ⊕ (3 ⊗ 2)_{a+b} ⊕ 1_{2b}`. In the real form `so(10) = u(5) ⊕ 𝔭`, with `𝔭` the real form of `Λ^2(5) ⊕ Λ^2(5bar)` (dimension 20), there are two real irreducible pieces of dimension 12: `A ⊂ u(5)`, the real form of `(3,2)_{a−b}` and its conjugate, and `B ⊂ 𝔭`, the real form of `(3,2)_{a+b}` and its conjugate. The other pieces have dimensions 8, 3, 1, 1, 6 and 2. The realifications of two complex irreducible representations `V, V'` are isomorphic only if `V' ≅ V` or `V' ≅ V̄`. Here `(3,2)_{a+b} ≇ (3,2)_{a−b}` because `b ≠ 0`, and `(3,2)_{a+b} ≇ (3bar,2)_{b−a}` because `3 ≇ 3bar`. So `A` and `B` each occur once. *Dichotomy.* `Lie(K)` is `Ad(H)`-invariant. An invariant subspace of a completely reducible representation is the sum of its intersections with the isotypic components. So `Lie(K)` contains `A` or meets it in `0`, and the same holds for `B`. *Not both.* Suppose `A, B ⊆ Lie(K)`. In complex coordinates `A` contains `E_{14} − E_{41}` and `i(E_{14} + E_{41})`. Their bracket is `2i(E_{11} − E_{44})`, whose component along the traceless block-scalar direction `i\,diag(2,2,2,−3,−3)` is nonzero (inner product `2 + 3 = 5`). As a vector space `su(5) = su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ A ⊕ R·i\,diag(2,2,2,−3,−3)`, and the bracket closure of `ψ(su(3) ⊕ su(2)) ⊕ A` lies in `su(5)` and is `ad(su(3) ⊕ su(2))`-invariant. It is therefore the sum of its intersections with the isotypic components, of which the trivial one is `R·i\,diag(2,2,2,−3,−3)`. It contains `su(3)`, `su(2)`, `A` and an element with a nonzero trivial component, so it is `su(5)`. So `Lie(K) ⊇ su(5)`, and `Lie(K)` is invariant under `Ad` of the corresponding connected subgroup. `𝔭 ⊗ C = 10 ⊕ 10bar` with `10 ≇ 10bar`, so `𝔭` is irreducible over `R`: a real invariant subspace has a conjugation-invariant complexification, and neither `10` nor `10bar` is conjugation invariant. So the `SU(5)`-span of `B` is `𝔭 ⊆ Lie(K)`. `[𝔭, 𝔭] ⊕ 𝔭` is a nonzero ideal of `so(10)` (Jacobi, with `[u(5), 𝔭] ⊆ 𝔭` and `[𝔭, 𝔭] ⊆ u(5)`), so it is `so(10)` (simplicity; `LW1_5.md` line 150, S′ step 5: "The algebra \(\mathfrak{so}(10)\) is simple. *Rebuilt:* let \(I\neq0\) be an ideal"), and `[𝔭, 𝔭] = u(5)`. So `Lie(K) = so(10)`, `K` contains the connected group `Spin(10)`, and `K = Spin(10)`. *Count.* A proper `K` misses `A` or `B`, so `dim K ≤ 45 − 12 = 33`. ∎

**Corollary C.3 (the frozen measure has no Haar density).** Let `K` be as in Lemma B and `μ` a `K`-gauge measure. Let a finite window of `rZ^4` have links `L_W` and endpoint sites `S_W` with `12|L_W| > 45|S_W|`; every periodic box (`|L| = 4|S|`) and every cube of side at least 16 links qualifies. The marginal of `μ̃` on `L_W` is the law of `Ψ_W(u|_{L_W}, g|_{S_W})`, so it is concentrated on the image of a smooth map from a manifold of dimension `|L_W|dim K + 45|S_W| ≤ 33|L_W| + 45|S_W| < 45|L_W|`. A `C^1` image of a manifold of lower dimension has Haar measure zero (cited). So that marginal is singular, `μ̃` has no Haar density, and `μ̃` is not in T0-start's `Spin(10)` class.

## 4. Relational channels on finite registers

`LIT_HIGGS.md` line 71 states the worry this section settles: "FMS starts with commuting classical Higgs coordinates. A fermion bilinear or a quantum-link tensor in this repo has noncommuting components. Substitution into a classical polynomial does not automatically preserve projector identities."

**Setting.** Let `𝔅` be a unital `*`-algebra with an action `κ` of `Spin(10)` by `*`-automorphisms (`𝔄` with the global gauge action, or a local algebra with the gauge action of one site). Let `R` be a real orthogonal representation on `R^d`. `M_d(𝔅)` is the `*`-algebra of `d × d` matrices over `𝔅`, with `(Φ^*)_{ab} = (Φ_{ba})^*`. Call `Φ ∈ M_d(𝔅)` *covariant* if `κ_g(Φ_{ab}) = (R(g)^TΦR(g))_{ab}` for all `g, a, b`. Convention: `κ_g(γ_{x,a}) = Σ_bR(g)_{ba}γ_{x,b}` for the vector representation `R`.

**Lemma R (relational channels).**
(i) For every complex polynomial `p`, `p(Φ)` is covariant, and `p(Φ)r(Φ) = (pr)(Φ)`.
(ii) Let `λ_1, …, λ_m` be distinct, `q(t) = Π_j(t − λ_j)` and `P_j(t) = Π_{i≠j}(t − λ_i)/(λ_j − λ_i)`. Then `Σ_jP_j(Φ) = 1`, and each of `P_j(Φ)P_k(Φ) − δ_{jk}P_j(Φ)` and `ΦP_j(Φ) − λ_jP_j(Φ)` equals `s(Φ)q(Φ)` for some polynomial `s`. So if `q(Φ) = 0`, the `P_j(Φ)` are covariant idempotents that sum to `1`, annihilate one another, and obey `ΦP_j(Φ) = λ_jP_j(Φ)`. If moreover `Φ^* = −Φ` and every `λ_j` is imaginary, then `P_j(Φ)^* = P_j(Φ)`.
(iii) If `𝔅 ⊆ B(𝒦)` and `κ_g = Ad V(g)` for a unitary representation `V` on `𝒦`, then `Φ`, as the operator `Σ_{ab}E_{ab} ⊗ Φ_{ab}` on `C^d ⊗ 𝒦`, commutes with every `R(g) ⊗ V(g)`, and so does every `p(Φ)`. If `q(Φ) = 0`, then `C^d ⊗ 𝒦 = ⊕_j ran P_j(Φ)` is a decomposition into subrepresentations of the diagonal representation `R ⊗ V`.
(iv) *The one-site matter adjoint.* Let `M_x ∈ M_{10}(𝔄)` have entries `M_x^{aa} = 0` and `M_x^{ab} = (i/2)γ_{x,a}γ_{x,b}` for `a ≠ b`. Then
\[
M_x^2=4i\,M_x-\tfrac94\,1\quad\text{exactly.}
\]
So `q(t) = (t − 9i/2)(t + i/2)`, `M_x^* = −M_x`, `P_{9i/2}(M_x) = (1/10)(γ_{x,a}γ_{x,b})_{ab}`, and `P_{−i/2}(M_x) = 1 − P_{9i/2}(M_x)`. On `C^{10} ⊗ C^{32}`, `P_{9i/2}(M_x) = ΓΓ^*/10` with `Γψ = Σ_ae_a ⊗ γ_{x,a}ψ`; it has rank 32, and its complement has rank 288. Both ranges are invariant under the diagonal action of `Spin(10)`.

*Proof.* (i) `M_d(𝔅)` is associative, so polynomials in one element multiply as polynomials, whatever the commutators of the entries. Entrywise `κ_g` is an automorphism of `M_d(𝔅)` that sends `Φ` to `R^TΦR`. So it sends `p(Φ)` to `p(R^TΦR) = R^Tp(Φ)R`, because `R` is a scalar orthogonal matrix. (ii) `Σ_jP_j(t) − 1` has degree below `m` and vanishes at the `m` points `λ_i`, so it is zero. `P_j(λ_i) = δ_{ij}`, so `P_jP_k − δ_{jk}P_j` vanishes at every `λ_i` and is divisible by `q`. `(t − λ_j)P_j(t) = q(t)/Π_{i≠j}(λ_j − λ_i)`. Apply (i). For the adjoint: `P_j(Φ)^* = \bar P_j(Φ^*) = \bar P_j(−Φ)`, with `\bar P_j` the polynomial with conjugated coefficients. With `\bar λ_i = −λ_i`, `\bar P_j(−t) = Π_{i≠j}(−t + λ_i)/(−λ_j + λ_i) = P_j(t)`. (iii) `(1 ⊗ V(g))Φ(1 ⊗ V(g)^*) = Σ_{ab}E_{ab} ⊗ (R^TΦR)_{ab} = (R^T ⊗ 1)Φ(R ⊗ 1)`; multiply by `R ⊗ 1` on the left and `R^T ⊗ 1` on the right. An idempotent that commutes with a representation has an invariant range and an invariant kernel. (iv) `(M_x^2)_{ac} = Σ_{b≠a,c}(i/2)^2γ_aγ_bγ_bγ_c`. For `a = c` there are nine terms, each `−1/4`, so the entry is `−9/4`. For `a ≠ c` there are eight terms, each `−(1/4)γ_aγ_c`, so the entry is `−2γ_aγ_c = 4i·(i/2)γ_aγ_c = 4iM_x^{ac}`. The diagonal of `4iM_x` is zero. The roots of `t^2 − 4it + 9/4` are `9i/2` and `−i/2`. `((i/2)γ_bγ_a)^* = (−i/2)γ_aγ_b`, so `M_x^* = −M_x`. `P_{9i/2}(t) = (t + i/2)/(5i)` gives diagonal entries `1/10` and off-diagonal entries `γ_aγ_b/10`. `Γ^*Γ = Σ_aγ_a^2 = 10`, so `ΓΓ^*/10` is a projection of rank `rank Γ = 32`. Invariance of the ranges is (iii). ∎

[check] (1): on `C^{10} ⊗ C^{32}` with Jordan-Wigner Majoranas, `‖M_x^2 − 4iM_x + 9/4‖ = 0`, and the eigenvalues `9i/2` and `−i/2` occur 32 and 288 times.

The first range is the image of Clifford multiplication `10 ⊗ (16 ⊕ 16bar) → 16bar ⊕ 16`; the second is its kernel, the gamma-traceless part (`144 ⊕ 144bar`, credited from memory; only the ranks are proved). So the relational channels of the one-site matter adjoint are two Clebsch-Gordan channels of the diagonal action. Their ranks split the `10` in the ratio `1 : 9`, not along a stabilizer. With a commuting (classical) `Φ` on the adjoint orbit of `ψ(Y)`, `q` has the roots `±ia, ±ib` and the `P_j(Φ)` are the FMS projectors of `LIT_HIGGS.md` section 6, of ranks 3, 3, 2, 2 at each point, onto the stabilizer types `3, 3bar, 2, 2bar` of the `10` relative to `Φ`. The identification of such a channel with an `H`-type relative to the field is a statement about a limit of large representations; it is not proved here.

Channel operators are gauge invariant. If `ψ_a` and `ψ'_b` are covariant multiplets at `x` (`κ_g(ψ_a) = Σ_cR(g)_{ca}ψ_c`), then `Σ_{ab}ψ_a^*P_j(Φ)_{ab}ψ'_b` is fixed by every `κ_g`, because `Σ_{ab}R_{ca}(R^TPR)_{ab}R_{eb} = P_{ce}`. So Proposition V applies to them: Theorem L is idle on relational channels too.

## 5. The gluon count

**The Gaussian small-field model `𝒩(K, σ, D)`.** `K ⊆ Spin(10)` is closed with Lie algebra `𝔨`; `T_1, …, T_n` is a `⟨,⟩`-orthonormal basis of `𝔨` (`n = dim K`), and `σ_A^2 > 0` are squared couplings, diagonal in that basis and invariant under `Ad(K)`. `𝒞` is a finite set of closed paths, and `D(C, C')` is a positive semidefinite real matrix on `𝒞` (the free propagator summed over the links of `C` and `C'`). The model consists of centred jointly Gaussian real variables `X^A_C` with `E[X^A_CX^B_{C'}] = δ_{AB}σ_A^2D(C, C')`, and `X_C = Σ_AX^A_CT_A ∈ 𝔨`. For a finite-dimensional unitary representation `ρ` of `Spin(10)` on `C^{d_ρ}`, the *second-order loop variable* is `w_{ρ,C} = 1 + tr(dρ(X_C)^2)/(2d_ρ)`. It is the second-order Taylor polynomial of `tr ρ(exp X_C)/d_ρ`: the first-order term `tr dρ(X_C)` vanishes, because `tr∘dρ` is a Lie map to the abelian `C` and `[so(10), so(10)] = so(10)`. Path ordering enters only beyond second order. `𝒩` is the leading term of a small-field measure. It is not T0-start's class, and nothing here says how close a member of that class is to `𝒩`.

**Lemma D (the gluon count).** In `𝒩(K, σ, D)`:
(D1) For every `ρ` there is `c_ρ ≥ 0` with `tr(dρ(X)dρ(Y)) = −c_ρ⟨X, Y⟩` on `so(10)`; `c_{10} = 2`, and `c_ρ > 0` if `ρ` is nontrivial.
(D2) `E[w_{ρ,C}] − 1 = −(c_ρ/(2d_ρ))D(C, C)S_1`, with `S_1 = Σ_Aσ_A^2`.
(D3) `Cov(w_{ρ,C}, w_{ρ',C'}) = (c_ρc_{ρ'}/(2d_ρd_{ρ'}))D(C, C')^2S_2`, with `S_2 = Σ_Aσ_A^4`.
(D4) Hence, when the denominators are nonzero, `Cov(w_{ρ,C}, w_{ρ',C'})/((E w_{ρ,C} − 1)(E w_{ρ',C'} − 1)) = 2r_{CC'}^2/N_eff`, with `r_{CC'} = D(C, C')/\sqrt{D(C, C)D(C', C')}` and `N_eff = S_1^2/S_2`. Always `N_eff ≤ dim K`, with equality exactly when all `σ_A^2` are equal.
(D5) For `K = Spin(10)` with `Ad`-invariant couplings, all `σ_A^2` are equal and `N_eff = 45`. For `K` as in Lemma B, `N_eff ≤ 33`.
(D6) *One-point blindness, two-point separation.* `𝒩(Spin(10), σ', D)` with `σ'^2 = S_1/45` has the same `E[w_{ρ,C}]` as `𝒩(K, σ, D)` for every `ρ` and every `C`. At these matched one-point data its connected correlators are those of `𝒩(K, σ, D)` times `N_eff/45 ≤ 33/45`.

*Proof.* (D1) `B(X, Y) = tr(dρ(X)dρ(Y))` is real (both factors are anti-Hermitian), symmetric, and `Ad`-invariant by cyclicity of the trace. Write `B(X, Y) = ⟨βX, Y⟩`. Then `β` is symmetric for `⟨,⟩` and commutes with every `ad_Z`. Its eigenspaces are `ad`-invariant subspaces of `so(10)`, that is ideals, and `so(10)` is simple (`LW1_5.md` line 150). So `β = −c_ρ`. `tr(dρ(X)^2) ≤ 0`, so `c_ρ ≥ 0`, with equality only if `dρ = 0`. `c_{10} = 2` is the definition of `⟨,⟩`. (D2) `E[tr(dρ(X_C)^2)] = Σ_{AB}tr(dρ(T_A)dρ(T_B))E[X^A_CX^B_C] = −c_ρD(C, C)S_1`. (D3) For centred jointly Gaussian `a, b, c, e`, Isserlis' formula `E[abce] = E[ab]E[ce] + E[ac]E[be] + E[ae]E[bc]` follows by comparing the `t_1t_2t_3t_4` coefficients of `E[(Σt_ia_i)^4] = 3E[(Σt_ia_i)^2]^2`, the fourth moment of one centred normal variable. So `Cov(ab, ce) = E[ac]E[be] + E[ae]E[bc]`. Expand both traces in the basis `T_A` and use (D1), `tr(dρ(T_A)dρ(T_B)) = −c_ρδ_{AB}`: `Cov(tr dρ(X_C)^2, tr dρ'(X_{C'})^2) = c_ρc_{ρ'}Σ_{A,A'}Cov(X^A_CX^A_C, X^{A'}_{C'}X^{A'}_{C'}) = c_ρc_{ρ'}Σ_{A,A'}2(δ_{AA'}σ_A^2D(C, C'))^2 = 2c_ρc_{ρ'}D(C, C')^2S_2`. Divide by `4d_ρd_{ρ'}`. (D4) Divide (D3) by the product of the two instances of (D2). Cauchy-Schwarz gives `S_1^2 ≤ nS_2`, with equality exactly for constant `σ_A^2`. (D5) An `Ad`-invariant symmetric form on `so(10)` is a multiple of `⟨,⟩`, by the argument of (D1). Lemma B gives `dim K ≤ 33`. (D6) (D2) depends on `σ` only through `S_1`, and `45σ'^2 = S_1`. The new `S_2` is `45σ'^4 = S_1^2/45 = (N_eff/45)S_2`. ∎

[check] (2): on the 12-dimensional Standard Model algebra inside `so(10)`, the Gram matrices of `tr_{10}` and `tr_{45}` are `−2·1` and `−16·1` to `3·10^{−16}` and `3·10^{−15}` (D1: `c_{10} = 2`, `c_{45} = 16`). With 8, 3 and 1 generators at `σ^2 = 0.01, 0.02, 0.005`, `N_eff = 10.38 ≤ 12`, and a Monte Carlo of `4·10^5` samples gives `Var(w)/(E w − 1)^2 = 0.1930` against `2/N_eff = 0.1926` (D4 with `C = C'`).

So in the Gaussian model, unprojected loops of every representation fix `N_eff` through two one-point values and one connected two-loop correlator. The unbroken class has `N_eff = 45`; every proper `K ⊇ H` has `N_eff ≤ 33`. The one-point data alone never separate them. The separation is of the size of `S_2`, that is order `g^4`.

### 6.2 The open lemma

**Lemma CH1-5O-E′ (open; CH1-5O-E in reading R-E′).** There exist an `L5-O` update, a stationary, gauge-invariant, cell-symmetric state `ω`, a Case I embedding with `α` even and group `H`, a closed `G_∞` with `H ⊆ G_∞ ⊊ Spin(10)`, and pinned data in the form of Definition SC — a block scale `r`, a fixed finite list `Λ_r` of gauge-invariant block-loop observables that contains the vector-representation block plaquettes, the standard identification (each loop observable mapped to its trace formula on `G`-valued links of `rZ^4`, for `G = G_∞` and for `G = Spin(10)`), the classes of section 6.1 with coupling bound `g_*`, and `δ` — such that:

(b′) no `μ'` in T0-start's `Spin(10)` class meets the bounds of (a′).

Lemma B bounds the candidates: `dim G_∞ ≤ 33`. Lemma D says, in the Gaussian model, where (b′) can be decided on unprojected loops: in the two-point products, at order `g^4`. So (b′) is certifiable on such lists only if `δ` is pinned below the `N_eff` separation, roughly `(1 − dim G_∞/45)` times the connected two-loop correlator; GA2's own budget is "`O((a/r)^p) + O(g_i(r)^4)`" (`TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` line 387), of the same order. ### 6.3 Four attacks, each failing at a named step

They differ from A1-A4 of `CH1_5O_DRAFT.md` section 5.3 (promote Theorem L to the effective group; the centre; finite registers and Proposition Q; the dark witness and its `SU(5)` lift) and from the three attacks of `CH1_5_DRAFT.md` section 5.2 cited there.

**Attack B1 (kill: carry Theorem L into the comparison data through relational channels).** Claim: CH1 needs the light fermions of the matching theory, and a gauge-invariant comparison can see fermions only through relational channels `Σψ_a^*P_j(Φ)_{ab}ψ'_b` made with a Higgs-like composite `Φ`. On `L5-O` the entries of `Φ` do not commute (Proposition Q), so the `P_j(Φ)` are not projectors, and each relational channel is a mixture of the fixed-frame `H`-types of `ψ`. By Theorem L with Lemma X, a channel that holds a light `(3,2)` then holds a light colour exotic at the same scale, and no `G_∞` theory with light content `F_t` matches. *Fails at the step "a relational channel inherits the fixed-frame `H`-types of its fields".* Two exact facts block it. Channel operators are gauge invariant (section 4), so their vectors in `H_ω` are fixed by `U` and carry only the type `(1,1)_0` (Proposition V(iv)); Theorem L says nothing about them, and no property of their expectations is excluded by it (Proposition V(iii)). And noncommuting entries do not spoil the projector identities: they hold exactly once `q(Φ) = 0` (Lemma R(ii)). The channels are then exact subrepresentations of the diagonal action (Lemma R(iii)), not mixtures of fixed-frame types. What a finite composite lacks is a different thing: its channels follow its own spectrum, not the stabilizer split of the `10` (Lemma R(iv)); that is Attack B4. In R-E′ the comparison data are pure-gauge loops, so B1 has nothing to act on there; it would bear on a later comparison with matter.

**Attack B2 (kill: complementarity makes (b) impossible).** Claim: every `G_∞` theory is a `Spin(10)` gauge-Higgs theory with the same gauge-invariant data (Theorem C). So whenever (a) holds, a `Spin(10)` measure meets the same bounds, (b) fails, and CH1-5O-E is false for every update and every state. *Succeeds in reading R-Esym* (Corollary C.1). *Fails on CH1-5O-E′ at the step "T0-start's `Spin(10)` class contains the frozen measure `μ̃`".* It does not: `μ̃` is singular with respect to Haar measure on every large window, because `dim G_∞ ≤ 33` (Lemma B, Corollary C.3), and the class asks a Haar density. Whether some member of the class comes within `δ` of `μ̃`'s data is (b′) itself. In the Gaussian model the answer is no for the two-point data, by the factor `N_eff/45` (Lemma D(D6)).

**Attack B3 (kill: Gaussian blindness).** Claim: at small coupling, Wilson loops in every `Spin(10)` representation take the same values in a `G_∞` theory and in a `Spin(10)` theory at the rescaled coupling `σ'^2 = S_1/45` (Lemma D(D6)). The list `Λ_r` must hold the vector-representation plaquettes, and any loop of `L5-O` is a loop in a `Spin(10)` representation. So (b′) never holds within the budget, and CH1-5O-E′ is false. *Fails at the step "Definition SC sees only one-point functions".* Definition SC compares "every product `O` of at most two observables of `Λ_r`". The connected two-loop correlators fix `N_eff` (Lemma D(D4)), which is 45 for the unbroken class and at most 33 for every candidate `G_∞`. What survives of B3 is the pinning constraint of section 6.2 on `δ`.

**Attack B4 (build: an FMS composite at one site).** Claim: `L5-O` carries even, covariant adjoint composites at every site: the matter bilinears `M_x^{ab}`, the six link-end generators `L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}`, and their sums. They are in a representation on which the centre `Z` acts trivially, as Proposition N(ii) requires of bosonic data. In product states of coherent states, `ω(Φ_x)` can be placed on an orbit whose stabilizer has Lie algebra `u(3) ⊕ u(2) ⊇ ψ(𝔤_SM)` (for example `Φ_x = M_x + Σ_ℓL_{ℓ,x}` with plane values proportional to `(1, 1, 1, 3, 3)/2`), and Attack A2's representation theory allows `G_∞ = H·Z`. FMS projectors `P_j(Φ_x)` then split the `10` into `3, 3bar, 2, 2bar` relative to `Φ_x`, gauge-invariant gates built from them give the coset gluons a mass, and the state matches a `G_∞` theory. *Fails at the step "a composite of finite size defines `H`-type channels".* For a classical field on that orbit the four channels have ranks 3, 3, 2, 2 at each point. For a covariant `Φ_x` with entries in a finite-dimensional algebra, the channels are the spectral subspaces of `Φ_x` on `10 ⊗ V`; they are invariant under the diagonal action of all of `Spin(10)` (Lemma R(iii)), and their number and ranks are set by the spectrum of `Φ_x`, not by a stabilizer. For the matter adjoint the computation is explicit: `M_x^2 = 4iM_x − 9/4`, and the two channels have ranks 32 and 288 on `10 ⊗ C^{32}`, a split of the `10` in the ratio `1 : 9`; they are the pieces `16bar ⊕ 144` and their conjugates (Lemma R(iv)). The gauge average of the product state, which is the state the law sees, has `ω(Φ_x) = 0` (Theorem E(E2)). The orbit of `ω(Φ_x)` in the product state is a property of a lift (`CH1_5O_DRAFT.md` Proposition P, Attack A4); gauge-invariant data keep only invariant functions of `Φ_x`, whose relational content at finite size is the spectral content above. `H`-type channels need a composite whose relational projectors approach the classical ones, that is a block composite in a large representation with `q(Φ̄)` small in the state. That is a dynamical property of a stationary state. It is part of what CH1-5O-E′ asks, not a consequence of the kinematics.

### 6.4 What would settle it

*True:* an `L5-O` update and a stationary state that meet (a′) and (b′). The route on file is FMS at block scale: a block composite `Φ̄` with `ω(q(Φ̄)^*q(Φ̄))` small for the minimal polynomial of a proper orbit, plus a regime in which the coset gluons are massive and the `G_∞` gluons weakly coupled. It needs, in order, a plaquette class (GA1-P-5), a weakly coupled regime (Y2-W[`L5-O`]), and T0-start for the factors of `G_∞` and for `Spin(10)`. *False:* a theorem that every stationary gauge-invariant state of every update has, at every scale, either `N_eff = 45` in its two-loop data or no small-field match at all. Proposition V and Theorem C show that exactness of the gauge group cannot supply such a theorem: the frozen model is exactly gauge invariant, obeys the analogues of Theorem E, matches a proper subgroup, and in its Gaussian small-field form has `N_eff ≤ 33`. *Cheapest guide (not a proof):* the two-loop ratio of Lemma D(D4) in a small quantum-link model with an `SO(N)` gauge group, `N ≥ 4`, an adjoint matter bilinear and a magnetic term.
