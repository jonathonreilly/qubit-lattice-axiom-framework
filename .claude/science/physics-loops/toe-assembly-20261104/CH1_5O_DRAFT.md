## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**The no-go is proved, and it holds for every state.** Let `ω` be any gauge-invariant state of an `L5-O` update (irreducible registers, plus any finite number of layers of gauge-invariant gates). Invariance under every local `Spin(10)_x` gives invariance under the global gauge action `κ`, which commutes with the tick and with the cell symmetries. So `Spin(10)` acts on the GNS space of `ω` by a unitary representation that fixes the cyclic vector, commutes with every function of the tick, and sends `−1` to the identity (Lemma G). Exact consequences (Theorem E): `ω` is even; the one-point data of every covariant multiplet form a `Spin(10)`-invariant vector, so no covariant order parameter exists; an operator carried at one site has zero correlation with every operator that the site's gauge group fixes, so correlations between operators carried at different sites vanish at every tick; a fermion-odd local operator has zero correlation with every local operator of disjoint gauge support. **Main result (Theorem L).** For every bounded operator `F` on the GNS space that commutes with the global gauge action (every spectral projection of the tick is one), the `H`-types of fields with nonzero `F`-weight are the `H`-types of a tensor-class `Spin(10)` representation. **Lemma X** (new): every tensor-class `Spin(10)` multiplet that contains a `(3,2)` component contains a colour-exotic component, an `su(3)` representation other than `1`, `3`, `3bar`. So the light content of a gauge-invariant state is never one family, nor any number of families, under any embedding. The answer to the lane's question is **no** in the sense in which `Spin(10)` acts on a state's data, namely through the gauge action on the state's GNS space. **CH1-5O-i as worded is refuted** (Corollary L.3): its clause (b) reads the `H`-content of fields of a gauge-invariant `ω`, and Theorem L excludes it for every update and every state. ## 1. Setting

### 1.1 Updates, gauge maps and states

**Updates.** An *`L5-O` update* is the tick of `L5-O[R]` (`LW1_5_DRAFT.md` section 8.1) with `R` an irreducible register of `𝔨 = so(20)` (for example `S_+`), followed or preceded by finitely many layers of gauge-invariant gates. A gauge-invariant gate is a local unitary that commutes with every local gauge unitary below. `CH1_5_DRAFT.md` line 39 makes the same assumption: "Statements for `L5-O` assume irreducible `so(20)` registers (as `L5-O[S_+]`), which is Lemma Z's hypothesis, unless marked." `𝔄` is the quasi-local algebra of matter and links. `α` is the tick automorphism (`LW1.md` E1: a finite product of commuting finite gates per layer).

**Local gauge maps.** `LW1_5_DRAFT.md` Theorem G(iii): "the `e^{iθ𝒢_x^{cd}}` give a representation of `Spin(10)` on the space of site `x` and its six link ends." Write `V_x(g) ∈ 𝔄` for the unitary of `g ∈ Spin(10)` at `x`, and `α^G_{x,g} = Ad V_x(g)`. For each `x`, `g ↦ α^G_{x,g}` is an action of `Spin(10)` by automorphisms. `VC1_5_DRAFT.md` line 64: "Unitaries at different sites commute: they share at most one link, where they act on different ends." For a local `A`, its *gauge support* `S(A)` is the finite set of sites at which `A` has matter or a link end (both ends of every link that `A` acts on). Gauge maps at sites outside `S(A)` fix `A`. Each `α^G_{x,g}` maps an operator supported on a set of sites and links to an operator supported on the same sets, because each factor of `V_x(g)` acts inside its own tensor factor.

**Gauge average.** `VC1_5_DRAFT.md` Lemma 1.1 builds the average `E: 𝔄 → 𝔄^G` over the local gauge groups. Its part (c), line 73: "A state `ω` is gauge invariant exactly when `ω = ω∘E`. Restriction is a bijection from gauge-invariant states of `𝔄` onto states of `𝔄^G`, with inverse `φ ↦ φ∘E`." Its proof also gives `E∘α^G_{x,h} = E` (line 76: "The converse holds since `E∘α^G_{x,h} = E`"). Remark 3.1, line 203: "if the gauge sites `S(A)` and `S(B)` are disjoint, then `E(AB) = E(A)E(B)`". (The integrand factorizes over the two disjoint sets of sites, and Haar measure on a finite product is the product measure.)

**States.** A state `ω` of `𝔄` is *gauge invariant* if `ω∘α^G_{x,g} = ω` for every `x` and `g` (LW1's conjugation reading). It is *stationary* if `ω∘α = ω`. It is *cell symmetric* if `ω∘β_s = ω` for the lifts `β_s` of the cell symmetries `s ∈ 𝒢` (`VC1_5_DRAFT.md` Lemma C).

**Parity and the centre.** `Θ` is the fermion-parity automorphism of `𝔄`; `𝔄^{even}` and `𝔄^{odd}` are its eigenspaces. Let `ϖ ∈ Spin(10)` be the exponential of the rotation by `π` in each of the five coordinate planes. Its unitary at `x` is the element `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` section 1.2 calls `ω_x = exp(iπΣ_j𝒢_x^{2j−1,2j})`. Lemma K0(ii) there: "`ω_x^2 = z_x`", and `z_x = exp(2πi𝒢_x^{12})` is the unitary of `−1 ∈ Spin(10)`. So `ϖ^2 = −1`. `ϖ` maps to `−1_{SO(10)}`, which is central in `SO(10)`. `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` Lemma B10's proof shows that such a lift is central: "`g ↦ gωg^{-1}ω^{-1}` is continuous, takes values in `{±1}`, and equals `1` at `g = 1`." Write `Z = {1, ϖ, −1, −ϖ}`. It is the centre of `Spin(10)` (cited: the centre is cyclic of order 4).

### 1.2 Embeddings and `H`-types

`H` is the group of a Case I embedding `ψ: 𝔤_SM → so(10)` (`CG1_KILL_DRAFT.md` Lemma C, line 57: "`su(3)` acts on `C^3` as the `3`. `su(2)` acts on `R^4 = C^2` as the `2` ... `ψ(Y) = (ia·1_3) ⊕ (ib·1_2)`"), with `(a, b) = (α, β)` coprime. The orientation `3bar` on `C^3` is allowed; Lemma C identifies it with `3` up to an automorphism. Case II embeddings carry no `(3,2)` in any tensor-class representation (Theorem O-II), so every statement below about `(3,2)` is empty for them. `F_t` is the family of CG1K section 1.

An *`H`-type* is an irreducible representation `τ = (σ_3, σ_2)_y` of `𝔤_SM`. A quasi-local operator `A` *has `H`-type `τ`* if it lies in a finite-dimensional space of quasi-local operators that the global gauge action of `H` (section 2) maps to itself, on which that action is a sum of copies of `τ`. An `H`-type is *colour exotic* if `σ_3` is none of `1`, `3`, `3bar`. A colour-exotic type is a member of no `F_t` and of no `F_t^*`, and it is charged under `SU(3)`.

For an integer weight `n ∈ Z^5`, its *colour spread* is `max(n_1, n_2, n_3) − min(n_1, n_2, n_3)`. `CG1_KILL_DRAFT.md` Lemma W(W4), line 84: in Case I, "a weight `n` restricts to: the `su(3)`-weight of `(n_1, n_2, n_3)` modulo `R(1,1,1)`". The spread does not change when `(c, c, c)` is added, so it is a function of the `su(3)`-weight. The weights of `3` are the classes of `e_i`, those of `3bar` the classes of `−e_i`, and that of `1` is `0`. Each has spread `1` or `0`.

### 1.3 Three readings of "`Spin(10)` acts through `H` at long distance"

Each is a reading of words already in CH1, CG1K-H and EW1. None is adopted. Section 5.1 gives the four-test check.

- **R-A (action reading).** `Spin(10)` acts on the data of a gauge-invariant state through the global gauge action `κ` (Lemma G). "Acts through `H` on the long-distance content" means: a part of the content selected by the dynamics (light fields, light vectors of the GNS space) is a representation of `H` in which some `H`-types occur without the other `H`-types of the `Spin(10)` multiplets that contain them. This is how CH1-5O-i's clause (b) reads "`H`-content" for fields of a gauge-invariant `ω`.
- **R-L (lift reading).** A *lift* of `ω` is a state `ω̃` of `𝔄` with `ω̃∘E = ω`. "`Spin(10)` is broken to `G_∞`" means: some stationary lift is invariant under the local gauge maps of `G_∞` at every site and under no larger group. This is the lattice form of fixing a gauge (for example a unitary gauge).
- **R-E (effective reading).** "`Spin(10)` is broken to `G_∞` at long distance" means: at some scale, the gauge-invariant correlations of `ω` agree with those of a continuum gauge theory whose gauge group is `G_∞`, in the comparison form of GA2 (`Y2W_DRAFT.md` Definition SC, with its data pinned).

CG1K's readings R-B and R-M (`CG1_KILL_DRAFT.md` lines 46-47) read CG1's family content, not breaking. Corollary L.3 says what Theorem L does to them.

## 2. What exactness leaves visible

### 2.1 The global gauge action

**Lemma G (global gauge action).**
(i) For `g ∈ Spin(10)` and local `A`, put `κ_g(A) = (∏_{x∈S}α^G_{x,g})(A)` for a finite set `S ⊇ S(A)`. This does not depend on `S`. Each `κ_g` extends to an automorphism of `𝔄`, and `g ↦ κ_g` is an action of `Spin(10)`. For each `A ∈ 𝔄`, `g ↦ κ_g(A)` is norm continuous.
(ii) `κ_g` commutes with the tick `α`, with conjugation by every gauge-invariant gate, and with every lift `β_s`.
(iii) Every gauge-invariant state `ω` satisfies `ω∘κ_g = ω`.
(iv) `κ_{−1}` is the identity of `𝔄`, and `κ_ϖ = Θ`.
(v) Let `ω` be gauge invariant, with GNS triple `(H_ω, π, Ω)`. There is one strongly continuous unitary representation `U` of `Spin(10)` on `H_ω` with `U(g)π(A)Ω = π(κ_g(A))Ω`. It fixes `Ω`, `U(−1) = 1`, and `U(ϖ)π(A)Ω = π(Θ(A))Ω`. If `ω` is stationary, there is one unitary `U_α` with `U_απ(A)Ω = π(α(A))Ω`; it commutes with every `U(g)`, and so does every spectral projection of `U_α`. The same holds for each `β_s` when `ω` is cell symmetric.

*Proof.* (i) The maps at different sites commute (`VC1_5_DRAFT.md` line 64). A gauge map keeps the support of an operator (section 1.1). So `∏_{x∈S(A)}α^G_{x,g}(A)` has gauge support inside `S(A)`, and the maps at sites outside `S(A)` fix it. Hence the product over any `S ⊇ S(A)` gives the same element. Since each `α^G_{x,·}` is an action and the sites commute, `κ_gκ_h = κ_{gh}` on local elements. On the local algebra of a finite set of sites and the links with both ends in it, `κ_g` is the restriction of a finite product of inner automorphisms, so it is a `*`-automorphism of that algebra. It is isometric, so it extends to `𝔄`. On a local `A`, `g ↦ κ_g(A)` is a finite product of continuous maps into a finite-dimensional algebra. A general `A` is a norm limit of local `A_k`, and `‖κ_g(A) − κ_g(A_k)‖ = ‖A − A_k‖` for every `g`, so the limit is uniform in `g` and continuity passes to it.
(ii) `LW1_5_DRAFT.md` Theorem G(i): "every `h_ℓ` commutes with every `𝒢_z^{cd}`; hence so does every cube gate `e^{iaH_C}`, at every angle". Theorem G(ii): "The electric gates of section 8 are built from the commutant of the end generators, so they are unchanged too." Extra gates commute with every `V_x(g)` by definition. So `α∘α^G_{x,g} = α^G_{x,g}∘α` for every `x` and `g`. For local `A`, take `S ⊇ S(A) ∪ S(α(A))`; then `κ_g(α(A)) = ∏_{x∈S}α^G_{x,g}(α(A)) = α(∏_{x∈S}α^G_{x,g}(A)) = α(κ_g(A))`. For lifts, `VC1_5_DRAFT.md` Lemma C(iii): "`β_g` maps every Gauss generator at `x` to the same generator at `gx`". So `β_s∘α^G_{x,g} = α^G_{sx,g}∘β_s`, and the same computation gives `β_s∘κ_g = κ_g∘β_s`.
(iii) On local `A`, `ω(κ_g(A))` is `ω` of finitely many local gauge maps applied to `A`, which is `ω(A)`. Continuity gives the rest.
(iv) `LW1_5_DRAFT.md` Lemma Z, line 238: "(i) for every site `x`, conjugation by the local `−1 ∈ Spin(10)_x` is the identity on the whole quasi-local algebra". So `κ_{−1} = id`. Line 239: "(ii) the element `−1_{10} ∈ SO(10)` applied at every site of a finite region `Λ` (with its link changes) acts as the fermion parity automorphism on every operator supported, with all its links, inside `Λ`". `ϖ` lifts `−1_{10}`, and a local `A` is supported, with all its links, inside `S(A)`. So `κ_ϖ(A) = Θ(A)`. Lemma Z's hypothesis (one scalar `ε` on every register) holds for irreducible registers.
(v) Put `U(g)π(A)Ω = π(κ_g(A))Ω`. This is well defined and isometric, because `‖π(κ_g(A))Ω‖^2 = ω(κ_g(A^*A)) = ω(A^*A)` by (iii). It maps the dense subspace `π(𝔄)Ω` onto itself (the inverse is `U(g^{-1})`), so it extends to a unitary. `U(g)U(h) = U(gh)` holds on the dense subspace. `‖U(g)π(A)Ω − U(h)π(A)Ω‖ ≤ ‖κ_g(A) − κ_h(A)‖`, so `U` is strongly continuous on a dense set of uniformly bounded operators, hence everywhere. `U(−1) = 1` and the formula for `U(ϖ)` follow from (iv). `U_α` is built the same way from `ω∘α = ω`. On the dense subspace, `U_αU(g)π(A)Ω = π(α(κ_g(A)))Ω = π(κ_g(α(A)))Ω = U(g)U_απ(A)Ω`. The spectral projections of `U_α` lie in the von Neumann algebra generated by `U_α` (spectral theorem, cited), so they commute with every operator that commutes with `U_α` and `U_α^*`. The lifts are the same. ∎

### 2.2 Exact consequences

**Theorem E (what a gauge-invariant state can show).** Let `ω` be a gauge-invariant state of an `L5-O` update.
(E1) `ω` is even (`ω∘Θ = ω`), and `ω = ω∘E`.
(E2) *No covariant order parameter.* Let `A_1, …, A_d ∈ 𝔄` satisfy `κ_g(A_i) = Σ_jρ(g)_{ji}A_j` for a continuous representation `ρ` of `Spin(10)` on `C^d`. Then `v = (ω(A_1), …, ω(A_d))` satisfies `v = ρ(g)^Tv` for every `g`. So every element of `Spin(10)` fixes the one-point data. If `ρ` has no nonzero invariant vector, `v = 0`.
(E3) *Operators carried at a site.* Let `A ∈ 𝔄` have `∫α^G_{x,g}(A)dg = 0` (Haar measure on `Spin(10)`). This holds, for example, when `A` is carried at `x` (`CG1_KILL_DRAFT.md` line 43) by a representation with no invariant vector. Then `ω(AC) = ω(CA) = 0` for every `C ∈ 𝔄` that every `α^G_{x,g}` fixes. In particular, if `A` is carried at `x` by a representation with no invariant vector and `B` is carried at `y ≠ x`, then `ω(A^*α^n(B)) = ω(α^n(B)A^*) = 0` for every `n ∈ Z`.
(E4) *Fermions.* Every fermion-odd `A ∈ 𝔄` has `E(A) = 0`. If `A` and `C` are local, `A` is fermion odd and `S(A) ∩ S(C) = ∅`, then `ω(AC) = ω(CA) = 0`.

## 3. The light content is a `Spin(10)` representation

### 3.1 Colour exotics

**Lemma X (every tensor-class multiplet with a `(3,2)` has a colour exotic).** Let `W` be a finite-dimensional representation of `Spin(10)` on which `−1` acts as the identity. Let `H` be the group of a Case I embedding, with either orientation of `C^3`. If `W|_H` has a component of type `(3,2)_y` for some `y`, then `W|_H` has a colour-exotic component.

*Proof.* *Weights.* `CG1_KILL_DRAFT.md` Lemma W, line 81: "(W1) Every representation of `Spin(10)` on which `−1` acts trivially has weights `n ∈ Z^5`." Line 83: "(W3) Weight multiplicities are invariant under the signed permutations of the five coordinates with an even number of sign changes." Every permutation of the coordinates is such an element (no sign change).
*A weight over `(3,2)`.* Let `T` be the torus of the five coordinate planes and `T_H ⊂ T` the torus of the Cartan subalgebra of `ψ(𝔤_SM)` (W4's proof: these Cartan elements "are block-diagonal rotations in these planes"). A `T_H`-weight vector of the `(3,2)_y` component is a sum of `T`-weight vectors of `W`. So `W` has a `T`-weight `n` whose restriction is a weight of `(3,2)_y`. By (W4), `(n_1, n_2, n_3)` is the class of `e_i` (or of `−e_i` for the other orientation), so as a multiset it is `{k+1, k, k}` (or `{k, k, k−1}`), and `n_4 − n_5 = ±1`. So `n` has a nonzero coordinate among the first three and another among the last two.
*A weight of spread at least 2.* Case (a): some coordinate of `n` is zero. Choose two nonzero coordinates and one zero coordinate. A permutation puts them in slots 1, 2 and 3. Flipping the signs of slots 1 and 3 changes only slot 1, since slot 3 is zero; the same holds for slots 2 and 3. So a weight `n'` of `W` has colour part `(|u|, −|v|, 0)` with `u, v ≠ 0`. Its colour spread is `|u| + |v| ≥ 2`. Case (b): no coordinate is zero. Put any three coordinates in slots 1-3, say `(u, v, w)`. If `u < 0`, flip slots 1 and 3. Then if slot 2 is positive, flip slots 2 and 3. The result has slot 1 positive and slot 2 negative, so its colour spread is at least `|u| + |v| ≥ 2`.
*Conclusion.* By (W4), the `su(3)`-weight of `n'` is the class of `(n'_1, n'_2, n'_3)`, whose spread is at least 2. The weights of `1`, `3` and `3bar` have spread at most 1 (section 1.2). The `n'`-weight space of `W` is nonzero, and as a `T_H`-weight space it is the sum of the corresponding weight spaces of the components of `W|_H`. So some component has an `su(3)`-weight of spread at least 2. Its `su(3)`-part is none of `1`, `3`, `3bar`. ∎

(2): the 16 weights of the `16` have colour spread at most 1, and only the colour types `1`, `3`, `3bar`. (3): `Λ^1 = 10` has no colour-exotic weight and no `(3,2)` weight; `Λ^2`, `Λ^3`, `Λ^4` and `Λ^5` each have both (6, 30, 66 and 84 exotic weights).

### 3.2 The main theorem

**Theorem L (the content seen through any gauge-commuting operator is a `Spin(10)` representation).** Let `ω` be a gauge-invariant state of an `L5-O` update, and `U` the representation of Lemma G(v). Let `F` be a bounded operator on `H_ω` that commutes with every `U(g)`. Call `‖Fπ(A)Ω‖` the *`F`-weight* of `A ∈ 𝔄`. Fix a Case I embedding with group `H`.
(i) If a quasi-local field of `H`-type `τ` has nonzero `F`-weight, so does a local field of `H`-type `τ`; it can be taken fermion odd if the first one is.
(ii) If a local field `A` of `H`-type `(3,2)_y` has nonzero `F`-weight, then a local field `A_X` of a colour-exotic `H`-type has nonzero `F`-weight. `A_X` lies in the span of the `κ_g(A)`, so its gauge support lies in `S(A)`, and it is fermion odd if `A` is.
(iii) So the set of `H`-types of quasi-local fields with nonzero `F`-weight contains a colour-exotic type whenever it contains a `(3,2)` type. The same holds for fermion-odd fields alone.
(iv) Precisely: that set is the set of `H`-types that occur in the restriction to `H` of a tensor-class representation of `Spin(10)`, namely the direct sum of the spaces `T(V)` of the proof over all local fields `A`.
If `ω` is stationary, every spectral projection `E_α(Δ)` of the tick's unitary `U_α` is such an `F` (Lemma G(v)). If `ω` is also cell symmetric, so are products of these with spectral projections of the translation unitaries.

*Proof.* (ii) Let `V` be the span of `{κ_g(A) : g ∈ Spin(10)}`. It lies in the finite-dimensional algebra of the sites and links of `A`, because each `κ_g` maps that algebra to itself (section 1.1). Define `T: V → H_ω` by `T(B) = Fπ(B)Ω`. Then `T(κ_g(B)) = FU(g)π(B)Ω = U(g)T(B)`. So `M = T(V)` is a finite-dimensional subspace invariant under the unitary representation `U`, with `U(−1) = 1` on it (Lemma G(v)). The orthogonal complement of a `U`-invariant subspace of `M` is `U`-invariant, because `U` is unitary; so `M` is an orthogonal sum of irreducible subspaces `M_1, …, M_s`. `T` intertwines the action of `H` by `κ` on `V` with its action by `U` on `M`. An intertwiner maps each `H`-isotypic component of `V` into the component of the same type of `M`, and since `T` is onto `M`, it maps it onto that component. `A` lies in the `(3,2)_y`-component of `V` and `T(A) ≠ 0`, so `M` has a nonzero `(3,2)_y`-component. That component is the sum of the `(3,2)_y`-components of the `M_i`, so some `M_i|_H` contains `(3,2)_y`. `−1` acts on `M_i` as the identity. By Lemma X, `M_i|_H` has a component of a colour-exotic type `X`. So the `X`-component of `M` is nonzero, and it is the image of the `X`-component of `V`. Choose `A_X` in the `X`-component of `V` with `T(A_X) ≠ 0`. It is a local field of `H`-type `X` in the span of the `κ_g(A)`. `Θ = κ_ϖ` commutes with every `κ_g` because `ϖ` is central, so `V` consists of odd operators when `A` is odd.
(i) Let `ψ` have `H`-type `τ` and `Fπ(ψ)Ω ≠ 0`. Let `P_τ = d_τ∫_H\overline{χ_τ(h)}\,κ_h\,dh`, with `χ_τ` the character and `d_τ` the dimension of `τ`. On every finite-dimensional space invariant under the `κ_h`, `P_τ` is the projection onto the `τ`-component (character formula, cited). It is bounded on `𝔄` and maps each local field to a local field with the same gauge support. `P_τψ = ψ`. Choose local `A_k → ψ`, odd if `ψ` is odd (take odd parts, as in Corollary IR). Then `P_τA_k` is a local field of type `τ`, odd if `A_k` is, and `Fπ(P_τA_k)Ω → Fπ(ψ)Ω ≠ 0`. So it is nonzero for large `k`.
(iii) follows from (i) and (ii).
(iv) If a quasi-local field of type `τ` has nonzero `F`-weight, (i) gives a local field `A` of type `τ` with `T(A) ≠ 0`, and `T(A)` lies in the `τ`-component of `T(V)` for that `A`. Conversely, every `H`-type that occurs in some `T(V)` is the image of the component of the same type of `V`, so a local field of that type has nonzero `F`-weight. Each `T(V)` is a representation of `Spin(10)` with `U(−1) = 1`, and so is their direct sum. ∎

So `Spin(10)` never acts through a proper subgroup on any part of a gauge-invariant state's content that the dynamics can select. Every operator built from the tick, the cell symmetries and other gauge-invariant data commutes with `U`, and the content it selects is a `Spin(10)` representation. Stationarity is needed only to have `U_α`; cell symmetry only for translation projections. The proof uses gauge invariance only through the global action, which local gauge invariance forces (Lemma G(iii)).

### 3.3 The correlation form

**Corollary L.2 (correlations).** Let `ω` be gauge invariant and `V` a finite-dimensional space of quasi-local operators that every `κ_g` maps to itself. Let `γ, γ'` be automorphisms of `𝔄` that commute with every `κ_g` (by Lemma G(ii), any power of the tick composed with any lift of a cell symmetry). Put `b(A, B) = ω(γ(A)^*γ'(B))`.
(i) `b` is invariant: `b(κ_g(A), κ_g(B)) = b(A, B)`. With a `κ`-invariant inner product on `V` and the `λ`-isotypic part of `V` written as `λ ⊗ M_λ` (`λ` irreducible), `b(e ⊗ m, e' ⊗ m') = ⟨e, e'⟩\,b_λ(m, m')` for a sesquilinear form `b_λ` on `M_λ`, and `b` vanishes between different `λ`.
(ii) Let `(γ_j, γ'_j)` be a family of such pairs (for example translates at growing distance). If a field `A ∈ V` of `H`-type `(3,2)_y` has two-point functions `b_j(A, A)` that do not decay exponentially in `j`, then a field of `V` of a colour-exotic `H`-type has two-point functions that do not decay exponentially.

*Proof.* (i) `b(κ_g(A), κ_g(B)) = ω(κ_g(γ(A)^*γ'(B))) = b(A, B)`, by Lemma G(iii) and the commutation of `γ, γ'` with `κ_g`. Write `b(A, B) = ⟨A, KB⟩` for one linear `K` on `V` (finite dimension). Invariance of `b` and of the inner product gives `Kκ_g = κ_gK`. By Schur's lemma for complex irreducible representations, `K` maps each isotypic part into itself and acts on `λ ⊗ M_λ` as `1 ⊗ K_λ`. Put `b_λ(m, m') = ⟨m, K_λm'⟩`.
(ii) `A` lies in the `(3,2)_y`-component of `V`, which is the sum over `λ` of `λ_{(3,2)} ⊗ M_λ`, with `λ_{(3,2)}` the `(3,2)_y`-component of `λ|_H`. Write the `λ`-part of `A` as `Σ_ke_{λ,k} ⊗ m_{λ,k}` with `e_{λ,k}` orthonormal in `λ_{(3,2)}`. By (i), `b_j(A, A) = Σ_λΣ_kb^j_λ(m_{λ,k}, m_{λ,k})`, a finite sum. If every term decayed exponentially in `j`, so would the sum. So one term does not, and its `m_{λ,k}` is nonzero, so `λ|_H` contains `(3,2)_y`. `−1` acts on `V` as the identity (Lemma G(iv)). By Lemma X, `λ|_H` has a unit vector `e_X` of a colour-exotic type `X`. The field `e_X ⊗ m_{λ,k}` has `H`-type `X`, and by (i) its two-point functions are `b^j_λ(m_{λ,k}, m_{λ,k})`. ∎

**Corollary L.4 (any quasi-local field, with a uniform gap).** Let `ω` be gauge invariant, stationary and cell symmetric, and let `γ_j = α^{n_j}∘τ_{t_j}` with `t_j ∈ 2Z^3`. Suppose there are `C, μ > 0` such that `|ω(φ^*γ_j(φ))| ≤ C‖π(φ)Ω‖^2e^{−μj}` for every fermion-odd quasi-local `φ` of a colour-exotic `H`-type (a uniform gap for those fields). Then every fermion-odd quasi-local `ψ` of `H`-type `(3,2)_y` satisfies the same bound.

*Proof.* `ω` is invariant under `γ_j`, so `γ_j` has a unitary `U_j` on `H_ω` with `U_jπ(A)Ω = π(γ_j(A))Ω`, and `U_j` commutes with `U` (Lemma G(ii), (v)). Put `v = π(ψ)Ω` and `v_λ` its part in `H_λ`. `v_λ` has `H`-type `(3,2)_y`, so `v_λ = Σ_ke_k ⊗ m_k` with `e_k` an orthonormal basis of the `(3,2)_y`-component of `λ`. ### 4.2 Fermion parity is a central gauge element

**Proposition N (gauged fermion parity).** On every `L5-O` update:
(i) `𝔄^G ⊂ 𝔄^{even}`. No gauge-invariant quasi-local operator is fermion odd. So no product of a fermion-odd field with bosonic fields (a Higgs-type dressing) is gauge invariant.
(ii) Let `V ⊂ 𝔄^{even}` be a finite-dimensional space that every `κ_g` maps to itself. Then every `z ∈ Z` acts on `V` as the identity. So the stabilizer in `Spin(10)` of every vector of `V`, and of every vector of every representation built from such spaces by sums, tensor products and duals, contains `Z`.
(iii) For a Case I embedding with `α` even, `±ϖ ∉ H`. So a stabilizer as in (ii) that contains `H` contains `H·Z`, which is strictly larger than `H`.
(iv) If reading R-E is taken with a completeness clause (every local gauge-invariant observable of the effective description is matched by a gauge-invariant observable of the law), then the effective description has no fermion-odd gauge-invariant local observable.
(v) `L5-U` has the same structure, with `−1_5 ∈ U(5)` in place of `ϖ`. The Standard Model is different. It has fermion-odd gauge-invariant local observables: `ν^c` itself, and the contraction `ε_{ab}Φ^aL^b` of the Higgs doublet (hypercharge `1/2`) with the lepton doublet (hypercharge `−1/2`). And its group has no element that acts as `−1` on the lepton doublet and as `+1` on the Higgs doublet.

*Proof.* (i) Local elements of `𝔄^G` are dense in `𝔄^G` (`VC1_5_DRAFT.md` Lemma 1.1(b)). A local `B ∈ 𝔄^G` is fixed by every local gauge map, so by `κ_ϖ`, which is `Θ` (Lemma G(iv)). Continuity of `Θ` gives the rest. (ii) `κ_ϖ = Θ` is the identity on `𝔄^{even}`, `κ_{−1}` is the identity on `𝔄` (Lemma G(iv)), and `−ϖ` is their product. A group element that acts trivially on each space acts trivially on sums, tensor products and duals. (iii) `CG1_KILL_DRAFT.md` Theorem O-I(iii), line 115: all members are fermion odd "exactly when `α` is even. That holds exactly when `−1_{SO(10)}` is not in the image of `H` in `SO(10)`." Both `ϖ` and `−ϖ` map to `−1_{SO(10)}`. (iv) By (i), the law has no fermion-odd gauge-invariant observable, and the completeness clause carries this to the effective description. (v) For `L5-U`: section 2.2 quotes Lemma Z-U and CG1K line 178 ("this is the action of `−1_5` at the sites of a region"); `−1_5` is central in `U(5)` and acts on even operators as `+1`. For the Standard Model: an element acting as a scalar on each irreducible field lies, by Schur's lemma applied to the `(3,2)` field, in the centre of each simple factor, so it is `(ζ^k1_3, (−1)^e1_2, e^{iθY})`. On a field of triality `0`, doublet parity `1` and hypercharge `q/6` it acts as `(−1)^ee^{iqθ/6}`. The lepton doublet has `q = −3` and the Higgs doublet `q = 3`. The two conditions `(−1)^ee^{−iθ/2} = −1` and `(−1)^ee^{iθ/2} = 1` multiply to `(−1)^{2e} = −1`, which is false. ∎

So on both five-mode laws every fermion carries the charge of a central gauge element that no bosonic order breaks. Under the completeness clause, the effective description of either law cannot be the Standard Model with `ν^c` a total singlet: its gauge group must act on `ν^c` and on `ε_{ab}Φ^aL^b`, for example through a gauged fermion parity such as `ϖ`. In grand unified models with tensor Higgs fields the centre of `Spin(10)` likewise stays unbroken and acts on fermions as `−1` (credited from memory, not used). EW1's wording, "breaks at long distance to `SU(3) x U(1)_em`", has to be read up to this element on either five-mode law. That is a remark about wording, not a failing step.

### 4.3 No classical covariant field at finite register size

**Proposition Q (covariant multiplets of commuting operators are trivial).** Let `𝔅` be a finite-dimensional C*-algebra and `K` a connected compact group acting on `𝔅` by automorphisms `θ_k`, continuously in `k`. Let `Φ_1, …, Φ_d ∈ 𝔅` span a space that the `θ_k` map to itself, on which `K` acts by an irreducible representation other than the trivial one. Then the `Φ_i` and `Φ_j^*` do not all commute with one another.

*Proof.* Suppose they all commute. The C*-algebra `𝒞` they generate is commutative, and each `θ_k` maps it onto itself, since `θ_k` is a `*`-automorphism that maps the generators into their span. A finite-dimensional commutative C*-algebra is the span of finitely many pairwise orthogonal minimal projections `p_1, …, p_N`. Each `θ_k` permutes them. Distinct ones satisfy `‖p_i − p_j‖ = 1`. So `k ↦ θ_k(p_i)` is a continuous map from `K` to a finite set with the discrete topology. `K` is connected, so the map is constant: `θ_k(p_i) = p_i`. So `θ_k` is the identity on `𝒞`, and every `Φ_i` is fixed by `K`. Then `K` acts trivially on their span, which contradicts the hypothesis. ∎

On `L5-O`, take `𝔅` the algebra of any finite set of sites and links, and `K` the gauge group at one site of the set, or the global action of `Spin(10)` or of `H` on the set (each maps `𝔅` to itself, section 1.1). So no multiplet of `L5-O` in a nontrivial irreducible representation has commuting entries, at any finite register size: a classical Higgs field does not exist on the lattice. The same proof holds on `L5-U` and on `L_cube` with spin-`S` links. Compare `GA2_Y2_DRAFT.md` Theorem N (line 61: "If `𝐔` has a bounded inverse, then `τ = 0`"), which excludes invertible covariant link matrices on finite registers. Proposition Q is a different statement: it concerns any multiplet in any finite-dimensional algebra, invertible or not. It needs `K` connected; for a finite group the multiplication operators on `L^2(K)` commute (`GA2_Y2_DRAFT.md` Proposition N4).

### 5.3 Four attacks, each failing at a named step

These differ from the three attacks of `CH1_5_DRAFT.md` section 5.2 (free updates; PS-8 on the elementary content; `Spin(10)`-symmetric gapping of `16 ⊕ 16bar`).

**Attack A1 (kill: promote Theorem L).** Claim: `Spin(10)` acts unbroken on the GNS space of every gauge-invariant state (Lemma G, Theorem L), so the effective group is `Spin(10)`, and CH1-5O-E is false. *Fails at the step "the effective group acts on `H_ω` through `U`".* R-E compares only gauge-invariant observables (`Λ_r ⊂ 𝔄^G`), on which every `κ_g` is the identity (Theorem E). Theorem L says what `U` does on `H_ω`; R-E's group acts on the fields of the comparison model. No step links the two. The attack also proves too much. Lemma G and Theorem L use only an exact local gauge group and a gauge-invariant state, so the same inference would exclude every effective breaking on every such law, including the breaking of `U(1)_N` on `L5-U` that `CH1_5_DRAFT.md` leaves open (line 302: "whether VC1 breaks `U(1)_N`"), and the standard Higgs regime of lattice gauge-Higgs models (credited from memory, context only).

**Attack A2 (kill: the centre).** Claim: the centre `Z` lies in every stabilizer of bosonic data (Proposition N(ii)); every site carries a spinor-class Gauss charge in every state (`LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` Theorem K (K2): "In every state, at every time, in finite or infinite volume, the matter at `x` carries the same spinor-class charge"); every fermion is odd under `ϖ`. So no `H`-type order can arise, and `G_∞ = Spin(10)`. *Fails at the step "a closed subgroup that contains `Z` and `H` is all of `Spin(10)`".* Take the standard embedding, `ψ(Y) = i\,diag(−2, −2, −2, 3, 3)/6` on `C^5`. Let `v_1 = ψ(Y)` in the adjoint representation `Λ^2`. Let `v_2 = f ⊗ f` in `Λ^5(C^{10}) ⊗ Λ^5(C^{10})`, with `f = f_1 ∧ ⋯ ∧ f_5` and `f_j = e_{2j−1} − ie_{2j}`. `Z` acts trivially on both, because `−1_{SO(10)}` acts on `Λ^2` and on `Λ^5 ⊗ Λ^5` as `(−1)^2` and `(−1)^{10}`. The centralizer of `v_1` in `so(10)` has dimension 13 ([check] (4)): it is `u(3) ⊕ u(2)` inside `u(5)`, because `ψ(Y)` has the four distinct eigenvalues `±i/3, ±i/2` with multiplicities 3, 3, 2, 2. `U(5)` acts on `f` by `det^{±1}`, so on `v_2` by `det^{±2}`, and the elements of `u(3) ⊕ u(2)` that fix `v_2` are those of trace zero ([check] (4b): dimension 12). Trace zero and block scalar means `i\,diag(s, s, s, t, t)` with `3s + 2t = 0`, which is the direction of `ψ(Y)`. So the joint stabilizer is a closed subgroup with Lie algebra `ψ(𝔤_SM)`, of dimension 12, and it contains `Z` (`−1_5` has `det^2 = 1`, and it lifts to `±ϖ`). So `Z` and `H` together allow `G_∞ = H·Z` up to finitely many components. The background spinor-class charges are central: every `g` fixes them up to the scalar `z_x = −1`, so they select no subgroup. And "fermions are `ϖ`-odd" only asks `ϖ ∈ G_∞`. Whether the law produces such order is CH1-5O-E itself.

**Attack A3 (kill: finite registers).** Claim: by Proposition Q no classical Higgs field exists at any finite register size, so no Higgs-type order exists, and `G_∞ = Spin(10)`. *Fails at the step "R-E needs a classical lattice field".* R-E compares gauge-invariant correlations with a model; the model's Higgs field need not be an operator of `𝔄`. Proposition Q is also a statement at fixed finite size. Averages over many mutually commuting regions are nearly classical: if `X_{i,a}` lies in the `i`-th of `N` mutually commuting algebras and `X̄_a = N^{-1}Σ_iX_{i,a}`, then `[X̄_a, X̄_b] = N^{-2}Σ_i[X_{i,a}, X_{i,b}]`, so `‖[X̄_a, X̄_b]‖ ≤ N^{-1}\max_i‖[X_{i,a}, X_{i,b}]‖`. Exact commutativity fails at every finite size; approximate commutativity at block scale is not excluded. That is a ZM1 question.

**Attack A4 (build: the dark witness and its `SU(5)` lift).** Claim: `ω_0` realizes `Spin(10) → SU(5) ⊇ H`, because its stationary lift `Φ_0` is invariant exactly under a group containing `SU(5)` at every site (Proposition P). *Fails at the step "the lift's order appears in the gauge-invariant correlations of `ω_0`".* Let `C` be a closed lattice path that uses each of its links once, and let `W_C` be gauge invariant and a sum of products with exactly one factor `U^{ab}_ℓ` on each link `ℓ` of `C` (the vector-representation loops, which include the block plaquettes that `Λ_r` must contain). `ω_0(W_C) = Φ_0(E(W_C)) = Φ_0(W_C)`. `Φ_0` is a product over links, so `Φ_0(W_C)` is a sum of products of the numbers `⟨0|U^{ab}_ℓ|0⟩_ℓ`. Each is zero: `U^{ab} = iη^y_aη^x_b`, and `η^y_a` changes the occupation of one `y`-end rishon mode while `η^x_b` changes that of one `x`-end mode, so `U^{ab}|0⟩` is orthogonal to `|0⟩`. So `ω_0(W_C) = 0`. By the expansion in `Y2W_DRAFT.md` line 66, "`D_ω(Ŵ) = ω(Ŵ^†Ŵ) + ω(ŴŴ^†) − 2ω(Ŵ) − 2ω(Ŵ^†) + 2`", this gives `D_{ω_0}(cW_C) ≥ 2` for every `c ≠ 0`. So SC-loop (`Y2W_DRAFT.md` line 62) fails in `ω_0` for every `η < 2`, at every scale, for these loops. A weakly coupled model gives such loops expectations near a nonzero value, so (a) of CH1-5O-E fails for `ω_0` with any small `δ`. `ω_0` matches the infinite-coupling end. The `SU(5)` order of its lift is a choice of gauge, invisible in its gauge-invariant data.

### 5.4 What would settle it

*True:* an `L5-O` update and state that meet (a) and (b). This needs, in order, a plaquette class (GA1-P-5), a weakly coupled regime of the gauge sector (Y2-W[`L5-O`]; its option (a1) answers the fine-scale classicality part), and a Higgs-type mechanism built from even composites at some block scale (Attack A2 shows the representation theory allows `H·Z`). *False:* a theorem that every gauge-invariant stationary state of every `L5-O` update matches, at every scale, either the unbroken class or no weakly coupled class.
