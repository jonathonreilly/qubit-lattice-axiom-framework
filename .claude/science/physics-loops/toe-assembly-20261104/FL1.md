## 2. Cell group and lifts

**Theorem 1 (G: geometric inverter).** Every turn \(\gamma=(Q,t)\in\mathscr G\) fixes an even-cube centre \(p\in c_0+2\mathbb Z^3\). A half-turn \(\eta\in\mathscr G\) about \(p\) inverts it.

*Proof.* An order-three proper signed permutation has a three-cycle on the axes and equals \(DP^{\pm1}D^{-1}\), for a diagonal sign matrix \(D\), with \(P(v_1,v_2,v_3)=(v_3,v_1,v_2)\). Its translation obeys \((I+Q+Q^2)t=0\). For \(Q=P\), \(t'=t/2\) has coordinate sum zero and \(v=(0,t'_2,-t'_1)\) solves \((I-P)v=t'\). Conjugation by \(D\), and the analogous calculation for \(P^{-1}\), gives an integral solution in every case. Thus \(p=c_0+2v\) is fixed. The half-turn \(H_0(v_1,v_2,v_3)=(-v_2,-v_1,-v_3)\) obeys \(H_0PH_0^{-1}=P^{-1}\). Set \(H=DH_0D^{-1}\) and \(\eta=(H,(I-H)(p-c_0))\). Its translation is even, it fixes \(p\), and its linear part inverts \(Q\). □

**Theorem 2 (K: point stabilizers).** If \(K\le O\) contains a turn \(Q\) and no inverter of \(Q\), then \(K=\langle Q\rangle\cong C_3\) or the tetrahedral group \(T\) of order 12.

*Proof.* Lagrange gives orders 3, 6, 12 or 24. Order 24 contains an inverter by Theorem 1. At order 6, \(\langle Q\rangle\) has index two and an involution exists. If the involution commuted with \(Q\), their product would have order six, which no proper signed permutation has; hence it inverts \(Q\). An index-two subgroup contains all squares. The eight order-three rotations and the three coordinate half-turns are squares, so an order-12 subgroup is precisely \(T\). Its rotations outside \(\langle Q\rangle\) move the body-diagonal axis of \(Q\); it has no inverter. □

**Theorem 3 (S and L: canonical lifts).** Within the canonical lift family, each geometric element has two lifts differing by \(z\); lifts compose within the family. Every turn has exactly one lift \(a\) with \(a^3=1\), and that lift fixes the matter mode at each fixed site. If \(b\) lifts an inverter, \(bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\). Under IR-P, the internal factors satisfy \(STS^{-1}=T^{-1}\) and \(T^3=1\).

*Proof.* The face products of transformed and original staggered edge signs agree. Their ratio is a flat \(\mathbb Z_2\) edge field, hence has a site-sign potential, unique up to a global sign on the connected lattice. Composition preserves this condition. Thus \(a^3\) is either \(1\) or \(z\), and replacing \(a\) by \(za\) reverses the choice. At a fixed site its sign has cube one, hence is one. Both \(bab^{-1}\) and \(a^{-1}\) lift the inverse turn and have cube one, so uniqueness makes them equal. The conjugated \(SU(2)\) factor is the unique order-three lift of the inverse rotation. Cancelling it in the tensor product gives the internal relation. □

**GAP S1 / covariance.** Pass 1 defines *all* lifts merely as signed geometric maps commuting with the full tick, then proves uniqueness from preservation of the individual edge signs. The latter does not follow from the former, especially at exceptional parameters. The proof establishes uniqueness only for the canonical edge-preserving family. Nor does covariance of an unspecified plaquette layer show it preserves the same lifts without a compatibility condition. For O, the free walk uses \(W\)-conjugated lifts, as `LW1_5.md` A2-5 states. The claims below use these corrected domains.

**Examples and reported checks.** For \(\gamma_0x=(x_3,x_1,x_2)\), the image sign \((-1)^{y_1(y_2+y_3)}\) gives an order-three canonical lift. For \(\eta_0x=(1-x_2,1-x_1,1-x_3)\), \((-1)^{y_1(y_2+1)}\) gives a lift whose square is one: the two exponents along its orbit agree mod 2. Pass 1 reports `check_fl1.py` and `check_fl1b.py` checked their commutation with the free layers and inversion on an \(8^3\) torus. It also reports that two coordinate face half-turn lifts square to parity and anticommute, and an edge half-turn lift squares to one. These finite checks are not proofs for unspecified layers.

## 3. Turn charges and matching

**Theorem 4 (A and A1).** Under IR-P and Theorem 3's lift hypotheses, if the state keeps a turn and an inverter, then \(n_r(1)=n_r(2)\) for every irreducible gauge representation \(r\). Thus \(\dim M_r\equiv n_r(0)\pmod2\); a representation occurring once has charge zero.

*Proof.* Both internal factors commute with the gauge action and act on \(M_r\). If \(Tv=\omega v\), the relation \(STS^{-1}=T^{-1}\) sends \(Sv\) to the \(\bar\omega\)-eigenspace. Invertibility of \(S\) gives a bijection in both directions. The parity formula follows by adding eigenspace dimensions. □

**Corollary 4.1 (A0).** For every weight \(w(r)\), \(\sum_r w(r)\sum_q\hat q\,n_r(q)=\sum_r w(r)\sum_q\hat q^3 n_r(q)=0\). This includes the indicated dimension-weighted, simple-gauge-index-weighted, and signed charge-count sums. Subject to the standard instanton zero-mode formula, the corresponding light 't Hooft vertex phase is one. These results hold for any \(N\) satisfying the hypotheses.

*Proof.* Each summand contains \(n_r(1)-n_r(2)=0\), and \(\hat q^3=\hat q\). The vertex statement additionally uses \(2T(r)k\) zero modes per Weyl field at instanton number \(k\); this input was cited but not rebuilt or checked in pass 1. □

If an inverter normalizes the gauge action via an automorphism \(\sigma\) instead of commuting with it, the same argument pairs \(r\) with \(r\circ\sigma^{-1}\). A weighted sum still vanishes if its weight is \(\sigma\)-invariant. This is a separate conditional extension, not IR-P(b).

**Corollary 4.2 (A2).** If the charges are unpaired for some \(r\), no inverter of the turn is kept. At an even-cube centre \(p\) fixed by the turn, the linear parts of the kept elements fixing \(p\) form \(C_3\) or \(T\). Thus the state breaks at least one exact inverter symmetry if the update has the compatible lifts.

*Proof.* Apply the contrapositive of Theorem 4. A point-fixing element whose linear part inverts \(Q\) inverts the affine turn about \(p\). Theorem 2 then classifies the point stabilizer. □

**GAP A2 scope.** The conclusion concerns the subgroup fixing \(p\), not necessarily the full point-group image of a space group with translations or nonsymmorphic elements. Pass 1's assertion that a count can survive *only* in a phase whose entire kept point group is \(C_3\) or \(T\) is stronger than its proof. PS-1c excludes a separately added term that itself breaks the specified full lift symmetry to \(T\), but a symmetric update can have a symmetry-breaking state. Pass 1 reports #10022's copy mass preserves exactly \(T\); that source was not independently retrieved here.

**Theorem 5 (A3: conditional congruences).** Assume V0, a family with four weak doublets and no other weak-charged members, and a turn charge constant on each family copy. For a common nonzero charge \(q\), \(N\equiv0\pmod3\). For independently chosen nonzero charges \(q_k\), the condition is \(\sum_k q_k\equiv0\pmod3\), satisfiable exactly for \(N\ge2\). All-zero charges satisfy it for every \(N\).

*Proof.* Each doublet has \(2T=1\), so V0 is \(4\sum_k q_k=0\pmod3\). For even \(N\) use pairs \((1,2)\); for odd \(N\ge3\), use one triple \((1,1,1)\) plus pairs. One nonzero charge cannot sum to zero. □

**GAP in FL1-i(3).** \(C_3\) or \(T\) plus uniform nonzero charge is sufficient under V0, not an established necessary-and-sufficient condition for the numerical congruence. Other charge patterns can also satisfy it. AM-turn plus an inverter-keeping reference phase yields V0 only if the matching premise applies to both phases and such a reference phase exists; neither is established.

**Theorem 6 (D: abstract data).** For each \(N\ge1\) and either law, there are representations of the canonical lifts of \(\mathcal K=2\mathbb Z^3\rtimes\langle\gamma_0,\eta_0\rangle\) on \(\mathbb C^2\otimes F^{\oplus N}\) satisfying IR-P algebraically with all turn charges zero.

*Proof.* Let \(a,b,z\) be the lifts in the examples. They satisfy \(a^3=b^2=z^2=1\), \(bab^{-1}=a^{-1}\), with \(z\) central. Their point-group projections give six distinct elements, so the subgroup generated by \(a,b\) has six and meets \(\langle z\rangle\) trivially. Even translations lift with sign one and conjugate geometrically, since the example signs have period two. Choose the order-three spin lift \(\Lambda_3\) of the turn and a spin lift \(\lambda\) of the half-turn, with \(\lambda^2=-1\). Map \(a\mapsto\Lambda_3\), \(b\mapsto i\lambda\), \(z\mapsto-1\), and translations to one. These matrices satisfy all the displayed relations and translation conjugations. Tensor with \(1_W\). The spin factor for \(b\) is \(\lambda\) and its internal factor is \(i\); the internal factor for \(a\) is one. Thus IR-P's algebraic clauses and zero turn charges follow. □

The construction is data, not a state, mirror gap, or phase. The cited U-family perturbative traces and specified \(SU(2)\), mod-16 tests vanish per family under their stated formulas (`CH1_5U.md` 3.2–3.4); its A5-glob is conditional. CH1 gives the listed standard-family perturbative and weak-doublet checks for \(H\). Zero turn charge kills the displayed turn sums. **GAP D:** “every anomaly condition on file” and “anomaly matching on all exact symmetries fixes no \(N\)” are not proved: the full crystalline/mixed anomaly and UV matching are not evaluated. Abstract data with \(N=1,2\) are not counterexamples to a statement quantified over actual mirror-gapped phases. They refute only a purported implication from the displayed representation and congruence conditions alone.

## 4. Free spectrum and tastes

**Theorem 7 (B1–B3: free nodes).** Let \(0\le\mu<2\varepsilon<\pi\), \(s=\sin\varepsilon\). For each complex flavour of U, the free quasienergies obey \(\mu\le |w|\le2\varepsilon\). At \(\mu=0\), zero occurs only at cell momentum \(K=0\), with an eight-dimensional zero fibre. The linearized walk has two Weyl nodes of each handedness. O has the same spectrum, with the zero fibre shifted to \(K_*=(\pi,\pi,\pi)\). At \(\mu>0\) neither free walk has a zero node.

*Proof.* `LW1.md` B10 gives \(\cos w=\cos a_e\cos a_o+\sin a_e\sin a_o\chi(K)\), \(\chi(K)=\frac13\sum_i\cos K_i\), and the stated bounds and unique zero. At zero, unitarity and \(V+V^*=2I\) imply \(V=I\). The principal logarithm gives an analytic Hermitian \(h=i\log V\) near zero with \(V=e^{-ih}\), \(h^2=w^2I\), and \(h=\sum_iK_i\Gamma_i+O(K^2)\). By `LW1.md` C1, \(w^2=(s^2/3)|K|^2+O(K^4)\). Comparing quadratic terms gives \(\{\Gamma_i,\Gamma_j\}=2(s^2/3)\delta_{ij}I\). Set \(\gamma_i=\sqrt3\Gamma_i/s\) and \(\chi_c=i\gamma_1\gamma_2\gamma_3\). This is a central Hermitian involution. The antiunitary \(\Theta=D_s\mathcal K\) of B10 sends \(K\) to \(-K\) and commutes with \(V\); logarithm uniqueness gives \(\Theta\Gamma_i\Theta^{-1}=\Gamma_i\), so \(\Theta\chi_c\Theta^{-1}=-\chi_c\). Its two eigenspaces have dimension four each. On the minus eigenspace the Clifford generators are \(\sigma_i\otimes1_2\); on the plus eigenspace they are \(-\sigma_i\otimes1_2\). To see this, diagonalize the third involution; the first exchanges its two eigenspaces, and the product relation determines the second. The two velocity determinants have opposite signs. `LW1_5.md` §7.1 gives the unitary O conjugation and momentum shift. □

**Theorem 8 (B4–B5: free taste representation).** On each handedness of the massless zero fibre, the order-three turn has internal eigenvalues \(\omega,\bar\omega\). Its inverter exchanges the two eigenspaces. No one-dimensional taste subspace is invariant under both.

*Proof.* Canonical rotations preserve the walk and send \(K\) to \(QK\). Their signs are period two: the displayed turn and the edge half-turn generate \(O\), and their signs have this period. Differentiation of the covariance relation makes them rotate the three \(\gamma_i\). The spin generators \(-\frac i4\sum_{ij}\epsilon_{kij}\gamma_i\gamma_j\) are \(\sigma_k/2\) on both chiral blocks. Removing a spin lift leaves an operator in the commutant, so each block factors as \(\Lambda\otimes\tau_\pm\) on \(\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\). The turn lift has order three. Its only fixed cell residues are \(000\) and \(111\), with sign \(+1\) by Theorem 3; its fibre trace is therefore 2. The order-three spin factor has trace \(-1\), so \(\operatorname{tr}\tau_++\operatorname{tr}\tau_-=-2\). The proper inverter preserves the central chirality operator, and Theorem 3 pairs the \(\omega\) and \(\bar\omega\) eigenspaces within each block. A two-dimensional unitary order-three matrix with paired nontrivial eigenspaces has spectrum either \(\{1,1\}\) or \(\{\omega,\bar\omega\}\). The trace sum forces the second in both blocks. The inverter exchanges its two distinct eigenlines, proving the last assertion. Conjugation by \(W\) transfers the result to O. □

Pass 1 reports finite-torus checks of the fibre trace and covariance; these were not rerun. The conclusion about 0 or 2 retained tastes applies to an invariant **subspace of this fixed free taste representation**. **GAP B5:** an arbitrary interacting symmetric gap need not be represented by choosing such a subspace; mixing, composite excitations, and changes in the phase require separate analysis. The free count's dependence on \(\mu\) does not prove that the interacting family count varies with \(\mu\).

## 5. Three-element structures and the carrier question

**Theorem 9 (C1–C3).** Each site has three incident links in each cube tiling. Its cell-group stabilizer is \(C_3\), and its nontrivial elements cyclically permute those links. The cell-momentum triples \(\{(\pi,0,0),(0,\pi,0),(0,0,\pi)\}\) and \(\{(0,\pi,\pi),(\pi,0,\pi),(\pi,\pi,0)\}\) are \(O\)-orbits cycled by each turn. A three-cycle matrix whose cube is one has eigenvalues \(1,\omega,\bar\omega\).

*Proof.* `LW1.md` A2 identifies the site stabilizer as the rotations about the body diagonal through the site and its even-cube centre. Each site has one neighbour along each axis in each preserved tiling, and the stabilizer cycles the axes. Signed permutations act on cell momenta modulo \(2\pi\); their signs do not change a component equal to \(\pi\), so the two stated sets are orbits. For a monomial three-cycle with entries \(m_1,m_2,m_3\), the characteristic polynomial is \(t^3-m_1m_2m_3\), and its cube is \(m_1m_2m_3I\). □

**Corollary 9.1 (C4, corrected).** The six stated momentum fibres contain no zero node of either free walk, for any allowed \(\mu\).

*Proof.* By Theorem 7 their only possible zero momenta are 0 for U and \((\pi,\pi,\pi)\) for O. Neither belongs to the two triples. □

**GAP C4:** links at a site are geometric operators, not momentum eigenstates. The free dispersion does not prove that “none of these structures is light.” Nor does the three-cycle algebra establish a physical family carrier. The conditional charge calculation requires a specified three-dimensional operator space and an order-three action. The eight staggered corner Bloch vectors fold into the eight-dimensional U cell fibre, and under \(W\) into the shifted O fibre. This establishes a basis relation, not an identification of the abstract hw=1 triplet with a physical light-family sector. At zero mass Theorems 7–8 resolve that fibre into two tastes of each handedness; a selected three-corner subspace is not thereby an invariant three-family sector.

**GAP FL1-i (draft's proposed theorem).** CH1's one-site operator content, the free taste count, the three geometric structures, and Theorems 4–6 do not prove that no candidate carrier could fix the interacting family count. They show only the stated free and conditional facts. They also do not prove that a site has no emergent or nonlocal family index. The statement that these candidates alone have not supplied a proof is supported; a quantified no-go is not. The reported #10022 copy mass uses a different symmetry premise, so its congruence is not automatically transferable to a full-symmetry phase. The slab-note comparison identifies a possible premise difference, but without checking the slab models and their matching assumptions it does not reconcile them. “Every \(N\)” here means the displayed algebraic constraints allow each \(N\), not that each is dynamically realized.

## 6. Open obligations and proposed readings

**FL1-V (open).** For either law, do all admissible phases that realize a positive family count realize the same count? If so, is it three? The question must specify the domain of updates, states and IR prescriptions. If there are no such phases, a universal equality is vacuous and cannot establish an observed family count; existence is a separate obligation.

The three pass-1 attacks stop at these steps:

2. **Counting carriers:** Theorem 7 counts free nodes; Theorem 9 identifies geometric triples. No step identifies a unique carrier for an interacting chiral family or proves its multiplicity invariant. CH1's site composites do not settle their long-distance spectrum.

The free massless walk is vector-like; positive mass has no zero node. CH1's quasi-free exclusion and `CH1_5U.md` R2 hold only under their own hypotheses and reading.
