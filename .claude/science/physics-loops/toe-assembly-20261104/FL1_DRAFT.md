## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**Proved: none of the three candidate carriers fixes `N_f` on either law. One named lemma stays open.** (i) *The turn.* Every three-fold turn in the laws' symmetry group `𝒢 = 2Z^3 ⋊ O_{c_0}` is conjugate to its inverse by a half-turn about a perpendicular axis (Lemma G). On the laws' lifts the relation holds exactly once the turn's lift is taken of order three (Lemma L). So in every phase that keeps a turn and that half-turn, under the long-distance hypothesis IR-P, the turn charges of the light content pair up inside every gauge multiplet: charge 1 and charge 2 occur equally often (Theorem A). Every turn-odd anomaly value then vanishes: the turn-gauge-gauge and turn-gravity-gravity sums, the turn phase of every light 't Hooft vertex, and the `Spin × Z_3` count of García-Etxebarria and Montero. The #10022 condition holds for every `N_f`. Its premise "the light copy carries a nonzero turn charge" fails for every lone copy (Corollary A1). A count can survive only in a phase whose kept point group is `C_3` or `T` (Corollary A2). PS-1c forbids every update term that breaks the laws' point group to such a group; #10022's copy mass is such a term. So the turn route needs a state that breaks the point group. (ii) *The cell and its tastes.* At `μ = 0` the free tick of each law has exactly two left-handed and two right-handed light Weyl nodes per complex flavour, all at one cell momentum. At `μ > 0` it has none (Theorem B). In each handedness the two nodes carry turn charges 1 and 2, and no line in their span is kept by the turn and its inverter. So a symmetric gap keeps both or neither: 0 or 2 light tastes per handedness, never 1 or 3, and the free count depends on the free datum `μ`. (iii) *Witnesses.* For every `N ≥ 1` and both laws there is light-content data with `N` family copies that meets the clauses of IR-P with a turn and an inverter kept, and every anomaly condition on file (Corollary D). It is data, not a constructed phase. So anomaly matching on the laws' exact symmetries does not fix `N_f`. (iv) The laws do contain three-element structures permuted by a turn: the three links of one tiling at a site, and the three cell momenta `(π,0,0)`, `(0,π,0)`, `(0,0,π)` (Proposition C). The law selects none of them as a carrier, and each carries the charges `{0, 1, 2}`, which pair. **Open, after three attacks: FL1-V,** whether all admissible phases of a law that realize CH1's light family have the same family multiplicity. Attack 1 (anomaly matching) fails at Theorem A. Attack 2 (counting the law's carriers) fails at Theorem B and Proposition C. ### 1.2 Sign lifts

**Definition (sign lift).** For `g ∈ 𝒢` with linear part `Q`, a *sign lift* is an automorphism `β_g` of the matter-and-link algebra of the law that sends each site mode at `x` to `d_g(gx)` times the same mode at `gx`, with `d_g : Z^3 → {±1}`, and maps link registers geometrically, as in the proofs of `LW1_5.md` B1-U and B1-5, and that commutes with the tick. `α_P` denotes the global sign automorphism `c_{x,j} ↦ −c_{x,j}` (`γ_{x,a} ↦ −γ_{x,a}` on `L5-O`), links fixed. It is fermion parity on the matter algebra. It is central and `α_P^2 = id`.

**Lemma S (sign lifts).**
(S1) Every `g ∈ 𝒢` has a sign lift. Two sign lifts of the same `g` differ by `id` or by `α_P`.
(S2) A composite of sign lifts is a sign lift of the composite element, and the inverse of a sign lift is a sign lift of the inverse element.
(S3) If `γ ∈ 𝒢` has order 3, exactly one of its two sign lifts, called `β̂_γ`, satisfies `β̂_γ^3 = id`.
(S4) If `x` is fixed by `γ`, then `β̂_γ` sends each site mode at `x` to itself.
(S5) Example, [check]. For the turn `γ_0 : x ↦ (x_3, x_1, x_2)` about the body diagonal through `c_0`, the sign `d(y) = (−1)^{y_1(y_2+y_3)}` gives `β̂_{γ_0}`. For the half-turn `η_0 : x ↦ (1 − x_2, 1 − x_1, 1 − x_3)` about the axis through `c_0` in direction `(1,−1,0)`, the sign `d(y) = (−1)^{y_1(y_2+1)}` gives a sign lift. On the free tick these satisfy `β_{η_0} β̂_{γ_0} β_{η_0}^{-1} = β̂_{γ_0}^{-1}`.

*Proof.* (S1) `LW1.md` A2's proof (line 213) constructs the signs: "The transformed edge signs and original signs have the same face products. Their ratio has product one on every face. Fix its value at one site and define a site sign by multiplying the ratios along an edge path to that site. This is path independent". The construction is purely about the signs `η_i`, which are the same in `L_cube`, `L5-U` and `L5-O`. `LW1_5.md` B1-U and B1-5 use these signs for the dressed laws, with the geometric link maps. Uniqueness: a site sign `d` with the required edge ratios is fixed by its value at one site, since the lattice is connected. So the two choices are `d` and `−d`. They differ by `α_P`. (S2) The composite maps each site mode at `x` to a sign times the mode at `g'gx`, maps links geometrically, and commutes with the tick. The inverse of `β_g` maps the mode at `x` to `d_g(x)` times the mode at `g^{-1}x`, and also commutes with the tick. (S3) By (S2), `β_γ^3` is a sign lift of the identity. By (S1) it is `id` or `α_P`. If it is `α_P`, then `(α_P β_γ)^3 = α_P^3 β_γ^3 = α_P α_P = id`, since `α_P` is central. The two lifts cannot both have cube `id`: their cubes differ by `α_P^3 = α_P`. (S4) `β̂_γ` sends a mode `c` at the fixed site `x` to `d c` with `d = ±1`. Then `c = β̂_γ^3(c) = d^3 c = d c`, so `d = 1`. (S5) is a scratch computation (`check_fl1.py`, `check_fl1b.py`): on an `8^3` torus both signed permutation matrices commute with `R_e` and `R_o`, `β̂_{γ_0}^3 = 1`, and the conjugation relation holds. It is an example; nothing below uses it as a step. ∎

The edge half-turn about `(1,1,0)`, `x ↦ (x_2, x_1, 1 − x_3)`, has sign lift `d(y) = (−1)^{y_1 y_2}`, and its square is `+1`. The face half-turns `x ↦ (1−x_1, 1−x_2, x_3)` and `x ↦ (x_1, 1−x_2, 1−x_3)` have sign lifts `(−1)^{y_2}` and `(−1)^{y_3}`. Each squares to `−1`, and the two anticommute [check]. Two commuting elements of `O` with anticommuting lifts show that the lifted point group is a projective representation of `O` in the nontrivial class. This is #10016's "the cell rep is spinorial", rebuilt for the face half-turns. Nothing below needs it.

### 1.3 Light content, family count, and the hypothesis IR-P

**Definition (light content and family count).** A *light content* is a finite-dimensional Hilbert space `W` of left-handed Weyl field components, with a unitary representation `ρ` of a compact group `G_∞`, the long-distance gauge group of the phase. On `L5-U`, `G_∞` is `U(5)` or a closed subgroup. On `L5-O`, `H ⊆ G_∞ ⊊ Spin(10)` (CH1-5O-i). Right-handed fields enter as their left-handed conjugates. Let `F` be the one-family content as a `G_∞`-representation: on `L5-U` the twisted family `R_u = 10_u ⊕ 5bar_{−3u} ⊕ 1_{5u}` of CH1-5U-i or the standard-embedding content of CH1 Theorem F; on `L5-O` the family `F_t` of CH1 section 4.1 under `H`. The light content has *family count* `N` if `W ≅ F ⊗ C^N` as `G_∞`-representations. This is the quantity FL1(a) calls "the number of light chiral families".

**Hypothesis IR-P (crystalline form of the light sector).** The phase keeps a subgroup `𝒦 ⊆ 𝒢`: its state is invariant under the sign lifts of `𝒦`. The light left-handed fields span `C^2 ⊗ W`. The group generated by the sign lifts of `𝒦` acts on them by a unitary representation `U` with:
(a) `U(β_g) = Λ(β_g) ⊗ τ(β_g)`, where `Λ(β_g) ∈ SU(2)` lies over the rotation part `ḡ ∈ SO(3)` of `g`, and `τ(β_g)` is a unitary on `W`;
(b) every `τ(β_g)` commutes with `ρ(G_∞)`;
(c) `U(α_P) = −1`.
The pair `(Λ, τ)` is fixed only up to `(−Λ, −τ)` for each lift. The charge statements below remove this freedom by a stated choice.

IR-P is the form #10022 uses ("On each light chirality block (2 spin states) the turn factorises as a spin-1/2 rotation (det 1) times a scalar taste phase lambda", its lines 57-58) and the form of #10016 ("Write each rotation as V = rho(spin) tau(taste)", its line 29). It is the long-distance reading of a lattice rotation as a Lorentz rotation times an internal symmetry, which the crystalline equivalence principle (Thorngren and Else; Debray) supplies for invertible phases.

**Turn charges.** Let `γ ∈ 𝒦` have order 3. Take `β̂_γ` from (S3). Take `Λ(β̂_γ)` to be the element of order 3 in `SU(2)` over the rotation `γ̄`. ## 2. Group lemmas

Write an element of `𝒢` as `(Q, t)`, acting by `x ↦ Q(x − c_0) + c_0 + t`, with `Q ∈ O` and `t ∈ 2Z^3`. A *turn* is an element of order 3. An *inverter* of a turn `γ` is an element `η` with `ηγη^{-1} = γ^{-1}`. Let `P` be the cyclic permutation matrix, `(Pv)_1 = v_3`, `(Pv)_2 = v_1`, `(Pv)_3 = v_2`, and `H_0` the half-turn about `(1,−1,0)`, `H_0 v = (−v_2, −v_1, −v_3)`.

**Lemma G (every turn of the laws has an inverter).** Let `γ = (Q, t) ∈ 𝒢` be a turn. Then:
(G1) `Q` is one of the eight rotations `D P^{±1} D^{-1}`, `D` a diagonal sign matrix, and `(I + Q + Q^2) t = 0`.
(G2) `γ` fixes a point `p ∈ c_0 + 2Z^3`, the centre of an even cube, and acts as the rotation `Q` about `p`.
(G3) Let `H = D H_0 D^{-1}` when `Q = D P^{±1} D^{-1}`. Then `H ∈ O`, `H Q H^{-1} = Q^{-1}`, and `η := (H, (I − H)(p − c_0))`, the half-turn `H` about `p`, lies in `𝒢` and is an inverter of `γ`.

*Proof.* (G1) The linear part of `γ^3` is `Q^3 = I`. If `Q = I`, then `γ^3` is the translation by `3t`, so `t = 0` and `γ = id`, against order 3. So `Q` has order 3. Its underlying permutation of the axes is a 3-cycle: if it fixed an axis `e_i`, then `Q e_i = ±e_i` and `Q` would act on the other two axes as a signed permutation of a plane, whose order is 1, 2 or 4; so the order of `Q` would divide 4, and `Q^3 = I` would force `Q = I`. So `Q = D_1 P^{±1}` with `D_1 = diag(s_1, s_2, s_3)`. `det Q = 1` and `det P = 1` give `s_1 s_2 s_3 = 1`. For `D = diag(d_1, d_2, d_3)`, `P D P^{-1} = diag(d_3, d_1, d_2)`, so `D P D^{-1} = D (P D P^{-1}) P = diag(d_1 d_3, d_2 d_1, d_3 d_2) P`. With `d_1 = 1`, `d_2 = s_2`, `d_3 = s_1` this is `diag(s_1, s_2, s_1 s_2) P = D_1 P`, since `s_3 = s_1 s_2`. The case `P^{-1}` is the same with `P^{-1} D P = diag(d_2, d_3, d_1)`. So `Q = D P^{±1} D^{-1}`. The translation part of `γ^3` is `(I + Q + Q^2)t`, which must vanish.
(G2) A point `p = c_0 + 2v`, `v ∈ Z^3`, is fixed exactly when `(I − Q)(2v) = t`, that is `(I − Q)v = t'` with `t' = t/2 ∈ Z^3`. For `Q = P`: `(I − P)v = (v_1 − v_3, v_2 − v_1, v_3 − v_2)`, and `(I + P + P^2)t' = (s,s,s)` with `s = t'_1 + t'_2 + t'_3`. So `s = 0` by (G1). Then `v = (0, t'_2, −t'_1)` solves it: the first two coordinates match by inspection, and the third is `−t'_1 − t'_2 = t'_3`. For `Q = P^{-1}`: `I − P^{-1} = −P^{-1}(I − P)`, and `P^{-1}` maps the integer vectors with zero coordinate sum onto themselves. For `Q = D P^{±1} D^{-1}`: `I − Q = D(I − P^{±1})D^{-1}`, and `D` preserves `Z^3`. So `p` exists. Then `γ(x) = Q(x − p) + p`, because both sides are affine with linear part `Q` and agree at `p`.
(G3) `H_0` is a signed permutation matrix with determinant `1`: `det [[0,−1,0],[−1,0,0],[0,0,−1]] = 1`. Direct substitution: `H_0 P H_0^{-1} v = H_0 P (−v_2, −v_1, −v_3) = H_0 (−v_3, −v_2, −v_1) = (v_2, v_3, v_1) = P^{-1} v`. So `H_0 P H_0^{-1} = P^{-1}`, and inverting both sides gives `H_0 P^{-1} H_0^{-1} = P`. Conjugating by `D` gives `HQH^{-1} = Q^{-1}`, and `H` is again a signed permutation matrix of determinant one. The translation part of `η` is `(I − H)(2v) ∈ 2Z^3`, so `η ∈ 𝒢`. `η` fixes `p`. So `ηγη^{-1}` fixes `p` and has linear part `HQH^{-1} = Q^{-1}`. By (G2) applied to both, `ηγη^{-1}` and `γ^{-1}` are the rotation `Q^{-1}` about `p`. ∎

**Lemma K (the subgroups that keep a turn and no inverter).** Let `K ⊆ O` be a subgroup that contains a rotation `Q` of order 3. If no element of `K` conjugates `Q` to `Q^{-1}`, then `K = ⟨Q⟩` or `K = T`, where `T` is the group of 12 rotations made of the identity, the eight rotations of order 3, and the three half-turns about the coordinate axes.

*Proof.* `|K|` divides 24 and is divisible by 3, so `|K| ∈ {3, 6, 12, 24}`. If `|K| = 24`, `K = O` contains `H` of Lemma G. If `|K| = 6`, `K` has an involution `h`: the elements that differ from their inverses come in pairs, so an even number of elements equal their own inverses, and the identity is one of them. `⟨Q⟩` has index 2, so it is normal, and `hQh^{-1} ∈ {Q, Q^{-1}}`. If `hQh^{-1} = Q`, then `K = ⟨Q, h⟩` is abelian of order 6, hence cyclic, with an element of order 6. No rotation in `O` has order 6: the orders of signed permutation rotations are 1, 2, 3, 4. So `hQh^{-1} = Q^{-1}`. If `|K| = 12`, `K` has index 2. A subgroup of index 2 contains the square of every element. Every rotation of order 3 is a square (`R = (R^{-1})^2`), and every half-turn about a coordinate axis is the square of a quarter-turn. That gives `1 + 8 + 3 = 12` elements, so `K = T`. Finally, `T` has no inverter of `Q`, so `⟨Q⟩` and `T` do occur. For `h ∈ O`, `hQh^{-1}` is the rotation by the same angle about `h` applied to the axis of `Q`. So an inverter maps the axis line of `Q` onto itself. A coordinate half-turn moves every body diagonal to a different one: for example `diag(1,−1,−1)(1,1,1) = (1,−1,−1)`, which is not `±(1,1,1)`. An order-3 rotation about a different body diagonal fixes that diagonal and permutes the other three cyclically, so it moves the axis of `Q`. An element of `⟨Q⟩` commutes with `Q`, and `Q ≠ Q^{-1}`. ∎

**Lemma L (the lifts invert exactly).** Let `γ ∈ 𝒢` be a turn and `η ∈ 𝒢` an inverter of `γ`. Let `β̂_γ` be the order-3 sign lift of (S3) and `β_η` any sign lift of `η`. Then
(L1) `β_η β̂_γ β_η^{-1} = β̂_γ^{-1}`.
(L2) Under IR-P with `γ, η ∈ 𝒦`, take `Λ(β̂_γ)` of order 3 and any `Λ(β_η)`. Then `τ(β_η) τ(β̂_γ) τ(β_η)^{-1} = τ(β̂_γ)^{-1}` and `τ(β̂_γ)^3 = 1`.

*Proof.* (L1) By (S2), `β_η β̂_γ β_η^{-1}` and `β̂_γ^{-1}` are sign lifts of `ηγη^{-1} = γ^{-1}`. By (S1) they are equal or differ by `α_P`. The cube of the first is `β_η β̂_γ^3 β_η^{-1} = id`. The cube of the second is `id`. The cube of `α_P β̂_γ^{-1}` is `α_P`, since `α_P` is central of order 2. So the two are equal.
(L2) Apply `U` to (L1): `U(β_η)U(β̂_γ)U(β_η)^{-1} = U(β̂_γ)^{-1}`. By IR-P (a) the left side is `[Λ(β_η)Λ(β̂_γ)Λ(β_η)^{-1}] ⊗ [τ(β_η)τ(β̂_γ)τ(β_η)^{-1}]` and the right side is `Λ(β̂_γ)^{-1} ⊗ τ(β̂_γ)^{-1}`. In `SU(2)`, `Λ(β_η)Λ(β̂_γ)Λ(β_η)^{-1}` has order 3 and lies over `η̄γ̄η̄^{-1} = γ̄^{-1}`. Over a rotation by `2π/3` exactly one element of `SU(2)` has order 3 (section 1.3). `Λ(β̂_γ)^{-1}` has order 3 and lies over `γ̄^{-1}`. So the two `SU(2)` factors are equal. For a nonzero matrix `A`, `A ⊗ X = A ⊗ Y` forces `X = Y`. The second claim is in section 1.3. ∎

## 3. The turn: charges pair whenever an inverter is kept

**Theorem A (turn charges pair).** Let `L` be `L5-O` or `L5-U`, at any admissible free data, with any register and any PS-1c-covariant plaquette class. Consider a phase that satisfies IR-P with a kept group `𝒦` that contains a turn `γ` and an inverter `η` of `γ`. Then for every irreducible representation `r` of `G_∞`,
\[
n_r(1) = n_r(2).
\]

*Proof.* By IR-P (b), `T := τ(β̂_γ)` and `S := τ(β_η)` commute with `ρ(G_∞)`. So they act on `M_r = Hom_{G_∞}(r, W)` by `φ ↦ T∘φ` and `φ ↦ S∘φ`, and the actions keep the relations of Lemma L: `STS^{-1} = T^{-1}` and `T^3 = 1`. Let `Tv = ωv`. From `STS^{-1} = T^{-1}` we get `ST = T^{-1}S`, so `T^{-1}(Sv) = S(Tv) = ω Sv`, so `T(Sv) = ω̄ Sv`. So `S` maps the `ω`-eigenspace of `T` into the `ω̄`-eigenspace. `S` is injective. So `n_r(1) ≤ n_r(2)`. The same argument with `ω̄` in place of `ω` gives `n_r(2) ≤ n_r(1)`. ∎

*Remark (kept symmetries that move the gauge group).* If a phase keeps lifts composed with gauge transformations, `τ(β_η)` may only normalize `ρ(G_∞)`, with `τ(β_η) ρ(g) τ(β_η)^{-1} = ρ(σ(g))` for an automorphism `σ`, while `τ(β̂_γ)` still commutes with `ρ(G_∞)`. Then `τ(β_η)` maps `M_r` to `M_{r^σ}`, with `r^σ := r ∘ σ^{-1}`, and the same proof gives `n_{r^σ}(2) = n_r(1)`. Every turn-odd sum `Σ_r w(r) Σ_q q̂ n_r(q)` with `w(r^σ) = w(r)` still vanishes: reindexing `r ↦ r^σ` turns `Σ_r w(r) n_r(2)` into `Σ_r w(r) n_r(1)`. This covers dimensions, Dynkin indices of simple factors and squares of abelian charges. Weights that `σ` reverses are not controlled here.

**Corollary A0 (every turn-odd value vanishes).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem A, write `q̂ ∈ {0, 1, −1}` for the representative of `q ∈ Z_3`. Then:
(i) The light content has as many Weyl field components of turn charge 1 as of charge 2: `Σ_r dim(r) n_r(1) = Σ_r dim(r) n_r(2)`. So the García-Etxebarria-Montero count of their eq. (4.10), `Σ_i s_i` with `s_i = +1, −1, 0` for charges `1, 2, 0`, is exactly `0`.

## 5. Where a three lives in the laws

**Proposition C (three-element structures).** On both laws:
(C1) Each site `x` has three links in each tiling. Its stabilizer in `𝒢` consists of the identity and the two turns about the body diagonal through `x` and the centre of its even cube. These turns permute the three links of each tiling at `x` cyclically.
(C2) The three cell momenta `(π,0,0)`, `(0,π,0)`, `(0,0,π)` form one orbit of `O`, and every turn about a cube centre permutes them cyclically. The same holds for `(0,π,π)`, `(π,0,π)`, `(π,π,0)`.
(C3) Let a turn act on a three-dimensional space of operators or fields by a matrix with one nonzero entry in each row and column, arranged along a 3-cycle, and let its order-3 lift act with cube `1`. Then its eigenvalues are `1, ω, ω̄`: the turn charges are `{0, 1, 2}`. They pair, as Theorem A requires.
(C4) None of these structures is light in the free tick: at `μ = 0` the only light momentum is `K_*`, and at `μ > 0` nothing is light.

*Proof.* (C1) `LW1.md` A2's proof (line 215): "A rotation about a site must fix the centre of its even cube; its stabilizer consists of the identity and the two turns about that body diagonal." A site `x = 2b + r` lies in one even cube, `2b + {0,1}^3`, and its even-tiling links join it to `x + (1 − 2r_i) e_i`, `i = 1, 2, 3`. Its odd-tiling links join it to its three neighbours in its odd cube. `𝒢` preserves each tiling (`LW1_5.md` line 205, proof of S1-S4: "The spatial group preserves each tiling"). A stabilizer turn fixes `x` and permutes the coordinate directions by a 3-cycle (Lemma G (G1)). So it permutes the three links of each tiling at `x` and fixes none of them. (C2) `O` acts on cell momenta by `K ↦ QK` mod `2π`. A signed permutation sends `π e_i` to `±π e_j ≡ π e_j`, so the set `{π e_1, π e_2, π e_3}` is invariant, and a turn's 3-cycle on the axes cycles it. The second set is the image of the first under `K ↦ K + (π,π,π)`, which commutes with the action. (C3) Such a matrix `M` with entries `m_1, m_2, m_3` has characteristic polynomial `t^3 − m_1 m_2 m_3` and `M^3 = m_1 m_2 m_3 I`. `M^3 = I` gives `m_1 m_2 m_3 = 1`, so the eigenvalues are the three cube roots of unity. (C4) Theorem B (B1)-(B2). For `L5-U`, `χ(K) = ±1/3` at the six momenta of (C2), so `w(K) > 0`. For `L5-O` the light momentum is `(π,π,π)`, and the six momenta of (C2) are not `(π,π,π)`. ∎

## 6. The settlement of FL1-i on the two laws

**Theorem FL1-i (no candidate carrier fixes the count).** Let `L` be `L5-O` or `L5-U`, at any admissible free data, with any register and any PS-1c-covariant plaquette or mirror-gapping class.

3. *The turn.* Every turn of `L`'s symmetry group has an inverter in that group (Lemma G), and on the lifts the inversion is exact (Lemma L). 4. *Witnesses.* For every `N ≥ 1` there is light-content data with family count `N` that meets clauses (a)-(c) of IR-P with a turn and an inverter in its kept group, and passes every anomaly condition on file (Corollary D).

So none of the three candidates on file — the `2^3` cube cell, the taste structure, #10022's turn argument — fixes `N_f` on either law. *Proof.* Item 1 is cited from frozen `CH1.md` and the PS-5 text. Items 2, 3 and 4 are Theorem B, Lemmas G and L with Theorem A and Corollaries A0-A3, and Corollary D. ∎

## 7. What stays open: FL1-V, after three attacks

**Definition.** Let `L ∈ {L5-O, L5-U}`. A phase of `L` *realizes `N` families* if it meets CH1-5U-i (for `L5-U`) or CH1-5O-i (for `L5-O`) with "exactly one" family replaced by "exactly `N` copies of the family": a covariant finite-depth update of `L`'s form, a stationary state invariant under the kept symmetries, and light content `F ⊗ C^N` under CH1's hypothesis IR, with every other fermion-odd excitation gapped. Four-test check: (1) pass, it restates CH1's frozen target with `N` left free; (2) pass; (3) pass, it decides nothing; (4) pass. Not adopted.

**FL1-V (open).** For each `L`: do all phases of `L` that realize some number of families realize the same number? If yes, `N_f` is law-level, and FL1 asks whether it is 3. If no, `N_f` is registered data of the realized state (`realized_state_primitive`: "a quoted number must be invariant over the law-admissible family, else it is registered data").

**Attack 1: fix `N` by anomaly matching on the law's exact symmetries (towards yes).** Route: compute every anomaly value of the law's exact symmetries on `F ⊗ C^N` — gauge, gravitational, point group through IR-P, the `Spin × Z_3` count — and match it to the law's own value, which does not depend on the phase. A value linear in `N` and nonzero per family would constrain `N`. *Where it fails.* The gauge and gravitational values are zero per family (`CH1.md` Theorem U-A, line 165, and `CH1_5U.md` Theorem 3.2 on `L5-U`; `CH1.md` line 175 on `L5-O` under `H`). Every turn-odd value is zero for every `N` in each phase that keeps an inverter (Theorem A, Corollary A0). Witness data exist for every `N ≥ 1` (Corollary D). The step "the light content carries a turn-odd value that grows with `N`" is false whenever an inverter is kept. In a phase that breaks the inverters it holds only with one common nonzero copy charge (Corollary A3), and whether a family-realizing phase has that charge pattern is not a statement about the law.

**Attack 2: fix `N` by counting the law's carriers (towards yes).** Route: show that every family-realizing phase draws its light family from one carrier structure of the law whose size is fixed: the cell's residues, the free tastes, or a three-element structure. *Where it fails.* The free tastes are not a fixed number over the free data: two per handedness at `μ = 0`, none at `μ > 0` (Theorem B). Under a turn and its inverter they come only in pairs (B5). The law also contains carriers of three (Proposition C (C1), (C2)), and they are not light in the free tick (C4). The family itself is made of composite operators (`CH1.md` Theorem F, line 129: `c`, `ccc`, `c^5`; Corollary U-T″, line 201, for the twisted family at one site), and dressing by the three links of a tiling changes how the site stabilizer acts on them (C1). The step "the law fixes which carrier is light" has no kinematic proof: it is the dynamics of CH1-i.

**Attack 3: two phases with different `N` (towards no).** Route: exhibit, for one law, two phases that realize `N ≠ N'` families. *Where it fails.* No phase that realizes any number of families is known for either law: CH1-5U-i and CH1-5O-i are open (`CH1.md` section 5, Amendments 1 and 2). The free ticks are not such phases: their light content is vector-like (`CH1.md` F1 and F3, lines 37 and 45; Theorem B: two plus two Weyl nodes per flavour at `μ = 0`), a symmetric gap of the free node removes tastes only in pairs (B5), and `CH1_5U.md` R2 (line 59) shows that a number-conserving quasi-free state whose elementary fields are gapped has no light field of any charge. So no witness phase is available, and the construction step fails.

**Where the lemma stands.** The three attacks fail at named steps: Theorem A with Corollary D (attack 1), Theorem B with Proposition C (attack 2), the absence of any family-realizing phase (attack 3). The route on file that would make the count law-level and equal to `0 mod 3` is #10022's, restated for these laws: every family-realizing phase of `L` breaks the inverters of a turn, with a kept point group `C_3` or `T` at its centre, and gives all copies one common nonzero turn charge; then V0 gives `N ≡ 0 (mod 3)` (Corollary A3 (ii)). The sublattice sign `(−1)^{x_1+x_2+x_3}` is fixed by `T` and reversed by the inverters: under `γ_0` the coordinate sum is unchanged; under the coordinate half-turn `x ↦ (1−x_1, 1−x_2, x_3)` it changes by the even number `2 − 2x_1 − 2x_2`; these two kinds generate `T`; and under `η_0` it becomes `3 − (x_1+x_2+x_3)`, of the other parity. Number operators carry no sign under the lifts, so the staggered density `Σ_x (−1)^{x_1+x_2+x_3} n_x` is a `T`-invariant order parameter that every inverter reverses. Whether the vacuum of either law has such an order is VC1's question.
