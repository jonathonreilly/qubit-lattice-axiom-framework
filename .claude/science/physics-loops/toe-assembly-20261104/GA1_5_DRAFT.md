2. *Completeness (GA1(a)(3)).* Inside each flux sector the gauge-invariant algebra is a tensor product of site algebras (Theorem C0). 3. *Classical energy (GA1(a)(4)).* Proved in a coherent-state sense for every `m` and for `L5-O[S_+]`: on an explicit gauge-covariant family of product states the link symbol is exactly `c_m V` with `V` unitary, `c_m = min(m,10-m)/10`, so the plaquette symbol is exactly `c_m^4 tr V_p`, the Wilson form, which tends to the Yang-Mills magnetic energy at small fields (Theorem E). ## 2. Geometry of links, plaquettes and covariant families

**Notation.** Sites are `x ∈ Z^3`. A link `ℓ = (x, x+e_i)` has base `x`. Its parity is `x_i mod 2`; call it even or odd. A plaquette in the plane `{i,j}` with base `x` has corners `x, x+e_i, x+e_j, x+e_i+e_j`. Its type is the unordered pair `{x_i mod 2, x_j mod 2}`: `{0,0}` is an even face, `{1,1}` an odd face, `{0,1}` mixed. The tilings `T_e, T_o` and the cell group `𝒢 = 2Z^3 ⋊ O_{c_0}`, `c_0 = (1/2,1/2,1/2)`, are those of `LW1.md` section 1. Put `ε_i(x) = (-1)^{x_i}` and `s(x) = ε_1ε_2ε_3`.

**Lemma G1 (incidences).** (a) Every link lies inside exactly one tiling cube, of its own parity. (b) Every link lies in exactly four plaquettes: two faces of its tiling cube and two mixed plaquettes. (c) At every site `x` the even links are `ε_i(x)e_i` and the odd links are `-ε_i(x)e_i`, `i = 1,2,3`.

*Proof.* (a) If `x_i` is even, both `x_i` and `x_i+1` lie in `{x_i, x_i+1}`, and each other coordinate lies in `{2⌊x_j/2⌋, 2⌊x_j/2⌋+1}`. So `ℓ` lies in the even cube based at `2⌊x/2⌋`. An odd cube covers `{2k+1, 2k+2}` in direction `i`, which never contains both `x_i` and `x_i+1` for even `x_i`. The odd case is the same with the tilings exchanged. (b) The plaquettes containing `ℓ` lie in the planes `{i,j}`, `j ≠ i`, with bases `x` and `x-e_j`. Their types are `{x_i, x_j}` and `{x_i, x_j - 1}` mod 2. For each `j` exactly one of them has both parities equal to `x_i`. That one is a face of the tiling cube of `ℓ`, since its four links have the parity of `ℓ` and lie in that cube by (a). The other is mixed. (c) The link `(x, x+e_i)` has parity `x_i`; the link `(x-e_i, x)` has parity `x_i - 1`. ∎

**Lemma G2 (orbits).** (a) The stabilizer in `𝒢` of a site is cyclic of order 3. At the origin it is generated by the cyclic permutation of coordinates. (b) The stabilizer of a tiling cube acts on it as its full rotation group `O` (order 24). (c) The even faces, the odd faces and the mixed plaquettes are three `𝒢`-orbits, with 6, 6 and 12 members per cell.

*Proof.* (a) Write `g(x) = Q(x - c_0) + c_0 + 2t`. Then `g(0) = 0` if and only if `(1,1,1) - Q(1,1,1) = -4t`. The left side has entries in `{0, 2}`, so it vanishes and `Q(1,1,1) = (1,1,1)`. The determinant-one signed permutations fixing `(1,1,1)` are the three cyclic permutations. `𝒢` is transitive on sites (`LW1.md` A2), so every stabilizer is conjugate to this one. (b) For the even cube with centre `c_0 + 2t`, every `Q` with translation `2(t - Qt)` fixes it. For the odd cube with centre `c_1 = c_0 + (1,1,1)`, every `Q` with `t = ((1,1,1) - Q(1,1,1))/2 ∈ Z^3` fixes it. Translations carry these to all tiling cubes. (c) Let `Q e_i = δ_i e_{π(i)}`. A plaquette with in-plane directions `i, j` and normal `k` goes to one with in-plane directions `π(i), π(j)`. Its base coordinate along `π(i)` is `x_i` if `δ_i = 1` and `-x_i` if `δ_i = -1`, so in-plane parities are carried along with the axes. Its normal coordinate parity flips exactly when `δ_k = -1`. Translations by `2Z^3` change no parity. So the type is invariant. Within a type, the cyclic permutations move the plane; a quarter turn about `e_k` exchanges the two in-plane parities and keeps the normal parity; a half turn about an in-plane axis flips the normal parity and keeps the in-plane ones. These moves act transitively on the 3 × 2 × 2 = 12 mixed classes per cell, and on the 3 × 2 classes of each face type. ∎

**Lemma G3 (the three covariant pairings).** For `σ ∈ {0, +1, -1}` define the pairing `P_σ` at each site `x`: the even link `ε_i(x)e_i` is paired with the odd link `-ε_{i'}(x)e_{i'}`, where `i' = i` if `σ = 0` and `i' = i + σ s(x)` mod 3 otherwise. Then (a) each `P_σ` is `𝒢`-invariant; (b) every curve of `P_0` is a straight line; (c) every curve of `P_{±1}` is invariant under a nonzero translation in `2Z^3`, hence infinite.

*Proof.* (a) With `Q e_i = δ_i e_{π(i)}` and `x' = g(x)`, the computation in G2(c) gives `ε_{π(i)}(x') = δ_i ε_i(x)`. So `g` maps the even link `ε_i(x)e_i` to `ε_i δ_i e_{π(i)} = ε_{π(i)}(x')e_{π(i)}`, the even link of `x'` along `π(i)`, and likewise for odd links. The image pairing at `x'` is `i ↦ π(i'(π^{-1}(i)))`. For `σ = 0` this is the identity. For `σ = ±1` it is the shift `i ↦ i + σ s(x)` conjugated by `π`, which is the shift by `sign(π) σ s(x)`. Now `s(x') = ∏_i δ_i ε_i(x) = (∏δ_i) s(x) = sign(π) s(x)`, because `det Q = sign(π)∏δ_i = 1`. So the image is `P_σ` at `x'`. (b) `P_0` pairs `e_i`-links with `e_i`-links, so its curves are lines. (c) Follow the `P_{+1}` curve through the origin, where `ε = (+,+,+)` and `s = +1`. Step by step, with `ε` and `s` at each site: leave `0` along `+e_1` (even); at `(1,0,0)`, `ε = (-,+,+)`, `s = -1`, the arriving link is the even link of axis 1, paired with the odd link of axis 3, which is `-e_3`; at `(1,0,-1)`, `ε = (-,+,-)`, `s = +1`, the arriving odd link of axis 3 pairs with the even link of axis 2, `+e_2`; at `(1,1,-1)`, `ε = (-,-,-)`, `s = -1`, even axis 2 pairs with odd axis 1, `+e_1`; at `(2,1,-1)`, `ε = (+,-,-)`, `s = +1`, odd axis 1 pairs with even axis 3, `-e_3`; at `(2,1,-2)`, `ε = (+,-,+)`, `s = -1`, even axis 3 pairs with odd axis 2, `+e_2`; at `(2,2,-2)`, `ε = (+,+,+)`, the arriving link is the odd link `-e_2`, paired with `+e_1`. The translation `τ` by `(2,2,-2)` lies in `𝒢`, maps the curve to the curve through `τ` of the link `(0,-1,0)-(0,0,0)`, which is the link `(2,1,-2)-(2,2,-2)` on our curve. A curve of a pairing is determined by any one of its links. So the curve is `τ`-invariant and infinite. Every `P_{+1}` curve is a `𝒢`-image of one of the three curves through the origin, which the cyclic permutations exchange. `P_{-1}` is the same argument with the shift reversed. [check] `/tmp/toe-ga15/helix.py` traced the same six steps from an independent group-orbit construction of the pairing (343 sites, consistent). ∎

**Lemma G4 (pictures of a disjoint covariant family).** Let `ℱ` be a `𝒢`-invariant family of pairwise disjoint finite sets of links. For `X ∈ ℱ` and a site `v` put `X_v = X ∩ star(v)`, where `star(v)` is the set of six links at `v`. Then every nonempty `X_v` is a single link, the even triple at `v`, or the odd triple at `v`.

*Proof.* Let `Z_3` be the stabilizer of `v`. By G1(c) and G2(a) it permutes the even triple cyclically and the odd triple cyclically. For `g ∈ Z_3`, `g(X) ∈ ℱ`, so `g(X) = X` or `g(X) ∩ X = ∅`; hence `g(X_v) = X_v` or `g(X_v) ∩ X_v = ∅`. If `X_v` is `Z_3`-invariant it is a union of orbits: the even triple, the odd triple, or all six. Otherwise `X_v, gX_v, g^2X_v` are pairwise disjoint, so `|X_v| ≤ 2`. Two links of one triple are moved onto a set that meets them, so a two-element `X_v` is one even and one odd link; its three images form a perfect pairing, which is `P_0`, `P_{+1}` or `P_{-1}` at `v` (the pairs `(ε_i e_i, -ε_{i+k} e_{i+k})`, `k = 0,1,2`). Now use transitivity: `g ∈ 𝒢` carries the partition of `star(v)` by `ℱ` to the partition of `star(gv)`. If some `X_v` is all six links, then for a neighbour `w` across `ℓ ∈ X_v` choose `g` with `g(v) = w`; `g(X)` contains `star(w) ∋ ℓ`, so `g(X) = X`, and induction gives `X` = all links, infinite. If some `X_v` is a pair, every site's partition contains a full pairing `P_σ`, every link lies in a pair-part of its own set, and the set containing a link contains its whole `P_σ`-curve, which is infinite by G3. Both contradict finiteness. ∎

**Theorem D (disjoint covariant sublayers keep the two tilings apart).** Let a sublayer consist of link-only gauge-invariant gates `g_X`, `X ∈ ℱ`, with `ℱ` as in G4. Put `q^e_v = Σ p_{ℓ,v}` over the even links at `v` and `q^o_v` over the odd links (`L5-U`; `p_{ℓ,v}` is the rishon number at the `v`-end of `ℓ`). Then every gate commutes with every `q^e_v` and every `q^o_v`. No gate of such a sublayer moves a rishon between an even and an odd link at any site.

*Proof.* `g_X` commutes with `Q_v = N_v + Σ_{ℓ ∋ v} p_{ℓ,v}` (gauge invariance, `LW1_5.md` section 6). It acts only on the links of `X`, so it commutes with every summand outside `X_v`, hence with `Σ_{ℓ ∈ X_v} p_{ℓ,v}`. By G4, `X_v` is a single link or one triple. In each case `q^e_v` and `q^o_v` are sums of `Σ_{ℓ∈X_v}p_{ℓ,v}` and of operators on factors outside `X`. ∎

## 3. The candidate class GA1-P-5

**Loop operators.** For a traversal `t` of a link `ℓ` define the matrix `𝒰(t)`. On `L5-U`: `𝒰(t) = U_ℓ` if `t` runs from source to target and `𝒰(t) = U_ℓ^†` (entries `(U_ℓ^{kj})^†`) otherwise. On `L5-O`: `𝒰(t) = U_ℓ` forward and `𝒰(t) = -U_ℓ^T` backward. For a closed walk `c = (t_1, …, t_n)` put `W(c) = tr(𝒰(t_n) ⋯ 𝒰(t_1))`. For a plaquette `p` with boundary walk `∂p` put `B_p = W(∂p) + W(∂p)^†`.

**Lemma L (loops).** (a) Each `W(c)` commutes with every local gauge generator. (b) The lift of any `g ∈ 𝒢` (`LW1_5.md` B1-U and A2-5) maps `W(c)` to `W(gc)`. (c) On `L5-O`, `W(c)` is Hermitian and `W(c^{-1}) = W(c)` for a plaquette; on `L5-U`, `W(c^{-1}) = W(c)^†` for a plaquette. Hence `B_p` is invariant under the lifts and independent of the orientation of `∂p`.

*Proof.* (a) The row index of `𝒰(t)` belongs to the head of `t` and the column index to its tail: `LW1_5.md` Q and G give `[L_y, U^{ab}]` acting on `a` and `[L_x, U^{ab}]` on `b`, and `U` section 4 gives the same for rishons. In `𝒰(t_{k+1})𝒰(t_k)` the contracted index sits at the shared site. There the two factors transform with the site group by mutually inverse actions on that index, exactly as in the hop cancellation of `LW1_5.md` G and U. Each contracted corner is invariant; disjoint corners commute. (b) `L5-U`: the lift sends `r^{z}_k` to `r^{gz}_k`. If `gℓ` keeps the stored orientation, `U_ℓ ↦ U_{gℓ}`. If it reverses it, `U_ℓ^{jk} = r^y_j r^{x†}_k ↦ r^{gy}_j r^{gx†}_k = (U_{gℓ}^{kj})^†`. In both cases `𝒰(t) ↦ 𝒰(gt)`. `L5-O`: the lift sends `U_ℓ` to `U_{gℓ}` or to `-U_{gℓ}^T` (`LW1_5.md` A2-5). The definition of `𝒰` was chosen so that `𝒰(t) ↦ 𝒰(gt)` in all four cases (forward or backward `t`, kept or reversed `ℓ`). (c) A plaquette uses each link once, and entries on different links commute (even operators on different tensor factors). So transposes and adjoints of products reverse the order: `W(c^{-1}) = tr ∏ (-𝒰^T) = (-1)^4 W(c)` on `L5-O`, and `W(c)^† = W(c)` there because each `U^{ab}` is Hermitian. On `L5-U` the same computation gives `W(c^{-1}) = W(c)^†`. ∎

**Definition (candidate class GA1-P-5; proposed, not adopted).** For `L5-U[m]` or `L5-O[R]`, a plaquette layer is a finite product `W_B = W_r ⋯ W_1` of sublayers. A sublayer is `W_s = ∏_{X ∈ ℱ_s} g_X`, where: `ℱ_s` is a `𝒢`-invariant family of finite sets of links meeting each cell in finitely many members; each `g_X` is a unitary in the gauge-invariant algebra of the links of `X` (link-only); the gates of one sublayer commute pairwise; and the lift of every `g ∈ 𝒢` maps `g_X` to `g_{gX}`. The tick is `U_B = W_B W_E M_o M_e`, as in `LW1.md` section 1. The gate parameters are the free constants of the member.

**Theorem P.** (a) Each sublayer, and `W_B`, define automorphisms of the quasi-local algebra that fix every local gauge transformation. (b) `U_B` is cell covariant. (c) The class contains, for all real `θ_e, θ_o`, the **tiling-cube layer** `W^{cube} = ∏_{C ∈ T_o} e^{-iθ_o B_C} ∏_{C ∈ T_e} e^{-iθ_e B_C}`, with `B_C = Σ_{p ⊂ C} B_p` over the six faces of `C`.

*Proof.* (a) Gates are link-only, hence even, pairwise commuting and locally finite. `LW1.md` E1's construction applies verbatim; gauge invariance of each gate gives the fixed points. (b) The lift of `g` conjugates `W_s` to `∏ g_{gX}`, which is `W_s` again because the family is invariant and the gates commute. The base tick is covariant (`LW1_5.md` B1-U, A2-5). (c) Tiling cubes of one tiling have disjoint link sets (G1(a)), so the gates of one sublayer commute. `B_C` is gauge invariant by Lemma L(a). By G2(b) the stabilizer of `C` acts as `O`, which permutes the faces; by Lemma L(b,c) it fixes `B_C`; translations carry `B_C` to `B_{C'}`. ∎

**Four tests of the class (proposed, not adopted).** (1) It names no group, coupling, spectrum or propagation property: pass. (2) The conditions are a fixed finite list: pass. (3) It settles no downstream question: sections 6.2 and 6.3 exhibit members on both sides of the propagation question: pass. (4) Each condition is a reason already on file: commuting sublayers are E1's hypothesis, covariance is PS-1c, gauge invariance is PS-6, link-only is PS-6r's register: pass. *The tiling-cube member alone* passes (1), (2) and (4) as a candidate. Adopted as the law's only magnetic class it would fail test 3: by Theorem F it fixes in advance that the empty-matter gauge sector does not propagate.

**Remark (where the mixed plaquettes go).** By G1(b) the tiling-cube layer omits the mixed plaquettes, half of each orientation. By G2(c) the mixed plaquettes form one orbit in which every link lies in two members. So no sublayer can hold one gate per mixed plaquette with disjoint supports, and by Theorem D no disjoint covariant sublayer couples the even and odd links at all. A mixed corner needs a sublayer whose gates overlap and still commute.

## 4. Completeness of loops and hops (GA1(a)(3))

**Setting (`L5-U[m]`).** Fix a finite region: a set `S` of sites, the links `L` with both ends in `S`, and the matter at `S`. The local gauge group is `G_R = ∏_{v∈S} U(5)_v`, acting on matter at `v` and on the `v`-ends of links. `A^G_R` is its commutant in the region algebra. A *sector* `σ` is a joint eigenvalue of all `N_v` and all `p_{ℓ,v}`, with `p_{ℓ,x} + p_{ℓ,y} = m`. Since `Λ^m(C^5 ⊕ C^5) = ⊕_p Λ^p C^5 ⊗ Λ^{m-p} C^5`, each sector space factorizes over sites after a fixed reordering of fermionic factors (a sign per sector, which even operators do not see):
`H_σ = ⊗_{v∈S} Star_v^σ`, `Star_v^σ = Λ^{N_v}C^5 ⊗ ⊗_{ℓ∋v} Λ^{p_{ℓ,v}}C^5`.
Write `Star_v^σ = ⊕_λ V_λ ⊗ M^v_λ[σ]` for the `U(5)` isotypic decomposition (`V_λ` irreducible, `M` the multiplicity space). The *slots* at `v` are the matter and the six link ends. For slots `a ≠ b` put `E^{(v)}_{ab} = Σ_k f^†_{a,k} f_{b,k}`; it moves one fermion at `v` from slot `b` to slot `a`, keeping its colour.

**Rishon form of loops and hops (credit: the rishon picture of Brower, Chandrasekharan and Wiese, rebuilt here).** For `U^{jk} = r^y_j r^{x†}_k`, the row index of `𝒰(t)` is an annihilator at the head of `t` and the column index a creator at its tail. In `W(c)` the index contracted at a corner `v` joins the creator at the tail of `t_{k+1}` with the annihilator at the head of `t_k`. Regrouping the four bilinears into corner pairs moves each single fermion operator past a fixed number of others, so
`W(∂p) = ± ∏_{corners v} E^{(v)}_{out(v), in(v)}`,
one rishon moved at each corner, from the end of the arriving link to the end of the leaving link. Likewise the hop piece `h^+_ℓ = Σ_{jk} c^†_{y,j}U_ℓ^{jk}c_{x,k} = E^{(y)}_{matter, ℓ} E^{(x)}_{ℓ, matter}`.

**Theorem C0 (neutral factorization).** Let `𝒩_R` be the gauge-invariant operators of the region that commute with every `N_v` and every `p_{ℓ,v}`. Then
`𝒩_R = ⊕_σ ⊗_{v∈S} 𝒮^σ_v`, with `𝒮^σ_v = End_{U(5)}(Star^σ_v) = ⊕_λ 1_{V_λ} ⊗ End(M^v_λ[σ])`.

*Proof.* Commuting with all numbers makes an operator block diagonal over `σ`. On `H_σ` the group acts by the tensor product of the site actions. For a compact group `∏_v G_v` acting factor-wise, the Haar average `𝔼(X) = ∫ gXg^{-1} dg` is the projection onto the commutant, and on elementary tensors it is `⊗_v 𝔼_v`; so the commutant is the tensor product of the site commutants. Each site commutant is `⊕_λ 1 ⊗ End(M_λ)` by complete reducibility and Schur's lemma. ∎

**Lemma CL (corner lemma).** Let `a ≠ b` be slots at `v`, and let `σ' = σ + e_a - e_b` at `v` (other numbers equal). If `λ` occurs in both `Star^σ_v` and `Star^{σ'}_v`, the `λ`-component of `E^{(v)}_{ab}`, a map `M_λ[σ] → M_λ[σ']`, is nonzero.

*Proof.* `E_{ab}` commutes with `U(5)_v`, so it acts as `⊕_λ 1 ⊗ (E_{ab})_λ`. Put `J_+ = E_{ab}`, `J_- = E_{ba} = J_+^†`, `H = E_{aa} - E_{bb}`. Then `[J_+, J_-] = H` and `[H, J_±] = ±2J_±`. Fix the numbers of all other slots and of `p_a + p_b`. The `λ`-multiplicity spaces of these sectors form a finite-dimensional space on which `(J_+, J_-, H)` act, and the set is closed under adjoints, so the representation of `sl(2)` is completely reducible (orthogonal complements of invariant subspaces are invariant). In an irreducible summand with highest weight `n_0`, put `w_k = J_-^k w_0`; then `J_+ w_k = k(n_0 - k + 1) w_{k-1}`, so `J_+` maps the weight `h` line onto the weight `h + 2` line whenever both are weights. The weight of `M_λ[σ]` is `h = p_a - p_b`; that of `M_λ[σ']` is `h + 2`. If `h ≥ -1`, every summand with weight `h+2` also has weight `h`, so `J_+` maps onto the nonzero space `M_λ[σ']`. If `h ≤ -1`, every summand with weight `h` also has weight `h+2`, so `J_+` is injective on the nonzero space `M_λ[σ]`. ∎

**Theorem C (completeness criterion).** Let `𝒢_R` be the `*`-algebra generated by `𝒩_R`, the plaquette loops `W(∂p)` (`p` with all links in `L`) and the hop pieces `h^+_ℓ` (`ℓ ∈ L`). For a tuple `λ⃗ = (λ_v)_{v∈S}` let `S(λ⃗)` be the set of sectors with `λ_v ⊂ Star^σ_v` for all `v`. Join `σ` and `σ'` when they differ by the moves of one plaquette or one hop and both lie in `S(λ⃗)`. Then `𝒢_R = A^G_R` if and only if every `S(λ⃗)` is connected (condition **CONN_R**).

*Proof.* `A^G_R` is the commutant of the group image, so its commutant is the algebra `𝒜_G` the group generates. In the isotypic decomposition `H_R = ⊕_{λ⃗} (⊗_v V_{λ_v}) ⊗ 𝔐_{λ⃗}`, `𝔐_{λ⃗} = ⊕_{σ∈S(λ⃗)} ⊗_v M^v_{λ_v}[σ]`, the group algebra is `⊕_{λ⃗} End(⊗_v V_{λ_v}) ⊗ 1`, because the `⊗_v V_{λ_v}` are pairwise inequivalent irreducibles of the product group.

(If.) Since `𝒢_R ⊆ A^G_R` and `𝒢_R = 𝒢_R''` (finite dimension), it suffices to show `𝒢_R' ⊆ 𝒜_G`. Let `Y` commute with `𝒢_R`. It commutes with the sector projections in `𝒩_R`, so `Y = ⊕_σ Y_σ`. By C0 the commutant of `𝒩_R|_σ` is `⊕_{λ⃗} End(⊗V_{λ_v}) ⊗ 1`, so `Y_σ = ⊕_{λ⃗} y_{σ,λ⃗} ⊗ 1`. A plaquette maps `H_σ` to `H_{σ+∂p}` as `± ⊗_v E^{(v)}` over its corners. Its `λ⃗`-component is `⊗_v (E^{(v)})_{λ_v} ⊗ 1`, nonzero by Lemma CL when `σ, σ + ∂p ∈ S(λ⃗)`. From `YW = WY` on that component, `(y_{σ+∂p,λ⃗} - y_{σ,λ⃗}) ⊗ w = 0` with `w ≠ 0`, so `y_{σ+∂p,λ⃗} = y_{σ,λ⃗}`. Hops are the same with two corners. If every `S(λ⃗)` is connected, `y_{σ,λ⃗}` does not depend on `σ`, and `Y ∈ 𝒜_G`.

(Only if.) If `S(λ⃗) = S_1 ⊔ S_2` with no join between them, let `Y` be the projection onto the `λ⃗`-isotypic parts of the sectors in `S_1`. It commutes with `𝒩_R`. A plaquette or hop component from a sector in `S_1` lands in a sector outside `S(λ⃗)` (zero `λ⃗`-component) or in `S_1`. So `Y` commutes with `𝒢_R`, while `Y ∉ 𝒜_G`. ∎

**The exact sense of completeness.** Theorem C is GA1(a)(3) in exact form for `L5-U`. Within each flux sector the gauge-invariant algebra is the tensor product of the site algebras (C0), and it lies in `𝒢_R`. Every transition between two plaquette-adjacent or hop-adjacent sectors that share a type `λ⃗` is realized with nonzero amplitude (CL). The whole local algebra is generated exactly when CONN holds. Two remarks fix the edges of this statement. First, the neutral site algebras are needed as generators in the following local sense: the gauge-invariant algebra of a star region (one site with its six links, no matter at the neighbours) contains every `𝒮^σ_v`, which is noncommutative (for example `E_{ab}E_{ba}` and `E_{bc}E_{cb}` do not commute on one-rishon ends), while no plaquette and no hop lies in that region. Whether the neutral algebras are themselves generated by plaquettes, hops and numbers on larger regions is not decided here. Second, on `L5-O[S_+]` the end groups split each register as `S_+ = (16,16) ⊕ (16',16')`, so C0 holds there with the end chiralities as sector labels; the analogue of Lemma CL is not proved here.

**CONN, open after three attacks.**
- *A1, site saturation.* At one site, the set of `σ_v` carrying a given `λ` is connected under single moves `E_{ab}` (Lemma CL plus highest weights). Fails at: a plaquette step moves four sites at once, so site-wise paths need not combine into plaquette paths inside `S(λ⃗)`.
- *A2, homology.* `σ' - σ` is a sum of plaquette boundaries and hop paths (the region's cell complex has trivial first homology when it is a box). Fails at: at a corner where two plaquettes of the decomposition meet, the two orders pass through `σ_v + e_ℓ - e_a` or `σ_v + e_c - e_ℓ`, and nothing proved here keeps either in `S(λ⃗)`.
- *A3, exchange.* With Howe duality for `(U(5), U(7))` (not rebuilt here), each site set is the weight set of an irreducible `gl(7)`-module, which has the matroid exchange property, so each corner has a detour through one of four perpendicular ends. Fails at: a detour changes the plaquette used, which changes the moves at its other three corners; the four exchanges are not synchronized.

## 5. The classical energy of the link sector (GA1(a)(4))

**The question made exact.** Theorem N (`GA2_Y2.md` 2.1-2.2) rules out a finite register that carries an invertible, exactly covariant link. So "classical" cannot mean that the link operator becomes a group element. The sense used here is the coherent-state symbol: a gauge-covariant family of states labelled by classical link fields, on which the expectation of the layer's generator is a function of the field. GA1(a)(4) then asks whether that function tends to the Yang-Mills energy at small fields. The *classical energy* of a member of GA1-P-5 is the symbol of its generator, `Σ_p θ_p B_p` for the plaquette part and `τ_E Σ_ℓ (p_{ℓ,x} - m/2)^2` for `W_E`.

**Lemma E0 (reference state, `L5-U[m]`).** Put `c_m = min(m, 10-m)/10`. There is a state `ω_ref` on one register `Λ^m C^{10}`, invariant under `Γ(u ⊕ u)` for every `u ∈ U(5)` (the same colour rotation at both ends), with `ω_ref(U^{jk}) = -c_m δ_{jk}` and `ω_ref(r^{x†}Tr^x) = (m/10) tr T`.

*Proof.* For `m ≤ 5`, let `φ_j = (e^y_j + e^x_j)/√2` and let `ω_ref` be the normalized trace on `Λ^m(span φ)`. Its one-body matrix `Γ(u,w) = ω(f^†(u)f(w)) = ⟨w, Γu⟩` is `(m/5)Π_φ`, by `U(5)`-invariance of the trace on `Λ^m(span φ)`. Since `y`- and `x`-modes anticommute, `ω(r^y_j r^{x†}_k) = -⟨e^y_j, Γ e^x_k⟩ = -(m/5)·(1/2)δ_{jk}`. Also `⟨e^x_l, Γ e^x_k⟩ = (m/10)δ_{lk}`. For `m ≥ 5` use `10 - m` holes in the modes `ψ_j = (e^y_j - e^x_j)/√2`: `Γ = 1 - ((10-m)/5)Π_ψ` gives `-((10-m)/5)(-1/2)δ_{jk}` inside, hence `ω(U^{jk}) = -((10-m)/10)δ_{jk}`, and `(m/10)δ` on the `x`-block. Both spans are invariant under `u ⊕ u`, and a normalized trace is invariant under every unitary of its space. ∎

**Theorem E (coherent symbols; `L5-U[m]`, all `m`).** For a link field `𝐕 = (𝐕_ℓ)`, `𝐕_ℓ ∈ U(5)`, let `ω_𝐕 = ⊗_ℓ ω_ref ∘ Ad Γ(1 ⊕ V_ℓ)^†` with `V_ℓ = -𝐕_ℓ^†`, tensored with any gauge-invariant matter state. Then:
1. `ω_𝐕(U_ℓ) = c_m 𝐕_ℓ` (as a matrix of expectations).
2. *Covariance.* For a gauge transformation `g = (g_v)`, `ω_𝐕 ∘ Ad(g) = ω_{𝐕^g}` with `𝐕^g_ℓ = g_y^{-1} 𝐕_ℓ g_x` (the classical gauge action, up to the convention for `Ad`).
3. *Loops.* For every plaquette, `ω_𝐕(W(∂p)) = c_m^4 tr 𝐕_{∂p}`, where `𝐕_{∂p}` is the classical holonomy (ordered product, `𝐕` forward, `𝐕^†` backward). Hence `ω_𝐕(B_p) = 2c_m^4 Re tr 𝐕_{∂p}`, the Wilson form.
4. *Small fields.* Let `𝐕_ℓ = exp(i a A_ℓ)` with `A` the restriction of a smooth `u(5)`-valued field. Then `Σ_{p} Re tr(1 - 𝐕_{∂p}) = (a/2) Σ_x a^3 Σ_{i<j} tr F_{ij}(x)^2 + O(a^2)` on any fixed box, which tends to `(a/2)∫ Σ_{i<j} tr F_{ij}^2 d^3x`, the Yang-Mills magnetic energy. For the tiling-cube member the sum runs over tiling faces only and equals half of the all-plaquette sum up to `O(a)`, because each orientation keeps 4 of its 8 plaquettes per cell, spread uniformly (G1(b), G2(c)). So the classical magnetic energy of `W^{cube}` is `-(θ c_m^4) × (a/2)∫ tr F^2 + const + O(a^2)`; the plaquette constant `θ` and `c_m^4` set the coupling.

*Proof.* (1) With `Γ(w) f^†(e) Γ(w)^† = f^†(we)`, `Ad Γ(1⊕V)^†` sends `r^{x†}_k` to `Σ_l \overline{V_{kl}} r^{x†}_l` and fixes `r^y`. So `ω_𝐕(U^{jk}) = Σ_l \overline{V_{kl}} ω_ref(U^{jl}) = -c_m \overline{V_{kj}} = c_m (-V^†)_{jk} = c_m 𝐕_{jk}`. (2) `Γ(g_y ⊕ g_x) = Γ(g_y ⊕ g_y) Γ(1 ⊕ g_y^{-1}g_x)`; the first factor fixes `ω_ref` (E0), and the second composes with `Γ(1 ⊕ V)`. (3) Entries on different links are even operators on different tensor factors and the state is a product over links, so the expectation of the trace of the ordered product is the trace of the ordered product of expectation matrices. A backward traversal uses `U^†`, whose expectation matrix is `(c_m𝐕)^†`. (4) For a smooth field, `𝐕_{∂p} = exp(i a^2 F_{ij}(x_p) + O(a^3))` by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula. For Hermitian `X`, `2 Re tr(1 - e^{iX}) = tr X^2 + O(‖X‖^4)`. Summing over the `a^{-3}`-many plaquettes per orientation in a fixed box gives the Riemann sum. The tiling-face statement follows because the field is smooth and the kept plaquettes form a fixed periodic half of each orientation. ∎

**Proposition E′ (electric symbols are constant on the family).** For every function `f`, `ω_𝐕(f(p_{ℓ,x}, p_{ℓ,y}))` does not depend on `𝐕`; and `ω_𝐕(r^{x†}_ℓ T r^x_ℓ) = (m/10) tr T`, zero for traceless `T`. Every gauge-invariant operator on one link end is a function of its number `p`: `Λ^p C^5` is irreducible, so Schur's lemma applies. In particular the end `SU(5)` Casimir is `3p(5-p)/5` (normalization `tr T^aT^b = δ^{ab}/2` in the fundamental), and at `m = 5` the law's electric term is `(p - 5/2)^2 = 25/4 - (5/3)C_2`.

*Proof.* `Γ(1 ⊕ V)` acts within the `x`-end modes, so it commutes with `p_x` and `p_y`. It conjugates the `x`-block `(m/10)1` of the one-body matrix to itself. The Casimir value is `k(N-k)(N+1)/(2N)` for `Λ^kC^N`, at `N = 5`. ∎

**The same on `L5-O[S_+]`.** `GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 3.4 gives a joint eigenvector of the ten commuting involutions `U^{aa}` with `ω(U^{ab}) = s_a δ_{ab}`, `s_a = ±1`, and zero end-generator expectations. Rotating the `x`-end by the `Spin(10)_x` action on `S_+` (which turns the index `b` of `U^{ab}` as a vector, `LW1_5.md` Q) gives a gauge-covariant family with `ω_R(U) = D R^T`, `D = diag(s)`, so the symbol ranges over a coset of `SO(10)` in `O(10)` with `c = 1`. A plaquette has two forward and two backward traversals, so with `𝒰(backward) = -U^T` its symbol is `tr` of the classical holonomy, and item 4 holds with `c = 1`. The end chirality `Γ^x` commutes with `Spin(10)_x`, so its symbol is constant, as in E′.

**What this proves and what it does not.** GA1(a)(4) holds for the magnetic energy in the coherent-symbol sense on both laws, with an explicit normalization `c_m^4` (`L5-U`) or 1 (`L5-O[S_+]`). It is exact at every field strength and becomes Yang-Mills at small fields. It is consistent with Theorem N: the symbol is a scaled unitary, the operator is never an invertible covariant link, and the fluctuations are not small (`GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 3.2: `D_ω(cW) ≥ 2/K_m^2` for every state and every loop through a link once). The electric half of the Yang-Mills energy is not a symbol on this family: every electric operator of a fixed register is a function of end numbers (or end chirality), with constant symbol (E′). In a symbol picture it would enter as the generator of the field's motion, which needs a parameter that the fixed register does not have. That is GA2's Y2-W question, not GA1's.

## 6. Does the gauge sector propagate?

Definitions are those of `LZ1.md` section 1: a D1 state, its GNS triple `(H, π, Ω)` with tick implementer `Ũ` and cell translations `T_β` (translation by `2β`), the physical sector `H_phys = closure(π(A^G)Ω)`, bands `(d, F, V)`, nodes, and propagation (some `N_j ≠ 0`).

### 6.1 The empty-matter reduction (`L5-U`)

**Theorem R.** Let the tick be `U_B = W_B W_E M_o M_e` with `W_B` in GA1-P-5, and let `ω` be a D1 state with `ω(N_x) = 0` for every `x`. Put `𝕍 = W_B W_E` (link-only). Then:
(a) `H_phys` is the closed span of `π(A)Ω` over link-only gauge-invariant local `A`;
(b) for every link-only local `A`, `Ũπ(A)Ω = π(𝕍^* A 𝕍)Ω`.

*Proof.* Work on a finite region containing all supports and gates involved (E1). Let `P_0` project onto empty matter there. Since `N_x ≥ 0` and `ω(N_x) = 0`, the density matrix satisfies `ρ = P_0 ρ P_0`. (a) Split a gauge-invariant `A` into components `A_d` with `[N_x, A_d] = d_x A_d`. Gauge invariance conserves `Q_x = N_x + Σ_ℓ p_{ℓ,x}`, and every link total is fixed, so `Σ_x d_x = 0`. A component with some `d_x < 0` kills `P_0`, so only `d = 0` survives: `AP_0 = A_0P_0 = P_0A_0P_0 = |vac⟩⟨vac| ⊗ A_link`, with `A_link = ⟨vac|A_0|vac⟩` link-only. `A_link` commutes with the link part of every Gauss generator, because the matter vacuum is annihilated by `c^†Tc`. Then `(A - A_link)P_0 = 0`, so `π(A)Ω = π(A_link)Ω`. (b) Each cube generator `H_C` ends in a matter annihilator, so `H_C P_0 = 0`, `e^{-iaH_C}P_0 = P_0` and, taking adjoints, `P_0 e^{-iaH_C} = P_0`. `W_B, W_E` and `A` commute with `P_0`. Hence `U_B P_0 = 𝕍P_0` and `P_0 U_B = 𝕍P_0`, and `U_B^*AU_B P_0 = U_B^* P_0 A 𝕍 = (P_0U_B)^* A 𝕍 = P_0 𝕍^* A 𝕍`. So `(U_B^*AU_B - 𝕍^*A𝕍)P_0 = 0`, and the two GNS vectors agree. ∎

D1 states with empty matter exist for every member: empty matter tensored with the tracial link state is stationary (`𝕍` is unitary), gauge invariant and cell invariant.

### 6.2 The tiling-cube class does not propagate

**Theorem F.** Let every gate of `W_B` act on the links of a single tiling cube (for example `W^{cube}`, any `θ_e, θ_o, τ_E`). Let `ω` be an empty-matter D1 state of `L5-U[m]`. Then every band with image in `H_phys` is identically `1` on every connected `C^1` neighbourhood of a node. No band in `H_phys` propagates.

*Proof.* By G1(a) each link lies in exactly one tiling cube, so `𝕍 = ∏_C 𝕍_C`, a product of commuting unitaries on the disjoint link sets of the cubes (`𝕍_C` collects the gates of `C` in sublayer order and the electric gates of its links). For a finite set `𝔉` of tiling cubes, the gauge-invariant algebra `𝒜^G_𝔉` of their links is finite dimensional and invariant under `A ↦ 𝕍^*A𝕍`. By Theorem R, `π(𝒜^G_𝔉)Ω` is a finite-dimensional `Ũ`-invariant subspace of `H_phys`, so it has an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors with eigenvalues in a finite set `Λ_𝔉`. These subspaces increase with `𝔉`, there are countably many, and their union is dense in `H_phys` (Theorem R(a)). So `Ũ|_{H_phys}` has pure point spectrum in the countable set `Λ = ∪Λ_𝔉`. The image of a band is `Ũ`-invariant and lies in `H_phys`, so `M_V` has spectral measure supported in `Λ`; the spectral projection of `M_V` for `Λ^c` is multiplication by `1_{Λ^c}(V(K))`, so the eigenvalues of `V(K)` lie in `Λ` for almost every `K`. Now follow `LZ1.md` Theorem 2.2: near a node, `Re` and `Im` of `tr V(K)^r` are `C^1` and take countably many values almost everywhere; a `C^1` function with a nonzero derivative is strictly monotone along the segments of a small box, which by Fubini gives uncountably many values on a set of positive measure; so these functions are constant on connected open sets. Newton's identities make the characteristic polynomial constant. At the node all eigenvalues are 1, and a unitary with all eigenvalues 1 is the identity. ∎

### 6.3 A propagating band in the physical sector (counterexample)

**Construction (`L5-U[m]`, `m ∈ {2,4,6,8}`).** For a site `v` let `a_e(v)` be the opposite corner of its even cube and `a_o(v)` that of its odd cube. Both are fixed-point-free involutions of `Z^3` commuting with `𝒢` (G2(b)). The point reflection `P_C` through the centre of a tiling cube `C ∋ v` maps `star(v)` onto `star(a(v))`. It induces the colour-preserving mode bijection `(ℓ, v-end, k) ↦ (P_Cℓ, a(v)-end, k)` and hence a unitary `ι_v : Star^{link,σ}_v → Star^{link,P_Cσ}_{a(v)}` intertwining `U(5)_v` with `U(5)_{a(v)}`. Here `Star^{link,σ}_v = ⊗_{ℓ∋v} Λ^{p_{ℓ,v}}C^5`, the six link ends only. So `ι_v` maps the `λ`-isotypic summand `V_λ ⊗ M_λ` at `v` to the `λ`-summand at `a(v)`.

The **swap gate** `S_{\{v,a(v)\}}` acts on the link-end modes at `v` and `a(v)` only. On a sector pair with `σ_{a(v)} = P_Cσ_v`, on the summand `(V_λ ⊗ M_λ) ⊗ (V_μ ⊗ M_μ)` it is `1 ⊗ 1 ⊗ SWAP_ι` if `λ = μ` (exchange of the two multiplicity factors through `ι_v`) and the identity if `λ ≠ μ`. On every other sector pair it is the identity. The layer is `W^{swap} = S_o S_e`, where `S_e` is the product of the swap gates over the antipodal pairs of all even cubes and `S_o` over all odd cubes.

**Lemma S1.** `W^{swap}` belongs to GA1-P-5.

*Proof.* Each gate is supported on the twelve links at `v` and `a(v)`, and is unitary. It commutes with `U(5)_v` and `U(5)_{a(v)}` because it acts only on multiplicity factors, inside isotypic blocks, and is block diagonal over sectors; it commutes with every other site group because it acts on no other end. It preserves each end number, so it is even in the modes of each end, and gates of different pairs act on disjoint sets of modes: the gates of one sublayer commute. The pairing `v ↔ a_e(v)` commutes with `𝒢`. A point reflection commutes with every rotation about its centre and is carried to the reflection of the translated cube by translations, so the lift of every `g ∈ 𝒢` (a colour-preserving mode map) conjugates `ι_v` to `ι_{gv}` and the gate of `{v, a(v)}` to the gate of `{gv, a(gv)}`. ∎

**Lemma S2.** Let `σ^*` be the sector with `p_{ℓ,z} = m/2` at every end. Then `𝒮^{σ^*}_v = End_{U(5)}(Star^{link,σ^*}_v)` is noncommutative, so some `U(5)`-type `λ_0` has `dim M_{λ_0}[σ^*_v] ≥ 2`.

*Proof.* For ends `a ≠ b` at `v` with equal numbers, the colour-preserving exchange `Π_{ab}` of their modes commutes with `U(5)_v` and preserves `σ^*`, so `Π_{ab} ∈ 𝒮^{σ^*}_v`. Let `p = m/2 ∈ {1,2,3,4}`; there are at least three distinct `p`-subsets `A, B, C` of the five colours. On the basis vector with contents `(A, B, C)` at ends `(a, b, c)`, `Π_{ab}Π_{bc}` gives contents `(C, A, B)` and `Π_{bc}Π_{ab}` gives `(B, C, A)`, up to signs. These are different basis vectors, so the two products differ. A finite-dimensional `⊕_λ End(M_λ)` is commutative only if every `dim M_λ ≤ 1`. ∎

**Theorem S (counterexample to "no finite-register layer propagates").** For `L5-U[m]`, `m ∈ {2,4,6,8}`, all `ε, μ` in the window and all `τ_E`, with plaquette layer `W^{swap}`: there is a D1 state `ω` and a band `(1, F, V)` with image in `H_phys ∩ H_even` and fibre `V(K) = exp(-iK·(1,1,1))`. Every `K` with `K·(1,1,1) ∈ 2πZ` is a node, and `N_j = ∂_j(K·(1,1,1)) = 1` for each `j`. The band propagates.

*Proof.* *State.* Let `ω` be: empty matter; links in the sector `σ^*`; on each link-end star `Star^{link,σ^*}_v` the density `ρ_v = (1_{V_{λ_0}}/dim V_{λ_0}) ⊗ (1_{M}/dim M)` on the `λ_0`-summand. Since `H_{σ^*}` factorizes over stars (section 4), this is a product over sites of finite-dimensional states, hence a state of the quasi-local algebra; each link's marginal is the product of its two end marginals. It is invariant under each `U(5)_v` (it is `1` on `V_{λ_0}`), so it is locally gauge invariant and centre invariant. It is invariant under translations by `2Z^3` (uniform). *Stationarity.* By Theorem R(b)'s identity `U_BP_0 = 𝕍P_0 = P_0U_B`, it suffices that `𝕍ρ𝕍^* = ρ` on each finite region. `W_E` is a scalar on `σ^*`. Every swap gate preserves `σ^*` (point reflections map the uniform sector to itself) and, on the support of `ρ`, exchanges two `λ_0`-multiplicity factors, each carrying `1/dim M`; so it fixes `ρ`. *Vectors.* Pick `a ∈ End(M_{λ_0})` with `tr a = 0`, `tr a^*a = dim M`. Let `A_v ∈ 𝒮^{σ^*}_v` be `1_V ⊗ a` on the `λ_0`-summand of `σ^*` at `v`, and `0` on other summands and sectors. It is local, link-only and gauge invariant. Then `ω(A_v) = 0`, and `ω(A_v^*A_w) = δ_{vw}` for the normalized choice, since `ω` is a product over stars. *Dynamics.* On the support of `ρ` (every star in `λ_0`, sector `σ^*`), a swap gate on `{v, a(v)}` conjugates `A_v` to `A_{a(v)}` and fixes `A_w` for `w` outside the pair; `W_E` commutes with `A_v`. With `𝕍 = S_oS_eW_E`, Theorem R(b) gives `Ũπ(A_v)Ω = π(𝕍^*A_v𝕍)Ω = π(A_{a_e(a_o(v))})Ω`. At the origin, `a_o(0) = (-1,-1,-1)` (odd cube `{-1,0}^3`) and `a_e(-1,-1,-1) = (-2,-2,-2)` (even cube `{-2,-1}^3`). Translation covariance gives `a_e(a_o(2b)) = 2b - 2(1,1,1)` for every `b`. *Band.* Put `u_b = π(A_{2b})Ω`, an orthonormal family, and `F(φ) = Σ_b \hat φ_b u_b` for `φ(K) = Σ_b \hat φ_b e^{iK·b}`. `F` is an isometry from `L^2(T^3)` (normalized Haar measure). `T_βF(φ) = Σ_b \hat φ_b u_{b+β} = F(e^{iK·β}φ)`, and `ŨF(φ) = Σ_b \hat φ_b u_{b-(1,1,1)} = F(e^{-iK·(1,1,1)}φ)`. So `(1, F, e^{-iK·(1,1,1)})` is a band in `H_phys`; each `u_b` is an even vector. With `h = i log V = K·(1,1,1)` near a node, `N_j = 1`. ∎

**What propagates here.** Each `A_v` commutes with every end number: it lies in the neutral algebra `𝒩` of section 4 and carries no electric flux. The moving object is a colour-multiplicity state of the link ends, carried along the body diagonals by gauge-invariant swaps. So Theorem S decides the question as posed (a band with a node and a nonzero leading generator in the physical sector of a gauge-invariant stationary state) and shows that this criterion does not by itself pick out a gauge boson. The ballistic one-way fibre is not a Clifford cone; it says nothing about LZ1's common cone.

### 6.4 Flux: a symmetric Clifford automaton has no glider

The natural flux analogue of Theorem S is a Clifford circuit on "flux qubits", one per tiling cube. Credit: partitioned (Margolus-type) reversible automata and their gauge-invariant versions (Arrighi, Di Molfetta, Eon; abstracts not fetched) are the closest prior constructions.

**Lemma T.** Suppose each tiling cube `C` carries Hermitian involutions `X_C, Z_C` in the gauge-invariant algebra of its links, with `X_CZ_C = -Z_CX_C` and operators of different cubes commuting. Let `W = W_oW_e`, where `W_e = ∏_{C ∈ T_e} CX(N_e(C) → C)` applies `X_C` exactly when the parity of `∏_{C' ∈ N_e(C)} Z_{C'}` over a set `N_e(C)` of odd cubes is odd, and `W_o` is the same with the tilings exchanged. Let the control sets be covariant: `N_e(gC) = gN_e(C)` and `N_o(gC) = gN_o(C)` for `g ∈ 𝒢`. Then for no `n ≥ 1` and no nonzero `γ ∈ Z^3` does `W^n` map a nonzero finite product of the `X`'s and `Z`'s to `±` its translate by `2γ`.

*Proof.* A controlled flip by a parity of controls is the product of single-control CNOTs. CNOTs map `X`-products to `X`-products and `Z`-products to `Z`-products, so it suffices to treat each part. Index even cubes by `b ∈ Z^3` (centre `c_0 + 2b`) and odd cubes likewise, and write an `X`-product as a pair `(a_e, a_o)` over `R = GF(2)[z_1^{±1}, z_2^{±1}, z_3^{±1}]`. Conjugation by `W` acts as `M = [[1, \bar N_e], [\bar N_o, 1 + \bar N_o \bar N_e]]`, where `\bar N` is the Laurent polynomial of the control offsets; `det M = 1`. A glider gives a finite-support eigenvector of `M^n` with eigenvalue `z^γ`, so `z^{2γ} + t_n z^γ + 1 = 0` for `t_n = tr M^n`, that is, `t_n = z^γ + z^{-γ}` (characteristic 2). A rotation `Q` about `c_0` acts on `R` by `z^b ↦ z^{Qb}`; on odd indices it adds a fixed shift, which conjugates `M` by a diagonal monomial matrix and does not change traces. Covariance of the control sets gives `Q·t_n = t_n` for all `Q` in the rotation group `O`. Hence `{Qγ, -Qγ} = {γ, -γ}` for all `Q`. A 3-fold rotation `R` then fixes `γ` (`Rγ = -γ` would give `γ = R^3γ = -γ`), so `γ` lies on every body-diagonal axis, and `γ = 0`. The `Z`-part evolves by the inverse transpose and is treated the same way. ∎

### 6.5 A reading for "light gauge boson" and the open flux question

**Proposed reading R-Φ (not adopted).** A band in `H_phys` is *flux-carrying* if its image is orthogonal to `closure(π(𝒩)Ω)`, where `𝒩` is the algebra of gauge-invariant local operators that commute with every end number (section 4). Four tests: (1) it states no part's target and fixes no answer, but it does sharpen what GA1's phrase "a light gauge boson" asks for, so test 1 is conditional; (2) closed: pass; (3) it settles nothing: Theorems F and S and Lemma T leave the flux question open: pass; (4) it adds no structure or constant: `𝒩` is defined by the law's own numbers: pass. The owner may specify; nothing here depends on adopting it.

**GA1-5-Φ (open after three attacks).** *Some member of GA1-P-5 for `L5-U[m]` and some D1 state have a propagating flux-carrying band in `H_phys`.*
- *Attack 1, tiling-cube gates.* Fails: in every empty-matter D1 state no band in `H_phys` propagates (Theorem F).
- *Attack 2, disjoint covariant sublayers with mixed corners.* Fails: such sublayers never move flux between an even and an odd link (Theorem D), so flux must cross between the tilings through gates with overlapping supports that still commute, or through matter. The attack stops there: the plaquette terms that share a link do not commute (`LW1.md` GA1-P), and no commuting overlapping flux-moving family was found.
- *Attack 3, a Clifford flux automaton.* Fails for one logical qubit per tiling cube with covariant controls: no glider (Lemma T). More logical modes per cube, permuted by the cube's rotation group, escape Lemma T's trace argument; the attack stops at constructing such an equivariant family of anticommuting pairs inside the gauge-invariant cube algebra.

### 6.6 `L5-O[S_+]`

Lemma L, Theorem P, Theorem D's geometry and Theorem E carry over (sections 3 and 5). Theorems R, F and S use that the number-conserving hop kills empty matter. That fails on `L5-O`. **GA1-5-O (open after three attacks):** *some member of GA1-P-5 for `L5-O[S_+]` and some D1 state have a propagating band in `H_phys`.*
- *Attack 1, empty matter.* Fails: "The empty matter state alone need not be fixed by a dressed Majorana hop: it can create a pair" (`LW1_5.md` B8b-5), so Theorem R has no analogue and the matter gates do not drop out.
- *Attack 2, the hop kernels.* The vectors with empty matter and every link in the joint kernel `K_ℓ` of the pair-creation operators are fixed by the hops (B8b-5). Fails at: `K_ℓ` is defined through a complex structure on the rishon Majoranas that `Spin(10)` does not preserve, so it is not a union of `Spin(10)` multiplicity blocks, and the swaps of section 6.3 need not preserve it.
- *Attack 3, the tracial state.* It is stationary for every tick and gauge invariant. Fails at: there the Majorana hops act on the link ends and do not commute with the multiplicity swaps, so the operator `A_v` of Theorem S does not move to a single translate.
