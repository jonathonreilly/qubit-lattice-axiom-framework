## 1. Definitions inferred by the distiller

1. **Operator link.** For a nonzero Hilbert space H, write \(\mathbf U=\sum_{ab}e_{ab}\otimes U^{ab}\in B(\mathbb C^N\otimes H)\). Invertible means boundedly invertible there. Left and right infinitesimal covariance mean respectively
\[ [1\otimes L,\mathbf U]=(\tau\otimes1)\mathbf U,\qquad [1\otimes L,\mathbf U]=-\mathbf U(\tau\otimes1), \]
with L bounded self-adjoint and τ Hermitian. The signs follow the two end conventions.
2. **L5-U register.** On each link use ten CAR modes, five at each end, in \(\Lambda^m\mathbb C^{10}\), \(1\le m\le9\). Different links are ordinary tensor factors. Put \(U^{jk}=r^y_jr^{x\dagger}_k\), \(N^v=\sum_jr^{v\dagger}_jr^v_j\), and \(E^v_{ab}=r^{v\dagger}_ar^v_b\). A loop W is the trace of the ordered product of these matrices, using the adjoint on a reversed link. Matrix indices are contracted along the loop. Matter is suppressed.
3. **Defect and coherence.** For a state ω, \(D_\omega(Y)=\omega((Y-1)^\dagger(Y-1)+(Y-1)(Y-1)^\dagger)\). For L5-U set \(M=\sum_{jk}(U^{jk\dagger}U^{jk}+U^{jk}U^{jk\dagger})\), \(\kappa=2\sum|\omega(U^{jk})|^2/\omega(M)\). For Hermitian L5-O links use \(\kappa=\sum\omega(U^{ab})^2/\sum\omega((U^{ab})^2)\). These are state-dependent. κ is not gauge invariant. \(D_\omega(Y)\) is unchanged by every gauge unitary that commutes with Y.
4. **SC, proposed comparison.** Fix a state ω, a scale \(r=L^ka\), a finite list Λ of observables and adjoints, an identification ι with commuting Wilson functions, multiplicative on the listed products, both orders of a product going to the same commuting product, with \(ι(O^\dagger)\) the complex conjugate of \(ι(O)\) (referee closure, GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md), and δ > 0. SC(r;δ) means that a measure µ in the specified proposed T0-start class at coupling \(g\le g_*\) matches the expectation of each listed observable and each product of at most two of them, including both orders, within δ. Constants, normalizations, state, map and list must be fixed before the comparison. SC is a question, not an adopted premise. It is a necessary component of a suitable stronger comparison, not a sufficient definition of GA2.
5. **SC-loop.** For a fixed family \(\mathcal W_r\), SC-loop(r;η) means that one nonzero complex c satisfies \(D_\omega(cW)\le\eta\) for every \(W\in\mathcal W_r\). The same c is used throughout. It is only a diagnostic.
6. **Boundary cube and proposed readings.** A block cube has edge length \(n=L^k\) fine links. Its six outward-oriented face observables are traces around the fine boundary paths. For an order σ, \(\Theta_c^\sigma\) is their product, σ(1) acting first. F chooses one fixed order; S uses \(\Theta_c^S=720^{-1}\sum_\sigma\Theta_c^\sigma\); B includes all 720 orders. No reading is adopted. A product of loops is U(1)-blind if every fine link is traversed equally in both directions. The corresponding commuting Wilson function is a product of normalized loop traces.
7. **Cube skeleton.** At each skeleton link, the particle end is the head of the earlier face's step; the other is the hole end. At a skeleton point v, \(X^e=E^e\) at a particle end and \(X^e=1-E^e\) at a hole end. Block corners have three ends; inner points of block edges have two. The resulting point operators are traces of products of the X matrices.
8. **Thresholds.** \(\epsilon_\mu\) bounds \(\mathbb E_\mu(1-\Re w_p)\); \(\epsilon_\Theta\) bounds \(\mathbb E_\mu(1-\Re\Theta)\). Neither bound is assumed to hold in the hard-core class without proof. The Y2-W-L threshold is \(\eta_*=8\epsilon_\Theta+6\delta\), when the cube observable and its normalization are actually included in SC. This corrects `Y2W_DRAFT.md` line 159, which used \(8\epsilon_\mu(g_*) + 6\delta\) for the cube family. Theorem 3.1 needs the small-field bound of the listed observable's own image. By `GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md` R1, \(\epsilon_\Theta\le36\epsilon_\mu\) (referee closure, GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md).

## 2. Spectral obstruction

**Theorem 2.1 (N).** If the operator link in Definition 1 is invertible and obeys either covariance equation, then \(\tau=0\).

*Proof.* In the left case \((1\otimes L-\tau\otimes1)\mathbf U=\mathbf U(1\otimes L)\). These bounded operators are similar. Diagonalize τ, with eigenvalues λa. The first spectrum is the union of \(\operatorname{spec}L-\lambda_a\); the second is \(\operatorname{spec}L\). Comparing their maxima and minima gives \(\min_a\lambda_a=\max_a\lambda_a=0\). In the right case the same two operators are similar in the reverse direction. □

**Corollary 2.2 (N1–N3, N3′).** A finite register with a nontrivial infinitesimal link representation has no invertible covariant link. In particular \([E,U]=qU\), E Hermitian, q ≠ 0, has no invertible finite-dimensional solution. For each c > 0, \(\|\mathbf U^\dagger\mathbf U/c-1\|\ge1\). For compact Lie G this applies whenever its continuous finite-dimensional image ρ(G) is infinite.

*Proof.* Choose a nonzero generator τ and use Theorem 2.1. A singular finite-dimensional matrix has a zero singular value, giving the norm bound. For compact Lie G and continuous unitary ρ with infinite image, suppose \(d\rho=0\). Then \(\rho(\exp X)=\exp(d\rho(X))=1\) for every \(X\in\mathfrak g\). Since \(\exp(\mathfrak g)\) generates \(G_0\), ρ is trivial on \(G_0\); as \(G/G_0\) is finite, its image would be finite, a contradiction. Thus some X has nonzero Hermitian \(\tau=-i\,d\rho(X)\). For the continuous unitary representation V on the finite register, write \(V(e^{tX})=e^{itL}\) with L Hermitian. Differentiating \((1\otimes V(e^{tX}))\mathbf U(1\otimes V(e^{tX}))^*=(e^{it\tau}\otimes1)\mathbf U\) at zero gives \([1\otimes L,\mathbf U]=(\tau\otimes1)\mathbf U\). Theorem 2.1 applies (referee closure, GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md). □

For LW1's spin S link, \(J|S\rangle=0\) and \([E,J]=J\). Its rank is 2S. The normalized shift approaches the bilateral shift on each fixed finite-flux subspace as S grows; under the embeddings \(\iota_S:|m\rangle\mapsto e_m\) into \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\) or \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z+\tfrac12)\), with bilateral shift \(Be_m=e_{m+1}\), \(\|\iota_S(J_S/c)\iota_S^*-B\|\ge\|(\iota_S(J_S/c)\iota_S^*-B)e_S\|=1\) for every \(c>0\), since \(J_S|S\rangle=0\); so no rescaling of \(J_S\) converges in operator norm to B (referee closure, GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md).

**Theorem 2.3 (N4).** If \(K=\rho(G)\) is finite, \(L^2(K)\) carries an entrywise-commuting unitary link with covariance at both ends.

*Proof.* Let \(U^{ab}\) multiply by \(k_{ab}\), and let \(V(g,h)f(k)=f(\rho(g)^{-1}k\rho(h))\). Pointwise unitarity and commuting multiplication are immediate. Conjugation gives the two end actions, with inverses according to convention. □

**Corollary 2.4 (N5, scoped).** In any representation π of a law whose site Gauss generator \(\mathcal G_x^T\) is a bounded local algebra element, no bounded operator matrix with nonzero τ and exact covariance under \(\pi(\mathcal G_x^T)\) is invertible.

*Proof.* C*-representations are contractive, so \(\pi(\mathcal G_x^T)\) is bounded. Apply Theorem 2.1. □

**GAP N5/block.** The draft identifies every coarse endpoint transformation with one fine-site generator. A block transformation may instead be generated by a sum over its sites, or may be an emergent action. Corollary 2.4 holds for every bounded coarse generator, including a finite sum over a block (`GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md` R3(a)). It fails for an unbounded summed-flux generator (R3(b)).

**Corollary Y2-0, scoped** (referee closure, GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md). Let π represent the law's quasi-local algebra on \(K\ne0\), x be a site, \(\mathfrak h\subseteq\mathfrak g\) a subalgebra, and \(T\mapsto\tau^T\) a representation of \(\mathfrak h\) by Hermitian \(n\times n\) matrices, not identically zero.

**Theorem 2.5 (rishon identities and ranks).** For Majoranas \(\{\eta_i,\eta_j\}=2\delta_{ij}\), \(U^{ab}=i\eta^y_a\eta^x_b\) gives \((\mathbf U^\dagger\mathbf U)^{ab}=N\eta^x_a\eta^x_b\). On the full spinor R, \(\operatorname{rank}\mathbf U\le\dim R\); on either half-spinor it is at most its dimension. For complex rishons, \((\mathbf U^\dagger\mathbf U)^{kl}=r^x_kN^yr^{x\dagger}_l\) and on sector m, \(\operatorname{rank}\mathbf U\le\binom{2N}{m+1}\).

*Proof.* The Majorana products satisfy \(U^{ca}U^{cb}=\eta^x_a\eta^x_b\). Factor \(\mathbf U=iAB\), where \(B(e_b\otimes v)=\eta^x_bv\) and \(Av=\sum_ae_a\otimes\eta^y_av\). On a half-spinor the intermediate space is the other half-spinor. For complex rishons factor through the m+1 sector by first creating at x and then annihilating at y. The product formula follows from CAR. □

For N=10 the half-spinor has dimension 512, so the 5120-dimensional link matrix has rank at most 512. The stated Majorana construction is a special representation; the rank bound is not asserted for every representation of so(20).

## 3. Small-coupling diagnostics

**Theorem 3.1 (Lemma 0 and 0′).** Let a listed \(\widehat W=cW\) have commuting image w with \(|w|\le1\) and \(\mathbb E(1-\Re w)\le\epsilon\). Under SC with that measure,
\[D_\omega(\widehat W)\le 8\epsilon+6\delta.\]
This applies to a normalized plaquette or listed cube product.

*Proof.* Expand D. The two quadratic and two linear expectations differ from their commuting images by at most \(2\delta+4\delta\). The image is \(2\mathbb E|w-1|^2\), and \(|w-1|^2\le 4(1-\Re w)\). □

**Theorem 3.2 (F, W1).** Let E be self-adjoint with spectrum in K consecutive integers and \([E,Y]=Y\). For every state and complex z,
\[\omega((Y-z)^\dagger(Y-z)+(Y-z)(Y-z)^\dagger)\ge 2|z|^2/K^2.\]
For L5-U let \(K_m=\min(m,10-m)+1\). A loop using a link exactly once obeys \(D_\omega(cW)\ge2/K_m^2\). If \(\Phi=\sum_{\ell\in S}\sigma_\ell N^x_\ell\), \(\sigma_\ell=\pm1\), and \([\Phi,O]=O\), then \(D_\omega(cO)\ge2/[|S|(K_m-1)+1]^2\).

*Proof.* It suffices to use a unit vector ψ. Let \(p_j=\|P_j\psi\|^2\), zero outside the K levels, and write \(B_j=P_{j+1}YP_j\). The contribution of each adjacent pair to the defect is at least \(|z|^2(p_{j+1}-p_j)^2/(p_j+p_{j+1})\), by projection and minimization over their common matrix element; the same formula holds if one p vanishes. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the sum at least half the squared total variation. The total variation is at least \(2\max p_j\ge2/K\). Mixed states follow by affinity. A link's \(N^x\) has K_m levels and U raises it by one; reverse the sign of E for a reverse traversal. The sum Φ has the stated spectral interval. □

**Corollary 3.3 (W1′).** If \(cW_p\) matches a normalized U(5) plaquette with small-field parameter εµ as in Theorem 3.1, then \(2/K_m^2\le8\epsilon_\mu+6\delta\). The floor is 1/18 at m=5 and 1/2 at m=1,9.

*Proof.* Combine Theorems 3.1 and 3.2. □

This constrains a U(1)-charged fine plaquette, not the SU(5) coupling alone. A cube product is neutral on every link, so the flux proof cannot exclude an SU(5) window. The surface bound decreases with its number of links; it is not itself an upper bound on the actual defect.

**Theorem 3.4 (W2).** On L5-U, \((U^{jk})^2=0\), \(M=5m-2N^xN^y\ge m(10-m)/2>0\), and \(\kappa\le1/2\). At m=5 the Slater state of orbitals \((r_j^{y\dagger}+\sum_kV_{kj}r_k^{x\dagger})/\sqrt2\), V unitary, has singular values 1/2 for \(\omega(\mathbf U)\) and \(\kappa=1/6\). On the L5-O half-spinor, \((U^{ab})^2=1\) and \(\kappa\le1/10\), attained by a joint eigenstate of the ten commuting \(U^{aa}\), with zero end-generator expectations.

*Proof.* CAR gives nilpotence and \(U^{jk\dagger}U^{jk}=n^y_j(1-n^x_k)\), \(U^{jk}U^{jk\dagger}=n^x_k(1-n^y_j)\). Summing yields M and the lower bound. For any square-zero X, projection onto ran X and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(|\omega(X)|^2\le\omega(X^\dagger X+XX^\dagger)/4\); mixed states follow by convexity. Sum over entries. In the Slater state rotate the x-modes by V, \(s_j^\dagger=\sum_k V_{kj}r_k^{x\dagger}\). These are CAR modes and \(N^x=\sum_j s_j^\dagger s_j\). Put \(b_j^\dagger=(r_j^{y\dagger}+s_j^\dagger)/\sqrt2\); the state is \(\prod_j b_j^\dagger|0\rangle\). Expanding it gives one occupation vector for each subset of the five pairs whose fermion is at s, each with amplitude \(\pm2^{-5/2}\). Thus \(N^x\) is binomial with parameters \(5,1/2\), and \(N^y=5-N^x\), so \(\omega(N^xN^y)=25/2-(5/4+25/4)=5\), yielding \(\omega(M)=15\). For \(j\ne l\), \(r_j^y s_l^\dagger\) moves a fermion between pairs and has zero expectation; for \(j=l\), a two-mode calculation gives \(\langle0|b_jr_j^ys_j^\dagger b_j^\dagger|0\rangle=-1/2\). Hence \(\omega(U^{jk})=-\overline{V_{kj}}/2\), so \(\omega(\mathbf U)=-V^\dagger/2\), with five singular values 1/2, and κ=1/6 (referee closure, GA2_Y2_REFEREE.md). For Majoranas, each fixed row consists of ten anticommuting Hermitian involutions, so the squared expectations in that row sum to at most 1; ten rows give κ≤1/10. The ten diagonal entries commute. A joint eigenvector lies in a definite chirality sector, with signs constrained by chirality. Row saturation makes all off-diagonal expectations zero. Each nontrivial end bilinear anticommutes with a suitable diagonal entry, so its expectation vanishes. □

**Theorem 3.5 (W4).** For independent copies in \(\phi^{\otimes k}\), averaging each link entry preserves its mean, divides its centered second moments and commutators by k, and changes coherence to \(k\kappa_1/[1+(k-1)\kappa_1]\). Thus the states of Theorem 3.4 give \(k/(k+5)\) and \(k/(k+9)\).

## 4. Cube products

**Theorem 4.1 (B0, C, K, T).** Every U(1)-blind product commutes with each \(N^x_\ell\). On one link,
\[
(U^{j'k'})^\dagger U^{jk}=E^y_{j'j}(\delta_{kk'}-E^x_{kk'}),\quad
U^{jk}(U^{j'k'})^\dagger=E^x_{kk'}(\delta_{jj'}-E^y_{j'j}).
\]
Each ordered cube product factors into commuting point operators \(O_v\), the trace of the X matrices at v in the induced cyclic order. Each corner has one or two particle ends; four corners have each number. Each inner point has one particle end.

*Proof.* U raises \(N^x\), its adjoint lowers it, and distinct links commute. The two displayed identities follow by CAR. Expand all six traces, group the two entries on each link, and sum indices point by point. End bilinears at different points commute. At a corner the three comparisons of face order form a cycle, so one or two hold. Each block edge gives one particle end to its two corners; 12 edges and eight corners imply four of each type. Along an edge the same face is earlier on each fine link, giving one particle end at each inner point. □

**Theorem 4.2 (S, P, R).** A colour occupied at all ends of a point, or empty at all ends, drops out of its point operator. Particle–hole conjugation maps sector m to 10−m and maps \(\Theta^\sigma\) to \((\Theta^{\sigma^{rev}})^\dagger\). With one rishon on each of three ends, the corner operators are \(1-S_{ij}-S_{ik}+Z\) for one particle end and \(S_{ij}-Z\) for two, where S exchanges colours and Z is a 3-cycle. They vanish on symmetric states, equal 4 and −2 on antisymmetric states, and the two-particle operators are idempotent on the remaining S3 sector. At an inner point the operator is \(1-S\), hence 2 on antisymmetric states.

*Proof.* Expand each point trace over subsets of hole ends. For a universally occupied spectator, a nonzero term containing its colour has all indices equal to that colour; its constants cancel over the nonempty set of hole ends. An empty spectator contributes nothing. The product of the ten Majorana operators \(r+r^\dagger\) on each link sends \(r\) to \(-r^\dagger\), so m goes to 10−m; on simple even-length boundary loops it sends W to \(W^\dagger\), and reverses the order after taking the adjoint. On a one-rishon end \(E_{ab}=|a\rangle\langle b|\); expanding traces gives the permutation formulas. On the trivial and sign representations S=±1 and Z=1. On their orthogonal complement \(1+Z+Z^2=0\) and \(S_{12}+S_{13}+S_{23}=0\); \(SZ\) and \(ZS\) are the two transpositions other than S, so \(SZ+ZS=-S\) on that sector; these identities give \((S-Z)^2=S-Z\). □

\[D_\omega(c\Theta^\sigma)+D_\omega(c'\Theta^{\sigma^{rev}})\ge2.\]

*Proof.* For m≤5 put all m rishons on each link at its σ-particle end, in the same m colours. Each point operator and its adjoint has eigenvalue m. An order with different particle ends has an empty particle end and annihilates the state, as does its adjoint. This proves the F and S statements; use particle–hole conjugation for m≥6.

For 2≤m≤6 assign to each skeleton point v a spectator count \(s_v\in\{0,1,2\}\) with \(s_v+s_w=m-2\) across each link: use a constant for even m and alternating counts for odd m. At each corner antisymmetrize one active rishon across its three ends, with \(s_v\) common spectator colours; at each inner point antisymmetrize across its two ends. Each link then contains m rishons. Theorem 4.2 gives 4 or −2 at each corner and 2 at each inner point, for every order and adjoint. Four corners of each type give 4096. Use particle–hole conjugation for m=7,8.

For m=1, let Pσ project onto configurations with each rishon at the σ-particle end. Both \(\Theta^\sigma\) and its adjoint are supported there. Thus \(D(c\Theta^\sigma)\ge2(1-\omega(P_\sigma))\). Reversal exchanges particle and hole ends, so \(P_\sigma P_{\sigma^{rev}}=0\). Sum the bounds. Conjugate to m=9. □

**Theorem 4.4 (S∞).** At any fixed block scale and m, there is a state with SC-loop(r;0) for all symmetrized boundary block cubes at once. For m≤5 put all m rishons of each fine link on a block edge toward the even block endpoint on e1 edges and toward the odd block endpoint on e2 and e3 edges, in the same m colours. \[\|(1-e_-^O)\psi\|^2\le18\sum_{i=1}^2\|(c\Theta_i-1)\psi\|^2.\]

Each of the twelve exchanges of ends on different axes belongs to an octant and equals −1 on its antisymmetric subspace. If ε is the largest \(\|(1+\tau)\psi\|\), then \(\epsilon^2\le72\max_O S_O\), where \(S_O\) is the two-term sum above. These exchanges generate every permutation of six ends in at most 15 steps. The six-colour antisymmetrizer vanishes because \(\Lambda^6\mathbb C^5=0\), so telescoping gives \(\|\psi\|\le15\epsilon\). Hence the 28-term sum of squared defects is at least \(\|\psi\|^2/16200\). Sum over orthogonal number sectors and use D≥the first squared defect. If all 28 D values were at most η, then \(28\eta\ge1/16200\). Particle–hole conjugation gives m=8. □

**Theorem 4.6 (fixed translated order).** If every unit cube uses the same order of its six face types, then for m=1 or 9 two cubes sharing an e1 link have mutually exclusive particle-end supports. Their defects sum to at least 2.

*Proof.* The four cubes meeting the link demand the four cyclic precedence relations \(-e_2<-e_3<+e_2<+e_3<-e_2\) for the same occupied end. They cannot all hold or all fail. Two cubes therefore demand opposite ends. The support argument of Theorem 4.3 applies. Use particle–hole conjugation for m=9. □

The flux states of Theorems 4.3–4.4 have definite end numbers and hence κ=0 on every skeleton link. For the S∞ state, an e1–e2 block plaquette of suitable orientation annihilates the state. If its normalized image is w and SC includes both \(\widehat W^\dagger\widehat W\) and \(\widehat W\widehat W^\dagger\), SC fails when \((1-\epsilon_\mu)^2>\delta\), assuming \(0\le\epsilon_\mu\le1\). Indeed \(\mathbb E|w|^2\ge(1-\epsilon_\mu)^2\), but the first expectation is zero. Particle–hole conjugation exchanges the two products. Thus zero SC-loop defect does not establish SC.

**Laws.** LW1's \(L_{cube}\) has spin-S U(1) links; its abelian GA2 clause is separate from non-abelian T0 and remains open. L5-U has U(5) rishon links, embedding u(10), with \(\dim R=\binom{10}{m}\le252\). L5-O has Spin(10) acting on states and SO(10) on its vector link, with so(20) embedding and a finite representation R; the proposed half-spinor has dimension 512. An exact SU(2) subgroup does not by itself decouple a pure SU(2) theory. These descriptions depend on the cited five-mode construction; no physical continuum identification follows.

**Y2-W and attacks.** Y2-W asks whether the VC1 state and some allowed constants produce a controlled small-coupling window for L5-U. Attack 1 fails because a cube product is U(1)-blind and raises no end-number grading. Attack 2 fails at “a small electric-to-magnetic angle makes the fine link classical”: Theorems 3.2 and 3.4 are independent of those constants. Attack 3 fails at “block states are aligned products”: Theorem 3.5 assumes that alignment, while the actual block map and state are open. These are failures of particular arguments, not proofs that a window is absent. The draft's further inference that an effective coupling must first decrease toward the infrared is unsupported without a defined coupling and flow.

**Y2-W-L and Y2-W-L0.** These ask for SC-loop on the specified U(1)-blind cube family, in the VC1 state, and its one-cube kinematic floor. Under F and S the one-cube floor is zero; under B it is zero for m=2,...,8 and at least 1 for m=1,9. Under S Theorem 4.4 gives zero defect on all cubes in an explicit state, but does not identify that state with VC1. Under B the all-cube floor holds for m=1,2,8,9 at the thresholds in Theorem 4.5. Conditional on SC listing all those operators with one common normalization and on the stated small-field bounds, Theorem 3.1 transfers those floors to a failure of that SC comparison. It does not establish failure of GA2 without proving those hypotheses are necessary for GA2.

**Y2-W-LB(m), m=3,...,7.** Is there a positive all-orders, all-cube floor at a common corner? Open. Three draft attacks stop respectively at extending the antisymmetric spectator states to all octants, assuming an empty or full end in every sector, and assuming general multi-rishon corner operators are permutations of three single-rishon colours. The first family cannot extend with one common active three-colour antisymmetrization to six ends; that excludes the family, not all states. Numerical corner searches in the draft are not a classification.

**Kernel and programme requests.** If a finite \(G\times G\) representation embeds equivariantly in \(L^2(G)\), its transfer can be extended by zero and has a finite-rank kernel in group variables. Peter–Weyl gives the embedding for a direct sum of distinct \(V_j\otimes V_j^*\). For integer S the LW1 U(1) spin register is the Fourier band \(|j|\le S\); half-integer S needs a different global representation or twist. **GAP:** a band-limited kernel is not automatically a positive probability density or outside the hard-core class. Its comparison with that class is open.

The proposed **ZM1-G** asks for a specified block map and controlled gauge-invariant correlator comparison to a reflection-positive group-valued measure in the relevant RG class, with quantified errors. The proposed **T0-start[G]** asks the YM programme to prove its scale-uniform CT1–CT4 bounds and T0 conclusions from every admissible small-coupling start in its inductive class, uniformly in the start, and identify the resulting theory up to scale. **T0-rate** additionally asks for the GA2 rate and running-coupling error. These are requests, not established branch results; the class and topology must be pinned before the claims are testable. T0 is stated for Wilson SU(2); SU(5), SO(10), matter, anisotropy and matching are separate extensions. Alternative requests **T0-QLR[G]** and **T0-D[G]** ask respectively for a controlled joint quantum-link/register limit and an extra-dimensional reduction with T0's conclusions. Neither is proved. The cited YM draft says existence is not proved; this note does not verify that branch's proofs.
