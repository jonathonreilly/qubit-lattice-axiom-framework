## 2. Form of the time step

**Theorem 2.1 (F1–F2).** A positive unitary is the identity; a self-adjoint unitary squares to one. On a finite-dimensional space, if \(U=\sum_k e^{-i\theta_k}P_k\), the Hermitian logarithms \(H\) satisfying \(e^{-iH}=U\) are block diagonal on the \(P_k\), with block spectra in \(\theta_k+2\pi\mathbb Z\). Each defines \(T_H=e^{-H}>0\) with \(T_H^i=U\). The tick alone does not select this logarithm or its imaginary-time correlators.

*Proof.* The spectral theorem puts the eigenvalues of a positive unitary at 1, and those of a self-adjoint unitary at ±1. Any \(H\) commutes with its exponential, so its eigenvalues in the \(k\)-block have the stated form; the converse follows by exponentiation. For \(U=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-i\theta})\), \(H_j=\operatorname{diag}(0,\theta+2\pi j)\), \(A=B=\sigma_x\) and \(\psi=e_1\), the imaginary-time correlator is \(e^{-t(\theta+2\pi j)}\), while integer-tick correlators depend only on \(U\). □

The positive operators in this theorem are not thereby local transfer matrices of a Euclidean measure. Self-adjointness is only a necessary condition for a literal self-adjoint unitary transfer; \(U^2=1\) is not enough for positivity. The draft's generic non-involution example takes empty matter, integer \(S\ge1\), no magnetic layer, and fluxes differing by 0 versus 1 on a link: their \(U^2\) eigenvalue ratio is \(e^{-2i\tau_E}\).

**Theorem 2.2 (F3 and F3′).** On the empty-matter subspace the matter layers are identity. For the cube-face layer with equal angles,
\[U=e^{-i\tau_BH_B^{cf}}e^{-i\tau_EH_E}e^{ia_oH_o}e^{-ia_eH_e},\qquad H_B^{cf}=\sum_C B_C,\]
with the corresponding electric and hopping terms. The same identity holds for a layer of mutually commuting plaquette terms. On a finite box, for skew-Hermitian \(A,B\),
\[\|e^Ae^B-e^{A+B}\|\le\tfrac12\|[A,B]\|,\quad \|(e^Ae^B)^n-e^{n(A+B)}\|\le\tfrac n2\|[A,B]\|.\]
Thus scaling two angles by \(\delta\) gives an \(O(t\delta)\) finite-box error at \(n=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\), plus the rounding error.

*Proof.* A dressed hop annihilates empty matter. Cube gates on distinct cubes have disjoint links, and cube matter gates in each tiling have disjoint sites; commuting exponentials combine. For the bound, Duhamel compares \(X(s)=e^{sA}e^{sB}\) and \(Y(s)=e^{s(A+B)}\). Its integrand contains \(e^{sA}Be^{-sA}-B\), of norm at most \(s\|[A,B]\|\). Integrate from 0 to 1; telescope powers using unitarity. □

**Theorem 2.3 (F4).** On a finite periodic spatial graph with \(V,L,P\) and \(N_t\) periodic times, let
\[w=\exp\!\left[\beta_s\sum_{t,p}\sigma_p(t)+\beta_t\sum_{t,\ell=(x,y)}\sigma_\ell(t)\tau_y(t)\sigma_\ell(t+1)\tau_x(t)\right],\quad\beta_t>0.\]
With \(G_x^0=\prod_{\ell\ni x}X_\ell\), \(P_0=\prod_x(1+G_x^0)/2\), \(\tanh h^*=e^{-2\beta_t}\), and \(A^2=2\sinh(2\beta_t)\),
\[\sum_{\sigma,\tau}w=2^{|V|N_t}\operatorname{Tr}(P_0T^{N_t}),\quad T=A^{|L|}e^{\frac{\beta_s}{2}\sum_p Z_p}e^{h^*\sum_\ell X_\ell}e^{\frac{\beta_s}{2}\sum_p Z_p}>0.\]
The symmetric real-angle tick and this transfer are evaluations of the same entire matrix function at real and imaginary angles, up to the displayed scalar and a similarity between symmetric and ordered real-time products.

*Proof.* Temporally gauge fix all but the last slice. The discarded temporal links give \(2^{|V|(N_t-1)}\); the last-slice gauge variables contribute \(\sum_sG_s=2^{|V|}P_0\). The one-link kernel is \(e^{\beta_t}1+e^{-\beta_t}X=Ae^{h^*X}\). Splitting the diagonal spatial factor equally on each side gives \(T\), by cyclicity and gauge commutation. \(T=D^{1/2}KD^{1/2}>0\). Substitution of imaginary angles proves the analytic identity. □

## 4. The invariant-algebra obstruction

**Theorem 4.1 (R).** Let a C*-algebra \(\mathcal C\) contain infinitely many nonzero orthogonal projections \(p_j\), let \(\Phi:\mathcal C\to\mathfrak A\) be an injective *-homomorphism, and let \(\mathfrak B\cong M_D\) be a unital subalgebra of \(\mathfrak A\). For \(0\le\delta<1/2\), at most \(D/(1-2\delta)\) images have distance at most \(\delta\) from \(\mathfrak B\). The same conclusion holds for orthogonal positive norm-one elements.

*Proof.* Matrix units identify \(\mathfrak A\) with \(M_D\otimes(\mathfrak B'\cap\mathfrak A)\). Apply any state on the second factor to obtain a unital positive map \(E:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak B\) fixing \(\mathfrak B\). If \(X=\Phi(p_j)\) is within \(\delta\) of \(\mathfrak B\), replace the approximant by its Hermitian part. Positivity gives \(\|X-E(X)\|\le2\delta\); hence \(Q_j=E(X)\ge0\) has \(\|Q_j\|\ge1-2\delta\). Orthogonality gives \(\sum_{j\in F}Q_j\le1\), so \(|F|(1-2\delta)\le\sum\operatorname{Tr}Q_j\le D\). Positive orthogonal norm-one elements have finite sums of norm one, and an injective *-homomorphism is isometric, so the same count applies. □

**Corollary 4.2 (R1).** For an infinite compact group \(G\), assume the source local link algebra contains \(B(L^2(G))\), with left and right gauge translations. No injective readout into a finite-register quasi-local law has the uniform exponential locality of Definition 1. This includes the stated group-valued U(1) and SU(2) targets, at every finite zoom rung.

*Proof.* Peter–Weyl gives infinitely many irreducible blocks in \(L^2(G)\). Their nonzero orthogonal projections commute with both endpoint translations, so they are invariant and supported on one link. At fixed source support radius choose \(R\) so the common locality error is below \(1/2\). The corresponding finite target region has a finite full matrix algebra. Theorem 4.1 gives a contradiction. □

This is distinct from the already proved bounded-generator/charged-link obstruction `GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 2.1 and scoped Corollary Y2-0. It excludes a whole invariant-algebra readout only under its specified local algebra and uniformity hypotheses. It does not exclude finitely many correlators, an approximate low-flux dictionary, a finite truncation, a different source algebra, or group-valued variables in a Euclidean measure. Even the C*-algebra generated by one irrational-angle U(1) electric unitary has infinitely many orthogonal positive norm-one functions, so Theorem 4.1 also covers that specified smaller source. The exact group-valued ladder first fails at U(1) on this reading, before dynamics. No such conclusion follows from mere finite registers without the named readout assumptions.

**Theorem 5.2 (U1-LS).** On a fixed finite box with \(N_C\) cubes, integer \(S\), and empty matter, normalize \(J\) by \(c_S=\sqrt{S(S+1)}\). Let \(U_Q\) use normalized quantum-link plaquettes and \(U_W\) the bilateral shifts on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\), with the same cube-face angles and electric term. Embed \(|m\rangle\mapsto|m\rangle\) by \(\iota\). If \(\psi\) is a unit vector with all link fluxes at most \(M\) in magnitude and \(M+(n+1)K+1\le S/4\), put \(\tau=|\tau_B|\), \(t_K=e^{16\tau}(16\tau)^{K+1}/(K+1)!\). Then
\[
\|\iota U_Q^n\psi-U_W^n\iota\psi\|\le nN_C\left[\frac{60\tau e^{16\tau}(M+(n+1)K)^2}{S(S+1)}+2t_K\right]+n^2N_Ct_K.
\]
For fixed box, \(n,M,\tau_B\), choose \(K=\lceil(\log S)^2\rceil\); the error is \(O((\log S)^4/S^2)\).

*Proof.* The normalized raising coefficient is \(\kappa_+(m)=\sqrt{1-m(m+1)/[S(S+1)]}\), with the analogous lowering coefficient. On \(|m|\le M'\), \(|\kappa_\pm-1|\le(M'+1)^2/[S(S+1)]\). A plaquette shifts each of four distinct links and its coefficient differs from one by at most five times this bound under the stated cutoff. A cube has twelve oriented terms, so its generator difference is bounded by 60 times the coefficient bound. Both cube-generator norms are at most 16. The order-\(K\) exponential tail is at most \(t_K\); each retained generator application shifts any flux by at most one. Telescoping powers and summing the series bounds one gate by \(60\tau e^{16\tau}(M'+K)^2/[S(S+1)]+2t_K\). Cubes have disjoint link supports, so a layer costs at most \(N_C\) times this bound and leaks at most \(N_Ct_K\) outside the expanded flux cutoff. Electric evolution commutes with the embedding. Telescope \(n\) ticks, including the accumulated leakage, to obtain the display. Stirling's bound makes \(t_K\) smaller than any fixed inverse power of \(S\). □

The cutoff, time and volume are essential. At the top flux, \(J|S\rangle=0\) but the bilateral shift moves \(|S\rangle\), so uniform operator-norm convergence fails; this defect is already in `GA2_Y2.md` Corollary 2.2. The digest records analogous, conditional energy-priced Peter–Weyl results in `docs/GAUGE_WILSON_PW_COMPRESSION_SHELL_AND_ENERGY_PATH_BOUNDS_BOUNDED_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-09-07.md` and `docs/GAUGE_WILSON_LOCAL_OBSERVABLE_FINITE_REGION_PW_APPROXIMATION_BOUNDED_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-09-07.md`, both unaudited. An infinite-lattice local-observable version needs a specified state comparison and finite light-cone reduction; the displayed theorem alone does not supply an infinite-volume state bridge.

The specific D-theory route cited in `LIT_QLM.md` reduces one Euclidean dimension: three spatial directions plus one time give three after reduction, not four. An additional direction is extra structure. This is a dimension count for that route, not a no-go theorem for all quantum-link limits.

## 6. SU(2) subgroup and frozen spectators

**Theorem 6.1 (S and SU2-C).** In the setup of Definition 8, a plaquette monomial with spectator index \(j\) at a corner annihilates any vector with that mode empty at both incident link ends, or full at both. Let \(H_E^{(2)}=\sum_\ell(N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,x}+k-m/2)^2\), and let \(H_B^{(2)}\) be the cube-face plaquette sum with only block indices. Then \(\alpha_2=\operatorname{Ad}(e^{-i\theta H_B^{(2)}}e^{-i\tau_EH_E^{(2)}})\) preserves \(\mathfrak B_2\) and its restricted \(SU(2)\)-invariant algebra. Its inclusion into the full restricted-invariant algebra is unital, injective, strictly local and cell covariant. For every \(\omega\in\mathcal S_J\), \(B\in\mathfrak B_2\), and integer \(t\ge0\),
\[\omega(\alpha^t(B))=\omega(\alpha_2^t(B)).\]
On the frozen summand, each link has \(m'\) block rishons and the electric term is \((N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,x}-m'/2)^2\): this is the specified Trotterized \(U(2)\) rishon quantum-link model with cube faces, not an established pure SU(2) Wilson theory.

*Proof.* A contracted corner index is annihilated on one incident link end and created on the other. One factor kills an empty-empty vector and the other a full-full vector. The block-only terms preserve block number and commute with the restricted Gauss action; cube supports are link-disjoint. The cell lifts preserve flavours and the block, by `LW1_5.md` B1-U. On each finite light cone, positivity confines any \(\omega\in\mathcal S_J\) to the frozen subspace. Matter hops vanish there, all spectator-index plaquette terms annihilate it, and the remaining terms preserve it. The end number is \(N^{\mathsf B}+k\). Thus each full gate and its block counterpart agree on that subspace, and the equality follows for the entire finite gate word. □

`LW1_5.md` Theorem U and Proposition T already establish the exact restricted subgroup and the block/doublet/singlet decomposition. They do not establish this state reduction or a pure SU(2) continuum limit. The inclusion above is into the subgroup invariants; it is not automatically an inclusion into the full \(U(5)\)-invariant algebra.
