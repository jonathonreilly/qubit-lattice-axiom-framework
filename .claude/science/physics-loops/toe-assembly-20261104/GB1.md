## 2. Form of the time step

**Theorem 2.1 (F1–F2).** A positive unitary is the identity; a self-adjoint unitary squares to one. On a finite-dimensional space, if \(U=\sum_k e^{-i\theta_k}P_k\), the Hermitian logarithms \(H\) satisfying \(e^{-iH}=U\) are block diagonal on the \(P_k\), with block spectra in \(\theta_k+2\pi\mathbb Z\). Each defines \(T_H=e^{-H}>0\) with \(T_H^i=U\). The tick alone does not select this logarithm or its imaginary-time correlators.

*Proof.* The spectral theorem puts the eigenvalues of a positive unitary at 1, and those of a self-adjoint unitary at ±1. Any \(H\) commutes with its exponential, so its eigenvalues in the \(k\)-block have the stated form; the converse follows by exponentiation. For \(U=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-i\theta})\), \(H_j=\operatorname{diag}(0,\theta+2\pi j)\), \(A=B=\sigma_x\) and \(\psi=e_1\), the imaginary-time correlator is \(e^{-t(\theta+2\pi j)}\), while integer-tick correlators depend only on \(U\). □

The positive operators in this theorem are not thereby local transfer matrices of a Euclidean measure. A unitary tick is a self-adjoint transfer only if \(U=U^*\), which forces \(U^2=1\); and \(U^2=1\) does not give positivity. The draft's generic non-involution example takes empty matter, integer \(S\ge1\), no magnetic layer, and fluxes differing by 0 versus 1 on a link: their \(U^2\) eigenvalue ratio is \(e^{-2i\tau_E}\).

**Theorem 2.2 (F3 and F3′).** On the empty-matter subspace the matter layers are identity. For the cube-face layer with equal angles,
\[U=e^{-i\tau_BH_B^{cf}}e^{-i\tau_EH_E}e^{ia_oH_o}e^{-ia_eH_e},\qquad H_B^{cf}=\sum_C B_C,\]
with the corresponding electric and hopping terms. The same identity holds for a layer of mutually commuting plaquette terms. On a finite box, for skew-Hermitian \(A,B\),
\[\|e^Ae^B-e^{A+B}\|\le\tfrac12\|[A,B]\|,\quad \|(e^Ae^B)^n-e^{n(A+B)}\|\le\tfrac n2\|[A,B]\|.\]
Thus scaling two angles by \(\delta\) gives an \(O(t\delta)\) finite-box error at \(n=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\), plus the rounding error.

*Proof.* A dressed hop annihilates empty matter. Cube gates on distinct cubes have disjoint links, and cube matter gates in each tiling have disjoint sites; commuting exponentials combine. For the bound, Duhamel compares \(X(s)=e^{sA}e^{sB}\) and \(Y(s)=e^{s(A+B)}\). Its integrand contains \(e^{sA}Be^{-sA}-B\), of norm at most \(s\|[A,B]\|\). Integrate from 0 to 1; telescope powers using unitarity. □

## 4. The invariant-algebra obstruction

**Theorem 4.1 (R).** Let a C*-algebra \(\mathcal C\) contain infinitely many nonzero orthogonal projections \(p_j\), let \(\Phi:\mathcal C\to\mathfrak A\) be an injective *-homomorphism, and let \(\mathfrak B\cong M_D\) be a unital subalgebra of \(\mathfrak A\). For \(0\le\delta<1/2\), at most \(D/(1-2\delta)\) images have distance at most \(\delta\) from \(\mathfrak B\). The same conclusion holds for orthogonal positive norm-one elements.

*Proof.* Matrix units identify \(\mathfrak A\) with \(M_D\otimes(\mathfrak B'\cap\mathfrak A)\). Apply any state on the second factor to obtain a unital positive map \(E:\mathfrak A\to\mathfrak B\) fixing \(\mathfrak B\). If \(X=\Phi(p_j)\) is within \(\delta\) of \(\mathfrak B\), replace the approximant by its Hermitian part. Positivity gives \(\|X-E(X)\|\le2\delta\); hence \(Q_j=E(X)\ge0\) has \(\|Q_j\|\ge1-2\delta\). Orthogonality gives \(\sum_{j\in F}Q_j\le1\), so \(|F|(1-2\delta)\le\sum\operatorname{Tr}Q_j\le D\).
