**The three targets of this lane.** (1) For R-SUM and for R-IA: does the lift, or a block image of it, lie in or compare with a hypercubic-invariant class of T0-start's kind; what is the anisotropy as a function of the law's data; is isotropy restored at long distance, at what rate. (2) The weakest anisotropic form of T0-start that this bridge can feed, and what the YM side must prove to accept it. (3) The first failing step for each reading.

**Answer in one paragraph (details in §§3-7).** The main result is Theorem D, the cube factorization. Every gate of the pure-gauge cube-face test tick acts inside one tiling cube, and the tiling cubes have disjoint link sets. So the tick, the R-SUM transfer \(e^{-a_0H}\) and the R-IA transfer \(T_{\rm loc}\) are tensor products over cubes. They commute with an extra gauge group, one \(SU(2)\) per cube corner. Each lift then has a unique cube-product ground state, and its volume-first limit (\(N=N_t\to\infty\)) is that product state. In it, gauge-invariant observables whose spatial supports are two or more lattice units apart have zero connected correlation at every Euclidean time separation. A single-cube observable keeps a strictly positive temporal autocorrelation at every time. So the spatial correlation length is zero and the temporal one is not, at every parameter of both readings. No rescaling of time restores isotropy, at any scale (Corollary D6). Any continuum limit of a family of these lifts, or of bounded-range block images of them, has a connected two-point function that vanishes on all separated tests once it is rotation invariant up to a time rescaling. So such a family cannot meet T0-start's conclusion (ii) or T0-rate, whatever class \(\mathcal A_{k_0}(g)\) is pinned to. Theorem A answers the open item GB1E-ς: the exact R-SUM lift is never exchange invariant. Its new step: an exchange conjugates the unit time translation into a unit spatial translation, and a cycle lemma turns invariance of a lift under a slice symmetry into commutation with its transfer. The weakest anisotropic T0-start this bridge can feed is therefore none, while the magnetic layer is the cube-face class (§6). A candidate full-plaquette layer, four commuting cube tilings, removes the factorization; it is four-tested in §8 and not adopted. Under it, isotropy restoration is an open lemma with three failed attacks (§5.4).

## 3. The two lifts restated, and the cube structure

### 3.1 The box, the tiling and the tick

Fix \(N\ge4\) even, \(\Lambda=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^3\), positively oriented spatial bonds \(\mathcal L\), \(\mathcal K=L^2(SU(2),dU)\) and \(\mathcal H=\mathcal K^{\otimes\mathcal L}\), as in `GB1E.md` §1. The **tiling cubes** \(\mathcal C\) are the unit cubes whose base corner \(b\) has \(b_1\equiv b_2\equiv b_3\pmod 2\). For \(C\in\mathcal C\) write \(\ell(C)\) for its twelve bonds and \(\mathcal K_C=\mathcal K^{\otimes\ell(C)}\).

`GB1QL.md` §1.3, line 70: "The **Kogut-Susskind tick** is `U_KS = W_B W_E` with `W_B = ∏_{C∈𝒞} g_C`, `g_C = e^{−iθ B_C}`, and `W_E = ∏_ℓ e^{−iτ_E E²_ℓ}`." Here \(B_C=\sum_{p\subset C}B_p\) over the six faces, \(B_p=\operatorname{tr}X_p+(\operatorname{tr}X_p)^*=2\operatorname{Tr}X_p\) for \(SU(2)\), with the gauge-covariant ordering \(X_p=U_{\ell_4}^*U_{\ell_3}^*U_{\ell_2}U_{\ell_1}\) (`GB1QL.md` line 66). `GB1.md` Theorem 2.2 (line 59): "A dressed hop annihilates empty matter", so on empty-matter states the full test tick is \(W_BW_E\).

**Lemma 3.1 (the tiling; rebuilt from `GB1E.md` line 133 and `GB1.md` line 33).** (a) Every bond lies in exactly one tiling cube, so \(\{\ell(C)\}_{C\in\mathcal C}\) partitions \(\mathcal L\), and \(|\mathcal C|=N^3/4\). (b) A spatial plaquette is a face of a tiling cube exactly when it is in \(CF\), and then of exactly one. (c) Every site is a corner of exactly two tiling cubes, one with all base coordinates even and one with all odd.

*Proof.* (a) A direction-1 bond from \(y\) to \(y+e_1\) lies in the cube with base \(b\) iff \(b_1=y_1\), \(b_2\in\{y_2,y_2-1\}\), \(b_3\in\{y_3,y_3-1\}\). The parity condition \(b_2\equiv b_3\equiv y_1\) picks exactly one \(b_2\) and one \(b_3\). Other directions follow by relabeling. Counting gives \(12|\mathcal C|=|\mathcal L|=3N^3\). (b) A \((1,2)\)-plaquette with base \(y\) is a face of the cube with base \(b\) iff \(b_1=y_1\), \(b_2=y_2\), \(b_3\in\{y_3,y_3-1\}\). Such a tiling cube exists iff \(y_1\equiv y_2\), the \(CF\) condition of `GB1E.md` §1, and then \(b_3\) is unique. (c) A cube with base \(b\) has \(x\) as a corner iff \(b=x-\varepsilon\), \(\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^3\). The condition \(x_i-\varepsilon_i\equiv c\) for all \(i\) has exactly one solution \(\varepsilon\) for each \(c\in\{0,1\}\). \(\square\)

So \(\mathcal H=\bigotimes_{C\in\mathcal C}\mathcal K_C\) after reordering factors; \(A=\sum_\ell E_\ell^2=\sum_CA_C\) with \(A_C=\sum_{\ell\in\ell(C)}E_\ell^2\); and \(H_B=\sum_{p\in CF}B_p=\sum_CB_C\), where \(B_C\) acts on \(\mathcal K_C\) only.

### 3.3 The cube-wise gauge group

Let \(\mathcal G=SU(2)^\Lambda\) act by \(R(h)f(U)=f(h\cdot U)\), \((h\cdot U)_{xy}=h_y^{-1}U_{xy}h_x\) (`GB1E.md` §1), and put \(P_0=\int_{\mathcal G}R(h)\,dh\). Let \(\mathcal G_\square=\prod_{C\in\mathcal C}SU(2)^{{\rm corners}(C)}\) act by \(R_\square(g)=\bigotimes_CR_C(g_C)\), where \(R_C(g_C)\) transforms each bond \((x,y)\in\ell(C)\) to \(g_{C,y}^{-1}U_{xy}g_{C,x}\). The map \(h\mapsto(h|_{{\rm corners}(C)})_C\) embeds \(\mathcal G\) in \(\mathcal G_\square\), and \(R(h)=R_\square(h|)\), because a bond transforms only through its endpoints, which are corners of its cube. **Lemma 4.2 (the \(SU(2)\) heat kernel is strictly positive).** For every \(t>0\), \(k_t(g)>0\) for all \(g\in SU(2)\); hence \(m_t:=\min k_t>0\).

*Proof.* By `GB1E.md` Theorem 7.1, at \(g\) with eigenvalues \(e^{\pm i\psi}\), \(k_t(\psi)=\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-tk(k+2)/4}(k+1)\sin((k+1)\psi)/\sin\psi\). Put \(m=k+1\), so \(k(k+2)=m^2-1\), and \(q=e^{-t/4}\in(0,1)\). With \(\vartheta(\psi)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}q^{m^2}e^{im\psi}\), one has \(-\vartheta'(\psi)=2\sum_{m\ge1}mq^{m^2}\sin m\psi\), so \(k_t(\psi)=e^{t/4}(-\vartheta'(\psi))/(2\sin\psi)\) on \((0,\pi)\).

*Step 1 (triple product, the part used).* Let \(P(z)=\prod_{n\ge1}(1+q^{2n-1}z)(1+q^{2n-1}z^{-1})\). The product converges uniformly on compact subsets of \(\mathbb C^*\), since \(\sum_nq^{2n-1}(|z|+|z|^{-1})<\infty\). So \(P\) is holomorphic on \(\mathbb C^*\) and has a Laurent expansion \(P(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}c_mz^m\). Shifting the index in each factor gives \(P(q^2z)=P(z)\,(1+q^{-1}z^{-1})/(1+qz)=P(z)/(qz)\). Comparing coefficients, \(c_mq^{2m}=c_{m+1}q^{-1}\), so \(c_{m+1}=q^{2m+1}c_m\) and \(c_m=q^{m^2}c_0\) for all \(m\in\mathbb Z\). Hence \(P(e^{i\psi})=c_0\vartheta(\psi)\). At \(\psi=0\), \(P(1)=\prod_n(1+q^{2n-1})^2>0\) and \(\vartheta(0)>0\), so \(c_0>0\). Since \(P(e^{i\psi})=\prod_n|1+q^{2n-1}e^{i\psi}|^2\) and \(q^{2n-1}<1\), \(\vartheta(\psi)>0\) for all real \(\psi\).

*Step 2.* Differentiate \(\log\vartheta=\log P(e^{i\psi})-\log c_0\) term by term (the series of derivatives converges uniformly): \(\vartheta'/\vartheta=-\sum_n2r_n\sin\psi/(1+2r_n\cos\psi+r_n^2)\), \(r_n=q^{2n-1}\). Therefore
\[
k_t(\psi)=e^{t/4}\,\vartheta(\psi)\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{r_n}{1+2r_n\cos\psi+r_n^2}\qquad(0<\psi<\pi).
\]
The right side is continuous on \([0,\pi]\) and strictly positive there (each denominator is at least \((1-r_n)^2>0\)). \(k_t\) is continuous (`GB1E.md` Theorem 2.2), so the identity extends to \([0,\pi]\), and \(k_t>0\) on all of \(SU(2)\). Compactness gives \(m_t>0\). \(\square\)

**Lemma 4.3 (positive cube kernels).** (a) \(T_{{\rm loc},C}\) has the kernel \(e^{(\beta/4)B_C(U)}\prod_{\ell\in\ell(C)}k_t(U_\ell U_\ell'^{-1})\,e^{(\beta/4)B_C(U')}\), which lies between \(e^{-12|\beta|}m_t^{12}\) and \(e^{12|\beta|}k_t(e)^{12}\). *Proof.* (a) The kernel of \(e^{-tA_C}\) is \(\prod_\ell k_t(U_\ell U_\ell'^{-1})\) (`GB1E.md` Theorem 2.2), and \(|B_C|\le24\) (six faces, \(|2\operatorname{Tr}X|\le4\)). Trace-norm convergence (Lemma 4.1(b)) implies \(L^2\) convergence of kernels, which keeps a.e. inequalities on a subsequence; then use Lemma 4.2 and \(\|V_C\|\le24|\theta|\). (c) Positivity and injectivity: \(e^{-tA_C}\) and \(e^{-a_0H_C}\) are positive and injective, and \(e^{(\beta/4)B_C}\) is invertible. **Lemma 4.4 (simple top eigenvalue for a positive kernel; after Perron, Frobenius and Jentzsch).** Let \((Y,\nu)\) be a probability space and \(X\ge0\) a compact self-adjoint operator on \(L^2(Y,\nu)\) with a real kernel \(\kappa\) and constants \(0<c\le\kappa\le c'\) a.e. Then \(\lambda_0=\|X\|\) is a simple eigenvalue, with an eigenvector \(\Omega>0\) a.e. If \(\sigma\) is a \(\nu\)-preserving bijection with \(\kappa(\sigma y,\sigma y')=\kappa(y,y')\) a.e., then \(\Omega\circ\sigma=\Omega\) a.e.

*Proof.* The kernel is real, so the top eigenspace is spanned by real vectors. Let \(\psi\) be a real unit top eigenvector. Since \(\kappa>0\), \(\langle|\psi|,X|\psi|\rangle-\langle\psi,X\psi\rangle=\iint\kappa\,(|\psi(y)||\psi(y')|-\psi(y)\psi(y'))\ge0\). The variational characterization of \(\lambda_0\) then makes \(|\psi|\) a top eigenvector too, and makes the integral zero. With \(\kappa>0\), this forces \(\psi(y)\psi(y')\ge0\) for a.e. pair, so \(\psi\) has a.e. constant sign. Also \(|\psi|=\lambda_0^{-1}X|\psi|\ge\lambda_0^{-1}c\int|\psi|>0\) a.e. Two orthogonal real top eigenvectors would both be a.e. nonzero with constant sign, so their inner product could not vanish. Hence the eigenvalue is simple, with \(\Omega=|\psi|\). For \(\sigma\): \(f\mapsto f\circ\sigma\) is unitary, commutes with \(X\) and keeps positivity, so \(\Omega\circ\sigma\) is a positive unit top eigenvector, equal to \(\Omega\). \(\square\)

**Lemma 4.5 (temporal gauge; rebuilds what is used from Creutz's transfer-matrix gauge fixing).** Let \(\mu\) be the transfer-kernel lift of a bounded positive operator \(T\) whose kernel \(\kappa\ge0\) satisfies \(\kappa(h\cdot U,h\cdot U')=\kappa(U,U')\) for all \(h\in\mathcal G\). Let \(F\) be a bounded, measurable, gauge-invariant function of the 4D configuration that depends only on spatial bonds at times \(r_1,\dots,r_2\) and temporal bonds at times \(r_1,\dots,r_2-1\), with \(w=r_2-r_1<N_t\). Put \(\tilde F(U_{r_1},\dots,U_{r_2})=F(U,V\equiv1)\) and let \(\mathfrak F\) be the operator with kernel \(\mathfrak F(U_{r_1},U_{r_2})=\int\tilde F\prod_{r=r_1}^{r_2-1}\kappa(U_r,U_{r+1})\prod_{r_1<r<r_2}dU_r\). Then \(\mathbb E_\mu F=Z^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(\mathfrak FT^{N_t-w}P_0)\), with \(Z=\operatorname{Tr}(T^{N_t}P_0)\).

*Proof.* Write the density as \(\prod_r\kappa(U_r,V_r\cdot U_{r+1})\) (`GB1E.md` §1). With \((V\cdot U)_{xy}=V_y^{-1}U_{xy}V_x\) one has \(g\cdot(V\cdot U)=(Vg)\cdot U\), site by site. Define \(g_{r_1}=1\), \(g_{r+1}=V_rg_r\) for \(r_1\le r<r_2\), and new variables \(\tilde U_r=g_r\cdot U_r\). Covariance gives \(\kappa(U_r,V_r\cdot U_{r+1})=\kappa(g_r\cdot U_r,(V_rg_r)\cdot U_{r+1})=\kappa(\tilde U_r,\tilde U_{r+1})\) inside the window. The 4D gauge transformation equal to \(g_r\) on slice \(r\) maps a temporal bond \(V_r\) to \(g_{r+1}^{-1}V_rg_r=1\) inside the window (this is the action under which the density is invariant), so gauge invariance of \(F\) gives \(F=\tilde F(\tilde U_{r_1},\dots,\tilde U_{r_2})\). The factor leaving the window becomes \(\kappa(\tilde U_{r_2},(V_{r_2}g_{r_2})\cdot U_{r_2+1})\). At fixed \(V\), \(U_r\mapsto\tilde U_r\) preserves Haar measure; then \(V_{r_2}\mapsto V_{r_2}g_{r_2}\) preserves Haar measure. The integrand no longer depends on \(V_{r_1},\dots,V_{r_2-1}\), which integrate to one. The remaining chain, with its temporal bonds, gives \(T^{N_t-w}\) and the gauge average \(P_0\), as in `GB1E.md` Theorem 3.1. \(\square\)

The **cube support** \(S(F)\) of such an \(F\) is the set of tiling cubes containing a spatial bond on which \(\tilde F\) depends. If the sites touched by the spatial bonds of \(F\) and of \(G\) are at sup-distance at least 2, then \(S(F)\cap S(G)=\emptyset\): all twelve bonds of a cube have their endpoints within sup-distance 1 of each other.

### 4.2 Theorem D: both lifts factorize over cubes

Fix one reading. Under R-SUM put \(T=e^{-a_0H}\), \(T_C=e^{-a_0H_C}\) (\(\theta\in\mathbb R\), \(\tau_E,a_0>0\)). Under R-IA put \(T=T_{\rm loc}\), \(T_C=T_{{\rm loc},C}\) (\(\beta\in\mathbb R\), \(t>0\)). Tiling cubes are images of one another under torus translations preserving \(\mathcal C\) (by \(2\mathbb Z^3\) and by \((1,1,1)\)), so the spectrum of \(T_C\) does not depend on \(C\). Put \(q=\lambda_1/\lambda_0<1\) and \(\varepsilon_m=\operatorname{Tr}(T_C^m)/\lambda_0^m-1\).

**Theorem D.** For every parameter of either reading:

(D2) If an operator \(X=X_S\otimes1\) acts only on the factors \(\mathcal K_C\), \(C\in S\), then \(U_{KS}^{-n}XU_{KS}^n\) acts only on those factors, for every \(n\in\mathbb Z\). The empty-matter test dynamics moves nothing from one tiling cube to another. The same holds for the quantum-link tick \(U_{(s)}\) of `GB1QL.md` §1.3 at every \(s\), and for every cube-face rung on empty matter, since only the gate supports are used.

(D3) Let \(F\) be as in Lemma 4.5, with window length \(w\) and \(S=S(F)\). Write \(\mathfrak F=\mathfrak F_S\otimes T_{\mathcal C\setminus S}^w\) and \(\omega_\infty(F)=\lambda_0^{-w|S|}\langle\Omega_S,\mathfrak F_S\Omega_S\rangle\). On the cubic torus \(N=N_t\ge4\) even, with \(N>w\),

(D5) For every \(C\) and every integer \(\tau\ge0\), with \(B_C(r)\) the slice function \(B_C\) at time \(r\),
\[
\lim_{N=N_t\to\infty}\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(B_C(0),B_C(\tau))=\sum_{n\ge1}|\langle\varphi_n,B_C\Omega_C\rangle|^2\Big(\frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_0}\Big)^{\tau}>0 .

*Proof.* (D1) By Lemma 4.3(c) the kernel of \(T_C\) is invariant under the corner group; Lemma 4.4 with \(\sigma\) a corner gauge transformation gives \(R_C(g)\Omega_C=\Omega_C\). So \(R_\square(g)\Omega=\Omega\) for \(g\in\mathcal G_\square\); with \(R(h)=R_\square(h|)\), \(P_0\Omega=\Omega\). The eigenvalues of \(T=\bigotimes_CT_C\) are the products of eigenvalues of the factors, all positive, and the top one is reached only when every factor is at \(\lambda_0\), which is simple. Under R-SUM, the top eigenvector of \(e^{-a_0H}\) is the ground state of \(H\).

(D2) Lemma 4.1(a): \(U_{KS}^n=\bigotimes_CU_C^n\). For \(U_{(s)}\) and the other cube-face rungs the same regrouping applies, because each of their gates acts inside one \(\mathcal K_C\) (or its finite-register analogue).

**What Theorem D says in words.** Both lifts, and the test tick itself, are products of independent one-cube systems glued only by the Gauss projection. In the volume-first limit the glue disappears, because the cube-product vacuum already satisfies every Gauss law. The limit state is a product over tiling cubes. Its spatial correlations stop at two lattice units. Its temporal correlations do not stop: they stay positive at every time separation and decay geometrically, no faster than some \((\lambda_n/\lambda_0)^\tau\) and no slower than \((\lambda_1/\lambda_0)^\tau\). The extra symmetry \(\mathcal G_\square\), one \(SU(2)\) per cube corner, is exact.

### 4.3 Theorem A: the exact lift is never exchange invariant (answers GB1E-ς)

**Lemma 4.6 (cycle lemma).** Let \(n\ge3\), \((Y,\nu)\) a probability space, and \(f:Y\times Y\to\mathbb R\) bounded, measurable and symmetric. If \(\sum_{j=1}^nf(y_j,y_{j+1})=0\) for \(\nu^n\)-a.e. \((y_1,\dots,y_n)\), indices mod \(n\), then \(f=0\) a.e.

*Proof.* Put \(a(x)=\int f(x,u)\,d\nu(u)\) and \(c=\iint f\). Fix \((y_1,y_2)=(x,y)\) and integrate over \(y_3,\dots,y_n\) (Fubini, for a.e. \((x,y)\)). The term \(j=1\) gives \(f(x,y)\); \(j=2\) gives \(a(y)\); \(j=n\) gives \(\int f(u,x)\,du=a(x)\) by symmetry; the other \(n-3\) terms give \(c\) each. So \(f(x,y)=-a(x)-a(y)-(n-3)c\) a.e. Integrating in \(y\): \(2a(x)=-(n-2)c\), so \(a\) is a.e. constant. Integrating in \(x\): \(c=-(n-2)c/2\), so \(c=0\), \(a=0\) and \(f=0\) a.e. \(\square\)

**Lemma 4.7 (a slice symmetry of a lift commutes with its transfer).** Let \(\mu\) be the transfer-kernel lift of a bounded self-adjoint \(T\) that commutes with \(\mathcal G\) and has a real symmetric kernel, with \(N_t\ge3\). Suppose the kernel \(\bar\kappa\) of \(TP_0\) satisfies \(0<c\le\bar\kappa\le c'\) a.e. Let \(s\) be an isometry of the spatial torus, acting on slice configurations by permuting bonds (with inversion where it reverses orientation), and let \(R_s\) be the induced unitary on \(\mathcal H\). If \(\mu\) is invariant under the 4D isometry that acts as \(s\) on every time slice, then \(R_s\) commutes with \(TP_0\).

*Proof.* Integrating the temporal bonds of slice \(r\) in \(\prod_r\kappa(U_r,V_r\cdot U_{r+1})\) gives \(\bar\kappa(U_r,U_{r+1})\). So the spatial marginal of \(\mu\) has density \(Z^{-1}\prod_r\bar\kappa(U_r,U_{r+1})\) with respect to Haar measure. The 4D isometry maps spatial bonds of each slice to spatial bonds of the same slice, so invariance of \(\mu\) gives invariance of this marginal under \(s\) on every slice. Put \(f(U,U')=\log\bar\kappa(s\cdot U,s\cdot U')-\log\bar\kappa(U,U')\), bounded and symmetric. Then \(\sum_rf(U_r,U_{r+1})=0\) a.e., so \(f=0\) a.e. by Lemma 4.6. Equality of the kernels is the commutation. \(\square\)

**Theorem A.** On the cubic torus \(N=N_t\ge4\) even, for every \(\theta\in\mathbb R\), \(\tau_E>0\) and \(a_0>0\), the exact R-SUM lift \(\mu_{\rm ex}\) is not invariant under any isometry of the 4-torus lattice that maps the time direction to a spatial direction. So the possible exceptional set in `GB1E.md` Theorem 7.2 is empty, and GB1E-ς is answered: no nonzero \(\theta\) gives exchange invariance.

*Proof.* At \(\theta=0\), \(\mu_{\rm ex}\) is the local lift at \(\beta=0\), which is not exchange invariant (`GB1E.md` Theorem 7.2). Let \(\theta\neq0\) and suppose \(\mu_{\rm ex}\) is invariant under such an isometry \(g\). It is invariant under the unit time translation \(\tau_t\), because its density is invariant under the cyclic shift of slices. Hence it is invariant under \(g\tau_tg^{-1}\), the translation by the unit vector \(g(e_t)=\pm e_i\), which acts slice by slice. The kernel of \(e^{-a_0H}\) is the product of the cube kernels, so by Lemma 4.3(b) it, and its gauge average \(\bar\kappa\), lie between positive constants. Lemma 4.7 then says that the translation unitary \(R\) commutes with \(e^{-a_0H}P_0\). On \(P_0\mathcal H\), which \(R\) preserves, \(e^{-a_0H}\) is positive and injective. Borel functional calculus (the logarithm) makes \(R\) commute with \(H\) restricted to \(P_0\mathcal H\). \(R\) commutes with \(A\), since it permutes bonds. The constant function \(1\) is in \(P_0\mathcal H\cap D(A)\), with \(A1=0\). Hence \(\theta\,(RH_BR^{-1}-H_B)1=0\): the function \(H_B\circ\tau_{\mp e_i}-H_B\) vanishes in \(L^2\). But a unit translation along \(e_i\) changes the parity of the \(i\)-th base coordinate only. So it maps each \(CF\) plaquette in a plane containing \(e_i\) to a non-\(CF\) plaquette (Lemma 3.1(b)). The traces \(\operatorname{Tr}X_p\) of distinct plaquettes are nonzero and pairwise orthogonal: each lies in \(\bigotimes_{\ell\in\partial p}H_1\otimes\bigotimes_{\ell\notin\partial p}H_0\), since matrix entries of \(U\) are degree-one harmonics, and distinct plaquettes have distinct bond sets. So \(H_B\circ\tau_{\mp e_i}\neq H_B\), a contradiction. \(\square\)

**The new step, against the three failed attacks of `GB1E_REFEREE.md` §3.2.** Attack (A) added character observables and could not exclude common zeros; attack (B) used convexity in \(\theta\) and could not separate \(CF\) from non-\(CF\) means; attack (C) asked for a spatial Markov property of the exact kernel. Theorem A uses none of these. It uses the group relation \(g\tau_tg^{-1}=\tau_{\pm e_i}\), and Lemma 4.7, which turns invariance of the measure under a slice symmetry into commutation with the transfer. Lemma 4.7 needs the strictly positive kernel bound, which rests on Lemma 4.2.

**Corollary A1.** For \(\theta\ne0\), \(\mu_{\rm ex}\) is not invariant under any unit spatial translation, nor under any spatial coordinate site reflection. (`GB1E.md` Theorem 7.2 proved the first for \(\mu_{\rm loc}\) only; Theorem 6.1 proved for \(\mu_{\rm ex}\) only that the transfer does not commute with the site reflection.) *Proof.* Lemma 4.7 with \(s\) the translation or the reflection, then the last four sentences of the proof of Theorem A; a site reflection exchanges \(CF\) and non-\(CF\) plaquettes in the planes containing its normal (`GB1E.md` Theorem 6.1). \(\square\)

## 5. Long-distance isotropy and its rate

### 5.1 No restoration at any scale, for either reading

**Corollary D6 (the volume-first state is maximally anisotropic).** For every parameter of either reading, let \(\omega_\infty\) be the volume-first limit of Theorem D (D3). For every lattice isometry \(g\) of \(\mathbb Z^4\) that maps the time direction to a spatial direction, \(\omega_\infty\circ g\neq\omega_\infty\). More precisely, for each tiling cube \(C\) and each \(\tau\ge3\): \(\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_\infty}(B_C(0),B_C(\tau))>0\), while \(\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_\infty}(g\cdot B_C(0),g\cdot B_C(\tau))=0\).

*Proof.* The first inequality is (D5). The observable \(g\cdot B_C(r)\) depends on the bonds \(g(b)\), \(b\in\ell(C)\) at time \(r\). The time coordinate of those bonds becomes the coordinate along \(g(e_t)=\pm e_i\). So the sites touched by the spatial bonds of \(g\cdot B_C(0)\) and \(g\cdot B_C(\tau)\) differ by at least \(\tau-1\ge2\) in that coordinate, and (D4) gives covariance zero. \(\square\)

**What this answers.** Target (1) asked whether isotropy is restored at long distance, with a rate. For both lifts of the cube-face test family, at every \(\theta,\tau_E,a_0\) (R-SUM) and every \(\lambda\) (R-IA): no. The spatial connected correlations of the volume-first state vanish beyond two lattice units at every Euclidean time separation. The temporal autocorrelations are positive at every separation. No rescaling of time, by any factor, makes these two families of correlations match: a vanishing function cannot be rescaled into a positive one. The anisotropy does not shrink with distance. In the language of renormalized anisotropy (Klassen's \(\xi=a_s/a_t\), §2), the spatial propagation speed is zero at every scale. So the exact anisotropy, as a function of the law's data \((\theta,\tau_E)\) and of the step \(a_0\) or \(\lambda\), is constant: spatial speed zero. The tree-level \(\xi_{cl}\) of `GB1E.md` Theorem 7.3 is a static stiffness ratio, not this quantity (§5.2). Theorem D also covers R-ISO: the tree-level isotropic step \(c_{\lambda\tau_E}=\lambda|\theta|\) of `GB1E.md` Theorem 7.3 is one of the \(\lambda\) above, so it restores nothing.

### 5.2 The classical picture, and why the tree-level dictionary did not see it

**Proposition F (classical cube-face dynamics).** (a) The classical flow of \(H_{\rm cl}=\tau_E\sum_\ell|E_\ell|^2+\theta\sum_CB_C(U)\) on \(T^*SU(2)^{\mathcal L}\) is the product of the twelve-bond flows of the tiling cubes. Linearized at \(U\equiv1\), every normal mode lives in one cube: every band is flat, every group velocity is zero. (b) For the abelian linearization, the kernel of the cube-face flux map \(a\mapsto(da)(p)\), \(p\in CF\), has dimension \(7N^3/4\). The kernel of the full plaquette flux map has dimension \(N^3+2\) (exact cochains, \(N^3-1\), plus \(H^1(T^3)\), 3). So the cube-face classical vacuum has \(3N^3/4-2\) more flat directions per Lie-algebra direction than the Wilson one. In the R-IA Euclidean action with \(\beta>0\), the configurations of maximal weight are, modulo 4D gauge, the static configurations that are pure gauge cube by cube; for \(\beta<0\) the same holds after the sign field of `GB1E.md` Theorem 6.1.

*Proof.* (a) \(H_{\rm cl}\) is a sum of functions of disjoint sets of canonical pairs (Lemma 3.1), so the flows commute and act on disjoint factors. (b) A cube is a connected graph with 8 vertices and 12 edges, so its cycle space has dimension \(12-8+1=5\), spanned by its six faces. Zero flux on the faces of \(C\) means \(a|_{\ell(C)}\) is the coboundary of a function on the 8 corners, a space of dimension 7 modulo constants. The cubes have disjoint bond sets, so the kernel is the direct sum over \(N^3/4\) cubes. For the full map, zero flux on all plaquettes means \(a\) is a closed 1-cochain of the cubical 3-torus. \(\square\)

[check] `/tmp/toe-GB1ISO/scripts/check_gb1iso.py` (output `out.txt`), \(N=4\): 16 tiling cubes; each of the 192 bonds lies in exactly one; each site is a corner of exactly two cubes; flux-map kernels 112 (cube faces) and 66 (all plaquettes), as (b) predicts.

`GB1E.md` Theorem 7.3 matched quadratic forms on smooth configurations: "Spatial plaquettes contribute stiffness \(\beta\) on half the plaquettes and zero on the rest" (line 178). That matching is correct for smooth fields, and the referee confirmed it as "a definition by classical matching" (`GB1E_REFEREE.md` item 48). Proposition F shows what it does not see. Smooth fields are not preserved by the cube-face dynamics, and the zero-flux set contains non-smooth, lattice-scale configurations. A static stiffness ratio is not a propagation speed here.

### 5.3 Families and continuum limits, whatever the class

**Corollary D7 (class-independent).** Let \((\mu_k)\) be any sequence of cube-face lifts, of either reading, with any parameters and lattice spacings \(a_k\to0\). Let \(\rho_k(x)\) be bounded gauge-invariant local densities, each depending on bonds within sup-distance \(r_0\) (lattice units) of \(x\), and \(O_k(f)=a_k^4\sum_xf(x)\rho_k(x)\). Suppose the connected two-point functions \(S_k(f,h)=\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_\infty^{(k)}}(O_k(f),O_k(h))\) converge, for test functions \(f,h\) with disjoint supports, to the off-diagonal part of a distribution \(S\). Suppose \(S\) is invariant under the group \(D_cSO(4)D_c^{-1}\), \(D_c={\rm diag}(c,1,1,1)\), for some \(c>0\) (\(c=1\) is O(4)). Then \(S(f,h)=0\) for all \(f,h\) with disjoint supports. The same holds with \(\mu_k\) replaced by its image under any gauge-covariant block map whose block variables depend on fine bonds within a bounded distance of the block.

*Proof.* If the spatial projections of \({\rm supp}f\) and \({\rm supp}h\) are at distance \(d>0\), then for \(a_k(2r_0+2)<d\) every pair \(\rho_k(x),\rho_k(y)\) with \(x\in{\rm supp}f\), \(y\in{\rm supp}h\) has spatial supports at sup-distance at least 2 lattice units, so (D4) gives \(S_k(f,h)=0\), and \(S(f,h)=0\). By a partition of unity and density of tensor products, the distribution \(S(x,y)\) vanishes on the open set \(W=\{(x,y):x_s\neq y_s\}\), where \(x_s\) is the spatial part. For each \(x\neq y\) there is \(Q\in D_cSO(4)D_c^{-1}\) with \((Qx-Qy)_s\neq0\): rotate \(D_c^{-1}(x-y)\) to a spatial vector, and \(D_c\) fixes spatial vectors. Invariance carries the vanishing on \(W\) to the open sets \(Q^{-1}W\), which cover \(\{x\neq y\}\). For block images, a coarse gauge-invariant local observable is a fine gauge-invariant local observable with a bounded spatial support, and the same argument applies with a larger \(r_0\). \(\square\)

So any limit of these lifts that is rotation invariant, even up to a time rescaling, has a connected action-density two-point function that is zero on all separated tests. T0-start[G] (ii) asks for "a non-trivial theory (NT1, NT2)", and NT2 asks that "gauge-invariant correlators at scale r equal their perturbative values at leading order" (`GOAL.md` line 31). At leading order the connected two-point function of the action density is a sum of squares of the free field-strength two-point function, which is not zero at separated points. So no family of cube-face lifts, and no family of bounded-range block images of them, can satisfy T0-start's conclusion (ii). T0-rate compares the start's correlators with that limit at separation \(r\gg a\); it fails too, since the start's correlators at spatial separation \(r\ge2a\) are exactly zero in the volume-first state. This holds for every choice of \(\mathcal A_{k_0}(g)\): if T0-start's conclusion holds for a class, the class contains no such family. The decision of (H-class) no longer waits for the class to be pinned, for these lifts.

**Corollary D8 (an infinite-volume measure exists, for these lifts).** For either lift, the torus measures converge as \(N=N_t\to\infty\), on every bounded local function, to a gauge-invariant probability measure \(\nu_\infty\) on the bonds of \(\mathbb Z^3\times\mathbb Z\). *Proof.* For a bounded local \(f\), the torus expectation equals that of its average over the gauge transformations at the finitely many sites it touches, which is gauge invariant and local; (D3) gives the limit \(\omega_\infty\) of that average. The limits are positive, normalized and consistent, and the configuration space is a product of compact spaces, so the Riesz-Kolmogorov extension gives \(\nu_\infty\). \(\square\) This settles, for the cube-face lifts only, the infinite-volume half of `GB1E.md` GAP 2 ("A consistent infinite-volume measure [...] not established"). Corollary D7 shows that it does not help them past (H-class).
