## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**Refuted in the plain reading, at the readout, before dynamics.** Theorem A (section 2.4): for every \(1\le m\le9\), for `L5-O[S_+]`, for every admissible update, every class of states, every zoom rung and every block map, there is no unital *-homomorphism at all from the invariant algebra of a \(\mathbb Z_2\) lattice gauge theory (pure, or with matter, or the five-mode test law \(L5[\mathbb Z_2]\) of Theorem 5M) into the law's invariant algebra \(\mathfrak A^G\). Locality, covariance and injectivity are not needed. **The named step** is the unit: \(\mathfrak A^G\) has a character (a one-dimensional representation), and the \(\mathbb Z_2\) invariant algebra has none. The character is the locked state (\(m\) even) or the oriented state (\(m\) odd) of `ZM1INV.md`, rebuilt here as Lemma 2. The \(\mathbb Z_2\) algebra has no character because its electric operator \(X_\ell\) and its plaquette \(Z_p\), \(\ell\in\partial p\), are gauge-invariant self-adjoint unitaries that anticommute (Lemma 3). Corollary B: \(\mathfrak A^G\) contains no such pair at all. So the three suggested routes (the centre \(\mathbb Z_2\), a Krauss-Wilczek remnant, a sector with frozen \(U(1)\) charges) all fail at the same step in this reading, and attacks (Z-a) and (Z-c) are explained by it. **Robustness.** Dropping "unital" alone does not escape: uniform exponential locality forces \(\Phi(1)=1\) (Proposition C). The obstruction does not touch the \(U(1)\) rung: the spin-\(S\) \(U(1)\) quantum-link source has a character (Proposition D(i)). It needs the unit: \(\mathfrak A^G\) of `L5-U[m]` has corners that contain two anticommuting self-adjoint unitaries (Proposition D(ii)). It holds for every subgroup reading whose group contains the torus used in Lemma 2 (Proposition D(iii)). **What is left.** A readout into a sector representation instead of \(\mathfrak A^G\) (candidate reading R-SEC, section 2.8, not adopted) removes this step; the \(\mathbb Z_2\) rung under R-SEC is not decided here. `[check]`: the weight space of the witness is one-dimensional on a plaquette (\(m=1,\dots,9\)) and on a cube (\(m=1,\dots,8\)), matter included.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Local invariants are dense

**Lemma 1.** For either named law, \(\bigcup_R\mathfrak A^G(R)\) is norm dense in \(\mathfrak A^G\). For a finite site set \(S\supseteq S(R)\), the average \(E_S(B)=\int_{K_S}\operatorname{Ad}\Gamma_S(g)(B)\,dg\) over \(K_S=\prod_{x\in S}K_x\) (normalized Haar measure) maps \(\mathfrak A(R)\) onto \(\mathfrak A^G(R)\), is contractive, and fixes \(\mathfrak A^G\).

*Proof.* The Gauss generator at \(x\) is a sum of commuting even bilinears, one on each factor at \(x\) (matter, six ends). So \(\Gamma_x(g)\) factors as \(\Gamma_x^{\rm in}(g)\Gamma_x^{\rm out}(g)\), with \(\Gamma_x^{\rm in}\) on the factors of \(R\) and \(\Gamma_x^{\rm out}\) on the others. \(\Gamma_x^{\rm out}\) is even and acts on factors outside \(R\), so it commutes with \(\mathfrak A(R)\). Hence \(\operatorname{Ad}\Gamma_x(g)\) maps \(\mathfrak A(R)\) into itself. For \(y\notin S(R)\), \(\Gamma_y\) acts only outside \(R\) and fixes \(\mathfrak A(R)\) pointwise. Generators at different sites act on disjoint modes and commute, so \(\Gamma_S(g)=\prod_{x\in S}\Gamma_x(g_x)\) is a norm-continuous unitary representation of the compact group \(K_S\) on finitely many factors. The integral converges in norm. Haar invariance makes \(E_S(B)\) invariant under every \(K_x\), \(x\in S\); the other sites fix it. So \(E_S(\mathfrak A(R))\subseteq\mathfrak A^G(R)\), and \(E_S\) fixes invariant elements. Each \(\operatorname{Ad}\) is isometric, so \(\|E_S(B)\|\le\|B\|\). Given \(A\in\mathfrak A^G\) and \(\varepsilon>0\), pick \(B\in\mathfrak A(R)\) with \(\|A-B\|<\varepsilon\). Then \(\|A-E_{S(R)}(B)\|=\|E_{S(R)}(A-B)\|<\varepsilon\). □

### 2.2 Every named law has a character

Fix a product vector \(\Psi\) (its restriction to a region \(R\) is \(\Psi_R\)):

- **(a) `L5-U[m]`, \(m=2k\), \(1\le k\le4\) (locked).** Empty matter. Every link end holds exactly the flavours \(\{1,\dots,k\}\).
- **(b) `L5-U[m]`, \(m=2k+1\), \(0\le k\le4\) (oriented).** Empty matter. On every link \((x,x+e_i)\) the lower end \(x\) holds \(\{1,\dots,k\}\) and the upper end holds \(\{1,\dots,k+1\}\).
- **(c) `L5-O[S_+]` (empty).** Every matter mode and every link mode empty.

In (c) the empty link vector lies in \(S_+\). `LW1_5.md` section 4, proof of R: "On \(S_+\), the product of the ten commuting \(2T_{2j-1,2j}\) is chirality \(+1\)". With \(f_j=(\eta_{2j-1}+i\eta_{2j})/2\) one has \(2T_{2j-1,2j}=i\eta_{2j-1}\eta_{2j}=2f_j^\dagger f_j-1\). So the chirality is \(\prod_{j=1}^{10}(2n_j-1)=(-1)^{N}\), and \(S_+\) is the even-number subspace. The empty vector has \(N=0\).

**Lemma 2 (characters; ZM1INV Theorem 2.1 and R1, rebuilt).** In each case (a), (b), (c), every \(A\in\mathfrak A^G(R)\) satisfies \(A\Psi_R=\chi(A)\Psi_R\) with \(\chi(A)=\langle\Psi_R,A\Psi_R\rangle\). The functional \(\chi\) extends uniquely to a character of \(\mathfrak A^G\). So \(\mathfrak A^G\) has a character for every \(1\le m\le9\) and for `L5-O[S_+]`.

*Proof.* *Step 1 (weights).* For `L5-U`, \(x\in S(R)\) and \(j=1,\dots,5\), let \(h_{x,j}\) be the number of quanta of flavour \(j\) in the factors of \(R\) at \(x\): \(n_{x,j}\) if \(x\) is a site of \(R\), plus \(r^\dagger_{\ell,x,j}r_{\ell,x,j}\) for each link \(\ell\) of \(R\) at \(x\). The Gauss generator \(\mathcal G_x^{E_{jj}}\) equals \(h_{x,j}\) plus terms on factors outside \(R\). An invariant \(A\in\mathfrak A(R)\) commutes with \(e^{i\theta\mathcal G_x^{E_{jj}}}\) for all \(\theta\); the outside terms commute with \(A\); so \(A\) commutes with \(e^{i\theta h_{x,j}}\) for all real \(\theta\), hence with \(h_{x,j}\) (finite spectrum: \(h\) is a polynomial in \(e^{i\theta h}\) for small \(\theta>0\)). For `L5-O` use \(H_{x,j}\), the part in \(R\) of \(\mathcal G_x^{2j-1,2j}\): a sum of \(n-\tfrac12\) over the factors of \(R\) at \(x\), since \(M^{2j-1,2j}=n_j-\tfrac12\) (`LW1_5.md` line 40) and \(L^{2j-1,2j}=n_{e,j}-\tfrac12\) by the identity above. The occupation basis of \(\mathcal H_R\) (on each `L5-O` link, the even-number basis vectors) is a joint eigenbasis. So \(A\) maps each joint eigenspace into itself.

*Step 2 (the weight space of \(\Psi_R\) is \(\mathbb C\Psi_R\)).* Let \(v\) be an occupation basis vector with the weights of \(\Psi_R\): matter sets \(M_x\), end sets \(F_e\subseteq\{1,\dots,5\}\), \(|F_{\rm lower}|+|F_{\rm upper}|=m\) on each link. Let \(d_x\) be the number of ends at \(x\) of links of \(R\), \(u_x\) the number of links of \(R\) whose upper end is \(x\), and \(n_R\) the number of links of \(R\).

(a) The weights are \(h_{x,j}=d_x\) for \(j\le k\) and \(0\) for \(j>k\). So no matter mode and no end holds a flavour \(>k\). Each end holds at most \(k\) flavours and each link holds \(2k\): every end holds exactly \(\{1,\dots,k\}\). The ends then supply all of \(h_{x,j}=d_x\), so \(M_x=\varnothing\). Thus \(v=\Psi_R\).

(b) The weights are \(h_{x,j}=d_x\) (\(j\le k\)), \(h_{x,k+1}=u_x\), \(0\) (\(j\ge k+2\)). Sum over \(x\) and \(j\): \(\sum_x|M_x|+mn_R=k\sum_xd_x+\sum_xu_x=2kn_R+n_R=mn_R\). So all \(M_x\) are empty. Then every end holds every \(j\le k\), no end holds \(j\ge k+2\), and each link holds \(2k+1\): exactly one end of each link holds \(k+1\). Orient each link toward that end. Its in-degree at \(x\) is \(h_{x,k+1}=u_x\), the in-degree of the lower-to-upper orientation. Let \(D\) be the set of links where the two orientations differ, oriented lower-to-upper. Equal in-degrees give \(\mathrm{in}_D(x)=\mathrm{out}_D(x)\) at every \(x\). If \(D\neq\varnothing\), follow \(D\)-links from a tail: each vertex entered has an exit, so the walk never stops. Each step raises the coordinate sum by one, so no vertex repeats, which is impossible in a finite set. So \(D=\varnothing\) and \(v=\Psi_R\).

(c) \(H_{x,j}(\Psi_R)\) is \(-\tfrac12\) times the number of factors of \(R\) at \(x\), the least possible value. Each term \(n-\tfrac12\) is at least \(-\tfrac12\), with equality only at \(n=0\). Every mode of \(\mathcal H_R\) lies in exactly one such sum. So \(v\) has every mode empty: \(v=\Psi_R\).

*Step 3 (character).* By Steps 1-2, \(A\Psi_R=\chi(A)\Psi_R\) for \(A\in\mathfrak A^G(R)\). Then \(AB\Psi_R=\chi(B)A\Psi_R=\chi(A)\chi(B)\Psi_R\), \(\chi(A^*)=\overline{\chi(A)}\), \(\chi(1)=1\) and \(|\chi(A)|\le\|A\|\). \(\Psi\) is a product vector, so \(\chi\) does not depend on \(R\) (the matter part is the Fock vacuum, whose restriction to a subregion is the vacuum of that subregion). By Lemma 1, \(\chi\) extends by continuity to all of \(\mathfrak A^G\); multiplicativity and \(\chi(1)=1\) survive the limit. □

The witness is a state of the algebra, not a claimed vacuum. It need not satisfy any Gauss sector, stationarity or cell invariance; Lemma 2 uses none of these. For the record, `ZM1INV.md` R1(b) proves the oriented state is invariant under the base tick, and `GA15PHI.md` Theorem 8 proves the oriented sector is frozen under link-only invariant operators at \(m=1,9\).

### 2.3 A \(\mathbb Z_2\) source has no character

**Lemma 3.** (a) Let \(\mathfrak B\neq0\) be a unital C*-algebra that contains self-adjoint unitaries \(X,Z\) with \(XZ=-ZX\). Then \(\mathfrak B\) has no character. (b) The invariant algebra \(\mathfrak A_1^G\) of every \(\mathbb Z_2\) source (section 1.4) contains such a pair: \(X=X_\ell\) and \(Z=Z_p\), for any plaquette \(p\) and any \(\ell\in\partial p\). (c) The same holds for the image of \(\mathfrak A_1^G\) in any nonzero representation, in particular on any Gauss sector \(G_x=\epsilon_x\), and for every nonzero quotient of \(\mathfrak A_1^G\).

*Proof.* (a) If \(\chi\) is a character, \(\chi(X)^2=\chi(X^2)=1\) and \(\chi(Z)^2=1\). Then \(\chi(X)\chi(Z)=\chi(XZ)=\chi(-ZX)=-\chi(Z)\chi(X)\), so \(\chi(X)\chi(Z)=0\). This contradicts \(\chi(X)^2\chi(Z)^2=1\).

(b) \(X_\ell\) commutes with every \(X_{\ell'}\) and with matter, hence with every \(G_x=P_x\prod_{\ell'\ni x}X_{\ell'}\) (or \(G^0_x\)). For \(Z_p\): if \(x\) is not a corner of \(p\), \(G_x\) and \(Z_p\) act on disjoint factors and commute. If \(x\) is a corner, exactly two links of \(\partial p\) contain \(x\); each contributes one sign \(X_{\ell'}Z_{\ell'}=-Z_{\ell'}X_{\ell'}\), and \(P_x\) commutes with link operators. The two signs cancel. So \(X_\ell,Z_p\in\mathfrak A_1^G\). Both are self-adjoint unitaries. In \(X_\ell Z_p\), the factor \(Z_\ell\) occurs once, so \(X_\ell Z_p=-Z_pX_\ell\). The proof is the same for one-mode matter, for the five-mode test law \(L5[\mathbb Z_2]\) of Theorem 5M (where \(P_x=(-1)^{N_x}\)), and for a source on the coarse lattice of a zoom rung.

(c) A nonzero unital representation \(\pi\) sends \(X_\ell,Z_p\) to anticommuting self-adjoint unitaries; apply (a) to \(\pi(\mathfrak A_1^G)\). A character of a quotient \(\mathfrak A_1^G/I\) composes with the quotient map to a character of \(\mathfrak A_1^G\). □

Kitaev's text (arXiv:quant-ph/9707021, section 1) states the companion fact for the toric code, "These operators commute with each other because star(s) and boundary(p) have either 0 or 2 common edges." Lemma 3(b) uses the case of one common edge.

### 2.4 Theorem A: no ZM1-INV readout of a \(\mathbb Z_2\) source

**Theorem A.** Let the law be `L5-U[m]`, any \(1\le m\le9\), or `L5-O[S_+]`, with invariant algebra \(\mathfrak A^G\) (section 1.2). Let \(\mathfrak B\) be a unital C*-algebra with no character. Then no unital *-homomorphism \(\Phi:\mathfrak B\to\mathfrak A^G\) exists. In particular, for every \(\mathbb Z_2\) source \(\mathfrak A_1^G\), and every nonzero quotient of it:

1. no unital *-homomorphism \(\mathfrak A_1^G\to\mathfrak A^G\) exists, whether or not it is injective, cell covariant or local;
2. hence no ZM1-INV readout of a \(\mathbb Z_2\) source into either named law exists, for any admissible update, any class of states, any zoom rung and block map, and any value of the free constants \(\varepsilon,\mu,\tau_E,m,\omega_0\);
3. hence GB1-Z2[L5], as stated at `GB1_REFEREE.md` line 233, is false in the plain reading of GB1 Definition 1, at algebra level and at sector level alike.

*Proof.* Suppose \(\Phi\) exists. Let \(\chi\) be the character of \(\mathfrak A^G\) given by Lemma 2. Then \(\chi\circ\Phi\) is a *-homomorphism \(\mathfrak B\to\mathbb C\) with \((\chi\circ\Phi)(1)=\chi(1)=1\), a character of \(\mathfrak B\). This contradicts the hypothesis. Lemma 3 gives the hypothesis for \(\mathbb Z_2\) sources and their nonzero quotients. Items 2 and 3 follow, because a ZM1-INV readout is in particular a unital *-homomorphism into \(\mathfrak A^G\), and both algebra-level and sector-level equality (GB1 Definition 2) presuppose one. □

**The first failing step, as GB1(a) asks.** The steps of GB1(a) are, in order: a rung \(n\); a readout \(\Phi\) of ZM1-INV form; equality of the link sector with the named theory, with its time step. The first step that fails is the second, and within it the clause "unital". The failure needs nothing about \(n\), the block map, the update or the state. It is a property of the two algebras alone: the law's invariant algebra has a one-dimensional representation (Lemma 2), and the \(\mathbb Z_2\) invariant algebra has none (Lemma 3).

**GB1(a)'s premise.** GB1(a) begins (`TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` line 435): "Fix a gauge group `G` and a framework law `L` with an exact local `G` frame group". Read literally, neither named law is a candidate for \(G=\mathbb Z_2\): their exact frame groups are \(U(5)\) and \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) (`LW1_5.md` section 4, U: "Its generator-level site frame group is exactly \(U(5)\)"; R: "the generator-level site frame group is \(SO(10)\)" on sites, \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) on state spaces by Q and G). In that reading the premise is the first failing step, and Theorem 5M's test laws are the answer GB1 already gives. GB1-Z2[L5] reads the premise more widely, letting \(\mathbb Z_2\) sit inside the frame group, as GB1 does for \(SU(2)\) through R-SUB. Theorem A refutes GB1-Z2[L5] in that wider reading. Its step does not use the premise.

**Zoom rungs and R-form.** At rung \(n\) the readout still lands in the fine law's \(\mathfrak A^G\); the block map only fixes where images are localized. Under R-form a coarse law is again `L5-U[m₁]` or `L5-O`, and Lemma 2 gives it a character too. So a readout that factors through a coarse law also fails, at the coarse step. `GB1.md` line 163 records the other branch: if R-form bound GB1's source, "the ladder would be empty by premise". Under either reading of R-GB1Φ, no \(\mathbb Z_2\) rung exists inside the named laws through a ZM1-INV readout.

**The link sector.** GB1(a) speaks of "the framework's link sector, restricted to the gauge-invariant algebra". If the readout's target is read as the link-only invariant algebra \(\mathfrak A^G_{\rm link}\) (invariant elements of the link algebra), nothing changes: \(\mathfrak A^G_{\rm link}\) is a unital subalgebra of \(\mathfrak A^G\), the character of Lemma 2 restricts to it, and the proof of Theorem A applies.

**The five-mode test law.** Lemma 3 covers \(L5[\mathbb Z_2]\). So Theorem 5M's algebra-level \(\mathbb Z_2\) equality cannot be carried into `L5-U[m]` or `L5-O[S_+]` by any ZM1-INV readout. Theorem 5M stands as stated; it concerns a different law.

### 2.5 Corollary B and the three suggested routes

**Corollary B.** In \(\mathfrak A^G\) of `L5-U[m]` (any \(m\)) or `L5-O[S_+]`: (i) there are no self-adjoint unitaries \(X,Z\) with \(XZ=-ZX\); (ii) more generally, there are no invertible \(a,b\) with \(ab=\lambda ba\) for a scalar \(\lambda\neq1\). (iii) Hence Theorem A holds for every \(\mathbb Z_N\) source, \(N\ge2\), and for every finite abelian group \(G\neq1\) in place of \(\mathbb Z_2\).

*Proof.* (ii) With \(\chi\) from Lemma 2, \(\chi(a)\chi(a^{-1})=1\), so \(\chi(a)\neq0\neq\chi(b)\); then \(\chi(a)\chi(b)=\lambda\chi(b)\chi(a)\) forces \(\lambda=1\). (i) is the case \(\lambda=-1\). (iii) For \(\mathbb Z_N\) links \(\mathbb C^N\), let \(X_\ell\) be the shift and \(Z_\ell\) the clock, \(Z_\ell X_\ell=\omega X_\ell Z_\ell\), \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/N}\), and let \(G_x=\prod_{\ell\text{ out of }x}X_\ell\prod_{\ell\text{ into }x}X_\ell^{-1}\) (times a matter charge, if any). \(X_\ell\) commutes with every \(G_x\). The oriented plaquette \(Z_p=Z_{\ell_1}Z_{\ell_2}Z_{\ell_3}^{-1}Z_{\ell_4}^{-1}\) is invariant: at each corner one incident link is entered and one left, and the two phases cancel. \(X_\ell Z_p=\omega^{\pm1}Z_pX_\ell\) for \(\ell\in\partial p\). Both are unitary. So the source invariant algebra contains a pair as in (ii), has no character (the argument of (ii) applied to its own character), and Theorem A's proof applies. A finite abelian group is a product of cyclic groups; use one cyclic factor of order \(\ge2\). □

**Why (Z-a) and (Z-c) failed.** (Z-a) (`GB1.md` line 183) put "\(X_\ell=2n^{(5)}_{\ell,x}-1\) and \(Z_\ell=U^{55}_\ell+U^{55\dagger}_\ell\)" on a frozen one-flavour block. These operators are not in \(\mathfrak A^G\); they are invariants of a restricted subgroup on a frozen class. Corollary B(i) shows that no repair can place an exact anticommuting electric-magnetic pair inside \(\mathfrak A^G\) itself. (Z-c) (`GB1.md` line 185) used "the centre \(\mathbb Z_2\subset U(5)\) and R-SUB", that is, invariants of the centre alone, a larger algebra than \(\mathfrak A^G\). Its recorded failure is a smuggle-test failure ("1 F ... 4 F"), not an algebraic one. Theorem A shows that, in the plain reading, \(\mathfrak A^G\) cannot host any \(\mathbb Z_2\) source at all.

**The three routes of the lane brief, in the plain reading.**

1. *The centre \(\mathbb Z_2\) of \(U(5)\) or \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\).* The centre acts inside \(K_x\), so every element of \(\mathfrak A^G\) is already centre invariant. A readout into \(\mathfrak A^G\) fails by Theorem A. A readout into the centre invariants is the subgroup reading of (Z-c); Proposition D(iii) does not reach it (the centre is finite and contains no torus), and this file does not decide it.
2. *A Krauss-Wilczek remnant.* As an exact algebraic statement, a \(\mathbb Z_2\) remnant would give a gauge-invariant unitary loop with eigenvalues \(\pm1\) and a gauge-invariant flux-parity unitary that anticommutes with it. Corollary B(i) excludes every such pair in \(\mathfrak A^G\). As a statement about a phase (Fradkin-Shenker's charge-2 Higgs region, a long-distance property of a state), it is untouched; see Proposition D(iv).
3. *A sector with frozen \(U(1)\) charges.* Freezing charges restricts the class of states. It does not change the algebra the readout lands in, which stays \(\mathfrak A^G\), and Theorem A applies. A frozen sector can help only if the readout itself changes, for example its target (candidate reading R-SEC, section 2.8).

### 2.6 Proposition C: dropping "unital" alone does not escape

One might drop the word "unital" and keep the rest of GB1 Definition 1. The uniform locality clause then restores it.

**Lemma 4 (invariant elements are even).** For either named law, every \(A\in\mathfrak A^G\) satisfies \(\theta(A)=A\), where \(\theta\) is the matter-parity automorphism (\(c_{x,j}\mapsto-c_{x,j}\), links fixed).

*Proof.* On \(\mathfrak A(R)\), \(\theta=\operatorname{Ad}P_S\) with \(P_S=\prod_{x\in S}(-1)^{N_x}\) for any finite \(S\supseteq S(R)\). By Lemma 1 and the continuity of \(\theta\), it suffices to show that each \(A\in\mathfrak A^G(R)\) commutes with \(P_S\) for \(S=S(R)\). For `L5-U`, \(A\) commutes with \(V=\prod_{x\in S}e^{i\pi Q_x}\), \(Q_x=N_x+\sum_{\ell\ni x}p_{\ell,x}\) (the centre of each \(K_x\), `LW1_5.md` line 228). Write \(V=P_S\,L\). In \(L=\prod_{x\in S}\prod_{\ell\ni x}(-1)^{p_{\ell,x}}\), a link with both ends in \(S\) contributes \((-1)^{p_{\ell,x}+p_{\ell,y}}=(-1)^m\), a scalar. A link with one end in \(S\) is not a link of \(R\) (both ends of a link of \(R\) lie in \(S(R)\subseteq S\)), so its factor commutes with \(A\). Hence \(A\) commutes with \(P_S=VL^{-1}\). For `L5-O` use \(V=\prod_{x\in S}\omega_x\), \(\omega_x=e^{i\pi\sum_j\mathcal G^{2j-1,2j}_x}\). Its site factor is \(-iP_x\) (`LW1_5.md` line 171: "The site factors are respectively \(-1\) and \(-iP_x\)"). Its end factor at a link end \(e\) is \(\prod_je^{i\pi(n_{e,j}-1/2)}=(-i)^5(-1)^{N_e}\). For a link with both ends in \(S\) the two end factors give \(-(-1)^{N_\ell}=-1\) on \(S_+\), a scalar. The rest is as for `L5-U`. □

**Proposition C.** Let \(\Phi:\mathfrak A_1^G\to\mathfrak A^G\) be a nonzero *-homomorphism, not assumed unital, with the uniform exponential locality of `GB1.md` Definition 1. Assume the block map separates sites: for every \(R\) there are source sites \(x,x'\) whose fine \(R\)-neighbourhoods \(B=N_R(\mathrm{block}(x))\) and \(B'=N_R(\mathrm{block}(x'))\) are disjoint. (Every zoom block map by cubes of side \(L^n\) does.) Then \(\Phi(1)=1\). So Theorem A applies to \(\Phi\).

*Proof.* \(P=\Phi(1)\) is a projection, and \(P\neq0\) since \(\Phi(a)=\Phi(1)\Phi(a)\). The input \(1\) is supported on the single site \(x\) and on the single site \(x'\), with support radius \(0\). Let \(C_0,\xi_0\) be the constants for radius \(0\). Choose \(R\) with \(\delta=C_0e^{-R/\xi_0}<1/8\), and \(x,x'\) as assumed. There are \(A\in\mathfrak A(B)\) and \(A'\in\mathfrak A(B')\) with \(\|P-A\|\le\delta\) and \(\|P-A'\|\le\delta\). Let \(\theta\) be the matter-parity automorphism. It is isometric, maps \(\mathfrak A(B)\) and \(\mathfrak A(B')\) into themselves, and fixes \(P\) by Lemma 4. Replacing \(A\) by \((A+\theta A)/2\) and \(A'\) likewise, take both even. \(\mathfrak A(B)\) is a full matrix algebra (finitely many CAR modes and finite registers). Let \(E(Y)=\int_{\mathcal U(\mathfrak A(B))}uYu^*\,du\), Haar measure on its unitary group; \(E\) is contractive on \(\mathfrak A\). It maps \(\mathfrak A(B)\) into its centre, \(E(A)=\operatorname{tr}(A)1\). An even element of \(\mathfrak A(B')\) commutes with all of \(\mathfrak A(B)\) (\(B\cap B'=\varnothing\)), so \(E(A')=A'\). Then \(\|\operatorname{tr}(A)1-A'\|=\|E(A-A')\|\le\|A-A'\|\le2\delta\), and \(\|P-\operatorname{tr}(A)1\|\le3\delta<1/2\). A projection within distance \(<1/2\) of a scalar \(\lambda\) is \(0\) or \(1\): otherwise its spectrum contains \(0\) and \(1\), and both \(|\lambda|<1/2\) and \(|1-\lambda|<1/2\). So \(P=1\). □

So in GB1 Definition 1 the clause "unital" is implied by the others, given a separating block map. For a *-homomorphic readout of a \(\mathbb Z_2\) source, Theorem A and Proposition C together say: avoiding the obstruction requires changing the target algebra \(\mathfrak A^G\), or giving up uniform locality for the unit, or giving up a separating block map. Dropping one word does none of these.

### 2.7 Proposition D: the reach of Theorem A

**Proposition D.** (i) *The \(U(1)\) rung is not touched.* The invariant algebra of the spin-\(S\) \(U(1)\) quantum-link source (`GB1.md` Definition 7, or the five-mode \(L5[U(1),S]\) of Theorem 5M; pure or with matter) has a character. So Theorem A's step says nothing about the \(U(1)\) rung.

(ii) *The unit is essential.* For every \(1\le m\le9\), \(\mathfrak A^G\) of `L5-U[m]` contains a nonzero projection \(P_{\rm std}\) and self-adjoint \(X,Z\in P_{\rm std}\mathfrak A^GP_{\rm std}\) with \(X^2=Z^2=P_{\rm std}\) and \(XZ=-ZX\). So non-unital *-homomorphisms \(M_2\to\mathfrak A^G\) exist.

(iii) *Subgroup readings of full rank.* Let \(H\) be a closed subgroup of \(K\) that contains the torus used in Lemma 2 (the diagonal torus of \(U(5)\); for `L5-O`, the torus generated by the five \(\mathcal G^{2j-1,2j}\)). If \(\mathfrak A^G\) is read as the \(H\)-invariants (R-SUB with \(H\)), Theorem A holds unchanged. Examples: \(H=T^5\), \(U(4)\times U(1)\), \(U(3)\times U(2)\). Not covered: \(SU(5)\), \(S(U(3)\times U(2))\), the Standard Model \(SU(2)\), the centre \(\mathbb Z_2\).

(iv) *Not touched at all.* Correlator-level and universality bridges (`GB1.md` line 13: "A failed specified route does not rule out a correlator-level or universality bridge"); an emergent \(\mathbb Z_2\) phase in a state, including a Krauss-Wilczek remnant in the phase sense; Euclidean routes through E-1 to E-4; readouts into an algebra other than \(\mathfrak A^G\).

*Proof.* (i) The Gauss generator is \(\mathcal G_x=N_x+\sum_{\rm out}E_\ell-\sum_{\rm in}E_\ell\) (`GB1.md` line 165), with \(E_\ell=S^z_\ell\). Take the product vector with empty matter and \(E_\ell=+S\) on every link \((x,x+e_i)\). Its weight at \(x\) in a region \(R\) is \(w_x=S(\mathrm{out}_R(x)-\mathrm{in}_R(x))\). Let an occupation basis vector have matter numbers \(N'_x\ge0\) and fluxes \(E'_\ell=S-\delta_\ell\), \(\delta_\ell\in\{0,1,\dots,2S\}\), with the same weights. Then \(N'_x=\sum_{\rm out}\delta_\ell-\sum_{\rm in}\delta_\ell\). Summed over \(x\in S(R)\) the right side is \(0\), so all \(N'_x=0\). Then \(\delta\) is a nonnegative integer flow with inflow equal to outflow at every vertex, on links oriented lower-to-upper. If \(\delta\neq0\), follow links with \(\delta>0\) from a tail: every vertex entered has an exit, and the coordinate sum rises at each step, which is impossible in a finite set. So \(\delta=0\), the weight space is one-dimensional, and Step 3 of Lemma 2 (with the \(U(1)\) average of Lemma 1) gives a character. Chandrasekharan and Wiese's \(U(1)\) quantum link (arXiv:hep-lat/9609042, Eq. (3.16), \(U=S^+\)) is this source at \(N_x\equiv0\).

(ii) Fix a site \(v\) and three links \(\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_3\) at \(v\), with ends \(e_1,e_2,e_3\) at \(v\). Put \(q=1\) if \(m\le6\) and \(q=4\) if \(m\ge7\); then \(0\le m-q\le5\), so \(p_{e}=q\) is allowed. Let \(P=\prod_{i=1}^3[p_{e_i}=q]\), an invariant projection. With \(E^{e}_{ab}=r^\dagger_{e,a}r_{e,b}\), put \(T_{12}=\sum_{a,b}E^{e_1}_{ab}E^{e_2}_{ba}\). Entries on different links commute, so \(T_{12}=\operatorname{tr}(E^{e_1}E^{e_2})\) is invariant under simultaneous conjugation, that is, under \(U(5)_v\); it acts only on \(v\)-ends, so other sites fix it; and \(T_{12}^*=T_{12}\). It preserves end numbers, so it commutes with \(P\). On \(\operatorname{Ran}P\), for \(q=1\), \(T_{12}|c\rangle|d\rangle=\sum_{a,b}\delta_{bc}\delta_{ad}|a\rangle|b\rangle=|d\rangle|c\rangle\): the transposition of the colours at \(e_1,e_2\). For \(q=4\) write \(h_a=r^\dagger_a\) (hole annihilators) and \(|c\rangle=r_c|{\rm full}\rangle\); then \(E_{ab}=\delta_{ab}-h^\dagger_bh_a\), and on one hole per end \(T_{12}=5-1-1+\mathrm{swap}=3+\mathrm{swap}\). Let \(s=(T_{12}-c_q)P\) and \(t=(T_{23}-c_q)P\), \(c_1=0\), \(c_4=3\). These are invariant and act on \(\operatorname{Ran}P\) as the transpositions \((12),(23)\) of three colour labels. So \(s^2=t^2=P\) and \(c:=st\) satisfies \(c^3=P\). Let \(P_{\rm sym},P_{\rm alt}\) be the averages of \(\pi\) and of \(\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)\pi\) over the six words of \(S_3\) in \(s,t\), and \(P_{\rm std}=P-P_{\rm sym}-P_{\rm alt}\). These are invariant projections that commute with \(s,t\). Let \(Q=(P+c+c^2)/3\), the projection onto \(c\)-fixed vectors. Since \(scs=c^2\), \(\operatorname{Ran}Q\) is \(s\)-invariant and \(S_3\) acts on it through \(S_3/\langle c\rangle\); so \(\operatorname{Ran}Q\subseteq\operatorname{Ran}(P_{\rm sym}+P_{\rm alt})\), and \((P+c+c^2)P_{\rm std}=0\). \(P_{\rm std}\neq0\): the vector \(|112\rangle+\omega|121\rangle+\omega^2|211\rangle\), \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}\), tensored with any allowed state elsewhere, lies in \(\operatorname{Ran}P\) and is a \(c\)-eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\neq1\), so it is not in \(\operatorname{Ran}(P_{\rm sym}+P_{\rm alt})\). Put \(X=sP_{\rm std}\) and \(Z=(2t+s)P_{\rm std}/\sqrt3\). Using \(ts=c^{-1}=c^2\) and \(c+c^2=-1\) on \(\operatorname{Ran}P_{\rm std}\): \(Z^2=(4+2(c+c^2)+1)P_{\rm std}/3=P_{\rm std}\), and \(XZ+ZX=(2(c+c^2)+2)P_{\rm std}/\sqrt3=0\). `GA15PHI.md` Theorem 2 found the same standard \(S_3\) module in a cube-corner multiplicity.

(iii) Lemma 2 Step 1 uses invariance under the torus only; Steps 2-3 and Lemma 1 (with \(K_S\) replaced by \(H_S\)) are unchanged. So the \(H\)-invariant algebra has the same character, and the proof of Theorem A applies.

(iv) Theorem A concerns unital *-homomorphisms into \(\mathfrak A^G\) and nothing else. □

Proposition D(i) and Lemma 3 together locate the obstruction: a \(\mathbb Z_2\) gauge theory has an invertible gauge-invariant plaquette that anticommutes with an invertible gauge-invariant electric operator. A \(U(1)\) quantum link lacks the first: its plaquette is a product of raising and lowering operators, which kill the states of extreme flux (consolidated #9926: "U is nilpotent (U^2 = 0), not unitary"), so it is not invertible.

### 2.8 A candidate reading for the owner: R-SEC

One way around Theorem A and Proposition C is to let the readout land in the algebra the sector sees, instead of \(\mathfrak A^G\). This is recorded as a candidate. It is not proposed for adoption here, and nothing below uses it.

**R-SEC (candidate, not adopted).** "At sector level, with a class \(\mathcal S\) of states fixed in advance, let \(\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S}=\{A\in\mathfrak A^G:\omega(A^*A)=0\ \forall\omega\in\mathcal S\}\). Assume \(\mathcal S\) is invariant under the tick and under \(\omega\mapsto\omega(B^*\cdot B)/\omega(B^*B)\) for \(B\in\mathfrak A^G\), \(\omega(B^*B)>0\). The readout is a unital injective *-homomorphism \(\Phi:\mathfrak A_1^G\to\mathfrak A^G/\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S}\), cell covariant, and exponentially local in the images of the local algebras. Sector-level equality is GB1 Definition 2 with \(\alpha\) acting on the quotient."

Under the two closure assumptions, \(\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S}\) is a closed two-sided ideal: it is a closed left ideal by \(\omega(A^*B^*BA)\le\|B\|^2\omega(A^*A)\), and the second closure makes it a right ideal as well. Tick invariance of \(\mathcal S\) gives \(\alpha(\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S})=\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S}\), so \(\alpha\) acts on the quotient.

Four tests. (1) It rewrites the readout clause of GB1(a), which is the owner's ZM1-INV text, but it does not state the rung's conclusion: **C**, as for R-EQ. (2) The class \(\mathcal S\) is the lane's choice, fixed in advance but otherwise free, so a lane could choose it to suit the target: **C**. (3) It does not decide whether the VC1 state lies in \(\mathcal S\) (GB1-SC stays open), nor any Euclidean or continuum question: **P**. (4) It removes a requirement and uses the class that GB1 Definition 2 already has: **P**. Result: C C P P.

**Proposition E (what R-SEC still forbids).** Under R-SEC, if some \(\omega\in\mathcal S\) satisfies \(\omega\ge\lambda\tilde\chi\) for some \(\lambda>0\) and some state \(\tilde\chi\) of \(\mathfrak A\) whose restriction to \(\mathfrak A^G\) is a character, then no \(\mathbb Z_2\) readout exists. In particular \(\mathcal S\) must not contain the witnesses of Lemma 2.

*Proof.* For \(A\in\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S}\), \(\lambda|\tilde\chi(A)|^2=\lambda\tilde\chi(A^*A)\le\omega(A^*A)=0\). So \(\tilde\chi|_{\mathfrak A^G}\) vanishes on \(\mathfrak J_{\mathcal S}\) and defines a character of the quotient. Apply the proof of Theorem A. □

A familiar special case of R-SEC is the observable algebra of one Gauss sector: take \(\mathcal S\) to be the states carried by that sector. `LW1_5.md` section 1 reads gauge invariance as "invariance under conjugation, not necessarily a zero Gauss-charge sector", so this quotient is not the plain reading. Proposition E decides it whenever the sector carries a character state; the witnesses of Lemma 2 are product vectors, not \(SU(5)\) singlets, so a sector of locally invariant vectors need not contain them.

Proposition D(ii) shows that the kinematic room exists: corners of \(\mathfrak A^G\) away from the witnesses carry anticommuting pairs. Whether an admissible update and a sector give the Trotterized \(\mathbb Z_2\) theory exactly under R-SEC is a new question, created by this candidate. It is named **GB1-Z2[L5,SEC]** and is not worked here. It arises only if the owner adopts R-SEC or a similar reading.
