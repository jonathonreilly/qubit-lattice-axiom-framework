## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**GB1-FORM: Hamiltonian.** Every law of the family is exactly a Trotterized Kogut–Susskind-type lattice gauge theory: its tick is a product of electric, magnetic and hopping exponentials (Proposition F3). The tick is never a positive transfer matrix unless it is trivial (F1). Positive transfer matrices with \(T^{\,i}=U\) exist, one for each lift of the quasi-energies, but the law fixes none (F2). For \(\mathbb Z_2\) the Wilson transfer matrix is the tick's analytic continuation to imaginary angles (F4). A Euclidean target therefore needs named extra inputs E-1 to E-4. **GB1-Z2: proved in Hamiltonian form.** On one-mode sites with \(L^2(\mathbb Z_2)\) links, a rung law `L_Z2` is gauge invariant, cell covariant and nearest-neighbour under NN-a; its exact frame group is \(\mathbb Z_2\); its tick is the Trotterized `Z_2` gauge theory with one fermion, at algebra level with the identity readout; and in matter-empty states it is the pure `Z_2` theory (Theorems Z2-A to Z2-C). Euclidean `Z_2` results do not transfer without E-1. **GB1-U1.** A new obstruction, Theorem R: no ZM1-INV readout from a lattice gauge theory with \(L^2(G)\) links, \(G\) infinite, into any finite-register law exists, because its single-link invariant algebra holds infinitely many orthogonal projections. So the exact bridge fails at the readout, before dynamics and before Theorem N. The strongest true statements are exact equality with the Trotterized U(1) quantum link model (cube-face plaquettes) and a large-register approximation over the family \(S\to\infty\) at rate \(O((\log S)^4/S^2)\) for flux-bounded states. D-theory fails at the dimension count. **GB1-SU2.** The exact bridge fails at the readout (Corollary R1). Inside `L5-U`, through Proposition T, the \(SU(2)\) block evolves exactly as the Trotterized \(U(2)\) rishon quantum link model in matter-empty states with frozen spectator rishons (Theorem SU2-C). Outside those states the block does not evolve on its own (Proposition SU2-B); on `L5-O` spectators cannot be frozen (Lemma SU2-O). **The first rung whose proof fails** is U(1) for the exact group-valued bridge, at the readout; at sector level no rung fails for quantum links. **GB1-H.** CA-I's group-valued Haar links and exact Wilson action cannot be delivered by any exact bridge; reflection positivity, the Euclidean form, hypercubic symmetry, the coupling trajectory and the \(\operatorname{tr}F^2\) observable class each need a named extra input.

## 1. GB1-FORM: Hamiltonian or Euclidean target

**Setting.** On a finite periodic box with even side (tilings and signs as in `LW1.md` §1), a law's tick is a unitary \(U\) on a finite-dimensional space \(K\). On \(\mathbb Z^3\) it is the automorphism \(\alpha\) of `LW1.md` E1. All statements in this section are made on finite boxes. The laws in view have the form \(U=W_BW_EM_oM_e\): matter cube layers \(M_e=\prod_{C\in\mathcal T_e}e^{-ia_eH_C}\), \(M_o=\prod_{C\in\mathcal T_o}e^{ia_oH_C}\) with number-conserving hops; an electric layer \(W_E\); a magnetic layer \(W_B\) of commuting link-only gates.

**Proposition F1 (the tick is not a positive transfer matrix).** (a) A unitary \(U\) with \(U\ge0\) equals \(1\). (b) A unitary \(U\) with \(U=U^*\) satisfies \(U^2=1\).

*Proof.* \(U\) is normal with spectrum on the unit circle. If \(U\ge0\) its spectrum also lies in \([0,\infty)\), hence equals \(\{1\}\); the spectral theorem gives \(U=1\). If \(U=U^*\) then \(U^2=U^*U=1\). □

*Use.* The transfer operator of a measure that is reflection positive in time is positive (mirror between time slices) or self-adjoint (mirror through a slice); this is the standard Osterwalder–Schrader construction, cited as context and not used as a proof step. So Y1's phrase "with the update as transfer" can hold only for ticks with \(U^2=1\). The laws' ticks are not involutions at generic constants. Example: on matter-empty vectors of `L_cube` with integer \(S\ge1\) and no magnetic layer, \(U=e^{-i\tau_E\sum_\ell E_\ell^2}\). Two flux states that differ by \(E_\ell=0\) versus \(E_\ell=1\) on one link give \(U^2\) eigenvalues whose ratio is \(e^{-2i\tau_E}\), so \(U^2\) is not even a scalar unless \(\tau_E\in\pi\mathbb Z\).

**Proposition F2 (the tick fixes no transfer matrix).** Let \(U=\sum_k e^{-i\theta_k}P_k\) be unitary on a finite-dimensional space, with distinct eigenvalues and spectral projections \(P_k\). (a) The Hermitian \(H\) with \(e^{-iH}=U\) and \(HP_k=P_kH\) for all \(k\) are exactly \(H=\sum_kH_k\), where \(H_k\) acts on \(\operatorname{ran}P_k\) with spectrum in \(\theta_k+2\pi\mathbb Z\). Each gives a positive invertible \(T_H=e^{-H}\) with \(e^{-iH}=U\). (b) Integer-tick correlators \(\langle\psi,AU^nB\psi\rangle\) are the same for all such \(H\). The imaginary-time correlators \(\langle\psi,A\,T_H^{\,\tau}B\psi\rangle\), \(\tau>0\), are not: on \(\mathbb C^2\) with \(U=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-i\theta})\), \(\psi=e_1\), \(A=B=\sigma^x\), the choices \(H_j=\operatorname{diag}(0,\theta+2\pi j)\), \(j\in\mathbb Z\), give \(e^{-\tau(\theta+2\pi j)}\), pairwise different for each \(\tau>0\).

*Proof.* (a) \(e^{-iH}\) is a function of \(H\), so on each eigenspace of \(H\) it is a scalar \(e^{-i\lambda}\); it equals \(U\) exactly when every eigenvalue of \(H\) on \(\operatorname{ran}P_k\) lies in \(\theta_k+2\pi\mathbb Z\). Conversely any such block sum exponentiates to \(U\). \(T_H\) is a positive function of a Hermitian operator. (b) The first quantity involves \(U\) only. The example is a direct computation: \(\langle e_1,\sigma^xT_{H_j}^\tau\sigma^xe_1\rangle=\langle e_2,T_{H_j}^\tau e_2\rangle\). □

So positive transfer matrices with \(T^{\,i}=U\) exist in abundance; what fails is that the law selects one. A Euclidean target needs a lift of the quasi-energies from the circle to the line, and a relation between the framework's state and that lift (a ground or KMS state). Neither is law data; the state is VC1's question (PS-7r).

**Proposition F3 (Trotter identity).** (a) On the matter-empty subspace \(K_0\) (vectors annihilated by every \(c_{x,j}\)), \(M_e\) and \(M_o\) act as the identity, so \(U|_{K_0}=W_BW_E|_{K_0}\). (b) For the tiling-cube magnetic layer of GA1-P-5 with \(\theta_e=\theta_o=\tau_B\), \(W^{cube}=e^{-i\tau_BH_B^{cf}}\) with \(H_B^{cf}=\sum_{C\in\mathcal T_e\cup\mathcal T_o}\sum_{p\subset C}B_p\). (c) For a magnetic layer of pairwise commuting plaquette gates \(e^{-i\tau_BB_p}\) on a set \(\mathcal P\) of plaquettes, \(W_B=e^{-i\tau_B\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}B_p}\). (d) On all of \(K\), \(U=e^{-i\tau_BH_B}e^{-i\tau_EH_E}e^{ia_oH_o}e^{-ia_eH_e}\), with \(H_T=\sum_{C\in\mathcal T_T}H_C\) and \(W_E=e^{-i\tau_EH_E}\); for `L_cube` \(H_E=\sum_\ell E_\ell^2\), for `L5-U` \(H_E=\sum_\ell(p_{\ell,x}-m/2)^2\).

*Proof.* (a) Every edge term of \(H_C\) has the form \(c^\dagger_{y}(\cdot)c_{x}\) or \(c^\dagger_x(\cdot)c_y\), with the annihilator on the right; so \(H_CK_0=0\) and the power series gives \(e^{\pm iaH_C}|_{K_0}=1\). (b) By `GA1_5.md` Theorem 2.1 (G1), every link lies in exactly one tiling cube. Hence distinct tiling cubes, of either tiling, have disjoint link sets, and their operators \(B_C=\sum_{p\subset C}B_p\) commute. A product of commuting exponentials is the exponential of the sum. (c) The same. (d) Cubes of one tiling are disjoint, so their \(H_C\) commute; apply (b)-(c) to each layer. □

Thus every law of this family is, exactly, a Trotterized (Floquet) lattice gauge theory of Kogut–Susskind type: four exponentials of electric, magnetic and two hopping Hamiltonians. Trotterized gauge-invariant evolutions of this kind are standard in digital simulation (Lamm–Lawrence–Yamauchi, arXiv:1903.08807). The tick is not \(e^{-i\tau(H_E+H_B)}\). The following bound says how close it is when the angles are small.

**Proposition F3′ (small angles).** For skew-Hermitian \(A,B\) on a finite-dimensional space, \(\|e^{A}e^{B}-e^{A+B}\|\le\tfrac12\|[A,B]\|\), and \(\|(e^Ae^B)^n-e^{n(A+B)}\|\le\tfrac n2\|[A,B]\|\). With \(A=-i\delta bH_B\), \(B=-i\delta eH_E\) and \(n=\lfloor t/\delta\rfloor\), the error is at most \(\tfrac{t\delta}{2}\,be\,\|[H_B,H_E]\|\) plus \(O(\delta)\) from the rounding of \(t/\delta\).

*Proof.* Put \(X(s)=e^{sA}e^{sB}\), \(Y(s)=e^{s(A+B)}\). Then \(X'=(A+B)X+(e^{sA}Be^{-sA}-B)X\), so by Duhamel \(X(1)-Y(1)=\int_0^1Y(1-r)(e^{rA}Be^{-rA}-B)X(r)\,dr\). Since \(\tfrac{d}{dr}e^{rA}Be^{-rA}=e^{rA}[A,B]e^{-rA}\), the middle factor has norm at most \(r\|[A,B]\|\); the outer factors are unitary. Integrate. For \(n\) steps telescope \(X^n-Y^n=\sum_jX^{n-1-j}(X-Y)Y^j\). □

The small-angle limit is a limit over the free constants, that is, over a family of laws; one law has fixed angles. Its real-time limit \(e^{-it(bH_B+eH_E)}\) is the Kogut–Susskind evolution on finite links. Creutz's limit (Phys. Rev. *Proof.* Gauge fixing: put \(g_x(0)=1\) and \(g_x(t+1)=g_x(t)\tau_x(t)\) for \(t\le N_t-2\). The map from \((\sigma,\tau)\) to (new \(\sigma\), \(s\), the old \(\tau_x(t)\) for \(t\le N_t-2\)) is a bijection, and \(w\) depends on the first two only; this gives the factor \(2^{|V|(N_t-1)}\). ### 2.2 Compiling link-only unitaries on one-mode sites

`GA1_5.md` Lemma U compiles link-only unitaries on five-mode sites. Its Step 1 needs the even algebra of one site to be non-commutative (\(M_{16}\oplus M_{16}\)). On one-mode sites the even algebra of one site is \(\operatorname{span}\{1,n_x\}\), which is commutative, so Step 1 fails as written. The following replaces Steps 1-2 and keeps Steps 3-4.

**Lemma U1 (one-mode sites).** Let \(L'\) be a finite connected set of links with \(|L'|\ge2\), each with a finite-dimensional register \(H_\ell\), on one-mode sites. Let \(\mathcal E_\ell=\mathcal A^+_{xy}\otimes B(H_\ell)\) for \(\ell=(x,y)\), where \(\mathcal A^+_{xy}\) is the even part of the CAR algebra of the modes at \(x,y\). For every unitary \(G\) on \(\bigotimes_{\ell\in L'}H_\ell\), \(G\otimes1\) (identity on all matter and all other links) is a finite product of unitaries, each in some \(\mathcal E_\ell\), \(\ell\in L'\). Each factor is an even nearest-neighbour gate in the sense of NN-a, read with \(F_x=\Lambda\mathbb C^1\) (reading R-NN1, section 6).

*Proof.* Let \(\mathfrak g\) be the real Lie algebra generated by the skew-Hermitian elements of the \(\mathcal E_\ell\), \(\ell\in L'\). Write \(\gamma_x=c_x+c_x^\dagger\); then \(\gamma_x^2=1\) and distinct Majoranas anticommute.

*Step 1′.* Let \(\ell=(x,y)\) and \(\ell'=(y,z)\) be distinct links of \(L'\) sharing \(y\); then \(x\ne z\). Put \(T=i\gamma_x\gamma_y\in\mathcal A^+_{xy}\) and \(T'=i\gamma_y\gamma_z\in\mathcal A^+_{yz}\), both Hermitian. For Hermitian \(A\) on \(H_\ell\) and \(B\) on \(H_{\ell'}\), \(i(A\otimes T)\) and \(i(B\otimes T')\) lie in \(\mathfrak g\). Since \(A,B\) act on different factors and commute with matter, \([i(A\otimes T),i(B\otimes T')]=-(A\otimes B)\otimes[T,T']\). Moving \(\gamma_y\) twice gives \(\gamma_y\gamma_z\gamma_x\gamma_y=-\gamma_x\gamma_z\), so \([\gamma_x\gamma_y,\gamma_y\gamma_z]=2\gamma_x\gamma_z\) and \([T,T']=-2\gamma_x\gamma_z=2iS\), with \(S=i\gamma_x\gamma_z\). Hence \(i(A\otimes B\otimes S)\in\mathfrak g\).

*Step 2′.* \(S\) is Hermitian, \(S^2=1\), and \(S\) commutes with link operators. For Hermitian \(M=A\otimes B\), \(M'=A'\otimes B'\): \([i(M\otimes S),i(M'\otimes S)]=-[M,M']\otimes S^2=-[M,M']\otimes1\in\mathfrak g\). Real-bilinearity extends this to all Hermitian \(M,M'\) on \(H_\ell\otimes H_{\ell'}\), because Hermitian product operators span the Hermitian operators over \(\mathbb R\). The operators \(i[M,M']\) span the traceless Hermitian operators (the fact used in `GA1_5.md` Lemma U Step 2). With \(i1\in\mathfrak g\), \(\mathfrak u(H_\ell\otimes H_{\ell'})\otimes1\subseteq\mathfrak g\).

*Steps 3-4.* These are `GA1_5.md` Lemma U Steps 3 and 4, verbatim: Step 3 inducts over connected subsets \(J\subseteq L'\) using only the inclusion just proved and brackets of link-only operators; Step 4 uses `LW1.md` lines 198-205 (the group of finite words in one-parameter subgroups is the analytic subgroup of the generated Lie algebra) to write \(G\otimes1=e^{i\Theta}\otimes1\) as a finite word. □

### 2.3 Admissibility

**Theorem Z2-A.** For all free data:
1. *(Gauss.)* Each \(G_x\) is a Hermitian unitary; the \(G_x\) commute; every layer of \(U_{\mathbb Z_2}\) commutes with every \(G_x\).
2. *(Cell covariance, PS-1c.)* For each \(g\in\mathscr G\), the lift \(\alpha_g\) (the permutation of sites and links, with `LW1.md` A2's site sign gauge \(c_x\mapsto d(x)c_x\), and \(X_\ell\mapsto X_{g\ell}\), \(Z_\ell\mapsto Z_{g\ell}\)) commutes with \(\alpha\) and maps \(G_x\) to \(G_{gx}\). The lifts act transitively on sites.
3. *(NN-a, read by R-NN1.)* \(\alpha\) is a composition of finitely many layers, each a locally finite product of even unitaries on pairwise disjoint edge triples.
4. *(PS-6r.)* The register is finite; the frame \(\mathbb Z_2\) acts on it at each end by \(X_\ell\); \(Z_\ell\) is the raising and lowering operator the Gauss law needs: it anticommutes with \(X_\ell\), so it flips the \(\mathbb Z_2\) flux.

*Proof.* 1. \(P_x\) is even, so it commutes with every matter operator at other sites and with every \(P_y\); link factors commute with matter; \(X\)'s commute. In \(h_\ell\), \(\ell=(x,y)\), conjugation by \(G_x\) changes the sign of \(c_x\) (through \(P_x\)) and of \(Z_\ell\) (through \(X_\ell\)); the two signs cancel; the same holds at \(y\); other \(G_z\) commute. A plaquette meets each of its corners in two links, so \(Z_p\) picks up two signs. \(X_\ell\) commutes with \(G_x\). Exponentials and products keep commutation.
2. This is `LW1.md`'s gauged covariance proof (lines 251-253) with \(J_\ell\) replaced by \(Z_\ell\), and with one simplification: \(h_\ell\) is symmetric under \(x\leftrightarrow y\), so a reversed link needs no register map (`LW1.md` uses \(e^{-i\pi S^x}\) there). A2's sign correction restores the \(\eta_i\): the ratio of transformed to original edge signs is \(d(x)d(y)\) on each edge, and \(c_x\mapsto d(x)c_x\) multiplies \(c^\dagger_yc_x+c_x^\dagger c_y\) by \(d(x)d(y)\). Each tiling is preserved (A2), so \(\alpha_g(H_C)=H_{gC}\) with the same angle. \(W_E\) and \(W_B\) are products over all links and all plaquettes, which every lattice isometry permutes, and they commute with site sign gauges. \(P_x\mapsto P_{gx}\) and the six links at \(x\) go to the six links at \(gx\). Transitivity is A2's.
3. *Matter layers.* `LW1.md`'s dressed factorization proof (lines 196-205) applies verbatim: \(-iH_C=\sum_jX_j\) with \(X_j=-i\eta\,h_\ell/\sqrt3\) skew-Hermitian and supported in one edge triple, so \(e^{\mp ia H_C}\) is a finite word in the \(e^{tX_j}\); each factor is even. Cubes of one tiling are disjoint, so the \(k\)-th factors of all cubes form one layer. *Electric layer.* The links \((x,x+e_i)\) with \(x_i\) even (respectively odd) form a perfect matching for each \(i\); the six matchings give six layers of single-link gates. *Magnetic layer.* Each \(e^{i\tau_BZ_p}\) is a unitary on the four links of \(p\), a connected set; Lemma U1 writes it as a word of edge-triple gates of \(p\)'s links. Plaquettes form \(24\) orbits under \(2\mathbb Z^3\) (three orientations, eight base residues). `GA1_5.md` Proposition C(ii)'s scheduling applies as written: choose \(k\) with \(2k\) larger than the compiled support diameter, run each class (orbit, \(\beta\bmod k\)) in parallel, and use that the plaquette gates commute, so the order of classes does not matter. Finitely many layers result; only the composite is required to be gauge invariant and covariant (NN-a).
4. \(X_\ell\) is the regular action of the generator of \(\mathbb Z_2\) on \(L^2(\mathbb Z_2)\), the same at both ends since \(\mathbb Z_2\) is abelian and \(g^{-1}=g\). \(Z_\ell\) is multiplication by the group element, the exact unitary link of `GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 2.3 (N4) for \(K=\mathbb Z_2\). □

**Lemma Z2-F (the exact frame group is `Z_2`).** Let \(\varphi\) be a frame at \(x\) (a CAR automorphism fixing every other mode and every link and preserving the algebra at \(x\)), and let \(\operatorname{Ad}L\) be a register change (conjugation by a unitary on finitely many links). If \(\operatorname{Ad}L\circ\varphi\) fixes \(h_\ell\) for one link \(\ell=(x,y)\), then \(\varphi(c_x)=\pm c_x\). Conversely \(\varphi(c_x)=-c_x\) is compensated by \(L=\prod_{\ell\ni x}X_\ell\), and then the whole tick is fixed. So the generator-level exact frame group at each site is \(\mathbb Z_2\); the U(1) phases of `L_cube` are not exact here.

*Proof.* By `LW1.md` B3, \(\varphi(c_x)=uc_x\) or \(vc_x^\dagger\) with \(|u|=1\) or \(|v|=1\). Put \(Z'=\operatorname{Ad}L(Z_\ell)\), a nonzero link operator; \(\operatorname{Ad}L\) fixes matter. In the first case \(\operatorname{Ad}L\varphi(h_\ell)=Z'\otimes(u\,c_y^\dagger c_x+\bar u\,c_x^\dagger c_y)\). The matter operators \(c_y^\dagger c_x\) and \(c_x^\dagger c_y\) are linearly independent, and the algebra is a tensor product of matter and links, so equality with \(Z_\ell\otimes(c_y^\dagger c_x+c_x^\dagger c_y)\) forces \(uZ'=Z_\ell=\bar uZ'\); hence \(u=\bar u=\pm1\). In the second case the image has matter part \(v\,c_y^\dagger c_x^\dagger+\bar v\,c_xc_y\), which is linearly independent of \(c_y^\dagger c_x\) and \(c_x^\dagger c_y\); equality would force \(vZ'=0\), impossible. The converse is Theorem Z2-A.1, since \(G_x=P_x\prod_{\ell\ni x}X_\ell\) implements it. □

### 2.4 The bridge

**The target \(LGT_{\mathbb Z_2,f}\).** The Trotterized Kogut–Susskind `Z_2` lattice gauge theory on \(\mathbb Z^3\) with one fermion species (Fradkin–Shenker type matter, here a fermion): link Hilbert space \(L^2(\mathbb Z_2)=\mathbb C^2\); Wilson link \(U_\ell=Z_\ell\), exactly unitary; electric operator \(X_\ell\); Gauss operators \(G_x=(-1)^{n_x}\prod_{\ell\ni x}X_\ell\); electric and magnetic Hamiltonians \(H_E=-\sum_\ell X_\ell\), \(H_B=-\sum_pZ_p\) over all plaquettes; hopping Hamiltonians \(H_e=\sum_{C\in\mathcal T_e}H_C\), \(H_o=\sum_{C\in\mathcal T_o}H_C\); time step \(U=e^{-i\tau_BH_B}e^{-i\tau_EH_E}e^{ia_oH_o}e^{-ia_eH_e}\).

**Theorem Z2-B (algebra-level equality).** \(U_{\mathbb Z_2}\) is the time step of \(LGT_{\mathbb Z_2,f}\). The identity map \(\Phi=\mathrm{id}:\mathfrak A^G\to\mathfrak A^G\) is a readout of ZM1-INV form (unital, injective, *-homomorphism, cell covariant, strictly local, hence exponentially local with any constants), at zoom rung \(0\), and \(\alpha\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\alpha\).

*Proof.* Proposition F3(c)-(d): the plaquette gates commute, so \(W_B=e^{-i\tau_BH_B}\); \(W_E=e^{-i\tau_EH_E}\); cubes of one tiling are disjoint, so \(M_e=e^{-ia_eH_e}\) and \(M_o=e^{ia_oH_o}\). Everything else is Theorem Z2-A. □

The content of Theorem Z2-B is Theorem Z2-A: the law is admissible under NN-a and PS-1c, and its link register is the full group algebra with the exact Wilson link. Theorem N does not apply because the image of \(\mathbb Z_2\) is finite.

**Theorem Z2-C (pure `Z_2` gauge theory, sector level).** Let \(\mathfrak A_1\) be the quasi-local algebra of the links alone, \(G^0_x=\prod_{\ell\ni x}X_\ell\), \(\mathfrak A_1^{G}\) the commutant of all \(G^0_x\) in \(\mathfrak A_1\), and \(\alpha_1(A)=(W_BW_E)^*A(W_BW_E)\): the pure Trotterized Wegner–Kogut–Susskind `Z_2` theory. Let \(\Phi:\mathfrak A_1^{G}\to\mathfrak A^G\) be the inclusion.
1. \(\Phi\) is a unital injective cell-covariant strictly local *-homomorphism into \(\mathfrak A^G\).
2. For every state \(\omega\) of \(\mathfrak A\) with \(\omega(n_x)=0\) for all \(x\), every link-only \(A\) and every \(t\ge0\): \(\omega(\alpha^t(A))=\omega(\alpha_1^t(A))\). If moreover \(\omega(G_x)=1\) for all \(x\), then \(\omega\circ\Phi\) satisfies the pure-gauge Gauss law \(\omega(G^0_x)=1\).
3. Every state \(\omega_1\) of \(\mathfrak A_1\) is the restriction to \(\mathfrak A_1\) of the product state \(\omega=\omega_{\rm vac}\otimes\omega_1\), where \(\omega_{\rm vac}\) is the matter Fock vacuum; this \(\omega\) satisfies item 2's hypothesis.
4. The same holds with \(\omega(n_x)=1\) for all \(x\) (filled matter), except that then \(G_x=-G^0_x\): the link sector is the `Z_2` gauge theory with one static charge at every site.

*Proof.* 1. A link-only operator commutes with every \(P_x\), so it commutes with \(G_x=P_xG^0_x\) exactly when it commutes with \(G^0_x\). The other properties are those of an inclusion that commutes with the lifts of Theorem Z2-A.2 (link-only lifts are permutations).
2. By `LW1.md` E1, \(\alpha^t(A)=V^*AV\) for a finite ordered word \(V\) of the gates in the backward light cone of \(A\), all inside a finite region \(\Lambda\). The restriction of \(\omega\) to the algebra of \(\Lambda\) is a density matrix \(\rho\) with \(\operatorname{tr}(\rho\,n_x)=0\) for \(x\in\Lambda\). Since \(N_\Lambda=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}n_x\ge0\) has kernel \(|0_\Lambda\rangle\otimes(\text{links})\), \(\rho=|0_\Lambda\rangle\langle0_\Lambda|\otimes\rho_L\). Each matter gate fixes \(|0_\Lambda\rangle\otimes\phi\) (Proposition F3(a)). Hence \(V(|0_\Lambda\rangle\otimes\phi)=|0_\Lambda\rangle\otimes V^L\phi\), where \(V^L\) is \(V\) with its matter gates deleted, and \(\omega(V^*AV)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_L(V^L)^*AV^L)\). \(V^L\) is a word of the link layers in their order; gates of a layer that miss the current support commute with it and with the other gates of that layer, so they cancel, and \((V^L)^*AV^L=\alpha_1^t(A)\). The Gauss statement: \(\omega(G_x)=\omega(P_xG^0_x)\) and \(P_x=1\) on \(\operatorname{ran}\rho\).
3. The product state exists on the quasi-local algebra (matter and links are tensor factors) and has \(\omega(n_x)=0\).
4. \(h_\ell\) also annihilates the filled vector (each term creates at a filled site), so the matter gates fix it; on it \(P_x=-1\). □

**Proposition Z2-D (the pure-gauge bridge is not algebra level).** There is a discrete set \(D\subset\mathbb R\), containing \(0\) and the same for every even cube, such that for every law `L_Z2` with \(a_e\notin D\) (all other constants arbitrary), \(\alpha\circ\Phi\ne\Phi\circ\alpha_1\) for the inclusion \(\Phi\) of Theorem Z2-C. In the window \(0<a_e<\pi\), \(D\) has finitely many points.

*Proof.* Let \(\ell\) lie in the even cube \(C\). \(X_\ell\in\mathfrak A_1^G\), and \(\alpha_1\) is an automorphism of \(\mathfrak A_1\) that preserves \(\mathfrak A_1^G\); put \(B=\alpha_1^{-1}(X_\ell)\in\mathfrak A_1^G\). From \(U_{\mathbb Z_2}^*=M_e^*M_o^*(W_BW_E)^*\), \(\alpha(B)=M_e^*M_o^*\,X_\ell\,M_oM_e\). Odd cube gates contain no \(Z_\ell\), so they commute with \(X_\ell\); even cube gates other than \(C\)'s commute with it too. So \(\alpha(B)=e^{ia_eH_C}X_\ell e^{-ia_eH_C}\). If \(\alpha\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\alpha_1\) then \(\alpha(B)=X_\ell\). Put \(f(a)=e^{iaH_C}X_\ell e^{-iaH_C}-X_\ell\), an entire function into the finite-dimensional algebra of \(C\). Its derivative at \(0\) is \(i[H_C,X_\ell]=\tfrac{i\eta}{\sqrt3}[Z_\ell,X_\ell]\otimes(c_y^\dagger c_x+c_x^\dagger c_y)=-\tfrac{2\eta}{\sqrt3}Y_\ell\otimes(c_y^\dagger c_x+c_x^\dagger c_y)\ne0\). So \(f\not\equiv0\), and its zero set \(D\) is discrete. Translations by \(2\mathbb Z^3\) carry \(H_C\) to \(H_{C'}\) for even cubes, so \(D\) does not depend on \(C\). A discrete closed set meets the compact interval \([0,\pi]\) in finitely many points. □

The proposition concerns the inclusion. It does not exclude some other readout that intertwines exactly; none is constructed here.

**What transfers through \(\Phi\).** Theorems Z2-B and Z2-C are identities, so every statement about the Trotterized Hamiltonian `Z_2` theory, with or without the fermion, holds for `L_Z2` in the stated sectors, verbatim. Two examples: at \(\tau_E=0\) every \(Z_p\) and every \(G_x\) commutes with \(U_{\mathbb Z_2}\) on the matter-empty sector, so the toric-code stabilizers of the pure theory are conserved there; the gauge structure is exact at every angle, which is the property stressed by Lamm–Lawrence–Yamauchi (arXiv:1903.08807). Rigorous results proved for the **Euclidean** `Z_2` Wilson model (Wegner's duality, Osterwalder–Seiler's strong-coupling analysis, Fradkin–Shenker's phase structure) are statements about the transfer matrix \(T=A^{|L|}F(-i\beta_s,-ih^*)\) of Proposition F4, the imaginary-angle slice. They do not transfer without E-1 to E-4 of section 1. **GB1-Z2 is proved in its Hamiltonian form, with the first failing step of a Euclidean hand-off at E-1.**

## 3. GB1-U1: U(1)

Three candidate statements are tested: exact equality with the Kogut–Susskind theory on \(L^2(U(1))\) links (the "exact bridge"); exact equality with a U(1) quantum link model; and a large-register limit with a rate. The exact bridge fails, and it fails earlier than Theorem N: at the readout itself.

### 3.1 The readout fails before any dynamics

`GA2_Y2.md` Theorem N and Corollary Y2-0 exclude identifications that carry the Wilson link to a register operator covariant under the law's bounded Gauss generators. Its Scope paragraph says: "It does not exclude an identification of gauge-invariant correlators". ZM1-INV imposes "no map on charged fields or Gauss generators", so Theorem N does not touch a ZM1-INV readout. The following theorem does. It uses only the invariant algebra.

**Theorem R (readout dimension bound).** Let \(\mathcal C\) be a C*-algebra containing nonzero, mutually orthogonal projections \(\Pi_1,\Pi_2,\dots\) (infinitely many). Let \(\mathfrak A\) be a unital C*-algebra and \(\mathfrak B\subseteq\mathfrak A\) a unital C*-subalgebra with \(\mathfrak B\cong M_D\). Let \(\Phi:\mathcal C\to\mathfrak A\) be an injective *-homomorphism and \(0\le\delta<\tfrac12\). Then it is false that \(\operatorname{dist}(\Phi(c),\mathfrak B)\le\delta\|c\|\) for every \(c\in\mathcal C\). More precisely, at most \(D/(1-2\delta)\) indices \(n\) satisfy \(\operatorname{dist}(\Phi(\Pi_n),\mathfrak B)\le\delta\).

*Proof.* *Matrix units.* Let \(e_{ij}\) be matrix units of \(\mathfrak B\), \(\sum_ie_{ii}=1\). For \(X\in\mathfrak A\) put \(X_{ij}=\sum_ke_{ki}Xe_{jk}\). Then \(e_{pq}X_{ij}=e_{pi}Xe_{jq}=X_{ij}e_{pq}\), so \(X_{ij}\) lies in the relative commutant \(\mathfrak B^c=\mathfrak B'\cap\mathfrak A\), and \(\sum_{ij}e_{ij}X_{ij}=\sum_{ij}e_{ii}Xe_{jj}=X\). *Expectation.* Choose a state \(\varphi\) of \(\mathfrak B^c\) and put \(E(X)=\sum_{ij}\varphi(X_{ij})e_{ij}\in\mathfrak B\). \(E\) is linear and unital, and \(E(b)=b\) for \(b\in\mathfrak B\) (then \(b_{ij}\) is the scalar \(b\)-entry times \(1\)). \(E\) is positive: for \(X\ge0\) and \(v\in\mathbb C^D\), with \(w=\sum_jv_je_{j1}\), one computes \(\sum_{ij}\bar v_iv_jX_{ij}=\sum_k e_{k1}(w^*Xw)e_{1k}\), a positive element of \(\mathfrak B^c\); so \(\langle v,E(X)v\rangle=\varphi(\cdot)\ge0\). *Hermitian approximation.* Let \(X=\Phi(\Pi_n)\), a projection, nonzero because \(\Phi\) is injective, so \(\|X\|=1\). If \(\|X-b\|\le\delta\) with \(b\in\mathfrak B\), then also \(\|X-b_h\|\le\delta\) for \(b_h=(b+b^*)/2\), since \(X\) is Hermitian. Write \(X=b_h+R\) with \(R=R^*\), \(\|R\|\le\delta\). From \(-\delta\le R\le\delta\) and positivity, \(\|E(R)\|\le\delta\). So \(\|X-E(X)\|=\|R-E(R)\|\le2\delta\), and \(Q_n:=E(\Phi(\Pi_n))\ge0\) has \(\|Q_n\|\ge1-2\delta\). *Counting.* For distinct indices \(n_1,\dots,n_N\) of this kind, \(\sum_k\Pi_{n_k}\) is a projection, so \(\Phi\) of it is a projection \(\le1\), and \(\sum_kQ_{n_k}\le E(1)=1\). In \(M_D\), \(\operatorname{Tr}Q\ge\|Q\|\) for \(Q\ge0\). Hence \(N(1-2\delta)\le\sum_k\operatorname{Tr}Q_{n_k}\le\operatorname{Tr}1=D\). □

**Corollary R1 (no ZM1-INV readout from group-valued links).** Let \(G\) be a compact group whose image in the link representation is infinite, and let \(LGT_G\) be any lattice gauge theory whose link Hilbert space is \(L^2(G)\) with gauge transformations acting by left and right translation, and whose quasi-local algebra contains the bounded operators of each link (Kogut–Susskind for \(U(1)\) and \(SU(2)\) are examples). Let \(L\) be any law with finite-dimensional site and link registers (PS-5 or `LW1.md` sites; PS-6r). Then there is no readout \(\Phi:\mathfrak A_{1}^{G}\to\mathfrak A^{G}\) of ZM1-INV form. The obstruction is present at every zoom rung and for every time step, state and comparison class.

*Proof.* Fix a link \(\ell\). By Peter–Weyl, \(L^2(G)=\bigoplus_\pi V_\pi\otimes V_\pi^*\) over infinitely many irreducible \(\pi\) when \(G\) is infinite. The projection \(Q_\pi\) onto the \(\pi\)-block is convolution by the character, which commutes with left and right translations; so each \(Q_\pi\) is gauge invariant, supported on \(\ell\), and the \(Q_\pi\) are mutually orthogonal and nonzero. For \(G=U(1)\) these are the spectral projections \(\mathbf 1\{E_\ell=n\}\), \(n\in\mathbb Z\). ZM1-INV's exponential locality, in the form of `ZM1.md` (Z-a) with constants depending on the support radius only, gives for the radius-zero input set at \(\ell\) a finite region \(\Lambda_r\) with \(\operatorname{dist}(\Phi(B),\mathfrak A(\Lambda_r))\le Ce^{-r/\xi}\|B\|\) for every \(B\) supported on \(\ell\). The local algebra \(\mathfrak A(\Lambda_r)\) of a finite region of a finite-register law is a full matrix algebra \(M_D\) (finitely many CAR modes and finite links). Choose \(r\) with \(Ce^{-r/\xi}<\tfrac12\) and apply Theorem R with \(\mathcal C\) the C*-algebra generated by the \(Q_\pi\). □

*Remark (smaller target algebras).* The proof of Theorem R uses only that the \(\Pi_n\) are mutually orthogonal positive elements of norm one: for such elements \(\|\sum_{k\le N}\Pi_{n_k}\|=\max_k\|\Pi_{n_k}\|\le1\) (they commute and have disjointly supported Gelfand transforms), and \(\Phi\) is isometric. So the corollary also holds if the target's algebra at a link is only generated by one unitary \(e^{i\theta E_\ell}\) with \(\theta/2\pi\) irrational, whose C*-algebra \(C(S^1)\) has no nontrivial projections: bump functions with disjoint supports supply the elements. If the algebra contains \(e^{i\theta E_\ell}\) for all \(\theta\in2\pi\mathbb Q\), it contains the indicator functions of \(E_\ell\equiv2^{j-1}\bmod2^j\), \(j\ge1\), which are mutually orthogonal projections.

For a finite group (\(\mathbb Z_2\)) \(L^2(G)\) is finite-dimensional and nothing is excluded, as section 2 found. The same argument excludes a cell-covariant exponentially local readout whose target is a continuum theory with infinite-dimensional local algebras; that is not needed here.

*Scope.* Corollary R1 excludes a ZM1-INV readout of the whole invariant algebra. It does not exclude: identification of finitely many correlators in one state; approximate statements whose error grows with the flux or representation label, as in Theorem U1-LS below; or a readout from a finite truncation of \(LGT_G\) (a quantum link model), as in Theorem U1-QL below. **The first failing step of the exact U(1) bridge is the existence of the readout \(\Phi\).** It fails before dynamics enters, and before the charged-link step where Theorem N would fail.

### 3.2 Quantum-link equality

**The law `L_cube^cf[S]`.** `L_cube` (`LW1.md` §2, spin-\(S\) links, \(J_\ell=S^+_\ell\), \(E_\ell=S^z_\ell\), dressed hop \(c_y^\dagger J_\ell c_x+\mathrm{h.c.}\), \(W_E=\prod_\ell e^{-i\tau_EE_\ell^2}\)) with the plaquette class, open in `LW1.md`, filled by the tiling-cube layer \(W_B=\prod_{C\in\mathcal T_o}e^{-i\tau_BB_C}\prod_{C\in\mathcal T_e}e^{-i\tau_BB_C}\), \(B_C=\sum_{p\subset C}(J_p+J_p^\dagger)\), the `L_cube` analogue of `GA1_5.md`'s \(W^{cube}\) with \(\theta_e=\theta_o=\tau_B\). Free data: \(S,\varepsilon,\mu,\tau_E,\tau_B,\omega_0\). Write \(\mathcal P^{cf}\) for the faces of tiling cubes: a plaquette in the \((i,j)\) plane with base \(x\) is a face of an even (odd) tiling cube exactly when \(x_i,x_j\) are both even (both odd). So \(\mathcal P^{cf}\) is a checkerboard half of the plaquettes in each plane.

**Theorem U1-QL.** For all free data:
1. `L_cube^cf[S]` is admissible: every layer commutes with every \(\mathcal G_x\) (`LW1.md` B4); the update is cell covariant; it has finite nearest-neighbour depth under NN-a read by R-NN1.
2. *(Algebra level.)* Its tick is the time step of the Trotterized U(1) quantum link model \(QLM_{U(1)}^{cf}[S]\) with one fermion: link space \(\mathbb C^{2S+1}\), link operator \(U_\ell=S^+_\ell\) (Chandrasekharan–Wiese Eq. (3.16)), Gauss generator \(\mathcal G_x=n_x+\sum_{\text{out}}E_\ell-\sum_{\text{in}}E_\ell\), \(H_E=\sum_\ell E_\ell^2\), \(H_B^{cf}=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P^{cf}}(J_p+J_p^\dagger)\), and \(U=e^{-i\tau_BH_B^{cf}}e^{-i\tau_EH_E}e^{ia_oH_o}e^{-ia_eH_e}\). The identity is a ZM1-INV readout with \(\alpha\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\alpha\).
3. *(Sector level.)* With \(\mathfrak A_1\) the link algebra and \(\alpha_1=\operatorname{Ad}(e^{-i\tau_BH_B^{cf}}e^{-i\tau_EH_E})\), the inclusion of the pure-QLM invariant algebra (the commutant of the \(\mathcal G^0_x=\sum_{\rm out}E_\ell-\sum_{\rm in}E_\ell\)) satisfies Theorem Z2-C's items 1-4 verbatim, with \(\mathcal G_x=n_x+\mathcal G^0_x\) in place of \(G_x=P_xG^0_x\); in item 4 every site carries a static charge.
4. *(Not algebra level.)* For \(a_e\) outside a discrete set, the inclusion of item 3 does not intertwine \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha_1\).

*Proof.* 1. B4 covers dressed edges and plaquette monomials. Covariance: `LW1.md`'s gauged covariance proof ends "The same covariance conclusion holds for an additional \(W_B\) if that entire layer is cell covariant." \(\mathscr G\) preserves each tiling (A2); a lift maps \(J_p\) to \(J_{gp}\) or \(J_{gp}^\dagger\) (a reversed link is handled by \(e^{-i\pi S^x}\), which sends \(J\) to \(J^\dagger\)), so \(B_C\mapsto B_{gC}\), and site sign gauges fix link-only operators. Depth: the matter and electric layers as in Theorem Z2-A.3; each \(e^{-i\tau_BB_C}\) is a unitary on the twelve links of \(C\), a connected set, so Lemma U1 compiles it with sites of \(C\); cubes of one tiling have disjoint sites and links, so the words run in parallel (`GA1_5.md` Proposition C(i), last sentences). 2. Proposition F3(b),(d). 3. The proof of Theorem Z2-C uses only Proposition F3(a) and E1. 4. As Proposition Z2-D, with \(E_\ell\) in place of \(X_\ell\): \(i[H_C,E_\ell]=\tfrac{i\eta}{\sqrt3}(-c_y^\dagger J_\ell c_x+c_x^\dagger J_\ell^\dagger c_y)\ne0\) is not link-only. □

*Remark (why cube faces).* Each link lies in two cube faces and two mixed plaquettes (`GA1_5.md` Theorem 2.1). Mixed plaquettes form one orbit of \(\mathscr G\), so a cell-invariant sublayer containing one single-plaquette gate on a mixed plaquette contains all of them. Two mixed plaquettes that share a link have non-commuting \(B_p\), because \([J_\ell,J_\ell^\dagger]=2E_\ell\ne0\). GA1-P-5 requires the gates of a sublayer to commute pairwise, so single-plaquette gates on mixed plaquettes are not in the class; `GA1_5.md` Theorem 2.2 excludes disjoint supports that contain a mixed plaquette. Whether another member of the class gives the full plaquette sum is not decided here. In each plane the cube faces are a checkerboard, so the classical small-field energy of \(H_B^{cf}\) is half the full plaquette sum's, isotropically.

### 3.3 A large-register limit with a rate

The free datum \(S\) of `L_cube` indexes a family of laws. Pure gauge, integer \(S\ge1\), a finite periodic box with \(N_C\) tiling cubes. Normalize \(\tilde J=J/c_S\), \(c_S=\sqrt{S(S+1)}\) (a choice of the plaquette angle \(\tau_B/c_S^4\) in units of \(B_C\)). Compare \(U_Q=\prod_Cg^Q_C\,e^{-i\tau_EH_E}\), \(g^Q_C=e^{-i\tau_B\sum_{p\subset C}(\tilde J_p+\tilde J_p^\dagger)}\), with the Kogut–Susskind cube-face tick \(U_W\) on \(\ell^2(\mathbb Z)\) links, \(g^W_C=e^{-i\tau_B\sum_{p\subset C}(U_p+U_p^\dagger)}\), \(U_\ell|k\rangle=|k+1\rangle\). Let \(\iota|m\rangle=|m\rangle\) embed each link (an isometry with \(\iota E=E\iota\)), \(P_{\le M}\) project onto configurations with all \(|E_\ell|\le M\), \(\tau=|\tau_B|\), and \(t_K=e^{16\tau}(16\tau)^{K+1}/(K+1)!\).

**Theorem U1-LS.** Let \(n,M,K\ge1\) with \(M+(n+1)K+1\le S/4\), and let \(\psi=P_{\le M}\psi\) be a unit vector. Then
\[
\|\iota U_Q^n\psi-U_W^n\iota\psi\|\le nN_C\Big[\frac{60\,\tau e^{16\tau}(M+(n+1)K)^2}{S(S+1)}+2t_K\Big]+n^2N_Ct_K .
\]
With \(K=\lceil(\log S)^2\rceil\), for fixed \(n,M,\tau_B\) and box, the right side is \(O((\log S)^4/S^2)\).

*Proof.* *(i) Coefficients.* \(\tilde J|m\rangle=\kappa_+(m)|m+1\rangle\), \(\tilde J^\dagger|m\rangle=\kappa_-(m)|m-1\rangle\), \(\kappa_\pm(m)=\sqrt{1-m(m\pm1)/(S(S+1))}\). For \(|m|\le M'\le S-1\), \(m(m\pm1)\in[-\tfrac14,M'(M'+1)]\), and \(1-\sqrt{1-x}\le x\), \(\sqrt{1+y}-1\le y/2\) give \(|\kappa_\pm-1|\le\epsilon:=(M'+1)^2/(S(S+1))\). *(ii) One application.* \(\tilde J_p\) and \(U_p\) send the configuration \(m\) to the same configuration \(m'\), injectively; the coefficient of \(\tilde J_p\) is a product of four link factors, each evaluated on its own link, so \(|\prod\kappa-1|\le(1+\epsilon)^4-1\le5\epsilon\) when \(\epsilon\le1/16\). Summing twelve terms: \(\|(\iota B^Q_C-B^W_C\iota)\chi\|\le60\epsilon\|\chi\|\) for \(\chi\) with fluxes \(\le M'\) on \(C\)'s links. *(iii) Norms and tails.* \(\|B^W_C\|\le12\), \(\|B^Q_C\|\le12(1+\tfrac1{8S(S+1)})^4\le16\). Each application of \(B_C\) changes each link flux by at most one. Expanding \(e^{-i\tau_BB}\) to order \(K\) leaves a remainder of norm \(\le t_K\), and the kept terms raise fluxes by at most \(K\). *(iv) One gate.* For \(\chi=P^C_{\le M'}\chi\) with \(M'+K\le S/4\): by telescoping \((B^Q)^k-(B^W)^k\) and (ii)-(iii), \(\|\iota(B^Q)^k\chi-(B^W)^k\iota\chi\|\le k16^{k-1}60(M'+K)^2/(S(S+1))\) for \(k\le K\); summing the series gives \(\|\iota g^Q_C\chi-g^W_C\iota\chi\|\le\gamma(M'):=60\tau e^{16\tau}(M'+K)^2/(S(S+1))+2t_K\). *(v) Layers and ticks.* The electric layers are diagonal and equal under \(\iota\). Cubes have disjoint links, so a gate on one cube does not change fluxes on another; telescoping over the cubes gives \(\|(\iota W^Q_B-W^W_B\iota)\chi\|\le N_C\gamma(M')\) for \(\chi=P_{\le M'}\chi\), and (iii) gives \(\|(1-P_{\le M+(t+1)K})U_Q\phi\|\le\|(1-P_{\le M+tK})\phi\|+N_Ct_K\), so the flux leak after \(t\) ticks is at most \(tN_Ct_K\). Telescoping over ticks, \(\|\iota U_Q^n\psi-U_W^n\iota\psi\|\le\sum_{t<n}\big(N_C\gamma(M+tK)+2tN_Ct_K\big)\), which is the bound. For the rate, \((16\tau)^{K+1}/(K+1)!\le(16e\tau/(K+1))^{K+1}\le e^{-(\log S)^2}\) once \(K+1\ge16e^2\tau\). □

On \(\mathbb Z^3\) the same bound holds for a local observable, with \(N_C\) the number of cubes in its backward light cone, because both ticks have the same finite-depth geometry. The bound is not uniform in \(M\), and cannot be: at flux \(S\), \(\tilde J|S\rangle=0\) while \(U|S\rangle=|S+1\rangle\), the norm-one defect recorded in `GA2_Y2.md` §2 after Corollary 2.2. It compares one family of laws with Kogut–Susskind at fixed angles; it is not a property of one law. For `L5-U` the register is fixed (\(1\le m\le9\)), so there is no such family inside that law (`GA2_Y2.md` §5, row (a1)). The main-branch Peter–Weyl notes prove the analogous statement for an SU(3) cutoff with electric energy in place of flux, and say "No claim of a uniform all-energy fixed-R approximation follows". The counting step of Theorem R says why such a claim cannot be added for a positive dictionary: if \(\Psi\) is a positive linear map from the flux projections into \(M_D\) with \(\Psi(\sum_k\Pi_{n_k})\le1\), at most \(D/(1-2\delta)\) of the \(\Psi(\Pi_n)\) have norm at least \(1-2\delta\).

### 3.4 The D-theory route

D-theory obtains a \(d\)-dimensional gauge theory from a \((d+1)\)-dimensional quantum link model whose extra Euclidean direction is small; Chandrasekharan–Wiese's abstract: "When formulated with a fifth Euclidean dimension, universality arguments suggest that dimensional reduction to four dimensions occurs." Four-dimensional Yang–Mills therefore needs a quantum link model on a four-dimensional spatial lattice. **First failing step: the dimension count.** The Lattice axiom gives \(\mathbb Z^3\); with one time direction there are four directions, and reduction by one leaves three (`LIT_QLM.md` §1: "Reducing the repo's three spatial directions plus time by one direction would leave three Euclidean dimensions"). A fourth spatial index (a layer label) is extra structure; `GA2_Y2.md` §5's row (a2) records its four-test result (tests 1 and 4 fail). The reduction itself is, in `LIT_QLM.md`'s classification, a "physics-level effective-action and universality argument, not a rigorous correlator convergence theorem".

### 3.5 Outcome for U(1)

The strongest true statements found are: **exact quantum-link equality** (Theorem U1-QL: algebra level with the fermion; sector level for the pure quantum link model) and a **large-register approximation with a rate** over the family `L_cube^cf[S]`, for flux-bounded states and finitely many ticks (Theorem U1-LS). The exact Kogut–Susskind bridge fails at its first step, the readout (Corollary R1). The D-theory route fails at the dimension count.

## 4. GB1-SU2: SU(2)

**4.1 The exact Kogut–Susskind bridge.** Corollary R1 applies with \(G=SU(2)\): the projections onto the spin-\(j\) blocks \(V_j\otimes V_j^*\) of \(L^2(SU(2))\), \(j=0,\tfrac12,1,\dots\), are gauge invariant, mutually orthogonal and infinitely many. No ZM1-INV readout from the Kogut–Susskind SU(2) theory into either five-mode law exists, at any rung. The charged-link step also fails (`GA2_Y2.md` Corollary Y2-0(ii), stated there for T0's SU(2) slice space).

**4.2 The quantum-link bridge inside `L5-U`, through Proposition T.** `LW1_5.md` §4 (Theorem U and the paragraph after it): "Every closed subgroup, in particular the Standard Model \(SU(2)\), has the restricted Gauss law at every angle in either law. For \(L5\)-U the link **operators** \(U^{jk}\) decompose into a \((2,2)\) block, two doublet types and singlet blocks under its two endpoint \(SU(2)\)'s." Take SU(2) on modes \(\{4,5\}\) (the block \(\mathsf B\)); call modes \(\{1,2,3\}\) spectators. Use `L5-U[m]` with the tiling-cube layer (`GA1_5.md` \(W^{cube}\), angles \(\theta_e,\theta_o\)); `GA1_5.md` Proposition C(i) and Amendment 1 make it NN-a. On link \(\ell=(x,y)\): \(U^{jk}_\ell=r^{\ell,y}_jr^{\ell,x\dagger}_k\), end numbers \(n^{(j)}_{\ell,v}=r^{\ell,v\dagger}_jr^{\ell,v}_j\), block end number \(N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,v}=n^{(4)}_{\ell,v}+n^{(5)}_{\ell,v}\). Let \(\mathfrak B_2\) be the C*-algebra generated, link by link, by the block entries \(U^{ab}_\ell\), \(a,b\in\mathsf B\), and the block end bilinears \(r^{\ell,v\dagger}_ar^{\ell,v}_b\). A plaquette monomial is one term of \(W(\partial p)\): one entry per link. At each corner \(v\) of \(p\) the trace contracts one index, which is annihilated on one link end at \(v\) and created on the other (this is the gauge-invariance computation of `GA1_5.md` Theorem 3.1).

**Lemma S (spectators drop out at frozen points).** If at a corner \(v\) a spectator mode \(j\) is empty on both link ends of \(p\) at \(v\), or full on both, every monomial with corner index \(j\) at \(v\) annihilates the vector. (This is the mechanism of `GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 4.2 (S), "A colour occupied at all ends of a point, or empty at all ends, drops out of its point operator".)

*Proof.* The monomial is a tensor product over the four links. Its factor at one end annihilates mode \(j\) (zero if empty); its factor at the other end creates it (zero if full). □

**Theorem SU2-C (sector-level equality with the U(2) quantum link model).** Fix \(1\le m\le9\) and a set \(J\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), \(k=|J|\), with \(m'=m-2k\in\{1,2,3\}\) (such \(J\) exist for every \(m\)). Let \(\mathcal S_J\) be the states with \(\omega(N_x)=0\) at every site, and \(\omega(n^{(j)}_{\ell,v})=1\) for \(j\in J\), \(=0\) for \(j\in\{1,2,3\}\setminus J\), at every link end. Put \(H^{(2)}_E=\sum_\ell(N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,x}+k-m/2)^2\), \(H^{(2)}_B=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P^{cf}}(W^{\mathsf B}(\partial p)+W^{\mathsf B}(\partial p)^\dagger)\), where \(W^{\mathsf B}\) is the trace over block indices only, and \(\alpha_2=\operatorname{Ad}(e^{-i\theta H^{(2)}_B}e^{-i\tau_EH^{(2)}_E})\) (take \(\theta_e=\theta_o=\theta\)). Then:
1. \(\alpha_2\) is an automorphism of \(\mathfrak B_2\) and of its \(SU(2)\)-invariant part \(\mathfrak B_2^{SU(2)}\); on the summand where every link holds \(m'\) block rishons it is the Trotterized \(U(2)\) rishon quantum link model with cube faces (Brower–Chandrasekharan–Wiese's rishon form with \(N=2\)), electric term \((N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,x}-m'/2)^2\).
2. The inclusion \(\Phi:\mathfrak B_2^{SU(2)}\to\mathfrak A^{SU(2)}\) is a unital injective strictly local cell-covariant *-homomorphism into the invariant algebra of Proposition T's restricted Gauss law.
3. For every \(\omega\in\mathcal S_J\), \(B\in\mathfrak B_2\) and \(t\ge0\): \(\omega(\alpha^t(B))=\omega(\alpha_2^t(B))\).

*Proof.* 1. \(H^{(2)}_E,H^{(2)}_B\) are sums of commuting local elements of \(\mathfrak B_2\) (block plaquettes on distinct cubes act on disjoint links; within a cube one exponential), commute with the block \(SU(2)\) Gauss generators, and preserve each link's block number. 2. For \(T\in\mathfrak{su}(2)\) on the block modes, \(\mathcal G^T_x=c_x^\dagger Tc_x+\sum_{\ell\ni x}r^{\ell,x\dagger}Tr^{\ell,x}\); link operators commute with the matter term, so an element of \(\mathfrak B_2\) invariant under the block \(SU(2)\) commutes with \(\mathcal G^T_x\). Cell covariance: the lifts of `LW1_5.md` B1-U send "every rishon mode at its \(z\)-end to the same flavour at the \(gz\)-end of the image link", so they preserve \(\mathfrak B_2\), its \(SU(2)\)-invariant part, and each class \(\mathcal S_J\). 3. On a finite region containing the light cone, \(\omega\) is a density matrix supported on vectors with empty matter and frozen spectators (the same kernel argument as Theorem Z2-C). On such vectors: matter gates act trivially (Proposition F3(a)); by Lemma S every monomial of \(B_C\) with a spectator index annihilates them, so \(B_C\) acts as its block part and maps them to vectors of the same kind; \(p_{\ell,x}=N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,x}+k\), so \(W_E\) acts as \(e^{-i\tau_EH^{(2)}_E}\). Conclude as in Theorem Z2-C. □

**Proposition SU2-B (outside frozen states the block does not evolve on its own).** For each \(m\) there is a discrete set \(D_m\ni0\) such that for \(\theta_e\notin D_m\) (all other constants arbitrary): there are two matter-empty states \(\omega,\omega'\) equal on \(\mathfrak B_2\) with \(\omega(\alpha(N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,v}))\ne\omega'(\alpha(N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,v}))\), for \(\ell\) in an even cube \(C\). Hence no dynamics on \(\mathfrak B_2\), and no readout through the inclusion of \(\mathfrak B_2\), reproduces \(\alpha\) on all matter-empty states; algebra-level equality fails.

*Proof.* On matter-empty states \(\alpha\) acts on link operators as \(\operatorname{Ad}(W_BW_E)\) (the argument of Theorem Z2-C, item 2), so \(\omega(\alpha(N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,v}))=\omega(X)\) for every matter-empty \(\omega\), where \(X=W_E^*f(\theta_e)W_E\) and \(f(\theta)=e^{i\theta B_C}N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,v}e^{-i\theta B_C}\) (other cubes' gates commute with \(N^{\mathsf B}\)). Decompose operators on \(C\)'s links by their charges under the commuting family \(\operatorname{Ad}n^{(j)}_{\ell',w}\), \(j\le3\). Every element of \(\mathfrak B_2\) has charge zero. \(\operatorname{Ad}W_E\) preserves each charge component, since \(W_E\) is a function of end numbers. \(f'(0)=i[B_C,N^{\mathsf B}_{\ell,v}]=\sum_t\pm i\,t\) over the monomials \(t\) of the plaquettes of \(C\) through \(\ell\) whose corner index at \(v\) is in \(\mathsf B\). Among them are monomials with a spectator index at another corner; these have nonzero spectator charge. Distinct monomials are linearly independent (tensor products of linearly independent single-link entries, each nonzero on \(\Lambda^m\mathbb C^{10}\) for \(1\le m\le9\)). So the nonzero-charge part \(g(\theta)\) of \(f(\theta)\) is entire, \(g(0)=0\), \(g'(0)\ne0\), and its zero set \(D_m\) is discrete. For \(\theta_e\notin D_m\), \(X\) is a self-adjoint operator on \(C\)'s links outside \(\mathfrak B_2\). Let \(X_\perp\ne0\) be its Hilbert–Schmidt component orthogonal to the unital *-algebra \(\mathfrak B_2|_C\); it is self-adjoint and traceless. Take \(\rho=1/D\) and \(\rho'=1/D+sX_\perp\) (\(s>0\) small) on \(C\)'s links, the same factor elsewhere, and empty matter. They agree on \(\mathfrak B_2\), and \(\operatorname{tr}((\rho'-\rho)X)=s\|X_\perp\|_2^2\ne0\). □

**Lemma SU2-O (no freezing on `L5-O`).** On `L5-O`, each link entry \(U^{ab}=i\eta^y_a\eta^x_b\) is a Hermitian unitary (`GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 3.4: \((U^{ab})^2=1\)). So every plaquette monomial is unitary and annihilates no nonzero vector; Lemma S has no analogue, and the route of Theorem SU2-C fails at its first step on `L5-O`. □

**4.3 Outcome for SU(2).** The exact Kogut–Susskind bridge fails at the readout (Corollary R1), as for U(1). Inside `L5-U`, through Proposition T, the strongest statement proved is sector level: in matter-empty states with frozen spectator rishons the block evolves exactly as the Trotterized \(U(2)\) rishon quantum link model (Theorem SU2-C). Its gauge group is \(U(2)\), not \(SU(2)\): the determinant terms that Brower–Chandrasekharan–Wiese add to reach \(SU(N)\) are absent, and the extra \(U(1)\) is part of the law's exact \(U(5)\). Outside frozen states, and at the algebra level, the step "the \(SU(2)\) block evolves on its own" fails (Proposition SU2-B). Whether VC1's vacuum lies in some \(\mathcal S_J\) is VC1's question; `LW1_5.md` Theorem F-U already says that no pure, locally gauge-invariant, site-transitive state is half filled, and \(\mathcal S_J\) has empty matter, so PS-7r's half-filled sea is not in \(\mathcal S_J\).

**The first rung whose proof fails.** For the exact group-valued bridge: rung U(1), at the readout (Corollary R1). For the quantum-link bridge at sector level: no rung fails (`Z_2`: Theorem Z2-C; U(1): Theorem U1-QL; SU(2): Theorem SU2-C, with target group \(U(2)\)). For the quantum-link bridge at algebra level, without matter: every rung fails at "the gauge block evolves on its own" (Propositions Z2-D, U1-QL.4, SU2-B).
