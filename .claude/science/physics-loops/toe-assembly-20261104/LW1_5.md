## 1. Definitions

Use the lattice, tilings, spatial group, signs, time convention and quasi-local algebra of `LW1.md` §1. Each site has five CAR modes, with
\[
 c_j=(\gamma_{2j-1}+i\gamma_{2j})/2,\quad N=\sum_jc_j^\dagger c_j,\quad P=(-1)^N=(-i)^5\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_{10},\quad M^{ab}=\tfrac i2\gamma_a\gamma_b.
\]
Then \(n_j-1/2=M^{2j-1,2j}\) and
\([M^{ab},\gamma_c]=i(\delta_{bc}\gamma_a-\delta_{ac}\gamma_b)\).
The site algebra is \(M_{32}(\mathbb C)\), with even and odd Fock spaces of dimension 16. Links commute with matter and are ordinary tensor factors. A site frame fixes the algebra of every other site and every link pointwise and maps the algebra of its site onto itself (B5B Definition F). A register change is conjugation by a unitary on finitely many links. A frame is compatible with a generator or gate if some register change makes their combined action fix it. Gate-wise compatibility asks this separately for every gate.

For a finite Hermitian \(H\), let \(\mathcal E(H)=\{a:a(\lambda-\lambda')\in2\pi\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\ \lambda\ne\lambda'\in\operatorname{spec}H\}\). The set meets every bounded interval finitely. The finite and infinite transport, record and probability conventions are those of `LW1.md` §1, with the relevant finite site alphabet and its spectral projectors. Gauge invariance means invariance under conjugation, not necessarily a zero Gauss-charge sector.

## 3. Site frames and hop bounds

**C and C1.** Site frames form
\[
G_{\rm full}=((U(16)\times U(16))/U(1))\rtimes\mathbb Z_2,\qquad \dim G_{\rm full}=511.
\]
The Bogoliubov subgroup is \(O(10)\); its number-preserving subgroup is \(U(5)\). For one mode, \(G_{\rm full}=O(2)=U(1)\rtimes\mathbb Z_2\).

*Proof.* A frame preserves the CAR grading: it fixes an odd mode at another site, so it preserves whether a site element commutes or anticommutes with that mode. Every automorphism of a matrix algebra is inner, and grading preservation makes its implementer homogeneous. Conversely a homogeneous implementer extends as a frame. Even unitaries are block diagonal on \(H_+\oplus H_-\); odd ones exchange the blocks. Quotient by common scalar phases. A real orthogonal change of the ten Majoranas obeys the same Clifford relations, hence extends uniquely to an automorphism. Its action on the volume element sends \(P\) to \(\det(R)P\). Commutation with the complex structure on the Majoranas gives exactly \(U(5)\). The one-mode case has one-dimensional parity blocks. ∎

**C2.** The fixed site algebra of \(SO(10)\) is \(\operatorname{span}\{1,P\}\); that of \(O(10)\) or \(G_{\rm full}\) is \(\mathbb C1\). The fixed algebra of \(U(5)\) is spanned by the six spectral projections of \(N\).

*Proof.* The quadratic Majoranas generate the even Clifford algebra, which by dimension is \(\operatorname{End}(H_+)\oplus\operatorname{End}(H_-)\). Its commutant is the two block scalars. A reflection exchanges the parity blocks. For \(U(5)\), each \(\Lambda^k\mathbb C^5\) is irreducible: diagonal number differences separate occupation basis vectors and \(c_i^\dagger c_j\) connects all \(k\)-subsets. Distinct \(k\) have distinct central charges. ∎

**M and M.1.** Suppose a cube generator has faithful one-mode inflow at \(x\): \(H=\sum_b\gamma_{x,b}B_b+H'\), where the odd \(B_b\) lie outside \(x\) and are linearly independent, and \(H'\) is even and outside \(x\). Every compatible frame is Bogoliubov. If \(H\) also conserves total matter number, it is in \(U(5)\). The same holds for a compatible gate \(e^{iaH}\) when \(a\notin\mathcal E(H)\).

*Proof.* Order the modes at \(x\) first in a Jordan–Wigner representation. The odd site slices are \(\gamma_bP_x\), with independent outside coefficients. A link unitary preserves their independence. Equality of the generator and its transformed image forces each transformed \(\gamma_b\) into their span. Hermiticity and the CAR make the coefficient matrix real orthogonal. In the number-conserving case project onto outside number charge \(-1\). It isolates \(\sum_j c^\dagger_{x,j}X_j\), with independent \(X_j\); the \(B_b\) and the pairs \((X_j,X^\sharp_j)\) are related by an invertible change of basis (referee closure, LW1_5_REFEREE.md), so the same argument preserves the creation span. The CAR make the resulting matrix unitary. Outside \(\mathcal E(H)\), a polynomial interpolates \(H\) from \(e^{iaH}\), so a gate symmetry fixes \(H\). ∎

For the specified hops, independence follows by isolating one neighbour and one link. For \(L5\)-O the 100 mixed generators are independent in every nontrivial representation because \(\mathfrak{so}(20)\) is simple. For \(L5\)-U the \(r^y_jr^{x\dagger}_k\) are independent on every sector \(1\le m\le9\): each has a distinct nonzero occupation-basis transition. Hence M applies.

**Exceptional angles.** If a frame is compatible with both cube gates, it satisfies the bound unless both angles are exceptional. Their intersection with the parameter window is a finite set, since each cube generator is finite dimensional and both angles lie in bounded intervals. Pass 1 reports enlarged compatible algebras in smaller single-link analogues, including a two-mode \(U\) link at \(m=2,a=\pi/\sqrt2\); these are numerical checks, not proofs of the cube case. **LW1-5-X** asks about the remaining points. **LW1-5-T** asks about a symmetry of the whole update; generator interpolation does not classify it.

## 4. Exact gauge transformations

**Q and G.** On an \(L5\)-O link, \(L_y^{cd}=T_{cd}\), \(L_x^{cd}=T_{10+c,10+d}\), and
\[
 [L_x^{cd},U^{ab}]=i(\delta_{db}U^{ac}-\delta_{cb}U^{ad}),\qquad
 [L_y^{cd},U^{ab}]=i(\delta_{da}U^{cb}-\delta_{ca}U^{db}).
\]
Every hop and cube gate commutes with every \(\mathcal G_x^{cd}\). The local transformations implement \(SO(10)\) on site algebras and a representation of \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) on site and link-end state spaces.

*Proof.* The displayed brackets follow from the Clifford relations. In \([\mathcal G_x^{cd},\sum_b U^{ab}\gamma_{x,b}]\), the two terms cancel after relabelling. The same holds at the other end; disjoint supports commute. Exponentiate. The rotations generated by \(M^{cd}\) span \(SO(10)\), and the Lie algebra representation integrates to its simply connected cover. The specified electric operator commutes with both end algebras. ∎

**R.** For an irreducible nontrivial register, reflection is compatible with a cube generator exactly when \(R\simeq R^\sigma\), the twist by reflection at one end. This is true for the vector register and false for \(S_+\). Hence for \(S_+\), the generator-level site frame group is \(SO(10)\), and the gate-wise group is that group outside the exceptional set. All its elements are symmetries at every angle.

*Proof.* An intertwiner of \(R\) with its reflected twist, placed on each incident link, cancels the matter reflection. Conversely, isolate the matter Majorana at each neighbour: invariance forces the link unitary to implement the reflected action on every \(U^{ab}\). Their commutators generate \(\mathfrak{so}(20)\). Irreducibility makes the generated matrix algebra full, so this induces an intertwiner. Let \(s\) be the end swap, \(\tilde s\) a lift, and \(Q\) an intertwiner of \(R\) with \(R\circ\sigma\). On a link with \(x\) as target the needed twist is \(\sigma'=\operatorname{Ad}_s\sigma\operatorname{Ad}_s^{-1}\), and \(\rho(\tilde s)Q\rho(\tilde s)^{-1}\) intertwines \(R\) with \(R\circ\sigma'\) (referee closure, LW1_5_REFEREE.md). On \(S_+\), the product of the ten commuting \(2T_{2j-1,2j}\) is chirality \(+1\), but reflection changes its sign, a contradiction. M gives the upper bound. ∎

**U.** For \(L5\)-U, every \(\mathcal G_x^T\) commutes with every hop and electric gate. Its generator-level site frame group is exactly \(U(5)\), also gate-wise outside the exceptional set.

*Proof.* At each end, the two summands in the Gauss generator transform a matter operator and a rishon operator oppositely. Thus each contracted end factor, and hence the hop, is invariant. End numbers commute with the Gauss generators. M gives the upper bound and the explicit action of \(\Gamma(u)\) at the site and on each link end gives the lower bound. ∎

Every closed subgroup, in particular the Standard Model \(SU(2)\), has the restricted Gauss law at every angle in either law. For \(L5\)-U the link **operators** \(U^{jk}\) decompose into a \((2,2)\) block, two doublet types and singlet blocks under its two endpoint \(SU(2)\)'s. With \(SU(2)\) on modes 4 and 5, this follows by splitting each index into \({4,5}\) and \({1,2,3}\); the fundamental and its dual of \(SU(2)\) are equivalent. These extra blocks and the full group remain part of the law. A pure \(SU(2)\) Yang–Mills limit is not established. GA2 needs the corresponding continuum bridge; see `TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` §6.

**Non-Bogoliubov embedding S′.** Let \(S_+(g)\) be the half-spin representation on \(H_+\), and set \(f(g)=\operatorname{Ad}(S_+(g)\oplus1_{H_-})\). This embeds \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) in \(G_{\rm full}\); \(f(-1)=\operatorname{Ad}P\). The operators in \(\operatorname{Hom}(H_-,H_+)\) are 16 copies of the half-spin representation. The faithful one-mode hop excludes this embedding; the full-frame alternative below retains it.

*Proof.* (referee closure, LW1_5_REFEREE.md) Read \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) as the group generated by the \(e^{i\theta M^{ab}}\) on the site space \(H=H_+\oplus H_-\). It is the image of the simply connected group in the site representation, and it is all that S′ uses.

1. *Vector map.* Each generator acts on \(\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\gamma_a\}\) by a plane rotation, by the bracket in §1. So \(g\gamma_ag^{-1}=\sum_bR(g)_{ba}\gamma_b\) with \(R(g)\in SO(10)\), and \(R\) is onto \(SO(10)\). If \(R(g)=I\), then \(g\) commutes with every \(\gamma_a\), so \(g=\lambda1\). **Theorem SC (static spinor class).** (referee closure, LW1_5_REFEREE.md) Take finite link registers with commuting endpoint \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) actions, as in F and K, and a law whose gates commute with every \(\mathcal G^{cd}_x\). (b) Let \(O\) be local. Let \(S\) be a finite site set that contains every site where \(O\) has matter and both ends of every link in its support. For \(T\subseteq S\) put
\[

\]

(c) For \(s\in\{\pm1\}^S\) put \(\Pi^s=\prod_{x\in S}(1+s_xz_x)/2\). Then \(O_T\Pi^s=\Pi^{s\circ T}O_T\), where \(s\circ T\) flips the signs of \(s\) on \(T\).

So \(\omega'\circ\alpha^n\) and \(\omega\circ\alpha^n\) differ in centre class exactly on \(T\) at every tick. A spinor-class charge does not move.

*Proof.* (a) \(z_x\) is a product of commuting unitaries: the site factor \(-1\) and the six end factors \(\epsilon_{\ell,x}\) (as defined above). ## 6. Singlets, operators and filling for \(L5\)-U

Let \(Q_x=N_x+\sum_{\ell\ni x}p_{\ell,x}\). On an even periodic box of \(V\) sites,
\[
 \sum_xQ_x=N_{\rm tot}+3mV. \tag{6.1}
\]
A finite-box vector state is locally \(U(5)\)-invariant exactly when its vector is \(SU(5)\)-invariant and an eigenvector of \(Q_x\); its eigenvalue is a multiple of five. Indeed a vector invariant as a state transforms by a character; \(SU(5)\) has no nontrivial continuous character, and its centre acts as \(e^{2\pi iQ_x/5}\). The converse follows by exponentiation.

**Σ-U.** With the unshifted generators, a zero Gauss-sector vector at \(x\) has empty matter and all six local link ends empty. It exists locally iff \(m\le5\), and not simultaneously at all sites of a periodic box for any \(m\ge1\). Under LW1's conjugation reading, pure locally gauge-invariant box states exist for every \(m=1,\ldots,9\), with site numbers respectively
\[
 n_m=2,4,1,3,0\text{ or }5,2,4,1,3.
\]
They can be chosen translation invariant as states; full point-group invariance and stationarity remain open (LW1-5U-Σc).

*Proof.* \(Q_x=0\) forces every nonnegative summand to vanish; (6.1) rules out all sites at once. For the conjugation reading, products \(\bigotimes_i\Lambda^{a_i}\mathbb C^5\) have an \(SU(5)\) singlet iff \(\sum_i a_i\equiv0\pmod5\): necessity is the centre; sufficiency follows by repeatedly embedding \(\Lambda^{a+b}\) when \(a+b\le5\), or \(\Lambda^{a+b-5}\) using the invariant volume form otherwise. Fix on each oriented link the same admissible end numbers \(\alpha,m-\alpha\). At each site the six ends total \(3m\); the listed \(n_m\) makes \(n_m+3m\) divisible by five. Choose the same local singlet at each site. Its vector state is invariant under local gauge transformations and translations. ∎

**Z-U.** A local operator with definite \(Q_x\) charges has total charge equal to its matter-number change, and its matter parity is \((-1)^{\sum q_x}\). Thus no fermion-odd local operator is invariant under all \(U(5)_x\). At one site the annihilator monomials \(c\), \(ccc\), \(c^5\) transform under \(SU(5)\times U(1)_N\) as \(\overline5_{-1},10_{-3},1_{-5}\); their adjoints give conjugates. The five-annihilator is nevertheless an odd \(SU(5)\) singlet. More generally the odd site algebra is \(\bigoplus_{a-b\;\mathrm{odd}}\Lambda^aV\otimes(\Lambda^bV)^*\).

*Proof.* Sum the \(Q_x\)'s over a finite set containing both ends of every supported link. Every supported link contributes its fixed total \(m\), and other link terms commute with the operator. The sum therefore has the same commutator as total matter number. Parity is number change modulo two. Exterior powers give the listed transformation types and charges. ∎

**H-U and F-U.** Local \(U(5)\) invariance gives site density matrix \(\sum_{k=0}^5 p_k\Pi_k/\binom5k\), with arbitrary probabilities \(p_k\); filling can take any value in \([0,5]\). *Proof.* Schur's lemma and irreducibility of the exterior powers give the marginal; a product of site density matrices and tracial links realizes each mean. Transitivity makes \(q_x=q\) and the mean filling constant. Apply (6.1). The pure-state character argument gives \(q\in5\mathbb Z\). The same sum for \(L_{\rm cube}\) gives integer occupation 0 or 1 for its analogous finite-box pure, gauge-invariant, site-transitive states. Infinite volume is not covered. ∎

**Finite transport, dynamics and records.** On any finite active region, take the mutually orthogonal joint spectral projections \(P_\xi\) of the chosen site values and the active unitary \(W\). This proves B6–B7 for both alphabets and the stipulated site-change cost.

For support factorization, order matter modes cube by cube. Every active gate and each site-value projector is even, so the block for the layer is an ordinary tensor product of the cube blocks and unused link identities. A finite tensor product is nonzero exactly when all factors are nonzero. Active gates outside a finite union commute with its observables and cancel from its Born marginal. The link-only electric gate commutes with all matter projectors and preserves block nonzeroness. Each layer contains disjoint finite gates; conjugation of a local observable involves finitely many of them. The inverse construction and norm completion give the quasi-local automorphism, as in LW1 E1.

For finite edge factorization, the finite cube Hilbert space gives a finite-dimensional Lie algebra generated by the twelve skew-Hermitian edge hops. Let \(H\) be the subgroup of finite products of their one-parameter exponentials and \(L\) its connected analytic group. The span of the adjoint \(H\)-orbits of the generators is their whole Lie algebra: it contains the generators and is closed under their commutators; conversely, \(\operatorname{Ad}_h\), \(h\in H\subseteq L\), preserves the Lie algebra (referee closure, LW1_5_REFEREE.md). A basis of such orbit elements gives a product map with surjective derivative at the identity. The inverse function theorem makes \(H\) open in connected \(L\), so \(H=L\). Each cube exponential is therefore a finite word of even gauge-invariant edge exponentials. This argument uses neither a fixed site dimension nor the false full-universality assertion.

For infinite supported couplings, apply the finite result on increasing unions of active cubes, extend the finite measures arbitrarily, and take a weak limit on the compact product of the finite alphabets. Each fixed marginal and each clopen cube-support condition eventually holds and therefore holds in the limit. For invariant optimizers, the supported invariant couplings form a nonempty compact convex set. The site-change cost is a continuous affine cylinder expectation, so its minimizers form a compact convex face. On dyadic cell boxes, subadditivity makes entropy per cell decrease to its infimum; finite-block entropy is continuous, so this limit is upper semicontinuous. A binary mixture adds at most \(\log2\) entropy per box; dividing by volume proves the limiting entropy is affine. It therefore attains its maximum on the cost-minimizing face, and its maximizers form a nonempty compact convex set. This proves E3–E6 with pair alphabet \(\{\pm1\}^{16}\) or \(\{0,\ldots,5\}^{16}\).
