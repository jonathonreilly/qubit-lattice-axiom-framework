## 1. Definitions

Use the lattice, tilings, spatial group, signs, time convention and quasi-local algebra of `LW1.md` §1. Each site has five CAR modes, with
\[
 c_j=(\gamma_{2j-1}+i\gamma_{2j})/2,\quad N=\sum_jc_j^\dagger c_j,\quad P=(-1)^N=(-i)^5\gamma_1\cdots\gamma_{10},\quad M^{ab}=\tfrac i2\gamma_a\gamma_b.
\]
Then \(n_j-1/2=M^{2j-1,2j}\) and
\([M^{ab},\gamma_c]=i(\delta_{bc}\gamma_a-\delta_{ac}\gamma_b)\).
The site algebra is \(M_{32}(\mathbb C)\), with even and odd Fock spaces of dimension 16. Links commute with matter and are ordinary tensor factors. A site frame fixes the algebra of every other site and every link pointwise. A register change is conjugation by a unitary on finitely many links. A frame is compatible with a generator or gate if some register change makes their combined action fix it. Gate-wise compatibility asks this separately for every gate.

For a finite Hermitian \(H\), let \(\mathcal E(H)=\{a:a(\lambda-\lambda')\in2\pi\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\},\ \lambda\ne\lambda'\in\operatorname{spec}H\}\). The set meets every bounded interval finitely. The finite and infinite transport, record and probability conventions are those of `LW1.md` §1, with the relevant finite site alphabet and its spectral projectors. Gauge invariance means invariance under conjugation, not necessarily a zero Gauss-charge sector.

## 3. Site frames and hop bounds

**C and C1.** Site frames form
\[
G_{\rm full}=((U(16)\times U(16))/U(1))\rtimes\mathbb Z_2,\qquad \dim G_{\rm full}=511.
\]
The Bogoliubov subgroup is \(O(10)\); its number-preserving subgroup is \(U(5)\). For one mode, \(G_{\rm full}=O(2)=U(1)\rtimes\mathbb Z_2\).

*Proof.* A frame preserves the CAR grading: it fixes an odd mode at another site, so it preserves whether a site element commutes or anticommutes with that mode. Every automorphism of a matrix algebra is inner, and grading preservation makes its implementer homogeneous. Conversely a homogeneous implementer extends as a frame. Even unitaries are block diagonal on \(H_+\oplus H_-\); odd ones exchange the blocks. Quotient by common scalar phases. A real orthogonal change of the ten Majoranas obeys the same Clifford relations, hence extends uniquely to an automorphism. Its action on the volume element sends \(P\) to \(\det(R)P\). Commutation with the complex structure on the Majoranas gives exactly \(U(5)\). The one-mode case has one-dimensional parity blocks. ∎

**C2.** The fixed site algebra of \(SO(10)\) is \(\operatorname{span}\{1,P\}\); that of \(O(10)\) or \(G_{\rm full}\) is \(\mathbb C1\). The fixed algebra of \(U(5)\) is spanned by the six spectral projections of \(N\).

*Proof.* The quadratic Majoranas generate the even Clifford algebra, which by dimension is \(\operatorname{End}(H_+)\oplus\operatorname{End}(H_-)\). Its commutant is the two block scalars. A reflection exchanges the parity blocks. For \(U(5)\), each \(\Lambda^k\mathbb C^5\) is irreducible: diagonal number differences separate occupation basis vectors and \(c_i^\dagger c_j\) connects all \(k\)-subsets. Distinct \(k\) have distinct central charges. ∎

**M and M.1.** Suppose a cube generator has faithful one-mode inflow at \(x\): \(H=\sum_b\gamma_{x,b}B_b+H'\), where the odd \(B_b\) lie outside \(x\) and are linearly independent, and \(H'\) is even and outside \(x\). Every compatible frame is Bogoliubov. If \(H\) also conserves total matter number, it is in \(U(5)\). The same holds for a compatible gate \(e^{iaH}\) when \(a\notin\mathcal E(H)\).

*Proof.* Order the modes at \(x\) first in a Jordan–Wigner representation. The odd site slices are \(\gamma_bP_x\), with independent outside coefficients. A link unitary preserves their independence. Equality of the generator and its transformed image forces each transformed \(\gamma_b\) into their span. Hermiticity and the CAR make the coefficient matrix real orthogonal. In the number-conserving case project onto outside number charge \(-1\). It isolates \(\sum_j c^\dagger_{x,j}X_j\), with independent \(X_j\), so the same argument preserves the creation span. The CAR make the resulting matrix unitary. Outside \(\mathcal E(H)\), a polynomial interpolates \(H\) from \(e^{iaH}\), so a gate symmetry fixes \(H\). ∎

For the specified hops, independence follows by isolating one neighbour and one link. For \(L5\)-O the 100 mixed generators are independent in every nontrivial representation because \(\mathfrak{so}(20)\) is simple. For \(L5\)-U the \(r^y_jr^{x\dagger}_k\) are independent on every sector \(1\le m\le9\): each has a distinct nonzero occupation-basis transition. Hence M applies.

**Exceptional angles.** If a frame is compatible with both cube gates, it satisfies the bound unless both angles are exceptional. Their intersection with the parameter window is a finite set, since each cube generator is finite dimensional and both angles lie in bounded intervals. Pass 1 reports enlarged compatible algebras in smaller single-link analogues, including a two-mode \(U\) link at \(m=2,a=\pi/\sqrt2\); these are numerical checks, not proofs of the cube case. **LW1-5-X** asks about the remaining points. **LW1-5-T** asks about a symmetry of the whole update; generator interpolation does not classify it.

## 4. Exact gauge transformations

**Q and G.** On an \(L5\)-O link, \(L_y^{cd}=T_{cd}\), \(L_x^{cd}=T_{10+c,10+d}\), and
\[
 [L_x^{cd},U^{ab}]=i(\delta_{db}U^{ac}-\delta_{cb}U^{ad}),\qquad
 [L_y^{cd},U^{ab}]=i(\delta_{da}U^{cb}-\delta_{ca}U^{db}).
\]
Every hop and cube gate commutes with every \(\mathcal G_x^{cd}\). The local transformations implement \(SO(10)\) on site algebras and a representation of \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) on site and link-end state spaces.

*Proof.* The displayed brackets follow from the Clifford relations. In \([\mathcal G_x^{cd},\sum_b U^{ab}\gamma_{x,b}]\), the two terms cancel after relabelling. The same holds at the other end; disjoint supports commute. Exponentiate. The rotations generated by \(M^{cd}\) span \(SO(10)\), and the Lie algebra representation integrates to its simply connected cover. The specified electric operator commutes with both end algebras. ∎

**R.** For an irreducible nontrivial register, reflection is compatible with a cube generator exactly when \(R\simeq R^\sigma\), the twist by reflection at one end. This is true for the vector register and false for \(S_+\). Hence for \(S_+\), the generator-level site frame group is \(SO(10)\), and the gate-wise group is that group outside the exceptional set. All its elements are symmetries at every angle.

*Proof.* An intertwiner of \(R\) with its reflected twist, placed on each incident link, cancels the matter reflection. Conversely, isolate the matter Majorana at each neighbour: invariance forces the link unitary to implement the reflected action on every \(U^{ab}\). Their commutators generate \(\mathfrak{so}(20)\). Irreducibility makes the generated matrix algebra full, so this induces an intertwiner. On \(S_+\), the product of the ten commuting \(2T_{2j-1,2j}\) is chirality \(+1\), but reflection changes its sign, a contradiction. M gives the upper bound. ∎

**U.** For \(L5\)-U, every \(\mathcal G_x^T\) commutes with every hop and electric gate. Its generator-level site frame group is exactly \(U(5)\), also gate-wise outside the exceptional set.

*Proof.* At each end, the two summands in the Gauss generator transform a matter operator and a rishon operator oppositely. Thus each contracted end factor, and hence the hop, is invariant. End numbers commute with the Gauss generators. M gives the upper bound and the explicit action of \(\Gamma(u)\) at the site and on each link end gives the lower bound. ∎

Every closed subgroup, in particular the Standard Model \(SU(2)\), has the restricted Gauss law at every angle in either law. For \(L5\)-U the link decomposes into \((2,2)\), doublet and singlet blocks under its two endpoint \(SU(2)\)'s. These extra blocks and the full group remain part of the law. A pure \(SU(2)\) Yang–Mills limit is not established. GA2 needs the corresponding continuum bridge; see `TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` §6.

**Non-Bogoliubov embedding S′.** Let \(S_+(g)\) be the half-spin representation on \(H_+\), and set \(f(g)=\operatorname{Ad}(S_+(g)\oplus1_{H_-})\). This embeds \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) in \(G_{\rm full}\); \(f(-1)=\operatorname{Ad}P\). The operators in \(\operatorname{Hom}(H_-,H_+)\) are 16 copies of the half-spin representation. The faithful one-mode hop excludes this embedding; the full-frame alternative below retains it.

*Proof.* The implementer is even. Its lower block makes a scalar implementer possible only when \(S_+(g)=1\); the half-spin representation is faithful because none of the three nonidentity elements of the Spin(10) centre acts trivially. On off-diagonal operators conjugation acts by \(S_+\) on the output alone. A continuous homomorphism \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\to O(10)\) kills \(-1\): a nontrivial one has dimension-45 image \(SO(10)\), and an injective one would identify groups with centres of orders four and two. Thus \(f\) is not Bogoliubov; M gives the hop bound. ∎

**Full-frame alternative A.** A finite-register law can keep all \(G_{\rm full}\): take a 512-element orthonormal basis of odd site operators and a link register with one 512-dimensional slot per end. Contract each odd site basis vector with the conjugate slot representation and hop between slots. The contraction is invariant under every site frame; the hop is even. This records an alternative, not a proposed law. It shows that PS-6 alone does not select \(SO(10)\) or \(U(5)\).

## 5. Centre, singlets and filling for \(L5\)-O

Put \(z_x=e^{2\pi i\mathcal G_x^{12}}\), \(\omega_x=e^{i\pi\sum_j\mathcal G_x^{2j-1,2j}}\), and \(\epsilon_{\ell,x}=e^{2\pi iL_{\ell,x}^{12}}\). The site factors are respectively \(-1\) and \(-iP_x\); \(z_x=-\prod_{\ell\ni x}\epsilon_{\ell,x}\) and \(\omega_x^2=z_x\). For an \(\mathfrak{so}(20)\) link the two \(\epsilon\)'s coincide. These identities follow by exponentiating \(n_j-1/2\) and the commuting end actions.

**F and K.** Under the Bogoliubov action the site algebra is \(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{10}\Lambda^k\mathbb C^{10}\). Odd matter operators carry the odd exterior powers, hence tensor rather than spinor class. The site Fock spaces themselves are the two half-spin representations. For any finite link registers with commuting endpoint Spin(10) actions and any gate commuting with the Gauss generators, \(\operatorname{Ad}z_x\) fixes all matter, and the spectral projections of \(z_x\) are conserved. If each end at \(x\) is end-pure, \(\operatorname{Ad}z_x\) fixes the whole local algebra. If every link is link-pure, a local operator homogeneous under all \(\operatorname{Ad}z_x\) is spinor class at an even number of sites.

*Proof.* Antisymmetrized Clifford multiplication maps \(\Lambda^k\mathbb C^{10}\) equivariantly onto the degree-\(k\) monomials; all such monomials form a basis. The site factor of \(z_x\) is scalar, so it acts trivially on matter. A gate commuting with the generators commutes with their exponentials. End-purity makes every link factor scalar. For pairing, multiply the \(z_x\)'s over a finite set containing the operator's support and both ends of its supported links. Internal link products are scalar; boundary factors act outside the support. Their adjoint action is identity, so an even number of centre eigenvalues can be negative. ∎

**Mixed registers.** On \(R=S_+\oplus\mathbb C^{20}\), an off-diagonal operator \(X=|v\rangle\langle s|\) changes the Spin(20) centre eigenvalue. It is spinor class at both ends; \(\gamma_{x,a}X\) is fermion odd and has the same centre classes. The hop and electric gate conserve each link's centre class. Thus such an operator connects static centre sectors; it is not generated by those gates. The broader end-mixed construction in `LW1_5O_DRAFT.md` Proposition E(ii) also gives a one-ended half-spin operator, outside the specified \(\mathfrak{so}(20)\) family.

**GAP: scope of the pass-1 no-16 conclusion.** F and K prove the statements just given. They do not prove that *every* fermion-odd operator with arbitrary mixed registers is tensor class: the explicit \(\gamma X\) above is a counterexample. Conservation of a local centre charge rules out dynamically changing its eigenvalue, but by itself does not exclude every possible propagating excitation within a fixed charge sector or every nonlocal construction. **LW1-5-16** remains named for a usable light spinor excitation; the pass-1 “refuted for CG1” conclusion is conditional on its stated field and finite-local-operator reading.

**Σ.** If every one of the six link ends at a site has the same scalar centre class \(\epsilon=\pm1\), then \(z_x=-\epsilon^6=-1\). No vector is a local Spin(10) singlet. This includes one uniform irreducible \(S_+\) or vector register. It does not preclude invariant mixed states: the tracial state is invariant under conjugation.

**GAP: vector-class claim for reducible registers.** The pass-1 Lemma Z(ii) assumes only a common scalar action of \(-1\in\operatorname{Spin}(20)\), then treats the lift of \(-I_{20}\) as scalar. That need not follow on a reducible register: \(S_+\oplus S_-\) has the same \(-1\) eigenvalue on both summands but opposite chirality. Off-diagonal link operators are changed by the lift of \(-I_{20}\). The parity identification in Z(ii) holds if that lift is also scalar, in particular for an irreducible register; Z(i)'s local \(-1\) statement only needs end-purity.

**S1–S4.** For a pattern of \(S_+\) and \(\mathbb C^{20}\) links, local singlets exist simultaneously on any finite set of sites exactly if each such site has an odd number of \(S_+\) links. The two-tiling assignment with \(S_+\) on one tiling and the vector register on the other satisfies this. For any covariant end-pure assignment, opposite centre classes on the two tilings are necessary. A uniform reducible \(S_+\oplus\mathbb C^{20}\) register conserves each link class and contains the two-tiling assignment as a sector.

*Proof.* Necessity follows from \(z_x=-(-1)^{s(x)}\). For sufficiency decompose each link into endpoint factors: \(S_+\) into paired half-spin factors, the vector into \((10,1)\oplus(1,10)\). At each site choose the matter parity so that the sum of the \(\mathbb Z_4\) centre classes is zero. Such a tensor product has a singlet: \(16\) and \(\overline{16}\) pair, \(10\otimes16\) contains \(\overline{16}\), \(16\otimes16\) contains \(10\), and \(10\otimes10\) contains \(1\); repeatedly use these summands. Tensor the local singlets. Each site has three links of each tiling. The spatial group preserves each tiling, is transitive on its links, and contains a reversal of every link; hence a covariant end-pure assignment has one class per tiling and the stated necessary condition. Centrality makes each reducible-link class conserved. ∎

The construction gives consistent even local states on the infinite lattice; it does not by itself prove purity, stationarity or full spatial symmetry there. Two register classes and the reducible alternative are recorded, not proposed: pass 1 finds test 4 fails unless a part requires the added structure. **LW1-5-Σ** asks what VC1 needs and which sector actually supplies it. On a finite region the isotypic decomposition \(\bigoplus_\lambda V_\lambda\otimes W_\lambda\) gives invariant algebra \(\bigoplus_\lambda1\otimes\operatorname{End}(W_\lambda)\); the state \(1_{V_\lambda}/\dim V_\lambda\otimes|w\rangle\langle w|\) is invariant and pure when restricted to that algebra. This is not a proof of a stationary infinite vacuum. With one \(S_+\) class there is a fixed spinor centre charge at every site and no link vector invariant at both ends. **LW1-5-BG** asks whether the resulting long-distance theory meets GA2.

**H.** Every state invariant under all local \(\operatorname{Spin}(10)\) transformations has \(\omega(n_{x,j})=1/2\) for each mode.

*Proof.* Gauge-average \(M_x^{ab}\). Its average is invariant in the adjoint representation of the simple Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{so}(10)\), hence zero. Use \(n_j-1/2=M^{2j-1,2j}\). This proves mean filling, not selection of the free sea or of a gauged vacuum. ∎

## 6. Singlets, operators and filling for \(L5\)-U

Let \(Q_x=N_x+\sum_{\ell\ni x}p_{\ell,x}\). On an even periodic box of \(V\) sites,
\[
 \sum_xQ_x=N_{\rm tot}+3mV. \tag{6.1}
\]
A finite-box vector state is locally \(U(5)\)-invariant exactly when its vector is \(SU(5)\)-invariant and an eigenvector of \(Q_x\); its eigenvalue is a multiple of five. Indeed a vector invariant as a state transforms by a character; \(SU(5)\) has no nontrivial continuous character, and its centre acts as \(e^{2\pi iQ_x/5}\). The converse follows by exponentiation.

**Σ-U.** With the unshifted generators, a zero Gauss-sector vector at \(x\) has empty matter and all six local link ends empty. It exists locally iff \(m\le5\), and not simultaneously at all sites of a periodic box for any \(m\ge1\). Under LW1's conjugation reading, pure locally gauge-invariant box states exist for every \(m=1,\ldots,9\), with site numbers respectively
\[
 n_m=2,4,1,3,0\text{ or }5,2,4,1,3.
\]
They can be chosen translation invariant as states; full point-group invariance and stationarity remain open (LW1-5U-Σc).

*Proof.* \(Q_x=0\) forces every nonnegative summand to vanish; (6.1) rules out all sites at once. For the conjugation reading, products \(\bigotimes_i\Lambda^{a_i}\mathbb C^5\) have an \(SU(5)\) singlet iff \(\sum_i a_i\equiv0\pmod5\): necessity is the centre; sufficiency follows by repeatedly embedding \(\Lambda^{a+b}\) when \(a+b\le5\), or \(\Lambda^{a+b-5}\) using the invariant volume form otherwise. Fix on each oriented link the same admissible end numbers \(\alpha,m-\alpha\). At each site the six ends total \(3m\); the listed \(n_m\) makes \(n_m+3m\) divisible by five. **Z-U.** A local operator with definite \(Q_x\) charges has total charge equal to its matter-number change, and its matter parity is \((-1)^{\sum q_x}\). Thus no fermion-odd local operator is invariant under all \(U(5)_x\). *Proof.* Sum the \(Q_x\)'s over a finite set containing both ends of every supported link. Every supported link contributes its fixed total \(m\), and other link terms commute with the operator. **H-U and F-U.** Local \(U(5)\) invariance gives site density matrix \(\sum_{k=0}^5 p_k\Pi_k/\binom5k\), with arbitrary probabilities \(p_k\); filling can take any value in \([0,5]\). If it is pure and locally gauge invariant, \(q\in5\mathbb Z\), giving the values \(n_m\) above and never \(5/2\).

*Proof.* Schur's lemma and irreducibility of the exterior powers give the marginal; a product of site density matrices and tracial links realizes each mean. The same sum for \(L_{\rm cube}\) gives integer occupation 0 or 1 for its analogous finite-box pure, gauge-invariant, site-transitive states. Infinite volume is not covered. ∎

**D: the five-mode selector.** If one reads \(S_+=\oplus_{k\text{ even}}\Lambda^k\mathbb C^N\) as Weyl species, the \(SU(N)\) representation always has a symmetric invariant bilinear on \(\Lambda^0\), so the no-mass condition does not select five.
