## 2. Tools and definitions inferred by the distiller

The following constructions and conventions are inferred mathematical definitions, not adopted physical axioms or readings. In particular, the product state below is the consistent product functional on local algebras; it need not be represented by an infinite tensor-product vector. The meanings of D1, the named lifts, (G1), (Z-a), R-form and the band definition are those in the cited frozen files.

**Setting S.** Sites are `Z³` with nearest-neighbour links `ℓ = (x, x+e_i)`. Each site carries a finite-dimensional graded matter factor `F_x` (CAR modes, Jordan–Wigner order fixed). Each link carries a Hilbert space `H_ℓ`, an ordinary tensor factor. For `L5-U` and `LU[s,m]` the rishon register is an ordinary tensor factor because every register operator conserves rishon number and is even. A compact group `K_x` acts at each site by even unitaries on `F_x` and on the `x`-end of each link at `x`, and trivially elsewhere; actions at distinct sites commute. A finite region `R` is a finite set of matter factors and link factors; `S(R)` is its sites together with all endpoints of its links; `𝔄(R)` is the algebra of `R` (a full matrix algebra when `R` is finite and all factors are finite-dimensional); `𝔄^G(R) = 𝔄(R) ∩ 𝔄^G`. Both live laws are of this form (`LW1_5.md` §2), and so are `L5[SU(2), s]` (`GB1QL.md` §1.5) and the families of section 5.

**Lemma T0 (local commutation; ported).** If `A ∈ 𝔄^G(R)` and `x ∈ S(R)`, then `A` commutes with the in-region part of every gauge unitary at `x`. *Proof.* The gauge unitary at `x` is (in-region part) times (out-of-region part); both are even, the second commutes with `A`, and `A` is fixed by the product (`GB1Z2L5.md` Theorem 2 proof, first paragraph). □ Local invariants are dense in `𝔄^G` (`GB1Z2L5.md` Theorem 1).

**Lemma T1 (extremal witness).** In Setting S, let `T_x ⊆ K_x` be a torus, and decompose `F_x` and each `H_ℓ` into joint weight spaces of the torus generators (for `H_ℓ`, joint weights `(w, w′)` of the `x`-end and `y`-end actions). Let `Ψ = ⊗_x ψ_x ⊗ ⊗_ℓ ψ_ℓ` be a product of joint weight vectors, with weights `w*_x` and `(w*_{ℓ,x}, w*_{ℓ,y})`. Suppose that for each finite `R` there are real linear functionals `λ_x` on the weights (`x ∈ S(R)`) such that:

- (W1) for each matter factor `F_x` in `R`, `w*_x` is the unique maximizer of `λ_x` over the weights of `F_x`, and its weight space is `Cψ_x`;
- (W2) for each link `ℓ = (x, y)` in `R`, `(w*_{ℓ,x}, w*_{ℓ,y})` is the unique maximizer of `λ_x(w) + λ_y(w′)` over the joint weights of `H_ℓ`, and its joint weight space is `Cψ_ℓ`.

Then the vector state of `Ψ`, restricted to `𝔄^G`, is a character.

*Proof.* Fix `R` and let `Ψ_R` be the restriction of `Ψ` to the factors of `R`. Put `Λ_R = Σ_{x∈S(R)} λ_x(G^{in}_x)`, where `G^{in}_x` is the tuple of in-region torus generators at `x`. On a product of weight vectors, `Λ_R` takes the value `Σ_{x} λ_x(w_x) + Σ_{ℓ=(x,y)} (λ_x(w_{ℓ,x}) + λ_y(w_{ℓ,y}))`, sums over matter factors and links of `R`. Each summand is at most its witness value, with equality only at the witness weight, by (W1)-(W2). So the top eigenvalue of `Λ_R` is attained exactly on the tensor product of the witness weight spaces, which is `CΨ_R`. By Lemma T0, every `A ∈ 𝔄^G(R)` commutes with `Λ_R`, so it preserves `CΨ_R`: `AΨ_R = c(A)Ψ_R`. Then `⟨Ψ, ABΨ⟩ = c(A)c(B)` for `A, B ∈ 𝔄^G(R)`, and `c(1) = 1`. Two local invariants lie in a common `𝔄^G(R)`. Multiplicativity and unitality pass to the norm closure by density and continuity of the state. □

**Corollary T1.1 (instances).** Each invariant algebra below has a character.
(a) `L5-U[m]`, every `1 ≤ m ≤ 9` (rebuilds `GB1Z2L5.md` Theorem 2 and `ZM1INV.md` R1). Take `λ_x(e_j) = c_j + ε_R h(x)`, with `0 > c_1 > c_2 > … > c_5`, `h(x) = x_1 + x_2 + x_3`, and `ε_R > 0` small for `R`. (W1) holds at empty matter. On a link the end weights are occupation sets `A, B ⊆ {1,…,5}` with `|A| + |B| = m`; the maximizing multiset takes colours `1, 2, …` twice each. For `m = 2k`, `A = B = {1,…,k}` is the unique maximizer. For `m = 2k+1` the two ends tie on colour `k+1`; the tilt adds `ε_R(h(x)|A| + h(y)|B|)` with `h(y) = h(x) + 1`, so the unique maximizer is `A = {1,…,k}`, `B = {1,…,k+1}`, the oriented state of `ZM1INV.md` R1.

**Lemma T2 (characterless pieces).** (a) A unital C*-algebra containing self-adjoint unitaries `X, Z` with `XZ = −ZX` has no character. (b) If a unital C*-algebra with no character embeds unitally in `𝔄^G`, then `𝔄^G` has no character. (c) Suppose `𝔄^G` contains operators `F_{ab}`, `1 ≤ a, b ≤ s`, with `F_{ab}* = F_{ba}`, `[F_{ab}, F_{cd}] = δ_{bc}F_{ad} − δ_{ad}F_{cb}`, each `F_{aa}` with integer spectrum, and `Σ_a F_{aa} = n·1`. If `s ∤ n`, `𝔄^G` has no character.

*Proof.* (a) `χ(X)² = χ(Z)² = 1` and `χ(X)χ(Z) = −χ(Z)χ(X)` force `χ(X)χ(Z) = 0`, a contradiction. (b) Compose. (c) A character takes each element into its spectrum: if `χ(A) ∉ spec A`, then `A − χ(A)1` is invertible and its image `0` is invertible, which is false. From the commutator, `χ(F_{aa}) − χ(F_{bb}) = χ([F_{ab}, F_{ba}]) = 0`. So all `χ(F_{aa})` equal one integer `c`, and `sc = n`. □

**Corollary T2.1 (two gauge-singlet modes).** If some site carries two CAR modes on which every `K_x` acts trivially, `𝔄^G` has no character. *Proof.* With Majoranas `γ_1, γ_2` of the first mode and `γ_3` of the second, `X = iγ_1γ_2` and `Z = iγ_2γ_3` are even, gauge invariant, self-adjoint unitaries, and they anticommute. Apply T2(a)-(b). □ One singlet mode is not enough: its even algebra `span{1, n}` is commutative.

**Lemma T3 (infinite registers).** (a) *U(1), Kogut–Susskind link.* Let `H_ℓ = ℓ²(Z)` with `E e_n = n e_n`, `U e_n = e_{n+1}`, the gauge action at the two ends generated by `∓E`, any matter, and a link algebra containing `e^{iθE}` and `U`. Then `𝔄^G` has no character. (b) *SU(2), Kogut–Susskind link.* Let `H_ℓ = L²(SU(2))` with the gauge action `U ↦ h_y U h_x^{-1}` and any link algebra containing the matrix-entry multipliers and `a_ℓ = (−1)^{k_ℓ}`. Then `𝔄^G` has no character. (c) If `H` is an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space, `B(H)` has no tracial state.

*Proof.* (a) `e^{iθE_ℓ}` commutes with every Gauss generator; the oriented plaquette `U_p = U_1U_2U_3*U_4*` is invariant; `e^{iθE_1}U_pe^{−iθE_1} = e^{iθ}U_p`. For `θ ∉ 2πZ` this is `ab = λba` with invertible `a, b` and `λ ≠ 1`; a character would give `χ(a)χ(b)(1 − λ) = 0` with `χ(a), χ(b) ≠ 0`. (This is `GB1Z2L5.md` Theorem 5's pair, now on an infinite register.) (b) RC-T3b proves the result using polynomial multipliers (referee closure, LW2_REFEREE.md; checked once, §9). (c) `H ≅ H ⊕ H` gives isometries `S_1, S_2` with `S_1S_1* + S_2S_2* = 1`; a tracial state `τ` would give `τ(1) = τ(S_1*S_1) + τ(S_2*S_2) = 2τ(1)`. □

### 4.1 Row 1: the trace obstruction

**What the proof uses.** `ZM1I1A.md` Theorem 1 transfers the trace: "A unital *-homomorphism `Φ:𝔄₁→𝔄` transfers the normalized trace: `τ∘Φ=τ₁`." Its proof needs finite matrix factors: "Finite CAR algebras are full matrices ... A tracial state on `M_n` ... has equal values `1/n` on diagonal units" (feature 1a). Theorem 1's covariance `−v_m` comes from the shared link: "The other end has number `m−p`" (feature 1c). Theorem 2 needs (G1) at singleton representatives (1b). Theorem 3's last step counts sites: "`(2LN)³` fine sites ... from at most `8(N+2c)³` coarse sites" (1d). The file states its own load: "The failing requirement is the exact generator identity (G1), together with locality and the fixed-form coarse algebra."

**Proposition 1.1 (V1a, Kogut–Susskind link).** If one link algebra of the fine law is `B(H_ℓ)` with `H_ℓ` infinite-dimensional, the fine algebra has no tracial state, so Theorem 1's step `τ∘Φ = τ₁` has no `τ`. If instead the link algebra is `K(H_ℓ) + C1`, the generators `e^{iθE_ℓ}`, `θ ∉ 2πZ`, are not in it, so (G1) cannot be stated.

## 6. LW2-G, separate branch: a fixed register with internal structure

### 6.1 Three readings of "internal structure", and the one worked here

### 6.3 What the internal branch buys, and what it costs

With fixed internal flavours the law escapes exactly one family of negatives: the character obstruction and the steps that consume a fine character: row 2 when `s ∤ m`, and row 3's first step when `2s ∤ m`. - (F1) *Flux.* `𝒦 = ⊕_{k⃗}⊗_ℓH_{k_ℓ}`, `k⃗ ∈ N^{12}`, with projections `Π_{k⃗}`. Put `P_{≤L} = Σ_{max k⃗ ≤ L}Π_{k⃗}`, `Q_L = 1 − P_{≤L−1}`, and `Π = P_{≤s}`, so `R := R_s^{⊗12} = ran Π`. `A = Σ_ℓE²_ℓ` equals `Σ_ℓc(k_ℓ)`, `c(k) = k(k+2)/4`, on `ran Π_{k⃗}`; on `ran Q_L`, `A ≥ c(L)`.
- (F2) *Two potentials.* `B = B_C` is multiplication by a real function with `|B| ≤ 24` (`GB1ISO.md` line 60). `B̃ = ιB^s_Cι^*` satisfies `B̃ = ΠB̃Π` and `‖B̃‖ ≤ 24`, by `‖U_s‖ ≤ 1` (`GB1QL.md` Proposition 4.2(d), line 147). Both change each link's flux by at most one, since `x_μ = x^+_μ + x^-_μ` and `P_μ = x^+_μY_s + Y_sx^-_μ` shift the degree by `±1` (`GB1QL.md` lines 55-57) and a plaquette uses each of its links once. Hence `P_{≤L−1}X = P_{≤L−1}XP_{≤L}` and `Q_{L+1}X = Q_{L+1}XQ_L` for `X ∈ {B, B̃}`.
- (F3) *Low-flux comparison* (`GB1QL.md` Lemma 4.7, line 219). For `v ∈ ran P_{≤M}` with `M ≤ s`, `‖(B̃ − B)v‖ ≤ 96δ_s(M)‖v‖`, `δ_s(M) = 2M(M+3)/(s(s+3))`.
- (F4) *Weighted norm.* Put `w(k⃗) = ∏_ℓ(k_ℓ+1)` and `|||y||| = Σ_{k⃗}w(k⃗)‖Π_{k⃗}y‖`. LW2's Lemma G3.1 gives `‖Π_{k⃗}y‖_∞ ≤ w(k⃗)‖Π_{k⃗}y‖`; so `|||y||| < ∞` gives a continuous representative with `‖y‖_∞ ≤ |||y|||`. By Cauchy–Schwarz, `|||P_{≤L}y||| ≤ d_L^6‖y‖` with `d_L = Σ_{k≤L}(k+1)²`. Heat factors contract `|||·|||`. For `X ∈ {B, B̃}`, `|||Xy||| ≤ c|||y|||` with `c = 24·6^{12}`: each `k⃗` has at most `3^{12}` neighbours `k⃗′`, and `w(k⃗) ≤ 2^{12}w(k⃗′)`.
- (F5) *Smoothing.* `|||e^{−uτA}y||| ≤ k_{2uτ}(e)^6‖y‖`, since `Σ_k(k+1)²e^{−2uτc(k)} = k_{2uτ}(e)` (`z_k(e) = (k+1)²`, `GB1E.md` line 54). Also `k_t(e) ≤ C₀(1 + t^{−3/2})` for an absolute `C₀`.
- (F6) *Reproducing vectors.* For `U ∈ SU(2)^{12}`, `e_U(W) = ∏_ℓΣ_{k≤s}z_k(W_ℓU_ℓ^{-1})` lies in `R`, `⟨e_U, f⟩ = f(U)` for `f ∈ R`, and `‖P_{≤L}e_U‖ = d_L^6` for `L ≤ s`. The embedded kernel of an operator `Y` on `R` is `⟨e_U, Ye_V⟩` (`GB1E.md` line 54: `Z_k` is the kernel of `Π_k`).
- (F7) *Targets.* `κ ≥ e^{−12|β|}m_t^{12}` and the exact R-SUM cube kernel is at least `e^{−24a₀|θ|}m_{a₀τ_E}^{12}` (`GB1ISO.md` line 60), with `m_t > 0` (`GB1ISO.md` Theorem 2.2).

### Referee §3.2: proof of RC-G3b (R-IA)

Let `S = e^{βB̃/4}` on `R` and `b = 6|β|`. The R-IA cube kernel is `K^{IA}_s(U,V) = ⟨e_U, Se^{−tA}Se_V⟩ = ⟨e^{−tA/2}Se_U, e^{−tA/2}Se_V⟩`.

1. *Telescoping.* With `w_U = (B̃ − B(U))e_U`, `(B̃^n − B(U)^n)e_U = Σ_{i=1}^{n}B(U)^{n−i}B̃^{i−1}w_U`, because `B(U)` is a scalar.
2. *Low flux.* For `1 ≤ M ≤ s`, `P_{≤M−1}(B − B(U))e_U = 0`. Indeed, for `f ∈ ran P_{≤M−1}`, (F2) puts `(B − B(U))f` in `ran P_{≤M} ⊆ R`, so `⟨f,(B − B(U))e_U⟩ = conj(((B − B(U))f)(U)) = 0` by (F6). Hence `P_{≤M−1}w_U = P_{≤M−1}(B̃ − B)P_{≤M}e_U`, and (F3), (F6) give `‖P_{≤M−1}w_U‖ ≤ 96δ_s(M)d_M^6`. Also `‖w_U‖ ≤ 48d_s^6`.
3. *High flux.* `(1 − P_{≤M−1})w_U ∈ ran Q_M`, and `B̃^{i−1}` maps `ran Q_M` into `ran Q_{M−i+1}` (F2). So for `i ≤ M`, `‖e^{−tA/2}B̃^{i−1}(1 − P_{≤M−1})w_U‖ ≤ e^{−(t/2)c(M−i+1)}24^{i−1}·48d_s^6`.
4. *Sum.* Let `r_U = e^{−tA/2}(S − e^{βB(U)/4})e_U = Σ_{n≥1}((β/4)^n/n!)e^{−tA/2}(B̃^n − B(U)^n)e_U`. Steps 1-3, with `i ≤ M/2` and `i > M/2` treated apart, give
 `‖r_U‖ ≤ (be^b/24)[96δ_s(M)d_M^6 + 48d_s^6e^{−(t/2)c(M/2)}] + 2d_s^6Σ_{n>M/2}nb^n/n!`.
 Take `M = ⌊s^{1/20}⌋`. Then `δ_s(M)d_M^6 ≤ 2(M+3)²(M+1)^{18}/s² → 0`; `d_s^6 ≤ (s+1)^{18}` while `c(M/2) ≥ M²/16`, so the second term tends to `0`; and `(M/2)!` outgrows every power of `s`, so the third does too. Hence `sup_U‖r_U‖ → 0`.
5. *Kernel.* Put `a_U = e^{βB(U)/4}e^{−tA/2}e_U`. Then `K^{IA}_s(U,V) = ⟨a_U + r_U, a_V + r_V⟩`, `‖a_U‖ ≤ e^{6|β|}k_t(e)^6`, and `⟨a_U, a_V⟩ = e^{β(B(U)+B(V))/4}∏_ℓK^{(t)}_s(U_ℓV_ℓ^{-1})` with `K^{(t)}_s = Σ_{k≤s}e^{−tc(k)}z_k`. G3(a)'s proof gives `sup|K^{(t)}_s − k_t| ≤ r_s(t) → 0`. So `sup_{U,V}|K^{IA}_s − κ| → 0`. The `P_μ` preserve real functions, traces of products of quaternion basis matrices are real, and distinct-link coefficients commute. Thus `B̃`, heat and the R-IA transfer preserve real functions, and their continuous kernels are real. By (F7) `K^{IA}_s ≥ (1/2)e^{−12|β|}m_t^{12}` for `s ≥ s₀(t,β)`. Every bound is per cube, so `s₀` does not depend on the box. `T^{IA}_s` is positive definite and gauge invariant; the lift statements follow as in G3(a). □

### Referee §4: RC-G3c for cube-face R-SUM


(referee closure, LW2_REFEREE.md)

**RC-G3c (cube-face factorized family only).** Fix `a₀ > 0`, `τ = τ_E > 0` and `θ ∈ R`. There is `s₀(a₀,τ,θ)`, independent of the box, such that for `s ≥ s₀` the embedded kernel of the R-SUM finite-register transfer `e^{−a₀H_s}` is strictly positive. Cube by cube it converges uniformly to the kernel of `e^{−a₀H}`. So for a fixed box and fixed `N_t` the lifts are gauge-invariant probability measures and converge in total variation to the exact lift.

*Setting.* One cube, with the facts (F1)-(F7) of section 3.1. Put `H = τA + θB` on `𝒦`, `H_s = τA + θB̃` on `R`, and `Ĥ = τA + θB̃` on `𝒦`. Since `B̃ = ΠB̃Π`, `Ĥ = H_s ⊕ τA` on `R ⊕ R^⊥`. So the kernel of `e^{−a₀Ĥ}` is `K_s + h_s`, where `K_s` is the kernel of `e^{−a₀H_s}` and `h_s` is the kernel of `e^{−a₀τA}(1 − Π)`. Telescoping the twelve link factors gives `sup|h_s| ≤ 12r_s(a₀τ)k_{a₀τ}(e)^{11} → 0`, with `r_s` as in G3(a). Let `K` be the kernel of `e^{−a₀H}`.

*Step 1 (Dyson bounds).* Let `X ∈ {B, B̃}` and `G = τA + θX`. The Dyson series `e^{−wG} = Σ_n(−θ)^n∫_{Δ_n(w)}e^{−u_0τA}Xe^{−u_1τA}⋯Xe^{−u_nτA}du` converges in norm; `Δ_n(w)` has volume `w^n/n!`. Three bounds follow, for `0 < w ≤ a₀`, with constants independent of `s`.
- (a) `|||e^{−wG}y||| ≤ e^{c|θ|w}|||y|||`, by (F4) term by term.
- (b) *Smoothing.* `|||e^{−wG}f||| ≤ S(w)‖f‖` with `S(w) = Σ_n((c|θ|w)^n/n!)C₀^6(1 + ((n+1)/(τw))^{3/2})^6 < ∞`. On the part of `Δ_n(w)` where `u_i` is the largest coordinate, `u_i ≥ w/(n+1)`. Bound the factors left of `e^{−u_iτA}` in `|||·|||`, that factor by (F5) with half its time, and the factors to its right in `L²`.
- (c) *Damping.* For `L ≥ 2`, `|||Q_Le^{−wG}f||| ≤ η_w(L)‖f‖` with `η_w(L) = e^{−(τw/2)c(L/2)}S(w) + Σ_{n≥L/2}((c|θ|w)^n/n!)C₀^6(1 + ((n+1)/(τw))^{3/2})^6`. By (F2), `Q_L` passes through the term as `e^{−u_0τA}Q_LXQ_{L−1}e^{−u_1τA}Q_{L−1}X⋯`, so for `n < L/2` every heat factor acts on `ran Q_{L/2}` and damps by `e^{−u_jτc(L/2)}`. Half of the largest factor smooths and the rest damps, which gives the first term. For `n ≥ L/2` use (b). `η_w(L) → 0` as `L → ∞`, uniformly for `w ∈ [a₀/4, a₀/2]`.

*Step 2 (kernel vectors).* By (b) and (F4), `f ↦ (e^{−wG}f)(U)` is a bounded functional of norm at most `S(w)`. Let `F_w(U)` (for `G = Ĥ`) and `G_w(U)` (for `G = H`) be its Riesz vectors, so `(e^{−wĤ}f)(U) = ⟨F_w(U), f⟩`. Then `F_w(U) = e^{−(w/2)Ĥ}F_{w/2}(U)`, `‖F_w(U)‖ ≤ S(w)`, and the kernel of `e^{−wĤ}` is `⟨F_{w/2}(U), F_{w/2}(V)⟩`. By (a) and (F4), `‖P_{≤L}F_w(U)‖ = sup{|(e^{−wĤ}f)(U)| : f ∈ ran P_{≤L}, ‖f‖ = 1} ≤ e^{c|θ|w}d_L^6`. By (c), `|||Q_LF_w(U)||| ≤ η_{w/2}(L)S(w/2)`. The same holds for `G_w`.

*Step 3 (Duhamel).* `e^{−a₀Ĥ} − e^{−a₀H} = −θ∫_0^{a₀}e^{−(a₀−u)Ĥ}(B̃ − B)e^{−uH}du`. Applied to `f ∈ 𝒦`, the integrand has finite sup norm, integrable in `u`: on `[0,a₀/2]` use (b) for `e^{−(a₀−u)Ĥ}`; on `[a₀/2,a₀]` use (a) for `e^{−(a₀−u)Ĥ}` and (b) for `e^{−uH}`. Test the operator identity against trigonometric polynomials `f, g`. For `0 < u < a₀` (the endpoints have measure zero), the kernel of `e^{−(a₀−u)Ĥ}(B̃ − B)e^{−uH}` is `⟨F_{a₀−u}(U),(B̃ − B)G_u(V)⟩`, which Step 4 bounds uniformly in `u, U, V` for each `s`, so Fubini applies. Step 1(c) approximates each positive-time evaluation map uniformly by finite-flux output maps, so the Riesz vectors are norm-continuous. Both sides are continuous kernels, so they agree everywhere, and the kernel difference is
`D(U,V) = −θ∫_0^{a₀}⟨F_{a₀−u}(U), (B̃ − B)G_u(V)⟩du`.

*Step 4 (estimate).* Fix `1 ≤ M ≤ s` and split `G_u(V) = P_{≤M}G_u(V) + Q_{M+1}G_u(V)`.
- (i) Low flux. By (F2), `|⟨F_{a₀−u}(U),(B̃ − B)P_{≤M}G_u(V)⟩| = |⟨P_{≤M+1}F_{a₀−u}(U),(B̃ − B)P_{≤M}G_u(V)⟩|`, and by (F3) and Step 2 this is at most `96δ_s(M)e^{2c|θ|a₀}d_{M+1}^{12}`.
- (ii) High flux, `u ≥ a₀/2`. The term is `(e^{−(a₀−u)Ĥ}y)(U)` with `y = (B̃ − B)Q_{M+1}G_u(V)`. By (a), (F4) and Step 2 it is at most `2ce^{c|θ|a₀}η_{u/2}(M+1)S(u/2)`.
- (iii) High flux, `u < a₀/2`. Since `B̃ − B` is self-adjoint, (F2) rewrites the term as `⟨Q_{M+1}(B̃ − B)Q_MF_{a₀−u}(U), G_u(V)⟩`, the conjugate of `(e^{−uH}y′)(V)` with `y′ = Q_{M+1}(B̃ − B)Q_MF_{a₀−u}(U)`. It is at most `2ce^{c|θ|a₀}η_{(a₀−u)/2}(M)S((a₀−u)/2)`.
In (ii) and (iii) the time arguments lie in `[a₀/4, a₀/2]`. So
`sup_{U,V}|D| ≤ |θ|a₀[96δ_s(M)e^{2c|θ|a₀}d_{M+1}^{12} + 2ce^{c|θ|a₀}sup_{w∈[a₀/4,a₀/2]}η_w(M)S(w)]`.
Take `M = ⌊s^{1/40}⌋`. Then `δ_s(M)d_{M+1}^{12} ≤ 2(M+3)²(M+2)^{36}/s² → 0`, and the second term tends to `0` by Step 1(c).

*Step 5 (conclusion).* `K_s = K + D − h_s → K` uniformly. The reality argument in RC-G3b applies also to `e^{−a₀H_s}`: its continuous kernel is real. By (F7), `K ≥ e^{−24a₀|θ|}m_{a₀τ}^{12} > 0`, so `K_s ≥ (1/2)e^{−24a₀|θ|}m_{a₀τ}^{12}` for `s ≥ s₀(a₀,τ,θ)`. The full kernel is a product of cube kernels, and gauge projection averages positive kernels, so positivity and `s₀` do not depend on the box. The lift statements follow as in G3(a). □

**How RC-G3c relates to A1-A3.** The three attacks fail as LW2 states them. RC-G3c uses two steps that none of them uses. (1) The R-SUM transfer factors over cubes (`GB1ISO.md` line 41), so the problem is one fixed cube and box-independence is automatic. (2) The finite-register potential is compared with the multiplication operator only at low flux (F3), where they agree up to `δ_s(M)`. High flux is damped by the heat factor and measured in the weighted norm `|||·|||`, which controls sup norms. Without cube-face factorization the box-independent threshold is not proved. This answers A2's obstacle: no sup bound on the kernel of `B̃` is needed. It does not contradict `GB1E.md` Theorem 8.1: `s₀` grows without bound as `a₀ → 0`. It also does not contradict `GB1E.md` line 206 ("It does not prove that the fixed-register kernels are probability measures"); that line describes Theorem 8.3, and RC-G3c is a separate argument.
