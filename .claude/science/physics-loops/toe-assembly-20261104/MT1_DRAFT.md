### 1.3 Lifts of the cell rotations

`O` is the group of determinant-one signed permutation matrices. For `Q ∈ O` put `g_Q(x) = Q(x − c_0) + c_0`, `c_0 = (1/2, 1/2, 1/2)`. Write `Q e_j = s_j e_{π(j)}` with signs `s_j` and a permutation `π`. `LW1.md` A2: "Every proper cube-centred rotation preserves each half-tick up to a common site sign gauge."

**Definition L (lifts).** A *lift* of `Q` on `ℓ^2(Z^3)` is a unitary `u = D P_Q`, with `P_Q|x⟩ = |g_Q x⟩` and `D` a diagonal unitary, that commutes with `R_e` and with `R_o`. On `ℓ^2(Z^3) ⊗ C^5` a lift is a unitary `Σ_x |g_Q x⟩⟨x| ⊗ W_x` (`W_x ∈ U(5)`) that commutes with `R_e ⊗ 1` and `R_o ⊗ 1`.

Commuting with `R_e` and `R_o` is the same as commuting with both half-steps, since `sin a_e ≠ 0 ≠ sin a_o` and `R_T^2 = 1` give `R_e = i(V_e − cos a_e)/sin a_e`, and likewise for `R_o`.

**Lemma 1.1 (lifts are cell-periodic and unique up to a phase).**
(i) Each `Q ∈ O` has a lift `ρ(Q) = Σ_x f_Q(x) |g_Q x⟩⟨x|` with signs `f_Q(x) = ±1`. The sign function `f_Q` depends only on the residue of `x`.
(ii) Any lift of `Q` on `ℓ^2(Z^3)` is a constant phase times `ρ(Q)`. Any lift on `ℓ^2(Z^3) ⊗ C^5` is `ρ(Q) ⊗ w` for one `w ∈ U(5)`.
(iii) `ρ(Q)|K, r⟩ = f_Q(r) |QK, g_Q(r)⟩`, with `g_Q(r) ∈ {0,1}^3`. So `ρ̂(Q) := Σ_r f_Q(r) |g_Q(r)⟩⟨r|` is a constant signed permutation matrix on `C^8`, and `ρ̂(Q) R_e(K) ρ̂(Q)^* = R_e(QK)`, `ρ̂(Q) R_o(K) ρ̂(Q)^* = R_o(QK)` for all `K`.

*Proof.* Existence of a sign lift is A2's sentence just quoted. Write `ε(e) := η(e) η(g_Q e)` for an edge `e`, where `η(e)` is the sign of the edge (its `η_i` at the lower endpoint). Comparing the matrix elements of `u R_T u^*` and `R_T` on the edge `{g x, g y}` shows that `u = D P_Q` commutes with `R_e, R_o` exactly when `f(x) f(y)^* = ε({x, y})` for every edge, where `f(x)` is the diagonal entry of `D` at `g x`. (ii) Two lifts of `Q` differ by a diagonal unitary that commutes with `R_e` and `R_o`. Every nearest-neighbour pair lies in exactly one cube of one tiling (A1), so `R_e + R_o` has a nonzero entry on every nearest-neighbour pair. A diagonal unitary commuting with it is constant on neighbours, hence constant on the connected lattice. On `C^5`-valued functions the same computation gives `W_y W_x^* ∈ U(1)` on neighbours, so `W_x = φ_x w` with phases `φ_x`, and the first case applies. (i) The value of `ε` is unchanged under `x ↦ x + 2b`: `η` depends only on residues, and `g(x + 2b) = g(x) + 2Qb`. So `s(b) := f(x + 2b) f(x)` does not depend on `x`: for two points `x, y`, `f(x+2b)f(x)f(y+2b)f(y)` is the product of `ε` along a path from `x` to `y` and along its translate, which is 1. Along the straight path `x, x + e_k, x + 2e_k` the two edges point in direction `k`, and their images point in direction `π(k)`. Two consecutive edges in one direction `i` have equal signs, because `η_i` does not depend on the `i`-th coordinate. So `s(e_k) = 1` for each `k`, and `f` is cell-periodic. (iii) For `x = 2b + r`, `g(x) = 2Qb + g(r)` and `g(r) ∈ {0,1}^3`. Then `ρ(Q)|K, r⟩ = f(r) Σ_b e^{iK·b} |2Qb + g(r)⟩ = f(r) |QK, g(r)⟩`, because `K·Q^{-1}b' = (QK)·b'`. The fibre identities restate `ρ(Q) R_T ρ(Q)^* = R_T`. ∎

On an even torus of side `L` (LW1: "Tori have even site side length \(L\), periodic signs and tilings") `g_Q` maps the torus to itself, since `g(x + Lb) = g(x) + LQb`, and `f_Q` is `L`-periodic. So Lemma 1.1 holds on every even torus. For `L5-O` the lifts are `W ρ(Q) W^* ⊗ w`, by the same lemma applied to `WVW^*`.

## 2. MT1(a)(1): the exchange sign, and what remains of MT1-i

### 2.1 The reading of "exchange sign"

Levin and Wen (arXiv:cond-mat/0302460, eq. (4)) give an algebraic test of statistics for particles moved by hopping operators `t_{ij}`, where `t_{ij}` moves a particle from site `j` to site `i`:

`t_{il} t_{ki} t_{ij} = e^{iθ} t_{ij} t_{ki} t_{il}`,

for "any three hopping operators" with `j, k, l` "(distinct) neighbors of i". The text after eq. (4) says that free hard-core bosons and fermions, with `t_{ij} = -t c_i^† c_j`, satisfy it with `e^{iθ} = +1` and `-1` respectively.

**Reading R-X.** "Exchange sign −1" in MT1(a)(1) means: the law's hopping operators, resolved by colour with one colour at the central site, satisfy eq. (4) with `e^{iθ} = -1`.

Four tests. (1) It fixes no target: bosonic hops give `+1`, so the reading can fail. (2) It is closed: one identity for each junction. (3) It settles no downstream question; it tests the hop algebra only. (4) It adds no structure: it uses the law's own hops and a published identity. **Passes.**

**Lemma 2.1 (exchange sign −1 for both laws).** Let `i` be a site and `j, k, l` three distinct neighbours. Let `X_{il}, X_{ki}, X_{ij}` be any operators on the registers of the links `{i,l}, {i,k}, {i,j}`; for example the colour components of the law's link operator in the term of `h` that moves matter along that link.
(a) `L5-U`. Fix colours `b, c, e, f`. Put `t_{il} = c^†_{i,e} X_{il} c_{l,b}`, `t_{ki} = c^†_{k,c} X_{ki} c_{i,e}`, `t_{ij} = c^†_{i,e} X_{ij} c_{j,f}`. Then `t_{il} t_{ki} t_{ij} = − t_{ij} t_{ki} t_{il}`.
(b) `L5-O`. Fix Majorana labels `b, c, e, f`. Put `t_{il} = (i/2) γ_{i,e} X_{il} γ_{l,b}`, `t_{ki} = (i/2) γ_{k,c} X_{ki} γ_{i,e}`, `t_{ij} = (i/2) γ_{i,e} X_{ij} γ_{j,f}`. Then `t_{il} t_{ki} t_{ij} = − t_{ij} t_{ki} t_{il}`.
The identities hold as operator identities, for every register and every state.

*Proof.* Link operators on distinct links commute with each other and with matter (`LW1_5.md` line 33). So both sides carry the same link factor `X_{il} X_{ki} X_{ij}`, and only the matter factors differ. (a) The left side's matter factor is `c^†_{i,e} c_{l,b} c^†_{k,c} (c_{i,e} c^†_{i,e}) c_{j,f}`. Here `c_{i,e} c^†_{i,e} = 1 − n_{i,e}`. The even operator `n_{i,e}` commutes with the modes at `j, k, l`, and `c^†_{i,e} n_{i,e} = 0`. So the factor is `c^†_{i,e} c_{l,b} c^†_{k,c} c_{j,f}`. The same steps turn the right side's factor into `c^†_{i,e} c_{j,f} c^†_{k,c} c_{l,b}`. The three operators `c_{j,f}, c^†_{k,c}, c_{l,b}` sit at distinct sites and anticommute pairwise. Reversing the order of three pairwise anticommuting operators gives the sign `(-1)^3`. So `c_{j,f} c^†_{k,c} c_{l,b} = − c_{l,b} c^†_{k,c} c_{j,f}`. (b) The left side's matter factor is `γ_{i,e} γ_{l,b} γ_{k,c} γ_{i,e}^2 γ_{j,f} = γ_{i,e} γ_{l,b} γ_{k,c} γ_{j,f}`, and the right side's is `γ_{i,e} γ_{j,f} γ_{k,c} γ_{l,b}`. The same reversal gives the sign `−1`. ∎ [check: `cl6.py` builds the identity of (a) with one colour, four sites and random link matrices; both sides agree up to the sign `−1`, and the left side is nonzero.]

So, in reading R-X, every excitation that the law's hops move has exchange sign `−1`, on both laws, whatever the registers and the state. At the free node (section 3.4) the light excitations are created by smeared CAR operators `c^†(f)`, which anticommute directly.

### 2.2 The law on qubit clusters

`LW1.md` line 28 lists "the CAR reading of one site mode and parity superselection" as a reading, and adds "No theorem here derives the CAR product". PS-5 gives each site the algebra `Cl(10) ≅ M_32(C)`. Inside a site the CAR is a theorem of that text: `LW1_5.md` line 29 builds `c_j = (γ_{2j-1} + iγ_{2j})/2` from the ten Clifford generators, and the Clifford relations are the CAR of the five modes. The question MT1-i leaves is the product across sites. The theorem below answers it for every operator the two laws use.

**Clusters.** For a site `x`, the cluster `C_x` consists of the five matter modes at `x` and, for each of the six links `ℓ ∋ x`, five complex modes at the `x`-end of `ℓ`. Under `L5-U` these are the rishons `r^{ℓ,x}_k`. Under `L5-O[S_+]` they are `d^{ℓ,x}_k = (η^{x}_{2k-1} + i η^{x}_{2k})/2` built from the ten auxiliary Majoranas that `LW1_5.md` line 80 attaches to that end (`η^y` at a target end, `η^x` at a source end, as in the Gauss generator of line 83). So `C_x` has 35 complex modes. Let `F_x` be its Fock space, of dimension `2^35`, and let `𝔉 = ⊗_x F_x` be the **ordinary** tensor product over the sites of a finite box or even torus.

**Theorem 2.2 (cluster presentation).** For `L5-U` with any `m`, and for `L5-O` with the register `S_+`, there is a unitary `J` from the law's Hilbert space onto the subspace `𝔉_c ⊂ 𝔉` cut out by one constraint per link — `p_{ℓ,x} + p_{ℓ,y} = m` for `L5-U`, and link chirality `+1` for `L5-O[S_+]` — such that:
(a) `J` maps each site value (`N_x`, `P_x`), each Gauss generator at `x`, each end number `p_{ℓ,x}` and each end chirality `Γ^x_ℓ` to an operator on the factor `F_x` alone;
(b) `J` maps each hop `h_ℓ`, `ℓ = (x, y)`, to a sum of products (operator on `F_y`)·(operator on `F_x`), and each electric gate to an operator on one factor;
(c) the centre element that acts as fermion parity at `x` — `Γ_x(-1)` in `L5-U`, `ω_x = e^{iπ Σ_j 𝒢^{2j-1,2j}_x}` in `L5-O` — is mapped to the parity of the cluster `C_x`, up to a constant phase;
(d) every local operator of the law that commutes with all these centre elements is mapped to an operator on the factors of the clusters it touches. This includes every gauge-invariant local operator, every gate, and every gauge-invariant plaquette gate that GA1-P-5 may add.

*Proof.* Step 1, one grading for all modes. Treat matter as species 0 and each link as a further species, in a fixed order. In the law, modes of one species anticommute, and modes of different species commute (`LW1_5.md` line 33; line 87 for rishons, "fermionic among themselves"). For `L5-O`, embed `S_+` in the full Fock space `S = S_+ ⊕ S_-` of the link's ten complex modes, on which each `η` acts. Let `A_g` be the CAR algebra in which every two distinct modes anticommute. The Klein map `κ(a) = (∏_{s' < s} (-1)^{N_{s'}}) a` for a mode `a` of species `s` satisfies the relations of `A_g`. Check: for `a` of species `s` and `b` of species `s' > s`, the string of `κ(b)` contains `(-1)^{N_s}`, which anticommutes with `a`; every other string factor commutes with `a` and `b`; and `a, b` commute in the law. So `κ(a)κ(b) = −κ(b)κ(a)`, and within one species the law's CAR is kept. On a product of factors that are each even within their species, the strings cancel in pairs, so `κ` maps the `A_g` expression to the law's expression. Every operator in (a)-(d) is a sum of such products: the `L5-U` hop term is `(r^y_j r^{x†}_k)(c^†_{y,j} c_{x,k})` (link-even times matter-even), the `L5-O` hop term is `(η^y_a η^x_b)(γ_{y,a} γ_{x,b})`, Gauss generators and site values are bilinears or functions of them, and the electric gates are functions of `p_{ℓ,x}` or of `Γ^x_ℓ = i^5 η^x_1⋯η^x_{10}`.
Step 2, the factorization in `A_g`. In `A_g` the hop factorizes by associativity: `c^†_{y,j} r^y_j r^{x†}_k c_{x,k} = (c^†_{y,j} r^y_j)(r^{x†}_k c_{x,k})` for `L5-U`, and `γ_{y,a} (i η^y_a η^x_b) γ_{x,b} = i (γ_{y,a} η^y_a)(η^x_b γ_{x,b})` for `L5-O`. Each bracket is a bilinear in the modes of one cluster. The Gauss generators `c^†_x T c_x + Σ_ℓ r^†_{ℓ,x} T r_{ℓ,x}` and `M^{cd}_x + Σ_ℓ L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}` (with `L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}` a bilinear of the `x`-end Majoranas, `LW1_5.md` line 123) are cluster bilinears. `N_x`, `P_x`, `p_{ℓ,x}` and `Γ^x_ℓ` are even in the modes of `C_x`.
Step 3, the Jordan-Wigner map by clusters. Order the modes cluster by cluster. The map `a ↦ (∏_{x' < x} Π_{x'}) a^{(x)}`, with `Π_{x'}` the parity of cluster `C_{x'}` and `a^{(x)}` the mode on `F_x`, identifies the Fock space of `A_g` with `𝔉`. It is the Klein map of Step 1 with clusters as species. On a product of factors each even within one cluster the strings cancel, so such a product maps to the product of the local factors. An operator commutes with every `Π_x` exactly when it is a sum of products of cluster-even monomials, so it maps to an operator on the clusters it touches.
Step 4, the centre. `Γ_x(-1)` acts as `−1` on `c_x` and on each `r^{ℓ,x}` (`LW1_5.md` Theorem U, "the explicit action of \(\Gamma(u)\) at the site and on each link end"), so it equals `(-1)^{N_x + Σ_ℓ p_{ℓ,x}} = Π_x`. For `L5-O`, `LW1_5.md` line 162 gives the site factor of `ω_x` as `−iP_x`, and line 199 gives an end factor as a product of the ten end Majoranas: "The \(x\)-end factor of \(\omega_x\) is \(\prod_j e^{i\pi T_{10+2j-1,10+2j}}=-\eta_{11}\cdots\eta_{20}\)". The product of the ten Majoranas of one end is a constant phase times the parity of the five end modes. So `ω_x` is a constant phase times `Π_x`.
Step 5, the constraints. The law's link space is the sector `Λ^m C^{10}` (`L5-U`) or `S_+` (`L5-O`) of the link's ten modes. In `𝔉` this sector is `p_{ℓ,x} + p_{ℓ,y} = m`, or chirality `+1`, which is a product of the two end parities up to a constant. Every operator in (a)-(d) preserves these sectors. Composing Steps 1 and 3 and restricting to the constrained subspace gives `J`. Claims (a)-(c) are Steps 2 and 4. For (d), gauge invariance under conjugation implies commuting with each centre element, and Step 3 applies. ∎

**Corollary 2.3 (MT1-i is closed for both five-mode laws).** Take `L5-U` with any `m`, or `L5-O[S_+]`. (i) Inside a site the CAR is the Clifford relation of PS-5's `Cl(10)`. (ii) Across sites the CAR reading is not an independent premise: for every operator the law uses, it is equivalent to the ordinary tensor product of 35-mode clusters, and each cluster is 35 qubits by its own finite Jordan-Wigner map (Theorem 2.2). (iii) The law's hops give exchange sign `−1` in reading R-X (Lemma 2.1). So the excitations moved by the law are fermions built from qubits by a local encoding: five qubits of the site and thirty qubits at its link ends, which PS-6r's registers already carry. The clause "with no added site content" of MT1(a)(1) belonged to PS-5's first attempt; D1 put the fallback text in the working set, and that clause is superseded. ∎

## 3. MT1(a)(2): spin

### 3.1 A cell is a module of `Cl(6)`

**Lemma 3.1.** (a) `A_1, A_2, A_3, Γ_1, Γ_2, Γ_3` are Hermitian involutions on `C^8`, and every two of them anticommute. (b) `R_e(K) = (A_1 + A_2 + A_3)/√3` and `R_o(K) = 3^{-1/2} Σ_j (cos K_j A_j − sin K_j Γ_j)`. (c) The 64 ordered products of distinct generators are orthogonal for `(X, Y) ↦ tr(X^*Y)`, so the six generate all of `M_8(C)`.

*Proof.* (a) `LW1.md` A1: "In a cube let \(A_i\) be its signed axis matching. Then \(A_i^2=I\). The two orders of traversing an \(i,j\) face have opposite signs, so \(A_iA_j=-A_jA_i\)." The `A_j` are real symmetric. `Z_j` is a real diagonal involution; `Z_j` commutes with `Z_k` and with `A_k` for `k ≠ j`, and `Z_j A_j = −A_j Z_j`, because `A_j` flips bit `j` and `A_k` keeps it. Then `Γ_j^* = −i A_j Z_j = i Z_j A_j = Γ_j` and `Γ_j^2 = −Z_j A_j Z_j A_j = Z_j^2 A_j^2 = 1`. For `j ≠ k`, `Γ_j Γ_k = −Z_j Z_k A_j A_k` and `Γ_k Γ_j = −Z_k Z_j A_k A_j = Z_j Z_k A_j A_k`, so they anticommute. `Γ_j A_k = i Z_j A_j A_k = −i A_k Z_j A_j = −A_k Γ_j` for `k ≠ j`, and `Γ_j A_j = i Z_j = −A_j Γ_j`. (b) The even cube of a cell is the cell, so `R_e` has the constant fibre `(A_1+A_2+A_3)/√3`. An odd edge in direction `j` joins `2b + r` (with `r_j = 1`) to `2(b + e_j) + (r − e_j)`, with sign `η_j(r) = η_j(r − e_j)`. With the convention of section 1.1, `Σ_b |2(b+e_j) + r − e_j⟩⟨2b + r|` sends `|K, r⟩` to `e^{-iK_j} |K, r − e_j⟩`. So the fibre of the odd `j`-matching is `Σ_{r_j = 1} η_j(r) (e^{-iK_j} |r − e_j⟩⟨r| + e^{iK_j} |r⟩⟨r − e_j|) = cos K_j A_j − i sin K_j Σ_{r_j=1} η_j(r)(|r − e_j⟩⟨r| − |r⟩⟨r − e_j|)`. The last sum is `Z_j A_j`. (c) Let `e_S` be the ordered product over a nonempty set `S` of generators. If `|S|` is even, any `g ∈ S` anticommutes with `e_S`; if `|S|` is odd and `|S| < 6`, any `g ∉ S` does. Then `tr e_S = tr(g e_S g) = −tr e_S = 0`. Products obey `e_S^* e_T = ± e_{S Δ T}`, so distinct monomials are orthogonal and each has norm `8`. Sixty-four orthogonal matrices span `M_8(C)`. ∎

So `V(K) = exp(iε n(K)·Γ̂) exp(−iε n_0·Γ̂)` at `μ = 0`, with `Γ̂ = (A_1, A_2, A_3, Γ_1, Γ_2, Γ_3)`, `n_0 = (1,1,1,0,0,0)/√3` and `n(K) = (cos K, −sin K)/√3`. The anticommutation of the matchings is the flux `π` through every face. The velocity matrices `Γ_j` arise because the odd tiling's matchings carry the Bloch phase.

### 3.3 The spinor class

**Label (spinor class).** A projective unitary action of `O` (each operator fixed up to a phase) has *spinor class* if the operators of the half-turns about two perpendicular face axes through `c_0` anticommute, and *linear class* if they commute. These are the only options, and the option does not depend on the phases: if `u_1 u_2 = λ u_2 u_1` and `u_1^2` is a scalar, then `u_1^2 u_2 = λ^2 u_2 u_1^2` forces `λ^2 = 1`. The spin-1/2 representation of SU(2) restricted to `O` has spinor class, since `iσ_1` and `iσ_2` anticommute. Every linear representation has linear class. Four tests: (1) it states no target and can come out either way; (2) it is closed; (3) it decides nothing downstream; (4) it adds no structure. **Passes.**

**Theorem 3.3 (the cell rotations have spinor class on odd states).** Let `h_1, h_2` be the half-turns about the axes through `c_0` parallel to `e_1` and `e_2`.
(a) One body. `ρ̂(h_1) = λ_1 Γ_2 Γ_3` and `ρ̂(h_2) = λ_2 Γ_3 Γ_1` with `|λ_k| = 1`. Hence `ρ̂(h_1) ρ̂(h_2) = −ρ̂(h_2) ρ̂(h_1)`, and `ρ̂(h_k)^2 = −λ_k^2`. Any lifts `u_1, u_2` of `h_1, h_2` on `ℓ^2(Z^3)`, or on any even torus, satisfy `u_1 u_2 = −u_2 u_1`.
(b) Five flavours. For the free walks of `L5-U` and `L5-O`, any lifts `u_k ⊗ w_k` (Lemma 1.1(ii)) whose products commute or anticommute up to a scalar, and whose squares are scalars — as they must be if the lifts of `{1, h_1, h_2, h_1h_2}` form a projective representation — anticommute. No choice of flavour twists `w_k` removes the sign.
(c) Fock space. On every even torus, `Γ(u_1) Γ(u_2) = (-1)^N Γ(u_2) Γ(u_1)`. So the half-turn operators anticommute on every vector with odd fermion number and commute on every vector with even fermion number: odd sectors carry the spinor class and even sectors the linear class. At infinite volume, `α_{u_1} α_{u_2} = θ α_{u_2} α_{u_1}`.
(d) Dressed laws. For the lifts constructed in `LW1.md` (gauged covariance after B4) for `L_cube`, in `LW1_5.md` A2-5 and B1-5 for `L5-O[S_+]`, and in `LW1_5.md` B1-U for `L5-U`, the automorphisms `α_{h_1}, α_{h_2}` of the quasi-local algebra satisfy `α_{h_1} α_{h_2} = θ α_{h_2} α_{h_1}`.

*Proof.* (a) `h_1 = diag(1, −1, −1)`, so `π = id` and `s = (1, −1, −1)`. By Lemma 3.2, `ρ̂(h_1)` fixes `A_1, A_2, A_3, Γ_1` and negates `Γ_2, Γ_3`. The unitary `Γ_2 Γ_3` does the same: a generator that anticommutes with both `Γ_2` and `Γ_3` commutes with their product, and `Γ_2 Γ_3 Γ_2 (Γ_2 Γ_3)^{-1} = −Γ_2`, `Γ_2 Γ_3 Γ_3 (Γ_2 Γ_3)^{-1} = −Γ_3`. So `(Γ_2 Γ_3)^{-1} ρ̂(h_1)` commutes with all six generators, hence with `M_8(C)` (Lemma 3.1(c)), and is a scalar. The same argument with `h_2 = diag(−1, 1, −1)` gives `Γ_3 Γ_1`. Then `ρ̂(h_1)ρ̂(h_2) = λ_1λ_2 Γ_2 Γ_1` and `ρ̂(h_2)ρ̂(h_1) = λ_1λ_2 Γ_3Γ_1Γ_2Γ_3 = λ_1λ_2 Γ_1 Γ_2`, which differ by the sign `−1`. Also `(Γ_2Γ_3)^2 = −1`. On `ℓ^2(Z^3)` each lift is a phase times `ρ(h_k)` (Lemma 1.1(ii)). Since `h_1 h_2 = h_2 h_1`, the commutator `u_1 u_2 u_1^{-1} u_2^{-1}` maps each fibre to itself and acts there by the matrix commutator, which is `−1`. On an even torus the same holds fibre by fibre. (b) `(u_1 ⊗ w_1)(u_2 ⊗ w_2) = −u_2u_1 ⊗ w_1w_2`. If this equals `λ (u_2 ⊗ w_2)(u_1 ⊗ w_1) = λ u_2u_1 ⊗ w_2w_1`, then `w_1 w_2 w_1^{-1} w_2^{-1} = −λ·1`. *Remarks.* (1) The same holds for the half-turns about two perpendicular edge axes, for example `(1,1,0)` and `(1,−1,0)`. By Lemma 3.2 their operators are constant multiples of `a b c ω_6` and `a b' c ω_6`, with `a = (A_1 − A_2)/√2`, `b = (Γ_1 − Γ_2)/√2`, `b' = (Γ_1 + Γ_2)/√2`, `c = Γ_3` and `ω_6` the product of the six generators: conjugation by a unit vector `v` of the generators' span is minus the reflection in `v^⊥`, and conjugation by `ω_6` is `−1`. The four vectors `a, b, b', c` anticommute pairwise, and exchanging `b` and `b'` in `a b c a b' c` costs five transpositions, so the two operators anticommute. (2) The sign lift of a quarter-turn satisfies `ρ̂(C_4)^4 = ρ̂(C_2)^2 = −1` for the face half-turn `C_2 = C_4^2`, since `ρ̂(C_2)` is a real multiple of a product of two `Γ`'s. Cheng and Wang (arXiv:1810.12308, section II.1) separate fermions by `R^M = 1` ("if the rotation only operates on the spatial degrees of freedom") from `R^M = P_f` (spin-1/2 electrons, "because 2π spin rotation results −1"), and note: "For insulators, one can simply redefine R by a U(1) rotation, and the two cases become equivalent." That freedom exists here too: `e^{iπN/4}` changes `ρ̂(C_4)^4`. It does not touch Theorem 3.3: a phase redefinition cannot change whether two operators anticommute. The cubic group therefore carries a phase-robust spinor invariant that one rotation axis alone does not. (3) With all edge signs `+1` the matchings commute, the lifts are plain permutations, and the half-turns commute. The flux `π` through every face is what makes the class spinor.

**Proposition 3.4 (matter parity is a gauge transformation: a lattice spin/charge relation).** Let `O` be a local operator and `Λ` a finite set of sites containing every site where `O` has matter and both ends of every link in its support. Then `θ(O) = Ad(∏_{x∈Λ} e^{iπ𝒢_x})(O)` on `L_cube`, `θ(O) = Ad(∏_{x∈Λ} Γ_x(−1))(O)` on `L5-U`, and `θ(O) = Ad(∏_{x∈Λ} ω_x)(O)` on `L5-O[S_+]`. Hence every gauge-invariant local operator is `θ`-even, and on the gauge-invariant local algebra `α_{h_1}` and `α_{h_2}` commute. On a finite torus, a vector that is an eigenvector of every centre element has fermion parity equal to the product of its centre eigenvalues, up to one constant sign fixed by the registers; so it carries the spinor class exactly when that product is minus the constant.

`CH1.md` Lemma S1 already states the operator form on `L_cube`: "For a local homogeneous operator of total charge \(Q\), its matter parity is \((-1)^Q\)." Proposition 3.4 adds the rotational side. ### 3.4 The free node: spin times taste

*Proof.* `V` is analytic in `K`, and `V(0) = e^{iεR_e} e^{−iεR_e} = I` since `R_o(0) = R_e` (Lemma 3.1(b)). So `dh(0)[K] = −sin ε e^{iεR} δ_1 = (sin ε/√3) Σ_j K_j e^{iεR/2} Γ_j e^{−iεR/2}`, where the last step uses `e^{iεR/2} Γ_j = Γ_j e^{−iεR/2}`. *Proof.* (a) For a cyclic triple `(a, b, k)`, `S_k = −(i/2) Γ_a Γ_b`, which is Hermitian because `(Γ_aΓ_b)^* = Γ_bΓ_a = −Γ_aΓ_b`, and `S_k^2 = −(1/4)(Γ_aΓ_b)^2 = 1/4`. ### 3.5 The rotation group that emerges, and spin 1/2

**Definition ER (emergent rotations from emergent boosts).** Let `h_1(q) = Σ_j q_j N_j`, with Hermitian `N_j` on `C^n`, be the leading generator at a node. Suppose there is `v > 0` with `N_iN_j + N_jN_i = 2v^2 δ_{ij}` (one isotropic cone). On Schwartz functions `ψ: R^3 → C^n`, let `q` act by multiplication, put `x_j = i∂/∂q_j`, and let `K_j = (x_j h_1 + h_1 x_j)/2` be the boosts of `h_1`. The emergent rotation generators are `J_k = (i/2v^2) Σ_{i,j} ε_{kij} [K_i, K_j]`, and the emergent rotation group is the group they generate. The light modes *transform as spin s* if every irreducible summand of the internal part of `J` (Lemma 3.7) has Casimir `s(s+1)`. If no such `v` exists, ER gives no rotation group at leading order.

Four tests. (1) It fixes no target: the spin is computed, and ER can give no group at all. (2) It is closed: one formula from `h_1`. (3) It settles no downstream question: whether `h_1` has one cone is checked, not assumed; the relation between boosts and rotations is the Poincaré algebra's `[K_i, K_j] ∝ ε_{ijk} J_k`, used as a definition. (4) It adds no structure or constant: it uses `h_1` and the position operator dual to `q`; `v` is read off `h_1`. **Passes.**

**Lemma 3.7 (the boost algebra).** Under ER's condition, `[K_i, K_j] = −i v^2 Σ_k ε_{ijk} (L_k + S^N_k)` with `L_k = Σ ε_{kab} x_a q_b` and `S^N_k = −(i/4v^2) Σ ε_{kab} N_a N_b`. So `J_k = L_k + S^N_k`. The `S^N_k` obey `su(2)` with Casimir `3/4`, and `[J_k, h_1] = 0`.

*Proof.* Put `D_j = [x_j, h_1] = i N_j`, a constant matrix. Then `K_i = x_i h_1 − D_i/2` and `h_1 x_j = x_j h_1 − D_j`. Expanding, `[x_i h_1, x_j h_1] = −x_i D_j h_1 + x_j D_i h_1`, `[x_i h_1, D_j] = x_i [h_1, D_j]` and `[D_i, x_j h_1] = x_j [D_i, h_1]`. Collecting terms, `[K_i, K_j] = −(x_i/2){D_j, h_1} + (x_j/2){D_i, h_1} + [D_i, D_j]/4`. ER's condition gives `{D_j, h_1} = 2iv^2 q_j` and `[D_i, D_j] = −[N_i, N_j] = −2N_iN_j` for `i ≠ j`. So `[K_i, K_j] = −iv^2(x_iq_j − x_jq_i) − N_iN_j/2 = −iv^2 Σ_k ε_{ijk}(L_k + S^N_k)`, because for cyclic `(i,j,k)`, `S^N_k = −(i/2v^2) N_iN_j`. The `su(2)` relations and the Casimir follow as in Theorem 3.6(a) for the unit vectors `N_j/v`. Finally `[L_k, q_j] = i Σ_b ε_{kjb} q_b` and `[S^N_k, N_j] = i Σ_a ε_{kja} N_a`, so `[L_k, h_1] = −[S^N_k, h_1]`. ∎

**Theorem 3.8 (spin 1/2 at the free node).** For the free walks of `L_cube`, `L5-U` and `L5-O` at `μ = 0`, the leading generator at the node meets ER's condition with `v = sin ε/√3`. The internal part of `J` is `S_k` (tensored with `1_5` for the five-mode laws, and conjugated by the constant residue matrix of section 1.2 for `L5-O`). Every irreducible summand has spin 1/2. Per cell and flavour the node space is `C^2_spin ⊗ C^4`, with `C^4` = two chiralities times two tastes. For `μ > 0` the rest states (`K = 0`) split into the eigenspaces `R_e = ±1` of the exact `V(0) = e^{iμR_e}`; each is invariant under the `S_k` and is spin 1/2 times `C^2`.

*Proof.* Theorem 3.5 gives `N_j = v G_j` with one `v`. Lemma 3.7 gives the internal part `S^N_k = −(i/4) Σ ε_{kab} G_aG_b`, which equals `S_k` by Theorem 3.6(a). Casimir `3/4` on a finite-dimensional unitary `su(2)` module forces every irreducible summand to be spin 1/2. The five-mode cases are `⊗ 1_5`, and the `L5-O` case is conjugation by a constant unitary, which keeps every relation. For `μ > 0`, `R_e` commutes with each `S_k` (Theorem 3.6(a)). ∎

*Remark (no spin from taste).* `h_1` also commutes with the internal unitaries `𝒦_int = {W : W G_j W^* = G_j}`, which contain the taste rotations. A group `{S(g) τ(g)}` with a nontrivial homomorphism `τ: SU(2) → 𝒦_int` would also act covariantly on `h_1` and would change the spins, as in the spin-from-isospin effect: for example `τ` = spin 1/2 on a taste doublet turns `1/2 ⊗ 1/2` into spins 0 and 1. Such a group does not commute with `𝒦_int`: SU(2) is perfect, so a nontrivial `τ` has a non-central image. ER's group `J = L + S` does commute with `𝒦_int`, because `S` lies in the algebra generated by the `G_j`. So ER is the choice that commutes with the internal symmetries, and it is fixed by the boosts.

**Corollary 3.9 (what lifts the cube's double cover to SU(2) at long distance).** At the free node:
(a) The cell rotations act on odd states in the spinor class (Theorem 3.3), and on the four states of each chirality irreducibly (Theorem 3.6(e)).
(b) Each lattice rotation is `ρ̂(Q) = S(Q̃) T(Q)`. The spin factor `Q ↦ ±S(Q̃)` is the restriction, to the binary octahedral group `{±Q̃ : Q ∈ O} ⊂ SU(2)`, of the internal action of ER's group.
(c) `ρ̂(Q)` equals a phase times an element of that action exactly when `Q` is the identity or a face half-turn.
(d) The taste factor `e^{iεR_e/2} T(Q) e^{−iεR_e/2}` commutes with every `G_j`, so it is an internal symmetry of `h_1`, and `S(Q̃)` is then a symmetry of `h_1` by itself.
(e) The taste factor has no continuous extension: there is no continuous homomorphism `φ: SU(2) → U(2)` and no function `c: O → U(1)` with `T_+(Q) = c(Q) φ(Q̃)` for all `Q`.
So, within Definition ER, what lifts the double cover into SU(2) at long distance is exactly ER's condition: one isotropic cone for the light generator. It makes ER's group exist, contains the spin factor of every lattice rotation, and makes the taste factor an internal symmetry.

*Proof.* (b) is the definition of `S(Q̃)` with Theorem 3.8. (c) If `ρ̂(Q) = λ S(g)`, then `ρ̂(Q)` commutes with every `A_j`, so `π = id` by Lemma 3.2, and `Q` is diagonal. The converse is Theorem 3.6(d). (d) `ρ̂(Q)` commutes with `R_e = (ΣA_j)/√3` (Lemma 3.2), and `S(Q̃)` commutes with `R_e`, so conjugating `ρ̂ = S T` by `e^{iεR_e/2}` gives `ρ̂ = S·(e^{iεR_e/2} T e^{−iεR_e/2})`, and the second factor commutes with each `G_j` because `T` commutes with each `Γ_j`. (e) Let `C_0 = {i n·σ : |n| = 1}`, the trace-zero elements of SU(2). Both lifts `±iσ_1` of the face half-turn `h_1` lie in `C_0`, and `T_+(h_1)` is a scalar (Theorem 3.6(d)), so `λ := φ(h̃_1)` is a scalar for the chosen lift `h̃_1`. Every element of `C_0` is `g h̃_1 g^{-1}` for some `g ∈ SU(2)`: for unit `n`, the lift `g` of a rotation taking `±e_1` to `n` gives `g (±iσ_1) g^{-1} = i n·σ`. ### 3.6 The cell group does not force one cone

At the free node ER's condition holds (Theorem 3.5). The question for the interacting laws is whether symmetry keeps it. The answer is no for the cell group, even with inversion and the free walk's antiunitary symmetry.

Weyl's inequality for Hermitian matrices puts the eigenvalues of `H^2/k^2` within `O(u^2)` of `1 + (2u/3) y`, so the speeds are `1 + y u/3 + O(u^2)`. `Y` is a sum of six distinct cubic monomials `Γ_iΓ_jA_k` with coefficients `±i`; by Lemma 3.1(c) they are orthogonal and traceless, so `Y ≠ 0` and `tr Y = 0`. So ER's condition fails for small `u ≠ 0`. (c) With the convention of section 1.1, `T_{2e_j}|K, r⟩ = e^{−iK_j}|K, r⟩`, and `Z_j` commutes with even translations. (d) A2's last paragraph: "The resulting operation sends \(V_e\) to \(e^{ia_eR_o}\) and \(V_o\) to \(e^{-ia_oR_e}\). These equal \(V_o,V_e\), respectively, exactly when \(a_e=a_o\)". ### 3.7 The open lemma

**Lemma MT1-S (open).** On `L5-O` or `L5-U`, in the vacuum of VC1, the light fermion-odd excitations have a node whose leading generator meets ER's condition (one isotropic cone).

**MT1-S implies MT1(a)(2).** Lemma 3.7 holds for every generator that meets ER's condition, and the internal part `S^N` then has Casimir `3/4`; so every light mode is spin 1/2 under ER's group, whatever its taste or flavour. Theorem 3.3 already gives the spinor class to every odd state of the dressed laws, so a light fermion is half-integral at the lattice level with or without MT1-S. MT1-S decides whether "half-integral" becomes "spin 1/2 under an emergent rotation group". This includes composite light fermions, such as the one-site family operators of `CH1.md` Corollary U-T″: their states are odd, so they carry the spinor class, and their spin 1/2 needs MT1-S for their own leading generator. MT1-S is the fermion part of LZ1-i ("a mechanism that fixes one common `c` across species", `TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` line 320), applied to the tastes and directions of one species.

**Attack A1 (symmetry).** Aim: the law's symmetry group forces every allowed marginal one-body term at the node into ER's form. *Fails at "forces".* Theorem 3.10 gives an allowed term, `u Z^G`, that breaks ER's condition at first order. It keeps the cell rotations, inversion and `Θ`, and it is flavour-blind, so it is also invariant under every internal symmetry acting on flavour. Of the symmetries named here only `σ` forbids it, and `σ` exists only at `μ = 0` and `τ_E = 0`. A symmetry of the dressed laws acting on residues outside this list is not known; LW1-5-T asks the analogous classification question for site frames. At `μ = 0 = τ_E` the [check] says that the cell rotations, inversion, `Θ` and `σ` leave only the free direction. Proving that by hand, proving that the dressed tick keeps `Θ` there, and proving that the interacting node carries the free point's symmetry action would give MT1-S on that set of constants.

**Attack A2 (continuity from the free walk).** Aim: the interacting generator is a small perturbation of the free one, and ER's condition persists. *Fails at two steps.* First, no small parameter joins the dressed laws to the free walk: `LW1_5.md` inferred definition 4 says "A free tick is an auxiliary c-number replacement of link operators; it is not the interacting tick or its vacuum", and the registers are fixed finite quantum links with no coupling that sends them to c-numbers. Second, even granted smallness, ER's condition is not an open condition: Theorem 3.10(b) gives arbitrarily small allowed perturbations that break it.

**Attack A3 (renormalization flow).** Aim: marginal anisotropies flow to one isotropic cone at long distance. Anber and Donoghue (arXiv:1102.0789, abstract) find that fields with different limiting velocities at a high scale can share one limiting velocity at low energies because of interactions, and that "The differences normally vanish only logarithmically". *Fails at "a controlled flow exists".* No renormalization flow of the gauged Floquet laws is constructed (ZM1-i is open). The term here is a matrix-valued anisotropy among the tastes and directions of one species, which the cited abstract does not treat. A logarithmic approach would also leave a Lorentz-violating remainder whose size is LZ1's question.

So MT1-S is open after three named attacks. No counterexample is known: the free walk satisfies it, and Theorem 3.10 shows only that symmetry does not force it.

## 4. MT1(a)(3): enough modes per cell for one family

### 4.1 Three readings

MT1(a)(3) asks that the excitations "per cell, carry enough modes for one family's chiral content (a 16 of `Spin(10)`, or the Standard Model multiplet)". MT1-ii names "the 16 per cell". The words admit three readings.

- **M1 (count).** The light one-body content of one cell, counted in two-component Weyl fields with right-handed fields counted by their left-handed conjugates, is at least 16 (15 without the right-handed neutrino). Four tests: (1) it fixes no target and can fail (it does on `L_cube`, below); (2) it is closed; (3) it settles no downstream question, since it claims no representation and no chirality; (4) it adds no structure. **Passes.**
- **M2 (free content).** The light one-body content of one cell at the free node, as a representation of the law's internal symmetry, contains one family: the 16 of `Spin(10)`, or `10 ⊕ 5bar` (with or without `1`) of `U(5)`. Four tests: (1) it carries CG1's content into MT1 at the free level, but it can fail and fixes nothing; (2) closed; (3) it settles no downstream question, since chirality stays with CH1 (`CH1.md` F3 makes free content vector-like in its pocket sense); (4) no structure. **Passes.**
- **M3 (operator content).** Some fermion-odd local operator space carried at one site, in the sense of `CH1.md` inferred definition 7, transforms as one family. Four tests: (1) it is CH1's own reading and fixes no MT1 target, since the frozen results decide it; (2) closed; (3) it settles nothing beyond those results; (4) no structure. **Passes.**

### 4.2 The free content per cell

**Proposition 4.1.** Let `μ = 0`. For either five-mode law, the light one-body space of one cell at the free node is `C^8 ⊗ C^5 = C^2_spin ⊗ C^2_chirality ⊗ C^2_taste ⊗ C^5`. It carries 20 two-component Weyl fields: 10 of each chirality, two tastes times five flavours. As left-handed fields, the content is `2·(5 ⊕ 5bar)` of the global `U(5)` of the `L5-U` free walk, with `U(1)_N` charges `±1`, and `2·10` (two vectors) of the global `SO(10)` of the `L5-O` free walk. Neither contains the `10` of `SU(5)` or the 16 of `Spin(10)`. For `L_cube` the same count is 4 Weyl fields per cell.

*Proof.* Theorem 3.5 and Theorem 3.6(b) give, per flavour, `C^8 = C^2_spin ⊗ C^4` with `C^4` two chiralities times two tastes, and each `C^2_spin` factor with fixed chirality and taste is one two-component Weyl field of the leading generator `v K·G`. The free walks are `V ⊗ 1_5` and `(WVW^*) ⊗ 1_5` (`LW1_5.md` line 255), so flavour multiplies the count by five. For `L5-U`, `V ⊗ 1_5` commutes with `1 ⊗ U(5)`, and the five matter modes of a site carry the defining representation, so the fields of one chirality are a `5` and the conjugates of the other chirality's fields are a `5bar`. For `L5-O`, `LW1_5.md` line 257 gives the free hop "\(h^{O,\rm free}_{xy}=i\sum_j(c^\dagger_{y,j}c_{x,j}-c^\dagger_{x,j}c_{y,j})\)", which is the replacement of \(U^{ab}\) by \(\delta^{ab}\) in `(i/2) Σ γ_{y,a} U^{ab} γ_{x,b}` and is invariant under one `SO(10)` rotation of the ten Majoranas at every site. The left-handed fields of one taste are the five complex fields of one chirality and the conjugates of the five of the other, which together form the real ten-dimensional vector. The `10` of `SU(5)` and the 16 of `Spin(10)` are not summands of `5`, `5bar` or the vector. For `L_cube` the flavour factor is absent. ∎

The count does not depend on the vacuum: it is the multiplicity of the free node. The content is vector-like, as `CH1.md` Theorem F3 requires of free updates, and as the paragraph after Lemma O-A records: "The corresponding O carrier is the real 10 ... The elementary U \(5\) also contains no \(Q\)."
