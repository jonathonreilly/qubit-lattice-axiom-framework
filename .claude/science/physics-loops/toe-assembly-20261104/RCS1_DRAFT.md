## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**Proved for both laws.** The local centre elements `c_z` that pin LZ1's odd bands lie in the centre of the gauge-invariant algebra `𝔄^G`, so every factor representation of `𝔄^G` has a fixed sign configuration `κ` (Theorem 1). Matter parity of any finite region equals, up to that configuration, a product of link fluxes over its boundary: a `Z_2` Gauss law (Theorem 2; the lattice analogue of Kijowski–Rudolph's global Gauss law). Hence an odd charge localized in a finite region always changes `κ` there, and then no cell translation is implemented; an odd sector with implemented translations is never localized (Theorem 3). This is the precise sense in which R-loc pins and an R-CS must carry flux to infinity. No fermion-odd element of `𝔄` is gauge invariant, so FMS-type odd composites do not exist (Theorem 4). A new general obstruction: if the tick preserves an asymptotic label that translations shift in a direction `j`, every band is flat in direction `j` at each node (Lemma B, Theorem 5); LZ1 Theorem 2.2's node statement is the case where every direction is shifted. **Decided under the cleanest candidate**, the Fredenhagen–Marcu string limit `R-CS^str(e)`: in an explicit D1 state `ω_AB` of `L5-U[m]`, `m ∈ {2,4}`, stationary for the base tick at all constants, the string sectors exist, translations and the tick are implemented on them, and every band in every covariant representation built from them has `N_2 = N_3 = 0` (for `e = e_1`). So the asserted odd Clifford node does not exist there (Theorem 6). In flavour-locked frozen states the string sectors are empty (Proposition 6.1). **Counterexamples.** To "an R-CS^str sector family with implemented translations and tick carries an odd Clifford node" (Theorem 6; it leaves open motion along the ray, `N_1`), and to "FMS composites supply the odd excitations" (Theorem 4). **Open by name.** RCS-P (§8): whether some D1 state of an update in LZ1's class has an `R-CS^str` sector family with no tick-conserved transverse label and an odd band of nonzero slope; three attacks failed (A1 frozen backgrounds, A2 the free-link limit, A3 loop condensation without a plaquette class), and one kill attempt failed. ## 1. Setting and definitions

These are precisifications for this file, not adopted readings. The laws, the update class, the centre implementers and the band vocabulary are those of `LZ1.md` §1 (lines 21-31).

1. **Algebras.** `𝔄` is the quasi-local algebra (`VC1_5.md` definition 1). `𝔄^G` is the fixed algebra of all local gauge automorphisms (`VC1_5.md` definition 3); local elements of `𝔄^G` are dense in it (`VC1_5.md` Theorem 2.1). The local gauge group at `z` is `G_z = U(5)` on `L5-U[m]`, acting through `exp(i𝒢^T_z)`, and the image of `Spin(10)` on `L5-O[S_+]`, acting through `exp(iΣθ_{cd}𝒢^{cd}_z)` (`LW1_5.md` §2 and §4 Q and G, U).
2. **Centre elements.** `c_z = exp(iπQ_z)`, `Q_z = N_z + Σ_{ℓ∋z} p_{ℓ,z}`, on `L5-U`; `c_z = ω_z = ∏_{j=1}^5 exp(iπ𝒢_z^{2j−1,2j})`, the lift of `−1_{10}`, on `L5-O[S_+]` (`LZ1.md` line 25).
3. **Regions.** For a finite set `Λ` of sites, `L_int(Λ)` is the set of links with both ends in `Λ`, `∂Λ` the set of links with exactly one end in `Λ`, and `in(ℓ)` that end. `P_Λ = ∏_{z∈Λ}(−1)^{N_z}` is matter parity of `Λ`.
4. **Link flux.** On `L5-U`, `f_{ℓ,z} = (−1)^{p_{ℓ,z}}`. On `L5-O[S_+]`, `f_{ℓ,z} = Γ^z_ℓ = i^5η^z_1⋯η^z_{10}`, the end chirality of `LW1_5.md` §2; on `S_+` both ends agree (`LW1_5.md` A2-5; `LZ1.md` Theorem 5.2's proof), so write `f_ℓ`. For `Λ` put `F_{∂Λ} = ∏_{ℓ∈∂Λ} f_{ℓ,in(ℓ)}`.
5. **Covariant representation.** A tuple `(H, π, T, Ũ)`: `π` is a representation of `𝔄^G` on a separable Hilbert space `H`; `β ↦ T_β` is a unitary representation of `Z^3`; `Ũ` is unitary; `T_βπ(A)T_β^* = π(τ_β(A))`, `Ũπ(A)Ũ^* = π(α(A))` and `ŨT_β = T_βŨ`. Here `τ_β` is the cell translation by `2β` and `α` the tick automorphism of an update in LZ1's class.
6. **Bands.** In a covariant representation, a band `(d, F, V)`, a node, the leading generator `N_j = ∂_jh(K_0)` with `h = i log V`, and a Clifford cone are defined word for word as in `LZ1.md` line 29, with `T_β, Ũ` as above. The proofs in §4 use only `T`, `Ũ` and separability. So they apply verbatim to LZ1's own setting, the GNS representation of a D1 state on `𝔄`.
7. **Configurations.** A representation `π` of `𝔄^G` is a *factor representation* if `π(𝔄^G)''` has trivial centre. Its *configuration* is `κ_π(z)`, defined in Theorem 1(c). Representations are *disjoint* if no nonzero operator intertwines them, and *quasi-equivalent* if each subrepresentation of one has a subrepresentation equivalent to a subrepresentation of the other; for factor representations quasi-equivalence is the same as not being disjoint.
8. **Localization.** For a finite site set `Λ`, `𝔄_{Λ^c}` is the C*-algebra generated by matter at sites outside `Λ` and by the registers of links with no end in `Λ`; `𝔄^G_{Λ^c} = 𝔄_{Λ^c} ∩ 𝔄^G`. A representation `π` is *localized in `Λ` relative to* `π_0` if `π|𝔄^G_{Λ^c}` and `π_0|𝔄^G_{Λ^c}` are unitarily equivalent. This is the lattice form of the Doplicher–Haag–Roberts criterion (credited, NOT VERIFIED). `Λ̂` is the set of sites at graph distance at most one from `Λ`.

## 2. The centre of the observable algebra and the `Z_2` Gauss law

**Theorem 1 (centre superselection; both laws).** For every update in LZ1's class:
(a) each `c_z` lies in `𝔄^G` and commutes with every element of `𝔄^G`; so `c_z` lies in the centre of `𝔄^G`;
(b) `α(c_z) = c_z` and `τ_β(c_z) = c_{z+2β}`;
(c) in every factor representation `π` of `𝔄^G`, each `π(c_z)` is a scalar `κ_π(z)`, with `κ_π(z)^2 = c_z^2`, a fixed scalar (`1` on `L5-U`, `−1` on `L5-O[S_+]`);
(d) quasi-equivalent factor representations have the same configuration; and `π∘α` has the configuration of `π`, while `π∘τ_β` has `z ↦ κ_π(z+2β)`;
(e) so if a factor representation `π` is quasi-equivalent to `π∘τ_β` for one `β`, then `κ_π(z+2β) = κ_π(z)` for every `z`.

*Proof.* (a) `c_z` implements a central element `g_c` of `G_z`: on `L5-U` the central `U(1)` is generated by `Q_z = 𝒢^{1}_z` and `c_z` implements `−1_5`; on `L5-O`, `−1_{10}` lifts to the central element `ω` of `Spin(10)` (`LW1_5.md` §4, centre proof item 2: "the centre is {±1,±ω}"). The implementers of `G_z` form a representation (`LW1_5.md` §4: the Lie algebra representation "integrates to its simply connected cover"; on `L5-U`, `[𝒢^1_z, 𝒢^T_z] = 0` for every `T`). So `u_g c_z u_g^* = u_{g g_c g^{-1}} = c_z` for `g ∈ G_z`. For `w ≠ z` the implementers of `G_w` act on site `w` and on the `w`-ends of links at `w`; `c_z` acts on site `z` and on `z`-ends. Site factors are even operators on different sites, and the two end actions on one link commute (`LW1_5.md` F and K: "commuting endpoint Spin(10) actions"; on `L5-U` they are even bilinears in disjoint rishon modes). So every local gauge automorphism fixes `c_z`, and `c_z ∈ 𝔄^G`. For `A ∈ 𝔄^G`, `Ad c_z` is a local gauge automorphism, so `c_zAc_z^* = A`. (b) Every gate in the update commutes with `c_z` (`LZ1.md` line 25: "Every gauge-invariant gate commutes with them"); the tick automorphism is built from finitely many gates near `z`, so `α(c_z) = c_z`. Translation covariance is stated in `LZ1.md` line 25. (c) `π(c_z)` commutes with `π(𝔄^G)` by (a) and lies in it, so it lies in the centre of `π(𝔄^G)''`, which is `C1`. `c_z^2` is `exp(2πiQ_z) = 1`, because `Q_z` has integer spectrum, or `ω_z^2`, the lift of the order-two central element, which is the scalar `−1` on every end-pure register (`LW1_5.md` Σ: "z_x=−ε^6=−1"). (d) A quasi-equivalence is implemented by an isomorphism `π(𝔄^G)'' → π'(𝔄^G)''` with `π(A) ↦ π'(A)`; it sends the scalar `π(c_z)` to `π'(c_z)`. The other two claims follow from (b): `π(α(c_z)) = π(c_z)` and `π(τ_β(c_z)) = π(c_{z+2β})`. (e) follows from (d). ∎

**Theorem 2 (the `Z_2` Gauss law; both laws).** For every finite site set `Λ`,
\[
 \prod_{z\in\Lambda} c_z \;=\; s_\Lambda\, P_\Lambda\, F_{\partial\Lambda},
\]
with the scalar `s_Λ = (−1)^{m|L_int(Λ)|}` on `L5-U[m]` and `s_Λ = (−i)^{|Λ|}(−1)^{|L_int(Λ)|} i^{|∂Λ|}` on `L5-O[S_+]`. Every factor in the identity lies in `𝔄^G`. Hence in every factor representation `π` of `𝔄^G`,
\[
 \pi(P_\Lambda)\,\pi(F_{\partial\Lambda}) \;=\; s_\Lambda^{-1}\prod_{z\in\Lambda}\kappa_\pi(z)\cdot 1 .
\]
The matter parity of every finite region is, up to a scalar fixed by the configuration, the product of the link fluxes through its boundary.

*Proof.* *`L5-U`.* `Q_z` is a sum of commuting integer-valued number operators, so `exp(iπQ_z) = (−1)^{N_z}∏_{ℓ∋z}(−1)^{p_{ℓ,z}}`. All these factors commute. In the product over `Λ` an interior link contributes `(−1)^{p_{ℓ,x}+p_{ℓ,y}} = (−1)^m`, by the register constraint `p_{ℓ,x}+p_{ℓ,y} = m`; a boundary link contributes `f_{ℓ,in(ℓ)}`. *`L5-O[S_+]`.* For one site or one end, write `X_{ab} = (i/2)χ_aχ_b` for Majoranas `χ`. Then `X_{ab}^2 = 1/4`, so `exp(iπX_{ab}) = 2iX_{ab} = −χ_aχ_b`. The five factors commute, so `∏_{j=1}^5 exp(iπX_{2j−1,2j}) = (−1)^5χ_1⋯χ_{10}`. For matter, `χ = γ_z` and `P_z = (−i)^5γ_{z,1}⋯γ_{z,10}` (`LW1_5.md` §1), so the site factor of `ω_z` is `−iP_z`, as `LW1_5.md` states. For the `z`-end of a link, the end generators `L^{cd}_{ℓ,z} = (i/2)η^z_cη^z_d` have the same form (`LW1_5.md` §2), and `Γ^z_ℓ = i^5η^z_1⋯η^z_{10}`, so the end factor is `iΓ^z_ℓ`. Site factors at different sites, end factors on different links, and the two end factors of one link all commute (as in Theorem 1(a)). An interior link contributes `(iΓ^x_ℓ)(iΓ^y_ℓ) = −1`, because `Γ^x_ℓ = Γ^y_ℓ` on `S_+` and `(Γ^x_ℓ)^2 = −(η^x_1⋯η^x_{10})^2 = 1`. This matches `LW1_5.md` line 199: "Their product is η_1⋯η_20=−Γ, which is −1 on S_+". A boundary link contributes `iΓ_ℓ`. Collect the scalars. *Invariance.* `P_z` commutes with the even site generators, `p_{ℓ,z}` with the end generators of `U(5)_z`, and `Γ^z_ℓ` with the even end generators; each commutes with the generators at every other site. The displayed consequence follows from Theorem 1(c). ∎

*Remarks.* (i) This is the `Z_2` analogue of Kijowski–Rudolph's global Gauss law (arXiv:hep-th/0104052, Eq. (5.8), "Φ∂Λ = QΛ"), with the lifted centre element in the rôle of their `Z_3` triality and the gauged fermion parity in the rôle of the global colour charge. The proof above is native and uses only the law's operators. (ii) Theorem 2 is the operator identity behind LZ1's line 25 ("the product of their adjoint actions ... equals matter parity θ"): conjugating by both sides of Theorem 2 gives LZ1's statement for operators supported inside `Λ`, since `F_{∂Λ}` then commutes with them.

## 3. Localized odd sectors carry no translation; translation-covariant odd sectors are not localized

**Theorem 3 (both laws).** Let `π_0` and `π` be factor representations of `𝔄^G`, and let `π` be localized in a finite `Λ` relative to `π_0` (definition 8), through a unitary `V`.
(a) `κ_π(z) = κ_{π_0}(z)` for every `z ∉ Λ̂`.
(b) Put `χ(π:π_0) = ∏_{z∈Λ̂} κ_π(z)/κ_{π_0}(z) ∈ {±1}`. For every finite `Λ' ⊇ Λ̂`,
\[
 \pi(P_{\Lambda'}) \;=\; \chi(\pi:\pi_0)\; V^{-1}\pi_0(P_{\Lambda'})V .
\]
So `χ` is the relative matter parity of `π`: the parity of every large region is `χ` times the transported parity of `π_0`.
(c) If `χ(π:π_0) = −1`, then `κ_π ≠ κ_{π_0}` at some site of `Λ̂`. If moreover `κ_{π_0}` is cell-periodic, then `π∘τ_β` and `π` are disjoint for every `β ≠ 0`.
(d) Consequently, if `κ_{π_0}` is cell-periodic and `π` is quasi-equivalent to `π∘τ_β` for one `β ≠ 0`, then `χ(π:π_0) = +1`: a factor representation with odd relative parity and one implemented cell translation is localized in no finite region relative to `π_0`.

*Proof.* (a) For `z ∉ Λ̂` no neighbour of `z` lies in `Λ`, so `c_z ∈ 𝔄^G_{Λ^c}`. Then `κ_π(z)1 = V^{-1}π_0(c_z)V = κ_{π_0}(z)1`. (b) Each link of `∂Λ'` has its inner end in `Λ'` and, since `Λ' ⊇ Λ̂`, neither end in `Λ`; so `F_{∂Λ'} ∈ 𝔄^G_{Λ^c}` and `V^{-1}π_0(F_{∂Λ'})V = π(F_{∂Λ'})`. Theorem 2 in `π` and in `π_0` gives `π(P_{Λ'}) = s^{-1}∏_{Λ'}κ_π·π(F)^{-1}` and `V^{-1}π_0(P_{Λ'})V = s^{-1}∏_{Λ'}κ_{π_0}·V^{-1}π_0(F)^{-1}V`. Insert `V^{-1}π_0(F)^{-1}V = π(F)^{-1}` and divide; by (a) the ratio of the two products over `Λ'` is the product over `Λ̂`, which is `χ`. Each ratio `κ_π(z)/κ_{π_0}(z)` is `±1` because both squares equal `c_z^2`. (c) If every ratio on `Λ̂` were `1`, then `χ = 1`. Let `D = {z : κ_π(z) ≠ κ_{π_0}(z)}`; it is finite by (a) and nonempty. By Theorem 1(d), `π∘τ_β` has configuration `κ_π(·+2β)`, which differs from `κ_{π_0}(·+2β) = κ_{π_0}` exactly on `D − 2β`. A finite nonempty subset of `Z^3` is not invariant under a nonzero translation. So `κ_{π∘τ_β} ≠ κ_π`, and the two factor representations are disjoint by Theorem 1(d). (d) is the contrapositive of (c). ∎

**How LZ1's pattern sectors fit.** Let `ω` be a D1 state whose restriction to `𝔄^G` is a factor state, so that every `c_z` is sharp, `π(c_z)Ω = κ_0(z)Ω`. If a local `A` has pattern `ε` (`Ad c_z(A) = ε_zA`), then `π(c_z)π(A)Ω = ε_zπ(A)π(c_z)Ω = ε_zκ_0(z)π(A)Ω`. So on LZ1's pattern subspace `H(ε)` (`LZ1.md` Theorem 2.1) the central values are `ε_zκ_0(z)`: a pattern is exactly a change of configuration. Every odd vector lies in a nontrivial pattern (`LZ1.md` Theorem 2.1). Theorem 3 says this is forced, not an accident of R-loc: any odd charge that is localized, in the sense of definition 8, changes the configuration somewhere, and the configuration is conserved by the tick (Theorem 1(b)) and moved by translations. An R-CS whose odd sectors carry translations must therefore keep the vacuum configuration everywhere and carry the odd parity in the boundary flux of arbitrarily large regions (Theorem 2). This is the lattice counterpart of the Buchholz–Fredenhagen "gauge-charge" (charges "measured at spacelike infinity", NOT VERIFIED beyond a search snippet) and of the non-localizability statement of Buchholz–Doplicher–Morchio–Roberts–Strocchi (arXiv:hep-th/9705089, Theorem 1).

**Theorem 4 (no gauge-invariant odd element; FMS composites; both laws).**
(a) Every element of `𝔄^G` is even under matter parity `θ`. In particular no fermion-odd local, quasi-local or composite operator is gauge invariant.
(b) Let `A ∈ 𝔄` be *carried at* `x`: `a(A) = A` for every local gauge automorphism `a` at every site `y ≠ x`. Then `Ad c_x(A) = θ(A)`. So a bosonic (even) element carried at `x` is fixed by `Ad c_x`, an odd one is negated, and every FMS-type product `Φ^†ψ` of an even `Φ` carried at `x` with an odd `ψ` carried at `x` is odd under `Ad c_x`, hence not gauge invariant.

*Proof.* (a) For local `A ∈ 𝔄^G`, take a finite `Λ` containing its matter sites and both ends of its links. `LZ1.md` line 25: the product of the adjoint actions of `c_z`, `z ∈ Λ`, "equals matter parity θ" on `A`. Each factor fixes `A`, so `θ(A) = A`. Local gauge-invariant elements are dense in `𝔄^G` and `θ` is continuous. (b) In the same product over `Λ ∋ x`, every factor with `z ≠ x` fixes `A`, because `Ad c_z` is a local gauge automorphism at `z`. So `θ(A) = Ad c_x(A)`. For the product, `Ad c_x(Φ^†ψ) = Φ^†·(−ψ)`. ∎

This is the FMS form of `EW1.md` Theorem 4.1 ("`c_F` acts as `−1` on every fermion-odd local operator and as `+1` on every bosonic one; it is a gauged fermion parity"), now for odd composites rather than for Higgs data. The gauged fermion parity is the reason no gauge-invariant composite can carry an odd band, on either law.

## 4. A conserved transverse label forces transverse flatness

**Lemma B (boost lemma).** Let `H` be separable, `β ↦ T_β` a unitary representation of `Z^3` on `H`, and `Ũ` a unitary commuting with every `T_β`. Let `n ∈ Z^3∖{0}` and `θ_* > 0`. Suppose that for each `θ ∈ [0,θ_*)` there is a unitary `B_θ` with `B_θŨ = ŨB_θ` and `B_θT_βB_θ^{-1} = e^{iθ n·β}T_β` for all `β`. Then for every band `(d,F,V)` and every node `K_0` there is `s_0 > 0` with `V(K_0+sn) = 1` for `|s| < s_0`. In particular `Σ_j n_jN_j = 0`.

*Proof.* *Step 0.* Choose an open ball `U ∋ K_0` on which `V` is `C^1` and `‖V(K) − 1‖ < 1`, and put `h = i log V` with the principal power series. Then `h` is `C^1` and Hermitian, `V = e^{−ih}`, `h(K_0) = 0`; shrink `U` so that `‖h‖ < π/2` on it.

*Step 1 (boosted bands).* Let `(S_θψ)(K) = ψ(K − θn)` on `L^2(T^3;C^d)`; it is well defined because `n` is an integer vector. One checks `M_βS_θ = e^{iθn·β}S_θM_β` and `M_VS_θ = S_θM_{V_θ}` with `V_θ(K) = V(K+θn)`. Put `G_θ = B_θFS_θ`, an isometry. From `T_βB_θ = e^{−iθn·β}B_θT_β`,
\[
 T_\beta G_\theta = e^{-i\theta n\cdot\beta}B_\theta F M_\beta S_\theta = B_\theta F S_\theta M_\beta = G_\theta M_\beta,\qquad
 \widetilde U G_\theta = B_\theta F M_V S_\theta = G_\theta M_{V_\theta}.
\]

*Step 2 (spectral intertwining).* The commuting unitaries `T_{e_1}, T_{e_2}, T_{e_3}, Ũ` have a joint projection-valued measure `P` on `T^3 × S^1`. Let `X` be a bounded operator from `L^2(T^3;C^d)` to `H`, and `w` a measurable function `T^3 → S^1`, with `T_βX = XM_β` and `ŨX = XM_w` (scalar multiplication). Then `P(Γ)X = XM_{1_Γ(K,w(K))}` for every Borel `Γ`. Indeed the identity holds for the four generators and their adjoints (`T_j^*X = XM_j^*` follows from unitarity), hence for every polynomial in them, hence by Stone–Weierstrass for every continuous function on `T^3 × S^1`; both sides are then regular projection-valued integrals that agree on continuous functions, so they agree on Borel sets.

*Step 3 (no eigenvalue branch moves along `n`).* Put `φ_p = tr h^p`, `p = 1,…,d`, which are `C^1` on `U`. Suppose `n·∇φ_p ≠ 0` at some point of `U` for some `p`. By continuity it is nonzero on an open ball `W ⊂ U`. The number of distinct eigenvalues of `h(K)` is lower semicontinuous, so it attains its maximum over `W` on an open ball `W' ⊂ W` and is constant there. On `W'` the distinct eigenvalues `λ_1 < ⋯ < λ_D` are continuous, their Riesz projections `P_r(K) = (2πi)^{-1}∮_{C_r}(z − h(K))^{-1}dz` are `C^1` with constant ranks, and `λ_r = tr(hP_r)/rank P_r` is `C^1`. Since `φ_p = Σ_r rank(P_r)λ_r^p`, some `r` has `n·∇λ_r ≠ 0` somewhere in `W'`. Choose a ball `B` and `s_1 > 0` with `B̄ + [0,s_1]n ⊂ W'` and `n·∇λ_r ≥ c > 0` on `B̄ + [0,s_1]n` (if the sign is negative, run the same argument with `n·∇λ_r ≤ −c`). For `θ ∈ [0, θ_1)`, `θ_1 = min(θ_*, s_1)`, let `Q_θ(K) = 1_B(K)P_r(K+θn)`, let `w_θ(K) = e^{−iλ_r(K+θn)}` on `B` and `1` elsewhere, and `X_θ = G_θM_{Q_θ}`. Then `V_θQ_θ = w_θQ_θ`, so `T_βX_θ = X_θM_β` and `ŨX_θ = X_θM_{w_θ}`. `X_θ ≠ 0`, because `G_θ` is isometric and `Q_θ ≠ 0` on the set `B` of positive measure. Let `Γ_θ = {(K, e^{−iλ_r(K+θn)}) : K ∈ B̄}`, a compact set. By Step 2, `P(Γ_θ)X_θ = X_θ`. For `θ' ≠ θ` and `K ∈ B`, `(K, w_{θ'}(K)) ∈ Γ_θ` would force `λ_r(K+θ'n) = λ_r(K+θn)`, since both lie in `(−π/2, π/2)`; this is false because `s ↦ λ_r(K+sn)` is strictly monotone on `[0,s_1]`. So `P(Γ_θ)X_{θ'} = 0`. Hence the closed ranges of the `X_θ`, `θ ∈ [0,θ_1)`, are uncountably many mutually orthogonal nonzero subspaces of `H`. Unit vectors chosen in them are pairwise at distance `√2`, which contradicts separability.

*Step 4.* So `n·∇φ_p = 0` on `U` for every `p`. By Newton's identities the characteristic polynomial of `h(K)` is constant along each segment `K + sn` in `U`. At `K_0` it is `λ^d`. So `h(K_0+sn)` is Hermitian with spectrum `{0}`, hence zero, for `|s| < s_0`. Differentiate at `s = 0`. ∎

**Theorem 5 (conserved labels).** Let `(H, T, Ũ)` be as in Lemma B. Suppose `H = ⊕_{c∈C} H_c` (orthogonal), `Z^3` acts on the index set `C`, `ŨH_c = H_c` and `T_βH_c = H_{β·c}` for all `c, β`, and there are a real function `q` on `C` and `n ∈ Z^3∖{0}` with `q(β·c) = q(c) + n·β`. Then every band in `H` satisfies `V(K_0+sn) = 1` near each node and `Σ_jn_jN_j = 0`. In particular no band in `H` has a Clifford cone at any node.

*Proof.* Let `Π_c` project onto `H_c` and `B_θ = Σ_c e^{iθq(c)}Π_c`. `Ũ` preserves each `H_c` and is unitary, so it commutes with each `Π_c` and with `B_θ`. `T_βΠ_cT_β^{-1} = Π_{β·c}`, so `T_βB_θT_β^{-1} = Σ_c e^{iθ(q(c)−n·β)}Π_c = e^{−iθn·β}B_θ`. Apply Lemma B. A Clifford cone needs `(Σ_jn_jN_j)^2 = v^2|n|^2 1` with `v > 0`. ∎

**Corollary 5.1 (LZ1 Theorem 2.2 at nodes, recovered).** In LZ1's decomposition `H_nt = ⊕_κ H(κ)` over nontrivial finite patterns (`LZ1.md` Theorem 2.1), `Z^3` acts freely on patterns. Choose one pattern in each orbit and set `q_j(τ_β κ_rep) = β_j`. Theorem 5 with `n = e_1, e_2, e_3` gives `N = 0` at every node of every band in `H_nt`, in particular of every odd band. LZ1 proves more (flatness on a whole neighbourhood); the point here is that the mechanism is only "a tick-conserved label moved by translations", which will reappear in charged sectors.

*Remark (what Lemma B needs and what it does not).* It does not use the Gauss law, finiteness of registers, or any property of the law beyond the commutation relations of `B_θ`. It fails, as it must, when no such `B_θ` exists: for example when the sector labels form a single translation orbit up to equivalence, so that sectors related by translations are not disjoint.

## 5. Candidate (a): the Fredenhagen–Marcu string limit

**Definition (R-CS^str(e); proposed reading, not adopted).** Fix a lattice axis `e`. Let `ω` be a locally gauge-invariant D1 state. For a site `x` and `r ≥ 1` let `y_i = x + ie` and `ℓ_i = (y_{i−1}, y_i)`, `i = 1,…,2r`, the straight path of `2r` links. Let `T_i` be the transport matrix of `ℓ_i` read from `y_i` to `y_{i−1}`: the law's link matrix or its adjoint with transposed indices, as in `CH1_5UQ.md` Theorem 4.2 ("`U` or `U†` with transposed indices") and `EW1.md` Definition 2.3; its left index transforms at `y_{i−1}` and its right index at `y_i`. On `L5-U` with links oriented along `+e`, `(T_i)_{ab} = (U^{ba}_{ℓ_i})^† = r^{ℓ_i,y_{i−1}}_a r^{ℓ_i,y_i†}_b`. Let `ψ^†_x` be the matter creator multiplet at `x` (`c^†_{x,a}` on `L5-U`, `γ_{x,a}` on `L5-O`). Put
\[
 B_{r,b}(x) = \sum_{a} \psi^\dagger_{x,a}\,(T_1T_2\cdots T_{2r})_{ab},\qquad
 \omega^{(x)}_r(A) = \frac{\sum_b \omega(B_{r,b}^*A\,B_{r,b})}{\sum_b\omega(B_{r,b}^*B_{r,b})}\quad(A\in\mathfrak A^G),
\]
when the denominator is positive. `B_{r,b}` is gauge invariant at every site except `y_{2r}`, where the open index `b` carries a static compensating charge, as in Barata–Nill's states "with an external electric charge" (arXiv:hep-th/9410229, §6). The *dipole variant* replaces the open index by a matter annihilator at `y_{2r}`, as in their dipole states. The *string sectors* are the GNS representations `π_x` of `𝔄^G` for the weak-* limit points `ω_∞^{(x)}` as `r → ∞`. Cell translations are plain shifts of sites and links on both laws, because the signs are cell-periodic, so `τ_β` maps `B_{r,b}(x)` to `B_{r,b}(x+2β)` and `ω_r^{(x+2β)} = ω_r^{(x)}∘τ_β^{-1}` in a D1 state. Limit points are chosen covariantly: one for each residue of `x` modulo `2Z^3`, then `ω_∞^{(x+2β)} = ω_∞^{(x)}∘τ_β^{-1}`. Each is odd in the sense of LZ1: `π(B_{r,b})Ω ∈ H_odd`. **MT1-S[R-CS^str(e)]** asks for a covariant representation of `𝔄^G` (definition 5) that is a direct sum of subrepresentations each quasi-equivalent to some `π_x`, and in it a band with a node whose leading generator is a Clifford triple.

Limit points exist whenever the denominators are positive, by weak-* compactness. Theorem 3 shows why this prescription can escape LZ1's pinning: `B_{r,b}` commutes with every `c_z`, `z ≠ y_{2r}`, so wherever `ω` is sharp in `c_z`, `ω_r^{(x)}` is sharp at the same value. In the limit the configuration is the vacuum's, and the odd parity sits in the flux of the ray (Theorem 2). Whether translations and the tick act, and whether bands move, depends on `ω`. The rest of this section decides it in two classes.

**Lemma D (asymptotic sharp values separate sectors).** Let `π_1, π_2` be representations of `𝔄^G`, each a direct sum of cyclic representations `(H_i^{(n)}, π_i^{(n)}, Ω_i^{(n)})`. Let `X_t` (`t ∈ N`) be self-adjoint local elements of `𝔄^G`, with `‖X_t‖ ≤ C` and supports that leave every finite set. Suppose there are reals `a ≠ b` such that, for each `n`, `π_1^{(n)}(X_t)Ω_1^{(n)} = aΩ_1^{(n)}` and `π_2^{(n)}(X_t)Ω_2^{(n)} = bΩ_2^{(n)}` for all large `t`. Then `π_1` and `π_2` are disjoint.

*Proof.* Let `ξ` be a unit vector in `H_i`. Given `ε > 0`, choose finitely many `n` and local `Y_n ∈ 𝔄^G` with `‖ξ − η‖ < ε` for `η = Σ_n π_i^{(n)}(Y_n)Ω_i^{(n)}`; this uses cyclicity and density of local elements in `𝔄^G` (`VC1_5.md` Theorem 2.1). Elements of `𝔄^G` are even (Theorem 4(a)), so `X_t` commutes with each `Y_n` once their supports are disjoint. For large `t` this gives `π_i(X_t)η = c_iη`, with `c_1 = a`, `c_2 = b`. So `|⟨ξ,π_i(X_t)ξ⟩ − c_i| ≤ 3Cε + 2|c_i|ε` for large `t`, and `⟨ξ,π_i(X_t)ξ⟩ → c_i`. If a nonzero intertwiner `S: H_2 → H_1` existed, its polar part would be a partial isometry `W` intertwining `π_2` and `π_1`, with `W^*W` commuting with `π_2`. For a unit `ξ` in the initial space of `W`, `⟨Wξ,π_1(X_t)Wξ⟩ = ⟨ξ,π_2(X_t)ξ⟩`. The left side tends to `a` and the right to `b`. ∎

**Proposition 6.1 (flavour-locked states have no string sectors; `L5-U`).** Call a product vector *flavour-locked* if at every site the six link ends carry one common flavour set. Let `ω = Φ∘E` with `Φ` flavour-locked; examples are the witness of `VC1_5.md` Theorem 5.5 (every end carries flavours `1,…,k`, matter empty) and `ω_F` of `CH1_5UQ.md` Theorem 3.3. Then `ω(B^*_{r,b}B_{r,b}) = 0` for every axis, every `x`, `r ≥ 1` and `b`, and the same holds for the dipole variant. So R-CS^str has no sector in these states.

*Proof.* `Σ_bB_{r,b}^*B_{r,b}` is gauge invariant, so `ω` of it equals `Σ_b‖B_{r,b}Φ‖^2`. Expand the matrix product. A term with interior indices `a_1,…,a_{2r−1}` creates flavour `a_1` at the `y_1`-end of `ℓ_1` and annihilates flavour `a_1` at the `y_1`-end of `ℓ_2`. Both ends sit at `y_1` and carry the same set, so the term needs `a_1` both absent and present. Every term vanishes. The dipole variant contains the same interior factor. ∎

This is the string form of the frozen-star mechanism: flavour-locked vectors lie in the common kernel of all `φ_{ℓ,x}, φ_{ℓ,x}^*` (`CH1_5UQ.md` Theorem 3.1), and charge cannot pass a locked site.

*Proof.* (a) Every hop `h_ℓ = c^†_yUc_x + c^†_xU^†c_y` ends in a matter annihilator, so `H_CΦ_AB = 0` for every cube and each cube gate fixes `Φ_AB`. (c) Let `C_o(x)` be the odd cube containing `x`, and `C_1,…,C_8` the even cubes meeting it. Near `y_{2r}` matter is empty and nothing acts. So `U_0^*B_{r,b}Φ_AB = e^{iφ_r}G_x^*B_{r,b}Φ_AB`, with `G_x` independent of `r` and `b`. With `α^{-1}(A) = U_0AU_0^*` this gives `ω_r^{(x)}(α^{-1}(A)) = ω_r^{(x)}(G_xAG_x^*)` for local `A` and large `r`. Let `r → ∞`. Define `W_x π_x(A)Ω_x = π_x(α(A))π_x(G_x^*)Ω_x`. Its norm is `ω_∞^{(x)}(G_xα(A^*A)G_x^*)^{1/2} = ω_∞^{(x)}(A^*A)^{1/2}`. Its range is dense, because `π_x(G_x^*)Ω_x` is cyclic and `α` is onto. So `W_x` is unitary, and `W_xπ_x(A)W_x^{-1} = π_x(α(A))`. Put `Ũ_x = W_x`. Cell translations are plain shifts of sites and links, since the signs `η` are cell-periodic; `ω_AB` is translation invariant, so `ω_∞^{(x+2β)} = ω_∞^{(x)}∘τ_β^{-1}` and `T_βπ_x(A)Ω_x = π_{x+2β}(τ_β(A))Ω_{x+2β}` is unitary. `G_{x+2β} = τ_β(G_x)` and `α∘τ_β = τ_β∘α` give `Ũ_{x+2β}T_β = T_βŨ_x`. `H` is a countable sum of separable spaces.

(d) Take `X_t = (p_{ℓ_t,y_{t−1}} − k)^2` on the ray links of `x`, for large `t`; these are gauge invariant, even and local. By (b), `π_x(X_t)Ω_x = Ω_x`. The ray of `x'` is disjoint from that of `x`, and `ω_∞^{(x')}` differs from `Φ_AB` only on its own ray and at `x'`, so `π_{x'}(X_t)Ω_{x'} = 0`. Lemma D gives disjointness. `W_x` intertwines `π_x∘α^{-1}` with `π_x`.

(e) Every summand of `π'` is quasi-equivalent to some `π_x`, and by (d) its transverse label `c = (x_2,x_3)` is then unique. Let `H'_c` be the closed span of the summands with label `c`. Summands with different labels are disjoint, hence orthogonal, so `H' = ⊕_cH'_c`. Let `Y` be a summand with label `c`. Then `Ũ'Y` carries a representation equivalent to `π'|_Y∘α^{-1}`, which is quasi-equivalent to `π_x` by (d). Its projection onto `H'_{c'}`, `c' ≠ c`, intertwines it with a sum of representations disjoint from `π_x`, so it vanishes. So `Ũ'H'_c ⊂ H'_c`, and the same argument for `Ũ'^{-1}` gives equality. Likewise `T'_βH'_c = H'_{c+2(β_2,β_3)}`, because `π_x∘τ_β^{-1}` is unitarily equivalent to `π_{x+2β}`. Apply Theorem 5 with `q(c) = c_2/2`, `n = e_2`, and with `q(c) = c_3/2`, `n = e_3`.

(f) A cell rotation about a cell centre maps the tilings to themselves and preserves `Φ_AB`; it maps `e_1`-rays to rays along another axis, and the argument transports. Dipole variant: its last factor is `c_{y_{2r}}`, which kills empty matter. ∎

**What Theorem 6 shows, and what it does not.** The string prescription removes the pinning of Theorem 3: in `ω_AB` the configuration is the vacuum's, translations and the tick act, and nothing forces flatness along the ray. What remains is a different conserved label, the transverse position of the ray. It is conserved because on empty matter the base tick acts by the electric gates alone (`LZ1.md` Theorem 6.1), which are diagonal in the end numbers, so it never moves the flux far along the ray. Lemma B turns that conservation into exact flatness in the transverse directions. Theorem 6 does not say that string sectors are flat in every D1 state. It says that a Clifford node in an R-CS^str sector needs a state in which rays with different transverse positions are not disjoint sectors, or in which the tick does not preserve the ray. Rays that become equivalent are Fredenhagen–Marcu's "free charge" condition, seen through Barata–Nill's flux ratio. That condition is the open lemma of §8.

## 6. MT1-S under the cleanest candidate

The cleanest candidate is R-CS^str(e): it is the published construction (Fredenhagen–Marcu through Barata–Nill), it is closed, it uses only the law's own link matrices, and Theorem 3 shows it is the minimal way an odd sector can keep the vacuum configuration. The decision splits into a general corollary and two explicit classes.

**Corollary 6.2 (both laws).** Let `ω` be a locally gauge-invariant D1 state of an update in LZ1's class, on `L5-U` or `L5-O[S_+]`, and `e` an axis. Suppose (i) string sectors `π_x`, `π_{x'}` whose rays have different positions transverse to `e` are disjoint, and (ii) each `π_x∘α^{-1}` is quasi-equivalent to `π_x`. Then in every covariant representation built from the `π_x` (as in Theorem 6(e)), every band is flat along the two directions transverse to `e` at every node. So MT1-S[R-CS^str(e)] fails in `ω`.

*Proof.* The proof of Theorem 6(e) uses only (i), (ii) and the translation covariance `π_x∘τ_β^{-1} ≅ π_{x+2β}`, which holds for every D1 state because `τ_β` maps the string operators at `x` to those at `x+2β`. ∎

**Decision.**
1. In the flavour-locked states of Proposition 6.1, which include the stationary witnesses of `VC1_5.md` Theorem 5.5 and `CH1_5UQ.md` Theorem 3.3, R-CS^str has no sector. So no odd excitation exists under it, and the asserted odd Clifford node does not exist.
2. In `ω_AB` (Theorem 6), sectors, translations and the tick all exist, and (i), (ii) hold. Every band is transversally flat, so the asserted odd Clifford node does not exist.
3. In a general D1 state, a Clifford odd node under R-CS^str(e) requires that (i) or (ii) fail. That is, rays at different transverse positions must give equivalent sectors (Fredenhagen–Marcu's free-charge situation), or the tick must change the ray at infinity. Whether some D1 state of an admissible update does this is RCS-P (§8). In the VC1 vacuum the question is open with VC1.

**Remark (LZ1 Theorem 5.1 under any R-CS of this kind).** Every global gauge transformation acts trivially on `𝔄^G`: on a local gauge-invariant element it equals the product of the finitely many local transformations at its sites, each of which fixes it. So in a representation of `𝔄^G` the `U(5)` clause of `LZ1.md` Theorem 5.1 is empty. Its content at `μ = 0 = τ_E` reduces to `MT1.md` C4 on `C^8`: if an R-CS node fibre is carried by a unitary, up to phases, onto `C^8` with the rotations, inversion, `Θ_d` and `σ` in their free node forms, then `N_j = aG_j` with real `a`. This is a conditional sufficiency route, not a decision. In the families of Theorem 6 it cannot be used: a cell rotation maps the `e_1`-ray sectors to `e_2`-ray sectors, which are disjoint from them by Lemma D (take `X_t` on the far links of an `e_1`-ray), so condition (R) has no implementer inside one family. Theorem 6 decides there without it.

## 8. The open lemma RCS-P and its attacks

**RCS-P (open).** There are an update in LZ1's class, a locally gauge-invariant D1 state `ω` of it on `L5-U` or `L5-O[S_+]`, and an axis `e`, such that some covariant representation built from the R-CS^str(e) sectors of `ω` has a band with a node whose leading generator is a Clifford triple. By Corollary 6.2, such an `ω` must violate (i) or (ii): rays at different transverse positions must give non-disjoint sectors, or the tick must change the ray at infinity.

**A1, frozen backgrounds (existence attack).** Use a stationary state in which link data are sharp, where every computation is exact. The candidates on file are the flavour-locked witnesses (`VC1_5.md` Theorem 5.5, `CH1_5UQ.md` Theorem 3.3), the empty-matter states and `ω_AB`. *Failing step:* "some frozen background has transversally equivalent rays". In the locked states the sectors vanish (Proposition 6.1). Let `ω = Φ∘E` be any D1 state of the base `L5-U` tick with `Φ` a product vector with empty matter and sharp end numbers; `ω_AB` is one. On empty matter the tick acts by the electric gates alone (`LZ1.md` Theorem 6.1: "each hop annihilates the state and the tick acts as the product of single-link electric unitaries `W_E`"), and every term of `B_{r,b}Φ` has the same end numbers. So the proofs of Theorem 6(c)-(d) apply verbatim whenever the string norms are positive: (i) and (ii) hold, and Corollary 6.2 gives transverse flatness. On `L5-O` no frozen instance is available here, because "The `L5-O` Majorana hop need not preserve empty matter" (`LZ1.md` line 132).

**A2, the free-link and Dirac-cloud limit (existence attack).** Make strings invisible: links close to c-number unitaries would make rays at different positions locally indistinguishable, as in the free tick. *Failing step:* "some state of the law makes a link nearly a c-number unitary". `CH1_5UQ.md` Theorem 4.2: "For any state `λ` on one register, the matrices `u_{jk}=λ(U^{jk})` ... obey `||u||,||v||≤1/2`." On `S_±` the coherence bound is `κ ≤ 1/10` (`EW1.md` line 234, quoting `GA2_Y2.md`). So no state makes the string operator act on a link as a c-number unitary, and the free-link picture is not available at the lattice scale. A Dirac-type superposition over rays replaces one label by a distribution of labels; in any state where flux far from matter is frozen, each ray in the superposition is again a tick-conserved label, and the cloud does not remove the obstruction of Theorem 5 without a dynamical mechanism that moves rays.

**A3, loop condensation (existence attack).** In Kitaev's toric code, string sectors at different positions are equivalent because closed strings act trivially on the ground state; Naaijkens proves transportability and translation covariance there (arXiv:1012.3857, Def. 4.1, Prop. 4.5). A vacuum with fluctuating closed flux loops would make (i) fail. *Failing step:* "the base tick, or an admissible layer, makes closed flux loops fluctuate in some D1 state". The base tick has no plaquette gate; the plaquette class is open law data (`LW1_5.md` §2: "a plaquette class and its constants are **OPEN law data**, GA1-P-5"). Without it, loop dynamics can arise only from matter hopping around plaquettes, and no D1 state with fluctuating loops is constructed in the frozen parts.

**K, kill attempt (refutation attack).** Prove (i) and (ii) in every D1 state, which would refute RCS-P by Corollary 6.2. *Failing step:* "the ray at infinity is conserved in every D1 state". In matter-filled states the hops act on the ray's links: a hop across a ray link changes its end numbers. In the tracial state, for example, matter is present on every site, so the step of Theorem 6(c), that every gate far along the ray acts trivially, is unavailable, and no other conservation law for the ray is known. So K stops; it is a failed search, not a no-go.

These are failures of the named routes. RCS-P is neither proved nor refuted. Its closest published analogue is the free-charge phase of Fredenhagen–Marcu (NOT VERIFIED) as described by Barata–Nill (arXiv:hep-th/9410229, §6, with energy bounds "for increasing values of r"), proved there by cluster expansion for Euclidean `Z_N` Higgs models, not for a Floquet tick with finite registers.
