## 2. Background and update

**Theorem 1 (background).** `ω'` is gauge invariant, translation invariant, `P_x=−1` and `z_x=+1` sharply at every site. It is a D1 state for every matter-activated update. Every base cube gate and its inverse is matter-activated.

*Proof.* `W((Λ⁸g)⊗(Λ⁸g))=det(g)W`; hence `σ` is fixed when `g` is unitary with determinant 1. `det S_+(g)=1` for Spin(10). The matter vector is fixed by `S_+⊕1`; full and empty direction-1 ends are fixed, and each paired transverse end state is fixed. The local gauge action factors over stars, so the state is gauge invariant. Translation preserves all factors and their orientations. End numbers at each site are `0,16,8,8,8,8`, so the centre formula gives `z_x=+1`; matter gives `P_x=−1`. Each matter-activated gate fixes the state on its support, so each layer and their composition fixes the state. Gauge invariance implies centre-adjoint invariance. In every hop one matter factor `E†` annihilates `H_-`; the cube generator therefore annihilates the all-`H_-` subspace and its exponentials, including inverses, fix it. □

**Theorem 2 (admissibility).** Each `t_C` is even, gauge invariant and matter-activated. The update `U` is in LZ1's class, is cell-covariant, and meets NN-a with the witness link register. It fixes `ω'`.

*Proof.* Endpoint gauge actions preserve end number, so each `Q_ℓ` is gauge invariant. It is also matter-even, and `h_ℓ` is even and gauge invariant by `CG16B.md` Theorem 3.1. Hence each projected term and `t_C` have these properties. Since `Q_ℓ` leaves matter unchanged, each projected hop annihilates all-`H_-` matter, and `t_C` fixes that subspace. Cubes in a tiling have disjoint supports; even translations preserve the signs and tilings. Base inverses have the same layer properties.

The cell lift carries each signed hop to its signed image hop, including reversed links (`LW1_5.md` B1-U and `CG16B.md` Theorem 3.1). It permutes endpoint numbers, and the four pairs defining `Q_ℓ` are closed under exchange. Thus it carries `X_C` to `X_{gC}` and preserves each tiling layer. The complete update is cell-covariant.

For exact NN compilation, use the proof of `CG16B.md` Lemma NN-W, not merely its statement for unprojected hops. The twelve projected terms are even, gauge-invariant elements of their respective finite-dimensional edge-triple algebras. Their skew generators generate a finite-dimensional Lie algebra. Finite words in their one-parameter groups contain a neighbourhood of the identity of its connected analytic subgroup, by the inverse-function argument in NN-W, and therefore the whole subgroup. ## 3. String limits and labels

**Theorem 3 (string sectors).** For the background `ω'`, `Σ_b‖B_{r,b}(x)Φ'‖²=16^{2r+1}`, in the field GNS notation for the product state. The states `ω_r^{(x)}` converge locally to `ω_x`. In their GNS representations `π_x`, every `z_y=1`, `χ_tot=−1`, and the boundary label is `−1`. These labels persist under quasi-equivalence and direct sums. If `x` and `x'` have different transverse coordinates, `π_x` and `π_{x'}` are disjoint.

*Proof.* Expanding the string gives one sequence of `2r+1` indices: one matter index and one target-end index per link. Each term removes a flavour from a full source and creates one at an empty target. The terms have unit norm and distinct sequences have distinct occupation patterns or orthogonal matter states. Thus the stated norm is the number of sequences. For a fixed finite support, choose the endpoint beyond every supported link and site on the ray. Tail occupation patterns are orthogonal and each boundary index has the same number of continuations. Summing the tail and normalizing makes the expectation independent of further length. Density and boundedness give the limit.

The centre values and labels are already proved for this situation in `CG16B.md` Proposition L: the background is D1, centre- and parity-sharp, the link register is link-pure, and each finite string commutes with `z_y` away from its far end and is matter-odd. The finite-region identity of `CG16B.md` Theorem 6.1 converts this into the same strong boundary-flux label, since all background centre values are retained. `RCS1.md` R2 proves persistence of the relative parity under quasi-equivalence and sums; the same uniformly bounded-net argument applies to the boundary label. Centrality spreads each sharp `z_y` value throughout the representation.

On each link far along the ray from `x`, its target-end number is sharply 1 in `ω_x`; on the same links it is sharply 0 in `ω_{x'}` if the lines differ. These bounded gauge-invariant observables escape every finite set. `RCS1.md` Lemma 6 therefore gives disjointness. □

## 4. Local dynamics and exact motion

**Theorem 4 (local implementation).** For any matter-activated LZ1-class update `𝒱`, and either background in Definition 5, each ray state satisfies `ω_x∘α_𝒱=ω_x∘Ad(G_x)` for some finite product `G_x∈𝔄^G` of its gates, where `Ad(G_x)(A)=G_x*AG_x`. Thus `π_x∘α_𝒱` is unitarily equivalent to `π_x`.

*Proof.* Initially a string vector has `H_+` matter only at its head `x`. Define `R₀={x}`. At layer `j` collect all gates whose site support meets `R_{j−1}`, and enlarge to `R_j` by their sites. There are finitely many such gates at each step. A gate outside this collection acts as identity on the current vector, since every site in its support remains in `H_-`; this holds even if the gate touches the long string's link registers. Gates within one layer commute. Induction shows that the finite ordered product `G_x` of collected gates acts on every finite string vector exactly as the full circuit. For a local observable use its finite backward light cone; gates outside it cancel in the conjugation. Equivalently compare the finite forward circuit on the vector with the finite light cone of the observable, inserting the remaining gates, which fix the vector at the appropriate stage. This proves the finite-string expectation identity. Pass to the local limit from Theorem 3. `G_x` is a gauge-invariant local unitary; `π_x(G_x)Ω_x` is cyclic, so GNS uniqueness gives the equivalence. The same argument applies to Example S, whose matter and ray links have the same sharp values. □

**Theorem 5 (one link and two layers).** Let `ψ_v` be a finite string vector with head at `v`, and `ψ'_w` the string with one extra first link `(w,v)` along `+e₁`, with the same far index. Then `h_{wv}ψ_v=ψ'_w`, `h_{wv}ψ'_w=16ψ_v` and `‖ψ'_w‖=4‖ψ_v‖`. If `x₁` is even, `U` sends the normalized string vector to `κ_x` times the normalized vector with head `x−2e₁` and two added links, where `κ_x=−η₁(x−e₁)η₁(x−2e₁)∈{±1}`. Consequently `ω_x(α_U(A))=ω_{x−2e₁}(A)`.

*Proof.* In the adjoint term of `h_{wv}`, `E†_{v,αi}E_{v,ai₀}=δ_{αa}|i⟩⟨i₀|` and `E_{w,βi}|i₀⟩=δ_{ii₀}|β⟩`. This appends the link with exactly the string's operator order. The other hop term annihilates the state. In the reverse action, the source is full before removal and the target empty before creation; `r^{w†}_βr^w_β=1` on the full source and `r^v_αr^{v†}_a=δ_{αa}` on the empty target. Summing `β` gives 16. Orthogonal-index counting gives the norm ratio 4.

For even `x₁`, the odd cube containing `x` contains `(x−e₁,x)` but not the next forward link; the even cube containing `x−e₁` contains `(x−2e₁,x−e₁)`. In each such cube the only nonzero projected term on the two relevant head vectors is the edge being traversed: other direction-1 edges have empty `H_+` matter, and transverse links have `(8,8)`, killed by `Q`. The projector equals 1 on the two relevant link occupations. The generator on the normalized two-vector span is `4η₁σ_x`, and `exp(−iπ(4η₁σ_x)/8)=−iη₁σ_x`. The two layers produce the stated phase and two-link displacement; other gates fix the vector. With fixed far endpoint, the resulting normalized string state is exactly the longer string state. Summing over far indices and taking the limit gives the state identity. □

The vector form used below is valid in the background field GNS representation: the Schrödinger implementer of `α_U`, defined by `Ûπ'(A)Ω'=π'(α_U^{-1}(A))Ω'`, maps a normalized finite string vector to `κ_x` times the displaced vector. This follows directly from the finite circuit action in Theorem 5 and invariance of `Ω'`; it does not require every gate collected in Theorem 4 to lie in the observable's backward cone.

## 5. Covariant representation and band

Let `x_c=(0,2c₂,2c₃)` for `c∈Z²`, and let `(H_c,π_c,Ω_c)` be the GNS triple of `ω_{x_c}`. Set `H=⊕_cH_c` and `π=⊕_cπ_c`. For even `x`, let `G^{(x)}=t_{C'}t_C` be the two gates moving the head from `x+2e₁` to `x`. Translation covariance and Theorem 5 give
`ω_{x+2e₁}∘α=ω_x`, `ω_x(G^{(x)} A G^{(x)*})=ω_{x+2e₁}(A)`, and `ω_{x+2β}(τ_β A)=ω_x(A)`.

**Theorem 6 (covariant implementers).** With `x=x_c` and `G=G^{(x)}`, define on cyclic vectors
\[
 \widetilde U\pi_c(A)\Omega_c=\pi_c(\alpha(A)G^*)\Omega_c,\quad
 T_{e_1}\pi_c(A)\Omega_c=\pi_c(\tau_{e_1}(A)G^*)\Omega_c,
\]
and for `β_⊥=(0,β₂,β₃)`, define `T_{β_⊥}π_c(A)Ω_c=π_{c+(β₂,β₃)}(τ_{β_⊥}A)Ω_{c+(β₂,β₃)}`. These extend to commuting covariant unitary implementers and a representation `T` of `Z³`. Moreover `ŨΩ_c=T_{e₁}Ω_c`.

*Proof.* The displayed state identities give the isometry norms, for example `ω_x(Gα(A*A)G*)=ω_{x+2e₁}(α(A*A))=ω_x(A*A)`. The range is dense because the automorphisms are onto and `G*` is unitary in `𝔄^G`. The maps implement the intended automorphisms; their values at `Ω_c` agree as stated.

For longitudinal commutation, both `ŨT_{e₁}` and `T_{e₁}Ũ` implement the same automorphism. It is enough to compare the cyclic vector. Their values are `π_c(α(G*)G*)Ω_c` and `π_c(τ_{e₁}(G*)G*)Ω_c`. Write `G'=G^{(x+2e₁)}` and `D=α(G*)G*−G'*G*`. The two-layer action and its inverse give, on normalized finite strings with fixed far end, `G*ψ_x=κψ_{x+2e₁}` and `G'*ψ_{x+2e₁}=κψ_{x+4e₁}`. With `π'(α(A))=Û*π'(A)Û` and `Ûψ_y=κψ_{y−2e₁}`, the first expression gives the same vector as the second. Therefore `ω_x(D*D)=0` after the finite-string limit. For transverse translations, `τ_{β_⊥}(G^{(x)})=G^{(x+2β_⊥)}`; compare cyclic vectors in the same way. The transverse maps compose exactly. The cyclic vectors generate their summands, so these comparisons establish all commutations and the group law. The countable direct sum is separable. □

**Theorem 7 (band and node).** Put `u_b=T_bΩ_0`. These vectors are orthonormal, `T_βu_b=u_{b+β}` and `Ũu_b=u_{b+e₁}`. The isometry `F(e^{iK·b})=u_b` defines a band `V(K)=e^{iK₁}`. The representation is a direct sum of straight-string sectors of relative parity and boundary label `−1`, disjoint across transverse lines; under R-16^str′ it is spinor class.

*Proof.* The state of `u_b` is `ω_{2b}` by the state identities and the translation group law. Different transverse coordinates lie in different direct summands. On one line, distinct longitudinal heads have opposite sharp `P_{2b}` values in the corresponding states, hence their vectors are orthogonal. The shift identities follow from Theorem 6. Parseval makes `F` an isometry and the shift identities give the band intertwinings. `V=1` exactly on the node plane; locally `i log V=−K₁`, giving `N`. The labels follow from Theorem 3. □

The Schrödinger head moves by `−e₁` cells per tick and the string grows. Rephasing the tick or longitudinal translation shifts the node plane but leaves the nonzero slope. The implementer is a chosen covariant implementer; this does not fix a unique physical charged-sector phase convention (`ZM1_OBS.md` §0).

**Theorem 8 (not a Clifford cone).** No one-dimensional band has a three-dimensional Clifford cone with positive speed. Thus Theorem 7's node is not one.

*Proof.* Its `N_j` are real scalars. The Clifford relations demand `N₁²=N₂²=v²>0` and `N₁N₂=0`, a contradiction. □

## 6. Transverse flatness and RC-W

*Proof.* Example S has the same `H_-` matter, `(16,0)` ray links, translation invariance and sharp centre and parity values. The proofs of Theorems 3 and 4 apply.
