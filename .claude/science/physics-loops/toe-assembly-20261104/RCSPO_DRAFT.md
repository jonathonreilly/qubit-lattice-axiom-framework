## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**The literal target has no instance on any `L5-O` register. Proved.** For every finite unitary `Spin(20)` register `R` on the links (the `L5-O` family of `LW1_5.md` §2), every update, and every background sharp in its local centre labels, the law's straight strings commute with every end action of the central `−1∈Spin(10)`. So every straight-string limit has boundary label `+1` and relative spinor flux `+1`: it is never spinor class (Theorem 1). On `L5-O[S_+]` no representation at all is spinor class (`CG16D.md` Theorem 4, ported). The step is named: *the string commutes with `ρ_ℓ(−1)` on every link, and on `S_+` that operator is the scalar `−1`*. Spinor-class sectors made otherwise (centre defects) are static on every link-pure register (`CG16D.md` Theorem 6(ii)). RC-W is not triggered, because no node is built. **What the `L5-O[S_+]` string sectors are.** Over a parity-sharp background sharp in every `Z_4` centre implementer, they have `χ_tot=−1`, keep every centre value, and carry relative `Z_4` boundary flux exactly `−1`: the class of the vector, never `±i` (Theorem 2). **The escape for these odd sectors is narrowed by a new mechanism.** The frozen texts say the link-raising mechanism is absent on `L5-O[S_+]`. That remains true. But the contracted string factorises, in a global Majorana picture, into site contractions `J_y` at the ray sites. Each `iJ_y` has spectrum `{−10,−8,…,10}`; `(iJ_y)^2=100−4C_y` with `C_y` the pair Casimir of the two ray ends at `y`; its kernel is the `126⊕126bar` level of that pair. So **every string state empties that level at every interior ray site, over every background**, and the string weight is a product of commuting local factors (Lemma 3). Consequences: on every *short-range-entangled* background (a finite-depth circuit image of a block product state) in which that level has weight `q>0`, ray sectors on distant parallel lines are disjoint, so with factor sectors no update gives a Clifford node (Theorem 4). The same theorem extends `RCSPC.md` Theorem 7 from block products to finite-depth circuit images on the U(16)-rishon witness and on `L5-U[m]`. A Clifford node with factor sectors needs a cone lift for the `126⊕126bar` level (Proposition 5). A second mechanism needs no entanglement hypothesis: the string flips the `Z_2` electric field `Γ` on its line, so every background sharp in a `Γ`-relation tying the ray's line to untouched lines has disjoint sectors on distinct lines (Proposition 6). On an explicit centre-sharp, parity-sharp background `ω_+` (Example 4.2) both mechanisms apply, and **no update gives a Clifford node on its odd sectors**, without any factoriality hypothesis (Corollary 6.1). **Open by name:** RCSPO-CL′, the statement that no centre-sharp D1 background of `L5-O[S_+]` has factor odd ray sectors that are quasi-equivalent across a finite-index group of transverse translations. It is proved for short-range-entangled backgrounds with `q>0`, for `Γ`-sharp line-tying backgrounds, and for `ω_+`. A counterexample, if one exists, must be long-range entangled or have `q=0`, and must keep `Γ` fluctuating, as in a deconfined `Z_2` phase. Three distinct attacks on the rest failed at named steps (§4.5): finite-depth factorisation (fails for long-range-entangled backgrounds), a static `Z_2` field after Kitaev (fails: the contracted loops have kernels, so they are not unitary flux operators, and their corner factors at a shared end do not commute), and a transport automorphism after Naaijkens (fails: the site factors have a kernel, the very level in question).

## 3. The setting on L5-O

### 3.1 The law and its registers

`LW1_5.md` §2: each site `x` has ten matter Majoranas `γ_{x,a}` (five complex modes). Each link `ℓ=(x,y)`, `y=x+e_i`, carries a finite unitary representation `R` of `Spin(20)`, with generators `T_{ij}` (`i,j=1,…,20`); write `η^{ℓ,y}_a` for the Clifford generators with index `a≤10` and `η^{ℓ,x}_b` for those with index `10+b`. The link matrix is `U^{ab}_ℓ=2T_{a,10+b}`. The local gauge group at `x` is `Spin(10)`, generated by `𝒢^{cd}_x=M^{cd}_x+Σ_{ℓ∋x}L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}`, where `M^{cd}_x=(i/2)γ_{x,c}γ_{x,d}` and `L^{cd}_{ℓ,x}` is `T_{cd}` or `T_{10+c,10+d}` according to the end. The register `S_+` is the 512-dimensional half-spin representation, on which `T_{ij}=(i/2)η_iη_j` with `η_1,…,η_{20}` Clifford generators. Then
\[
U^{ab}_ℓ=iη^{ℓ,y}_aη^{ℓ,x}_b ,
\]
a self-adjoint unitary. Two entries anticommute when they share exactly one index and commute otherwise. The end chirality is `Γ^{ℓ,z}=i^5η^{ℓ,z}_1⋯η^{ℓ,z}_{10}` at `z∈\{x,y\}`.

A one-line computation fixes the convention. With `Γ^{(20)}=i^{10}η_1⋯η_{20}` (the ten `η^{ℓ,y}` first), the product of the two end chiralities is directly `Γ^{ℓ,y}Γ^{ℓ,x}=Γ^{(20)}`. `RCS1.md` §1 item 2 says "On `S_+`, the two end chiralities agree." So `S_+` is the `+1` eigenspace of `Γ^{(20)}`, and on `S_+` we write `Γ_ℓ:=Γ^{ℓ,x}=Γ^{ℓ,y}`. Each `Γ_ℓ` commutes with both end actions (it is a product of an even number of Majoranas of one end, central in that end's even Clifford algebra, and even in the other end's Majoranas), so `Γ_ℓ∈𝔄^G`.

**Straight strings** (`RCS1.md` §5). Fix `x`, the axis `e_1`, `y_k=x+ke_1`, `ℓ_k=(y_{k−1},y_k)`. The transport factor is `(T_k)_{ac}=(U^{ca}_{ℓ_k})^*=iη^{ℓ_k,y_k}_cη^{ℓ_k,y_{k−1}}_a`: index `a` at the near end `y_{k−1}`, index `c` at the far end `y_k`. This matches `RCS1.md` R4 ("`±iη^{\rm far}_cη^{\rm near}_a`, with one sign for all indices"), with sign `+`. Put
\[
B_{r,b}(x)=\sum_{a_0,\dots,a_{2r-1}}γ_{x,a_0}(T_1)_{a_0a_1}(T_2)_{a_1a_2}\cdots(T_{2r})_{a_{2r-1}b},\qquad
ω^{(x)}_r(A)=\frac{\sum_bω(B^*_{r,b}AB_{r,b})}{\sum_bω(B^*_{r,b}B_{r,b})}\ (A∈𝔄^G),
\]
when the denominator is positive. Ray states `ω_x`, ray sectors `π_x`, covariant choice, "built from ray sectors", updates (every automorphism commuting with gauge transformations and cell translations), label structures, bands, nodes and Clifford nodes are exactly `RCSPC.md` D3-D7 and `LZ1.md` §1. The LZ1 update class appends finitely many layers of commuting, finite-support, gauge-invariant gates (`LZ1.md` line 36); NN-a is `TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` §3.7.

### 3.2 The global Majorana picture

**Lemma G (rebuilt; standard Jordan-Wigner with Klein factors).** Let `Λ` be a finite site set and `L` a finite set of links. Let `F_M` be the matter Fock space of `Λ` and `F_ℓ≅C^{1024}` the Fock space of the 20 Majoranas of `ℓ`, so that `S_+(ℓ)⊂F_ℓ`. On `F=F_M⊗\bigotimes_{ℓ∈L}F_ℓ` there are self-adjoint unitaries `\hat γ_{x,a}`, `\hat η^ℓ_i` that pairwise anticommute (one Clifford algebra for all of them), such that:
(i) `\hat γ_{x,a}=γ_{x,a}⊗1`;
(ii) every product of an even number of `\hat η^ℓ_i` with the same `ℓ` equals the same product of the `η^ℓ_i` acting in the `ℓ` slot, tensored with identities;
(iii) the subspace `F_+=F_M⊗\bigotimes_ℓS_+(ℓ)` is invariant under every polynomial in the hatted operators that is even in the Majoranas of each link separately ("link-even"), and the restriction of such a polynomial to `F_+` is the corresponding element of the law's algebra `𝔄(Λ,L)` (matter operators tensored with link operators). In particular `U^{ab}_ℓ`, `(T_k)_{ac}`, `L^{cd}_{ℓ,z}`, `Γ_ℓ` and `B_{r,b}(x)` are restrictions of the obvious link-even polynomials.

*Proof.* Order the links `ℓ_1,…,ℓ_n`. Let `P_M` be the matter parity on `F_M` and `P_j=(−1)^{N_j}` the parity of `F_{ℓ_j}`. Put `\hat γ_{x,a}=γ_{x,a}⊗1` and `\hat η^{ℓ_j}_i=P_M⊗P_1⊗⋯⊗P_{j−1}⊗η^{ℓ_j}_i⊗1⊗⋯⊗1`. Each is a self-adjoint unitary. `\hat γ` anticommutes with every `\hat η`, because `γ` anticommutes with `P_M`. For `j<k`, `\hat η^{ℓ_j}_i` anticommutes with `\hat η^{ℓ_k}_{i′}`, because the latter carries `P_j` in slot `j`, which anticommutes with `η^{ℓ_j}_i`, and every other slot commutes. Within one link the `η`'s anticommute. In a product of an even number of `\hat η^{ℓ_j}`, the Klein strings `P_M⊗P_1⊗⋯⊗P_{j−1}` occur an even number of times; they commute with slot `j` and square to `1`; so (ii) holds. A link-even polynomial is, by (i)-(ii), a polynomial in matter operators and in operators acting in single link slots by even products. Even products of the `η^ℓ` commute with `Γ^{(20)}_ℓ`, so they preserve `S_+(ℓ)`. Their restrictions to `S_+` are the spin representation of `so(20)`, which is the register. This is (iii). □

All operator identities below are proved for link-even polynomials on `F` and then restricted to `F_+`. A zero on `F` restricts to a zero on `F_+`. The hats are dropped from now on.

### 3.4 The short-range-entangled class

**Definition S.** For a finite site set `S` let `𝔄(S)` be the algebra of the matter at `S` and of the links with both ends in `S`, and `N_k(S)` the set of sites within lattice distance `k` of `S`. A state `ω` of `𝔄` is **`(k,D)`-short-range-entangled** (`SRE_{k,D}`) if there are:
(a) a partition of all tensor factors (matter sites and whole links) into finite blocks of diameter less than `D`, invariant under the cell translations;
(b) a product state `Φ` over these blocks whose block states are even;
(c) an even `*`-automorphism `α_C` of `𝔄` with `α_C(𝔄(S))⊆𝔄(N_k(S))` for every finite `S`;
such that `ω(A)=Φ(α_C(A))` for every `A∈𝔄^G`.

`k=0` with `α_C` the identity gives the backgrounds of `RCSPC.md` Theorem 7. A finite product of layers of nearest-neighbour gates has finite range, so every image of a block product under finitely many NN-a layers, and under finitely many ticks of an NN-a update, is in the class for some `k`. The definition names a class for a theorem; it is not a reading and is not proposed as a vacuum condition (§6).

### 4.2 The site mechanism on `L5-O[S_+]`

For a site `y` and two distinct links `ℓ,ℓ′∋y` put

**Lemma 3 (the site factors empty a level; `L5-O[S_+]`, every state).**
(a) `K_y(ℓ,ℓ′)` and `Q_y(ℓ,ℓ′)` are local, even elements of `𝔄^G` that commute with every `Γ_{ℓ″}`. `K_y(ℓ,ℓ′)≥0`, its spectrum lies in `\{0,4,16,36,64,100\}`, and `Q_y(ℓ,ℓ′)` is the projection onto its kernel. The same holds for `K_0`.
(b) `Q_kB_{r,b}(x)=0` for every `b` and every `1≤k≤2r−1`.
(c) `B_{r,b}(x)^*B_{r,b}(x)=K_0K_1⋯K_{2r−1}` for every `b`, a product of commuting positive factors.
(d) For every state `ω` of `𝔄` with `Σ_bω(B^*_{r,b}B_{r,b})>0`, `ω^{(x)}_r(Q_k)=0` for `1≤k≤2r−1`. Every ray state has `ω_x(Q_k)=0` for every `k≥1`, and `π_x(Q_k)→0` strongly as `k→∞`.

*Proof.* Work in the global picture of Lemma G.

*Step 1 (factorisation).* In `B_{r,b}`, the term with indices `a_0,…,a_{2r−1}` (and `a_{2r}=b`) is `i^{2r}` times the Majorana word

\[
B_{r,b}(x)=i^{2r}ς_r\,J_0J_1\cdots J_{2r−1}\,η^{ℓ_{2r},y_{2r}}_b,\qquad
J_0=\sum_aγ_{x,a}η^{ℓ_1,x}_a,\quad J_k=\sum_aη^{ℓ_k,y_k}_aη^{ℓ_{k+1},y_k}_a\ (k≥1).

Each `J_k` is a sum of bilinears in its own two families of ten Majoranas; the `J_k` act on pairwise disjoint families, so they commute with one another and with `η^{ℓ_{2r},y_{2r}}_b`.

*Step 2 (each `iJ` is a shifted number operator).* Let `ξ_a,ζ_a` (`a=1,…,10`) be the two families of a bracket, and `f_a=(ξ_a+iζ_a)/2`. ### 4.3 Short-range-entangled backgrounds: the transverse position is observable

Write `Q_{(s,c)}` for `Q_y(ℓ_{c,s−1},ℓ_{c,s})` at the site `y=(s,c)`: the `126⊕\overline{126}` level of the two direction-1 ends at `y`. It lies in `𝔄(\{(s−1,c),(s,c),(s+1,c)\})`. For a cell-translation-invariant state `ω`, `ω(Q_{(s,c)})` depends only on `s` modulo `2` and `c` modulo `2Z^2`.

**Theorem 4 (SRE backgrounds; `L5-O[S_+]`).** Let `ω` be a cell-translation-invariant state of `𝔄` in `SRE_{k,D}` (Definition S). Fix `x` and the axis `e_1`, put `c=x_⊥`, and suppose `q:=ω(Q_{(s,c)})>0` for all `s` of one parity `p`. Let `x′` lie on a line `c′` with `|c−c′|>2k+D+1`, and suppose ray limit points `ω_x`, `ω_{x′}` exist (positive denominators along the chosen lengths). Then `π_x` and `π_{x′}` are disjoint. If the ray sectors are chosen covariantly and are factor representations, every `Γ_x⊆Ze_1`, and no update gives a Clifford node on a covariant representation built from them (`RCSPC.md` Corollary 2).

*Proof.* Put `Z_s=Q_{(s,c)}` for `s≡p` (mod `2`). Lemma 3(d) gives `π_x(Z_s)→0` strongly as `s→∞`. Now fix local `A,A′∈𝔄^G`. Let `B′_b=B_{r,b}(x′)`; it acts on the matter at `x′` and on direction-1 links of line `c′`. For `s` large, the support of `Z_s` is disjoint from those of `A`, `A′` and `B′_b`, and `Z_s` is even; so `Z_s` commutes with all three, and
\[
\sum_bω(B′^*_bA^*Z_sA′B′_b)=ω(YZ_s),\qquad Y:=\sum_bB′^*_bA^*A′B′_b∈𝔄^G .
\]
Both `Y` and `Z_s` lie in `𝔄^G`, and so does `YZ_s`. By Definition S, `ω(YZ_s)=Φ(α_C(Y)α_C(Z_s))`, `ω(Y)=Φ(α_C(Y))` and `ω(Z_s)=Φ(α_C(Z_s))`. `α_C(Y)` lies in the algebra of the `k`-neighbourhood of `S_Y=\{x′\}∪(\text{line }c′)∪{\rm supp}A∪{\rm supp}A′`, and `α_C(Z_s)` in that of the `k`-neighbourhood of `S_Z=\{(s−1,c),(s,c),(s+1,c)\}`. A block of diameter less than `D` that meets both neighbourhoods would give `{\rm dist}(S_Y,S_Z)<2k+D`. The distance from `S_Z` to line `c′` is at least `|c−c′|`, more than `2k+D`; its distance to `x′`, `{\rm supp}A` and `{\rm supp}A′` exceeds `2k+D` for `s` large. So the two images lie in the algebras of disjoint unions of blocks. They are even, because `α_C` is even and `Y`, `Z_s` are. A product state of even block states factorises even elements on disjoint unions of blocks. Hence `ω(YZ_s)=ω(Y)ω(Z_s)=q\,ω(Y)`. The threshold on `s` does not depend on `r`, since `B′_b` stays on line `c′`. Dividing by `Σ_bω(B′^*_bB′_b)` and passing to the limit point, `ω_{x′}(A^*Z_sA′)=q\,ω_{x′}(A^*A′)` for all large `s`. So `π_{x′}(Z_s)→q1` weakly on the dense set `π_{x′}(𝔄^G)Ω_{x′}`, hence weakly everywhere, by the uniform bound. `RCSPC.md` Lemma 6 (strong convergence to `0` in one representation, weak convergence to `q≠0` in the other) gives disjointness. If `β_⊥≠0` then `|2nβ_⊥|>2k+D+1` for large `n`, so `nβ∉Γ_x`; as `Γ_x` is a group, `β∉Γ_x`. The last sentence is `RCSPC.md` Corollary 2. □

**Corollary 4.1 (the witness and `L5-U[m]`).** The same statement holds on the U(16)-rishon witness at any `m`, and on `L5-U[m]`, with `Z_s` the spectral projection of the target-end number of `ℓ_{c,2s}` at the lowest level `j_0` that `ω` occupies (`RCSPC.md` D9), and `q=ω(Z_s)>0` by the definition of `j_0`. *Proof.* `RCSPC.md` Lemma 5 (and, on `L5-U[m]`, the referee closure of `RCSPC.md` lines 331-338) gives `π_x(Z_s)→0` strongly; the rest of the proof of Theorem 4 is unchanged. □

So `RCSPC.md` Theorem 7, proved there for block products (`k=0`), extends to every finite-depth circuit image of a cell-invariant block product. The step that `RCSPC.md` attack A lacked, a positive factorisation bound for the string weight against `Z_s`, holds exactly in this class, because a finite-range automorphism cannot correlate regions farther apart than twice its range.

**Example 4.2 (the class is not empty on `L5-O[S_+]`).** Let `ω_+` be the state that is maximally mixed on the subspace `\{P_x=+1\}` of every site and on the subspace `\{Γ_ℓ=+1\}` of `S_+` of every link, a product over sites and links (`k=0`, `D=2`). The identity is the base tick followed by its own inverse layers, so it lies in LZ1's class, and it meets NN-a with no layers. `RCSP16.md` leaves the owner question on such cancellation open; `RCSPC.md` §4.7 uses the same update for the same purpose, non-vacuity only. In the Jordan-Wigner splitting of a link's 20 Majoranas into its two ends, `Γ^{ℓ,y}` and `Γ^{ℓ,x}` act on separate tensor factors (the Klein factor enters `Γ^{ℓ,x}` ten times), so the subspace `\{Γ_ℓ=+1\}` of `S_+` is `V_y⊗V_x`, with `V_z` the 16-dimensional `+1` eigenspace of the end chirality, and each end's generators act on its own factor. (In representation language, `16⊗16=10⊕120⊕126`; this is not used.) *Positive denominators and existence of the limits.* By Lemma 3(c), `Σ_bω_+(B^*_bB_b)=10\,ω_+(K_0K_1⋯K_{2r−1})`. ### 4.4 Sharp electric fields: the string is visible in every such state

The string flips `Γ` on every ray link (§3.3), and its weight commutes with every `Γ` (Lemma 3(a),(c)). So a background in which the `Z_2` electric field is sharp sees the ray directly. This is `RCSPC.md` Lemma RL in multiplicative `Z_2` form.

**Proposition 6 (sharp `Γ`-relations; `L5-O[S_+]`, any state).** Let `ω` be a state of `𝔄`, and `x,x′` sites on distinct lines `c≠c′` whose ray states exist. Suppose there are fixed lines `c_1=c,c_2,…,c_q` with `c′∉\{c_1,…,c_q\}` such that, for all large `s`, `ω` is sharp in `G_s=∏_{i=1}^qΓ_{ℓ_{c_i,s}}` with value `g_s∈\{±1\}`. Then `π_x` and `π_{x′}` are disjoint.

*Proof.* `G_s` is a self-adjoint unitary in `𝔄^G`. For `s>x_1`, the ray of `x` contains `ℓ_{c,s}` and no other link of `G_s`, so `B_{r,b}(x)G_s=−G_sB_{r,b}(x)` once `2r` passes `s`; by Lemma 3(a),(c), `B^*B=K_0⋯K_{2r−1}` commutes with `G_s`. Sharpness gives `ω(Y(G_s−g_s))=0` for every `Y` (Cauchy-Schwarz, as in Theorem 1(b)). So `ω^{(x)}_r(G_s)=−Σ_bω(B^*_bB_bG_s)/Σ_bω(B^*_bB_b)=−g_s`, and in the limit `G_s` is sharply `−g_s` in `ω_x`. The ray of `x′` meets no link of `G_s`, so `B′` commutes with `G_s` and `G_s` is sharply `g_s` in `ω_{x′}`. The family `X_s=g_sG_s` is uniformly bounded, self-adjoint, gauge invariant and local, with escaping supports; each cyclic vector is eventually an eigenvector, with eigenvalue `−1` in `π_x` and `+1` in `π_{x′}`. `RCS1.md` Lemma 6 gives disjointness. □

**Corollary 6.1 (an explicit background with no Clifford node for any update).** Let `ω_+` be the background of Example 4.2. For every update in the sense of `RCSPC.md` D5 (every automorphism commuting with the local gauge actions and the cell translations) and every separable covariant representation built from the `e_1`-ray sectors of `ω_+`, no band has a Clifford node. These sectors have `χ_tot=−1` and are vector class (Theorem 2).

*Proof.* Apply `RCSPC.md` Proposition 3 with `X_{c,s}=(1−Γ_{ℓ_{c,2s}})/2`. *(S1).* `ω_+` is sharp in every `Γ_ℓ`, with value `+1`. By the proof of Proposition 6, `Γ_{ℓ_{c,2s}}` is sharply `−1` in every ray state on line `c` (for `2s>x_1`) and sharply `+1` in every ray state on another line. Eventual eigenvectors of escaping local elements converge strongly (`RCS1.md` Lemma 6, proof), so `π_x(X_{c,s})→δ_{c,x_⊥}1` strongly. Covariance: `τ_β(X_{c,s})=X_{c+2β_⊥,s+β_1}`, since `τ_β` maps `ℓ_{c,2s}` to `ℓ_{c+2β_⊥,2(s+β_1)}`. *(S2).* Example 4.2 gives `ω_x(A)={\rm tr}(Π′K_0K_1⋯A)/{\rm tr}(Π′K_0K_1⋯)` for local `A`, where `Π′` flips `P_x` and the `Γ` of the ray links. Under the normalised trace with `Π′`, all ends of all links and all matter sites are independent, and each `K_k` couples only the two ray ends at `y_k`. So for local `Y` and translates `τ_{se_1}Y`, `τ_{te_1}Y` whose supports are separated by at least two links, the expectation factorises; and `ω_x(τ_{se_1}Y)` is constant once `τ_{se_1}Y` lies beyond the head, with a value that depends only on whether its support meets line `x_⊥`, hence only on `x_⊥`. That is (S2). Proposition 3 then gives a label structure with stabilisers `Ze_1` and no Clifford node, by `RCSPC.md` Theorem 1. Proposition 3 uses neither factoriality nor stationarity. □

So on `ω_+` the escape fails for every update, as on `RCSPC.md`'s four witness backgrounds (its Corollary 4). Two mechanisms see the ray in `ω_+`: the sharp electric field (Proposition 6) and the emptied level (Theorem 4). A background on which a Clifford node remains possible must defeat both: by Proposition 6 it may not be sharp in any `Γ`-relation that ties the ray's line to untouched lines, and by Theorem 4 and Proposition 5 it must be long-range entangled, or have `q=0` on the ray's line. In `Z_2` language: the electric field must fluctuate, as in a deconfined phase (Levin-Wen; Senthil-Fisher; §2).

### 4.5 The open lemma RCSPO-CL′ and three attacks on it

**RCSPO-CL′ (open; proposed wording, not adopted).** On `L5-O[S_+]`, no cell-translation-invariant D1 state that is parity-sharp and sharp in every `c_z` has covariantly chosen factor `e_1`-ray sectors with `π_x` quasi-equivalent to `π_{x+2β}` for every `β` in a subgroup of finite index of `Z^3`.

By `RCSPC.md` Corollary 2, RCSPO-CL′ implies: no update gives a Clifford node on factor odd `e_1`-ray sectors of such a state. It is the `L5-O[S_+]` counterpart of `RCSPC.md`'s RCSPC-CL′. **Proved cases.** (i) `SRE_{k,D}` backgrounds with `q>0` on the ray's line (Theorem 4). (ii) Backgrounds sharp in a `Γ`-relation that ties the ray's line to lines a displaced ray avoids (Proposition 6). (iii) `ω_+`, for every update, without factoriality (Corollary 6.1). **Remaining case.** A long-range-entangled background, or one with `q=0` on the ray's line, that is sharp in no such `Γ`-relation. The `Q`-form of the cone lift (Proposition 5) is not the right open statement: a background with `q=0` meets it trivially, by Cauchy-Schwarz (`ω(X′Q)=0` when `ω(Q)=0`), so the open statement is the quasi-equivalence form above.

**Attack 1 (finite-depth factorisation).** *Aim:* prove RCSPO-CL′ for every background by the factorisation of Theorem 4. This is `RCSPC.md` attack A with a new step: the exact factorisation of a finite-range image of a block product, which supplies the bound that attack A lacked. *What it gives:* the proved case (i), here and on the witness (Corollary 4.1). *The step that fails:* "`ω|_{𝔄^G}=Φ∘α_C|_{𝔄^G}` with `α_C` of finite range". A long-range-entangled state has no such form; this is the content of the cited circuit-depth results (Bravyi-Hastings-Verstraete; Chen-Gu-Wen; §2), which are not used as proof steps. A quasi-local `α_C` with exponential tails does not rescue the step. Its error term `|ω(YZ_s)−ω(Y)ω(Z_s)|` is bounded by a multiple of `e^{−d/ξ}‖Y‖`, while the needed bound is relative to `ω(Y)`, and `‖X′_r‖/ω(X′_r)` grows exponentially in the string length: `‖K_k‖=100` while `ω(K_k)` can be `10`, as in `ω_+`. And when `q=0` the emptied level carries no information at all.

**Attack 2 (a static `Z_2` gauge field, after Kitaev's honeycomb model).** *Aim:* refute RCSPO-CL′. Kitaev's model is "solved exactly by a reduction to free fermions in a static" `Z_2` gauge field (§2). If the link sector of `L5-O[S_+]` contained commuting, unitary, gauge-invariant flux operators that the dynamics conserves, a flux-free sector would make strings path independent, as in the toric code (Naaijkens, Lemma 3.6), and a free odd particle could carry a Dirac-type cone. *The step that fails:* there are no such flux operators among the contracted loops. (a) For a closed path `γ` of links `ℓ_1,…,ℓ_n` through `y_0,…,y_{n−1}`, the contracted loop `W_γ=Σ_{a_1,…,a_n}∏_k(T_k)_{a_{k−1}a_k}` (`a_0=a_n`) factorises exactly as in Step 1 of Lemma 3: `W_γ=ϑ\,J_{y_0}⋯J_{y_{n−1}}`, with `|ϑ|=1` and `J_{y_k}` contracting the two ends of `γ` at `y_k`. The reordering permutation does not depend on the indices, as in Step 1; each index `a_k` appears in one bracket only. So `W_γ=ϑ′∏_k(iJ_{y_k})` is a phase times a product of commuting self-adjoint operators, each with spectrum in `\{−10,…,10\}` and a nonzero kernel (Step 2). If `iJ_{y_k}v=0` then `W_γv=0`, because the factors commute. The kernel meets the physical space `F_+`: by Example 4.2's count, `\ker(iJ_{y_k})` contains vectors with both ends of chirality `+1`, and the other ends of those two links can be given chirality `+1` too. So `W_γ` has a nonzero kernel on `F_+` and is not a multiple of a unitary. (b) Two plaquettes `p,p′` that share a link `ℓ` meet at an end `u` of `ℓ`. With `η^0=η^{ℓ,u}`, `η^1=η^{ℓ_p,u}`, `η^2=η^{ℓ_{p′},u}`, the corner factors `J=Σ_aη^0_aη^1_a` and `J′=Σ_aη^0_aη^2_a` satisfy `[J,J′]=−2Σ_aη^1_aη^2_a`. (Terms with `a≠b` commute; for `a=b`, `η^0_aη^1_aη^0_aη^2_a=−η^1_aη^2_a` and `η^0_aη^2_aη^0_aη^1_a=η^1_aη^2_a`.) This commutator is `−2J″`, and `iJ″` has spectrum `\{−10,…,10\}` by Step 2, so it is nonzero. In Kitaev's model the link variables `u_{jk}=ib_jb_k` commute because each `b` enters one bond. Here every link Majorana enters every contraction at its end, because the hop and the strings must be `Spin(10)` invariant. So the toric-code and honeycomb mechanisms, which need commuting unitary flux operators, have no counterpart among the contracted loops. Whether some other long-range-entangled background has quasi-equivalent displaced sectors is not decided by this attack.

**Attack 3 (a transport automorphism, after Naaijkens' intertwiners).** *Aim:* decide RCSPO-CL′ by building, or excluding, a unitary intertwiner between `π_x` and `π_{x+2β}` from string operators. That would give the composition law that `RCSPC.md` attack C lacked ("straight-string limit sectors carry no composition law"). In the toric code the strings are unitary, and Naaijkens' Lemma 4.2 gives "a unitary intertwiner V from ρ_{γ1}^k to ρ_{γ2}^k". *New step:* on `L5-O[S_+]` the global picture gives the polar decomposition of the string. By Lemma 3, `B_{r,b}=V_b|B_{r,b}|` with `|B_{r,b}|=∏_{k=0}^{2r−1}|iJ_k|` and `V_b=ς_r∏_k{\rm sgn}(iJ_k)\,η^{ℓ_{2r},y_{2r}}_b` (use `J_k=−i(iJ_k)`, so `i^{2r}∏_kJ_k=∏_k(iJ_k)`). Here `{\rm sgn}(iJ_k)` is a self-adjoint partial isometry with kernel `Q_k`, and the far Majorana is unitary. If every `Q_k` were zero, `V_b` would be unitary and `{\rm Ad}V_b` would define transportable string automorphisms. *The step that fails:* `Q_k≠0` (Lemma 3: the kernel has dimension `252` in the pair space), so `V_b` is only a partial isometry, and `{\rm Ad}V_b` is a homomorphism only on the corner `∏_k(1−Q_k)`. Replacing `J_k` by another gauge-invariant site factor that flips both end chiralities does not help: by Remark 3.1 (standard representation theory, cited and not rebuilt), every such operator vanishes on `126⊕\overline{126}`. So straight-string sectors on `L5-O[S_+]` carry no unitary transport. Excluding an intertwiner instead needs control of the background, which is attack 1.

**Status.** RCSPO-CL′ is a lemma on which three distinct attacks failed at the named steps: finite-depth factorisation (no finite-range circuit for long-range-entangled states; no relative bound from exponential tails), a static `Z_2` field (contracted loops have kernels and their corner factors do not commute), and a transport automorphism (the site factors have the kernel `Q_k`). It is open. It is not claimed to be true or false.

## 5. RC-W and the mirror partner

**For the literal target: RC-W is not triggered.** No spinor-class straight-string sector exists on any `L5-O` register (Corollary 1.1), so there is no spinor-class node whose partner RC-W could force.

**For the odd (vector-class) sectors: no node is built, so RC-W forces nothing here.** In the classes of Theorem 4, Proposition 6 and Corollary 6.1 no Clifford node exists. In the open case of §4.5 none is constructed.

**What RC-W would say if a node were found (its own statement, not proved anew).** A Clifford node with fibre dimension `d=2` is a Weyl point in the sense of `CG16S.md` §4. Indeed `2×2` Hermitian `N_j` with `\{N_i,N_j\}=2v^2δ_{ij}`, `v>0`, are traceless: if `N=a1+b·σ` then `N^2=a^2+|b|^2+2a\,b·σ` is scalar only if `ab=0`, and `b≠0`. So `N_j=v\sum_kR_{jk}σ_k` with `R` orthogonal, and the chirality is `\det R=±1`. `CG16S.md` line 120 (Lemma RC-W): for `V` unitary on `ℓ^2(Z^3)⊗C^n`, commuting with cell translations and a finite product of block-diagonal layers with blocks in at most `D` adjacent cells, "`W₃[V]=0`". Its scope, line 140: at a quasienergy whose node set "is finite and consists of Weyl points, the net chirality is zero", and it leaves open "defects over a filled or entangled background", in a setting that "has no links". A `d=2` node in an odd string sector would therefore need a partner of opposite chirality only if that sector's band were shown to be such a walk. That is not established for string sectors, which live over link backgrounds, and the remaining case of §4.5 is exactly an entangled background. A Clifford node with `d=4` (Dirac type) carries two opposite chiralities in one fibre; RC-W asks no further partner for it.
