## 2. Character and gauge identities

**Theorem 1 (configurations).** The commutant of `S` on one mirror site is `span S=span{P^χ}`; each `P^χ` has rank one. `γ_{x,a}P_x^χ=P_x^{χρ_a}γ_{x,a}`. The eight `ρ_a` are distinct and label the odd lines. The configuration projectors are orthogonal, sum to one, commute with light and link operators, `G_x` and `N`, and satisfy `U_gP^{bold χ}=(∏_xχ_x(g))P^{bold χ}`. For an operator written in a collected linearly independent mirror-monomial basis, it commutes with every `P^{bold χ}` exactly when each nonzero mirror monomial has a code word of `C` at every site. In particular a genuine degree-four mixing monomial with mirror factor `γ_{x,a}γ_{y,a}`, `x≠y`, is off-diagonal in configurations.

*Proof.* The atom input gives each character once. Character orthogonality makes `P^χ` its rank-one projector, and the commutant is the 16-dimensional diagonal algebra `span S`. Conjugation by `g` changes `γ_a` by `ρ_a(g)`, proving the shift. Averaging `H` over all local `S_x` projects each collected mirror monomial onto itself or zero. It survives exactly when each site flavour set lies in `C^⊥=C`. This average is also `Σ_{bold χ}P^{bold χ}HP^{bold χ}`, so survival is equivalent to commuting with every projector. A singleton is not a code word. The other assertions follow from evenness of the projectors and the atom. □

**Theorem 2 (Gauss reduction).** Let `F_even` be the even total-matter Fock space and let `e_s`, `s=±1`, be a two-level basis. For a vector `Φ` with site parities `p_y`, define

`J(Φ⊗e_s)=Φ⊗_{x=0}^{L−1}|X_x=s∏_{y=0}^x p_y⟩`.

Then `J:F_even⊗C²→H_G` is unitary. A matter operator preserving all site parities maps to itself tensored with `1`. A matter operator odd at neighbouring sites and even elsewhere, multiplied by their link `Z`, maps to itself tensored with `1` on a non-wrap bond and with `σˣ` on the wrap bond. Further,

`J*X_xJ=(∏_{y=0}^xP_y)⊗σᶻ`, `J*O_xyJ=c_x†c_y⊗1`, and `J*(∏_x Z_x)J=1⊗σˣ`.

*Proof.* Gauss's equations give `X_x=P_xX_{x−1}` and force `∏P_x=1`; conversely the displayed labels solve every equation, with two choices of `s`. They give an orthonormal basis, hence a unitary. In the `X` basis, `Z` flips its sign. Flipping two neighbouring matter parities changes one link label, except on the wrap bond where it flips the reference label `s`. The same calculation for endpoints `x,y` changes exactly the intervening link labels. Flipping all links changes `s` alone. □

Thus at `τ_E=0` the holonomy `w=±1` is conserved for the displayed hopping models and produces a wrap-bond sign. An undressed charged bilinear has zero expectation in a gauge-invariant state. An electric term becomes `τ_E Σ_x[1−(∏_{y≤x}P_y)⊗σᶻ]/2`; it is a nonlocal string in the reduced matter description. This algebra does not prove confinement or require choosing `τ_E=0` in every other model.

## 3. An exact configuration-conserving family

Let `τ>0`, real `t,μ` on `Ŝ²`, `t(χ,χ′)=t(χ′,χ)`, and put

`κ_x(χ,χ′)=−t(χ,χ′)hop_x+μ(χ,χ′)(n_x−1/2)+μ(χ′,χ)(n_{x+1}−1/2)`,

`K_x=Σ_{χ,χ′}P_x^χP_{x+1}^{χ′}κ_x(χ,χ′)`, `H_A=τΣ_x(W_x+14)+Σ_xK_x`.

Set `t₀=t(1,1)≠0` and

`δ=max_{(χ,χ′)≠(1,1)}{|t(χ,χ′)−t₀|+(|μ(χ,χ′)−μ(1,1)|+|μ(χ′,χ)−μ(1,1)|)/2}`.

The bond-norm difference is at most `δ`. The terms are gauge, `S` and `U(1)_5` invariant; the sum is one-site translation and cell-reflection invariant. For example `t(χ,χ′)=t+(ε/2)(W(χ)+W(χ′))`, `μ=0` gives a degree-six dressed hop, with `t₀=t−14ε` and `δ≤16|ε|`.

**Theorem 3 (finite-ring spectrum).** `H_A` preserves every configuration. On configuration `bold χ` its mirror energy is `τΣ_x(W(χ_x)+14)` and its light operator is `Σ_xκ_x(χ_x,χ_{x+1})`. On the trivial configuration, the `μ` terms vanish at half filling and the minimum over both holonomies is

`E₀=−2|t₀|/sin(π/L)`, attained uniquely in `w=−1`; the `w=+1` minimum is `−2|t₀|cot(π/L)`.

Let `m` count nontrivial site characters. Every eigenvalue in that configuration is at least `E₀+m(14τ−2δ)`. If `δ<7τ`, the ground vector `Ω_A` is unique and `S`-invariant; all globally `S`-charged levels cost at least `14τ−2δ`, and the entire spectrum strictly below `E₀+14τ−2δ` is exactly the free half-filled light spectrum with both holonomies. For `1≤J≤L/8`, at least `J²` trivial-sector levels lie within `8π|t₀|J/L` of `E₀`. The first `w=−1` excitation costs `4|t₀|sin(π/L)`; the `w=+1` minimum costs `2|t₀|tan(π/(2L))`.

The ground ray is invariant under `T,R,U(1)_5` and holonomy. For `r=y−x`, its dressed two-point function is `sin(πr/2)/(L sin(πr/L))` for `t₀>0`, multiplied by `(−1)^r` for `t₀<0`. For disjoint supports, `⟨Ω_A,ABΩ_A⟩=0` when `A` is `S`-charged.

*Proof.* Theorem 1 gives configuration conservation and the block expression. At the trivial configuration the mirror is `ψ^{⊗L}`. Theorem 2 reduces the light chain to momenta `e^{ikL}=w`, with energy `−2t₀cos k`; the chemical potential sums to `2μ(1,1)(N−L/2)=0`. For `w=−1`, the `L/2` negative-energy modes are nonzero and uniquely occupied; summing the cosine progression gives `E₀`. For `w=+1`, two zero modes occur and the same sum gives the stated higher minimum. The sign of `t₀` shifts momenta by `π`.

A nontrivial site costs at least `14τ`. At most `2m` adjacent bond operators differ from the reference, each by norm at most `δ`. The reduced light space is the same half-filled even matter space, or empty when mirror parity is incompatible with Gauss's law. The min–max bound gives the claim. For `δ<7τ` no nontrivial configuration reaches the reference ground energy or the stated strict interval. A charged vector lies in configurations with nontrivial product character. The `J²` distinct particle-hole excitations at one Fermi point, with hole and particle indices `0,…,J−1`, each cost at most `8π|t₀|J/L`. The lowest such excitation and the two holonomy energies give the stated gaps. The Fourier geometric sum gives the two-point function. Each `g_x` fixes `ψ`; applying the product over the support of `A` changes an `S`-charged `A` by a nontrivial character while fixing `B` and the state, proving vanishing. Uniqueness gives the remaining symmetry rays. □

This proves S1–S3 for this family and the exact finite-volume low-energy comparison. The free ring has opposite Fermi points `±π/2`, a vector-like light pair. The correlation limit alone is not a complete proof of a `c=1` scaling theory for all fields, nor a general criterion for excluding all light fields.

## 4. Tick and finite-ring stationary state

Let `L_site=∏_x e^{−iτ(W_x+14)}`, `L_e=∏_{x even}e^{−iK_x}`, `L_o=∏_{x odd}e^{−iK_x}` and `U_A=L_oL_eL_site`. If `16τ<π` and `max‖κ_x(χ,χ′)‖<π`, the displayed gate generators are principal and R-GEN assigns `H_A`. Every layer is NN-a and the full tick has the stated symmetries.

**Theorem 4 (tick bands and finite states).** Assume also `0<|t₀|<π/2`. The tick preserves every configuration. On the trivial configuration and fixed holonomy it is the product of the odd and even free hopping layers. In two-site cells its one-body bands are `e^{±iε(q)}`, where

`cos ε(q)=cos²t₀−sin²t₀ cos q`, `0≤ε(q)≤2|t₀|`.

The only band touching is `q=π`, absent from the `w=−1` momenta on these rings. Filling the `e^{iε}` band yields a pure finite-ring vector `Ω_tick` in `H_{1/2}`, stationary up to phase and invariant as a ray under `S`, `U(1)_5`, `T²` and `R`. Its disjoint-support charged correlations vanish as in Theorem 3. For `x` even, `y` odd and cell separation `r`, the thermodynamic limit of its dressed two-point function has modulus `|cos t₀|/(2πr)+O(r⁻²)`.

*Proof.* Each gate commutes with every configuration projector. On the trivial block the chemical terms in each layer are multiples of `N−L/2` and vanish. The even and odd one-body cell matrices are `−t₀σˣ` and `−t₀(cos q σˣ+sin q σʸ)` in a consistent Fourier convention. Multiplication of their exponentials has determinant 1 and half-trace as displayed. The antiperiodic cell grid excludes `π`. Filling the separated band gives one particle per cell and an eigenvector of the many-body tick; commuting cell symmetries preserve its occupied subspace. The mirror remains a product of `ψ`, giving the charged correlation result.

The occupied-band projector is analytic away from the touching and has a single jump there. Expanding at `q=π+η`, the normalized Floquet axis tends to opposite signs of `(0,cos t₀,sin t₀)` on the two sides. The off-diagonal jump has modulus `|cos t₀|`. Fourier integration by parts yields the coefficient `1/(2πr)` and an `O(r⁻²)` remainder. The finite-grid sums converge to that integral. □

**GAP R-COR-purity.** Pass 1 identifies finite-ring pure states with an infinite-volume pure state without proving purity on the specified gauge-invariant quasi-local algebra. Finite-volume purity alone does not imply purity of a weak limit. The displayed local correlation limits and stationarity do not close C1. Thus R-COR is not established as a whole. Whether `Ω_tick` and the R-GEN ground state `Ω_A` differ by only `O(t₀)` on local observables is also open. The finite tick result does not solve SMG1's FLQ for a hopping mirror.

## 5. Half-filling twist

**Theorem 5 (twist; after Lieb–Schultz–Mattis and Yamanaka–Oshikawa–Affleck).** Let `h` be Hermitian, supported on sites `0,…,r` and intervening links, and commute with `N`, global `S` and all Gauss operators. Let `H=Σ_xT^xhT^{−x}`, `L≥2r+2`, on `H_{1/2}`. For any joint translation and `S`-character ground vector `Ψ₀`, there is an orthogonal unit vector `Φ` in its own `S` sector with

`⟨Φ,HΦ⟩≤E₀+C_h/L`, `C_h=2π²‖h‖Σ_{|d|≤d_r}d²`, `d_r=⌊(r+1)²/4⌋`.

For `r=1`, `C_h=4π²‖h‖`.

*Proof.* Put `U=exp((2πi/L)Σ_x x n_x)`. It preserves Gauss's law, filling and `S`. Translation gives `TUT*=U exp(−2πiN/L)=−U` on the sector, so both `U^{±1}Ψ₀` are orthogonal to `Ψ₀`. On a translated support, the wrap contributes an integral multiple of `2π`, and local number conservation reduces conjugation to the operator `D=Σ_{j=0}^r j n_{x+j}`. Decompose the local term into Fourier components under `ad D`. Since local particle number is conserved, possible eigenvalue differences satisfy `|d|≤d_r`; each Fourier component has norm at most `‖h‖`. Averaging the two twist energies cancels the linear term. Using `1−cos z≤z²/2` and summing `L` translates gives the bound; choose the lower of the two energies. □

**Corollary 5.1.** A fixed local `h` cannot have both a unique ground state and an `L`-uniform gap above it within its `S` sector. Under S1 and S3 the trivial-sector gap is at most `C_h/L`.

*Proof.* Use the variational principle in the sector; S1 places a unique ground vector in the trivial sector. □

One-site translation of the **generator**, fixed local range and fixed `‖h‖` are essential hypotheses. This proves only a second low state, not S2's diverging number of states. It neither proves S1 nor uniqueness, and does not establish a Luttinger limit. `SMG1.md` Theorem 3.1 independently already establishes a different translation-dependent quadratic obstruction.

## 6. A dressed degree-four family

Define the anti-Hermitian bond operators `D_x=Σ_{a=1}^8γ_{x,a}γ_{x+1,a}hop⁰_x` and `V_ε=V_oV_e`, `V_e=∏_{x even}e^{εD_x}`, `V_o=∏_{x odd}e^{εD_x}`. These operators commute with each site parity, Gauss law, `S` and `N`; `R` preserves each bond layer. They are genuine degree-four mirror-light gates. Use `‖εD_x‖<π` when invoking the principal-generator prescription.

**Theorem 6 (finite-depth dressing).** With the data of Theorems 3 and 4, put `H^ε=V_εH_AV_ε*`, `U^ε=V_εU_AV_ε*`, `Ω^ε=V_εΩ_A`, and `Ω_tick^ε=V_εΩ_tick`. The generator is a sum of terms on at most six consecutive sites and their links and preserves gauge law, `S`, `U(1)_5` and the cell group. Its spectrum in each global `S` sector of `H_{1/2}` is exactly that of `H_A`. If `δ<7τ`, Theorem 3's unique ground state and S1–S3 hold with the same constants. The tick is NN-a and `Ω_tick^ε` is a finite-ring stationary symmetric ray. Charged correlations vanish when supports are separated by more than four sites.

For constant `t=t₀`, `μ=0`, its first-order degree-four part includes

`−εt₀ Σ_{x,a}γ_{x,a}γ_{x+1,a}([hop⁰_x,hop_{x+1}]+[hop⁰_x,hop_{x−1}])`,

where `[hop⁰_x,hop_{x+1}]=Z_{x+1}(c_x†c_{x+2}−c_{x+2}†c_x)`. Degree-six companions arise from the commutator with `τΣW_x`. For the displayed tick decomposition, proposed R-GEN assigns `H_A`, since the principal generators of the dressing gates and their inverses cancel.

*Proof.* A depth-two circuit enlarges a two-site support by at most two sites at each end. Every gate commutes with the relevant global symmetries and gauge generators; its layers are invariant under the cell operations. Therefore conjugation preserves each sector and its entire spectrum. The conjugated circuit is an NN-a product and maps the tick eigenvector to another eigenvector. Conjugating local charged observables expands each support by at most two sites and preserves its character, so the local symmetry argument of Theorem 3 gives the charged correlation claim.

Expanding `V_εH_AV_ε*=H_A+εΣ_x[D_x,H_A]+O(ε²)` term by term gives the formula. Even mirror pairs commute with light operators. The same-bond light hops commute; the adjacent-bond CAR commutator is the displayed nonzero expression. Distinct mirror-pair and light monomials are independent in the collected basis. Finally, the signed principal generators of `V_e,V_o` and their inverse gates cancel in R-GEN's sum, while those of `U_A` sum to `H_A`. □

Pass 1 also argues that the dressed tick retains the long-distance light correlation for sufficiently small `ε`: the mirror expectation of `V_ε*c_xV_ε` equals `c_x+O(ε²)` as a bounded-support odd light polynomial, since the first-order mirror pair has zero product-state expectation. Circuit conjugation does not preserve configuration projectors, and the exact spectrum below threshold need not consist of states with undressed mirror in its original ground configuration.

## 7. Second-order mixing identity

Let `H_M=τΣ_x(W_x+14)`, `P=P^{bold 1}`, `Q=1−P`, and `H_4=Σ_{x,a}M_{x,a}Λ_x`. Consider either (I) `M_{x,a}=iγ_{x,a}γ_{x+1,a}`, `Λ_x=hop⁰_x`; or (II) `M_{x,a}=iγ_{x,a}Z_xγ_{x+1,a}`, with `Λ_x` a real polynomial in `n_x,n_{x+1}`. The operators are gauge, `S` and `U(1)_5` invariant. In case II a general polynomial can include higher-degree terms, so only its affine part is necessarily degree four.

**Theorem 7 (atomic resolvent).** For either case,

`PH_4P=0`, and `PH_4Q(QH_MQ)⁻¹QH_4P=(2/(7τ))Σ_xΛ_x² P`.

For case I, `Λ_x²=n_x+n_{x+1}−2n_xn_{x+1}`. Thus the formal leading second-order correction is `−(4ε²/(7τ))N+(4ε²/(7τ))Σ_xn_xn_{x+1}`. For case II, `Λ_x=n_x−1/2` yields a constant; `Λ_x=n_x+n_{x+1}−1` gives an attractive density coefficient `−4ε²/(7τ)`.

*Proof.* Each `M_{x,a}` sends the all-trivial mirror configuration to the configuration with `ρ_a` at both endpoints, whose atomic energy is `28τ`. For the rings considered, distinct `(x,a)` give distinct configurations. Returning to the trivial configuration requires the same pair. Since `M_{x,a}²=1` and `M_{x,a}` commutes with its light factor, the eight flavours contribute `8/(28τ)=2/(7τ)` on each bond. The first identity follows from the character shift. The light squares follow directly from CAR and `n²=n`. □

The operator identity is exact. Interpreting its negative as an effective Hamiltonian uses a perturbative expansion around `H_M`, with `H_L` corrections to denominators and higher orders not bounded here. It does not yield volume-uniform decoupling. The ratio `ε²/(τ|t|)` is a useful proposed weak-interaction diagnostic, not a proved necessary condition for R1.5-U. Pass 1's L14, Benfatto–Mastropietro, arXiv:0907.2837, concerns a specified effective chain; its full hypotheses and transfer to the original coupled model are not checked here.

## 8. Open obligations and failed routes

**S-GAP (open).** Do there exist `c₀,Δ>0` such that, for every `τ>0`, `|t|,|ε|≤c₀τ` and every allowed `L`, the `S`-charged spectrum of

`H_ε=τΣ_x(W_x+14)−tΣ_xhop_x+εΣ_{x,a}iγ_{x,a}γ_{x+1,a}hop⁰_x`

lies at least `Δτ` above its ground energy on `H_{1/2}`? This includes `t=0`. A unique ground state is a separate question. At `t=0`, Theorem 7's second-order light operator is minimized by the two alternating occupation configurations, but a truncated effective Hamiltonian does not decide the exact ground state.

Three routes in pass 1 do not prove S-GAP:

1. **Local Schrieffer–Wolff.** The cited Abanin–De Roeck–Ho–Huveneers construction gives an almost-conserved atomic energy with a small local remainder under its hypotheses. The draft's proposed bound for the block-diagonal part is itself not proved. More decisively, bounding the total remainder by the sum of local norms gives a bound growing with `L`, so Weyl's inequality does not yield the required uniform sector gap. The same volume obstacle is identified for a different Feshbach calculation in the Spin(10) note cited above.
2. **Absolute light-linked polymer expansion.** Pass 1 identifies a potential long-distance `|z|⁻²` contribution from two free light propagators, whose absolute sum over one space and one time dimension diverges logarithmically. This defeats the naive absolute bound if that contribution has a nonzero coefficient. The draft does not calculate the complete polymer weights or exclude cancellations, so it does not establish that every Kotecký–Preiss criterion fails. A multiscale treatment remains a proposed route.
3. **Fröhlich–Pizzo stability.** The theorem quoted in `SMG1_REFEREE.md` §2.5 requires a unique on-site ground vector and a gap above it. With the fifth mode unsplit, the reference on-site kernel is two-dimensional. Splitting it with `τ₅n_x` gives an empty, rather than half-filled, reference chain. The cited global-gap conclusion also cannot be applied unchanged to the one-site-invariant half-filled family in the presence of Theorem 5.
