## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**Proved here, natively,** for R1.5 on a framework tick in 1+1D (five-mode sites, NN-a gates, Z₂ parity links with `τ_E = 0`, rings `Z_L` with `L ≡ 0 mod 4` at half filling). (1) **Theorem A**, an exact construction: Fidkowski–Kitaev atoms on modes 1-4, a half-filled light chain on mode 5, and every symmetric mixing whose mirror factor is on-site (degree six or more; example: the light hop dressed by `W`). The generator conserves the mirror character configuration. Every `S`-charged state costs at least `14τ − 2δ`; below that energy the spectrum is exactly the free light ring's; the ground state is unique and symmetric; the light gap is at most `4π|t_0|/L`, and the number of light states below any fixed energy grows without bound. **Proposition A′**: the tick also conserves the configuration and has an exactly stationary, pure, symmetric state, with mirror correlations of compact range and a `1/r` light correlation. (2) **Lemma 3**: a symmetric generator conserves the configuration exactly when its mirror factors are on-site code words; degree-four mixing never does. (3) **Theorem B**, the Lieb–Schultz–Mattis twist rebuilt: for every one-site-translation-invariant symmetric generator with the light chain half filled, with any mixing and any mirror hop, the ground vector's own `S`-sector has another state within `C_h/L`. So no mixing gaps the light chain without ground-state degeneracy. (4) **Theorem C**: a symmetric depth-two circuit of degree-four gates turns Theorem A's model into one with genuine degree-four mixing at order `εt_0` and the same exact spectrum; the conjugated NN-a tick has an exactly stationary state. R-GEN of the displayed decomposition does not see these gates. (5) **Proposition D**: generic degree-four mixing induces at second order `−(2ε²/7τ)Σ_xΛ_x²` on the light chain, a nearest-neighbour density interaction `±4ε²/(7τ)` for hop- or density-type light factors. It is marginal in 1+1D, so R1.5's smallness must compare `ε²/τ` with `|t|`. **Open after three named attacks:** Lemma S-GAP, a uniform gap of the `S`-charged sectors at zero mirror hop with one degree-four term beside the free half-filled chain. Attack 1 (local Schrieffer–Wolff, L17 Theorem 3.1) fails at the extensive, quasi-exponentially small remainder; attack 2 (polymer expansion with the free light field) fails at the log-divergent sum of `|z|^{−2}` light links; attack 3 (Fröhlich–Pizzo Theorem 3.5) fails at its one-dimensional on-site ground space, which half filling excludes and whose conclusion Theorem B contradicts. **Also:** with `τ_E > 0` the electric term becomes a nonlocal string term (Lemma 2), so R1.5's light chain should take `τ_E = 0`. Golterman–Shamir's theorem is satisfied at R1.5, whose light content is vector-like; it bites first at R2. Two readings, R15-SPEC and R-COR, are proposed with tests `C, P, C, P`; neither is adopted.

## 2. Setting: the tick, the modes, the reading

### 2.1 Carrier, symmetries, sectors

The ring is `Λ = Z_L` with `L ≡ 0 (mod 4)`, `L ≥ 8`. Site `x` carries Majoranas `γ_{x,1}, …, γ_{x,10}` with `{γ_{x,a}, γ_{y,b}} = 2δ_{xy}δ_{ab}` and modes `c_{x,j} = (γ_{x,2j−1} + iγ_{x,2j})/2` (`SMG1.md` line 19). The **mirror** is modes 1-4 (`γ_{x,1..8}`). The **light** mode is mode 5: `c_x := c_{x,5}`, `n_x := c_x†c_x`, `N_5 := Σ_x n_x`. `P_x = (−1)^{N_x}` is the site parity. Link `ℓ_x = (x, x+1 mod L)` carries a qubit with Paulis `X_ℓ, Z_ℓ`, commuting with matter. The Gauss operators are `G_x = P_xX_{ℓ_{x−1}}X_{ℓ_x}` (`SMG1.md` line 41). The **gauge sector** is `H_G = {Ψ : G_xΨ = Ψ for all x}`, and the **half-filled sector** is `H_{1/2} := H_G ∩ {N_5 = L/2}`.

Symmetries. `S = ⟨g₁,…,g₄⟩` as in `SMG1.md` line 39; `g_x` is the copy of `g` on site `x`, and `U_g := Π_x g_x` implements the global action (each `g_x` is even, so `U_g` commutes with every `G_x`, light operator and link). A vector with `U_gΨ = ρ(g)Ψ` for all `g` has **`S`-character `ρ`**; it is **`S`-charged** if `ρ ≠ 1`. `U(1)_5` is `Ad e^{iφN_5}`. `T` is the one-site translation (sites and links). `R` is the reflection `x ↦ 1−x`: `γ_{x,a} ↦ (−1)^xγ_{1−x,a}` for `a ≤ 8` (`SMG1.md` line 77), `γ_{x,9}, γ_{x,10} ↦ γ_{1−x,9}, γ_{1−x,10}`, `ℓ_x ↦ ℓ_{−x}` with `X, Z` fixed. It maps even bonds `(2m, 2m+1)` to even bonds. The cell group is generated by `T²` and `R`. Ticks are NN-a (`TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` section 3.7). For a named decomposition into gates `e^{−iX_g}` with `‖X_g‖ < π`, R-GEN assigns `H = Σ_g X_g` (`SMG1.md` line 21; a proposal, owner Q1).

### 2.2 Characters and configurations

**Lemma 1 (characters).** (a) On one site's mirror space `ΛC⁴`, the operators commuting with every `g ∈ S` form `span S = span{P^χ : χ ∈ Ŝ}`, where `P^χ := 16^{−1}Σ_{g∈S} χ(g) g` is the rank-one projector onto the `χ`-line. (b) A local operator `A` supported on a finite set `X` satisfies `Ad g_x(A) = A` for every `g ∈ S` and `x ∈ X` if and only if `A = Σ_i m_i b_i` with each `m_i ∈ ⊗_{x∈X} span S_x` and `b_i` in the algebra of light modes and links. (c) `γ_{x,a}P^χ_x = P^{χρ_a}_xγ_{x,a}`, with `ρ_a(g) = (−1)^{u_a(g)}`, `u_a(g)` the indicator of `a` in the support of `g`. The eight `ρ_a` are the characters of the lines `c_aψ`. `W_xP^χ_x = W(χ)P^χ_x` with `W(1) = −14`, `W(ρ_a) = 0`, and `W(χ) = 2` for the seven other `χ ≠ 1`. (d) For `χ⃗ = (χ_x) ∈ Ŝ^Λ` put `P^{χ⃗} := Π_x P^{χ_x}_x`. These are commuting orthogonal projectors with sum `1`. Each commutes with every light and link operator, with every `G_x` and with `N_5`, and `U_gP^{χ⃗} = (Π_xχ_x(g))P^{χ⃗}`.

*Proof.* (a) By `SMG1.md` Theorem 2.2 each of the sixteen characters occurs once on `ΛC⁴`. An operator commuting with `S` preserves each isotypic line, so the commutant is the diagonal algebra in a character basis, of dimension 16. ### 2.3 Gauss reduction

**Lemma 2 (Gauss reduction on the ring).** Let `F_even` be the even subspace of the matter Fock space (all `5L` modes), and `C²` have basis `e_{+1}, e_{−1}`. For a matter vector `Φ` with definite site parities `p_y` and `s = ±1`, put `J(Φ ⊗ e_s) := Φ ⊗ ⊗_{x=0}^{L−1}|X = s Π_{y=0}^{x} p_y⟩_{ℓ_x}`, extended linearly. Then `J` is a unitary from `F_even ⊗ C²` onto `H_G`, and:
(i) a matter operator `A` commuting with every `P_y` satisfies `J*AJ = A ⊗ 1`;
(ii) if `B` is odd at `x` and at `x+1` and even elsewhere, then `J*(BZ_{ℓ_x})J = B ⊗ 1` for `0 ≤ x ≤ L−2`, and `J*(BZ_{ℓ_{L−1}})J = B ⊗ σ^x`;
(iii) `J*X_{ℓ_x}J = (Π_{y=0}^{x}P_y) ⊗ σ^z`;
(iv) for `0 ≤ x < y ≤ L−1`, the dressed bilinear `O_{xy} := c_x†(Π_{x≤x'<y}Z_{ℓ_{x'}})c_y` satisfies `J*O_{xy}J = c_x†c_y ⊗ 1`;
(v) the holonomy `Π_x Z_{ℓ_x}` maps to `1 ⊗ σ^x`.

*Proof.* On `H_G`, `G_x = 1` and `X² = 1` give `X_{ℓ_x} = P_xX_{ℓ_{x−1}}`. Starting from `X_{ℓ_{L−1}} = s`, induction gives `X_{ℓ_x} = sΠ_{y≤x}P_y`, and closing the ring forces `Π_yP_y = 1`. So `H_G` is exactly the span of the vectors `J(Φ ⊗ e_s)`, and `J` is isometric because distinct `(Φ, s)` give orthogonal link labels or matter vectors. Use the real convention `Z|X=σ⟩ = |X=−σ⟩`. (i) `A` keeps every `p_y`, so it keeps the link labels. (ii) `B` flips `p_x` and `p_{x+1}`. For `x ≤ L−2` the label `sΠ_{y≤x'}p_y` changes only at `x' = x`, and `Z_{ℓ_x}` flips exactly that link. For the wrap bond, `B` flips `p_{L−1}` and `p_0`; every label with `x' ≤ L−2` changes sign, and `Z_{ℓ_{L−1}}` flips the base `s`; the result is `J(BΦ ⊗ e_{−s})`, consistent because `(−s)Π_{y≤x'}p'_y = sΠ_{y≤x'}p_y`. (iii) Read off the labels. (iv) `c_x†c_y` flips `p_x` and `p_y`; the labels change exactly for `x ≤ x' < y`, which the string flips. (v) Flipping every link maps `s` to `−s` and keeps all products consistent. ∎

So at `τ_E = 0` every gauge-invariant generator made of terms of types (i) and (ii) reduces to `H_bulk ⊗ 1 + H_wrap ⊗ σ^x`. `1 ⊗ σ^x` is conserved; on its eigenspace `w = ±1` the generator is `H_bulk + wH_wrap` on `F_even`, a ring whose wrap terms carry the sign `w`. Undressed bilinears such as `c_x†c_y` anticommute with `G_x`, so their expectation vanishes in every vector of `H_G`; (iv) gives the dressed one.

**Remark (the electric term).** By (iii), `τ_EΣ_x(1−X_{ℓ_x})/2` becomes `τ_EΣ_x(1 − (Π_{y≤x}P_y) ⊗ σ^z)/2`. With the mirror in its even ground lines, this charges `τ_E` for each link with an odd number of light fermions on its left: a nonlocal string term. ### 3.1 Which mixing conserves the mirror configuration

**Lemma 3 (configuration-conserving terms).** Let `H` be a finite sum of local terms, each invariant under the global `S` action. Put `E(H) := Σ_{χ⃗} P^{χ⃗}HP^{χ⃗}`. Then `[H, P^{χ⃗}] = 0` for every `χ⃗` if and only if every mirror monomial in `H` has, at every site, a flavour set that is a word of the code `C` of `SMG1.md` Lemma 2.1; equivalently, if and only if the mirror factors of `H` lie in `⊗_x span S_x` (Lemma 1 (b)). A genuine degree-four mixing monomial `γ_{x,a}γ_{y,a}L` (`x ≠ y`, Lemma M (iv)) never meets this: `P^{χ⃗}γ_{x,a}γ_{y,a}P^{χ⃗} = 0` for every `χ⃗`.

*Proof.* If `[H, P^{χ⃗}] = 0` for all `χ⃗`, then `H = Σ_{χ⃗}P^{χ⃗}H = E(H)`; the converse is clear. Take a monomial `γ_μb_μ` as in the proof of Lemma 1 (b), with flavour set `f_x ⊂ {1,…,8}` at site `x`. By Lemma 1 (c), `γ_μP^{χ⃗} = P^{χ⃗ρ_μ}γ_μ`, where `(ρ_μ)_x = Π_{a∈f_x}ρ_a`, the character `g ↦ (−1)^{u(g)·f_x}`. So `P^{χ⃗}γ_μP^{χ⃗} = 0` unless `(ρ_μ)_x = 1` at every site, that is, unless `f_x` is orthogonal to every word of `C`, that is, `f_x ∈ C^⊥ = C` (`SMG1.md` Lemma 2.1). When all `f_x ∈ C`, each site factor is `±` an element of `S_x` and commutes with every `P^χ_x`, so `E(γ_μb_μ) = γ_μb_μ`. Hence `H = E(H)` exactly when every monomial of `H` has all `f_x ∈ C`. For `γ_{x,a}γ_{y,a}` with `x ≠ y`, `f_x = {a}` has weight one and is not in `C`. ∎

Lemma M (ii) constrains the *total* flavour set (it must lie in `C`); Lemma 3's condition is the stronger *site-by-site* one. The weakest configuration-conserving mixing has degree six, such as `W_xn_x` (`SMG1_REFEREE.md` line 249). So no exactly solvable class of the following kind contains degree-four mixing.

The eigenvalue sets of the two bands, `{e^{iε}}` and `{e^{−iε}}` with `0 < ε < π`, are disjoint, so every unitary commuting with the one-body tick preserves the lower-band subspace. `T²`, `R` and the holonomy commute with `U_A`, so they fix `Φ_F^tick` up to a phase. `g_x` fixes `ψ`. (e) (C2): the argument of (A6), since `g_XΩ_tick = Ω_tick`. (C3): by Lemma 2 (iv), `⟨O_{xy}⟩ = ⟨c_x†c_y⟩` in `Φ_F^tick`. For `x = 2m` and `y = 2m'+1` this is `(2/L)Σ_q e^{iq(m'−m)}Π(q)_{BA}`, where `Π(q)` is the lower-band projector, up to the sign convention of the cell Fourier transform. `Π` is real-analytic on `(−π, π)` and has one-sided analytic extensions at `π`. Indeed write `u(q) = cos ε(q) − i a⃗(q)·σ` with `a⃗ = sin ε·n̂`; `a⃗` is real-analytic, vanishes only at `q = π`, and vanishes there linearly (expansion below), so `a⃗(q) = (q − π)b⃗(q)` with `b⃗` analytic and `b⃗(π) ≠ 0`; and `Π = (1 − n̂·σ)/2` with `n̂ = sign(q − π)b⃗/|b⃗|` near `π`. The sum is a Riemann sum on a grid avoiding `π`, so it converges to `∫_{−π}^{π}(dq/2π)e^{iqr}Π(q)_{BA}`, `r = m' − m`. Expand at `q = π + η`: `h_o(π + η) = t_0σ^x + ηt_0σ^y + O(η²)`, so `u(π + η) = 1 − iη t_0∫_0^1 e^{−ist_0σ^x}σ^ye^{ist_0σ^x}ds + O(η²) = 1 − iη sin t_0(cos t_0σ^y + sin t_0σ^z) + O(η²)`. So the Floquet direction at `π ± 0` is `±n̂_0`, `n̂_0 = (0, cos t_0, sin t_0)` (sign of `t_0` absorbed), and the jump of `Π` at `π` is `±n̂_0·σ`. Its `BA` entry has modulus `|cos t_0| > 0`. One integration by parts for a function analytic on each side of a single jump gives `∫(dq/2π)e^{iqr}Π(q) = (jump)·e^{iπr}/(2πir) + O(r^{−2})`. ∎

So in Theorem A's class FLQ is solved in this sense: the tick itself conserves the mirror configuration and has an exactly stationary, symmetric, pure state with a gapped mirror and a gapless light chain. Whether `Ω_tick` agrees with the R-GEN ground state `Ω_A` to `O(t_0)` on local observables is not shown here. This is not R1's FLQ, which concerns a hopping mirror.

### 3.4 Theorem B: no mixing gaps the half-filled light chain without degeneracy

This is the global-twist Lieb–Schultz–Mattis argument (L13, Theorem 0 there, credited to Yamanaka–Oshikawa–Affleck and Koma), rebuilt for the gauged five-mode ring with `S`-sectors. It covers every mixing degree and every mirror hop.

**Theorem B.** Let `r ≥ 1` and let `h` be a Hermitian operator supported on sites `0, …, r` and links `ℓ_0, …, ℓ_{r−1}`, commuting with `N_5`, with every `U_g` and with every `G_x`. On `Z_L`, `L ≡ 0 mod 4`, `L ≥ 2r + 2`, put `H := Σ_{x∈Z_L} T^xhT^{−x}`. Let `E_0 := min spec(H|H_{1/2})` and let `Ψ_0 ∈ H_{1/2}` be a ground vector that is an eigenvector of `T` and of every `U_g`, with `S`-character `ρ_0`. Then there is a unit vector `Φ ∈ H_{1/2}` with `⟨Φ, Ψ_0⟩ = 0`, `U_gΦ = ρ_0(g)Φ` for all `g`, and

`⟨Φ, HΦ⟩ ≤ E_0 + C_h/L`,   `C_h := 2π²‖h‖Σ_{|d|≤d_r} d²`,   `d_r := ⌊(r+1)²/4⌋`.

For nearest-neighbour `h` (`r = 1`), `C_h = 4π²‖h‖`.

*Proof.* (1) `T`, the `U_g`, `N_5` and `H` commute pairwise and preserve `H_G` (`T` permutes the `G_x`), so the ground space of `H|H_{1/2}` has a joint eigenvector `Ψ_0`.
(2) Put `U := exp((2πi/L)Σ_{x=0}^{L−1} x n_x)`. It commutes with `N_5`, every `U_g`, every `G_x` (each `n_x` is even and commutes with `P_y` and the links) and every mirror operator. So `U^{±1}` preserve `H_{1/2}` and `S`-characters.
(3) `Tn_xT* = n_{x+1 mod L}`, so `TUT* = exp((2πi/L)Σ_x x n_{x+1}) = exp((2πi/L)[Σ_y y n_y − N_5 + Ln_0]) = Ue^{−2πiN_5/L}`, because `e^{2πin_0} = 1` and all `n_y` commute. On `H_{1/2}`, `TUT* = −U`. If `TΨ_0 = λΨ_0`, then `T(UΨ_0) = −λUΨ_0`; `T` is unitary, so `UΨ_0 ⊥ Ψ_0`. The same holds for `U*`.
(4) Fix `x` and write `h_x := T^xhT^{−x}`, supported on the window `y = x + j − Lσ_j`, `j = 0, …, r`, with `σ_j ∈ {0,1}` marking the wrap. Then `Σ_{y in window} y n_y = xN_{w} + D_x − LΣ_jσ_jn_{x+j}`, with `N_w := Σ_jn_{x+j}` and `D_x := Σ_j j n_{x+j}`. The last term contributes `e^{−2πiΣσ_jn} = 1`; `N_w` commutes with `h_x`, because `h_x` commutes with `N_5` and with every `n_y` outside the window. So `U*h_xU = e^{−(2πi/L)D_x}h_xe^{(2πi/L)D_x}`.
(5) `D_x` has integer spectrum. Put `h_x^{[d]} := (2π)^{−1}∫_0^{2π}e^{iφd}e^{−iφD_x}h_xe^{iφD_x}dφ`, so `‖h_x^{[d]}‖ ≤ ‖h‖`, `h_x = Σ_dh_x^{[d]}` and `U*h_xU = Σ_d e^{−2πid/L}h_x^{[d]}`. `h_x` preserves each eigenspace of `N_w`; on `N_w = k` the eigenvalues of `D_x` lie in an interval of length `k(r+1−k) ≤ (r+1)²/4`. So `h_x^{[d]} = 0` for `|d| > d_r`.
(6) Then `(U*h_xU + Uh_xU*)/2 − h_x = Σ_d(cos(2πd/L) − 1)h_x^{[d]}`, whose expectation in `Ψ_0` is at most `Σ_{|d|≤d_r}(2π²d²/L²)‖h‖`. Summing the `L` terms, `(⟨UΨ_0, HUΨ_0⟩ + ⟨U*Ψ_0, HU*Ψ_0⟩)/2 ≤ E_0 + C_h/L`. Take `Φ` to be whichever of `UΨ_0`, `U*Ψ_0` has the lower energy. ∎

**Corollary B1.** For fixed `h` as in Theorem B, no family `H_L` has, for all large `L`, a unique ground vector on `H_{1/2}` and a gap at least `Δ > 0` above it inside that vector's `S`-sector. So for every one-site-translation-invariant symmetric generator with the light chain half filled (any mixing, of degree four or more, with or without mirror hop), a uniform gap in the ground vector's `S`-sector forces ground-state (quasi-)degeneracy. If (S1) and (S3) of R15-SPEC hold, the `S`-trivial gap is at most `C_h/L`.

*Proof.* A unique ground vector is a joint eigenvector of `T` and the `U_g`; Theorem B then gives another vector of its sector within `C_h/L`. Under (S1) the unique ground vector lies in the `S`-trivial part (an `S`-charged ground vector would contradict (S1)). ∎

**Scope.** Theorem B needs one-site translation of the *generator*, as `SMG1.md` Theorem 3.1 does; the NN-a tick itself need not have it. It is a finite-volume statement: it gives the weak form of (S2) (a closing gap) but not the growing number of low states, which L13's Theorem 2 obtains under extra decay hypotheses (not rebuilt). It says nothing about `S`-charged sectors, so it does not give (S1). In R1.5 the light chain's gaplessness is therefore protected by the filling anomaly of `U(1)_5 × T` (the outline's "filling argument", line 182) against every symmetric mixing, *except* spontaneous breaking. What remains open for generic mixing is (S1) and (S3), not (S2).

### 3.5 Theorem C: exact control of a degree-four family by a symmetric circuit

For each bond `x` (the wrap bond included) put

`G_x := Σ_{a=1}^{8} γ_{x,a}γ_{x+1,a}·hop⁰_x`,   `hop⁰_x := c_x†c_{x+1} + c_{x+1}†c_x`.

### 3.7 The light-partner test and the failed attempts on file

**Golterman–Shamir (L9).** Their theorem assumes a translation-invariant lattice Hamiltonian of finite range that "depends on fermion fields only", an unbroken compact symmetry, a continuum limit of "relativistic free massless fermions and with no massless bosons", and a complete set of interpolating fields whose zero-frequency matrix has no zeros. It concludes that "the massless fermion spectrum in this charge sector is vector-like". At `τ_E = 0`, Lemma 2 removes the links, leaving fermions and a holonomy label. In Theorems A and C the light content *is* vector-like: below `14τ − 2δ` the spectrum is exactly that of the free half-filled ring, whose low-energy content is one right mover at `k = π/2` and one left mover at `k = −π/2` in the `U(1)_5` charge-one sector. So the theorem's conclusion holds and obstructs nothing at R1.5. Its "light partners", bound states that pair with gapped mirror states, would be light states carrying mirror quantum numbers. In Theorems A and C every `S`-charged state costs at least `14τ − 2δ`, so no such partner exists in these classes (proved, (A4) and (C-c)). The test bites first at R2, where the light content is meant to be chiral. L9's remark that derivative-free four-fermion operators are marginal in two dimensions is what Proposition D finds and what attack 2 below meets.

**Eichten–Preskill (L7 NOT VERIFIED; L8, L11 context).** L8 judges the proposal "unlikely to work". In Theorem A the strong coupling `τ` acts on mirror modes only, and the light mode enters only through configuration-conserving terms (A1). ### 3.8 Generic degree-four mixing: Lemma S-GAP, open after three named attacks

Theorems A and C give exact R1.5 models. Theorem B gives the light half of R15-SPEC for every symmetric one-site-invariant generator, except for degeneracy. **Lemma S-GAP (open).** There are `c_0 > 0` and `Δ > 0` such that for all `τ > 0`, `|t| ≤ c_0τ`, `|ε| ≤ c_0τ` and all `L ≡ 0 mod 4`, `L ≥ 8`, the generator

`H_ε = τΣ_x(W_x + 14) − tΣ_xhop_x + εΣ_xΣ_{a=1}^8 iγ_{x,a}γ_{x+1,a}hop⁰_x`

(case (I) of Proposition D) satisfies `spec(H_ε|S-charged part of H_{1/2}) ⊂ [E_0(L) + Δτ, ∞)`.

With S-GAP, Corollary B1 gives the weak form of (S2) in the `S`-trivial sector whenever the ground vector is unique. Uniqueness (S3) is a separate question. By Proposition D it should need `ε² ≪ τ|t|`; at `t = 0` the second-order light term `−(2ε²/7τ)Σ(hop⁰_x)²` is diagonal in occupations and is minimized exactly by the two alternating configurations (all `L` bonds singly occupied), so uniqueness is not expected there. It is not worked here.

**Attack 1: local Schrieffer–Wolff to all orders (L17, Theorem 3.1).** *Set-up.* `N := Σ_x(W_x + 14)` is a sum of commuting on-site terms with integer spectrum `{0, 14, 16}`, so `H_ε = τN + H'` with `H' := −tΣhop_x + εH_4` local and small against `τ`. This is L17's setting `νN + H` with `ν = τ`. L17 gives a quasi-local unitary `Y` with `YH_εY* = τN + D̂ + V̂`, `[D̂, N] = 0` and `‖V̂‖_{κ_{n*}} ≤ (2/3)^{n*}‖H'‖_{κ_0}`, `n* = ⌊(ν/ν_0)/(1 + ln(ν/ν_0))³⌋ − 2`. *What works.* Each `S`-charged vector lies in `N ≥ 14` (Lemma 1 (d)). The terms of `D̂` commute with `N`; a term supported away from an excitation acts as it does on `N = 0`. So `τN + D̂` plausibly has the bound S-GAP asks for, with `D̂`'s local norms in place of `δ`. *Step that fails:* the return from `τN + D̂` to `H_ε`. `V̂` is a sum of `L` local terms, each quasi-exponentially small in `τ/‖H'‖` but not zero. Weyl's inequality moves eigenvalues by up to `‖V̂‖ ∝ L·(2/3)^{n*}`, which is not uniform in `L`. L17 states that the iteration cannot be continued: "we cannot proceed ad infinitum". The usual alternative, treating `V̂` as a small perturbation, needs a global gap of `τN + D̂`, and its `N = 0` block is the gapless light chain. The comparator note `S` meets the same obstacle in its Feshbach bound (its line 235: "the bound retains v's volume dependence").

**Attack 2: polymer expansion in imaginary time with the light chain as a free field.** *Set-up.* Expand `e^{−βH_ε}` around `τN − tΣhop_x`. Mirror atoms are local in space and decay in imaginary time at rate at least `14τ`. Vertices of `εH_4` create and destroy mirror pairs, so mirror excitations form space-time polymers. Integrating the free light field gives, for each vertex set, a Wick sum of products of the free light propagator `g(z)`, `z ∈ Z × R`. S-GAP would follow from exponential decay in imaginary time of `S`-charged correlations: the charge must be carried by a mirror line, at rate `14τ`. That decay follows if the expansion of `log Z` and of the `S`-charged correlations converges absolutely. *Step that fails:* absolute convergence. At half filling `|g(z)|` decays like `|z|^{−1}`. Two vacuum polymers far apart are linked through their light bilinears by `g(z)g(−z)`, of size `|z|^{−2}`, and `Σ_{z∈Z×R, |z|≥1}|z|^{−2}` diverges logarithmically in two dimensions. So no Kotecký–Preiss-type criterion holds for the light-linked polymers. The divergent sum is the marginal quartic of Proposition D (L9, Sec. III). The resummation it needs is a multiscale fermionic RG of the kind of L14 and L15, not rebuilt here.

**Attack 3: gapped-chain stability (L18, Theorem 3.5, the route of `SMG1.md` Theorem 5.2).** *Set-up.* Reduce to a chain of identical on-site matrices plus small nearest-neighbour terms, as in `SMG1.md` Lemma 5.1 (open segment, Jordan–Wigner, dummy link). *Step that fails:* L18's hypothesis, quoted at `SMG1_REFEREE.md` §2.5: "0 is an eigenvalue of H corresponding to an eigenvector Ω ∈ C^M, and `H|_{{CΩ}^⊥} ≥ 1`". It requires a one-dimensional on-site ground space. A term `τ₅n_x` that splits the light mode (as in `SMG1.md` Theorem 4.1) leaves the light chain empty, not half filled. Without it, the on-site kernel `ψ ⊗ {|0⟩, |1⟩}` is two-dimensional. Moreover, the theorem's conclusion, a unique ground state with a uniform gap, would contradict Corollary B1 for this one-site-invariant half-filled generator. So no reduction of this kind can exist: the attack fails at a hypothesis that the target itself rules out, not merely at a technicality. A block-diagonalization relative only to the mirror's gap, with the light mode as a spectator, is attack 1.

**Status.** Lemma S-GAP is open after these three attacks. All three fail at the same place: the light chain is gapless and its induced coupling is marginal. The tool the outline assigns (line 182: "a constructive 1D RG [...] after its theorems are read in full") is the natural next attack: integrate the mirror atoms at the first scale with the light field as an external Grassmann source, then run the light chain's RG. L16 is a rigorous precedent for two Ising species with different couplings joined by a quartic term; it was read at abstract level only, and its fermionic method was not checked. Neither tool is rebuilt here. The mirror-hop version (`θ ≠ 0`) contains S-GAP and also R1-H.
