## 0.1 Outcome in one paragraph

**Five classes are stated, with proofs of what each detects; none is proposed.** Four facts hold for every class. (A) *Diagonal gauge invariance* (Lemma A): for any block map satisfying (G1) alone, any embedding sets, on both laws, every multi-time correlation of block observables in a locally gauge-invariant vacuum is invariant under each coarse local gauge transformation applied to all its entries at once. So every datum carries exactly the information of its gauge-invariant projection, and R-GI is the two-point case. (B) Every gauge-invariant element is matter-even (Lemma B, rebuilt from `RCS1.md` Theorem 4), so gauge-invariant data never reach the odd sector. (C) *Twists* (Theorem C): appending the gauge-invariant layer `∏_x e^{iφQ_x}` (`L5-U`, every `φ`) or `∏_x (−1)^{N_x}` (`L5-O`, `φ = π`) to the tick leaves every gauge-invariant datum unchanged, and also every R-CS^str string sector, but multiplies each charge-`q` band by `e^{−iqφ}`. So no class detects the quasi-energy origin of charged sectors; slopes, hence speeds, are unaffected. (F) *Band-limited forms* (Theorem F): any band-limited comparison whose error vanishes as `κ → 0` forces exact equality of the zero-momentum spectral data at every tick where they converge absolutely. For local charged fields in R-GI vacua these are the on-site return amplitudes (the referee's consequence, recovered). For gauge-invariant composites they are summed autocorrelations, including on-site terms. The classes: **(a)** local gauge-invariant observables, compared at separation `r` with a relative error `r^{−p}` against the correlation envelope; **(b)** (a) plus large loops with perimeter, area or Creutz normalisation; **(c)** smeared gauge-invariant composites in the band-limited form, with a flow-like variant; **(d)** (a) plus R-CS^str string data; **(e)** the scaling-limit class: (a) uniformly along the tower, with limit states. Proved per class: speeds of `H_phys` bands, Clifford cones included, transfer between scales under band-limited dynamical data, to order `|θ|^{p+1}` (Lemmas S, S′); fundamental loops vanish identically in every state sharp in one link's end grading, which includes RCS1's `ω_AB` and the locked witnesses (Lemma W1); area normalisation is blind to perimeter-law differences, and Creutz ratios cancel perimeter and corner terms exactly (Lemma W2–W3); R-CS^str data between strings with different far ends vanish in every locally gauge-invariant state, so (d) sees charged motion only along the string axis (Theorem E). Four-test results: (a) P P P P; (b) P C P P with Creutz ratios (area: P C C P; perimeter: P C P C); (c) P C P C; (d) P C C C; (e) C P P P. **Ranking for ZM1-I1:** (a), then (c), (e), (d), (b). **No counterexample to any frozen result. Open by name:** ZM1-I1[O] for each class O, not attacked, since §3.6 says ZM1-I1 is not worked until the owner chooses.

### 2.2 Definition O: correlation data and comparison classes

**Definition O.** A *datum* is a finite tuple `d = ((B_1, s_1), …, (B_n, s_n))` with `B_i ∈ 𝔄_1` and `s_i ∈ Z` (coarse ticks). Its fine and coarse values are
\[
 F_0(d)=\omega_0\big(\alpha^{Ls_1}(\Phi(B_1))\cdots\alpha^{Ls_n}(\Phi(B_n))\big),\qquad
 F_1(d)=\omega_1\big(\alpha_1^{s_1}(B_1)\cdots\alpha_1^{s_n}(B_n)\big),
\]
and its discrepancy is `Δ(d) = |F_0(d) − F_1(d)|`. For fixed `n` and times, `F_i` is multilinear in `(B_1, …, B_n)`; it extends linearly to finite linear combinations of data of one shape. ### 2.3 What "detect" means here

Fix one algebra (fine or coarse) and a set `𝒫` of pairs `(ω, α)` of a state and an update. The `O`-data of a pair are its values `F(d)`, `d ∈ O`, computed with `Φ = id`. A function `Q` on `𝒫` is *determined by* `O` if two pairs with equal `O`-data have equal `Q`. `O` *cannot detect* `Q` on `𝒫` if two pairs in `𝒫` have equal `O`-data and different `Q`. If ZM1-I1[O] held with zero error, it would carry exactly the `O`-determined properties between the two scales. With a nonzero error it carries them approximately; Theorem F and Lemma S below make this quantitative for the band-limited form.

## 3. Facts that hold for every class

**Lemma 3.1 (Haar projection).** Let `𝔊` be a compact group with normalised Haar measure and `ρ` a continuous representation on a finite-dimensional space `V`. There is an inner product on `V` for which `ρ` is unitary, and `P = ∫ρ(g)dg` is then the orthogonal projection onto the invariant vectors `V^𝔊`.

*Proof.* For any inner product `⟨·,·⟩_0`, the average `⟨u,v⟩ = ∫⟨ρ(g)u, ρ(g)v⟩_0 dg` is an invariant inner product. Left invariance of the measure gives `ρ(h)P = P`, so `ran P ⊆ V^𝔊` and `P^2 = P`. Inversion invariance and unitarity give `P^* = ∫ρ(g^{-1})dg = P`. Invariant vectors are fixed by `P`. □

**Lemma 3.2 (homomorphisms).** A unital *-homomorphism `Φ` of unital C*-algebras is contractive, and `Φ(e^{iA}) = e^{iΦ(A)}` for self-adjoint `A`.

*Proof.* If `λ ∉ spec a` then `Φ(a − λ)` has inverse `Φ((a − λ)^{-1})`, so `spec Φ(a) ⊆ spec a`. For self-adjoint `a` the norm is the spectral radius, so `‖Φ(a)‖ ≤ ‖a‖`; in general `‖Φ(a)‖^2 = ‖Φ(a^*a)‖ ≤ ‖a‖^2`. The exponential series converges in norm, and a bounded multiplicative linear map passes through it. □

**Lemma A (diagonal gauge invariance).** Let `ω_0` be invariant under every fine local gauge unitary, let `α` commute with them, and let `Φ` be a unital *-homomorphism satisfying (G1) for some nonempty, pairwise disjoint, finite sets `S_X`. Fix a coarse site `X`, `T` and `θ`, and put `g = e^{iθG^{(1),T}_X}`. For every datum `d`, let `g·d` replace each `B_i` by `gB_ig^*`. Then `F_0(g·d) = F_0(d)`. The same holds for `F_1` if `ω_1` is invariant under coarse local gauge unitaries and `α_1` commutes with them.

*Proof.* By Lemma 3.2 and (G1), `u := Φ(g) = exp(iθ Σ_{x∈S_X} G^T_x)`. Gauss generators at distinct sites commute: they are even bilinears in disjoint sets of modes (matter at `x` and the `x`-ends of links), and links commute with matter. So `u = ∏_{x∈S_X} e^{iθG^T_x}`, a product of commuting fine gauge unitaries. Then `Φ(gB_ig^*) = uΦ(B_i)u^*`. The tick fixes each factor of `u`, so `α^{Ls}(uYu^*) = uα^{Ls}(Y)u^*` for every `Y`. Hence the product in `F_0(g·d)` is `u(∏_i α^{Ls_i}(Φ(B_i)))u^*`. `ω_0` is invariant under each factor of `u`. The coarse statement is the same computation with `Φ = id`. □

Lemma A needs neither (G2), nor `|S_X| = 1`, nor translation invariance.

**Corollary A1 (multiplet rule; R-GI generalised).** Let `V_X` be the continuous unitary representation of `𝔊^{(1)}_X` (`U(5)` or `Spin(10)`) by local unitaries of `𝔄_1`, with `V_X(e^{iθT}) = e^{iθG^{(1),T}_X}`. Let `W_1, …, W_n ⊂ 𝔄_1` be finite-dimensional subspaces invariant under `Ad V_X`, with bases `B_i^{a}` and representations `ρ_i`. For times `s_i` write `d(a) = ((B_1^{a_1}, s_1), …, (B_n^{a_n}, s_n))`. Under Lemma A's hypotheses,
\[

\]
If `W_1 ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ W_n` has no nonzero invariant vector, `F_0(d(a)) = 0` for all `a`. The same holds for `F_1`.

*Proof.* `𝔊^{(1)}_X` is connected and exponentials generate it (a connected Lie group is generated by any neighbourhood of the identity, and the exponential covers one). So Lemma A gives `F_0(V_X(g)·d) = F_0(d)` for all `g`. Expand `V_X(g)·d(a)` by multilinearity and integrate over `g` (Lemma 3.1, with invariant inner products on the `W_i`). If the tensor product has no invariant vector, `P = 0`. □

*R-GI as a case.* Take `n = 2`, `X ≠ Y`, `W_1 = span_j{ψ_{X,j}^*}` and `W_2 = span_k{ψ_{Y,k}}`. `𝔊^{(1)}_X` acts on `W_1` by the fundamental (`L5-U`) or vector (`L5-O`) representation, nontrivial and irreducible, and trivially on `W_2`. ### 4.1 Band-limited forms: zero-momentum rigidity

**Setting F.** For `i = 0, 1` let `H_i` be a Hilbert space with a unitary representation `T^{(i)}` of `Z^3`, a unitary `Ũ_i` commuting with it, and vectors `v_{i,1}, …, v_{i,d}`. **Lemma F0.** If `Σ_β‖C_i(β,0)‖ < ∞`, then `c_i(·,0)` is continuous with values in positive semidefinite matrices, and `f ↦ ξ_i(f) := Σ_{a,β} f_a(β)T^{(i)}_βv_{i,a}` extends from finitely supported `f` to a bounded map on `l^2(Z^3)^d` with `‖ξ_i(f)‖^2 ≤ sup_θ‖c_i(θ,0)‖·‖f‖^2`. If also `Σ_β‖C_i(β,t)‖ < ∞`, then `⟨ξ_i(f), Ũ_i^tξ_i(g)⟩ = (2π)^{−3}∫⟨f̂(θ), c_i(θ,t)ĝ(θ)⟩dθ`.

*Proof.* For finitely supported `f, g`, `⟨ξ(f), Ũ^tξ(g)⟩ = Σ \bar f_a(β)g_b(β')C(β'−β, t)_{ab}`; insert `C(γ,t) = (2π)^{−3}∫c(θ,t)e^{iθ·γ}dθ`. `C(−β,0) = C(β,0)^*`, so `c(θ,0)` is Hermitian. `‖ξ(f)‖^2 ≥ 0` for every trigonometric polynomial `f̂`; take `f̂ = u·p_N(θ − θ_0)` with `|p_N|^2` a product of Fejér kernels, an approximate identity, to get `⟨u, c(θ_0,0)u⟩ ≥ 0`. The bound follows, and both sides of the last formula are continuous in `f, g` on `l^2`. □

**Theorem F (zero-momentum rigidity).** In Setting F:
1. Fix `t`, and suppose `Σ_β‖C_i(β,0)‖` and `Σ_β‖C_i(β,t)‖` are finite for `i = 0,1`. Suppose `|⟨ξ_0(f),Ũ_0^tξ_0(g)⟩ − ⟨ξ_1(f),Ũ_1^tξ_1(g)⟩| ≤ ε(κ)‖f‖‖g‖` whenever `f̂, ĝ` are supported in `{|θ| ≤ κ}`, `κ ∈ (0, κ_0]`, with `ε(κ) → 0` as `κ → 0`. Then `c_0(0,t) = c_1(0,t)`, that is `Σ_β C_0(β,t) = Σ_β C_1(β,t)`.
2. Suppose `Σ_β(1 + |β|)^n‖C_i(β,0)‖ < ∞` for an integer `n ≥ 0`, and `|‖ξ_0(f)‖^2 − ‖ξ_1(f)‖^2| ≤ Cκ^p max_i‖ξ_i(f)‖^2` for `f` with Fourier support in `{|θ| ≤ κ}`, `κ ≤ κ_0`, with `p > n`. Then `Σ_β β^k C_0(β,0) = Σ_β β^k C_1(β,0)` for every multi-index `|k| ≤ n`.

A relative budget `C(κ^p + tκ^{p+1})·max_i‖ξ_i(f)‖·max_i‖ξ_i(g)‖` implies the hypothesis of (1), with `ε(κ) = C(κ^p + tκ^{p+1})max_i sup_θ‖c_i(θ,0)‖`.

*Proof.* (1) For unit `u, w ∈ C^d` and `δ ∈ (0, κ_0]`, let `f̂ = (2π)^{3/2}|B_δ|^{−1/2}1_{B_δ(0)}u` and `ĝ` the same with `w`; then `‖f‖ = ‖g‖ = 1`. By Lemma F0 the difference equals `|B_δ|^{−1}∫_{B_δ}⟨u,(c_0 − c_1)(θ,t)w⟩dθ`, which tends to `⟨u,(c_0−c_1)(0,t)w⟩` by continuity, and is at most `ε(δ) → 0`. (2) For `0 < |θ_0| < κ ≤ κ_0` and unit `u`, the same construction with balls `B_δ(θ_0) ⊂ {|θ| < κ}` gives `|⟨u,(c_0 − c_1)(θ_0,0)u⟩| ≤ Cκ^p max_i⟨u,c_i(θ_0,0)u⟩`. Let `κ ↓ |θ_0|`. The difference `D = c_0 − c_1` is Hermitian, so `‖D(θ)‖ ≤ C'|θ|^p` near `0`, and `D` is `C^n`. Along each ray, `D(sω) = Σ_{j≤n}(s^j/j!)(ω·∇)^jD(0) + o(s^n) = O(s^p)`, so the polynomial vanishes: `(ω·∇)^jD(0) = 0` for all `ω ∈ R^3` and `j ≤ n`. A homogeneous polynomial in `ω` that vanishes identically has zero coefficients, so `∂^kD(0) = 0` for `|k| ≤ n`. Finally `∂^kD(0) = Σ_β(−iβ)^k(C_0 − C_1)(β,0)`. □

**Corollary F1 (ZM1-I1 as stated reduces to on-site equality).** Let `ω_0, ω_1` be locally gauge invariant and invariant under coarse cell translations. Take `v_{i,(r,j)} = π_i(Φ_i(ψ_{0,r,j}))Ω_i` (coarse site `(0,r)`, flavour `j`; `Φ_1 = id`). By Corollary A1, `C_i(β,t)_{(r,j),(r',k)} = 0` unless `β = 0` and `r = r'`, so `c_i(θ,t) = C_i(0,t)` for every `θ`, and all sums converge. ZM1-I1's bound has `ε(κ) = C(κ^p + tκ^{p+1}) → 0`. Theorem F(1) gives `C_0(0,t) = C_1(0,t)` for every `t`: this is `ZM1.md` line 367, "`ω_0(Ψ_{X,j}^* α^{Lt}(Ψ_{X,k})) = ω_1(ψ_{X,j}^* α_1^t(ψ_{X,k}))` for every coarse site `X`, all flavours `j, k` and every `t ∈ N`". The referee's consequence is the `O_ψ` case of Theorem F.

**Corollary F2 (gauge-invariant composites).** For local `O_1, …, O_d ∈ 𝔄_1^G`, a band-limited comparison whose error vanishes as `κ → 0` forces `Σ_β ω_0^T(Φ(O_a)^*, α^{Lt}(Φ(τ^{(1)}_βO_b))) = Σ_β ω_1^T(O_a^*, α_1^t(τ^{(1)}_βO_b))` exactly, at every `t` at which both sums converge absolutely. In the static relative form with summable moments it forces the moments of order `≤ n < p` to agree. These sums include `β = 0` and every short separation.

### 4.2 Band-limited dynamical data transfer speeds

**Lemma S (speed transfer).** In Setting F with `d = 1` and `Σ_β|C_i(β,0)| < ∞`, suppose that near `θ = 0` each side is one band: there are `κ_0 > 0` and continuous `w_i ≥ 0`, `λ_i : B_{κ_0} → (−π, π]` with `λ_i(0) = 0`, `w_1(0) > 0`, and `⟨ξ_i(f),Ũ_i^tξ_i(g)⟩ = (2π)^{−3}∫\bar{f̂}ĝ\,w_ie^{−iλ_it}dθ` for all `t ∈ N` and all `f, g` with Fourier support in `B_{κ_0}`. Suppose the relative budget `|⟨ξ_0(f),Ũ_0^tξ_0(g)⟩ − ⟨ξ_1(f),Ũ_1^tξ_1(g)⟩| ≤ C(κ^p + tκ^{p+1})N(f)N(g)`, `N(f) = max_i‖ξ_i(f)‖`, for Fourier support in `{|θ| ≤ κ}`, `κ ≤ κ_0`. Then `|λ_0(θ) − λ_1(θ)| ≤ C''|θ|^{p+1}` for small `θ`. If `λ_i(sn̂) = v_i(n̂)s + o(s)` along a direction `n̂`, then `v_0(n̂) = v_1(n̂)`.

*Proof.* Fix `θ_0` with `s = |θ_0|` small and `κ ∈ (s, κ_0]`. Normalised indicator profiles on balls `B_δ(θ_0)`, `δ → 0`, as in Theorem F, give `‖ξ_i(f_δ)‖^2 → w_i(θ_0)` and, after `κ ↓ s`,
\[
 |w_0e^{-i\lambda_0t}-w_1e^{-i\lambda_1t}|\le C(s^p+ts^{p+1})\max_iw_i \quad (\text{at }\theta_0,\ \text{all } t\in\mathbb N).
\]
At `t = 0` this gives `|w_0 − w_1| ≤ Cs^p max_i w_i`, so `max_iw_i ≤ 2w_1(θ_0)` once `Cs^p ≤ 1/2`. For small `s`, also `w_1(0)/2 ≤ w_1(θ_0) ≤ 3w_1(0)/2`. Put `δλ = λ_0(θ_0) − λ_1(θ_0)`; by continuity `|δλ| ≤ π/2` for small `s`. Since `w_1|e^{−iδλt} − 1| ≤ |w_0e^{−iλ_0t} − w_1e^{−iλ_1t}| + |w_0 − w_1| ≤ 2C(s^p + ts^{p+1})max_iw_i`, we get `|e^{−iδλt} − 1| ≤ C_1(s^p + ts^{p+1})` with `C_1 = 12C`. If `δλ ≠ 0`, let `t_* = ⌈π/(2|δλ|)⌉`. Then `|δλ|t_* ∈ [π/2, π]`, so `|e^{−iδλt_*} − 1| ≥ √2`. Hence `√2 ≤ C_1(s^p + t_*s^{p+1})`. For `s` so small that `C_1s^p ≤ 1/2`, this forces `t_*s^{p+1} ≥ 1/(2C_1)`, so `π/(2|δλ|) + 1 ≥ 1/(2C_1s^{p+1})`, and `|δλ| ≤ 2πC_1s^{p+1}` once `s^{p+1} ≤ 1/(4C_1)`. Divide by `s` and let `s → 0` along `n̂`. □

**Lemma S′ (several branches; cones included).** In Setting F with `Σ_β‖C_i(β,0)‖ < ∞`, suppose that for all `t ∈ N` and all `f, g` with Fourier support in `B_{κ_0}`, `⟨ξ_i(f),Ũ_i^tξ_i(g)⟩ = (2π)^{−3}∫⟨f̂(θ), c_i(θ,t)ĝ(θ)⟩dθ` with
\[
 c_i(\theta,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{n_i}W_{i,k}(\theta)\,e^{-i\lambda_{i,k}(\theta)t}\qquad(0<|\theta|<\kappa_0),
\]
continuous `W_{i,k} ≥ 0` and continuous real `λ_{i,k}`, `λ_{i,k}(0) = 0`, branch gaps `|λ_{i,k}(θ) − λ_{i,k'}(θ)| ≥ g|θ|` for `k ≠ k'` (`g > 0`), and weights `‖W_{i,k}(θ)‖ ≥ w > 0`. Suppose the absolute budget `|⟨ξ_0(f),Ũ_0^tξ_0(g)⟩ − ⟨ξ_1(f),Ũ_1^tξ_1(g)⟩| ≤ C(κ^p + tκ^{p+1})‖f‖‖g‖` for Fourier support in `{|θ| ≤ κ}`. Then for small `|θ|` every branch of either side lies within `C''|θ|^{p+1}` of a branch of the other. Along each ray, the sets of branch speeds `{v_{0,k}(n̂)}` and `{v_{1,k}(n̂)}` coincide, where they exist. A Clifford cone `±v|θ|` has `g = 2v`.

*Proof.* As in Theorem F(1), at `θ_0` with `|θ_0| = s` one gets `‖c_0(θ_0,t) − c_1(θ_0,t)‖ ≤ C(s^p + ts^{p+1})` for all `t`. Put `D_T(x) = T^{−1}Σ_{t<T}e^{ixt}`; then `D_T(0) = 1` and `|D_T(x)| ≤ π/(T|x|)` for `0 < |x| ≤ π`. Fix a branch `k` of side 0 and put `λ = λ_{0,k}(θ_0)`, `A_T = T^{−1}Σ_{t<T}e^{iλt}c_0(θ_0,t)`, and `B_T` the same with `c_1`. By the gap, `‖A_T − W_{0,k}‖ ≤ Σ_{k'}‖W_{0,k'}‖π/(Tgs)`. If `δ` is the distance from `λ` to the nearest branch of side 1, `‖B_T‖ ≤ Σ_{k'}‖W_{1,k'}‖π/(Tδ)`. Averaging the pointwise bound gives `‖A_T − B_T‖ ≤ C(s^p + Ts^{p+1})`. Let `S = max_i sup Σ_k‖W_{i,k}‖` and take `T = ⌈ηs^{−(p+1)}⌉` with `η = w/(4C)`. Then `w ≤ ‖W_{0,k}‖ ≤ C s^p + w/4 + Cs^{p+1} + πSs^p/(ηg) + πS/(Tδ)`. For small `s` the first, third and fourth terms sum to at most `w/4`, so `πS/(Tδ) ≥ w/2` and `δ ≤ 2πS/(Tw) ≤ (2πS/(ηw))s^{p+1}`. The same holds with the sides exchanged. Along a ray, the side-1 branches are `gs` apart and the nearest one is within `O(s^{p+1}) < gs/2`, so it is unique; by continuity it does not change with `s`. Divide by `s` and let `s → 0`. □

Lemmas S and S′ are the zoom's input to LZ1-i that this lane can prove: a class with band-limited dynamical data for a species carries its branches between scales up to `O(|θ|^{p+1})`, hence its speeds, Clifford cones included, provided the data's weights on each branch stay bounded below near the node.

### 4.3 Loops

**Lemma W1 (sharp gradings annihilate charged loops; both laws).** Let `E` be self-adjoint with finite spectrum, `ω` a state with `ω((E − n)^2) = 0`, and `Y` with `[E,Y] = qY`, `q ≠ 0`. Then `ω(Y) = 0`. The same holds if `E^2 = 1`, `ω((E − s)^2) = 0` with `s = ±1`, and `EY = −YE`.

*Proof.* Let `P` be the spectral projection of `E` at `n`. `(E − n)^2 ≥ c(1 − P)` with `c > 0` the least squared gap, so `ω(1 − P) = 0`. Cauchy–Schwarz gives `ω((1−P)Y) = ω(Y(1−P)) = 0`, so `ω(Y) = ω(PYP)`. `Y` maps the `n`-eigenspace into the `(n+q)`-eigenspace, so `PYP = 0`. For the second form, `Y` maps the `s`-eigenspace of `E` into the `−s`-eigenspace. □

*Applications.* On `L5-U` take `E = N^x_ℓ`, the end number of a link `ℓ`. A closed loop `W_C = tr(𝐔_{ℓ_1}⋯𝐔_{ℓ_n})` that traverses `ℓ` once satisfies `[N^x_ℓ, W_C] = ±W_C`, since `U^{jk}_ℓ = r^y_jr^{x†}_k` moves one rishon between the ends. In `RCS1.md`'s `ω_AB` (Theorem 8, line 96) and in the locked states of Theorem 7 (line 92), every end number is sharp: the product states have definite end occupations, and `E` preserves end numbers, which are gauge invariant. So **every closed fundamental loop has zero expectation in these D1 states**, at every size. Only loops that traverse each link equally often in both directions (U(1)-blind products, `GA2_Y2.md` §4) can be nonzero there. On `L5-O` take `E = Γ^x_ℓ`; `U^{ab}_ℓ = iη^y_aη^x_b` anticommutes with it, so loops that traverse `ℓ` an odd number of times vanish in states sharp in `Γ^x_ℓ`. No `L5-O` D1 state with this property is claimed here.

**Lemma W2 (area normalisation cannot see perimeter-law differences).** Let `C_{R,T}` be `R × T` rectangles, `w_i = −log|F_i(W_C)|`. If `w_i = 2μ_i(R + T) + c_i + o(R + T)` for `i = 0, 1`, then `|w_0 − w_1|/(RT) → 0` as `min(R,T) → ∞`, for any `μ_0, μ_1`. If `w_i = σ_iRT + O(R+T)`, the area-normalised difference tends to `|σ_0 − σ_1|`.

*Proof.* `(R + T)/(RT) ≤ 2/min(R,T)`. □

**Lemma W3 (Creutz ratios).** Put `χ(R,T) = w(R,T) + w(R−1,T−1) − w(R,T−1) − w(R−1,T)`, the logarithm of the inverse Creutz ratio. Then `χ` vanishes on every function of the form `f(R) + g(T)`, and `χ = σ` for `w = σRT + 2μ(R+T) + c`.

*Proof.* `χ` is the mixed second difference `Δ_RΔ_Tw`. It kills `f(R) + g(T)`, and `Δ_RΔ_T(RT) = 1`. □

*Remark (Theorem N at every scale).* `GA2_Y2.md` line 30, Theorem N: "If the operator link in Definition 1 is invertible and obeys either covariance equation, then \(\tau=0\)." Block maps do not escape it. A unital injective *-homomorphism `Φ` extends entrywise to an injective *-homomorphism of matrix algebras, and spectra are unchanged under injective *-homomorphisms of unital C*-algebras (standard spectral permanence). So `Φ(𝐔^{(1)})` is invertible only if `𝐔^{(1)}` is, and `𝐔^{(1)}` is not: `GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 2.5 bounds its rank on `L5-U[m]` by `C(10, m+1)`, which is less than the dimension `5·C(10, m)` for every `m ≥ 1`, and on the `L5-O` half-spinor by 512, against dimension 5120. (Rebuilt: on `L5-U`, `𝐔 = R_yR_x^*`, where `R_x^* : e_k ⊗ v ↦ r^{x†}_kv` maps into the `(m+1)`-rishon sector and `R_y : v ↦ Σ_je_j ⊗ r^y_jv` maps back; on `L5-O`, `𝐔 = iAB` with `B(e_b ⊗ v) = η^x_bv` mapping into the other half-spinor, of dimension 512.) Also, on `L5-U`, `(U^{(1)jk})^2 = r^y_jr^{x†}_kr^y_jr^{x†}_k = 0` because `(r^y_j)^2 = 0` (`GA2_Y2.md` Theorem 3.4), so `Φ(U^{(1)jk})^2 = 0`. Block loops are quantum-link loops at every scale, never Wilson loops. Passing loop data to T0 therefore needs an identification with constants, as in `GA2_Y2.md` §1 items 4–5.

### 4.4 Strings

**Theorem E (string data see only motion along the string).** Both laws. Let `ω` be locally gauge invariant and let the update commute with local gauge unitaries. Use `RCS1.md` §5 (line 81): `B_{r,b}(x) = Σ_aψ^†_{x,a}(T_1⋯T_{2r})_{ab}` along an axis `e`, with far end `y = x + 2re` and free index `b` there. Let `x'`, `r'` give a second string with far end `y'`. If `y ≠ y'`, then every product that contains `α^{s}(B_{r,b}(x))^*` and `α^{s'}(B_{r',b'}(x'))` once each, together with any evolved elements of `𝔄^G` in any order, has `ω`-expectation zero, for all `b, b'` and all ticks. If `y = y'`, the value is `δ_{bb'}` times the `b`-averaged value.

*Proof.* Apply Corollary A1 at the site `y`, with `Φ = id` on the law itself. Under `𝔊_y`, `B_{r,b}(x)` transforms through its index `b` alone. On `L5-U` its factor `(T_{2r})_{ab} = r^{ℓ,y_{2r−1}}_ar^{ℓ,y*}_b` contains a rishon creator at the `y`-end, which transforms in the fundamental representation. On `L5-O` the factor contains `η^y_b`, a vector. All contracted indices are invariant: at `x`, `Σ_aψ^†_{x,a}r^{ℓ_1,x}_a` (or its Majorana form) is invariant; at interior sites `Σ_cr^{ℓ_i,y_i*}_cr^{ℓ_{i+1},y_i}_c` is invariant. The second string is invariant under `𝔊_y`, because `y` is not its far end: `y` is off its path, or an interior site, or its starting point. Elements of `𝔄^G` are invariant, and evolution commutes with `Ad V_y`. So the entries span a tensor product of one nontrivial irreducible representation with trivial ones, which has no invariant vector. If `y = y'`, the only invariant in the product of the conjugate fundamental and the fundamental (on `L5-O`, of two vectors) is the contraction, which gives `δ_{bb'}`. □

So in every locally gauge-invariant state, finite R-CS^str(e) data carry charged-propagation amplitudes only between starting points on one line parallel to `e`. Transverse amplitudes vanish at every string length. This is a data-level companion of `RCS1.md` Corollary 9 (line 120), which assumes disjointness of transversely displaced sectors in the limit.

## 5. The five classes

By Corollary A2, in VC1 vacua every class carries exactly the information of its gauge-invariant projection. So all five classes below are gauge-invariant classes. They differ in the size of their observables and in their budgets. For each class: definition; what it detects on both laws; budget; ZM1-I1 under it; what it supplies; four tests. P means passed, C conditional, F failed.

### 5.1 Class (a): local gauge-invariant observables, separation form

**Definition (a).** For `R_0 ≥ 0`, let `𝔄_1^G(R_0)` be the elements of `𝔄_1^G` of norm at most 1 whose matter and links lie in the coarse sup-norm ball of radius `R_0` about coarse cell `0`; this set is closed under the coarse cell rotations about that cell's centre. The class `O_a` consists of the two-point data `d_β = ((A^*,0),(τ^{(1)}_βB,t))` with `A, B ∈ 𝔄_1^G(R_0)`, `β ∈ Z^3`, `t ∈ N`, for every `R_0`, and their images under the coarse cell group. Its budget is the *separation-envelope* form on truncated values:
\[
 |F^T_0(d_\beta)-F^T_1(d_\beta)|\le C_{R_0}\,(r^{-p}+t\,r^{-p-1})\,M(r,t),\qquad r=|\beta|_\infty\ge1,\quad M(r,t)=\sup_{|\beta'|_\infty\ge r}\ \max_{i=0,1}|F^T_i(d_{\beta'})| .
\]
One-point values have no small parameter and enter only through truncation. Entries include, on `L5-U`, `Q_X`, `N_X`, the hop `h^U_ℓ` (a meson on one link), plaquette loops, the U(1)-blind cube products of `GA2_Y2.md` §4 and functions of end numbers; on `L5-O`, `P_X`, the Majorana hop, the bilinears `G^{ab}_X G^{ab}_X` summed, and loops.

**What (a) detects (both laws).**
- *Determined.* By Lemma D(2) its data, over all `R_0`, determine the representation of tick and translations on `H_phys ⊖ CΩ`, hence every band with image in `H_phys`; all its data are matrix elements inside `H_phys`. So ZM1-I1[a] compares the long-distance tails of the physical sectors: decay rates (correlation lengths `ξ_0/L` against `ξ_1` in coarse units), power laws and their amplitudes.
- *Not detected.* Nothing in `H_phys^⊥`: the odd sector (Lemma B(4)) and the static Gauss background of `LZ1.md` RC1(ii) (Lemma D). By Theorem C the class cannot tell `U_0` from `K_φU_0`, so it cannot fix the quasi-energy origin of any charged sector. Local charged two-point data are not in the class; by Corollaries A1 and F1 they would add only on-site content. Invariant multi-point tensors of local charged fields, such as `ψ^*_X ⊗ ψ_X ⊗ ψ^*_Y ⊗ ψ_Y` at four times, are not in the class either; in a D1 state the excitations they create stay in their centre patterns (`LZ1.md` Theorem 2.1, line 39: "The tick preserves each pattern").
- *R-GI and Theorem N.* The class has no charged entries, so R-GI removes nothing from it. No entry needs a group-valued link, so Theorem N does not obstruct it.
- *Free tick.* With links replaced by c-numbers, the separation form for number-conserving composites is the residual GAP Z2-spatial (`ZM1.md` line 268: "physical separation `r` alone does not imply that support"). So (a) asks, even at the free tick, for something not yet proved.

**Budget.** Relative, against the envelope, with rate `r^{−p}`, `p ≥ 1`. Lemma V excludes an absolute form.

**ZM1-I1[a].** Let `ω_0` be a VC1 vacuum for `L5-U[m]`. There exist `L ≥ 2`, `p ≥ 1`, a block map `Φ` (Definition Z; (Z-a), (Z-b), (G1)–(G2), `|S_X| = 1`), a coarse law `α_1` of the `L5-U` form, an `α_1`-invariant state `ω_1` and constants `C_{R_0}` such that the separation-envelope bound holds for all `A, B ∈ 𝔄_1^G(R_0)`, all `β` with `|β|_∞ ≥ 1` and all `t ∈ N`. By Corollary A2(3), `ω_1` may be taken locally gauge invariant. By Theorem C(4), the statement is the same for `U_0` and `K_φU_0`.

**Supplies.**
- *LZ1-i.* The long-distance data of every species with image in `H_phys`: glueball-, meson- and photon-like bands. Not the odd species. Speed transfer is proved here only for band-limited data (Lemma S), not for the envelope form.
- *GA2.* `TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` line 562: "Y2 can hold only as universality of gauge-invariant correlators". Class (a) is that class at operator level. SC's lists (`GA2_Y2.md` §1 item 4: plaquettes and cube products) lie in `𝔄_1^G(1)`. ZM1-G (`GA2_Y2.md` line 169: "a specified block map and controlled gauge-invariant correlator comparison to a reflection-positive group-valued measure") then uses the same class, so the two comparisons compose: fine law to coarse law, coarse law to measure.
- *T0-start.* T0 concerns "the tr(F^2) sector of Wilson SU(2)" (`TOE_ASSEMBLY.md` line 550), whose OS functions are correlations of local gauge-invariant fields at separated points. The envelope form is their operator analogue. T0-rate would be stated for this class.

**Four tests.**

| Test | Result | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 target | P | It names data and a budget. It asserts no existence and fixes no `p`. |
| 2 open-ended | P | The set is closed: every gauge-invariant element of support `≤ R_0`, for every `R_0`, with a budget fixed by the data. |

## 6. Ranking

The criteria, in order. (i) Content in VC1 vacua: by Lemma A only the gauge-invariant projection counts, and all five classes are gauge invariant. (ii) Well-posedness on finite quantum links: Theorem N and Lemma W1. (iii) The four tests. (iv) Fit to ZM1-I1 as one interacting step. (v) What it supplies downstream.

1. **(a)** is first. It is the only class with P P P P. Its data determine the physical sector and nothing outside it (Lemma D). Its costs: even its free analogue is open (GAP Z2-spatial), and like every gauge-invariant class it cannot fix a charged quasi-energy origin (Theorem C).
2. **(c)** is second. It is closest to ZM1-I1's present band-limited form, and it carries speeds between scales for even species (Lemmas S, S′), the input LZ1-i's route A3 needs. Its budget asks for more than long-distance agreement: Theorem F forces exact equality of short-distance sums (Corollary F2). Tests: P C P C.
3. **(e)** is third. It has the most content and a route to charged structure that needs no choice of R-CS (item 14). But it is a tower statement, ZM1-i rather than ZM1-I1 (test 1 C).
4. **(d)** is fourth. It is the only class with charged-propagation data, but it sees motion only along its axis (Theorem E), adds nothing in the locked D1 states (`RCS1.md` Theorem 7), inherits the open owner choice R-CS (tests 2–4 C), and cannot fix the charged quasi-energy origin (Theorem C(3)).
5. **(b)** is fifth. Fundamental loops vanish identically in sharp-flux D1 states (Lemma W1), so the class needs a zero rule and U(1)-blind loops on `L5-U`. Area normalisation hides perimeter-law physics (Lemma W2); perimeter normalisation compares a UV-sensitive constant; Creutz ratios avoid both (Lemma W3). The classes nest: (a) ⊂ (b), (a) ⊂ (d), and (e) is (a) along the tower; (c) holds (a)-type observables to a different budget. A choice can combine them; that choice is the owner's.
