The chain proof makes C positive and strong for its endpoint B, independently of H and target accuracy. For arbitrary computable finite Hermitian H, it establishes that the complex Hilbert-Schmidt Gram determinant

    p(s)=det Gram(ad_(H+sC)^k B, k=1,...,m),
    d>=2, m=d(d-1), D=m(m+1),

is a real nonnegative polynomial of exact degree D with leading coefficient a=p_C>0, computable from C,B. The existing D+1-node strict search terminates. The missing quantitative obligation proposed here is an explicit positive threshold, matrix-data precision budget and resulting bounds on the selected point's spectral separations and all port transitions, uniform over H with a supplied norm bound. No efficient complexity in d is proposed.

Choose a positive rational radius Lambda<=Lambda0, h=Lambda/(D+2), and nodes s_j=(j+1)h for j=0,...,D. Finite differences suggest

    sum_j (-1)^(D-j) binomial(D,j) p(s_j) = a D! h^D.

Taking absolute values gives max_j p(s_j)>=L, where L=a D! h^D/2^D>0. With certified absolute evaluation error <=L/4 at every node, selecting the largest real approximation gives an actual value >=L/2. Alternatively strict certified threshold tests can terminate with threshold L/2. This would replace an unknown positivity margin by an explicit one. The smaller denominator available from nonnegativity is not needed and is not claimed here.

For a selected strong H_s, let omega_r run over all ordered nonzero eigenvalue gaps and let b_r be the corresponding complex B entries, r=1,...,m. The spectral-basis Krylov matrix has columns b_r omega_r^k. Its determinant gives

    p(s)=product_r |b_r|^2 * product_r |omega_r|^2
         * product_(r<t) |omega_t-omega_r|^2.

This identity needs to be checked for the complex adjoint convention and exact rank claim. Suppose K>=1 is a certified common bound on ||H+sC|| and b>=1 bounds ||B|| (operator norm; a Hilbert-Schmidt bound also suffices). Then |b_r|<=b, |omega_r|<=2K and |omega_t-omega_r|<=4K. With q=m(m-1)/2 and p(s)>=L/2, the conservative individual-factor estimates are proposed:

    min |b_r| >= sqrt(L/2) / [b^(m-1) (2K)^m (4K)^q],
    min |omega_r| >= sqrt(L/2) / [b^m (2K)^(m-1) (4K)^q],
    min_(r<t) |omega_t-omega_r| >= sqrt(L/2) / [b^m (2K)^m (4K)^(q-1)].

Repeated conjugate/sign factors may permit sharper bounds, but are not needed for these sufficient inequalities. Positive-gap separation follows from signed-gap separation. A fixed lower bound on ||B|| is not assumed; b is merely an upper bound enlarged to at least one. Literal scalar parts can stay in K without changing the gaps.

An independent proof of a usable absolute evaluation budget is needed. One possible route is a deterministic operator perturbation bound on the Krylov columns using the telescoping identity for powers of ad_H, then a Gram/determinant Lipschitz bound. The input H and C can be supplied by certified operator approximations; computational cost of producing these approximations must not be inferred from computability alone. For fixed dimension, fixed C,B, Lambda0 and norm bounds, a bound polynomial in Lambda^-1 should mean logarithmically many requested input bits, not a polynomial wall-clock oracle theorem. The degree D and constants can be very large.

Discriminators: a genuinely noncommuting H/C example, all D+1 interior nodes, omission of the complex adjoint, a lost leading coefficient or wrong grid-scale exponent, and an actual min-gap/transition comparison.
