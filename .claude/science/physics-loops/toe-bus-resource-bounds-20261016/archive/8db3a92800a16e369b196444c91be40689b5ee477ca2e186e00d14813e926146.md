## A rational matrix-input precision bound

The accepted chain constructs rational Hermitian C and rational Hermitian endpoint B. Only the complete H input needs approximation here. Fix dimension d, m=d(d-1), D=m(m+1), Lambda0, a certified K>=1 with ||H+sC||<=K throughout 0<=s<=Lambda0, and rational beta>=max(1,||B||_HS). Keep literal scalar terms in the physical generator; their absence from commutators is not permission to subtract them physically.

Let Htilde be a Hermitian rational approximation with ||H-Htilde||<=eta<=1. Put R=2(K+1), U=beta*m*R^m and A=2*beta*m^2*R^(m-1). Both ad_(H+sC) and ad_(Htilde+sC) have operator norm at most R on Hilbert-Schmidt space, and their difference has norm at most 2 eta. The power telescoping identity gives, for each k=1,...,m,

    ||ad_(H+sC)^k B - ad_(Htilde+sC)^k B||_HS
        <=2 k beta eta R^(k-1).

If V and Vtilde are the d^2-by-m matrices of vectorized columns, then

    ||V||_2,||Vtilde||_2 <= U,
    ||V-Vtilde||_2 <= A eta.

These follow from the Frobenius column bounds and sqrt(m)<=m. The complex Grams G=V^*V and Gtilde=Vtilde^*Vtilde obey

    ||G||_2,||Gtilde||_2 <= U^2,
    ||G-Gtilde||_2 <=2 U A eta.

Telescope the determinant by replacing its m columns one at a time. Each difference term has one column of norm at most ||G-Gtilde|| and m-1 columns of norm at most U^2. Hadamard's bound therefore gives

    |det G-det Gtilde| <= C_det eta,
    C_det=2 m A U^(2m-1).

No sign, eigenvalue nondegeneracy, determinant positivity margin or invertibility is needed for this Lipschitz bound. It applies at the bad nodes too. Once the finite-difference proposal supplies L>0, eta<=min(1,L/(4 C_det)) yields the required L/4 absolute polynomial evaluation bound simultaneously at all nodes. C,B,s_j and Htilde are rational (with exact Gaussian rational entries where necessary), so the determinant at Htilde can be evaluated exactly; this statement does not promise economical exact arithmetic.

Entrywise complex errors at most eta/d suffice for an operator-norm error eta. Hermitian symmetrization does not increase that norm error when H is Hermitian. For fixed d,C,B,Lambda0,K and a=p_C>0, the requested entry accuracy has order Lambda^D. The number of requested accuracy bits is O(1+log(1/Lambda)); that is not a time bound for arbitrary computable matrix names, nor a bound in d. The encoding length of an arbitrary rational Lambda is also separate; a dyadic radius family avoids hiding large rational denominators behind that accuracy-bit phrase.

## Spectral product cross-check

At a simple-spectrum selected point, vectorize in its eigenbasis. Columns k>=1 have no diagonal components, so the only nonzero rows are the m ordered off-diagonal entries. They are B_ab*(E_a-E_b)^k. The square matrix determinant is the product of all B_ab, all signed gaps, and the ordinary signed-gap Vandermonde. Taking its squared absolute value gives the proposed complex Gram product, with no missing d-dimensional diagonal block. Positive Gram determinant forces rank m; a repeated eigenvalue, repeated ordered gap or zero off-diagonal B entry lowers this rank. Thus the product may be used at a certified positive node without first assuming the very regularity it will certify.

Bounding every factor except one by b,2K,4K gives each of the proposal's three conservative lower bounds. Correlated conjugate factors occur multiple times; bounding those other copies above remains valid, even though exploiting them would improve the exponents. Since m>=2, q=m(m-1)/2>=1 and the exponent q-1 is nonnegative. An actual positive transition bound does not certify diagonal B entries, which are not part of this Gram.

## A separate two-level reduction

For H_s=h0(s)I+h(s).sigma and B=b0I+b.sigma with real h,b, let r=||h||>0. The two signed gaps are +/-2r, and the off-diagonal squared modulus is ||h cross b||^2/r^2. Therefore for m=2 the complex Gram determinant is

    p(s)=256 ||h(s)||^2 ||h(s) cross b||^4.

This is an independent Pauli/spectral derivation of the small-matrix formula, not a new execution. For C=I+Z, B=I+X+Z and H=Y+2Z it predicts

    p(s)=256(s^2+4s+5)(s^2+4s+6)^2.
