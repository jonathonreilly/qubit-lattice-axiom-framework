## 2. Reused interpolation and one finite strict selection rule

Define, in the complex Hilbert-Schmidt space,

    F_k(s)=ad_(H+sC)^k(B),       k=1,...,m,
    p(s)=det[Tr(F_k(s)^*F_l(s))]_(k,l=1)^m.           (2)

For real s this is a real nonnegative polynomial. The qualified chain proof gives its exact degree D and leading coefficient a: the leading term of F_k is `s^k ad_C^k(B)`, and no mixed word involving H has that degree. Hence the coefficient at D of the Gram determinant is exactly `p_C>0`. Complex noncommuting H does not change this conclusion.

For rational `0<Lambda<=min{1,Lambda_0}`, use exactly the canonical upper-half nodes

    x_j=1/2+j/[2(D+1)],
    s_j=Lambda x_j,        j=0,...,D.                   (3)

The leading-coefficient interpolation weights are

    w_j=1/product_(l!=j)(x_j-x_l)
       =(-1)^(D-j)[2(D+1)]^D/[j!(D-j)!],
    A_D=sum_j |w_j|=[4(D+1)]^D/D!.                   (4)

Thus exact coefficient extraction gives `a Lambda^D=sum_j w_j p(s_j)` and

    max_j p(s_j) >= L,
    L=a_0 Lambda^D/A_D>0.                            (5)

Nonnegativity permits dropping absolute values on the p's. The bound does not assume leading-term dominance near zero and is valid despite arbitrary lower-order cancellation. Equivalently the Dth finite difference on this grid has step `Lambda/[2(D+1)]`, so its value is `a D! [Lambda/(2(D+1))]^D`. The different step `Lambda/(D+2)` from the root proposal is not used here.

Obtain real rational approximations with

    |p_hat_j-p(s_j)|<=L/8.

Accept any node satisfying the exact rational inequality

    p_hat_j-L/8 > L/2.                              (6)

At a witness with p at least L, the left side is at least `3L/4`, so a node passes after this one fixed evaluation precision. No arbitrary exact-zero or equality-to-threshold decision is required. At an accepted node s,

    Lambda/2<=s<Lambda,     p(s)>L/2.                (7)

This is the existing compiler's strict interpolation rule specialized to (2). Section 5 supplies the promised concrete matrix-data precision for its evaluations.

## 3. Exact factorization, including zero and repeated cases

Let `G=H+sC` be any Hermitian matrix and choose an orthonormal eigenbasis, with energies `E_a`; no simplicity is assumed initially. List **all** ordered pairs `(a,b)`, `a!=b`, so the list always has m entries. Write

    omega_r=E_a-E_b,       b_r=<a|B|b>.

At this stage some omega's may be zero or repeated. Calling them all “nonzero gaps” before proving positivity would incorrectly hide those cases. In the matrix-unit orthonormal basis of the Hilbert-Schmidt space,

    (F_k)_ab=omega_r^k b_r,
    (F_k)_aa=0.                                      (8)

The full column matrix Q has size `d^2 by m`. Its d diagonal rows vanish; the remaining square matrix T has entries `T_(r,k)=b_r omega_r^k`, `k=1,...,m`. Therefore

    Q^*Q=T^*T,
    det(Q^*Q)=|det T|^2,
    det T=(product_r b_r omega_r)
                product_(r<t)(omega_t-omega_r),       (9)

up to a harmless ordering sign. The usual Vandermonde polynomial identity holds also at coincident or zero nodes. Hence, exactly,

    p(s)=product_r |b_r|^2
          *product_r |omega_r|^2
          *product_(r<t)|omega_t-omega_r|^2.          (10)

No extra `d`, factorial, realification factor, or square root of a Gram determinant is missing. The complex adjoint is essential: replacing it by a bilinear product need not give a nonnegative determinant. The eigenbasis transformation is unitary on the complex Hilbert-Schmidt space, so it preserves the Gram without a Jacobian factor.

If an energy repeats, an off-diagonal row in that eigenspace has omega zero and (10) vanishes. If two signed differences repeat, the Vandermonde vanishes. If a B transition vanishes at a simple-gap point, a row prefactor vanishes. Conversely all factors nonzero make the Gram positive. Thus the selected margin (7) establishes simplicity, distinct signed nonzero gaps and every off-diagonal B entry nonzero. Distinct signed nonzero gaps are equivalent to distinct positive gaps when energies are simple. The relation `b_ba=conj(b_ab)` and opposite signs of the paired gaps causes no complex-rank deficiency. Repeated factors can sharpen bounds but do not invalidate (10).

## 4. Individual spectral and port lower bounds

At a selected node, (1) gives

    |b_r|<=b,   |omega_r|<=2K,
    |omega_t-omega_r|<=4K.                         (11)

Take the square root of (10). Isolate any chosen single factor and bound every other factor by (11). Since `sqrt(p)>sqrt(L/2)`, the following conservative non-strict lower bounds hold:

    min_r |b_r| >= beta_* :=
       sqrt(L/2)/[b^(m-1)(2K)^m(4K)^q],

    min_r |omega_r| >= gamma_* :=
       sqrt(L/2)/[b^m(2K)^(m-1)(4K)^q],

    min_(r<t)|omega_t-omega_r| >= sigma_* :=
       sqrt(L/2)/[b^m(2K)^m(4K)^(q-1)].           (12)

For d>=2, m>=2 and q>=1, so the last exponent is never negative. The root proposal's individual-factor estimates are therefore correct. No positive lower bound on `||B||` is assumed: b is only an upper bound enlarged to at least one. Its off-diagonal nonvanishing is obtained from the determinant, not from its norm.

The minimum old energy spacing is at least `gamma_*`; all positive-gap differences are at least `sigma_*`; and every off-diagonal port modulus is at least `beta_*`. If a consumer includes zero when separating distinct adjoint eigenvalues, use `min{gamma_*,sigma_*}`. The diagonal zero eigenspace remains repeated and is not claimed simple.

For fixed d,C,B and bounds, write `L=ell Lambda^D`, `ell=a_0/A_D`. All three lower bounds are explicit fixed constants times `Lambda^(D/2)`. These are sufficient lower bounds at the selected point, not at every small bias and not claims of sharp asymptotics. Literal scalar parts may be kept in K; they make the estimate looser while leaving gaps unchanged. They are not removed from the physical ledger.

## 5. Determinant sensitivity to matrix inputs

This section gives a direct finite-dimensional evaluation estimate, without diagonalizing approximate matrices. Let `G,B` be as above and let Hermitian approximations satisfy

    ||Gtilde-G||<=eta<=1,
    ||Btilde-B||<=zeta<=1.

Set

    U=2(K+1),      v=b+1,
    A_det=2m(md)^m v^(2m-1) U^(2m^2).             (13)

On Hilbert-Schmidt matrices, `||ad_G||<=2||G||`, since `||GX-XG||_F<=2||G|| ||X||_F`. Both ad maps have norm at most U and their difference has norm at most `2eta`. The noncommuting telescoping identity

    Atilde^k-A^k=sum_(j=0)^(k-1)
                 Atilde^(k-1-j)(Atilde-A)A^j

therefore gives, for every k=1,...,m,

    ||Ftilde_k-F_k||_F
       <=sqrt(d) U^k [zeta+2kb eta/U].           (14)

Both individual column norms are at most `sqrt(d) v U^k`. Since U>=1, the two `d^2 by m` column matrices obey

    ||Q||,||Qtilde|| <=sqrt(md) v U^m =: M_Q,
    ||Qtilde-Q|| <=sqrt(md) U^m[zeta+2mb eta/U]. (15)

These are operator norms bounded through the Frobenius norm; the factors of d and m are explicit. Their m-by-m Gram matrices S and Stilde then satisfy

    ||S||,||Stilde|| <= R:=md v^2 U^(2m),
    ||Stilde-S|| <=2md v U^(2m)[zeta+2mb eta/U]. (16)

For any two m-by-m matrices of operator norm at most R, column telescoping of the determinant and Hadamard's inequality give

    |det Stilde-det S|<=m R^(m-1)||Stilde-S||.

Every mixed determinant has one difference column of norm at most `||Stilde-S||` and m-1 other columns of norm at most R. This does not require invertibility or a lower singular-value bound. Combining (13)–(16),

    |p_tilde-p|<=A_det[zeta+2mb eta/U].           (17)

The estimate remains valid near zero, repeated energies or repeated gaps. Its constants are deliberately conservative; the exponent `2m^2` in U is a norm-majorant exponent, not the exact polynomial degree D. No replacement of the complex Gram by a real-Hermitian rank calculation occurs.

## 6. A concrete entry-precision prescription

At every node s<=Lambda_0, approximate H,C,B by rational Hermitian matrices, each with operator error at most delta. Then

    eta<=delta(1+Lambda_0),    zeta<=delta.

Define the fixed rational constant and requested operator precision

    C_eval=A_det[1+2mb(1+Lambda_0)/U],
    delta=min{1/(1+Lambda_0), L/(8C_eval)}.       (18)

Equations (17)–(18) guarantee `|p_tilde(s_j)-p(s_j)|<=L/8` simultaneously at all D+1 nodes. The exact rational determinants of the rational approximate data are valid real rational `p_hat_j` for (6). This prescription incurs no uncharged numerical determinant-rounding error: evaluate the finite rational arithmetic exactly. If a rounded determinant implementation is used instead, reserve a separate absolute arithmetic allowance, for example matrix error <=L/16 plus certified arithmetic error <=L/16.

For an explicit entry oracle request, choose an integer r_bits>=0 such that

    2d 2^(-r_bits) <= delta.                    (19)

Request each real and imaginary part with error at most `2^(-r_bits)`. Its complex entry error is at most `sqrt(2)2^(-r_bits)<=2*2^(-r_bits)`, and the matrix Frobenius error is at most `2d2^(-r_bits)`. Replace each rational approximating matrix M by `(M+M^*)/2`; this preserves rationality, enforces Hermiticity and does not increase the operator error against the Hermitian target. Thus (19) supplies (18). The integer can be selected by exact rational comparisons, without deciding an equality involving an unspecified computable real.

Because `L=ell Lambda^D`, the least sufficient r_bits is at most

    max{0, ceil log2[2d max{1+Lambda_0,
                                  8C_eval/(ell Lambda^D)}]}.

For fixed d,C,B,a_0,K,b,Lambda_0 and `0<Lambda<=1`, this is `O(1+D log(1/Lambda))`; explicitly, a fixed integer r_0 satisfying

    2^r_0 >=2d max{1+Lambda_0,8C_eval/ell}

allows `r_bits=r_0+D ceil(log2(1/Lambda))`. One can again select integers by dyadic rational comparisons rather than implement a discontinuous ceiling on arbitrary real data. If C,B are exact rational source data, they can instead be retained exactly, setting their actual errors to zero in (17); the joint bound remains sufficient.

This is a bound on **requested absolute input precision** and the finite matrix calculation after those inputs are supplied. Arbitrary computable input oracles can take arbitrarily long to produce those bits. No polynomial wall-clock oracle theorem follows. The constants may be enormous in dimension and chain data. No efficient construction of C or a many-body source is supplied.

The approximate matrices are used for certified evaluation only. The selected rational s is certified for the actual nominal H,C,B through (17). Nothing authorizes replacing a literal old term by its rational approximation, losing positivity in installed blocks, or assigning this static evaluation tolerance to an entire long-runtime Hamiltonian fault budget. Those physical calibration and incident-strength requirements remain separate.
