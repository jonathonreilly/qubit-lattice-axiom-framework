ITEM 1  Genuine O action                                         VERDICT: CONFIRMED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Own construction. U_R = (R-correction) (Z fixes) (CZ network) P_R, where:
  - CZ(l@c', k@c') is applied for every pair of edges at a corner c' whose relative order
    differs between the canonical ordering and the R-pushed one;
  - one Z per edge whose sign comes out wrong;
  - the R-correction comes from my own GF(2) coboundary solve.

Results:
  - hop images: U_R A_{jk} U_R^dag = A_{Rj,Rk} exactly. 0 failures in all 24 x 2304 cases,
    with no sampling.
  - stabilizer images: every one of the 3392 generators maps to a generator with exactly
    the + sign, for all 24 R. 0 failures, no sampling. (The runner samples 400 per R.)
  - before the R-correction: 243/576 products are exact. The leftover is always a product
    of R terms (up to 384 of them), with no CZ leftover.
  - link orbits: 32, all free (size 24). The cocycle is a coboundary on every orbit.
  - exact group law after the correction.
    - Every operator here has the canonical form U = D P with
      D|y> = (-1)^{q(y)}|y>, q quadratic over GF(2), and no global phase.
    - Equality of (pi, CZ set, Z set) is therefore an exact operator identity on the whole
      2^3072-dimensional space, since a degree-<=2 multilinear GF(2) polynomial is fixed by
      its values.
    - Result: U_a U_b = U_ab for 576/576 pairs, and U_E = I.
    - This is a stronger certificate than the runner's. The runner's method is also valid,
      though: agreement on all single-qubit Paulis gives equality up to a phase, and the
      common fixed point |0...0> removes the phase.
  - locality: all CZ pairs are on-site, the Z fixes are single-qubit, and the R-corrections
    are single-qubit Z's on link halves. So U_R is (on-site diagonal Clifford) x (site
    permutation), as claimed. Restricted to the fixed site, it gives a linear rep of O on
    that site's 48 qubits.
  - robustness: the same holds (576/576) for two random translation-invariant orderings
    (SEED=1, 2).

Wording correction (minor, in the "Result 2" bullet on free orbits):
  "No rotation fixes a link: a link midpoint has a quarter-integer coordinate" is not enough
  on its own.
  - A link midpoint has coordinates (integer, half-odd, quarter-odd).
  - The quarter-odd coordinate forces a fixing rotation to fix that axis with sign +1.
  - The half-odd coordinate then excludes the C2/C4 about that axis. On the L-torus this
    step needs L even: for odd L, the C2 about the quarter axis fixes links whose half
    coordinate is L/2.
  - Please state both coordinates and "L even".

ITEM 2  Pair test                                                 VERDICT: CONFIRMED
                                                    (scope caveat: site-centred axes)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method:
  - Creators are my own random hop strings. Start points q are drawn from 7 positions per C2
    in the box [-1,1]^3, including an on-site pair at the origin site. Paths are 4 per q:
    random-weight Dijkstra, with and without random waypoints. Paths are built in unwrapped
    coordinates, so S^dag S^R is a contractible loop.
  - lambda is defined by S^dag (U S U^dag) = lambda * g, with g in the stabilizer group (the
    membership test tracks phases). This gives U S|psi> = lambda S U|psi> for every code
    state psi. It is a relative eigenvalue, independent of how U acts on the 8-dimensional
    logical space, so the vacuum/logical subtlety is handled correctly.

Results, under the corrected U (252 creators, all clean: each flips exactly B_q and B_Rq,
checked against every generator):
  - lambda = +1 for all 3 axis C2;
  - lambda = -1 for all 6 edge C2;
  - no dependence on q, on the path, or on the ordering (default, SEED=1, SEED=2).
  - Twisted (lambda_V = lambda_U (-1)^{w1}): +1 for all nine C2.

Checks of the twist (analytic, using the exact facts verified in item 1):
  - The twist factor is W S W^dag = S (iB_q)(iB_Rq), which is -1 relative to the vacuum.
  - W commutes with every U_g, because U maps the sets {B} and {R} to themselves with + sign.
  - W^2 = (-i)^{1536} prod B prod R = I exactly, and prod B prod R = I was verified.
  - So V_g = U_g W^{w1(g)} is an exact linear O action, and it is not merely projective.
  - The w1-odd elements are the 6 C4 and the 6 edge C2.

Creator independence:
  - 216 random local dressings S' = E S E^R (E a random 3-qubit Pauli on q's site) give the
    same lambda in every case.
  - So do creators whose endpoints are repetition defects instead of corner charges
    (E = X on a link half).
  - Analytic reason: the two commutation signs [E, S^R] and [E^R, S] are equal under U.
  - Creators carrying a winding loop either give the same lambda, or have S^dag S^R outside
    the stabilizer group (logical), so they are inadmissible. The runner skips such cases
    silently (`if r is None: continue`); it should count them.

Corroboration of the lambda = theta*eta premise in this model:
  - The independently computed fermion-level PSG (item 3) gives axis C2^2 = -1 and edge +1.
  - With theta = -1 this reproduces the qubit-level lambda's.
  - It also does so for bond-centred half-turns (below): qubit lambda = -1 and PSG (R T_a)^2 = +1.
  - The note may state that the conditional relation is cross-checked this way here.

NEW CAVEAT (recommend adding to the interpretation and to N6/N7). The note says "the
fermion's half-turn signs are those of a spin-1/2 particle". That holds only for half-turns
about axes through a site:
  - Bond-centred axis half-turns, V = U_R T_a with R a = -a, R an axis C2:
    - qubit pair test: lambda = -1 (bondc2.log), so eta = +1;
    - PSG: (R T_a)^2 = +1.
    - These are w1-even, so the W twist leaves them at +1, i.e. not spin-1/2-like.
  - Gauge- and twist-invariant commutator: on the fermion,
    G_{C4z} T_z G_{C4z}^-1 T_z^-1 = -1 (spacegroup.log, default flux). For a spin-1/2
    particle it is +1.
  - So the full space-group fractionalization is not that of a spin-1/2 particle. It is a
    staggered/magnetic-translation-like pattern. The note's claim is about O about one site,
    and the claim text itself stays correct, but the interpretation should say this.
  - Status of the inputs: the qubit-level result here is exact. The PSG commutator is exact
    at the fermion level (L = 4).

ITEM 3  Fermion level                                 VERDICT: CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTIONS
                                                      (conventions/labels)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Own code: GF(2) gauge-field solve, PSG gauge solve, quaternion 2O, SU(2) characters.
  - All 16 uniform flux patterns:
    - each is consistent, and the O and translation PSG exist;
    - square pi gives C2axis^2 = -1, C4^4 = -1, C2edge^2 = +1;
    - square 0 gives all +1;
    - translations commute iff square = plaquette flux.
  - Untwisted: no sign choice makes the action a linear rep of O or of 2O, consistent with
    the class w2 + w1^2. With i^{w1}, and also with (-i)^{w1}, it is a linear 2O rep with
    -1 -> -I, decomposing as E1/2 + E5/2 + 2 G3/2.
  - Real on-site hopping: the lowest level is G3/2 or E1/2+E5/2 (gap 1.2679 or 3.4641).
    - General reason, which the note/runner should state instead of only testing t1 = +-1:
      complex conjugation commutes with any real O-invariant H and with the real G_R. It
      maps the i^{w1}-twisted rep to the (-i)^{w1}-twisted rep, i.e. tensors by A2, so it
      swaps E1/2 and E5/2.
  - Chiral term: the isolated doublet has gap 2.5434, as claimed.

Conventions to state explicitly:
  - (a) The runner uses H = -t1 sum u c^dag c and puts i*lambda on H[next, cur] of
    outward-oriented triangles. My H = +t1 sum u ... with i*lambda on H[cur, next]
    reproduces the note at (t1, lambda) -> (-t1, -lambda).
    - The sign of t1 is physical, given the triangle flux: t1 -> -t1 is equivalent to
      triangle flux 0 <-> pi.
    - So the note should give H with its signs and orientation, not just "t1 = -1, chiral -0.5".
  - (b) The E1/2 vs E5/2 label is convention-dependent. With the opposite twist (-i)^{w1}
    (equivalently W^-1 instead of W, an equally exact action), the same ground doublet is
    E5/2 (fermion_minus_twist.log).
    - Requested wording: "a single isolated spinorial doublet (E1/2 for the twist i^{w1}
      with the stated signs; E5/2 for the opposite twist or the opposite chiral sign)".
    - The intrinsic statement is "an isolated two-dimensional spinorial irrep".

Optional addition: under the untwisted U, the class restricted to T and to each C4 is
already w2. This is because w1^2 vanishes on T and on Z4, where H^*(Z4;Z2) has x^2 = 0;
that is why C4^4 = -1 without the twist. The twist changes only the edge-C2 signs.

ITEM 5  "Why corners"                                 VERDICT: CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTIONS
                                                      (labelled as motivation: correct)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What is sound:
  - A Clifford, hop-preserving realization gives a C4-fixed vertex a local phase in
    {+-1, +-i}, so u^4 = 1, while w2 needs C4-lift^4 = -1.
  - Corners lie only on edge-C2 axes. There u = +-i is allowed, which gives a lift-square
    of -1.

What is false: "So the corners are the smallest site-local orbit that avoids the C4 axes."
  - The octant positions v + (+-1,+-1,+-1)/4 form a site-local orbit of size 8 < 12.
  - They lie only on C3 axes, so they avoid the C4 axes.
  - C3 carries no Z2 class (H^2(Z3;Z2) = 0), so the C4 argument does not exclude them.
  - Their spinorial shells (E1/2+E5/2+G3/2 or 2 G3/2) are listed in the companion note.
Requested wording: "the corners are the smallest site-local orbit lying only on C2 axes
(avoiding both C4 and C3 axes); the octant orbit (8 positions, on C3 axes) also avoids the
C4 obstruction and is not treated". Also make N1 say "octant positions" rather than only
"cube centres".
