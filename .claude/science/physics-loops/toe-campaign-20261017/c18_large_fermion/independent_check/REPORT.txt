ITEM 2  Kramers-type 2-fold degeneracy                 VERDICT: CONFIRMED; responsible
                                                       symmetry identified (not in the note)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Splitting of each pair over 2000 random k, for all five sets: at most 8e-15 (kp.log).

  - The responsible symmetry is A = I*K, inversion through the site times complex conjugation
    (affine.log).
    - The inversion PSG exists with momentum shift S_I = 0, so A maps k -> k.
    - H(k) = W_I H(k)^* W_I^T holds to 0.0.
    - A^2 = eta_I(c) eta_I(-c) = -1 on all 12 corners, so Kramers pairs form at every k.
    - Inversion alone maps lam -> -lam (residual 0 for that map). K alone is broken by the
      chiral term, as it must be: C17 showed a real H cannot isolate a doublet.

  - Status of A.
    - A is a symmetry of the SUPPLIED HOPPING FAMILY (on-site t1, link t2, chiral lam on the
      triangles).
    - It is not one of the framework's symmetries, which are proper rotations about sites and
      translations.
    - Neither the C17 nor the C18 runner constructs A at the qubit level.
    - Since the one-fermion sector of the qubit model with this Hamiltonian is exactly this
      hopping problem, the degeneracy does hold there.
    - It is not protected by O x T x U(1). Second-neighbour hops are products of two BKSF
      hops; they commute with every loop stabilizer and they respect the PSG.
      - Of the 36 independent O x T symmetrized second-neighbour terms, 28 break I*K
        (kp.log).
      - Adding one with strength 0.02 splits the Kramers pairs by up to 0.06 at generic k
        (weyl2.log).
    - So the "every band 2-fold" result is a property of this hopping family, not of the
      qubit code's symmetry. See item 5 for why this matters.

Requested wording: "every band is 2-fold degenerate because the supplied Hamiltonian has the
antiunitary symmetry I*K (inversion through a site times complex conjugation, with PSG signs),
which squares to -1; I*K is not among O, the translations and U(1), and generic O x T x U(1)
invariant second-neighbour hops break it".

ITEM 3  Isotropic mass, quartic anisotropy, affine action           VERDICT: CONFIRMED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Direct check, which the runner does not do: H(Rk + pi S_R) = W_R H(k) W_R^T.
    - It holds for all 24 R, at random k, for every flux pattern with commuting
      translations. The maximum residual is 1.6e-16 (affine.log).
    - W_R is a signed permutation from my own PSG solve. S_R = R s_R, where s_R is the
      translation-character of the gauge transform.
  - Fixed-point computation.
    - Mine agrees with the runner: +-(pi/2)(1,-1,1) for tube pi, and none for tube 0.
    - It is exact for a general reason. The fixed set of the affine action is k0 plus the set
      fixed by the linear O action mod 2pi, which is {0, (pi,pi,pi)}. So there are either 0 or
      exactly 2 fixed points. A grid of pi/4 confirms there are no others.
  - Symmetry argument.
    - With k = k* + dk: R(k* + dk) + pi S_R = k* + R dk (mod 2pi), so e_n(k* + R dk) = e_n(k* + dk).
    - Hence the gradient vanishes and the Hessian is an O-invariant symmetric 2-tensor, which
      is a multiple of 1 because T1 is irreducible.
    - The cubic term vanishes (the only cubic, xyz, is A2). There are two quartic invariants.
    - The argument is correct.
  - Numbers (kp.log).
    - Finite-difference Hessian: eigenvalues 0.043224 (x3), spread 5e-10. A q^2 fit along 13
      directions has spread 1e-12.
    - 1/m* is 0.04322 (t2 = 0.1), 0.11119 (t2 = 0.3) and 0.07806 (t2 = 0.2, lam = -0.3).
    - The quartic coefficient fits A + B sum n_i^4, with B = -1.65e-3 at t2 = 0.1.
    - Relative [111]-[100] difference: 2.03e-3, 5.06e-4, 1.27e-4 and 3.16e-5 at dk = 0.2, 0.1,
      0.05 and 0.025. Successive ratios are 4.00, i.e. dk^2 scaling.
  - Small caveat: "the Hessian is isotropic to 1e-8" is a finite-difference bound. The exact
    statement is the symmetry argument, and it should be cited as the reason.

ITEM 4  Irrep of the minimum doublet                 VERDICT: CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTIONS
                                                     (the label is a convention)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Own L = 4 real-space build: 768 modes, my gauge and my PSG (irrep.py).
    - H commutes exactly with all 24 G_R.
    - Ground level E = -3.555635, 2-fold, which equals the Bloch minimum. The next level is
      +0.0442 above.
    - The same holds at t2 = 0.3 (-3.838478), t2 = 0.2 / lam = -0.3, t2 = -0.1 and lam = +0.5.
  - L = 8 (sparse): ground pair -3.555635, then a 6-fold level at -3.54289.
    - L = 8 also contains k*. The rep at k* is L-independent for 4 | L, because the Bloch
      sewing matrices give the same D_R.
  - The twisted rotations i^{w1} G_R act on the doublet as a projective rep of O. I find ALL
    lifts to 2O by brute force over generator signs.
    - There are exactly TWO valid lifts for each twist. They differ by the sign character A2
      and give E1/2 and E5/2 respectively (irrep.log). For example, at lam = -0.5 with twist
      i^{w1}:
          lift(+,-) -> E5/2,   lift(-,-) -> E1/2.
    - The opposite twist (-i)^{w1} gives the same two matrix sets with the labels exchanged.
    - Reason: identifying the Z2 extension with 2O is unique only up to the outer automorphism
      g -> chi_A2(g) g, and that automorphism exchanges E1/2 and E5/2.
    - So the absolute label is fixed neither by the physics nor by the twist. It is fixed by an
      arbitrary lift choice; the runner's comes from the free variable of a GF(2) solve.
  - Convention-free content, all confirmed:
    (i)   the doublet is a 2-dimensional spinorial irrep of 2O (M(-1) = -I), with no
          G3/2 content;
    (ii)  in a fixed convention, the lam < 0 and lam > 0 doublets differ by A2;
    (iii) in the same convention, the k* lattice doublet carries the SAME irrep as the C17
          single-site cluster doublet (E = -3.4142, gap 2.5434): E1/2 for lam = -0.5 under
          lift(-,-), both;
    (iv)  the adjoint action on the doublet has the vector (T1) character for both labels.
          So the doublet's "spin vector" rotates with the lattice like a vector, whichever
          label is used.
Requested wording: "carries a two-dimensional spinorial irrep of 2O (E1/2 or E5/2; which one is
fixed only by the choice of lift, and it flips with lam; in the runner's convention E1/2 for
lam < 0, the same irrep as the C17 cluster doublet)".

ITEM 5  Long-wavelength claim (note item 4)          VERDICT: CONFIRMED WITH CORRECTIONS
                                                     (substantive: rests on I*K, not on O)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) Within the supplied I*K-symmetric family, H_eff = E0 + dk^2/2m* on a doublet is right.
    It is even stronger than stated:
    - every band is exactly 2-fold at every k, so in the doublet basis H_eff = e(k) * 1_2 to
      ALL orders;
    - there is NO spin-orbit term in the free Hamiltonian at any order;
    - in a radial (parallel-transport) gauge around k*, the sewing matrices are the
      k-independent D(g);
    - so the lattice O acts as D(g) x (orbital rotation), and the free continuum theory has
      SU(2)_spin x SO(3)_orbit. SO(3)_orbit is broken only by the quartic term, of relative
      size about 0.07 (dk a)^2 along [100] at t2 = 0.1, so "(a/w)^2" is right for the anisotropy.

(b) "Spin-orbit terms of relative size (a/w)^2" is not established by anything computed.
    - The free theory has none, by I*K.
    - Spin-orbit appears only with external fields, through the non-abelian (SU(2)) Berry
      connection of the Kramers pair.
    - I computed its curvature at k* by small Wilson loops (kp.log). It is traceless, and its
      eigenvalues are +-6.0e-4 a^2 in each coordinate plane (t2 = 0.1, lam = +-0.5); the
      U(1) part is below 1e-11.
    - It gives a Pauli/Thomas-type term of order f (grad V x k).sigma. Relative to V this is
      of order a^2/(w l_V). That is (a/w)^2 only if the potential varies on the packet scale.
    - So the statement is plausible, but it is outside the stated free scope. It should be
      worded as an estimate, or dropped.

(c) THE IMPORTANT CORRECTION. "By the standard symmetry argument" is not enough with O alone.
    - O has no improper elements, and the doublet at k* is E1/2 (or E5/2), with
      T1 x T1 containing A1 once.
    - So O allows a linear, isotropic term alpha dk.sigma at k*: a Weyl point, as at the
      "Kramers-Weyl" points of chiral crystals.
    - It is absent here only because of I*K (item 2).
    - Test (perturb.py, weyl.py, weyl2.py): add O x T x U(1) invariant second-neighbour hops
      of strength delta. I verified that they are O-invariant (residual 8e-16) and Hermitian.
      - The degeneracy at k* survives; it is protected by the 2O irrep.
      - 16 of the 28 I*K-breaking terms give alpha = (2/3) delta, isotropic, linear in |dk|.
        The other 12 split the pairs away from k* but give alpha = 0 at k* in this sector.
      - With delta = 0.005, the band minimum moves off k* to a shell |dk| = 0.072 / a, with
        m* alpha = 0.077. The drop is E(k*) - Emin = 1.2e-4, against m* alpha^2/2 = 1.3e-4.
      - With delta = 0.02, the shell is at |dk| = 0.24.
    - Relative to the kinetic term, the Weyl term is of order m* alpha w. It GROWS with the
      packet size w.
    - So for the "much larger than the grid" regime the claim needs I*K exactly. Any
      I*K-breaking perturbation, however small, changes the long-wavelength theory. The packet
      then sees a shell of minima, and spin is locked to momentum.
    - This was anticipated in the C17 exploration README ("in O without inversion, the E1/2
      doublet allows a k.sigma term"), but the C18 note does not address it.

Requested changes:
  - claim_scope and Result item 4: replace "by the standard symmetry argument" with "by the

  - Add to "Not claimed" and to N6/N7: "O alone allows an isotropic Weyl term alpha dk.sigma

  - The claim that the untwisted rotation is a fine-site permutation times on-block Clifford

    single-qubit Z fixes, and R-corrections that are single-qubit Z's on halves. The
    embedding preserves this because U_R permutes labels as (v,l) -> (Rv, Rl).

Corrections:
  (a) The spin-1/2 class, and the E1/2/E5/2 labels of item 4, need the TWISTED action
      V = U W^{w1}.
      - W contains exp(-i pi R_l/4), and R_l = Z Z acts on the two halves of a link, which lie
        in ADJACENT blocks.
      - So V is NOT a fine-site permutation times on-block Cliffords. It also has two-block,
        diagonal, non-Clifford gates.
      - Please state this. The on-site alternative W' proposed in the C17 check, which was not

  (b) Term ranges in fine units. The code's terms have finite range, Chebyshev extent, upper

  (c) Link-half repetition across blocks causes no equivariance problem. Rotations about a

  (d) The printed "consequences" line in the runner is not tested. Only the combinatorics is
