INDEPENDENT GROUP-THEORY CHECK (C8 note, statements 1-4 and the nondegeneracy argument)

Script: check_grouptheory.py   Output: out_grouptheory.txt   (22 PASS, 0 FAIL; runs in < 1 s)

Method
- 2O is built as 48 explicit SU(2) matrices. They are generated by the lifts of C4z and C3(111)
  and closed under multiplication. The script checks that the adjoint image is the 24 signed-
  permutation rotations of the cube, with exactly two lifts each.
- Subgroups are taken as preimages of the stated rotation sets, identified by their SO(3) images.
- 1-dim reps are found by brute force. Every assignment of roots of unity to a generating set is
  tried, extended, and checked against the full multiplication table. No abelianization formula is
  assumed.
- The character table of 2O comes from SU(2) spin-j restrictions and sign twists. The script checks
  that the 8 class functions are orthonormal and that the dimensions satisfy sum dim^2 = 48.
- Inductions use the Frobenius formula directly. They are cross-checked by computing restrictions.

(1) CONFIRMED.
- [2O,2O] = 2T (order 24) contains -1. Of the 1-dim reps of 2O there are exactly 2, and both send
  -1 to +1.
- D2 = {E, C2x, C2y, C2z}: the preimage is Q8, all six C2 lifts square to -1, and
  [Q8,Q8] = {+1, -1}. Its 4 one-dim reps all send -1 to +1.
- D2' = {E, C2z, C2(110), C2(1-10)}: the same holds. The preimage is again Q8, because lifts of
  pi-rotations about perpendicular axes anticommute (checked: U_z U_110 = -U_110 U_z).
- Extra: the other class of H^2(O;Z2) that is nontrivial in H^2(O;U(1)) is w2 + beta(sgn), the
  GL(2,3)-type group {i^{s(g)} g}. In it the edge-C2 lifts square to +1, but -1 is still a
  commutator. So it is excluded as well.

(2) CONFIRMED by enumerating all 16 normalized Z2-valued 2-cocycles on Z2 x Z2.

  | # of involutions with lift^2 = z | extension | 1-dim rep with z -> -1 | cocycles |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | Z2^3  | yes | 2 |
  | 1 | D4    | no  | 6 |
  | 2 | Z4xZ2 | yes | 6 |
  | 3 | Q8    | no  | 2 |

- Both lifts of an involution have the same square, so the count is well defined.
- Structural reason: (ab)^2 = a^2 b^2 [b,a] gives n = (number of squares equal to z) = the
  commutator bit, mod 2. So n is even iff the extension is abelian, and an abelian extension has a
  character that separates z from 1.
- In cohomological terms, the even patterns are exactly the kernel of H^2(D2;Z2) -> H^2(D2;U(1)) = Z2,
  i.e. the Bockstein images beta(chi) of characters chi of D2.
- The W-twists of note item 6 add exactly such a beta(chi). That is why they flip two signs at a time
  and cannot change the parity of n.

(3) CONFIRMED.
- The preimage of D3 = {C3(111) and its square, C2 about (1,-1,0), (0,1,-1), (-1,0,1)} is a
  nonabelian subgroup of order 12. Its order-6 C3 lifts cube to -1, and all C2 lifts have order 4.
- It has 4 one-dim reps, and exactly 2 of them send -1 to -1; these two are complex conjugates.
- In those 2 reps, every C2 lift goes to +-i, the order-6 C3 lift goes to -1, and the order-3 C3
  lift goes to +1.
- Bookkeeping caveat: the sign of i depends on the lift chosen (chi(-U) = -chi(U)).
  - With the convention U = -i n.sigma and n = (1,-1,0), (0,1,-1), (-1,0,1) (normalized, related
    cyclically by C3), one character sends all three lifts to +i and the other sends all three to -i.
  - Flipping one axis sign, for example (1,0,-1), flips that value.
  - So "the C2 lift -> +i versus -i" names the character only once a lift convention is fixed.

(4) CONFIRMED, and the other character gives the same answer.
- Inducing either spinorial character (C2 lift -> +i or -> -i) gives a 4-dim representation equal to
  G_{3/2}.
- G_{3/2} is checked to be irreducible, 4-dim, with -1 -> -4, and equal to the restriction of SU(2)
  spin 3/2.
- The two inductions must agree: Ind(conj chi) = conj(Ind chi), and G_{3/2} is real-valued.
- Frobenius cross-check: Res G_{3/2} contains each spinorial character once. Res E_{1/2} and
  Res E_{5/2} contain neither, because both restrict to the faithful 2-dim irrep of the order-12
  group.
- For completeness, the characters with -1 -> +1 induce A1 + T2 and A2 + T1.

JUDGEMENT ON THE NONDEGENERACY ARGUMENT

Verdict: SOUND as a statement about the site group of a position the fermion actually occupies,
provided the symmetry acts on the fermion operator there by a scalar. The consequence for O (and
already for D2, D2', D4, T) is correct. The general wording needs two qualifications.

The core step is correct.
- Suppose the fermion sits at a point v0 fixed by K, with one local state. Then each R in K acts on
  it by a phase a_R.
- The group law up to fermion parity says a_R a_S = eta(R,S) a_RS. So eta is the coboundary of a
  U(1)-valued function.
- Equivalently, R -> a_R is a 1-dim rep of the extension K_f with (-1)^F -> -1. Equivalently again,
  [eta] dies in H^2(K;U(1)).
- The cleanest form of the criterion is "[eta] lies in the image of the Bockstein of Hom(K,U(1))".
  For D2 this is the even-count condition of (2).
- This makes precise the note's "H^2(D3;U(1)) = 0" remark, which is correct (Schur multiplier of
  S3 = 0).

Subtleties
(a) The relevant group is the site group, not the point group.
  - The constraint binds only at positions whose stabilizer is K.
  - A one-state fermion whose occupied sites all have smaller site group (C1, C2, C3, C4, D3) can
    carry w2 of the whole point group. Its orbit multiplet is an induced spinor rep, as in the table.
  - So "a single-state fermion cannot carry w2 on O" needs an occupied site fixed by O, or by any
    Q8-type subgroup.
  - For BKSF that holds for vertex-centred rotations, since the fermions live on vertices.
  - For translation-invariant codes, a rotation about any lattice-preserving centre equals a vertex-
    centred rotation times a lattice translation. So the code's stabilizer always contains the
    vertex-centred group, and the argument does apply to the order-4 codes.
  - But the fractionalization class is really a property of the space-group action. Its restrictions
    to point groups about different Wyckoff centres can differ; the pi-flux toric control already
    shows a centre-dependent C2 sign. "The class on O" should therefore name the centre.
(b) Which notion of localization to use.
  - Region truncation of P_R is not a valid localization: the earlier check showed it breaks
    boundary generators.
  - The appropriate setting is algebraic. The bare permutation induces an automorphism of the
    encoded even fermion algebra, and the hypothesis is that it extends to a locality-preserving
    automorphism of the full CAR algebra of the form c_v -> a_R(v) c_{Rv}.
  - Under that hypothesis eta(R,S) is automatically site-independent and +-1. At the fixed vertex
    the a_R(v0) are scalars, so the argument goes through, with no "fixed-point local Hilbert space"
    needed.
  - This is the right setting for a lattice point group, because every element fixes the centre.
  - The open premise is exactly the monomial extension.
    - For the 72 covered pairs it is shown by the Majorana form, c_v -> -i s_v c_{Rv}.
    - The C2s of the order-4 codes (their axis C2 and their second edge C2) are among the 36 pairs
      that the Majorana form does NOT cover.
(c) The order-4 tension is sharper than "open". Given a common fixed vertex, monomial scalars with
    a_R^2 = -1 for all three C2 of D2' are inconsistent:
      a_z a_110 a_(1-10)^{-1} = +-i, which is not in {+1, -1}.
  - So at least one of the following must hold:
    - for those C2s the induced action is not monomial at v0, i.e. the even-algebra automorphism
      has no locality-preserving CAR extension (a genuine localization obstruction);
    - lambda = theta_f eta_f fails for those pairs;
    - one of the 36 dressed-pair values is wrong.
  - Recommended decisive test: for one order-4 code, compute the induced fermion map explicitly for
    the three vertex-centred C2s, and read off eta(R,S) for R != S (equivalently the commutator bit)
    directly, instead of inferring the class from squares.
  - A tensor-product qubit permutation with no fixed qubit is non-anomalous. That makes a true
    obstruction less likely than a breakdown of the monomial form or of the pair-test relation.
(d) The four-species stack is consistent with the no-go.
  - At a vertex fixed by O, the fermion has 4 states (one per species), and 2O acts by G3/2. The
    no-go is evaded by degeneracy, as it must be.
  - The single D3 species is fine because Dic3 has spinorial characters.

Not checked: anything about the actual codes, pair tests, or the Majorana map (pure group theory only).
