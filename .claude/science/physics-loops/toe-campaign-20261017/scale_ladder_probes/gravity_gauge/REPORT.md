## Gravity

- **G3, a TT graviton with a common cone. Partly resolved, and the obligation moves.**
  - Probe P-G1 counted local two-derivative quadratic forms.
    - Spin-1: SO gives 2 forms and signed permutations give 3; exactly 1 is gauge-invariant in every case.
    - Spin-2, d=3: SO(3) gives 4 and O_h gives 9; 1 is gauge-invariant.
    - Spin-2, d=4: SO(4) gives 4 and B4 gives 9; 1 is gauge-invariant.
  - The d=4 form gives ω²/k² = 1.000000 with two polarisations along [100], [110], [111] and [210].
  - So lattice symmetry plus exact linearised-diffeo redundancy forces linearised Einstein–Hilbert at leading order. This falsified the agent's initial expectation of an anisotropic graviton.
  - Controls:
    - 2% gauge-breaking B4 terms give ω²/k² = 1.036 along [110].
    - Mass terms number 2 under SO(4) and 3 under B4; none is gauge-invariant.
    - So both masslessness and isotropy rest on the redundancy.
  - P-G1b, Hamiltonian time with only spatial O_h:
    - An extra electric invariant g3 = Σ E_ii² is not fixed by gauge invariance.
    - At g3 = 0.1 the TT speed² values are {1, 1.10} along [100], {1, 1.075} along [110] and {1.033, 1.033} along [111]. This is leading-order birefringence.
    - GW170817 requires |c_g/c − 1| ≲ 1e-15, so g3 must be protected.
    - The trace ratio g2 leaves TT unaffected.
- **G2, a universal source. Reframed.** Constraint closure at β = −α is the protecting symmetry. Open: is the coarse matter stress its Noether source?
- **G1, carrier and action. Reframed.** Extract the hypercubic EFT coefficients of each kernel symbol instead of enlarging benches. A union of two cones signals a missing redundancy.
- **G4, supplied continuum actions. No help in the derivation direction.**
- **The cosmological constant** is flagged as an unprotected relevant operator.
- **Proposed, not run.**
  - P-G2: extract g3 and the full symbol from the curvature-member stencil and from the Regge Hessian.
  - P-G3: test the Noether identity for the coarse matter stress.

## Native transfer and spatial gauge

- **T3, separating saddle coefficients from continuum ones. Resolved as a diagnosis.**
  - Probe P-T1 used exact Bessel-determinant character coefficients.
  - Matching the fundamental representation gives τ_eff = (3/β)(1 + 7/(4β) + O(β⁻²)). The Richardson-extrapolated 1/β coefficient is 1.74997.
  - The residual mismatch is O(β⁻³), with r8·β³ ≈ 11.5.
  - So one-link 1/β coefficients are scheme: a coupling redefinition absorbs them.
  - The repo's ≥7/16 coefficient is in a different normalisation, and its relation to 7/4 was not checked.
- **T2, coupling identification. Reframed.** Identify the coupling by scheme-independent quantities: a Λ ratio, or m/√σ.
- **T4, a physical mass gap. Reframed, not resolved.**
  - A Gaussian-limit gap behaves like 2π/L, so it is kinematic.
  - A real gap needs gap·L → ∞ along the asymptotic-freedom line.
  - The invariant to carry is the unbroken centre 1-form symmetry (area law), with no transition in β. That is Balaban's problem, the Clay problem.
- **T1, action selection. Helped.** Universality means the action matters only through the coupling map plus irrelevant terms. Hamiltonian limits lose hypercubic protection, so speeds must be retuned per species.
- **Proposed, not run.**
  - P-T2: gap·L on cube, slab and larger blocks.
  - P-T3: the one-loop Λ ratio between the native and Wilson actions.
