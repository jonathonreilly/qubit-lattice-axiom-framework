# Goal

Name the dimension of the soldered bond-symmetric factors on one proper
orbit of `(1, 2, 3)`. Do not select a factor. Do not add a formation rate.

Cycle 2 asks whether order-blindness cuts that family to the exhibited
line. The answer on the cap-sign ansatz is no. Cycle 3 shows that inside
the span of those three signs, only the axes work. Cycle 4 exhibits
three opposite-cap lines outside that span. Cycle 5 exhibits a
transverse-cross kernel whose slice is orthogonal to all six cap signs.
The open target on this orbit is any further order-blind kernel. Cycle
6 counts the soldered bond space on the 48 signed permutations:
dimension 312, and 300 after far-end sum 1. Cycle 7 exhibits transverse
crosses at absolute heights 1 and 2 outside the six caps and the
modulus-3 cross. Their sum has moment `23/24`. Cycle 8 exhibits the
swapped-absolute transverse cross at each height 1, 2, and 3 outside
those nine kernels. The cross-height sum has moment `23/24`. Cycle 9
shows each one-cap-pair support has kernel dimension 3, spanned by the
cap and two crosses. Six patterns give dimension 18. Cycle 10 adds the
level-`+3` cap to the matched cross on `a·d = -3`. That sum has two
live directions and product mean 1. The opposite cap lines have moment
`7/6`. Cycle 11 names the single height-signature ansatz. A nonzero
slice-zero kernel on one signature is order-blind only when the
heights are opposite, and those six kernels are the cap-pair span.
The equal-height case split leaves only zero. A sum of two signatures
stays open. Cycle 12 shows an unequal signature beside any one partner
is forced to zero by the pair-moment squares. The kernel drops to that
partner. An equal-height signature beside one opposite signature is
not forced by that square test. Cycle 13 shows the joint slice kernel
on every pair is the direct sum, and the equal-height block is zero
beside one opposite or equal-height partner. The triple of
`(-3, -3)` with both modulus-3 opposite signatures has joint kernel
10. Cycle 14 shows the complement of those three signature kernels
is one line with moment `2` and uniform average `1/12`. Cycle 15
shows that a rational mixture of that line with the three signature
kernels cancels every two-neighbour moment only when the complement
coefficient and the equal-height block are both zero, so the mixture
lies in the eighteen. Cycle 16 shows that on the 48-point chiral
pair the height-linear family `1 + c (a·d)(b·(-d))` has far-end sum
48, and at `c = 1/10` the opposite-direction moment on `(1, 2, 3)` is
`71/50`, so that positive factor is not order-blind. Cycle 17 shows
that the transverse-sign cap at coefficient `1/2` has every partner
pattern of mean 1, and the bonds from `(1, 2, 3)` and `(-2, 1, 3)` to
`(1, 2, -3)` forbid every scale of the chiral cap. Cycle 18 shows
that the sign product of orbit chirality and the transverse sign, at
coefficient `1/2`, has every partner pattern of mean 1. The bonds on
`e_z` from `(1, 2, 3)`, `(2, -1, 3)`, and `(-2, -1, 3)` to
`(-2, -1, -3)` give determinant `4`, so that kernel is not a linear
combination of the two exhibited caps. Cycle 19 shows that the matched
and swapped transverse crosses on the caps `a·d = 3` and `b·d = -3`,
at coefficient `1/2`, each have neighbour-product mean 1. Five bonds
from `(-2, -1, 3)` give determinant `8`, so neither cross lies in the
span of the three caps. Cycle 20 shows that the matched and swapped
transverse crosses at absolute heights 1 and 2, at coefficient `1/2`,
each have neighbour-product mean 1. Nine bonds on `e_z` give
determinant `8`, so none of those four crosses lies in the span of the
five height-3 kernels.
