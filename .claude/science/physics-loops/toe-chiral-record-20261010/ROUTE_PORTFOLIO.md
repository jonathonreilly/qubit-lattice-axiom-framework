# Route portfolio — cycle 1

Taken: enumerate the soldered bond-symmetric factors on one proper orbit
of `(1, 2, 3)` and row-reduce the far-end sums.

Not taken in cycle 1: the 48-point chiral pair and other alphabets.

Cycle 2 takes the cap-sign ansatz on moduli 1, 2, and 3, and the
two-neighbour moment of a mixture. It does not take a general factor.

Cycle 3 takes linear combinations of those three signs. It does not take
a bond factor outside that span.

Cycle 4 takes the opposite cap `a·d = -m` for each modulus `1, 2, 3`.
It does not take a slice outside those six oriented caps.

Cycle 5 takes one transverse-cross kernel on the modulus-3 caps. It
does not take the 48-point chiral pair.

Cycle 6 takes the soldered bond-orbit count on the 48 signed
permutations of `(1, 2, 3)`. It does not take an order-blindness cut.

Cycle 7 takes the transverse-cross formula at absolute heights 1 and 2.
It does not take a kernel outside those three crosses.

Cycle 8 takes the swapped-absolute transverse cross at heights 1, 2,
and 3. It does not take a kernel outside those twelve.

Cycle 9 takes the six one-cap-pair supports. It does not take a kernel
with two live directions.

Cycle 10 takes the sum of the level-`+3` cap and the matched cross on
`a·d = -3`. It does not take a kernel outside the cap-pair span.

Cycle 11 takes one reversal class of endpoint heights. It does not take
a sum of two classes.

Cycle 12 takes one unequal height signature plus one partner. It does
not take an equal-height signature beside an opposite signature.

Cycle 13 takes one equal-height signature plus one opposite or
equal-height partner. It does not take the extra slice direction in
the triple `(-3, -3)`, `(-3, 3)`, `(3, -3)`.

Cycle 14 takes that complement line alone. It does not take a mixture
of the line with the three signature kernels.

Cycle 15 takes every rational linear mixture of that line with the
three signature kernels. It does not take a fourth height signature,
and it does not take the 48-point chiral pair.

Cycle 16 takes the height-linear family on the 48-point chiral pair.
It does not take the parent's chiral-cap line, and it does not classify
the order-blind subset.

Cycle 17 takes the transverse-sign cap on that alphabet. It does not
classify a factor outside both that cap and the chiral-cap line.

Cycle 18 takes the sign product of orbit chirality and the transverse
sign. It does not classify a factor outside the span of that product
and the two exhibited caps, and it does not classify the
300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 19 takes the matched and swapped transverse crosses on the
height-3 caps. It does not classify a factor outside those two crosses
and the three caps, and it does not classify the 300-dimensional
affine space.

Cycle 20 takes the matched and swapped transverse crosses at absolute
heights 1 and 2. It does not classify a factor outside those four
crosses and the five height-3 kernels, it does not take a sum across
heights, and it does not classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 21 takes the chirality, transverse-sign, and sign-product caps
at absolute heights 1 and 2. It does not classify a factor outside
those six caps and the nine kernels, it does not take a mixed-height
bond, and it does not classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 22 takes the parallel transverse-dot sign at absolute heights 1,
2, and 3. It names the 10-dimensional opposite-height ansatz and places
one kernel outside the fifteen. It does not base the 5-dimensional
complement, it does not take a mixed-height bond, and it does not
classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 23 takes the matched oblique transverse-dot sign at absolute
heights 1, 2, and 3. It places one kernel outside the fifteen and the
three parallel-dot kernels, and it raises the exhibited rank inside the
10-dimensional ansatz from 6 to 7. It does not base the remaining
3-dimensional complement, it does not take a mixed-height bond, and it
does not classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 24 takes the swapped transverse-dot sign at absolute heights 1, 2,
and 3. It places one kernel outside the fifteen, the three parallel-dot
kernels, and the three matched oblique dots, and it raises the exhibited
rank inside the 10-dimensional ansatz from 7 to 8. It does not base the
remaining 2-dimensional complement, it does not take a mixed-height
bond, and it does not classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 25 takes the orthogonal swapped cross at absolute heights 1, 2,
and 3. It places one kernel outside the fifteen, the three parallel-dot
kernels, the three matched oblique dots, and the three swapped dots,
and it raises the exhibited rank inside the 10-dimensional ansatz from
8 to 9. It does not base the remaining 1-dimensional complement, it
does not take a mixed-height bond, and it does not classify the
300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 26 takes the scaled cross-dot kernel at absolute heights 1, 2,
and 3. It places that kernel outside the twenty-seven comparison
kernels and raises the exhibited rank from 9 to 10, so the ten named
kernels are a basis of the opposite-height ansatz. It does not take a
mixture of those kernels, it does not take a mixed-height bond, and it
does not classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 27 takes every rational mixture of the ten kernels on one
opposite-height pair. The product-mean identity holds on the whole
span. It does not take a sum across two heights, and it does not
classify the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 28 takes two named sums of caps at heights 1 and 2. The two
chirality caps have moment `97/96`. The chirality cap plus the
sign-product cap has every two-direction mean equal to 1. It does not
classify the coefficient span of two bases, and it does not classify
the 300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 29 takes the parity span of seven kernels at two absolute
heights. The odd-odd and even-even products have rank 25. It does not
take the matched cross, the swapped transverse dot, or the scaled
cross-dot kernel, and it does not classify the 300-dimensional affine
space.

Cycle 30 takes the rational span of those three excluded kernels
against any ten-kernel partner. Every kernel cross sum vanishes exactly
when one of the two vectors is 0. It does not take a vector that also
has a parity-span component, and it does not classify the
300-dimensional affine space.

Cycle 31 takes each plane spanned by one parity kernel and one excluded
kernel. Twenty of those planes have full partner rank whenever both
coefficients are nonzero. The oblique-matched plane has a
three-dimensional partner kernel at ratios `1` and `-1`. It does not
take a sum of two parity kernels, and it does not classify the
300-dimensional affine space.
