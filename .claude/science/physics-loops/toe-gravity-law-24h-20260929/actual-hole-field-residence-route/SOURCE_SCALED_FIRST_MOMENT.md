# Source-scaled residence from a first total-field input moment

Author candidate, independently unchecked. This is a new consumer of the previously checked fixed-global-k finite-spin first-response theorem. It is not a bound for the actual arbitrary-background microscopic dark density. Its global count and global field restrictions are stated before the result and are not replaced by local density assumptions.

## 1. Actual leading law and checked input

Fix odd global N_B=k and W=1. Use the physical Gauss sector on Z3's finite-change representation or even tori L>=max(28,4k), with the full original leading finite-spin A_S=-i delta(Hbar_S+Delta_S)-kappa G_S/2. The extensive diagonal compensation Delta_S, exact spin zeros and original resolved/coherent mark families are the checked ones. Z_S(u)=exp(uA_S) is the actual terminal no-original-event amplitude. No higher microscopic normal-form term is silently included. Put

    C_S=S(S+1), epsilon² C_S=c_*:=delta/K,
    Q=1+sum_all_links |E_e|.

Q is a GLOBAL total-field weight. Its first moment is not a local energy-density hypothesis. Original first events end this response. The complete all-horizon amplitude comparison of finite-spin-fast-response/REPORTd0a96836 and its focused receipt c3669299 was reread completely before this proof. In particular, its terminal component gives, for spin-supported psi in D(Q²),

    ||Z_S(u)psi-Z_infinity(u)psi||
                             <=B_k/C_S ||Q²psi||, all u>=0,  (S1)
    ||Z_infinity(u)||<=C_k exp(-gamma_k u).                    (S2)

C_k,gamma_k,B_k are its explicit finite constants, independent of S and the allowed volume. They can grow very badly with k. That theorem proves neither a spin gap nor an all-time waiting moment; none is used here.

Let R_S be ANY positive bounded reward in this spin sector with

    0<=R_S<=(1+rS)I, r>=0 fixed.

Examples are the moving-hole local first-field reward, its dark part, or a fixed finite-link first-field reward. The reward may be amplified by an arbitrary spectator identity. Fix a PHYSICAL horizon T<infinity and define

    (M_S psi)(u)=epsilon R_S^(1/2) Z_S(u)psi,
                 0<=u<=T/epsilon².

The target estimate is

    ||M_S psi||² <= C_(k,r,T) ||Q^(1/2)psi||².                (S3)

Thus only an input FIRST total-field moment is required, even though(S1) was initially obtained on D(Q²). The factor epsilon² and the specified growing horizon are essential. We do not claim a finite integral to infinite fast time at each fixed spin.

## 2. Two bounds on each field band

Contraction of Z_S immediately gives

    ||M_S||² <=T(1+rS)<=b S,  b=T(r+1).                      (S4)

This is on the invariant spin-supported subspace. For a vector in D(Q²), use the spin bound on R_S BEFORE the comparison, then(S1)-(S2) and Minkowski in L2 time:

 ||M_S psi||
   <=epsilon sqrt(1+rS) [C_k/sqrt(2gamma_k)||psi||
                       +(B_k/C_S)sqrt(T/epsilon²)||Q²psi||].

No norm bound1+rS is applied to the rotor output outside the spin box. It was applied only to Z_S; the subsequent estimate uses the unweighted vector comparison. Squaring with (x+y)²<=2x²+2y² yields

 ||M_S psi||² <= a/S ||psi||²
                   + d0/S³ ||Q²psi||²,
 a=(r+1)c_* C_k²/gamma_k, d0=2T(r+1)B_k².                  (S5)

Here S/C_S=1/(S+1)<=1/S and S/C_S²<=1/S³. The full Delta_S price is inside B_k, not deleted.

Let P_j be the spectral projection 2^j<=Q<2^(j+1), j=0,1,..., restricted to the physical spin box. These commute with the box, Gauss and particle-sector projections. Write q_j=2^j. Since ||Q²P_j||<=4q_j², equations(S4)-(S5) imply

These are bounds on complete vectors within a band, including all field, charge and reference coherences. No band measurement is made.

## 3. Coherent dyadic interpolation to Q^(1/2)

For a finite band sum xi, triangle followed by Cauchy--Schwarz gives

 ||M_S Q^(-1/2)xi||
   <=sum_j c_j ||P_j xi||
   <=(sum_j c_j²)^(1/2)||xi||,
 c_j²=min{bS/q_j, a/(S q_j)+d q_j³/S³}.                    (S7)

The estimate adds amplitudes, not squared dephased inputs. Therefore it explicitly controls every cross-band coherence.

Split at q_j<=S. Since q_j is dyadic,

 sum_(q_j<=S) a/(S q_j)<=2a/S<=2a,
 sum_(q_j<=S) d q_j³/S³ <=(8/7)d,
 sum_(q_j>S) bS/q_j<2b.

Consequently

    C_(k,r,T)=2a+(8/7)d+2b                              (S8)

is a uniform bound on the squared operator norm of M_S Q^(-1/2). Finite band sums are dense. Q^(-1/2) is bounded and M_S is bounded at each fixed S by(S4), so the estimate extends to the entire sector. Taking xi=Q^(1/2)psi proves(S3) for every psi in D(Q^(1/2)). The Z3 domain is handled by this spectral approximation, not by assuming input D(Q²).

For positive trace-class tau with Tr(Q tau)<infinity, apply(S3) to a purification or sum over its positive spectral decomposition. Ancilla amplification is already allowed in(S1), so

 epsilon² integral_0^(T/epsilon²)
       Tr[R_S Z_S(u)tau Z_S(u)*]du <=C_(k,r,T) Tr(Q tau).    (S9)

This is uniform over the safe volumes, including total-field superpositions. The constant does not count the number of links, but its RIGHT SIDE is global.

## 4. The actual epsilon² source coefficient is sufficient in this restricted response

For any measurable positive, spin-supported input family tau_s in this fixed one-hole/global-k sector with integrable Tr(Q tau_s), form the explicitly declared leading-kernel positive injection

 eta(t)=kappa epsilon² integral_0^t
          Z_S((t-s)/epsilon²) tau_s Z_S((t-s)/epsilon²)* ds. (S10)

Tonelli for positive integrands and(S9), with horizon at most T, give

 epsilon^-2 integral_0^T Tr[R_S eta(t)]dt
       <=kappa C_(k,r,T) integral_0^T Tr(Q tau_s)ds.          (S11)

All original labels may be retained as direct-sum components of tau_s and its output, and arbitrary reference/history registers tensor throughout. The kernel uses the actual original j_mu loss and their terminal/no-event evolution. The theorem changes no mark convention and asserts no fictitious detected grade.

The actual positive-grade source coefficient is kappa epsilon² B_mu P_U sigma P_U B_mu*, B_mu=-F_a j_mu F_a, as previously checked. Equation(S11) shows exactly why its small coefficient can pay a growing finite-spin response cost, IF the input and response have the declared fixed-global-k/global-Q/leading-one-hole hypotheses. The true microscopic source generally does not: there may be arbitrary distant B records, other holes, a full D_epsilon response, and an extensive total-field moment. Thus(S10) is not asserted equal to its actual hole density. Replacing k by a local count, or Q by a regional field without a compatibility proof, would invalidate this use.

For block-diagonal inputs and rewards, an orthogonal direct sum of finitely many k sectors is allowed by applying the bound on each sector; no cross-sector coherence is silently discarded. For a growing set of sectors the exact sum of their C_(k,r,T) prices is retained; it is not declared finite or volume-uniform on the actual source.

## 5. Tightness and missing response strength

For a fixed low-field band Q<=R, (S5) tends to zero as S grows. The uniform relative estimate(S3) then implies vanishing of(S9) for fixed finite-first-moment inputs under compatible spin compression, or for families with uniformly bounded first Q moments and uniformly vanishing first-moment tails. For instance split a purification at Q<=R; the low part costs at most a/S+d0 R^4/S³, and the high part costs C Tr[Q 1_(Q>R)tau], with the squared-triangle factor2. A bounded first moment alone need not make the second term small. No such uniform integrability is supplied by the ordinary microscopic first-field theorem.

The conditional bound(S11) is nevertheless a real FIRST-moment residence estimate on physical growing horizons, without the nonexistent assumption of a uniform spin relaxation rate. It is distinct from a fixed-U kernel comparison, a fourth waiting-time moment for the first vacuum source, and a trace-norm-only source cutoff. The remaining actual-source obligation is a justified localization/decomposition of the complete response whose weighted prices can be summed in the actual state, or a different direct dark-current estimate. No source-cluster tail, global-k reduction, stationary preparation, microscopic limit or energy-tail theorem has been established here.

This derivation is analytic; no new computation is used.
