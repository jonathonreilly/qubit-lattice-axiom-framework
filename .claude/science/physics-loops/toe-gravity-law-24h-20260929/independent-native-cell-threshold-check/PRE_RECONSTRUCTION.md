Odd L avoids translation stabilizers of a four-site occupation: any stabilizer
acts freely on its four sites, has order dividing four, while every torus
translation has odd order. The physical zero-momentum orbit basis is therefore
normalized by sqrt(V) with no exceptional orbit weights. On a separated pair
configuration the normalized two-pair frame coefficient is Phi_incoming/V in
the physical occupation basis, or Phi_incoming/sqrt(V) in the orbit basis.
Consequently V times its Schur matrix should have the infinite incoming
normalization T0. Check this on arbitrary complex symmetric tensors, not only
a coherent diagonal sample. The frame lies in total momentum zero; translation
invariance means other momentum sectors do not enter its Schur minimization.

For fixed n=2, the polar Gram differs from identity by O(1/V), since the
excluded collision configurations form a fixed finite relative core. The
checked compression gap is O(L^-2), and raw minimizers after multiplying the
physical vector by sqrt(V) have bounded energy. Their deviation from the frame
may have l2 norm O(L), so norm convergence to the incoming profile is NOT a
valid shortcut. The finite-cell zero mode is not the infinite transient
Dirichlet inverse.

Use the 81 fields F_dt(r)=psi({r,r+d,0,t}), zero on overlap, for d,t the nine
forward physical graph edges. Every field is pinned at r=0 because the removed
edge and residual share that anchor. Actual annihilation rows give a uniform
sum of gradient energies and high-mode S energies. On the periodic cell the
mean-zero discrete l6 norm is bounded by the gradient l2 norm with a constant
independent of L. One elementary proof interpolates linearly on cubes, uses
finite-dimensional equivalence between vertex and interpolation norms, the
periodic mean-zero Poincare inequality, and the Euclidean Sobolev inequality.
The latter can itself be obtained from the W^(1,1) product/FTC inequality
applied to |u|^4 and Cauchy, so no dilute-gas theorem is imported.

The physical no-perfect-matching part has D>=1, so its l2 norm is bounded.

A quantitative lower bound would need a uniform finite-L cutoff estimate,
not just qualitative compactness. A logarithmic transition between radii
