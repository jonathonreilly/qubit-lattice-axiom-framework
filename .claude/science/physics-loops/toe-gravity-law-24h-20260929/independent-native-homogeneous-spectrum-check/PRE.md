A unique N-sector ground has no matrix elements of those neutral operators to any other charge sector, even if lower full-space energies occur there. Spectral denominators/ground errors can therefore use the N-sector gap; locality MUST still be proved on the full tensor product before restriction. The allowed geometry, local term norm, charge and support bounds must be explicit. N/L noninteger is an additional commensurability condition, not a consequence of dilute EOS. Degeneracy is already the alternative, not a positive local excitation gap theorem.

Take two far cuts. The opposite-twist family is exactly exp(i theta Q_left) H exp(-i theta Q_left), hence its sector spectrum is constant. A real odd Gaussian-integrated time kernel w_s(t)=.5 sign(t) erfc(|t|/(2sqrt(s))) has Fourier i(1-exp(-s omega²))/omega under integral exp(i omega t). Its Hermitian neutral generator D_s approximates parallel ground transport: iD_s psi=-((1-exp(-s(H-E)²))/(H-E)) Hdot psi. For the pure gauge derivative Hdot=i[Q_left,H], this simplifies to transport generated by Q_left minus its expectation; the error is bounded by exp(-s Delta²)||Q_left|| without a denominator. L1 norm of w_s is2sqrt(s/pi).

Truncate each filtered cut current to a strip far from the other cut. Full-space LR plus a time split at t~L/v gives error bounded by polynomial(L,s) times exp(-cL)+exp(-cL²/(v²s)). The two truncated generators have disjoint support and thus their unitaries commute. Their product approximately transports the ground around the exact gauge loop and returns a phase exp(-i2pi<Q_left>), since onsite charges are integers. The left unitary alone has small excess energy: right-region terms commute with it, and left-region terms commute with the right unitary, allowing the product-return bound to control the energy by2||H|| times the vector error. Translate the left cut by one site while keeping the right fixed. The right local unitary can be identical. The two gauge-loop phases differ by exp(plus/minus i2pi N/L), because the removed/added slice has expectation N/L in a translation eigenstate. This gives the twisted vector a translation expectation close to z exp(i2piN/L).
