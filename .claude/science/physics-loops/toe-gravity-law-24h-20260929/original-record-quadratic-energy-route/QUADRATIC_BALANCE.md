## 2. Linear-growth displacement calculus

The new step needed for Q2 is to retain first-field growth in a local commutator instead of taking its spin norm O(S). Here are sufficient explicit uniform estimates.

  m_p(A)=max(sup_alpha sum_beta |A_alpha,beta|(1+d)^p,
             sup_beta sum_alpha |A_alpha,beta|(1+d)^p).

Original normalized shifts and their finite products have uniformly bounded m_p; their diagonal coefficients, including normalized electric compensation, have uniformly bounded suprema. The inequality 1+d(alpha,gamma)<= (1+d(alpha,beta))(1+d(beta,gamma)) makes m_p submultiplicative. Thus local gate exponentials, their inverses and their fixed-order Taylor remainders have uniform m_p bounds on a common complex disk. Onsite W averaging deletes entries and its inverse divides a nonzero-grade entry by a nonzero integer, so both contract these norms. This is the actual finite-cone normal-form construction, not a global Schur bound for Y.

For a local linear-growth operator A, replace the row sum by its ratio to

  q_Z(alpha)=1+sum_(e in Z)|E_e(alpha)|,

and the column sum by its ratio to q_Z(beta); call the maximum ell_p(A). Multiplication of such an A by a bounded-displacement B on either side obeys
