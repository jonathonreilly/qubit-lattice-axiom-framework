# Finite-path history response and complete Gaussian leaf calculus

Author derivation, 2026-09-13. Conditional mathematical result; independent
review pending. This continues BLOCK01_DERIVATION.md on the same pinned
native carrier and charged-history interfaces. The quadratic dwell, ready
state, projective event rule, physical controls and occurrence schedule
remain supplied. Neither a physical clock nor an axiom amendment is derived.

## 1. Covariance convention and domain

There are m finite matter modes with CAR operators c_j. Use

    C_ij = Tr(rho c_j^dagger c_i),  0 <= C <= I.

For H=sum_ij c_i^dagger h_ij c_j with h Hermitian, supplied dwell U(t)=exp(-itH)
gives C(t)=u(t) C u(t)^dagger, u(t)=exp(-ith). This identity holds for any
state; Gaussian closure will only be used in Section 6 onward. It follows
from [H,c_i]=-sum_j h_ij c_j and differentiating U(t)^dagger c_i U(t).

The native source dictionary represents these even observables on its
specified finite edge carrier. After leaf Records, the surviving words keep
their original recorded-edge signs and original particle observables. A
branch-dependent resetting of the dictionary is not part of this argument.
Any fixed phase gauge changes u_x0 by endpoint phases, leaving its modulus
unchanged. Actual native implementation is conditional on that dictionary.

## 2. Rank-one response to the first leaf outcome

Start from the normalized full matter Fock trace. At site 0, use an initially
empty, previously unrecorded leaf, with 0 < r_0 < 1. Both outcomes have
positive probabilities z_0=(1+r_0^2)/2, z_1=(1-r_0^2)/2. The first leaf
calculation gives the conditional matter covariances

    C^(0) = I/2 - (1-r_0^2) P_0 / [2(1+r_0^2)],
    C^(1) = I/2 - P_0/2,
    P_0 = |0><0|.

No off-diagonal entry is generated by the local selective pulse on the
initial trace. In particular,

    C^(0)-C^(1) = kappa P_0,
    kappa = r_0^2/(1+r_0^2).                         (1)

After any common number-conserving quadratic dwell, the difference is
kappa u P_0 u^dagger. At mode x, apply a fresh, initially filled leaf pulse
with attenuation r_x. Its unchanged (actual bit 1) event has probability
r_x^2+(1-r_x^2) C_xx. Therefore

    p_x(1 | first=0) - p_x(1 | first=1)
       = (1-r_x^2) kappa |u_x0(t)|^2.              (2)

For an initially empty second leaf the difference for actual bit 1 has the
same sign and magnitude, since its bit-1 effect is (1-r_x^2)n_x; the sign
reverses if comparing the unchanged empty-leaf outcome instead. Formula (2)
reduces to Block 1 for a two-mode hopping rotation. Other untouched leaves
may exist, but no intervening measurement or outcome-dependent control is
included in this two-event theorem. Later sections handle such histories.

At r_0=0 the two post-matter states agree and this particular response
vanishes. At r_0=1 the first flip has zero probability, so a two-supported-
history comparison is unavailable; its formal limiting conditional state
is not a reachable branch. At r_x=1 the later event is uninformative.

The nonselective covariance is I/2-(1-r_0^2)P_0/2. Hence the unconditional
later filled-leaf probability is

    (1+r_x^2)/2
      - (1-r_x^2)(1-r_0^2)|u_x0(t)|^2/2.           (3)

This distinguishes a history-conditioned response from ordinary average
disturbance and from a global joint law with specified one-site marginals.

## 3. Nonzero propagation on every finite path

Let h be the zero-diagonal Hermitian nearest-neighbor hopping matrix on a
finite path. Choose two vertices 0 and x at graph distance d >= 1, with
every bond along their unique connecting path nonzero. In the exponential
series, (h^k)_x0=0 for k<d and for k-d odd. At k=d the unique shortest walk
contributes B=product of the oriented h entries along that path, B != 0.
Consequently, for real t near zero,

    u_x0(t) = (-it)^d B/d! + O(t^(d+2)),
    |u_x0(t)|^2 = |B|^2 t^(2d)/(d!)^2
                    + O(t^(2d+2)).                (4)

The remainder statement follows from the norm-convergent finite-matrix
exponential, and its parity from bipartiteness and the zero diagonal.
It proves a strictly positive response (2) for all sufficiently small
nonzero t on each such finite path. It does not say the response is nonzero
at every time, nor give a lower bound uniform in d.

In the usual straight native path layout, matter edges join virtual vertices
j and j+1, while sacrificial leaf targets sit at physical (2j,1,0). The
first and x-th leaf targets are separated by physical distance 2d. Choosing
identical supplied target program Records across the two first-outcome
histories and adding no intervening Record leaves the later target's full
nearest-neighbor Record shell identical. Blank physical sites on that shell
do not report the changed matter covariance. Thus (2) is a local-condition
collision for the explicit historywise conditional-law target whenever its
right side is positive. The source's bounded-support native gates have not
thereby been compiled into a fundamental nearest-neighbor dynamical law.

## 4. Infinite uniform comparator, derived directly

This section is a mathematical propagation comparator, not an infinite
native-carrier or physical-time construction. On l2(Z), let h=J(S+S^dagger),
J real, with S the bilateral shift. Since S and S^dagger commute and are
bounded, their exponential series multiply absolutely in operator norm.
The coefficient from 0 to d >= 0 is

    u_d0(t) = (-i)^d sum_(n>=0)
                (-1)^n (Jt)^(d+2n) / [n!(n+d)!].  (5)

For Jt<0 the expected overall sign of the amplitude changes; the following
bounds depend only on b=|Jt|. Formula (5) is also (-i)^d J_d(2Jt), where
J_d is the Bessel function defined by this convergent series. No asymptotic
formula or experimentally fitted constant is imported.

Since d!/(d+n)! <= (d+1)^(-n), the absolute series gives

    |u_d0(t)| <= min(1, b^d/d! exp[b^2/(d+1)]).     (6)

The bound by 1 follows independently from unitarity. If b^2<d+1, consecutive
unsigned terms in (5) have ratio b^2/[(n+1)(n+d+1)]<1. The alternating-series
remainder then gives the strictly positive lower bound

    |u_d0(t)| >= b^d/d! [1-b^2/(d+1)] > 0          (7)

for b>0. In particular 0<b<1 gives a nonzero tail at every finite d.
These tails are compatible with rapidly decaying propagation bounds. An
effective cone does not imply exact vanishing outside it.

For completeness, approximate the infinite path by its restriction to
[-L,L], with |d|<=L. Walk coefficients agree through all powers k<N,
N=2(L+1)-|d|, because a walk must first leave the interval and then return
to d to be omitted. Both operators have norm at most 2|J|, so

    |(u_L)_d0-u_d0|
       <= 2 exp(2b) (2b)^N/N!.                    (8)

This is a conservative sum-of-two-tails bound, using
sum_(k>=N) z^k/k! <= z^N exp(z)/N!. Thus the mathematical finite-path
propagators converge at fixed d,t. No infinite physical preparation or
infinite scheduled sequence follows from this estimate.

## 5. Exact price of discarding the remote outcome

Fix a later target and two positive-mass histories with the same local
Record condition. Suppose their target Bernoulli parameters are p_0,p_1,
with Delta=|p_0-p_1| from (2). Any one local parameter q has

    max_a TV(Bern(p_a),Bern(q)) >= Delta/2.          (9)

The triangle inequality proves the lower bound; q=(p_0+p_1)/2 attains it.
If retaining the true first-event weights z_0,z_1 and replacing only the
second conditional law by a common q, the complete two-event joint-law TV
error is z_0|p_0-q|+z_1|p_1-q|. Its minimum is

    min(z_0,z_1) Delta.                            (10)

To see this, outside the interval between p_0 and p_1 both summands improve
by moving inward. Inside it the cost is affine and minimized at the more
heavily weighted endpoint; equal weights allow any point in the interval.

Combining (6) with (2) bounds the minimax conditional error above by

    (1-r_x^2) kappa /2
       * min(1, b^(2d)/(d!)^2 exp[2b^2/(d+1)]).    (11)

The same prefactor times the square of (7) is a lower bound when that
inequality applies. This prices exactly one omitted earlier outcome in the
specified family. It is not yet a uniform approximation theorem for all
adaptive histories: selective normalization can amplify small differences.

No relay-time or communication-speed axiom has been assumed. An argument
that distance D requires D-1 intervening writes would additionally need a
locally causal occurrence/scheduling rule; nearest-neighbor content alone
does not establish that rule. Global joint-history coupling, messages,
altered schedules, alternate state families and changed instruments remain
live constructions. Equations (9)-(10) concern matching conditional laws
at both supported histories, not all possible readings of the axioms.

## 6. General Gaussian history: four explicit updates

Restrict now to gauge-invariant quasifree (Gaussian) matter states, meaning
all normally ordered moments are Wick determinants of C and number-changing
moments vanish. This restriction excludes generic interacting matter states.
Write j for the active matter mode, P=|j><j|, a=1-r^2, D=I+(r-1)P.

For an initially empty fresh leaf, the matter Kraus operations, up to phases
irrelevant to an even density, are K_same=r^(n_j) and K_flip=sqrt(a)c_j.
Their probabilities and normalized covariance updates are

    Z_same = 1-a C_jj,
    C_same = D [C + (a/Z_same) C P C] D;            (12)

    Z_flip = a C_jj,
    C_flip = C - C P C / C_jj.                     (13)

Only positive-probability branches are assigned conditional states. Thus
(13) is used only when a>0 and C_jj>0, and (12) only when Z_same>0.

For an initially filled fresh leaf, set Q=I-C. The hole version gives

    Z_same = 1-a Q_jj,
    C_same = I - D [Q + (a/Z_same) Q P Q] D;        (14)

    Z_flip = a Q_jj,
    C_flip = C + Q P Q / Q_jj.                     (15)

The hole covariance in an explicit particle-hole CAR convention is Q^T;
transposing its update yields (14)-(15), because P and D are real diagonal.
This detail avoids assuming a real matter covariance. Empty flips leave
mode j empty; filled flips leave it full. These operations add or remove a
matter particle at the expense of the supplied leaf, as the original pulse
conserves the combined matter/leaf number.

## 7. Proof of Gaussian closure and the updates

For 0<C<I, write X=C(I-C)^(-1), a positive matrix. On fermionic Fock space,
let Gamma(X)=direct-sum_k wedge^k X. In a unitary eigenbasis of X,
Gamma(X) has occupation weights product of the selected eigenvalues. Hence

    rho = Gamma(X)/det(I+X),
    Tr Gamma(X)=det(I+X),
    C = X(I+X)^(-1).

Exterior powers also give Gamma(A)Gamma(B)=Gamma(AB). The same-outcome
Kraus r^(n_j) is Gamma(D). Its output is proportional to Gamma(DXD), so
is Gaussian. Its covariance is DXD(I+DXD)^(-1). Apply the rank-one matrix
inverse identity with D^2=I-aP, or multiply out the proposed inverse, to
obtain (12). Its normalizer is Tr rho(I-a n_j)=1-a C_jj. This proves the
formula for nonsingular C and r>0. Approximate any boundary C by
(1-2epsilon)C+epsilon I and r=0 by positive r; in finite dimension, density
matrices, their unnormalized CP images and Wick moments are continuous.
Division is continuous at every branch whose limiting probability is
positive. Thus the closure and formula extend to all stated boundaries.

For the empty flip, take any equal-length row/column index lists I,J that
avoid j. Wick's identity gives the normalized poststate normal moment as
the determinant with the extra row/column j, divided by C_jj. Its Schur
complement is exactly det[(C-CPC/C_jj)_(I,J)]. The poststate has mode j
empty because c_j^2=0, so all normal moments involving that mode vanish.
Number-changing moments remain zero. Normal monomials span the operator
algebra; these moments prove that the entire output density is the Gaussian
state with covariance (13), not merely that two-point functions agree.
The filled case follows by particle-hole substitution with the transpose
just stated. These are finite-dimensional algebraic identities.

Consequently, arbitrary finite alternating sequences of supplied quadratic
dwells and fresh sharp-leaf events preserve this Gaussian state family on
each supported complete history. Recursively multiply the four Z factors
to obtain its complete probability. No postselection-only success ledger
or exponential table of all histories is required to evaluate one history.
Dense covariance storage is O(m^2); a rank-one leaf update takes O(m^2)
arithmetic operations, and a dense supplied-unitary update O(m^3). This is
an arithmetic-operation statement, not a uniform bit-precision bound near
rare branches, nor an implementation using only local physical Records.

The general efficient simulation and Gaussian-closure machinery are
established fermionic linear optics mathematics; see Sergey Bravyi,
[Lagrangian representation for fermionic linear optics](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0404180).
The purpose here is to derive the particular permanent-leaf interface and
its local-history response with its supplied assumptions visible. No claim
of novelty for Gaussian covariance propagation is made.

## 8. Next decisive checks and residual

Compare (12)-(15) with direct small Fock density matrices for complex,
off-diagonal covariances and every positive branch. Check full Wick moments,
not just occupations, and preserve null branches explicitly. Challenge the
transpose, post-flip occupation and branch normalizers. Check (4) from path
walk powers and (5) from a separate shift coefficient count. Computation
is a countercheck of the preceding derivations, not their general proof.

The leading open construction is now clear: can an actual renewed formation
mechanism provide the required local conditional statistic while preserving
earlier Records and continuing noncommuting matter? A global O(m^2) state
calculator does not answer that physical question. One can instead weaken
the matching target to global coupled marginals, restrict the live state
family, or explicitly transport classical sufficient information. The next
block must test these alternatives before treating an interface mismatch
as pressure on an axiom.

## 9. Conditioning stability and why rare histories need separate control

The covariance recursions also show exactly where a uniform approximation
argument would need another hypothesis. Let C and C_tilde be physical
covariances with operator-norm difference delta. For the empty same branch,
suppose both normalizers Z, Z_tilde are at least gamma>0. Since
||CPC-C_tilde P C_tilde|| <= 2 delta and |Z-Z_tilde| <= a delta, (12) gives

    ||C_same-C_tilde_same|| <= (1+a/gamma)^2 delta.  (16)

Here ||D||<=1. The same inequality applies to the filled same branch by
holes. For an empty flip with C_jj,C_tilde_jj >= eta>0, (13) gives

    ||C_flip-C_tilde_flip|| <= (1+1/eta)^2 delta.   (17)

The filled flip uses the analogous lower bound on hole occupation. These
are conservative bounds obtained by adding/subtracting the same numerator
and using the scalar inverse difference. A branch-probability floor gamma
on a flip implies eta=gamma/a when a>0. Quadratic dwell preserves the norm
of covariance differences. Composing (16)-(17) gives an explicit product
bound along any fixed supported event string satisfying the stated floors.
It may be large and is not an optimal stability constant.

There is no state-uniform Lipschitz constant when rare branches are allowed.
For two modes and 0<epsilon<1, compare the physical Gaussian covariances

    C = [[epsilon, sqrt(epsilon(1-epsilon))],
         [sqrt(epsilon(1-epsilon)), 1-epsilon]],
    C_tilde = diag(epsilon,1-epsilon).

The first is the projector of a one-particle Slater state; the second is a
product mixed Gaussian state. Their distance is sqrt(epsilon(1-epsilon)),
which tends to zero. At mode 0, an empty-leaf flip has the SAME positive
probability a epsilon for both when a>0. Formula (13) sends C to zero and
C_tilde to diag(0,1-epsilon), so their conditional output distance tends
to one. Thus small covariance error and equal event mass do not by
themselves control every normalized poststate. This example concerns the
general Gaussian input domain; it is not asserted to be a pair reachable
from the campaign's fixed ready state under its original schedule.

For complete unconditioned history laws a different bound can be stronger:
any fixed sequence of CP instruments with recorded outcomes is a quantum
channel to a classical output, so its total variation distance is bounded
by input trace distance. This does not control a rare conditional branch
or convert a covariance truncation into a trace-distance estimate. Each
of those conversions must be proved for the proposed approximation.
