## The order of operations

Differentiate the Euclidean two-point functions at external momentum zero
with a positive bare regulator mass m and a=w=1. Then take m to zero. Write
Delta_f=z_s-z_t and Delta_gamma=z_B-z_E, where Z=1+e^2 z. The photon
coefficient includes one four-component Dirac field with its filled negative
band. The literal spatial-link contact is included. There is no temporal
lattice direction and no clover term.

These limits also define anisotropic local matching coefficients relative to
a common O(4)-invariant continuum infrared subtraction. To see the equivalence,
choose a smooth radial function chi(nu^2+|k|^2) supported in a small ball
strictly inside the spatial Brillouin zone, equal to one near the origin.
Subtract the continuum QED derivative integrands multiplied by the SAME chi
from the lattice derivative integrands, at each positive m. The temporal and
spatial continuum contributions agree after four-dimensional angular
integration, so their differences vanish. Only the subtracted lattice
remainder contributes to either Delta.

This subtraction permits a uniform m->0 limit. In the small ball set
rho=(nu^2+|k|^2+m^2)^(1/2) and rho0=(nu^2+|k|^2)^(1/2). The Wilson Dirac
operator differs from its continuum counterpart by O(|k|^2); the sine
correction is O(|k|^3). Free propagators obey ||S||<=C/rho and
||S-S_cont||<=C |k|^2/rho^2, by the resolvent identity. A spatial derivative
of D differs from its continuum value by O(|k|), and a derivative of a link
vertex is bounded. The lattice photon denominator differs from rho0^2 by
O(|k|^4), with both denominators bounded below by c rho0^2. Consequently a
subtracted fermion first-derivative integrand is bounded by C/rho0^3:
terms contain at worst (|k|/rho^2+|k|^2/rho^3)/rho0^2 or
1/(rho rho0^2), plus the bounded-relative photon-denominator correction.
The link contact is no more singular than C/rho0^2. Each is integrable in
four dimensions, uniformly for m>=0.

For the photon second-derivative kernel the potentially logarithmic
continuum term has four fermion propagator powers. Replacing one propagator
by its O(|k|^2/rho^2) difference or one constant vertex/Dirac derivative by
its lattice correction similarly gives C/rho0^3 or better. Terms with a
second Dirac derivative have fewer propagators and obey the same bound.
The transverse static contact is independent of the external transverse
momentum, and the temporal contact has no external-frequency dependence.
Thus dominated convergence applies to the subtracted derivative differences.
Away from the ball the spatial Wilson operator is gapped, including at the
other corners; the continuous-frequency tails are integrable. These bounds
justify the regulator limit, not arbitrary exchanges of unsubtracted
conditionally convergent integrals.

## Fermion conversion term

After frequency integration the continuum part of Delta_f, with k=|k|,
E=(k^2+m^2)^(1/2), is

    f_cont=4 I1-(16 k^2/3+4 m^2) I2,
    I1=1/[2 E k(E+k)],
    I2=(2E+k)/[4 E^3 k(E+k)^2].

It vanishes pointwise at m=0 for k>0, but its radial integral does not tend
to zero. Set k=m sinh u and z=exp(-u). Including the radial k^2 factor,
the remaining dimensionless integral is

    integral_0^1 z(z-1)^2(z+1)^2(z^2+2)/[3(z^2+1)^2] dz = 1/12.

The spatial angular measure is 1/(2 pi^2); the conversion is therefore
1/(24 pi^2). This converts the frequency-first, exactly massless integral
into the stated fixed-m derivative limit. It is not a physical anisotropy
generated by Lorentz-invariant infrared physics.

The massless lattice density is absolutely integrable after the cancellation
within Delta_f. Tensor cubature with the cube-sector substitution gives

    integral f_lattice(k,0) = -0.1369980507815366,
    lim Delta_f = integral f_lattice(k,0)+1/(24 pi^2)
                = -0.1327763347964392.

The displayed decimals are numerical convergence estimates, not rigorous
interval enclosures. The conversion term is analytic.

## Photon spherical subtraction and conversion

For the integration-by-parts representation of Delta_gamma in the
preliminary note, its exactly massless density has leading term

    H(n)/|k|^3,    H(n)=(5 sum_i n_i^4-3)/12.

The spherical mean is zero since <sum n_i^4>=3/5. Its absolute integral is
not finite. Define the massless integral by removing a SPHERICAL small ball,
or equivalently by the O(4)-covariant subtraction above and its explicitly
calculated conversion. A cubical deletion without a conversion is a
different prescription.

Use cubic symmetry to partition the positive octant into three sectors and
write k=(t,tu,tv), 0<t<pi, 0<u,v<1. Put R=(1+u^2+v^2)^(1/2),
n=(1,u,v)/R, h=H(n)/R^3. The spherical small-ball prescription becomes

    I_sph = 3/pi^3 integral_0^1 du dv {
       integral_0^pi [t^2 f_gamma(t,tu,tv,0)-h/t] dt + h log R }.

The h log R term comes from the lower boundary t=epsilon/R. The log pi and
log epsilon terms integrate to zero because the sector integral of h
vanishes. The remaining radial integrand is integrable; symmetry makes the
next homogeneous correction less singular. This states precisely which
conditionally convergent integral the quadrature evaluates.

At finite m, the angular average of the leading continuum difference is

    m^2 k^2/[6(k^2+m^2)^(7/2)].

Its spatial radial integral is 1/(60 pi^2), since
integral_0^infinity t^4/(1+t^2)^(7/2) dt=1/5. Thus

    I_sph = 0.0101686257958615,
    lim Delta_gamma = I_sph+1/(60 pi^2)
                    = 0.0118573121899005.

The author cubature orders16,24,32,48,64 agree at about1e-14; the integrated
h control is below1.2e-17. Positive-m calculations down to3.2e-6 approach
the same limits. Those observations estimate numerical error only.

## Gauge and interpretation

The photon coefficients at this order have no internal photon propagator.
For the fermion longitudinal propagator variation, the exact link Ward
identity and the contact yield, after odd internal terms are integrated,

    delta_xi D_eff(p)/e^2 = (xi-1) [D(p) I0-D(p) J(p) D(p)],
    I0=integral 1/(K^2)^2,
    J(p)=integral S(p-k)/(K^2)^2.

These two terms must share an infrared regulator; neither is separately
asserted finite. In the region outside a small infrared ball, at m=0 the
second term starts at quadratic external momentum; the first is a common
multiple of i gamma_mu p_mu. Inside the ball subtract the same covariant
continuum kernel used above. Its linear contribution is O(4) covariant;
the lattice-minus-continuum difference has the integrable bound already
given, and its longitudinal hard linear part is again isotropic. Hence
the massless-limit Delta_f is independent of xi. At finite positive m,
individual off-shell derivatives need not be gauge independent. This
argument concerns the finite anisotropic linear matching coefficient,
not arbitrary off-shell Green functions.

The combination Delta_f-Delta_gamma/2 is approximately
-0.1387049908913894 in this bare a=w=1 convention. It is the order-e^2
coefficient relating the fermion and photon LOCAL dimension-four kinetic
terms after a common normalization. Identifying it with an observed limiting
speed still requires critical-mass tuning, the specified bare-speed choice,
a pole/renormalization prescription and control of nonlocal infrared terms.
The known logarithmic velocity flow on current main is not rederived here.
If the earlier tree curve a^2=1-m_bare is deliberately preserved through
the critical-mass subtraction, its induced order-e^2 bare-speed change must
be added explicitly; it is not implicit in the two integrals above.

No empirical bound, likelihood, parameter-counting penalty or unique-law
inference follows from these matching constants alone.
