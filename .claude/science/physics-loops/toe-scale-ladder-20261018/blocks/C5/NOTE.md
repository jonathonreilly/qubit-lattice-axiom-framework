# C5 — Finite-range walks on Z^3 carry no net Weyl chirality: W3 = 0 for every strictly local tick

**Block:** C5 of `toe-scale-ladder-20261018` (target: SCALE_LADDER_MAP.md, "C5").
**Date:** 2026-10-18. **Status:** worker output, unaudited. Adds no axiom, primitive or dynamics clause.
**Outcome class:** obstruction theorem (proved conditional on one standard algebraic K-theory
input, flagged as the load-bearing step), plus a sharp-boundary witness. No covariant walk with W3 = ±1
exists in the target class. The reason is strict finite range. Covariance, coin size and the soldering
action play no role.

## Result up front

Let `U: T^3 -> U(n)` be a translation-invariant walk with **finite range**: `U(k) = sum_{x in S} A_x e^{ik.x}`
with S a finite subset of Z^3. Any n, any stencil, any coin, any symmetry or none. Write
`W3[U] = (1/24 pi^2) ∫ tr (U^{-1}dU)^3` (convention in §0).

**Answer to the C5 question.** No D2-, D3-, D4- or O-covariant one-qubit walk, and no O-covariant
two-qubit walk inside or outside the Clifford family, has W3 ≠ 0, at any finite range. This is Theorem K, and
it needs no covariance. A net-chirality (single Weyl node) tick needs a unitary step of **infinite
range**. Exponentially decaying (analytic) hops suffice, as the O-covariant V_2 shows. Such a step is
not the time evolution of any continuous Bloch Hamiltonian (H), so it is a new kind of dynamics premise,
not a refinement of the existing walk class.

## 0. Conventions and premises

- **Walk (supplied premise, as in the parent notes).** `U(k) = sum_x A_x e^{ik.x}`, finitely many x,
  `U(k)U(k)^dag = 1`. Covariance under `G <= O` with the full-soldered coin action means
  `U(Rk) = D(R) U(k) D(R)^dag` (`docs/CUBIC_COVARIANCE_FREEZES_A_ONE_QUBIT_TICK_CELL_CLASSIFICATION_BOUNDED_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-10-16.md`,
  "Premises"). On a two-qubit site the action is `1 (x) D(R)`
  (`docs/TWO_QUBIT_SITE_UNFREEZES_THE_CUBIC_TICK_DOUBLER_FREE_DIRAC_WALK_BOUNDED_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-10-16.md`,
  Theorem 1). Theorem K uses none of this.
- **Winding.** With `A_a = U^{-1} ∂_a U`, `W3 = (1/24π²) ∫_{[0,2π)^3} Σ ε_abc tr(A_a A_b A_c) d³k`.
  Under this orientation (d k1 d k2 d k3) the positive control V_2 has W3 = -1 (s1 log, "c = -1"). The
  sign convention is fixed once and used throughout.
- **Net chirality.** That W3 equals the net Weyl chirality of the quasienergy bands is the
  Higashikawa–Nakagawa–Ueda identification (PRL 123, 066403 (2019); arXiv:1806.06868). It is **cited, not
  re-derived** here, except for the one-qubit sheet statement N, which is proved directly. The paper's text

## 1. Lemma Q — exact lattice integration (certificate used by the runners)

If U has range r per axis and is unitary, then `U^{-1} = U^dag` has range r, `∂_a U` has range r,
and `Σ ε tr(A_a A_b A_c)` is a trigonometric polynomial of degree ≤ 6r per axis. The N-point periodic
rule integrates `e^{imk}` exactly for |m| < N. So for N ≥ 6r+1 the grid sum equals W3 up to
floating-point rounding, and |sum| < 1/2 certifies the integer exactly. Runners use N = 6r+2 and a
second N = 6r+5. s3 checks the Fourier support of the integrand directly on two instances.

## 2. Proposition A — shift–coin products (elementary)

(i) *Additivity (Polyakov–Wiegmann).* For maps g, h from a closed oriented 3-manifold into GL_n,
`tr((gh)^{-1}d(gh))^3 = tr(g^{-1}dg)^3 + tr(h^{-1}dh)^3 + 3 d tr(g^{-1}dg ∧ dh h^{-1})`, so
W3[gh] = W3[g] + W3[h]. (ii) A constant has W3 = 0. (iii) A generalized conditional shift
`S = Σ_j P_j e^{ik.a_j}` is `C diag(e^{ik.a_1},…) C^{-1}` with C constant, and conjugation by a
constant leaves the form unchanged. For a diagonal map the form is `Σ_j (i da_j·dk)^3 = 0`, since a
scalar 1-form wedged with itself vanishes. Hence every product of coins and such shifts has W3 = 0.
The same argument gives W3 = 0 for any factor that depends on k only through one or two linear
combinations (it factors through T^1 or T^2). This covers the D2 product walk `q_x q_y q_z` of the
cubic-freeze note, its D3 and D4 shifts, split-step walks, and every "conditional shift + coin"
design. ∎

*Scalar phases.* For a scalar 1-form α and a matrix 1-form B, `tr((α+B)^3) = tr(B^3) + 3 α∧tr(B∧B)`,
and `tr(B∧B) = Σ B_ij∧B_ji = 0`. So `W3[λU] = W3[U]` for any scalar λ: T³ → U(1). For n = 2 this
settles the determinant-phase question. `det U = c e^{im.k}` (the cubic-freeze note, Theorem 0), and
W3[U] equals the degree of the SU(2) part. That part is defined on T³ when m = 0, which holds for every
G without fixed vectors (O, T, D4, D3, D2). Otherwise it is defined up to sign, and the 3-form is still
well defined. (s1 checks `e^{i(k1+2k3)}V` numerically.)

## 3. Lemma C — the two-qubit Clifford family

The task statement called the image "S⁴-like". In fact `(f, h1, h2, g1, g2, g3)` is a unit vector in R⁶.
So the image lies in `Phi(S^5)`, with `Phi(x) = x0 + i Σ_{j=1}^5 x_j Γ_j` and
`Γ = (τ1, τ2, τ3σx, τ3σy, τ3σz)` pairwise anticommuting Hermitian involutions. This matches the remark
after Theorem 6 of the two-qubit note.

**Lemma C.** `Phi^* tr(θ^3) = 0` identically on S⁵. Hence W3[Phi ∘ x] = 0 for every continuous
`x: T^3 -> S^5`, and every member of the family has W3 = 0.

*Proof 1 (exact certificate, s2).* All 20 components `T_ijl(x)` of the antisymmetrized
`tr(Φ^dag ∂_iΦ Φ^dag ∂_jΦ Φ^dag ∂_lΦ)` vanish as polynomials over Q(i). The same routine gives a
nonzero component (12) for `S^3 -> SU(2)`, so the zero is not an artefact.

*Proof 2 (by hand).* For matrix 1-forms, transposition reverses products, and the reordering of three
odd forms gives `tr((θ^T)^3) = -tr(θ^3)`, where `(g^T)^{-1}d(g^T) = (dg g^{-1})^T`, which is conjugate to
`(g^{-1}dg)^T`. The transposes `Γ_j^T` again form a Clifford system. The two irreducible 4-dimensional
representations of Cl(5) differ in the scalar `Γ1Γ2Γ3Γ4Γ5`. Under transposition this product becomes the
product in reversed order, which equals the original product because reversing five anticommuting
factors gives the sign `(-1)^{10} = +1`. So `Γ_j^T = C Γ_j C^{-1}` for one constant C, and
`Φ(x)^T = C Φ(x) C^{-1}`. Then `Φ^*ω = (Φ^T)^*ω = -Φ^*ω` for `ω = tr θ^3`, so `Φ^*ω = 0`. For Cl(3)
(SU(2)) the reversal sign is `(-1)^3 = -1`. Transposition then corresponds to `σ -> -σ`, and the
argument fails, as it must. ∎

(The weaker `H^3(S^5) = 0` argument of the two-qubit note gives the same conclusion for the integral.
Any map into `S^m` with `m ≥ 4` is null-homotopic on a 3-complex, which gives a third proof.) Lemma C is
not specific to cubic symmetry or to finite range. The "chiral" deformation of the Clifford mechanism
would need a Clifford triple with no partner, i.e. the S³ case. That case is the one-qubit problem
again, where Theorem K applies.

## 4. Proposition E — equivariance and the covariant positive control

*Transformation rule.* For a constant unitary D and `R ∈ GL_3(Z)`, `W3[k -> D U(R^{-1}k) D^dag] = det(R) W3[U]`.
Conjugation leaves the form invariant, and the change of variables gives the Jacobian sign. So
proper rotations give no constraint, and a parity element with any coin action would force W3 = 0
(s1: inversion flips the sign of W3 numerically; a rotation plus coin conjugation preserves it).

*No topological obstruction from O.* `V_M = (f + i g.σ)/|(f,g)|` with `f = M - Σ cos k_a` (O-invariant)
and `g = (sin k_a)` (O-equivariant, `v_0` of the parent notes) is exactly O-covariant
(`U(Rk) = D(R)U(k)D(R)^dag`, s1, all 24 elements, error 5e-16). It is real-analytic, so its hops decay
exponentially, but it is **not finite range**: `|(f,g)|^{-1}` is not a Laurent polynomial.

*Exact degree (preimage count).* `V_M = ±1` iff `g = 0`, i.e. at the 8 TRIM points p, with
`V = sign f(p)`. Near p, `g ≈ diag(cos p_a)(k - p)`, with chirality `χ_p = Π_a sign cos p_a`. In the
chart around ±1 by the g-coordinates, the orientation of S³ carries the sign ±1 (outward normal ±e_0).
So with one global convention sign c, `W3 = c Σ_{p: f(p)<0} χ_p = -c Σ_{p: f(p)>0} χ_p`, using
`Σ_all χ_p = 0`. This gives `W3[V_M] = c·(0, 1, -2, 1, 0)` on `M ∈ (3,∞), (1,3), (-1,1), (-3,-1), (-∞,-3)`.
The integral gives c = -1 and confirms all five values to 1e-11 (s1: N = 16…96, converging spectrally).

*Bands of V_2 (the requested band/node report, for the infinite-range control only).* The quasienergies
are `±ω(k)` with `cos ω = f/|(f,g)|`. All 8 TRIM are Weyl points. k = 0 sits at quasienergy π with
χ = +1. The other seven sit at quasienergy 0 with χ = -1 (three axis points), +1 (three face points) and
-1 (corner), so the 0-sheet sum is -1 and the π-sheet sum is +1. The net chirality is carried by the
single G-fixed π-node at k = 0. This is the "single net Weyl node" structure the target asked for,
realized only with infinite range.

(Equivariant degree bookkeeping, for orientation: preimages of a regular value fall into G-orbits, and an
orbit with stabilizer H contributes `|G:H|` times one local degree. Free orbits contribute multiples of
|G|, and G-fixed TRIM can contribute ±1. So for O no congruence beyond this forces W3 = 0, consistent with
V_2.)

## 5. Theorem K — the obstruction

**Theorem K.** Let `R = C[z1^±, z2^±, z3^±]` (`z_a = e^{ik_a}`) and `M ∈ GL_n(R)`. Then `W3[M] = 0`. In
particular every finite-range unitary walk on Z³ (`U^{-1} = U^dag ∈ M_n(R)`) has W3 = 0.

*Proof.*
1. W3 extends to continuous maps `T^3 -> GL_N(C)`. `tr(g^{-1}dg)^3` is closed on `GL_N(C)` (the usual
   computation `d tr θ³ = -tr θ⁴ = 0`). So W3 is a homotopy invariant of maps from the closed
   manifold T³. It is additive under products (Prop. A(i)), invariant under `M -> M ⊕ 1`, and zero on
   constants and on maps with a single nonconstant scalar diagonal entry.
2. **(Load-bearing input.)** For a regular Noetherian ring A, `K_1(A[t,t^{-1}]) ≅ K_1(A) ⊕ K_0(A)`, with
   the second summand embedded by `[P] -> [tP + 1 - P]` (Bass–Heller–Swan 1964 for K_1; Quillen's
   fundamental theorem 1973). Laurent polynomial rings over C are regular, and
   `K_0(C[Z^d]) = Z` (the same theorem in degree 0 with `K_{-1}` of regular rings zero, or Swan's
   Laurent extension of Quillen–Suslin). By induction, `K_1(C[Z^3]) = C^* ⊕ Z^3`, generated by constants
   and `z_1, z_2, z_3`. This equals the unit group `R^× = C^* × z^{Z^3}`, mapped in by
   `u -> [diag(u,1,…)]`. Equivalently, `SK_1(R) = 0`.
3. So `[M] = [diag(det M, 1, …, 1)]` in `K_1(R) = GL(R)/E(R)`. There are N and elementary matrices
   `E_1 … E_s` (each `1 + p e_ij`, `i ≠ j`, `p ∈ R`) with `M ⊕ 1_{N-n} = diag(det M, 1, …, 1)·E_1⋯E_s`.
4. Each `E_j` is null-homotopic through `1 + t p e_ij`, `t ∈ [0,1]`, and these are invertible for all t
   and k. So `W3[E_j] = 0`. `det M = c z^m` is a unit monomial, and `W3[diag(c z^m, 1, …)] = 0` by
   step 1.
5. By additivity, `W3[M] = W3[M ⊕ 1] = 0 + Σ_j W3[E_j] = 0`. ∎

*Remarks.* (a) The same argument kills every odd winding invariant beyond degree one on every
subtorus, in any dimension d. Since `K_1(C[Z^d]) = C^* ⊕ Z^d`, a finite-range walk's topological K¹
class is that of a translation (its determinant winding). This is the single-particle shadow of the
1D GNVW index being the only invariant. (b) Unitarity matters only through the polynomial inverse.
s4's control shows that the Fourier truncation of V_2, a Laurent polynomial matrix that is
**invertible but not unitary** (its inverse is not finite range), carries W3 = -1. (c) The K_0 version
of the same algebra is the theorem that strictly local projectors have zero Chern number (Dubail–Read,
PRB 92, 205307 (2015), proved there by other means). The two statements are siblings.

**Corollary D (distance and defect).** (i) If U is finite-range unitary and V is continuous with
`W3[V] ≠ 0`, then `sup_k ||U(k)-V(k)|| ≥ 1`. Otherwise `(1-t)U + tV = U(1 + t U^dag(V-U))` is invertible
for all t, which would give W3[U] = W3[V]. (ii) For fixed r, let `X_r` be the range-r Laurent matrices.
If range-r matrices `M_j` with `W3[M_j] ≠ 0` had unitarity defect `sup||M_j M_j^dag - 1|| -> 0`, their
coefficients would be bounded. A subsequence would converge uniformly to a unitary range-r U, and the
`M_j` would be invertible and uniformly close to U, so W3[U] ≠ 0, contradicting K. So
`eps_r := inf{defect : W3 ≠ 0} > 0`. Fourier truncations of V_2 show `eps_r -> 0` as `r -> ∞`. (s4 finds
range-1 matrices with W3 = -1 and defect 6.5e-3, an upper bound on eps_1. This is numerical.)

**Corollary N (per-sheet Nielsen–Ninomiya for one-qubit ticks).** Let U be a finite-range one-qubit walk
with `det U` constant (automatic under any G without fixed vectors, cubic-freeze note Thm 0). Write
`V = U/√det U = f + i g.σ`, and suppose ±1 are regular values of V (nondegenerate Weyl points). Then by
the chart-orientation count of §4, `0 = W3 = c Σ_{π-nodes} χ_p = -c Σ_{0-nodes} χ_p`. The Weyl points at
quasienergy 0 have zero net chirality, and so do those at π. The Floquet "π-escape" of
Nielsen–Ninomiya is unavailable to finite-range ticks. For degenerate nodes, replace χ_p by local
degrees.

**Corollary H.** If `U(k) = T exp(-i ∫_0^T H(k,t)dt)` with H continuous on `T^3 × [0,T]` (e.g. any
finite- or quasi-local-range drive with continuous Bloch Hamiltonian), then `s -> U_s(k)`, the evolution
up to time s, is a homotopy from 1, so W3 = 0. A nonzero-W3 Floquet operator, such as the one HNU
describe, therefore needs a Bloch Hamiltonian that is discontinuous on T³ (long-range, SLAC-type
hopping) or is not Hamiltonian-generated. *I could not read the HNU paper to check how they evade this.
This consequence is mine and is recorded as a question for the reviewer, not as a criticism of the
paper.*

## 6. Answers to the three tasks

1. **Clifford lemma.** Proved as Lemma C, pointwise. The image is S⁵, not S⁴; the needed fact is
   `π_3(S^5) = 0` or `H^3(S^5) = 0`. Numerically, six random smooth maps `T^3 -> S^5` give `|W3| ≤ 2e-17`.
   The W3 = -1 one-qubit control embedded as `x = (f,0,0,g)` splits into `V ⊕ V^dag` and gives 0. The
   explicit O-covariant member b* gives exact-grid W3 = 0 (N = 8, 13).
2. **Equivariance.** Proper rotations and coin conjugation preserve W3, so there is no sign obstruction.
   Covariance itself never forces W3 = 0: the O-covariant V_M realize -1 and +2. The vanishing is
   forced by finite range alone (Theorem K). In particular:
   - the O- and T-frozen walks (constants) and the D4 and D3 shifts trivially have W3 = 0;
   - the D2 product walk and every C3/C4/D2 walk, at any range, have W3 = 0;
   - longer range does not escape (K has no range hypothesis), and neither does a larger coin.
   For n = 2 the determinant phase drops out (Prop. A, scalar phases).
3. **Constructive search.** Superseded by K. It was run anyway as a falsification test, and no
   counterexample appeared:
   - s3: 21 walks, shift–coin products for n = 2, 3, 4 up to range 5, Clifford maps and the covariant
     D2/D3/D4 walks. All have exact-grid W3 = 0 and unit-monomial determinant, and preimage-count
     degree 0 for 3 random regular values each.
   - s5: unitaries solved for directly, with no structure imposed.
   - s4: adversarial unconstrained fits to V_2 at ranges 1–2. They keep W3 = -1 only while the unitarity
     defect stays positive.

## 8. Scope and what this does not do

- **Single-particle (quasi-free) only.** Theorem K is about one-particle Bloch unitaries. It says nothing
  about interacting QCAs. Nontrivial 3D (Clifford/fermionic) QCAs exist (Haah; Fidkowski–Haah–Hastings),
  and many-body chiral anomalies are a separate question.
- **Translation-invariant, one-site unit cell.** Larger unit cells reduce to larger n, so they are
  covered. Disordered or non-translation-invariant walks are not treated, though a coarse index version is
  plausible.
- **Strict finite range.** Quasi-local (exponentially decaying) walks escape, as the covariant V_2 shows.
  Whether the framework may supply such a step is an owner-level premise question. This note does not
  adopt one.
- It does not re-derive the HNU identity "W3 = net chirality" for general n. Only the one-qubit
  per-sheet form (Corollary N) is proved here.

  measurement-based dynamics are not, and the boundary is explicit.

## 11. Load-bearing step needing independent check

Step 2 of Theorem K: `K_1(C[z1^±,z2^±,z3^±]) = C^* ⊕ Z^3`, i.e. `SK_1 = 0`, together with the passage
from `[M] = [det M]` in `K_1` to a factorization `M ⊕ 1 = diag(det M,1,…)·∏ elementary`. Recommended

That module is finitely generated projective over R2, hence free by Swan–Quillen–Suslin. This is
sketched only; a reviewer should verify it.
