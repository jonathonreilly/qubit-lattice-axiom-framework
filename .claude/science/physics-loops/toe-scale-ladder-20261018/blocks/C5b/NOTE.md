# C5b: no chiral fermion with its anomaly partner at the cutoff. A finite-range walk balances Weyl chirality at every clean quasienergy, for any number of internal states

**Block:** C5b of `toe-scale-ladder-20261018`. Follows C5 (Theorem K) and C7.

## Result up front

Let `U: T^3 -> U(n)` be smooth and let `eps` be a quasienergy. Eigenvectors satisfy `U psi = e^{-iE} psi`.
The **level set** is `F_eps = {k : e^{-i eps} in spec U(k)}`. Call the level **clean** if `F_eps` is finite.
Then every point of `F_eps` is isolated: a band touching at quasienergy eps, or a non-degenerate isolated extremum of a single band (charge 0). No band crosses eps along a surface.

**Physics in one line.** A finite-range unitary walk is like a static lattice Hamiltonian in this respect: at every
quasienergy, the Berry flux of the states there (nodes and Fermi surfaces) sums to zero. The Floquet "π-escape", with
the anomaly partner at the cutoff `π/a`, requires `W3 ≠ 0`. That requires infinite range (C5, Theorem K), or interactions, or a
broken translation invariance. The C5 positive control `V_2` is exactly O-covariant and has exponentially decaying hops.
It shows that the escape is available as soon as strict finite range is relaxed.

## 0. Conventions

- Quasienergy E: `U psi = e^{-iE} psi`. `W3[U] = (1/24π²) ∫ Σ ε_abc tr(A_a A_b A_c) d³k`, with `A_a = U^{-1}∂_aU`. This is C5's
  convention; `V_2` has W3 = −1.
- `Ch(P) = (i/2π) ∫_Σ tr(P dP∧dP)` on an oriented closed surface. In code this is the Fukui–Hatsugai–Suzuki lattice sum. s0
  fixes its sign against a direct quadrature of the integral.
- Weyl chirality `χ = sign det v`. Near the node, the two touching bands obey `U ≈ e^{-i eps} exp(−i(t·δk + Σ v_ij δk_i σ_j))`
  on their 2-dimensional degenerate space. The code computes `H_a = i e^{i eps} D^† ∂_aU D`.
- With these conventions the C5 one-qubit statement reads as follows. In V_2 the 0-level nodes have Σχ = +1, and so does the
  π-node (s1). Both equal `−W3`. In the "fixed band" bookkeeping of HNU the π-sum appears with the opposite sign. That
  bookkeeping is the source of the phrase "+1 at 0, −1 at π". (The C5 note uses a third sign, `χ_C5 = Π sign cos p_a`, which equals −χ at its 0-nodes.)

## 1. Premises

- Walk class (supplied, as in C5): `U(k) = Σ_{x∈S} A_x e^{ik·x}`, with S finite and U unitary. One site per cell, any n.
  Covariance is not needed. It is used only to name test cases. On-site actions: C7 uses `1⊗D(R)`, and the C4z test uses
  `D = 1⊗e^{-iπσ3/4}`.
- Theorem K (C5): W3 = 0 on this class. Imported, not re-proved.
- Nothing else. Theorem L is pure differential topology.

## 2. Theorem L and its proof

**Setting.** U is smooth. The B_i are disjoint closed boxes (any compact 3-manifolds with piecewise-smooth boundary would do).
`F_eps ⊂ ∪ int B_i`. For each i there is a phi_i with `e^{-i phi_i} ∉ spec U(k)` for all k ∈ B_i. Take phi_i ∈ (eps, eps+2π).

**Claim.** `W3[U] = −Σ_i Ch(P_<^{(i)}|∂B_i)`. Here `P_<^{(i)}(k)` is the spectral projector of `H_reg^{(i)}(k)` onto
(phi_i − 2π, eps), and `H_reg^{(i)} = i Log_{phi_i} U`, whose spectrum lies in (phi_i − 2π, phi_i).

*Proof.*
1. **Global log off the level set.** On `T^3 \ F_eps`, `H_eps = i Log U` with branch cut at `e^{-i eps}` is smooth, by
   holomorphic functional calculus. Its spectrum lies in (eps, eps+2π). Put `M = T^3 \ ∪ int B_i` and
   `G(k,t) = exp(−itH_eps(k))` on M×[0,1]. Then `G(·,0) = 1` and `G(·,1) = U`. The form `ω = tr(g^{-1}dg)^3` is closed, so Stokes on M×I gives
   `∫_M ω(U) = ∫_{∂M×I} ω(G) = −Σ_i ∫_{∂B_i×I} ω(G)`. The orientation is `∂(M×I) = ∂M×I − M×∂I`, and ∂M is −∂B_i.
2. **Local factorization.** On B_i, `H_reg` is smooth. On B_i \ F_eps ⊃ ∂B_i the eigenvalues of H_reg in
   (phi−2π, eps) are shifted by +2π in H_eps and the others are unchanged. So `H_eps = H_reg + 2πP_<`, with the two
   terms commuting, and `G = g·h` with `g = exp(−itH_reg)` and `h = exp(−2πitP_<)`.
3. **Polyakov–Wiegmann.** `ω(gh) = ω(g) + ω(h) + −3 d tr(g^{-1}dg ∧ dh h^{-1})` (the review's sign; the note originally wrote +3, and the sign is irrelevant here). The exact term integrates to boundary
   terms on ∂B_i×{0} and on ∂B_i×{1}. There `h ≡ 1` (at t = 1, `exp(−2πiP) = 1`), so dh pulls back to 0 and those terms vanish.
4. **Regular part.** g is smooth on B_i×I, so Stokes gives `∫_{∂B_i×I} ω(g) = ∫_{B_i} ω(U)`.
5. **Lemma T.** For a projector family P on a closed surface Σ, `(1/24π²) ∫_{Σ×[0,1]} tr θ³ = Ch(P)` for
   `h = 1 + (z−1)P`, `z = e^{−2πit}`. The proof: `θ = −2πiP dt + B` with `B = (z−1)dP + |1−z|² P dP`. Using
   `P dP P = 0` gives `tr(P B∧B) = −|1−z|² tr(P dP∧dP)`, so `tr θ³ = 6πi |1−z|² dt∧tr(P dP dP)`. Then use
   `∫_0^1 |1−z|² dt = 2`. (s0 checks this by quadrature to the discretization error, on 3 random families.)
6. Collecting: `∫_{T^3} ω(U) = ∫_M ω + Σ∫_{B_i} ω = −Σ[∫_{B_i} ω(U) + 24π² Ch_i] + Σ∫_{B_i} ω(U) = −24π² Σ Ch_i`. ∎

**Independence of choices.** Changing phi_i moves whole bands that extend smoothly over B_i into or out of P_<. Their
Chern number on ∂B_i is 0. Shrinking or deforming B_i through boxes whose boundaries avoid F_eps does not change Ch,
because the bundle is continuous in between.

**Lemma S (local contributions).**
(a) *Nondegenerate Weyl node* p ∈ F_eps. On a small sphere, P_< is the lower band of the pair plus bands that extend over the
ball. With `P_− = (1 − n̂·σ)/2` one gets `tr(P dP dP) = −(i/4) ε_ijk n_k dn_i dn_j`, hence `Ch(P_−) = deg n̂ = sign det v = χ`,
and so `W3 = −Σ_{p∈F_eps} χ_p` on a clean level. Tilt does not enter, because it is a scalar.
(b) *Isolated multi-fold touching*: its contribution is Ch of the multi-dimensional P_<. Example: at a Dirac point, the lower
two bands have Ch = 0 (C7 walk, s2).
(c) *Simple Fermi sheet S* where band j crosses eps transversally. The boundary ∂N of a thin tube around S has two components.
P_< contains L_j only on the side where E_j < eps. The other contents of P_< extend over N and cancel. The contribution
`−Ch(P_<|∂N)` equals `Φ_S := Ch(L_j|S)`, with S oriented by ∇E_j. *Scope (review):* a tube around a large sheet need not admit a quasienergy phi absent on all of N, since other bands can sweep the circle along S. The proof of L does not need phi. It needs only a smooth H_reg on the region with `exp(−iH_reg) = U` and `H_eps − H_reg = 2πP'` on the boundary (the "cluster" form of L). On a thin tube take H_reg = H_eps on the bands away from e^{−i eps}, and the continuous branch E_j ∈ (eps−δ, eps+δ) on the spectrally isolated band j. Then P' = L_j on the side where E_j < eps, and (c) follows. A pocket sphere around a node at another quasienergy
gives `Φ = −χ`, which agrees with (a).

**General identity.** For every eps whose level set splits into nodes and simple sheets,
**`W3[U] = −Σ_{nodes at eps} χ_p + Σ_{sheets at eps} Φ_S`**. W3 does not depend on eps, so this is a constraint at every quasienergy.
At a clean level it reduces to `W3 = −Σ χ`. This is the HNU / Sun et al. / Bessho–Sato-type relation (see §6); it is derived here
with its exact hypotheses.

## 3. Corollaries

**NN (main).** U finite range ⇒ W3 = 0 (K) ⇒ `Σ χ_p = Σ_S Φ_S` at every level. Clean level ⇒ `Σ χ_p = 0`. The statement
holds for every n, every eps, and every stencil and coin. For n = 2 it is C5's Corollary N, now without the hypothesis
"det U constant", which is implied by D.

**D.** If F_eps is finite, `det U = exp(−i tr H_eps)` on the complement, and tr H_eps is continuous there. Loops avoiding a finite
set generate π_1(T^3). So det U has winding 0 on every loop. For a Laurent polynomial, `det U = c z^m`, so m = 0.

**Win.** Suppose the spectral set {(k,E): E ∈ (eps−Δ, eps+Δ)} lies over disjoint balls B_i. Then ∂B_i carries no spectrum in
the window, so P_<^{eps'}|∂B_i does not depend on eps' within the window. Moving eps' across one node of a ball changes the
ball's content from "node at eps'" to "pocket" without changing Ch. Under the single-pair hypothesis, Ch(P_<|∂B_i) = Ch(band j on ∂B_i) is the sum of the (j, j+1) node chiralities in B_i. Without that hypothesis this fails. With bands 1 < 2 < 3, a 1–2 node and a 2–3 node in one ball, and band 2 below the window on ∂B, one gets Ch = χ_{2–3} only (review §2.3). So `Σ_i Ch_i = Σ_{all Weyl nodes in the window} χ = −W3 = 0`.

**Ans.** "Net chirality +1 at quasienergy 0, −1 at π, nothing else at either level" is a pair of clean levels with
`W3 = −Σχ_0 = −Σχ_π` (the π-count again in the convention of §0), so `W3 ≠ 0`. That is impossible at finite range, for any n.
**Where finite-range constructions go instead:**
- (i) Both levels are clean and each is internally balanced. This is generic: C7's axial-coin deformation gives 2 Weyl nodes at 0 (±1) and 6 at π (3 of each sign).
- (ii) The 0-nodes are unbalanced and level 0 carries Fermi sheets with the opposite flux (witness T2, T3).
- (iii) The nodes leave the level, giving pockets (all coupled n = 3, 4 walks of s2).

In every case the compensation sits at the **same** quasienergy as the nodes. It never sits only at the cutoff.

computed at two meshes. FHS sums are exact integers when the link variables are nonzero. Agreement at two meshes is the stated

with a degeneracy test. Chirality comes from first-order degenerate perturbation theory.

## 5. Scope and limits

- Single-particle, translation-invariant, one site per cell (larger cells are larger n). Interacting QCAs and disorder are not treated.
- "Clean" means a finite level set. The window corollary covers nodes slightly off the level.
- **Not certified:** (1) completeness of the node lists inside boxes. Box Chern numbers are the rigorous object; a found node
  list is evidence. For T2, completeness follows from the certified Q_211 inventory, because degeneracies of e^{ik_x}Q are Q's nodes. (2) The sheet flux in
  the witness is inferred from Theorem L, not computed independently. (3) FHS mesh adequacy is checked by two-mesh agreement.
- The theorem does not exclude unbalanced chirality in a projected or adiabatic band subspace (Sun et al.). It shows that
  any such imbalance at a quasienergy is cancelled at that same quasienergy by the other bands' Fermi sheets, for finite-range
  or Hamiltonian-generated U. This consequence is mine; I read only search snippets of that paper.

## 6. Novelty and literature (search snippets only; full texts not read)

The level-wise relation "W3 = net chirality at a quasienergy" appears to be known: Higashikawa–Nakagawa–Ueda, PRL 123,
066403 (2019); Sun–Xiao–Bzdušek–Zhang–Fan, PRL 121, 196401 (2018), arXiv:1806.09296; Bessho–Sato, arXiv:2006.04204,
"Nielsen-Ninomiya theorem with bulk topology: duality in Floquet and non-Hermitian systems". Gomi–Tauber (arXiv:1906.10358) treat local indices.
Haldane (PRL 93, 206602 (2004); recalled) gives the static analogue, Σ Fermi-sheet Berry flux = 0.
Theorem L here is a self-contained proof with explicit hypotheses: boxes, cut, sheets, multi-fold touchings. **Novelty is unverified and likely nil.** Sun et al. (2018) is the most likely prior statement of the level-wise relation (the 1D per-cut statement and the 3D adiabatic two-band statement). This rests on a search snippet only; the full text was not read.
The repo-relevant content is the combination with Theorem K. It closes the C5b question for every n, and it upgrades C5's Corollary N
(n = 2, det constant) to all n with no det hypothesis.

## 7. Load-bearing step for independent check

Steps 2–5 of Theorem L: the factorization `exp(−itH_eps) = exp(−itH_reg)·exp(−2πitP_<)` on ∂B, the vanishing of the
Polyakov–Wiegmann boundary term (h ≡ 1 at t = 0, 1), and the sign of `Ch` in Lemma T. A different route would be
to show that `ν(B) := −Ch(P_<|∂B)` is additive and homotopy invariant, and then calibrate it on V_2 and on the trivial map. s1 is a partial check
of this kind, because it compares against W3 computed by the bulk integral, which uses a different method. The calibration of the overall sign (−) rests on s0/s1.

## 8. No-go discipline

- N1 evasion routes: infinite or quasi-local range (V_2 escapes), interactions, disorder, measurement-based steps, and projected
  subspaces. All are outside the class and named.
- N3 hidden premises: smoothness of U (automatic for finite range) and the clean-level or box hypotheses. Nothing else.
- N7 strongest objection: Theorem K is imported. Theorem L uses only Stokes and functional calculus.
- N8 effect on earlier claims: this extends C5's Corollary N and is consistent with C7 (vector-like Dirac nodes).
