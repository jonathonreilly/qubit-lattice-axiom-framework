## 2. A free-wave half-line estimate with its domain and velocity-spread term

Let g in H^1_0(I), normalized in L2(I), be extended by zero to the momentum circle (-pi,pi). Since its endpoint trace is zero, that extension is in periodic H^1. Define unitary Fourier maps

    (F_plus g)(n)  = (1/sqrt(2pi)) integral g(k)exp(+ikn) dk,
    (F_minus g)(n) = (1/sqrt(2pi)) integral g(k)exp(-ikn) dk,
                n in Z.                                (7)

For the position multiplication X, Fourier differentiation gives

    X F_plus g = F_plus(i g'),
    X F_minus g = F_minus(-i g').                        (8)

The H1 domain is preserved by multiplication by exp(-itE), because E is smooth with bounded derivative on the circle. Integration by parts proves (8) first for smooth periodic functions; H1 approximation and closedness of X extend it to this domain. Parseval therefore gives the exact equalities

    ||(X-v0 t) F_plus(exp(-itE)g)||2
        = ||i g'+t(E'-v0)g||2,
    ||(X+v0 t) F_minus(exp(-itE)g)||2
        = ||-i g'-t(E'-v0)g||2,   v0=E'(k0)>v.          (9)

The two Fourier signs have opposite propagation directions. No independence of initial position and velocity, no affine-dispersion approximation and no operator-order inequality between a position projection and E'(k) is used.

On n<0 the magnitude of n-v0t is at least v0t; on n>=0 the magnitude of n+v0t is at least v0t. Also |E'(k)-v0|<=2J sigma on I. For t>0, (9) and the triangle inequality prove

    ||1_(n<0) F_plus(exp(-itE)g)||2
          <= ||g'||2/(vt)+2J sigma/v,
    ||1_(n>=0) F_minus(exp(-itE)g)||2
          <= ||g'||2/(vt)+2J sigma/v.                    (10)

These are full-line analysis estimates for the two specified wrong half-lines; no negative clock sites are installed. The last term is a price of centering at the single constant velocity v0. It is not a physical lower bound on leakage. One may take the smaller of (10) and the already qualified integration-by-parts estimates at any time. In particular the latter still prove true decay to zero. The new estimate alone, with its nonzero floor, is **not** used to re-prove the exact past-limit isometry normalization.

This lemma is an elementary form of the known free-chain moment identity. Landed repository examples use X(t)=X+tV and the same Hilbert-space triangle argument, with compact sine packets and different targets. Section 8 records the precise prior-art distinction.

## 7. Discriminators and deliberately unused shortcuts

The preserved author controls and the analytic proof distinguish the following nearby wrong arguments.

1. **Wrong propagation sign.** For outgoing F_plus, i times the derivative of exp(-itE)g is exp(-itE)[i g'+tE'g]. Reversing the velocity sign leaves the exact symbolic defect 2tE'exp(-itE)g. Incoming F_minus has the opposite sign. This tests conventions, not just a positive bound.
2. **Dropping velocity spread.** The exact identity contains (E'-v0)g. For constant normalized g on the circle and E=2J(1-cos k), v0=0 gives ||X psi_t||^2=2J^2t^2 while ||g'||=0. This toy is not the narrow emitter packet; it invalidates the proposed general identity without that term. For a nonzero narrow packet the term is likewise not identically zero. The floor in (10) is a conservative proof cost, not an impossibility theorem for sharper estimates.
3. **Omitting the actual outgoing phase.** Constant normalized f on the circle and rho=exp(-7ik) have ||f'||=0 but ||(f rho)'||=7. Equation (18) therefore keeps the phase product. In the actual emitter bounds it contributes a leading kappa^-2 term. Replacing rho by r0 or by a fixed nominal output is not covered.
4. **Wrong normalization space.** A normalized full-line toy vector with positive-side norm3/5 and negative-side norm4/5 costs2/5 to normalize its positive restriction, whereas its full-line distance to that normalized restriction has squared norm4/5>16/25. Equation (20) is the restriction-only statement needed in (24).
5. **Wrong denominator integral coefficient.** The monotone substitution contributes2/v. Deleting that factor2 disagrees with the exact trigonometric evaluation of (13). No resonance grid is used to suggest it is harmless.
6. **Replacing the actual scalar or waveform.** The qualified joint review's nominal-waveform countercase at t=10^36 is still a countercase to a nominal-waveform claim for every t>=10^30, since that time lies in the new range. Its distinct fixed-nominal-nu phase issue also remains. No new quantitative onset counterexample is asserted from that inheritance.

Only the moment route is developed here. Higher-order pointwise integration by parts would have to pay cutoff derivative jumps and resonance derivatives; it was not silently invoked. A contour/exponential decay route would need a controlled complex continuation and all pole/branch contributions; no such theorem is assumed. Altering kappa, preparing a different packet, or inserting a supplied gate bond would change the present task. These unattempted alternatives are not ruled out, and the result is neither an optimum nor a broad negative conclusion.
