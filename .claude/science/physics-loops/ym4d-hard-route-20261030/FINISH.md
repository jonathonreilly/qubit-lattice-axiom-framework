## How we work

0. **Is it worth doing? Ask before every lane, check, edit or message, lead included.** It must name the open item on the critical path that it closes or kills (an obligation in `GOAL.md`, a row of `library/sa-ledger.md`'s top twenty, or a position in `library/route-decisions.md`), and what we would do differently if it succeeds or fails. No named item, or no change either way: do not do it. Each queue row opens with "Moves:" and that item. Each lead update lists what moved; work that moved nothing is named as waste, so it is not repeated.
1. **Literature first.** Each route or proof step starts from one wide search (Balaban, Dimock, the Sussman-Alpoge repository at `31a47c543775`, arXiv, older constructive field theory), recorded once in `library/lit-<topic>.md` and reused. A smaller lane reads those records and does a short targeted search.
2. **Build on published work; verify what the proof uses.** No firewall. Use any published result at once, labelled `unchecked`, credited by name (Balaban, Dimock, Sussman and Alpoge with Claude and Codex as authors of their text, Grok for its PRs #10133 to #10135). Before it counts as closed, it is re-derived in `library/` and checked by another provider. `library/sa-ledger.md` lists what the outline's Theorem 0 reads, with the twenty to verify first.
3. **Choose routes on evidence.** Ours and the outline's are both candidates, part by part. `library/route-decisions.md` records the choice, the reason and what would reverse it. Keep both live only while the evidence is open; budget follows evidence.
4. **Only closures count.** A lane ends with a proof, every step written, or an explicit counterexample. Try a counterexample first only when unsure. A smaller statement is attacked in the same session; after two reductions with no closure, stop and report to the lead. Checks redo the proof and report WRONG with the failure, or confirm it. Notes are short.
5. **Skeleton first, then fast (owner, 2026-11-03: "get the proof right and get it fast, and skelleton it first maybe"; "dont spend time on adverse readings").** `library/t0-skeleton.md` lists every statement T0 needs, in order, with its inputs and its state: proved and checked, proved and not yet checked, open, or cited only. Each lane fills one open node and updates its line. Each proof gets one check: the other model reads it line by line once. No hostile-referee passes, no reviews of reviews, no extra readings for scope or wording. When the next lane reads a result anyway, it does the check in its first section, not in a separate lane. Read the same step in their QED3 and YM3 manuscripts first. Freeze what passes; reopen only on WRONG or a changed input. Keep every machine on the critical path. Hardening (hostile readings, stress tests, robustness passes) waits until the whole proof exists (owner, 2026-11-03: "once we have the proofs we can do the reinforcement thing"); it then runs on the clean document of "What finishing means" item 4.
6. **Our proof first; help Sussman-Alpoge where it has real value (owner, 2026-11-03: "I dont view them as a rival, we should help where it makes sense to do so, and continue otherwise. Just hold a high bar for contributing").** Workers never post outside this repository. The lead may offer a GitHub issue to the Sussman-Alpoge repository only for a result checked line by line by the other model that they would value: an actual error in their text with the explicit failure, or a proof of something their outline leaves open or assumes. Not gaps in wording, missing references, notation or bookkeeping. The owner approves each issue's exact text before it is posted. No lane works on their statements unless our chain reads them.
7. **Say who checked what, in plain words (owner, 2026-11-03).** Write "checked line by line by Codex (or Claude), first failing step none" or "WRONG at <step>", and "proved by Claude, not yet checked by the other model". Never write "gated" or "ungated". A check is one reading by one other model: not an audit and not human review, and a checked result is still an implication from its named inputs. Check reports still go in `gates/`.
8. **Write about 80% of the way to ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (owner, 2026-11-03, after Karpathy's 2026-10-02 post).** Short sentences, about 20 words at most, one topic each, active voice, one meaning per word and the same term every time, no noun stacks longer than three words. Claim first, then evidence, then what it does not show. Math notation is exempt. This binds issue drafts, library notes and check reports. Notes are as short as the proof allows.

## Working efficiently

Measured on the pushes so far: a resumed push carries about 300,000 to 580,000 tokens of context on every step, and 70 to 90 percent of its active time is the model generating text. A fresh, narrow session avoids most of that: the first fresh check wrote 195,000 tokens for about $8 at API rates, where a resumed push wrote 202,000 for about $219.

- **Hardest first.** Owner, 2026-11-01: go after the hard statements first, to save grinding on the easy base. Work goes to the statement the proof is most likely to die on. A routine hand rebuild, or an elementary citation, waits until a hard-core attempt closes, unless it could be hiding a real gap.
- **One item, one session, run to closure.** Stay on the item until it is proved or refuted, including any smaller statement it reduces to, as long as the budget lasts. Do not move to unrelated items or tidy other files.
- **Do not read whole files.** Part files are 145 to 350 KB, and the folder holds about 1.6 MB of text. Find the statement with `grep -n`, then read the statement, its proof and what it cites.
- **Try to kill it first.** A short computation with pass and fail criteria written first can save a long argument. Report what a run can and cannot establish. A search that finds nothing does not prove a bound.
- **Save as you go.** Write your report section by section. A reader that dies at the output limit with nothing saved is a total loss.
- **Keep reports short.** At most ten defects, ranked by what each breaks. If a defect breaks the consumer's statement, say so and stop unless the rest is independent of it. Give a repair if you see one, marked as unproved.
- **No subagents** unless the item needs a separate reader. Each one is a full extra cost.
