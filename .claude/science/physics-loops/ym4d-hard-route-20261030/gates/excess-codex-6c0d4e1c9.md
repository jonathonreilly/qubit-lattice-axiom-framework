## Blind defects, ranked by what they block

1. **EX2′: spatially constant rotations cannot be discarded after differentiating in time.** At `E:389`, the difference of differentiated variances is bounded solely by `C(L)(n^+ N_d^+)^(1/2)`; the rotation rate is asserted to be bounded by `(N_d^+)^(1/2)`. The preceding `N_d` is defined through curvature derivatives in a rooted axial gauge (`E:377,385`). If two frames obey `F_2(t,z)=Ad(g_0(t))F_1(t,z)` even with *exactly spatially constant* `g_0`, then

   `Fdot_2 = Ad(g_0)(Fdot_1 + [g_0^-1 gdot_0,F_1])`.

   The second term affects the variance unless `F_1` is spatially constant or commutes with the rotation rate. For a concrete test of this inference, take a nonconstant small curvature and a pure gauge curve whose gauge transformation is stationary at root y but moving at root y''. In y's rooted frame `Fdot_1=0`; in y''’s frame the additional commutator need not vanish. Stokes reconstruction of the links in y's axial gauge does not control this independent root rotation. This refutes the *curvature-only frame-comparison step*, not the final estimate for the particular AN3 curve. The original undifferentiated comparison (`E:76`) is valid because constant rotations preserve variance. That reason does not extend to `Var(Fdot)`.

   **Repair — unproved here:** retain the constant root-rate term, control it using the link-variation input (A2), and show that its weighted square contributes a summable `C(L)s^2 f^2` at every frame change. The final return-to-chain-gauge term at `E:391` does not explicitly repair the earlier comparison at `E:389`. A proof must make this bookkeeping consistent with the definition of the root frame in `E:346,367`.

2. **LIFT: the asserted spatial tail is too strong for the Picard argument supplied.** `E:222` passes from an analytic map of finite range and `D Psi=I+O(C(L)f)` to

   `|Y(c)| <= C(L) Theta-tilde_c^2`, with `Theta-tilde_c = sum_{c''}(C(L)f)^{d(c,c'')} Theta_{c''}`.

   Let the range of the derivative's off-diagonal part be R parent blocks and its norm be q. A Neumann/Picard propagation over distance d uses about `ceil(d/R)` steps, giving weights of order `q^{ceil(d/R)}` on a **quadratic source**. This is already visible in the scalar inverse `I-qS_R`: a source at distance mR reaches the origin with coefficient `q^m`, whereas the claimed squared kernel would assign `q^{2mR}`. No constant depending only on L bridges these powers as f tends to zero. This scalar example diagnoses the inference, and is not claimed to be a counterexample to the actual nonlinear lift. Re-establish the weighted T1–T3 estimates and uniform kernel moments with that kernel. Smallness depending on L can make this repair affordable, but does not prove the printed tail. This blocks the fully quantified LIFT statement, rather than disproving existence of a local right inverse.
