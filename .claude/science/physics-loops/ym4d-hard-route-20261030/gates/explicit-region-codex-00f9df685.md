1. **The central comparison has not identified the actual region operation.** Exact lines: 69, “each Omega_(j+1)^c holds the rounded and buffered Omega_j^c”; 85, “The state cannot be ‘regions with histories’ in the form of Dimock's Theorem 3.1”; 200, “No attempt should carry them.”

   The reading of the logarithmic inequality is correct, but its geometric interpretation is not rebuilt. Dimock II, equations (145)–(148), states nesting and uses the outward rounding of the **small** set. Section 3.5 defines `bar Lambda_k` as the union of coarse cubes intersecting `Lambda_k`, then (234) buffers `(bar Lambda_k)^c`. These are not interchangeable operations: the complement of outward rounding of the small set can be strictly smaller than outward rounding of its complement. For example, remove a proper fine subcube from one coarse cube, leaving some small-set points in that coarse cube. Outward rounding of the remaining small set fills the coarse cube; rounding the removed subcube instead marks that coarse cube. This is a set-theoretic counterexample to identifying the two operations, not a construction of a complete admissible Dimock history. The full compatibility with nesting and buffers must be checked. [Dimock II, §§3.1.1, 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.5562v2).

   Dimock III (198) explicitly uses `(bar Lambda_k)^c`; its ensuing column bound is an **upper** bound on accumulated costs, not a lower bound proving that every fine defect has an eternal column. Losing this sufficient estimate at marginal scaling does not establish impossibility. [Dimock III, §3.5, (198)–(205)](https://arxiv.org/html/1304.0705).

2. **HOLES proves a probability estimate, not deterministic emptiness or impossibility of induction.** Exact lines: 83, “In any induction … the small-field region at the top is empty for K >= K_0”; 85, “Only regions of bounded age can be carried.”

   On a finite torus choose every link in a sufficiently small open neighbourhood of identity. By continuity, every plaquette angle is below T. This open product set has positive Haar measure, and the Wilson density is strictly positive at finite `x_0`. Under an induction that removes exactly the ancestors of rough cells, nothing is removed there, at any finite K. This is a positive-probability counterexample, not merely an exceptional measure-zero flat field.

   The valid probabilistic statement is strong. Write `c=T^3/192`, `ell=log L`, and let m be the fixed number of top cells. Under the hypotheses and correct containment,

   `P(some top cell has no rough fine plaquette) <= m exp[-c exp(2K ell-12x_KT^2)].`

   For fixed `x_K,T,L`, this tends to zero doubly exponentially. The printed `K_0` implies `nq >= exp(K ell-6x_KT^2)`; thus the advertised asymptotic scale is sound. Nothing changes the positive probability of the all-small event at finite K. An almost-sure eventual assertion would require a common probability space and a summability argument, not a deterministic cutoff `K_0`.

   Even a frequently empty top small region is not by itself a proof that a representation with regions is invalid: it must still be shown that the complementary integral cannot be estimated by another part of the induction. Nor does failure of permanent deletion imply a *uniform* bounded-age alternative; size-dependent healing, resummation and different tests have not been excluded. The displayed row (f) itself permits age growing with `log diam`.

   **Repair — proved for the stipulated mask:** retain the probability bound above, conditional on the exact flow bound. State the additional requirement of a nonempty top small region if that is what a particular method needs. **Unproved:** any universal architectural no-go, or the exact Wilson flow bound. The coefficient test itself needs no decimal: `pi>3` suffices. There is room to take, for example, `B=(1/2)log L`, since at `T<=1/2` the left side is at most `(3/2)log L`; proving that B bounds the actual flow remains necessary. A threshold eventually below a fixed T is covered by monotonicity only after identifying it with the same raw angle test.

4. **RD's legitimate fixed-measure identity is applied to an integral whose constraints can depend on the data.** Exact lines: 130–133, the definition of `Z(d;W)`; 139–143, “data are read at mixed plaquettes only” and “In the Gaussian limit the dependence is exactly classical.”

Data are read by every crossing constraint, not just by crossing Wilson plaquettes. Even the elementary constraint `d+u=w` with exterior action `u^2/2` gives the constrained integral `exp[-x(w-d)^2/2]`, despite there being no mixed action term. This demonstrates why action locality alone is insufficient; it is not offered as an SU(2) counterexample with this specific map.

   The exact Gaussian assertion already fails on the finite chart box. Let

   `Z(d)=int_(-R)^R exp[-x(u-d)^2/2] du`, with `0<d<R`.

   **Repair — unproved:** include all crossing blocking factors in the boundary data and establish a data-independent parametrization, or retain and estimate its derivative/domain terms. Prove a residual determinant/cutoff bound after factoring the common classical energy. The cancellation for wholly frozen identical plaquettes remains exact, but does not establish R2 at mixed plaquettes.

5. **The tower property is being used across a change of conditional law.** Exact lines: 111–113, `(3.1)` and its rarity interpretation; 177, “The prices multiply.”

   FIB controls the normalized integral **on K**. The actual fibre at a class parent also contains exits, precisely the other terms of (4.2). In general, if `G={V_k in V(K)}`, then

   `P(E|W)=P(G|W)P(E|W,G)+P(G^c|W)P(E|W,G^c).`

   A bound on the first conditional law does not bound the second. Declaring W class does not force G. Also, for a higher seed event H, a pointwise estimate for `P(E|V_1)` only on class V_1 yields

   `P(E cap H | top) <= p P(H | top) + P(V_1 nonclass cap H | top)`,

   provided the requisite reverse Markov property identifies the conditional kernel. The extra term cannot be deleted because one displayed configuration has class V_1 near the pack. A probability event ranges over other configurations too. A two-component mixture concentrated on exits with weight alpha gives the elementary obstruction: a zero chart probability can coexist with full-law probability alpha. No independence of pack and seed follows from the tower identity alone.

   **Repair — unproved:** prove a full-fibre bound including exits and the needed local-parent uniformity, or restrict the event explicitly to the chart/class-parent portion and keep the complementary terms. This is where a rarity consumer could silently read the old all-history payment again if its old proof were reused. It is not a proof that (HW) is indispensable.
