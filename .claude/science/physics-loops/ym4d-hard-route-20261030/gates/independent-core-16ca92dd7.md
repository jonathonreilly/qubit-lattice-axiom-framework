## Defects, ranked by what each breaks

**D1. Rough sets: an exchange error is not rough on "Z and a ball" when Z is long. Breaks the rough-set closure, that is (TAIL) for term (2), and the claim that a rough set shrinks by L per level.** Text 4.7, "What is rough, and the density step". Term (1) of 1 = prod_c (B_c + (A_c - B_c)), with A_c the product cut on xi_eff in cube c and B_c the cut on (v, xi^perp), is the term with one cube c in A_c - B_c. In the variables (v, xi^perp), B is fixed, and A_c reads xi_eff,c(v, xi^perp; W) = [ P_{Z,d}^+(W) (b_hat(v; W) - B') ]_c + xi^perp_c. P_{Z,d} is strictly local, and its right inverse is not, so xi_eff,c reads b_hat on all of Z, and through b_hat = v - s(W) - NL it reads V^#(W) on all of Z, hence W within R_c of all of Z. The amplitude is exponentially small in the distance from c, and A_c is a sharp function, so the term is not analytic in that W. *Concrete failure:* take Y long, with Z a thin line of n cubes containing no tube, and a coarse site w_0 outside Z, within R_c of a cube of Z at distance about n / 2 from c. The term has a step in w_0, so (TAIL) fails at w_0 unless w_0 is in the rough set, and then the zone of all of Z is rough, about n R_c^3 cubes. That is the case the rewrite was written to remove ("a zone of about n R_c^3 cubes, with R_c^3 >> L"). The sum over c does not help, and one factor exp(-p^2 / 3) does not pay a zone that grows with n. For Y of side M, the only case in Lemma 4.7c, Z sits inside the ball, and there is no defect. *Repair, unproved:* make the good sector free of sharp functions. Let N_good use an entire cut chi(xi_eff) at p, and an entire truncation Theta(b_hat) that is the identity up to exp(-p^2) on |b_hat| <= r and maps the real line into the chart. It is only a comparison measure, so it need not equal the true integrand off good cubes, which removes the objection of (CUT) that the chart ends at Dom. Then D(v; W) has no sharp factor, there is no exchange and no A_c, and sharp functions occur only at true bad cubes, where coordinates can be mixed, xi on a ball about the cube and v on the rest of Z at distance above the range of P, so that nothing reads across. Second route, weaker: carry a weight per cube of Rg(Y), which births do have and I(k)(c) drops; it pays the first generation of zones, but not a chain of them, since the zone grows by R_c^3 / L per generation.

**D2. At complex W the Gaussian of xi_eff has a complex centre, and the bound the text needs fails as p grows at fixed alpha. Breaks (EI) Output and the sup bound of log N_good on the class, so the contraction in Closure, which reads the class radius alpha; also (TAIL) at the cap rho_e.** Text 4.7, Two sectors, and stage (ii): "analytic in t on the region where |Im s| <= c on Y, per cube", "At complex W the same, with s(W)". s = 2^{1/2} P^+ (G_1 xi_1 + G_2 xi_2) has W-dependent kernels, so |Im s| is of the order of c_1(L) alpha |xi'_c|. The order of constants fixes alpha = c_2 eps_0 before p(g), and p -> infinity as g -> 0. So on a cube with |xi'_c| near p_4 = p / (2 C_s), |Im s| is about c_1 alpha p, above any fixed c once p > c / (c_1 alpha). The total variation of the complex Gaussian is then exp(c_1^2 alpha^2 p^2 / 2) per such cube. With p_0 > 2 this beats 1 / H_0, about 1 / (g p^3), as g -> 0, so at fixed xi' the activity has no small bound, and the library, which asks for a probability measure or a modulus bound, does not apply. Integrating xi' first would absorb the factor, since alpha is small, but the text expands at fixed xi'. The same at the cap of (TAIL), where w is of size rho_e in the tail of a long Y, and "cost exp(C(L) (c' C(L))^2) per link" omits the factor |xi'_c|. Analyticity is not in question, only the bound. *Repair, unproved:* shift each variable of each polymer to the real centre. The integrand of a Mayer polymer is analytic in xi_eff, the shift is |y| <= c_1 alpha p, and the side walls cost exp(-(p^2 - y^2) / 8), which alpha small allows. The entire good sector of D1 has no walls at all, so one repair covers both.

**D3. Lemma 4.7c says C absolute, and the constant c' of (TAIL) enters C. Breaks the recursion for cc_k as written.** (TAIL) asks c' small against C(L, M). The tail of e is controlled by Cauchy at radius c' g, so "The tail of e adds to Gamma a second-order form ... / (c' g)^2 ... of the same order as t_Y^2" is off by c'^{-2}: |F^*|^2 / (c' g)^2 = c'^{-2} t_Y^2 / Vol(Y). The same condition limits the polydisc, since a complex W near Y moves far links by about c g / D, and they must stay inside the tube, so c <= c' D, and Cauchy gives C of order c^{-3}. So the non-marginal part carries c'^{-2} (D / L)^a, and with c' a negative power of L the factor C M^{10 + a} L^{-a} of cc_{k+1} is not below 1/4. Rescaling t by 1 / c' does not help: the growth form then gives c'^{-3} P(M). *Repair, unproved:* take c' absolute for polymers of diameter below L, the only ones that need the contraction. What forces c' small is the shift cost at birth, and that should carry a factor g, since w of size c' g moves the kernels by C(L) c' g / epsilon_1, so s moves by C(L) c' g |xi'| / epsilon_1, not by c' C(L). Polymers holding most of a tube have stiffness of order L^6 in W_b, and they do need a small c', but they carry exp(-kappa (L / M)^4) and need no contraction. So c' should depend on the size of the polymer.

**D4. (GRAD) and the patch gauge of Step 4 use radial transport on patches that can have loops. Closes the attempt's own "worry left", with a repair.** Lemma 4.5k. (GRAD) writes V_0 = exp(i chi v_0) V_0^flat on B(x, tau_1), with tau_1 = A log L, which is above 2 P for L large, so the ball can hold tiles with a loop around them, and then no radial transport exists. A flat comparison field near V_0 exists on a fixed finite complex only by compactness, and the distance can be of order eps^{1/2}, not linear in eps, at a reducible flat connection. So |v_0| <= C tau^2 eps is not available, and with it the polynomial C(L) that the margin A_5 log L needs. Step 4 needs only smallness, which a choice of epsilon_2 after the Dehn function gives. (GRAD) needs linearity. *Repair, unproved but short:* no flat field is needed. d_l f_{k,x}(V_0) = <F, delta_l F>_x on the fine lattice, so |d_l f_{k,x}| <= ||F||_{l^2(x)} ||delta_l F||_{l^2(x)} <= C f_{k,x}^{1/2} C_up exp(-delta_0 tau_k(x; l)), one-shot, with no area and no compounding. With f_{k,x} <= C epsilon_2^2 that is (GRAD), at a better rate.
