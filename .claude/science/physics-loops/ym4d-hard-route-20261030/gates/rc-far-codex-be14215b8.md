### Rate pair: the forwarded D2 condition

**Derived here, from the user-supplied excerpt and the printed rates, not from a gate file.** With m=floor(d/2)-2, PAIR and a saturated endpoint tail give

    (c_* + kappa/2 - c_I) d <= (1/2) log x' + O_Theta(1).

For d of order lambda' comparable to x', a fixed rate pair therefore needs c_I-c_*>=kappa/2 (up to the stated vanishing logarithmic correction). The proposed choice (**R:29–35; F:166**) instead has

    c_I = sigma >= delta_# L/8,
    c_I-c_* = 2 max(kappa,kappa_s) >= 2 kappa.

Thus it has at least four times D2's asymptotic required endpoint margin. In the usual kappa_s<=kappa case, the left coefficient is -3 kappa/2. Positive c_* is available for L large. D2 does **not** force rho_f>=7 kappa: it concerns the endpoint rate, not the far-boundary rate. Separately, the capped extension needs rho_f>=2 kappa (**R:53–55**), while rho_f=delta_#/16 and kappa<=delta_#/96 give rho_f>=6 kappa, three times that requirement. For the slow tier take

    kappa_s <= min(existing consumer kappa_s, min(rho_s,mu_c)/32).

Then rho_s'=min(rho_s,mu_c)/16>=2 kappa_s, and the same endpoint margin pays 2 kappa_s. These are consistent scalar choices. Defects 1–3 prevent calling them *proved decay rates of the current*. The original slow c(L) endpoint criticized in the supplied D2 is not rescued merely by printing this new sigma.

### Constants and (TT)

    C_Z lambda_min >= ell_max
       + 2 kappa ell_max/(kappa_s b_L)

This is **U:1465** with the worst endpoint constants. At fixed positive kappa_s the left side grows linearly in X while the last term decreases logarithmically, so X can be increased after the rate is fixed. The floor condition at **U:1461** likewise can be met by increasing X for fixed positive l_1. The cut-amplitude constraint must also be retained (**U:1451; C:135–148**). Thus there is no scalar TT obstruction or forced dependence on j, K or volume.
