## 1. Highest risk: the mapped boundary and generated-observable induction does not close merely because the density induction closes

Its scalar history estimate covers q<=4, whereas the endpoint still asks for every required moment and cumulant partition (`parts/upper-chain.md:98–100,122,1314–1322,1351`). The producer underneath it is also unstable. The currently printed nonlinear (LIFT-1) bounds an exponentiated displacement using only its linear covariant curl (`library/energy-by-stages.md:47–51,65`). The latest energy report gives an exact SU(2) square: h=d omega has d h=0, but exp(tX)exp(tY)exp(-tX)exp(-tY) has Wilson action 2 sin^4(t)>0. This survives arbitrarily small t. There are two further reasons not to call this bookkeeping. **Cheap decision work, by hand:** first give one dependency diagram ordered by (level, construction stage), with the current source, boundary coercivity, regular completion and all mixture laws on it. ## 6. The RG route is reasonable; erasing the multiscale state and recovering observables afterwards may be the expensive choice

**From memory:** Balaban's organization keeps small-field backgrounds, their propagators and regularity estimates, the large-field decomposition and its renormalization, and the final effective-density induction as distinct tasks. Large-field regions and boundary dependence are carried in the representation before being summed. Dimock makes that organization unusually explicit in simpler models. The point is to close a carefully chosen space of effective densities and activities; a partition-function stability bound is not automatically a construction of all local correlators. I do not recall a theorem from either author that supplies T1, its current NT2, or the present boundary-data/current estimates.

For a limited bibliographic check, Balaban's [CMP 119 abstract](https://www.researchwithrutgers.org/en/publications/convergent-renormalization-expansions-for-lattice-gauge-theories/) expressly describes effective densities including large-field domains and preservation of their form. Dimock's [Part I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.1335) and [Part II](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.5562) treat small and large fields for scalar phi^4 in three dimensions; they are not a ready-made four-dimensional nonabelian theorem. These abstracts corroborate the broad organization only. The preceding methodological comparison remains **from memory**; no literature lemma is imported as proof.

Both attempts deliberately compress the state: mapped says “No R, no histories, no live regions,” and independent says nothing finer is carried and no histories are summed (`parts/hard-core--mapped.md:14`; `parts/hard-core--independent.md:233–235`). There is no contradiction in a level-only representation. But the alleged saving has returned as work: mapped must recreate all-age frozen data and joint marked corridors; independent must encode rough-set ancestry, site variables, large-noise labels and their compatibility in TAIL. The upper chain itself records why a bulk I(k) did not automatically yield I(k;D) (`parts/upper-chain.md:50–55,175–188`). My inference is that this effort is trying to prove *stronger uniform comparisons in a smaller state space* than the familiar organization requires. That may be an innovation; it has not yet demonstrated a saving.

**Architecture to try, not a proved escape:** retain an explicit multiscale region/boundary label in the induction, with the exact conditional partition function attached to it. Renormalize and pay each large-field contribution at its own scale before summing histories. Carry a finite set of localized observable insertions in the same induction: vacuum, one insertion, two separated insertions, then fixed higher order n. Extract the marginal action coefficient exactly and track errors in the insertion norm. This could avoid proving locality for an unnecessarily broad class of completed observables, or reconstructing normalized comparisons after all boundary information was discarded. It does not remove the need for large-field control or insertion renormalization, and it should first be compared on the same two-step example, not launched as a third full campaign.

A materially shorter *core formulation* already exists here: uniform mixed differences of the **normalized local generating functional**, with local contact terms subtracted and separated-source errors bounded. The stated polymer-to-UV-LOC-C mechanism makes only polymers meeting both supports contribute; all fixed higher cumulants then follow by finite differences and Cauchy estimates (`parts/upper-chain.md:365–391`). Test this with smooth scalar sources first, as independent proposes. It still requires proving the complex-source activities and normalization, and does not supply NT2's triple observable automatically. Keep finite physical volume and the terminal weak-coupling window explicit; postpone the volume extension until that theorem is real. Reusing only a constant-coupling pressure bound cannot bypass these issues (`GOAL.md:49–64`; `parts/upper-chain.md:28,41–42`).

## 7. Keep T1 as the destination; choose a smaller theorem with an exact claim as the next deliverable

T1 is not a useful near-term completion target while its interaction criterion and map-dependent tree limit are still being constructed. It is also not yet the full usual continuum Yang–Mills target: O(4) invariance and the mass gap are expressly deferred, and the adopted UV criterion does not select a vacuum (`GOAL.md:22–33`). This is a reason to state the theorem's scope precisely, not to add those harder conditions now. Freeze the observable algebra, normalization, block map and limits before more proof is written. In particular the current witness states L-adic scales and expressly leaves interpolation to arbitrary r open; the report must not silently replace that with an all-real-scale assertion (`parts/witness.md:870–890`).

**My nearer paper candidate is a deterministic constrained-energy theorem, not a claimed continuum theory.** For the explicitly fixed mapped blocking and admissible class/trial of the excess notes, seek: for sufficiently large fixed L there exist f_*(L), nu_*(L)>0 and D(L)<infinity such that, for every depth, compatible finite periodic lattice, allowed terminal parent of flux at most f_*, and positive sampled weight with log-Lipschitz rate nu<=nu_* in terminal units, the selected constrained minimum U and the specified admissible trial T satisfy

`A_U(w) <= (1 + D(L) nu) A_T(w)`.

The theorem must include the required existence/admissibility of these fields, or quantify only over fibres where these are explicitly assumed and say so in its title and statement. It must keep all intermediate class constraints and make D independent of depth, volume, position and weight normalization. This is precisely the substantive WAM target; the paper could include its FALL consequence only under the additional INPUT/TR hypotheses, or prove those too (`library/excess-shell-end.md:49–76,148–159,267–311,468–489,503–529`). It would establish a nonlinear, all-depth energy/localization result for a named SU(2) blocking. It would **not** establish probabilistic large-field rarity, a renormalized coupling trajectory, separated Schwinger functions, NT1, NT2, uniqueness or a mass gap. The scalar slab theorem alone is not that paper's main result. I consider this a plausible publishable unit if proved with a useful uniform bound; novelty relative to Balaban's detailed minimizer estimates has not been established by this bounded review.

**If a physical theorem is required instead**, name the next target “a Wilson-trajectory limit on bounded observables at fixed positive resolution, with nonzero time evolution.” Require one joint RP/hypercubic limit of the specified blocked observables, on the chosen trajectory; a strict difference between two separated connected correlators of one bounded clipped energy observable; and hence H nonzero on the vacuum orthocomplement. State the resolutions and the common subsequence. It would **not** assert point-local dimension-four fields, their arbitrary separated moments, vertex matching, asymptotic interaction, a unique vacuum, O(4) invariance or a mass gap. The existing floor theorem alone is not enough: it also holds for static and product examples. This stronger bounded-observable target would use the milestone plus repaired NT1-F, including tails and the map/fibre identification (`parts/upper-chain.md:22–24,65–76,625–649`; `parts/witness.md:168–170`; finding 4).

That physical target removes the most elaborate point-field and NT2 obligations, but still needs a substantial hard core and mean pullback control. The text does not justify promising it soon. Neither smaller theorem should be called T1 or used to weaken what T1 claims. The deterministic result is the more realistic bounded deliverable from the material inspected.
