4. **CC: the forest calculation survives, but the mark-to-leaf normalization is unspecified.** Exact source: type I is a whole brick (`QBR:71`), C_i is defined as “cells holding a mark … or above a mark” (`QBR:75`), and the merge proof charges distinct forest leaves to individual marks with coefficient 2N_c (`QBR:79`). A brick has t_0^4 cells, explicitly acknowledged in `QBR:279`. There is no specified representative-cell convention in this definition.

   Here is the part I can verify. For nonnegative nonincreasing weights and a forest generated by **one cell per mark**, a k-cell large cluster has at most k/2 distinct ancestors N_c levels later: the walk/colour estimate gives n≤81(1+2k/L)≤k/2 when k>324 and L≥648. Hence at least k/2 merges occur in that interval. Across disjoint starting clusters, their internal merges can be charged without exceeding all merges. Summing gives

   `Σ_i w_i a_i ≤ 2Σ_merges(v) Σ_(v−N_c≤i<v) w_i ≤ 2N_c Σ_merges(v) w_(v−N_c)`.

   Charge a merge to the first-child leaf of each non-first child subtree. These leaves are distinct; their levels k≤v imply w_(v−N_c)≤w_(k−N_c). New marks at later levels do not spoil this forest argument. Small clusters may be charged once per level to a descendant mark; the assumed tail sum of eps_i≤1 then bounds their total by |M| (`QBR:75,79,103`). Thus the mechanism genuinely has no depth factor. Negative weights are not allowed by this argument; the bare “w_i non-increasing” in `QBR:79` should say nonnegative as well (w_i=−1 and a singleton already falsify the unrestricted wording).

   Why that does not yet verify the displayed consequence for the defined marks: if C_k contains the **support cells** of one type-I brick, that one brick gives t_0^4 forest leaves and a_k=t_0^4>K_0. With constant positive weights its contribution at that single level already exceeds the claimed 2N_c per mark whenever t_0^4>2N_c, which is compatible with the displayed lower bound on N_c. If C_k instead means one representative, that counterexample does not apply, but the support and ancestor conventions need to say so. This is an ambiguity/conversion defect, not a counterexample to the intended per-mark bound with geometry-dependent constants.

   **Local repair:** define one representative per brick and prove the collars contain the full marks and the descendant witness supports; or count all support cells and replace the per-mark coefficient by the maximal number of such leaves. The latter costs a fixed factor depending on t_0, not depth, by exactly the calculation above. Its propagation through the displayed psi_b and certificate counts remains **unproved** here. Also define weights at negative indices (e.g. constant continuation at 0) and handle the last N_c levels when the finite hierarchy ends.
