## 2. Counterexample tests (done before the proofs)

**2.1 The signed profile is not a small perturbation of the fine form.** Item (2) of the queue row asks for each comparison "in the proved weighted form norm", the X-form norm of `a2-layers.md` §1, followed by the relative Schur estimate. For the profile this fails. Take a separated sequence of depth k+1 with every layer nonempty and nesting as in SA 6331, with b_i = b > 0 for all i (allowed: SA 9767 asks only -C <= x_q - x_{q'} <= C(q' - q)). On plaquettes of L_0 away from the transition zones, x_k(p) = x_0 = beta, so the relative weight in B units is w(p) = (x_k(p) - x_k)/x_k = b k g_k^2. For h supported in L_0 with ||d_U h||^2 >= (1/2)||h||_X^2 (a smooth bump in a Landau gauge), PX114-PX118 give |sum_p w(p) D^2 A_p[h, h]| >= (1/4) b k g_k^2 ||h||_X^2 for alpha_0 small. Once k >= 4/(b g_k^2), which a deep label allows (the number of layers is not bounded by 1/g_k^2), this exceeds ||h||_X^2. So the fine-form route fails for every gap c_L. It is also not needed. The profile never enters the fine operators: G_0, G, G_1 use the plain Delta^W (SA 10140, 9589). It acts only through B -> A(w, U(V(B))), and there the response decays across the layers. Section 6 proves that the resulting coarse perturbation is O(g_k^2), uniformly in depth.

**2.2 Unsubtracted counterterm arrays.** Taken alone, the array (b_j A(phi_j, .))_{j<=k} contributes sum_{j<=k} b_j g_k^2 times a positive form of A's size to the coarse Hessian. This is ~ c_0 k g_k^2, not uniformly small (SA 7864). Only the subtracted brackets E^(j) - b_j A(phi_j) are summable, with weights omega_n <= C(n+1)^{3/2} L^{-alpha_H(n-1)} at age n = k+1-j (SA 7864). The step Gaussian carries x_k = beta - sum_{j<=k} b_j, so the consumed grouping is the subtracted one. Section 7.

**2.3 The gauge-fixing gradient off the axial locus.** GF_k(V) = sum_{y,x}[1 - (1/2) Re tr V(y,x)] is absolute (SA 7182). At a complex pair P_c = (U_c, J), U_c = e^{i eta A'}U, with M^k U block axial, V_c(y,x) = e^{i theta_{yx}} with theta_{yx} = O(C_L alpha_1) and theta_{yx} not zero in general. Then J-hat_GF(V_c) is not zero, and the next-fibre Hessian PX104 differs from the formula A(P_c) by exactly -C_V^T E_V^(2)[J-hat_GF] C_V. This is the term the a2-layers gate found. It is O(alpha_1), not a counterexample to the half-gap, and it vanishes wherever V_c(y,x) = 1 exactly. The physical complex pairs, obtained by continuing the axial representative in the datum, are of that kind. Section 4.

**2.4 Decoupling parameters near the support.** If a parameter cube met the support Z_0, a walk of length zero with s^omega = s(cube) would put s into the diagonal of chi A(s) chi. At s = 0, or at s = -e^{kappa-bar}, the local diagonal block is then zero or of the wrong sign, and coercivity fails. The outline excludes this: only coarse cubes outside the collar Z_0^# carry parameters (SA 14160), and the first 2^4 cubes near a walk's start carry none (SA 10552, the factor e^{2^4 kappa-bar} and sigma_0). With that convention every s-dependent term crosses the collar. Section 8 proves the gap given the walk bounds, with the collar width chosen after kappa-bar.

**2.5 Complex coefficient ratio.** Re<u, tau A u> >= (Re tau) c_L||u||^2 - |Im tau| ||A|| ||u||^2. This needs an upper bound on ||A||. The bound is supplied in Section 5 (Lemma 5.1), so |tau - 1| <= c_L/(4(c_L + C_A)) suffices.

No counterexample to a consumed statement was found. Tests 2.1 and 2.2 kill two readings of item (2): the fine-form route for profiles, and comparison of the unsubtracted arrays. They fix where each comparison must be made.
