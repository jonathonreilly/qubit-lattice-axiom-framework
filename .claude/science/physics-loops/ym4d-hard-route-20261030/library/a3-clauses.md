## 2. Periodisation: Theorem 4, third paragraph

**Setting.** Fix \(L\) and a depth \(k\ge1\), and work in level-\(k\) units: level-\(k\) spacing \(1\), bare spacing \(\eta=L^{-k}\). For an integer \(\ell\) let \(T_\ell=\eta\mathbb Z^4/\ell\mathbb Z^4\), with its sublattices and centred blocks. The *single lattice* on \(T_\ell\) is the sequence \(\mathbf B_\ell\) with \(\Omega_0=\dots=\Omega_k=T_\ell\). Its layers \(\mathcal L_j\), \(j<k\), are empty, every top is a level-\(k\) site with \(\xi_y=1\), the determining set is the set of bonds of \(T_\ell^{(k)}\), every row is interior, and \(d_{\mathbf B}\) is path length in level-\(k\) units (SA 10120–10125). Hence \(d_{\mathbf B}(x,x')\ge|x-x'|_{T_\ell,\infty}\), the torus \(\infty\)-distance. The separation condition is vacuous. Let \(\mathfrak L_*\) be the set of admissible sides: those \(\ell\ge\ell_*\) for which \(\mathbf B_\ell\) is an admissible separated sequence above the torus floors of SA (1b)(ii) and the nonwrapping floors of `flat-port.md` §12. For example, take the multiples of the cube size that are at least \(\ell_*\). Here \(\ell_*\) depends on \(L\) only, \(\ell_*\) exceeds twice every stencil range used below, and \(m\mathfrak L_*\subset\mathfrak L_*\) for every integer \(m\ge1\).

Over \(\mathbf V_0=\mathbf 1\) the background is \(U_0=\mathbf 1\) (SA 9672) and \(\lambda_0=0\). Write \(\mathsf H_{k,\ell}=\partial_B\mathcal H(0)\) on \(\mathbf B_\ell\), and let its kernel \(H_\ell(b',c)\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g^c)\) (\(b'\) a bare bond, \(c\) a level-\(k\) bond) be defined by \((\mathsf H_{k,\ell}(e\delta_c))(b')=H_\ell(b',c)e\). Distances between bonds are \(\infty\)-distances between their midpoints.

\[
 \|H_\ell(b',c)\|\le C_1e^{-\theta|b'-c|_{T_\ell,\infty}}\qquad(\ell\in\mathfrak L_*),
 \tag{2.1}
\]
with \(C_1,\theta\) depending on \(L\) only, not on \(k\) or \(\ell\).

**Counterexample attempts first.** (a) *Torus zero modes.* Constant 1-forms have \(da=0\) and \(D^{\mathsf T}a=0\), but the rows see them (\(Ka\) is the same constant). Constant site functions are not in \(\ker Q'\). (I2)(a) holds on every admissible torus, so there is no zero mode to break uniqueness. (b) *Flat holonomy.* A nontrivial flat holonomy would break translation invariance of the kernel. It is excluded by \(\mathbf V_0=\mathbf 1\). (c) *Small tori.* For \(\ell<\theta^{-1}\) the bound below is trivial with a larger constant, and below \(\ell_*\) nothing is claimed (the outline's floors). (d) *The gate's family \(c_kK\).* It has a holomorphy strip and unbounded constants. Here it is excluded, because \(C_1\) in (2.1) does not depend on \(k\). No counterexample.

**Lemma \(\Pi\).1 (covering identity).** Let \(\ell\in\mathfrak L_*\) and \(m\ge1\). Let \(\pi:T_{m\ell}\to T_\ell\) be the covering map, and \(\pi^*\) the pullback of functions on bonds, sites, tops and rows. Then \(S^\circ_{m\ell}\pi^*=\pi^*S^\circ_\ell\) and \(S^{\circ-1}_{m\ell}\pi^*=\pi^*S^{\circ-1}_\ell\). Consequently, for a bare bond \(\tilde b'\) and a level-\(k\) bond \(\tilde c\) of \(T_{m\ell}\),
\[
 H_\ell(\pi\tilde b',\pi\tilde c)=\sum_{n\in\ell\mathbb Z^4/m\ell\mathbb Z^4}H_{m\ell}(\tilde b',\tilde c+n).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

*Proof.* At \(U=\mathbf 1\), each of \(d,d^{\mathsf T},D,D^{\mathsf T},K,K^{\mathsf T},Q',Q'^{\mathsf T}\) and the scalar masses is given on \(T_\ell\) and on \(T_{m\ell}\) by one translation-invariant stencil of range at most \(C_L\). The flat straightened row is the straight-segment average over its pillar (`p82-curved.md` (4.1); `flat-port.md` (2.1)). The plain pillar mean averages over a level-\(k\) block. The pairings of `p82-curved.md` §1 are translation-invariant sums with the same cell weights on both tori, so each transpose has the reflected stencil. Since \(2C_L<\ell_*\le\ell\), \(\pi\) is injective on every stencil, and so \((X_{m\ell}\pi^*f)(\tilde x)=\sum_{\tilde x'}X(\tilde x-\tilde x')f(\pi\tilde x')=(X_\ell f)(\pi\tilde x)\) for each such \(X\). If \(X_\ell\) and \(X_{m\ell}\) are invertible and \(X_{m\ell}\pi^*=\pi^*X_\ell\), then \(X_{m\ell}\pi^*X_\ell^{-1}=\pi^*\), so \(\pi^*X_\ell^{-1}=X_{m\ell}^{-1}\pi^*\). Apply this in turn to: \(A_s=D^{\mathsf T}D+Q'^{\mathsf T}m^{-2}Q'\), invertible on both tori (`p82-curved.md` Lemma 3.1(i), gated §3); \(Q'S^2Q'^{\mathsf T}\), positive definite on both; hence to \(S\), \(N\) and \(R=1-SQ'^{\mathsf T}NQ'S\); then to the block operator \(S^\circ\); and, by (I2)(a), to \(S^{\circ-1}\). The source \(e\delta_c\) on \(T_\ell\) pulls back to \(\sum_ne\delta_{\tilde c+n}\), \(n\in\ell\mathbb Z^4/m\ell\mathbb Z^4\). Evaluate the field component of \(\pi^*S^{\circ-1}_\ell(0,e\delta_c)=S^{\circ-1}_{m\ell}(0,\sum_ne\delta_{\tilde c+n})\) at \(\tilde b'\). \(\square\)

The identity is exact and finite, and it uses no bound.

**Lemma \(\Pi\).2 (image sum).** Let \(\ell\in\mathfrak L_*\) with \(\theta\ell\ge1\), and let \(\tilde b',\tilde c\) in \(T_{m\ell}\) satisfy \(|\tilde b'-\tilde c|_{T_{m\ell},\infty}\le\ell/2\). Then
\[
 \sum_{n\in\ell\mathbb Z^4/m\ell\mathbb Z^4,\ n\ne0}\|H_{m\ell}(\tilde b',\tilde c+n)\|\le C_2e^{-\theta\ell/2},\qquad C_2=4000\,C_1 .
\]

*Proof.* Represent \(\tilde b',\tilde c\) by points of \(\eta\mathbb Z^4\) with \(|\tilde b'-\tilde c|_\infty\le\ell/2\). For a class \(n\ne0\), let \(n'(n)\) minimise \(|n'|_\infty\) over \(n'\in n+m\ell\mathbb Z^4\). The classes are disjoint, so \(n\mapsto n'(n)\) is injective into \(\ell\mathbb Z^4\setminus\{0\}\). Also \(|\tilde b'-\tilde c-n|_{T_{m\ell},\infty}=\min_{n'\in n+m\ell\mathbb Z^4}|\tilde b'-\tilde c-n'|_\infty\ge|n'(n)|_\infty-\ell/2\). By (2.1) the sum is at most \(C_1\sum_{n'\in\ell\mathbb Z^4\setminus0}e^{-\theta(|n'|_\infty-\ell/2)}\). The shell \(|n'|_\infty=s\ell\) has \((2s+1)^4-(2s-1)^4=64s^3+16s\le80s^3\) points. With \(\theta\ell\ge1\) the sum is at most \(80C_1e^{-\theta\ell/2}\sum_{s\ge1}s^3e^{-(s-1)}\). Since \(s^3\le11e^{s/2}\) for \(s\ge1\) (\(s^3e^{-s/2}\) is largest at \(s=6\), where it is \(216e^{-3}<11\)), the series is at most \(11e^{1/2}/(1-e^{-1/2})<50\). \(\square\)

**Theorem \(\Pi\) (periodisation).** There is a kernel \(H_\infty(b',c)\in\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak g^c)\) on pairs of a bare bond and a level-\(k\) bond of the infinite lattice \(\eta\mathbb Z^4\), and a constant \(C_3\) depending on \(L\) only, such that:

- (i) \(H_\infty(b'+n,c+n)=H_\infty(b',c)\) for \(n\in\mathbb Z^4\) (level-\(k\) translations);
- (ii) \(\|H_\infty(b',c)\|\le C_1e^{-\theta|b'-c|_\infty}\);
- (iii) for every \(\ell\in\mathfrak L_*\) and all \(b',c\), \(H_\ell(\pi b',\pi c)=\sum_{n\in\ell\mathbb Z^4}H_\infty(b',c+n)\), absolutely convergent;
- (iv) if \(|b'-c|_\infty\le\ell/2\), then \(\|H_\ell(\pi b',\pi c)-H_\infty(b',c)\|\le C_3e^{-\theta\ell/2}\).

\(H_\infty\) is unique, since (iv) forces \(H_\infty(b',c)=\lim_{\ell\to\infty,\ \ell\in\mathfrak L_*}H_\ell(\pi b',\pi c)\). In the clause's notation, \(\mathsf H_k\) is the periodisation (iii) of the \(\mathbb Z^4\)-invariant kernel \(H_\infty\), and (iv) is the bound \(Ce^{-c\delta_0\ell_k/2}\) with \(\delta_0=\delta_w\), the walk rate of `p82-curved.md`, and \(c=\theta/\delta_w\) (\(=1/2\) for \(\theta=\theta_0\)).

*Proof.* *Step 1 (the limit).* Fix \(b',c\). For \(\ell\in\mathfrak L_*\) with \(\ell\ge\max(2|b'-c|_\infty,\theta^{-1})\) and any \(m\ge1\), (2.2) with \(\tilde b'=\pi_{m\ell}b'\), \(\tilde c=\pi_{m\ell}c\) gives \(H_\ell(\pi_\ell b',\pi_\ell c)-H_{m\ell}(\pi_{m\ell}b',\pi_{m\ell}c)=\sum_{n\ne0}H_{m\ell}(\tilde b',\tilde c+n)\). This has norm at most \(C_2e^{-\theta\ell/2}\) by Lemma \(\Pi\).2, since \(|\tilde b'-\tilde c|_{T_{m\ell},\infty}\le|b'-c|_\infty\le\ell/2\). For two such \(\ell,\ell'\), compare both with \(\ell\ell'\in\mathfrak L_*\): \(\|H_\ell-H_{\ell'}\|\le C_2(e^{-\theta\ell/2}+e^{-\theta\ell'/2})\). So the values form a Cauchy family; let \(H_\infty(b',c)\) be the limit. Letting \(m\to\infty\) in the first bound gives (iv) with \(C_2\) for \(\ell\ge\theta^{-1}\). For \(\ell<\theta^{-1}\), both terms are bounded by \(C_1\) (by (2.1) and (ii) below), so their difference is at most \(2C_1\le2e^{1/2}C_1e^{-\theta\ell/2}\). Take \(C_3=\max(C_2,2e^{1/2}C_1)\).

*Step 2 ((i), (ii)).* On each \(T_\ell\), \(S^\circ_\ell\) commutes with the level-\(k\) translations (the stencil argument of Lemma \(\Pi\).1, with a translation in place of \(\pi\)) and is invertible, so \(H_\ell\) is translation invariant. The limit inherits this, which is (i). For \(\ell>2|b'-c|_\infty\), \(|b'-c|_{T_\ell,\infty}=|b'-c|_\infty\), so (2.1) passes to the limit as (ii).

*Step 3 ((iii)).* Fix \(b',c,\ell\), and apply (2.2) on \(T_{m\ell}\) as \(m\to\infty\). As class representatives take the \(n\in\ell\mathbb Z^4\) with \(b'-c-n\in(-m\ell/2,m\ell/2]^4\). The half-open box is a fundamental domain of \(m\ell\mathbb Z^4\), so it meets each coset exactly once. For these \(n\), \(|b'-c-n|_{T_{m\ell},\infty}=|b'-c-n|_\infty\). So by (2.1) the \(n\)-th term is bounded by \(C_1e^{-\theta|b'-c-n|_\infty}\), which is summable over \(\ell\mathbb Z^4\) and independent of \(m\). For each fixed \(n\), eventually \(n\) is a representative and \(m\ell\ge\max(2|b'-c-n|_\infty,\theta^{-1})\). Then Step 1, applied to \((b',c+n)\), gives \(H_{m\ell}(\pi b',\pi(c+n))\to H_\infty(b',c+n)\). Dominated convergence gives (iii). \(\square\)

**Remarks.** (1) The same proof applies to every block of \(S^{\circ-1}\) at \(U=\mathbf 1\) (\(T,H,H^{\mathsf T},C_0^{-1}-1\) and the gradient outputs). It applies to any translation-invariant operator built from the flat local operators by finitely many inverses that exists on all admissible tori with a uniform exponential bound. ## 3. The linear package at complex references: Theorem P\(^{\mathsf c}\)

**The class.** Take a separated sequence without inward bonds, with \(\rho\ge\rho_0\), above the floors. A configuration \(\mathbf U\) is in the *complex regular class* at radii \((\alpha_0,\alpha_1)\) if \(\mathbf U=e^{i\eta A'}U\) (start-point convention) with:
- \(U\) real, satisfying (Reg\(_{\alpha_0}\)) of `p82-curved.md` §1 (real gauges on cubes of side \(c_{\rm reg}M\xi_j\) in \(\Omega_0\cap(\mathcal L_j\cup\mathcal L_{j+1})\), with \(\xi_j|A|+\xi_j^2|\nabla A|\le\alpha_0\));
- \(|\partial U-\mathbf 1|,|\partial\mathbf U-\mathbf 1|<\alpha_0\eta^2\xi_j^{-2}\) and \(\xi_j|A'|+\xi_j^2|\nabla_UA'|<\alpha_1\) on \(\mathcal L_j\).

This is the regular-pair class of SA 10129–10137 without its current clause, with its \(M_1\) equal to the walk size \(M\) and its \(O(1)\) equal to \(c_{\rm reg}\), as in `p82-curved.md` §1. Let \(\mathcal O\) be the set of such \(\mathbf U\in SL(2,\mathbb C)^{\rm bonds}\). It is open: the inequalities are strict and finitely many, and a small change of \(\mathbf U\) is absorbed into \(A'\) with \(U\) fixed.

**Operators at \(\mathbf U\).** They are defined by the formulas used at real \(U\), with bilinear transposes (`a3-derivative.md` §2). \(d_{\mathbf U},D_{\mathbf U},\nabla_{\mathbf U}\) use the transports \(\operatorname{Ad}\mathbf U(b)\). \(Q'_{\mathbf U}\) is the pillar mean of the contour transports of \(\mathbf U\): the group mean of SA 7065 is defined and holomorphic for nearby complex elements (`a3-derivative.md` §4.1). \(K_{\mathbf U}\) is the derivative at \(A=0\) of the straightened rows of \(e^{i\eta A}\mathbf U\), given by the holomorphic finite-step recursion (`a3-derivative.md` (5); `a3-balaban.md` §8.5). \(A_s(\mathbf U)\), \(\mathbb S(\mathbf U)\) and \(\mathbb M(\mathbf U)\) are as in `p82-curved.md` (3.1). When \(\mathbb S(\mathbf U)\) is invertible, let \(P_{\mathbf U}\) be its \(h\leftarrow g_1\) block, put \(R_{\mathbf U}=1-P_{\mathbf U}\), and form \(S^\circ(\mathbf U)\) and \(\Sigma(\mathbf U)\) as in `p82-curved.md` §1 and Lemma 3.1. The norms \(X_w,F_w,Y_w\) (`a3-derivative.md` (2)) and \(\mathcal X\) (`p82-curved.md` §3) use \(\nabla_{\mathbf U}\) and the Hermitian norm on \(\mathfrak g^c\). The real norms change by a factor \(1\pm C\alpha_1\), since \(|\nabla_{\mathbf U}f-\nabla_Uf|\le4|A'||f|\).

*This \(R_{\mathbf U}\) is the outline's \(R\).* Where \(A_s(\mathbf U)\) and \(Q'_{\mathbf U}S_{\mathbf U}^2Q'^{\mathsf T}_{\mathbf U}\) are invertible, solving the rows of \(\mathbb S\) in the order 3, 2, 4 (the pure algebra of `p82-curved.md` Lemma 3.1(i)) gives \(P_{\mathbf U}=S_{\mathbf U}Q'^{\mathsf T}N_{\mathbf U}Q'S_{\mathbf U}\). That is the formula of SA 10140, with its top weight \(a\) replaced by \(m^{-2}\). The weight does not matter. For fixed real \(U\), the continuations in \(A'\) built with the two weights are holomorphic on a ball, and they agree for real-valued \(A'\), where \(\mathbf U\) is real and \(R\) does not depend on the weight (`gates/p82-curved-gate.md` §3). The real-valued \(A'\) form a totally real subspace of full dimension, so the identity theorem makes the two continuations agree on the whole ball.

**Theorem P\(^{\mathsf c}\).** Fix \(L\ge L_0\) and \(\alpha_{\rm H}\in(0,1)\). There are \(\alpha^{\mathsf c}_*,\alpha_{1*}>0\) and \(C^\circ_{\mathsf c}\), depending only on \(L\) and \(\alpha_{\rm H}\), such that the following holds for every \(\mathbf U\) in the complex regular class with \(\alpha_0\le\alpha^{\mathsf c}_*\) and \(\alpha_1\le\alpha_{1*}\):
- (a) \(\mathbb S(\mathbf U)\), \(\mathbb M(\mathbf U)\) and \(S^\circ(\mathbf U)\) are invertible, and \(K_{\mathbf U}\) is onto;
- (b)–(e) of Theorem P hold at \(\mathbf U\) with \(C^\circ_{\mathsf c}\) in place of \(C^\circ\): the weighted value, gradient and multiplier bounds; the Hölder member at separations at most \(\xi_y\); \(D(1-R)D^{\mathsf T}:X_w\to F_w\); and the BGR11 row sums;
- (f) the same holds with the plaquette curl \(\mathfrak d_{\mathbf U}\) in place of \(d_{\mathbf U}\);
- (g) \(\mathbf U\mapsto R_{\mathbf U}\), \(S^\circ(\mathbf U)^{-1}\) and every block of \(\mathbb M(\mathbf U)^{-1}\) are holomorphic on the open set of such \(\mathbf U\).

**Counterexample attempts first.**
- (a) *Kato at complex links.* For complex \(\mathbf U\), \(|\operatorname{Ad}\mathbf U(b)v|\) can exceed \(|v|\) by \(e^{2\eta|\operatorname{Im}A'|}\). Then \(\Delta|u|\le|\Delta_{\mathbf U}u|\) fails by an error of order \(\eta^{-2}\cdot\eta|A'||u|\sim\alpha_1/(\eta m)\), which is larger than the admissible zeroth-order error \(\alpha/m^2\) by the factor \(m/\eta\). This is the gate's objection, and it is real. It is not a counterexample to Theorem P\(^{\mathsf c}\): Lemma 6.1' below uses Kato only at the real \(U\), and it treats \(\Delta_{\mathbf U}-\Delta_U\) as a first-order perturbation, whose large terms cancel between forward and backward shifts.
- (b) *Bare cells with arbitrary real links and a complex part* (the geometry of gate D3). The bare model at the real \(U\) handles arbitrary real links (Lemma 5.0). The complex part enters only through \(\eta|A'|\le\alpha_1\), so in the bare scaling it has relative size \(\alpha_1\). No failure.
- (c) *Large real current.* The real part may be staggered, with \(|J(U)|\) up to \(C\alpha_0\eta^{-1}\xi^{-2}\) (SA 10531). \(S^\circ\) contains no current, and Theorem P reads only (Reg). No failure.
- (d) *The derivative of the complex part.* Without \(\xi^2|\nabla_UA'|<\alpha_1\), Step 2(vi) below fails, because \(D^{\mathsf T}(\mathcal A\lambda)\) needs \(\nabla\hat A\). This is the complex analogue of test 2.4 of `p82-curved.md`. The class contains the bound (SA 10133). No failure.

**Step 1 (cell gauge of \(\mathbf U\)).** On a cube \(Z\) of (Reg\(_{\alpha_0}\)), let \(g\) be the real gauge with \(U^g=e^{i\eta A_0}\) and \(m|A_0|+m^2|\nabla A_0|\le\alpha_0\). As in `a3-derivative.md` 7.1, the exact plaquette Hessian PX114 (SA 11313–11327) has remainder \(|\mathcal R_p(v,w)|\le C\|P-\mathbf 1\|\eta^2|v|_p|w|_p\). This is a bound on convergent series in the bond variables, so it holds at the complex plaquette \(P=\partial\mathbf U(p)\), where \(\|P-\mathbf 1\|<\alpha_0\eta^2\xi^{-2}\). *Contraction.* Use the norm \(\|(a,\lambda)\|=\|a\|_X+\|\lambda\|_Y\). *Analyticity.* \(\Psi_P\) is holomorphic in \((\mathbf U,\mathbf J,B,a,\lambda)\): by Theorem P\(^{\mathsf c}\)(g), by the holomorphy of \(F_{\mathbf U}\), \(\mathcal Q^{\rm st}_{\mathbf U}\) and \(\mathsf b_{\mathbf U}\), and because it is affine in \(\mathbf J\).
