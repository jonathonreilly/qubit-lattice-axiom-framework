### The exact B102 target in the account's notation

Fix dimension \(d\), block factor \(L\), depth \(k\), fine spacing \(\eta\), and
an admissible nest \(\Omega_0\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_k\). Write

\[
 \ell_j=L^j\eta,\qquad
 \mathfrak B_k=\bigcup_{j=0}^k\Lambda_j,\qquad
 B^j(\Lambda_j)\cup B^{j+1}(\Lambda_{j+1})
       =\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+2}.
\]

The nest has the block and separation conditions (1) of CMP 102. At the end of
the nest put the later domains empty. Use the paper's incidence convention:
a bond belongs to a domain when at least one endpoint belongs to it, and a
plaquette when at least one corner belongs to it. Boundary data outside the
varied bonds are fixed. Define the real regular class by (2):

\[
 |U(\partial p)-1|<\varepsilon_0\eta^2\ell_j^{-2},\qquad
 |D_U^{\eta *}\partial U(b)|<\varepsilon_0\eta^2\ell_j^{-3}
       \quad(p,b\in\Omega_j).
 \tag{T1}
\]

The constraint class \(\mathfrak B_k(\mathfrak B_k,V)\) means

\[
 \bar U^{\,j}=V\quad\hbox{on }\Lambda_j.                 \tag{T2}
\]

These are Balaban's iterated nonabelian averages, not freely replaceable by
ordinary arithmetic means. The residual gauges equal \(1\) at determining sites.
The action is

\[
 S_\eta(U)=\sum_{p\in\Omega_0}\eta^{d-4}
                  [1-\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U(\partial p)].
 \tag{T3}
\]

The datum satisfies \(|\partial V(p')-1|<\varepsilon_1\), using the mixed-level
plaquettes of CMP 102 (7). When an edge lies in the finer part, its factor is
the intervening averaged transport defined there. This convention is part of
the hypothesis; ordinary same-level plaquettes alone are insufficient.

There are \(a_0,a_1,B_3>0\), depending only on \(d,L\), with \(B_3a_1\le a_0\).
For each such nest and datum with \(0<\varepsilon_1\le a_1\), there is a minimal
orbit in

\[
 \mathfrak U_k(\{\Omega_j\},B_3\varepsilon_1)
       \cap\mathfrak B_k(\mathfrak B_k,V).                \tag{T4}
\]

It is the unique critical orbit in the class with

\[
                   B_3\varepsilon_1\le\varepsilon_0\le a_0.
\]

The last uniqueness clause is in CMP 102 Theorem 1 and CA-II 8.4(a).
CA-I 4.9 quotes the existence and cube-gauge part only.
“Minimal” here retains the regular-class variational meaning. The proof below
does not assert an absolute minimum over unrestricted lattice configurations.

Let \(M'\) be a multiple of \(R_1M_1\). The cube has side \(2M'\ell_j\).
Its concentric enlargement has side \((2M'+4R_1M_1)\ell_j\), lies in

\[
 B^j(\Lambda_j)\cup B^{j+1}(\Lambda_{j+1}),
\]

and is not contained in \(B^{j+1}(\Lambda_{j+1})\). If

\[
             M'\le M(\varepsilon_1):=R_1M_1a_1/\varepsilon_1,
\]

there is a gauge on a neighbourhood of the cube such that

\[
 U^{u^{-1}}=e^{i\eta A},\qquad
 |A|<B_3M'\varepsilon_1\ell_j^{-1},\qquad
 |\nabla^\eta A|<B_3M'\varepsilon_1\ell_j^{-2}.           \tag{T5}
\]

These are exactly the quantitative cube clauses quoted in CA-I 35144–35199.
CMP 102 (9)–(10) also require the Hölder and second-order bounds. For each
fixed exponent ceiling below \(1\), their scale powers are respectively

\[
 B_4 M'\varepsilon_1\ell_j^{-2-\beta},\qquad
 B_3 M'\varepsilon_1\ell_j^{-3}
       \quad\hbox{for }\partial^{\eta *}\partial^\eta A,\ \Delta^\eta A.
 \tag{T6}
\]

The damaged exponent restriction in the OCR is not quoted verbatim. The
subunit Hölder ceiling and powers are read with CMP 99b (3.40)–(3.43) and
the first three members of CA-I `4.5:eq-chart-variation-decay-b`.
No endpoint estimate at exponent \(1\) is asserted.

For Proposition 9, fix a real datum \(V_0\) and the chosen axial representative

\[
                  U_0=U_k(V_0),\qquad V'=e^{iB}.
\]

For complex \(B\) in a sufficiently small uniform sup-norm ball, the axial
representative \(U_k(V'V_0)\) extends holomorphically. Its relative field, in the
normalised Landau gauge, is \(e^{i\eta\mathcal H(B)}\). For

\[
 x\in\Delta(y),\quad y\in\Lambda_j,\quad y'\in\Lambda_{j'},
\]

the first two derivative bounds required by CA-II 8.4 are

\[
 \left|{\delta\mathcal H(x)\over\delta B(y')}\right|
 \le C\ell_j^{-1}\ell_{j'}^{-d}e^{-\delta_0d(y,y')/8},\qquad
 \left|\nabla_{U_0}{\delta\mathcal H(x)\over\delta B(y')}\right|
 \le C\ell_j^{-2}\ell_{j'}^{-d}e^{-\delta_0d(y,y')/8}.     \tag{T7}
\]

The functional kernel is paired with the coarse measure \(\ell_{j'}^d\).
A coordinate derivative at a single bond is therefore \(\ell_{j'}^d\) times
this kernel. CMP 102 (190) has further Hölder and second-order members.
The present round must not replace them by a value estimate.

### H1. Constrained inverse, minimiser and variational equation

This ports the linear algebra in CMP 102 (105)–(111), (129)–(133) and
CA-I 13545–13679. Let \(E,F\) be finite real inner-product spaces,

\[
 K=K^*>0\text{ on }E,\qquad Q:E\longrightarrow F\text{ onto},\qquad G=K^{-1}.
\]

One may first restrict \(E\) to a fixed linear gauge slice. Put

\[
 C=QGQ^*,\quad H=GQ^*C^{-1},\quad P=I-HQ,\quad
 \mathcal G=G-GQ^*C^{-1}QG.                              \tag{H1.1}
\]

Then \(C>0\), \(QH=I\), \(QP=0\), \(P^2=P\),

\[
 \mathcal G=PG=GP^*,\qquad K\mathcal G=P^*,\qquad Q\mathcal G=0.
 \tag{H1.2}
\]

The unique minimum of \(\frac12(A,KA)\) on \(QA=B\) is \(HB\).
If \(W(A)\) is differentiable, the stationary equation for

\[
                   (A,J)+\tfrac12(A,KA)+W(A),\qquad QA=B,
\]

is precisely

\[
             A=HB+a,\qquad a=-\mathcal G[J+W'(HB+a)].     \tag{H1.3}
\]

**Proof.** If \(v\ne0\), surjectivity gives \(Q^*v\ne0\), and

\[
 (v,Cv)=(Q^*v,GQ^*v)>0.
\]

Thus all inverses in (H1.1) exist. Multiplying (H1.1) gives (H1.2).
For \(Qz=0\),

\[
                    (z,KHB)=(Qz,C^{-1}B)=0.
\]

Therefore the energy at \(HB+z\) is the energy at \(HB\) plus

\[
                         \tfrac12(z,Kz).
\]

It is strictly larger when \(z\ne0\). Stationarity is

\[
                    (z,Ka+J+W'(HB+a))=0\quad(z\in\ker Q).
\]

For any \(f\in E\), \(K\mathcal Gf=P^*f\) implies

\[
                 (z,K\mathcal G f)=(Pz,f)=(z,f).
\]

The positive restriction of \(K\) to \(\ker Q\) is invertible. Consequently
the preceding stationary equation has exactly the form (H1.3). Conversely,
(H1.3) gives both the constraint and the stationary equation. This proves
all the claims. The same identities extend holomorphically wherever the
complex inverses exist. Positivity itself is only asserted on the real space. ∎

For Balaban's dimensionless data, replace \(H\) in (H1.1) by \(H\ell^{-1}\).
This gives \(QH=\ell^{-1}\), exactly as in (129). It accounts for a scale
factor that the OCR can otherwise conceal.

### H2. The analytic constraint-flattening map

This is the argument of CMP 102 Proposition 3, ported through CA-I
13980–14082. Let \(X,Z\) be finite complex normed spaces and \(B_0>0\). Let \(L:X\to Z\)
and \(H:Z\to X\) satisfy \(LH=I\), \(\|H\|\le B_0\). Suppose

\[
 q(A)=LA+C(A),\qquad \|C(A)\|\le C_2\|A\|_X^2
                       \quad(\|A\|_X<c_4),              \tag{H2.1}
\]

where \(C\) is holomorphic. Choose \(r>0\) such that

\[
             3r<c_4,\qquad18C_2B_0r\le1.               \tag{H2.2}
\]

For \(\|A'\|_X<r\) there is a unique \(D(A')\) in \(\|D\|\le r/B_0\)
satisfying

\[
 D=C(A'-HD),\quad T(A')=A'-HD(A'),\quad q(T(A'))=LA'.     \tag{H2.3}
\]

Moreover

\[
 \|D(A')\|\le4C_2\|A'\|_X^2,\qquad \|T(A')\|_X\le2\|A'\|_X.
 \tag{H2.4}
\]

The maps are holomorphic. Their range contains \(\|A\|_X<r/4\), and there

\[
                         T^{-1}(A)=A+HC(A).             \tag{H2.5}
\]

**Proof.** The map \(x\mapsto C(A'-Hx)\) sends the closed ball

\[
                    \|x\|\le r/B_0
\]

into itself, since its norm is at most \(4C_2r^2\le r/B_0\).
For \(\|z\|\le2r\), apply the scalar Cauchy integral to

\[
                 t\longmapsto C(z+tv),\qquad |t|=r/\|v\|.
\]

For \(v\ne0\), it gives \(\|C'(z)v\|\le9C_2r\|v\|\); for \(v=0\) the
derivative bound is immediate. The entire closed disc lies in the
analytic domain by (H2.2); using a slightly smaller circle and passing to the
limit handles its boundary. Integrating the derivative on the segment
between two \(x\)'s gives contraction factor \(9C_2B_0r\le1/2\).
The successive differences of the iterates have a geometric majorant.
Their sum converges in the closed ball and solves (H2.3). The same contraction
bound proves uniqueness. Iterates are holomorphic and converge uniformly on
every smaller parameter polydisc. Passing their Cauchy integrals to the limit
proves holomorphy, without an extra implicit-function assumption.

Apply the construction on any radius \(s>\|A'\|_X\), \(s\le r\).
Uniqueness identifies its solution with the one just constructed. Then

\[
 \|D\|\le C_2(\|A'\|_X+B_0\|D\|)^2\le4C_2s^2.
\]

Let \(s\downarrow\|A'\|_X\). This proves the first bound in (H2.4);
the second follows from \(4B_0C_2r\le1\). Equation (H2.3) and \(LH=I\)
give its exact linearisation identity.

For \(\|A\|_X<r/4\), let \(A'=A+HC(A)\). Its norm is below \(r\), and

\[
                         C(A'-HC(A))=C(A).
\]

Also \(\|C(A)\|<r/B_0\). Uniqueness of the fixed point identifies

\[
                  D(A')=C(A),\qquad T(A')=A.
\]

Conversely \(A=T(A')\) implies \(A'=A+HC(A)\). This proves (H2.5). ∎

The finite algebra in this lemma does not supply the uniform constants in
(H2.1) for Balaban's iterated average. Nor does it construct his \(H\).

### H3. Weighted algebra and derivatives of the correction

The weighted argument is ported from CA-I 13402–13544 and 13980–14082.
Here is a version that keeps the fine and coarse measures explicit.
Let cells be indexed by \(y\), with scale \(\ell_y=L^{j(y)}\eta\) and metric

\[
 d(y,z)\ge g(|j(y)-j(z)|-1)_+.
\]

Assume \(\sum_z e^{-\delta d(y,z)/4}\le S\), uniformly in \(y\).
For \(|\alpha|\le A\), assume \(g\delta/4\ge A\log L\). Set

\[
 |f|_{\alpha,\rho}=\sup_y\rho_y\ell_y^{-\alpha}\sup_{\Delta(y)}|f|,
 \qquad \rho_y=e^{\kappa d(y,S_0)},\quad0\le\kappa\le\delta/2.
\]

If \(T\) obeys the cell estimate

\[
 \|1_{\Delta(y)}T f_z\|_\infty
       \le C\ell_y^a e^{-\delta d(y,z)}\|f_z\|_\infty,
 \quad\operatorname{supp}f_z\subset\Delta(z),            \tag{H3.1}
\]

then

\[
                       |Tf|_{\alpha+a,\rho}
                  \le CL^AS\,|f|_{\alpha,\rho}.         \tag{H3.2}
\]

**Proof.** Decompose \(f\) by the disjoint cells. The triangle inequality gives

\[
 \rho_y\ell_y^{-\alpha-a}|Tf(y)|
 \le C\sum_z e^{-\delta d(y,z)}{\rho_y\over\rho_z}
           \left({\ell_z\over\ell_y}\right)^\alpha
           |f|_{\alpha,\rho}.
\]

The weight ratio is at most \(e^{\delta d/2}\), and the scale ratio is at most

\[
                      L^A e^{\delta d/4}.
\]

The remaining exponential sums to \(S\). This proves (H3.2).
For overlapping enlarged cells, use a partition with bounded overlap and
include that overlap in \(C\). It must be uniform, not a count of fine sites. ∎

This proof starts with a cell estimate. If it is obtained from a fine kernel,
the integration is \(\sum_{x'}\eta^d\), and a cell has volume \(\ell_z^d\).
For a coarse kernel the integration is \(\sum_z\ell_z^d\). Thus the factor

\[
                                \ell_z^{-d}
\]

is cancelled exactly once. No largest fine-site count of a coarse block is
admitted as a uniform constant.

Suppose a bounded operator \(R\) has norm at most \(\theta<1\) in one of these
weighted norms. Then

\[
                         (I+R)^{-1}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty(-R)^n,
 \qquad\|(I+R)^{-1}\|\le(1-\theta)^{-1}.                \tag{H3.3}
\]

Indeed the partial sums have remainder \((-R)^{m+1}\) on multiplication by

\[
                                  I+R,
\]

and its norm tends to zero. The geometric tail proves convergence and the
bound. Applying this with a weight centred at the support gives exponential
off-diagonal bounds, without further losses at every term of the series.

For H2, put \(J_D=D'(A')\), \(T'=I-HJ_D\), and \(R=C'(T(A'))H\).
Differentiate (H2.3). A second differentiation in direction \(v\) gives

\[
 J_D=(I+R)^{-1}C'(T(A')),
 \qquad
 D''(A')[v,w]=(I+R)^{-1}C''(T(A'))[T'v,T'w].             \tag{H3.4}
\]

For completeness the second identity follows by differentiating

\[
                        J_Dw=C'(T(A'))T'w.
\]

The derivative of \(T'w\) is \(-HD''[v,w]\); move this term to the left.
Thus if \(C'\), \(C''\), \(H\) have the required weighted bounds and

\[
                                  \|R\|_\rho\le1/2,
\]

the same bounds follow for \(J_D,D''\). A weighted direction can pass through
either argument of \(C''\). Its locality bounds that transfer; holomorphy
alone does not. This is exactly the extra averaging premise of the account.

### H4. Uniform fixed-point part of Propositions 6 and 9

This ports CMP 102 (118)–(121), (174)–(177), and CA-I 14544–14669.
Let \(X\) carry \(N(A)=\max\{|A|_{(-1)},|\nabla_UA|_{(-2)}\}\), and
let \(Y\) carry \(|f|_{(-3)}\). Suppose

\[
 \|\mathcal G\|_{Y\to X},\ \|H_1\|_{Z\to X}\le B_0,
 \qquad \|V'(A)\|_Y\le C_4N(A)^2\quad(N(A)<a_3),         \tag{H4.1}
\]

with \(V'\) holomorphic. Choose \(r\) after these constants, with

\[
 4r<a_3,\quad32B_0C_4r\le1,\quad B_0|B|\le r,
                    B_0|J|_{(-3)}\le r/4.              \tag{H4.2}
\]

Then \(a\mapsto-\mathcal G[J+V'(a+H_1B)]\) has exactly one fixed point

## 4. One precise lemma attacked three ways

The next lemma is a necessary upstream part of skeleton 3–7. It is not a
claim that all other ledger entries have been proved.
It is the first-order part of CMP 99b Theorem 3.3 for the actual projected
operator, as used in CMP 102 (129)–(140).

**Open lemma O.** Fix \(d=4,L\), then admissible block and separation floors
as in CMP 96 (2.1)–(2.4) and CMP 99b Theorem 3.1.
Let the nest, determining rows and boundary conditions be exactly those of
CMP 99b (3.16)–(3.27).
For a real background \(U\), assume the local gauge bounds (3.35) and (3.36),
with \(\mathfrak M\alpha_0\) below a constant fixed after those floors.
Let \(Q_U,Q'_U\) be Balaban's field and site averages, with their stated
physical pairings. Let
\[
 \Delta_U^{\rm site}=D_U^*D_U,\qquad
 R_U=\operatorname{proj}_{\Delta_U^{\rm site}\ker Q'_U},\qquad
 K_U=\Delta_U^{\rm W}+D_UR_UD_U^*+Q_U^*\ell^{-2}Q_U.     \tag{O1}
\]
Here \(\Delta_U^{\rm W}\) is the Wilson Hessian in the chart
\(e^{i\eta A}U\), as defined by CMP 99b (3.7), (3.10).
The restriction to the first domain is the Dirichlet restriction of (3.27);
on a full torus use the torus version. Assume all boundary plaquettes
entering that Hessian have the regularity required by that convention.

Prove that \(K_U\) is positive definite and that there exist \(C,\delta>0\)
independent of fine spacing, depth, volume, admissible nest and \(U\), such
that for \(f\) supported in the enlarged cell \(\widetilde\Delta(y')\),
\[
 \sup_{\Delta(y)}|K_U^{-1}f|
       \le C\ell_y^2 e^{-\delta d(y,y')}\|f\|_\infty,\qquad
 \sup_{\Delta(y)}|\nabla_UK_U^{-1}f|
       \le C\ell_y e^{-\delta d(y,y')}\|f\|_\infty.        \tag{O2}
\]
The constants must obey Balaban's order: geometric floors first, small
background ceiling afterwards. In particular, they must not deteriorate
with the number of levels. This is one specified operator lemma; neither
the scalar Green function nor the componentwise Feynman inverse is a
substitute for it.

### Attack 1: obtain O from the full-gradient energy

At the flat field, write \(d\) for the exterior derivative on one-forms and
\(D\) for the site-to-bond gradient. Set \(P=I-R\).
The flat full-gradient form and the projected form differ exactly by
\[
 K_{\rm F}=d^*d+DD^*+Q^*\ell^{-2}Q,\qquad
 K_{\rm L}=K_{\rm F}-DPD^*,\qquad
 (a,(K_{\rm F}-K_{\rm L})a)=\|PD^*a\|^2.                 \tag{A1}
\]
Therefore positivity of \(K_{\rm F}\) alone does not give a perturbation
small in the curvature radius.

This failure can be checked on actual flat fields, not just arbitrary
positive matrices. Use a periodic nest with at least two site-average rows,
all at one nonzero level. Let \(\psi\) be nonzero coarse mean-zero data, and
let \(q=Q'^*\psi\). For flat block means \(q\ne0\) and has fine mean zero.
On mean-zero site fields the flat Laplacian \(\Delta=D^*D\) is invertible:
its quadratic form is a sum of squared nearest-neighbour differences, so its
kernel consists of constants on the connected torus.
Put \(f=\Delta^{-1}q\). For every \(\lambda\in\ker Q'\),
\[
                         (f,\Delta\lambda)=(q,\lambda)=0.
\]
Hence \(Rf=0\), \(Pf=f\). Define \(a=D\Delta^{-1}f\). Then
\[
                  da=0,\qquad D^*a=f\ne0,\qquad RD^*a=0.
\]
The term subtracted in (A1) equals the entire derivative part
\(\|da\|^2+\|D^*a\|^2=\|f\|^2\).
This happens at zero curvature.

The row mass may still control this direction. Establishing that control
requires a quantitative relation between \(Q D\Delta^{-1}f\) and \(f\);
it is not supplied by the full-gradient Poincaré inequality.
Thus the attempted estimate
\[
 \|PD^*a\|^2\le C\alpha_0
       \{\|da\|^2+\|D^*a\|^2+\|\ell^{-1}Qa\|^2\}
\]
cannot hold uniformly. The flat field is admitted for every positive
\(\alpha_0\); let that parameter tend to zero with the lattice and \(a\)
fixed. Its positive left-hand side cannot tend to zero.
This refutes that perturbation step, not O.
It explains why the outline at 11685–11717 returns to the constrained
one- and two-scale models instead of subtracting a small error.

### Attack 2: use one global gauge and perturb the flat inverse

The proposed route would write the whole background in a single gauge as
\(e^{i\eta A}\), with a global potential bound proportional to \(\alpha_0\).
It would then use
\[
             K_U^{-1}=K_1^{-1}
                      [I+(K_U-K_1)K_1^{-1}]^{-1}.         \tag{A2}
\]
That global gauge hypothesis does not follow from O's local hypotheses.

Take a periodic lattice large enough that the gauge cubes are contractible,
and an \(SU(2)\) element
\(h=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)\).
Set the links crossing one seam in direction 1 equal to \(h\), and all
other positive links to \(1\); reverse links are inverses.
Each plaquette has product \(1\): a plaquette crossing the seam contains
one \(h\) and one \(h^{-1}\), and a plaquette not crossing it contains
only identities. The current is zero. On every contractible gauge cube the
connection can be gauged to \(1\), by transporting from a root.
Thus it satisfies the local gauge and current bounds with every positive
\(\alpha_0\). Its winding holonomy is \(h\), up to conjugation.

Suppose the proposed global bound held for every such \(\alpha_0\).
On a fixed winding loop of \(n\) bonds, unitary telescoping gives
\[
 \left\|\prod_{r=1}^n e^{i\eta A_r}-1\right\|
 \le\sum_{r=1}^n\|e^{i\eta A_r}-1\|
 \le\eta\sum_{r=1}^n|A_r|
 \le C\alpha_0\eta\sum_{r=1}^n\ell_r^{-1}.
\]
The middle estimate follows by integrating
\(d(e^{it\eta A_r})/dt=i\eta A_re^{it\eta A_r}\).
The last sum is finite on this fixed lattice. Letting \(\alpha_0\downarrow0\)
contradicts \(\|h-1\|>0\).
Hence (A2) cannot be justified through this global gauge.

This is not a counterexample to O: covariant estimates allow the holonomy.
The viable repair is to use local frames and keep their transition
functions, precisely the route attempted next.

### 6.2 H9: normalization, geometry and the local flat input

Use \(s_y=\ell_y=L^j\eta\). To avoid a collision of symbols, write
\(B_U=\ell Q_U\) for the **raw field row**, with counting pairing on its
outputs, and \(\mathcal L_U\) for the operator called \(K_U\) in (O1).
The site rows still have pairing \(\sum_y s_y^4\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\).
CMP 99b (3.16), at \(d=4,a=1\), gives exactly
\[
 \|B_Ua\|_{\rm count}^2
 =\sum_c s_c^2|Q_Ua(c)|^2
 =\langle a,Q_U^*\ell^{-2}Q_Ua\rangle.                 \tag{H9.1}
\]
Thus P82's raw row mass has the correct normalization. The recursively
defined scalar masses in CMP 99b (3.24) need not be identified with this
field mass. H11 below proves that their choice does not change \(R_U\).

Here and below the bond sets are formed **before** their differences:
CMP 96 p. 224, (2.3), explicitly applies the layer definitions to bonds,
and CMP 99b (3.16) uses those layers. At a seam this retains the interior
and outward coarse rows and excludes inward finer rows. Translating the
corner-based blocks to the centre convention in FP1 changes labels only.
At the flat field the rows are
\[
 Q_c^0a=n^{-5}\sum_{x\in P_{c_-}}\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}
                    a_\mu(x+t\eta e_\mu),\qquad n=s_c/\eta.
                                                               \tag{H9.2}
\]
The site row is the normalized pillar mean. Telescoping (H9.2) on
\(a=D\lambda\) gives the difference of the two endpoint means divided by
\(s_c\). An outward endpoint mean is a mean of complete finer pillars.
Consequently \(Q^0D\ker Q'^0=0\), including the level-zero seam.
Taking the touching set *after* complementing the sites would instead add
inward rows; the explicit counterexample in flat-port §2.1 excludes that
exchange of operations. No such exchange is made here.

The local models needed below are exactly the one- and two-scale models
of FP1 Lemma 8, not FP1's current-lattice covariance of Theorem 1(1).

\[
 \|a\|_2^2+\|\nabla a\|_2^2
 \le C_L\big(\|d a\|_2^2+\|R D^*a\|_2^2+\|B^0a\|^2\big).
                                                               \tag{H9.3}
\]
Here the marked union of larger blocks can have arbitrary shape, and the
fine spacing may equal the smaller row scale. These two quantifiers are
needed at our interfaces. The hand proof is FP1 256–314 and flat-port

mode; the scalar splitting \(R=R_s+R_+\), the single-scale minimizing
lift, and strict tree gauge on the marked blocks give the two-scale
bound. The face estimate pays an unmarked neighbor's root path with its
retained bare bonds. There is no omitted fine-mesh or large-box limit.
This is a port of that proved inequality, with (H9.1)–(H9.2) checking its
operators; it is not a use of a check-record verdict.

### 6.3 H10: the comb rows satisfy the required coefficient comparison

This ports a1-balaban §7.6, especially (7.10)–(7.16), to the exact rows
in CMP 99b (3.14)–(3.19). The finite composition is CMP 98 (89)–(92),
and its one-step derivative is (124). SA PX141–PX154, 11507–11640,
provides the corresponding argument for the outline's mean. Credit the
respective authors as in the provenance. The two curved averages are
**not** asserted equal.

On the complete carrier of a row of length \(s=L^j\eta\), use one of
(3.35)'s gauges and write
\[
 s|A_0|+s^2|\nabla A_0|\le\alpha,\qquad U=e^{i\eta A_0}.
                                                               \tag{H10.1}
\]
Fixed adjacent-layer factors are absorbed in \(\alpha\). At one step,
the comb paths, exponentials, logarithms and endpoint straightening
factors are finite analytic formulas on a ball depending on \(L\) only.
At the identity the endpoint factors cancel the internal-path terms,
leaving the straight raw average \(S_L\), with row sum \(L\) and column
sum \(L^{-3}\). Taylor's formula on a smaller ball gives, for the
straightened raw derivative \(A_W\),
\[
 \|A_W-S_L\|_{\rm row}\le C_LuL,\qquad
 \|A_W-S_L\|_{\rm col}\le C_LuL^{-3},\qquad
 u=\|\log W/i\|_\infty.                                \tag{H10.2}
\]
A fixed fine link enters only a bounded number (depending on fixed
\(L\)) of one-step rows. The directional derivative bound controls
the absolute coefficient sum of each scalar output row; summing the
three color coordinates costs a fixed factor. These facts justify the
column estimate, rather than inferring it from a many-level dimension.

Here is the scale summation that makes (H10.2) useful. Put
\(n=L^j,t_r=L^r/n,W_r=\mathcal M^rU\), using the plain **comb** mean
for this reference chain. Let \(u_r=\|\log W_r/i\|_\infty\) and
\(v_r=\sup|\partial W_r-I|\). The one-step path expansion gives
\[
 u_{r+1}\le Lu_r+C_L(u_r^2+v_r),\qquad
 v_{r+1}\le L^2(1+C_Lu_r)v_r+C_Lv_r^2.                 \tag{H10.3}
\]
The coefficient \(L^2\) has a specific reason: at the identity the
coarse plaquette is the average of translated spanning fluxes, with
total weight \(L^2\). The nonlinear correction has a curvature factor.
To see this in the finite formula, fix a tree in the one-step read box
and express each non-tree link by its loop variables. With all loop
variables equal to one the coarse plaquette is one, for every tree
transport. Subtract this value and integrate the derivatives in the
loop variables. Subtracting their identity derivatives leaves
\(C_L(u_r+v_r)v_r\); tree transports and logarithms stay in the same
fixed analytic ball. This explains why an arbitrary factor \(C_L\)
is not multiplied into the leading \(L^2\) at each level.

Initially \(u_0\le\alpha/n,v_0\le C\alpha/n^2\). Divide (H10.3)
by \(t_{r+1}\) and \(t_{r+1}^2\). For precision, bootstrap
\(v_r/t_r^2\le K_v\alpha\) and \(u_r/t_r\le K_u\alpha\)
with separate constants. Choose \(K_v\) above twice its initial
constant, then \(K_u\) above twice its initial constant plus the
linear curvature transfer \(C_LK_v\sum_{r<j}t_r/L\).
Since \(\sum_{r<j}t_r<1/(L-1)\), decrease \(\alpha\) so all
remaining relative errors have sum below a fixed small number. The
product bound \(\prod(1+x_r)\le\exp\sum x_r\) then improves both
bootstrap bounds. First exit proves
\[
 u_r\le C_L\alpha t_r,\qquad v_r\le C_L\alpha t_r^2,
 \qquad \sum_{r<j}u_r\le C_L\alpha.                    \tag{H10.4}
\]

CMP 98 (89)–(92) says that the straightened derivative is the product
of the one-step derivatives along this same chain. Multiplying
(H10.2) and then telescoping that product against \(S_L^j\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 \|B_U\|_{\rm row}&\le C_Ls,&
 \|B_U-B^0\|_{\rm row}&\le C_L\alpha s,\\
 \|B_U\|_{\rm col,\,height\ j}&\le C_L\eta^4s^{-3},&
 \|B_U-B^0\|_{\rm col,\,height\ j}&\le C_L\alpha\eta^4s^{-3}.
\end{split}                                                     \tag{H10.5}
\]
The conversion is \(B_U=\eta A_{W_{j-1}}\cdots A_{W_0}\).
For example its difference column sum is bounded by
\(C_L\eta L^{-3j}\sum_r u_r\).
Thus no factor proportional to \(j\) survives.

The site transport in CMP 99b (3.19) is the product of comb transports
through these same scales. Its deviation from the identity is at most
\(C_L\sum_r u_r\). Consequently
\[
 |(Q'_U-Q'^0)(y,x)|\le C_L\alpha\eta^4s_y^{-4},
 \qquad Q'_U(y,x)=\eta^4s_y^{-4}\operatorname{Ad}T_{yx},
 \quad x\in P_y.                                      \tag{H10.6}
\]
Every \(T_{yx}\) is unitary. A row carrier reads only its own and an
adjacent layer, and there are only \(C_L\) carriers of a given scale
through a fine bond. For the following difference bound restrict to one
localization patch, including complete row reads, and use its one common
gauge: \(B^0\) means that patch's conjugated flat model. There is no
globally chosen flat comparison field. Schur's inequality, (H10.5),
and the physical fine measure give
\[
 \|(B_U-B^0)a\|_{\rm count}\le C_L\alpha\|s^{-1}a\|_2,
 \quad \|B_Ua\|_{\rm count}\le C_L\|s^{-1}a\|_2.        \tag{H10.7}
\]
The second, absolute bound holds globally by the same bounded-overlap
count; the first bound is used only on the specified patch.
For example, rescale the input to \(s^{-1}a\): a row sum contributes
\(s^2\), and a column contributes \(\eta^4s^{-2}\); their product is
\(C\eta^4\), as required by the fine pairing. The same calculation
with (H10.6) bounds the site mass and its difference in the scalar
norms below. These comparisons are in each carrier's local gauge.
Gauge covariance makes the resulting estimates intrinsic; it does
not produce a global small gauge. ∎

### 6.4 H11: scalar elimination and the massive local system

Put \(D=D_U\), \(q=\mathfrak d_U\), and
\[
 A_s=D^*D+Q'^*s^{-2}Q',\quad S=A_s^{-1},\quad
 N=(Q'S^2Q'^*)^{-1},\quad P=SQ'^*NQ'S.                 \tag{H11.1}
\]
All adjoints use H9's pairings. The needed spaces are
\[
\begin{split}
 \|a\|_E^2&=\|\nabla_Ua\|_2^2+\|s^{-1}a\|_2^2,\\
 \|\psi\|_{H_s^2}^2&=\|s^{-2}\psi\|_2^2+
        \|s^{-1}D\psi\|_2^2+\sum_{\mu,\nu}\|D_\mu D_\nu\psi\|_2^2,\\
 \|X\|_{\mathcal X}^2&=\|a\|_E^2+\|h\|_2^2+
                   \|\psi\|_{H_s^2}^2+\|s^2\mu\|_{\rm top}^2,
 \qquad X=(a,h,\psi,\mu).
\end{split}                                                     \tag{H11.2}
\]
Use zero-extension stencils at the actual Dirichlet boundary.

We first justify the scalar inverses in (H11.1). Flat scalar pillar
Poincaré, after normalizing the smaller local scale to one, is uniform
over pillars of side between one and \(L\); its elementary path proof
is in FP1 Lemma 2. Local gauge comparison uses
\(|D_U-D|\le C\alpha/s\) and (H10.6). Applying that inequality to
\(\chi_i\psi\), summing, and using (H9.4) gives
\[
 \|s^{-1}\psi\|_2^2\le C_L
   (\|D_U\psi\|_2^2+\|s^{-1}Q'\psi\|_{
   \rm top}^2)+(C_L\alpha^2+C_L/M_P^2)\|s^{-1}\psi\|_2^2.
                                                               \tag{H11.3}
\]
Cutoff oscillation inside a pillar costs \(C_L/M_P\) in the row term.
Bare cells have the identity mass. Choose \(M_P\) large and then
\(\alpha\) small to absorb the last term. Thus \(A_s>0\).
Furthermore (H10.6) with the exact transported formula gives
\(Q'Q'^*=I\) in the top pairing: different pillars are disjoint and
the squared transports sum to their normalized volume. Hence \(Q'\)
is onto and \(N\) exists.

The matrix \(P\) is the orthogonal projection onto
\(\operatorname{ran}(SQ'^*)\). Indeed it is self-adjoint, and
\(P^2=P\) by the definition of \(N\). Its orthogonal complement is
\(\ker(Q'S)\). For \(Q'\lambda=0\),
\(A_s\lambda=D^*D\lambda\), so
\[
 \ker(Q'S)=A_s\ker Q'=D^*D\ker Q'.
\]
Consequently \(R=I-P\) is exactly O's projection. Replacing the positive
scalar mass changes neither this range nor this orthogonal projection.
This is Balaban CMP 96 (2.17), CMP 99b (3.25), with existence supplied
here rather than assumed.

Consider the four-variable matrix
\[
 \mathbb M_q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 q^*q+DD^*+B^*B&-D&0&0\\
 -D^*&I&A_s&0\\
 0&A_s&0&-Q'^*\\
 0&0&-Q'&0
 \end{pmatrix}.                                      \tag{H11.4}
\]
It is the massive system in flat-port (8.1), rather than P82's five-variable

three equations give
\[
 h=PD^*a,\quad \psi=S(D^*a-h),\qquad
 (q^*q+DRD^*+B^*B)a=f.                                \tag{H11.5}
\]
Every coefficient of (H11.4) is a finite differential stencil or a
pillar/row average. In particular the nonlocal \(P\) is not a coefficient.

The local flat inverse of (H11.4) is already proved in flat-port
472–535; here are its ingredients and algebra, to specify the import
completely. At smaller scale one, (H9.3) gives the vector inverse
\(E^*\to E\). Scalar Poincaré gives \(S:L^2\to H^1\).
Since the row mass is bounded on \(L^2\),
\(\Delta Sf=f-Q'^*s^{-2}Q'Sf\) and the Fourier identity

### 6.6 H13: decay in Balaban's distance and the two pointwise bounds

Use Balaban's weighted contour distance, CMP 96 (2.46), pp. 231–233,
as requested by CMP 99b just before Theorem 3.1. This is the formula
called (2.36) in that paper's prose; the supplied CMP 96 text numbers

Each factor in (H12.6) has propagation at most \(h_*=C_LM_P\)
**in this contour distance**. Here is the local geometric check.
An interior read box contains only scales \(s,Ls\). Its interfaces
are aligned with the \(Ls\)-grid. Connect a finer endpoint to a corner
of its containing \(Ls\)-cell inside the finer region, at cost at
most \(4L\). Connect the resulting corners along the \(Ls\)-grid
within the read box. In the finer region subdivide each such edge
into \(L\) edges; in the coarser region retain it. Intersections with
the interface occur at aligned grid points. The resulting contour
is admissible and its weighted length is at most \(C_LM_P\).
Endpoint adjustments and complete row carriers only enlarge this
constant. Bare boxes use the fine grid. The read margins in H9 allow
these paths also for boxes that cross a torus coordinate cut, using
their embedded lift. This proves the claimed propagation bound.

For a dual source \(F\) already supported in the enlarged cell of
\(z\), the \(n\)-th term of (H12.6) has zero local output at \(y\)
if \(d(y,z)>(n+1)h_*+C_L\), by the triangle inequality for these
contours. Its operator norm is at most \(B'_L4^{-n}\).
Summing the tail, and weakening its rate once, gives
\[
 \|X\|_{\mathcal X,\operatorname{loc}(y)}
 \le C e^{-\delta_w d(y,z)}\|F\|_{\mathcal X^*},
 \qquad \delta_w=(\log2)/h_*.                          \tag{H13.1}
\]
The local norm restricts derivatives after they are evaluated, as in
P82 (9). No negative-order norm is multiplied by a sharp cell cutoff.
For \(F=(f,0,0,0)\), the enlarged source cell has volume at most
\(C_Ls_z^4\) and comparable scales throughout. Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
 \|F\|_{\mathcal X^*}\le C_Ls_z\|f\|_2
                         \le C_Ls_z^3\|f\|_\infty.    \tag{H13.2}
\]

We port P82 Lemmas 5–6 to the four-variable system, checking each
forcing term. Set \(Z_y=s_z^3e^{-\delta_w d(y,z)}\|f\|_\infty\).
By (H13.1), every local energy component is at most \(CZ_y\).
In an interior cube of side \(r=c_Ls_y\), with its larger read cube
still in a regular gauge patch, the third equation gives
\[
 \Delta_U h=Q'^*\mu-Q'^*s^{-2}Q'h.
\]
Its right side is bounded by \(Cs_y^{-4}Z_y\). Indeed
\(|\mu(y)|\le s_y^{-4}\|s^2\mu\|_{\rm top}\), and
\(|Q'h(y)|\le s_y^{-2}\|h\|_{2,P_y}\). Adjacent pillars cost only
fixed powers of \(L\). The real covariant local Poisson estimate gives
\[
 |D_Uh|\le Cs_y^{-3}Z_y.                              \tag{H13.3}
\]
The precise estimate being used is
\[
 |u|_{Q/2}+r|\nabla_Uu|_{Q/2}
 \le C\{r^{-2}\|u\|_{2,Q}+r^2|g|_{\infty,Q}\},
 \quad \Delta_Uu=g+Eu,\quad
 |Eu(x)|\le C\alpha r^{-2}\max_{x'\sim x}|u(x')|.       \tag{H13.4}
\]
Its proof is a3-balaban (7.19), 440–479, and P82 Lemma 5: the real
Kato inequality and the scalar Dirichlet Poisson kernel bound the
value; maximize the squared distance to the outer boundary times
the value to absorb \(Eu\). In a small local gauge the equation for
the ordinary Laplacian has drift \(O(\alpha/r)\) and value term
\(O(\alpha/r^2)\). The free interior gradient estimate, applied at
a maximizer of distance times gradient, absorbs that drift and
gives the gradient member. The scalar Poisson bound follows by
reflection and the maximum principle; the free gradient estimate
is proved in flat-port §4. This uses real unitarity, not a complex
maximum principle. The bounded-mesh-ratio case follows directly
from \(|u(x)|\le\eta^{-2}\|u\|_2\).

The first equation of the massive Wilson system is
\[
 (q^*q+DD^*)a=f+Dh-B^*Ba-V_Ua.                        \tag{H13.5}
\]
Expanding paired covariant shifts shows that
\(d_U^*d_U+DD^*\) differs from the component covariant Laplacian

\(C\eta^{-2}|U(\partial p)-I|\le C\alpha s_y^{-2}\).
For \(q=\mathfrak d_U\), SA PX117 gives
\(|q-d_U|_{\rm entry}\le C\alpha\eta s_y^{-2}\).
Multiplication by a difference matrix, whose entries are bounded

\(V_U\) in the same way, so these terms belong to \(E\) in (H13.4).

The remaining row force also has the correct density. H10.5 gives
\(|B_Ua(c)|\le Cs_c^{-1}\|a\|_{2,\operatorname{carrier}(c)}\)
by Cauchy–Schwarz. Its physical adjoint has entry
\(\eta^{-4}B_U(c,b)^*\), bounded by \(Cs_c^{-3}\).
There are only \(C_L\) relevant rows and comparable scales at a
given fine bond. Thus
\[
 |B^*Ba|\le Cs_y^{-4}\|a\|_{2,\rm loc}
             \le Cs_y^{-3}Z_y.                        \tag{H13.6}
\]
The direct \(f\) term vanishes away from its source cell; where it

Equations (H13.3), (H13.5)–(H13.6), and
\(\|a\|_{2,\rm loc}\le Cs_yZ_y\) now give from (H13.4)
\[
 |\mathcal L_U^{-1}f|_y\le Cs_y^{-1}s_z^3
                    e^{-\delta_w d(y,z)}\|f\|_\infty,
 \quad |\nabla_U\mathcal L_U^{-1}f|_y\le Cs_y^{-2}s_z^3
                    e^{-\delta_w d(y,z)}\|f\|_\infty.  \tag{H13.7}
\]
At bounded \(s_y/\eta\), in particular at the outer bare boundary,
\(|a|\le\eta^{-2}\|a\|_2\) and

 (s_z/s_y)^3\le L^3
             \exp\{6(\log L)d(y,z)/\rho\}.             \tag{H13.8}

Choose the separation floor also so

### 8.3 H17: differentiated sources and the unextended second-order bound

### 8.5 H19: common gauge chart, including coverage and transversality

This is the port of the proof in `a1-balaban.md` §3.8, lines
568–715, of CMP 99a (1.55)–(1.125). Its inverse premises are now
supplied by H11, H15–H18. In particular they concern the comb rows,
with the precise boundary conventions of H9. The statement is: for
a regular reference of radius \(C_1B_3\varepsilon_1\), with datum
mismatch at most \(C_1\varepsilon_1\), every sufficiently regular
competitor has exactly one normalized small Landau representative.
It has
\[
 N(A)\le B_1(\varepsilon_0+C_1\varepsilon_1),\quad
 RD^*A=0,\quad \mathcal Q(A)=b,
 \qquad |b|\le2dLC_1\varepsilon_1.                    \tag{H19.1}
\]
The chart also has an open thickening with prescribed small \(RD^*A\).
Only this finite-dimensional transversality is claimed for an
inhomogeneous slice; no second derivative bound from a bounded
inhomogeneous divergence alone is used.

**Proof.** For the scalar normalization use
\[
 H'=G'^2Q'^*(Q'G'^2Q'^*)^{-1},\quad Q'H'=I,
 \quad Q'G'R=0.
\]
The scalar part of H11–H12 and H17 gives
\[
 \|G'Rf\|_Z\le C|f|_{(-2)},\quad
 \|H'X\|_Z+|D^*DH'X|_{(-2)}\le C|X|,
 \quad |Rf|_{(-2)}\le C|f|_{(-2)},                   \tag{H19.2}
\]
where \(\|\lambda\|_Z=\max(|\lambda|,|\ell D\lambda|)\).
For example the second bound follows by composing two scalar Green
kernels and the scalar Gram inverse, with the cell measures of H17.5;
applying the scalar equation to the last Green factor gives its
Laplacian member. This uses the scalar, not the corrected vector,
Laplacian. All weighted sums are H15.5.

Here are the nonlinear and induction parts of the source proof.
Fix the axial representative by its node values and successive comb
contours. The reconstruction identity of a1 §3.6, (3.10), is obtained
by moving each contour onto the block tree and inserting the intervening
plaquettes. Its errors at intermediate scales below a fixed output
layer are bounded by
\(C\alpha_0 L^{2(n-j)}\); their sum is at most
\(C\alpha_0/(1-L^{-2})\). The datum mismatch passes with coefficient
one. At an outward bond, the outer endpoint's double-block contour
contributes at most \(2dL\) times that mismatch. This factor occurs
at that seam, not once at every lower level. Thus all truncations
of the pair admit the same small mismatch bound.

For one descent of a truncation the previous gauge gives a preliminary
potential with at most the fixed loss \(L^2\) in \(N\). The ascent
factors used in that gauge have deviations and covariant differences
bounded by \(C\alpha_2t_n\), \(t_n=L^{n-j}\). Multiplying them is
bounded by \(\exp(C\alpha_2\sum t_n)\). In particular the loss is
independent of the number of earlier descents.

Correct this preliminary gauge by \(e^{i\lambda'}\). Its normalized
comb constraint has the form
\[
 Q'\lambda'+C_g(u_1,\lambda')=0,\qquad
 |C_g(u_1,\lambda')|
       \le C(\alpha_3+\|\lambda'\|_Z)\|\lambda'\|_Z.    \tag{H19.3}
\]
To see the small factor, expand each endpoint product
\(e^{-i\lambda(x)}e^{i\operatorname{Ad}T_{xx'}\lambda(x')}\).
Its linear term is the exact transported difference. After its
contribution to \(Q'\lambda'\) is removed, the terms contain either
\(\lambda D\lambda\), a preliminary ascent-factor deviation, or
curvature times two gauge insertions. Their respective scale sums
are bounded by \(C(\alpha_3+\alpha_4)\alpha_4\sum t_n\) and
\(C\alpha_0\alpha_4^2\sum t_n^2\). The log series converges on the
same complex ball as H16. This proves (H19.3), including its analytic
and derivative versions, without a depth-dependent radius.

Set \(\lambda'=\lambda-H'D_g(\lambda)\), where
\(D_g=C_g(u_1,\lambda-H'D_g)\). H19.2–H19.3 make this a contraction
with constant \(C(\alpha_3+\alpha_4)<1/2\). Its inverse is obtained

\(g(0)=1\), \(|g(z)-1|\le C|z|\), and

 \lambda=G'RD^*A+G'Rg_4(\lambda,D\lambda,A,D^*A),
 \quad |g_4|_{(-2)}\le C\alpha_4 B_1(\alpha_0+\alpha_1).

Its terms are precisely \(g-1\), \(g_3\), and \(H'D_g\) after
substitution. Their difference quotients, or Cauchy's formula on a

 N(A)\le C\{\alpha_0+\alpha_1+\alpha_2N(A)+\alpha_2^2\}.

### 8.6 H20: the critical chart and its second derivatives

For an admitted reference as in H19, H2, H4, H6 and H18 give a unique

             +\tfrac12\langle A',\Delta_1A'\rangle+V(A').

 a_1=-\mathcal G_1J-\mathcal G_1V'(a_1+H_1b).          \tag{H20.2}

Geometric contraction proves (H20.1). Every critical point furnished
by H19 lies inside the larger uniqueness ball; conversely testing
(H20.2) against a tangent vector gives the Euler equation.

**Special regularity of the invariant lift.** Gauge invariance gives

 D^*\Delta v=j_J(v),\qquad |j_J(v)|_{(-4)}

 \Delta_\pi H=\Delta H-D RG'D^*\Delta H,
 \qquad

This is the content of CMP 102 (138), reconstructed using the Ward

 d_U^*d_U+DD^*=\Delta_U^{\rm comp}+\mathcal F_U,
 \qquad |\mathcal F_Uv|_{(-3)}\le C\alpha_0|v|_{(-1)}.  \tag{H20.5}

 |d_U^*d_UHv|_{(-3),\rho}
       +|\Delta_U^{\rm comp}Hv|_{(-3),\rho}

\]
This is a statement for the lift. It is not a second-order bound on

 \widetilde V=-\langle HD_3,J\rangle

H20.4 and H16 verify the premises of H6 for this formula; hence

 L_0a_0=P_0^*[-J+\Theta A'-\widetilde V'(A')].           \tag{H20.8}

**One-step reference construction.** We supply the construction behind

### 8.8 H22: the critical orbit is a minimum over the regular class

H21 gives a critical orbit. To justify the word **minimal** in (T4),
we prove positivity along the straightened segment to every regular
competitor, rather than infer a global regular-class minimum solely
from a positive Hessian at one point.

The required strengthening of H6 is
\[
 |V''(A')[v,v]|\le Cr\|v\|_E^2\quad\text{if }N(A')\le r,
 \qquad v\in\ker\mathcal B\cap\ker RD^*.             \tag{H22.1}
\]
Here \(E\) is H11.2. We give the energy estimate; it is not inferred
from an \(X\to Y\) estimate by changing norms.

The energy inverse and Gram gap in H18 imply
\(\|Hz\|_E\le C\|z\|_{\rm count,2}\). H16's local density bound
and physical row/column counts imply

 \|C'(A)v\|_{\rm count,2}\le Cr\|\ell^{-1}v\|_2.
\]

same small Neumann series. Consequently
\(\|HD'(A')v\|_E\le Cr\|v\|_E\) and
\(\|T'v\|_E\le C\|v\|_E\).
For a bounded counting covector \(w\), the symmetric fine-field
Hessian of \(\langle w,C(A)\rangle\) obeys
\[
 |\langle w,C''(A)[v,v]\rangle|
                   \le C|w|\|\ell^{-1}v\|_2^2.        \tag{H22.2}
\]
To check this Schur estimate, in (H16.2) put an arbitrary direction
with \(|v(b)|\le\ell_b\), rather than \(\ell_b^{-1}\).
For a fixed output fine bond its absolute row sum is at most
