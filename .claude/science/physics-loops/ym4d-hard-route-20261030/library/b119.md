## 3. Bottom-up hand proofs

### B1. Rooted tree summation, including CMP 116 (1.26)'s counting input

For a connected union \(X\) of \(M\)-cubes let \(m(X)\) be its number of cubes. Let \(d(X)\) be the length, divided by \(M\), of a tree meeting all its cubes. If the definition also requires the tree to lie inside \(X\), retain that requirement. Put \(c_d=5^d\), \(D_d=3^d-1\). Then

\[
 m(X)\le c_d(d(X)+1),\qquad
 \sum_{X\ni\square}e^{-a d(X)}
 \le {e^{a-a/c_d}\over1-D_d^2e^{-a/c_d}}
     =:C_{\rm tree}(a),
\tag{B1}
\]

provided \(D_d^2e^{-a/c_d}<1\). The constants are independent of mesh, cube size and volume.

**Proof.** The first part ports `4.6:lem-tree-cubes`, I:21544–21642, due to Sussman and Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text. In a tree of length \(\ell M\), choose a maximal collection of met cubes whose indices differ by at least three in sup norm. Every met cube lies within two index units of a selected one. Thus there are at most \(5^d\) times as many met cubes as selected cubes. Select one point of the tree in each selected cube. These points are at least \(2M\) apart. If there are at least two, the tree has length at least \(M\) inside the radius-\(M\) ball about each point: follow its path towards any other selected point until the first exit. The balls have disjoint interiors, so their total required length bounds their number by \(\ell\). With zero or one selected point, the bound \(\ell+1\) suffices. This proves the first inequality. A torus tree can be lifted edge by edge to its covering lattice; length is preserved and the lifted cubes dominate the number of distinct projected cubes. Thus the same estimate applies there.

The graph joining distinct touching cubes has degree at most \(D_d\). Order the neighbours. Each connected \(m\)-cube set containing \(\square\) has a deterministic rooted spanning tree, for example the tree from a breadth-first exploration in that order. Its depth-first traversal has \(2(m-1)\) edges. The traversal determines its visited set, so distinct sets have distinct such codes. There are at most \(D_d^{2(m-1)}\) codes. Since \(d(X)\ge m(X)/c_d-1\), summing over \(m\ge1\) gives the geometric series in (B1). Multiple edges or identifications on a small torus only reduce the degree bound. This is a counting proof, not a machine enumeration. \(\square\)

For a threshold \(r\ge0\), split a rate into \(a+b\), \(b>0\). The same proof gives
\[
 \sum_{\substack{X\ni\square\\d(X)\ge r}}e^{-(a+b)d(X)}
       \le C_{\rm tree}(a)e^{-br}.
\tag{B1a}
\]
For a union of \(q\) possible anchor cubes, multiply the right side by \(q\). This is the actual tree-sum implication used at II:62046–62075. It does not bound the activity attached to each polymer.

### B2. Radius comparison and polynomial losses

This ports I:10224–10302, `4.4:lem-radii-ratio`. Suppose
\[
 -c_5\le x_j-x_{j+v}\le B^\sharp v,\quad x_i\ge\gamma^{-2},
 \quad\alpha_i=Cx_i^{-1/2}(\log x_i)^q,
\]
and choose the coupling ceiling last so that
\[
 \gamma^2\le e^{-1},\quad B^\sharp\gamma^2\le1,\quad
 {q c_5\gamma^2\over\log\gamma^{-2}}\le\log(1+\beta_0).
\tag{B2a}
\]
Then
\[
             \rho_{j+v,j}:={\alpha_{j+v}\over\alpha_j}
                    \le(1+\beta_0)\sqrt{1+v}.
\tag{B2b}
\]

**Proof.** The upper slope bound gives \(g_{j+v}/g_j\le\sqrt{1+v}\). The lower bound gives \(x_{j+v}\le x_j+c_5\), whence
\[
 \log x_{j+v}\le\log x_j+c_5\gamma^2,
 \quad
 \left({\log x_{j+v}\over\log x_j}\right)^q
 \le\exp\!\left({q c_5\gamma^2\over\log\gamma^{-2}}\right).
\]
Multiply the two bounds. The constant \(C\) cancels. Apply the same argument with exponent \(p_0\) to the small-field radius \(\varepsilon_j\). Thus the polynomial-growth hypothesis of the cover theorem follows **at the pairs for which its slope hypothesis is supplied**. This proof does not manufacture an all-pairs flow theorem from a terminal-level bound. \(\square\)

We also need a quantitative way to absorb polynomial factors. For \(0<q_*<1\) and an integer \(m\ge0\),
\[
 \sum_{v\ge0}(v+1)^m q_*^v\le {m!\over(1-q_*)^{m+1}}.
\tag{B2c}
\]
Indeed, \((v+1)^m\le m!\binom{v+m}{m}\). The coefficient of \(q_*^v\) in the product of \(m+1\) geometric series is \(\binom{v+m}{m}\), by distributing \(v\) identical increments among the \(m+1\) factors. Multiplying the convergent positive series proves (B2c). In particular, every polynomial loss in age is absorbed by an arbitrarily small fixed loss of an exponential rate. All constants are fixed before the coupling.

### B3. The corrected R sum retains the printed six-power reserve

In this lemma only, \(\kappa>6\) denotes the coupling-power exponent \(\kappa_0\) of (T2), not the polymer decay rate. Fix \(\lambda>0\), \(c\ge0\). Assume, for every pair used in the sum,
\[
 x_j\ge x_n+\lambda(n-j)-c,\qquad x_i=g_i^{-2}\ge\gamma^{-2}.
\tag{B3a}
\]
These are the lower all-pairs envelope and ceiling, not a one-step lower bound. With \(\varpi_{jn}\) from (T2a), a ceiling on \(\gamma\), depending only on \(\kappa,\lambda,c\), gives
\[
 \sum_{j=1}^n g_j^\kappa\varpi_{jn}\le g_n^{\kappa-6}
                 \quad\hbox{for every }n.
\tag{B3b}
\]

**Proof.** Impose \(c\gamma^2\le1/2\). Then
\(x_j\ge\frac12(x_n+\lambda(n-j))\).
For any real \(r>2\), the decreasing function \((x_n+\lambda t)^{-r/2}\) gives
\[
\begin{split}
 S_r:=\sum_{j=1}^n g_j^r
 &\le2^{r/2}\sum_{v\ge0}(x_n+\lambda v)^{-r/2}\\
 &\le2^{r/2}\left(g_n^r+
             {g_n^{r-2}\over\lambda(r/2-1)}\right).
\end{split}
\tag{B3c}
\]
For the second inequality, each term with integer \(v\ge1\) is bounded by the integral on \([v-1,v]\); the integral over \([0,\infty)\) is the displayed antiderivative.

The exact identity
\[
 g_j^\kappa\varpi_{jn}
 =\max\{g_j^\kappa,g_n^4g_j^{\kappa-4}\}
 \le g_j^\kappa+g_n^4g_j^{\kappa-4}
\]
reduces the required sum to \(S_\kappa+g_n^4S_{\kappa-4}\). Both exponents exceed two. For \(\gamma\le1\), (B3c) bounds it by \(D_\kappa g_n^{\kappa-2}\), where

### B5. A smooth layered cover with depth-independent multiplicity

This is a construction of the geometric cover described on B119 pp.275–276. It is not a proof that the fields read on its cubes meet (T9).

Put \(a_s=M\ell_s\). Work on the covering lattice, or on a periodic grid with the displayed read cubes realized there. For \(s<k\), let \(D_s\) be the closed union of \(\pi_s\)-cubes making up \(\Omega_s\setminus\Omega_{s+1}\); put \(D_k=\Omega_k\). Let \(V_s\) contain the vertices of those cubes. For each \(v\in V_s\), take
\[
                 Q_{s,v}=v+[-a_s,a_s]^d.
\tag{B5a}
\]
It is a union of \(2^d\) grid cubes of its assigned scale \(s\). At any point, at most three scales have nonzero cutoff functions in the construction below, and these scales differ by at most two. At each scale at most \(2^d\) functions are nonzero. Both a partition \(\sum\chi_{s,v}=1\) and a squared partition \(\sum\zeta_{s,v}^2=1\) exist on \(\Omega_1\), subordinate to these cubes, with
\[
 |\partial^r\chi_{s,v}|+|\partial^r\zeta_{s,v}|
            \le C_{r,d,L}a_s^{-r}.
\tag{B5b}
\]
The constants do not depend on depth, mesh, volume or \(M\).

**Proof.** Choose once a nonnegative smooth function \(b\), positive on \((-1,1)\), zero outside that interval, with every derivative zero at the endpoints. For example take \(b(t)=e^{-1/(1-t^2)}\) inside. Let
\[
 q(t)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}b(t-m),\qquad h(t)=b(t)/q(t).
\]
At each \(t\), an integer lies at distance at most \(1/2\); hence \(q\) is positive and bounded below by \(\min_{[-1/2,1/2]}b>0\). The sum is locally finite and periodic. Compactness of a period bounds every derivative. Also \(\sum_m h(t-m)=1\).

Set \(h_{s,v}(x)=\prod_{\mu=1}^d h((x_\mu-v_\mu)/a_s)\) and retain only \(v\in V_s\). At \(x\in D_s\), choose a layer cube containing \(x\). Every full-grid function nonzero at \(x\) is centred at a vertex of that cube. Therefore all of them were retained and their sum is one. This remains true on faces by continuity. The layers cover \(\Omega_1\).

If \(h_{s,v}(x)\ne0\), then \(x\) is within \(2a_s\) of \(D_s\) and within \(a_s\) of \(\Omega_s\), since \(v\) is a vertex of a layer cube contained in \(\Omega_s\). For \(s\ge2\), the thickness \(\theta_{s-1}\ge La_{s-1}=a_s\) therefore puts such \(x\) in \(\Omega_{s-1}\).

Suppose functions of scales \(s<t\) are both nonzero, with \(t\ge s+3\). The scale-\(t\) statement puts \(x\) in \(\Omega_{t-1}\subset\Omega_{s+2}\). But \(D_s\subset\overline{\Omega_{s+1}^c}\), and
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_\infty(D_s,\Omega_{s+2})
             \ge\theta_{s+1}>2a_s.
\]
This contradicts the scale-\(s\) statement. Thus active scales differ by at most two. The full grid at one scale has at most two possible centres per coordinate and total weight one. Consequently
\[
 1\le S:=\sum h_{s,v}\le3,\qquad
 {1\over3\,2^d}\le T:=\sum h_{s,v}^2\le3
\tag{B5c}
\]
on \(\Omega_1\); the lower bound for \(T\) follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and the multiplicity \(3\,2^d\).

Take \(\chi_{s,v}=h_{s,v}/S\) and \(\zeta_{s,v}=h_{s,v}/\sqrt T\). Their supports stay in \(Q_{s,v}\). Wherever a derivative of their numerators is nonzero, all relevant scales satisfy \(a_{s'}/a_s\in[L^{-2},L^2]\). Thus a derivative of order \(r\) of \(S\) or \(T\) is bounded by a constant times \(a_s^{-r}\). Repeated product and chain rules, using (B5c), prove (B5b) for every fixed \(r\). Smooth vanishing handles the edges of supports. The argument is local; periodic images can be combined on a torus. It also bounds normalized lattice differences whose difference segments stay in \(\Omega_1\): integrate each derivative along those segments. An extension across the exterior boundary is a separate choice, not asserted here. \(\square\)

Nor does it prove the per-cube regularity of a twofold enlargement of \(Q_{s,v}\). That enlarged region may read lower layers. The operator theorem's actual determining rows and analytic data classes still have to be checked there.

### 4.2 New attack 1: factor through curvature coordinates before estimating the response

The earlier raw potential estimate paid \(\epsilon_1\). This attempt instead tries to pay plaquette curvature in a **local response norm**. The following elementary implication is useful.

**B9 — curvature coordinates with a bounded response moment.** Let \(F(z)\) be a holomorphic component of the normalized output curvature or current, in a star-shaped coordinate polydisc, with \(F(0)=0\). Suppose the field under consideration has coordinates \(z=(z_e)\) with \(|z_e|\le a_e\epsilon_0\), the segment \(tz\), \(0\le t\le1\), is in that polydisc, and
\[
 \sup_{0\le t\le1}\sum_e a_e\|\partial_e F(tz)\|\le C_*.
\tag{B9a}
\]
Then \(|F(z)|\le C_*\epsilon_0\).

**Proof.** The finite-dimensional fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\[
 F(z)=\int_0^1\sum_e\partial_eF(tz)z_e\,dt.
\]
The triangle inequality and (B9a) prove the result. A normally convergent infinite coordinate sum gives the same statement by dominated convergence. No numerical estimate is involved. \(\square\)

To try to apply B9, first work on an ordinary contractible box and fix a spanning-tree gauge. Every remaining link is a fundamental loop based at the tree root. A chosen plaquette filling expresses that loop as a product of transported plaquettes. If their deviations are \(P_i-I\), then
\[
 \prod_{i=1}^mP_i-I
 =\sum_{i=1}^m P_1\cdots P_{i-1}(P_i-I),\qquad
 \left\|\prod_iP_i-I\right\|
 \le e^{\sum_i\|P_i-I\|}\sum_i\|P_i-I\|.
\tag{A1}
\]
If the transport conjugations have norm at most \(C_T\), and the filling has \(A_e\) faces, (Q1) gives \(|z_e|\le C\,C_TA_e\eta^2\epsilon_0\) in the small logarithm chart. Thus B9 would apply with \(a_e=C C_TA_e\eta^2\). On a fixed box all these constants are finite. This is already a curvature, rather than a potential, estimate.

For Q119, however, the variables of the minimizing map are the **iterated nonabelian averages on a graded determining set**. They are not independent spanning-tree links. The derivative after changing coordinates contains the Jacobian of the gauge fixing and of those averages. The presently supplied inverse estimates control derivatives in their original data coordinates. They do not prove
\[
 \sup_{p,n,Z}\sup_t
       \sum_e C C_TA_e\eta^2\|\partial_eF(tz)\|\le2B_3
\tag{A2}
\]
in these curvature coordinates, or a uniform star-shaped path in their analytic domain. The elementary estimate using the maximum filling area and the sum of derivative norms instead gives a box-dependent constant. \tag{A3}
\]
These identities follow by differentiating and using \(P_Q^*=P_Q=P_Q^2\). They preserve \(QA\). For the curvature \(F=dA\), however,
\[
 \dot F=-dd^*F+dQ^*(QQ^*)^{-1}Qd^*F.
\tag{A4}
\]
The second term is the constraint pressure. Dropping it destroys preservation of \(QA\). It is nonlocal, and the energy identity does not make it a maximum-principle generator.

Here is a hand check of the precise missing inference. The positive projection
\[
 P={1\over5}\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{pmatrix}
\]
satisfies \(P^2=P\). Its semigroup is \(e^{-tP}=I-(1-e^{-t})P\). On \(f=(1,-1)\), whose supremum norm is one, its first component is
\[
            (e^{-tP}f)_1=1+\tfrac15(1-e^{-t})>1\quad(t>0).
\tag{A5}
\]
Nevertheless its Euclidean energy decreases. This example is **not** a lattice Yang–Mills counterexample. It shows why positivity of a projected quadratic form supplies no missing supremum maximum principle.

On a mesh of spacing \(\eta\), using energy alone gives \(\|F\|_\infty\le\eta^{-2}\|F\|_{2,\eta}\) in dimension four. The coefficient diverges as \(\eta\to0\). A usable replacement would have to bound the semigroup of (A4) in the locally scaled supremum norms uniformly, preserve the nonlinear average constraints, and control the corresponding complex flow inside the chart. None follows from (A3). Moreover the independent current in (Q1)'s pair cannot be substituted for the physical current in this evolution.

**Failure at an exact step:** the projected curvature evolution has no proved uniform supremum bound or invariant complex chart on the stated domain. Its explicit pressure term (A4) is the unestimated term. This route gets a genuine energy identity, but not the required path or (Q3).

### 4.4 New attack 3: a Ward identity factorization through the curl

The third attempt tries to supply (A2) by algebra rather than by a coordinate filling. At a flat base, let \(K\) be the derivative of one output curvature component. Gauge covariance gives \(KD=0\), where \(D\) is the infinitesimal input gauge action. On a contractible full cubical complex with its unrestricted gauge variables, \(\ker d=\operatorname{im}D\): a closed edge field has a potential, obtained by summing along paths, since elementary plaquette moves leave the sum unchanged. Therefore \(K\) factors algebraically through \(d\).

To see the factorization directly, define \(\widehat K(dA)=KA\). If \(dA=dA'\), the difference is a gradient and \(K(A-A')=0\). Hence this is well-defined on \(\operatorname{im}d\). Choosing a right inverse \(R\) of \(d\) on that image gives \(\widehat K=KR\). The estimate still reads
\[
             \|\widehat K\|\le\|K\|\,\|R\|.
\tag{A6}
\]
Algebra gives neither a uniform locally scaled norm nor localization of \(R\) on the graded constrained complex.

At a nonflat field the intended identity is more delicate. Before fixing the final representative, gauge covariance differentiated at \(W\) gives
\[
 DF_{\rm out}(W)[D_W\lambda]
                 =[\lambda,F_{\rm out}(W)]
\tag{A7}
\]
at the output base point, using the infinitesimal gauge \(e^{t\lambda}\), with the induced gauge action if coordinates are changed. Thus the longitudinal derivative is not zero. There is an exact lattice version of the accompanying curvature obstruction. Let \(\mathcal T_\mu\) transport a site matrix from \(x+\hat\mu\) to \(x\), and let \(P_{\mu\nu}(x)\) be the based plaquette, oriented so that the \(\mu\)-then-\(\nu\) path equals that plaquette times the \(\nu\)-then-\(\mu\) path. Multiplying the four bond matrices gives
\[
 (\mathcal T_\mu\mathcal T_\nu-\mathcal T_\nu\mathcal T_\mu)\lambda
  =(\operatorname{Ad}_{P_{\mu\nu}}-I)
                  \mathcal T_\nu\mathcal T_\mu\lambda.
\tag{A7a}
\]
This vanishes at a flat field but not at a general background. A covariant cochain contraction must retain these curvature terms and the fixed-row/boundary data; the flat exact-sequence argument cannot be applied unchanged.

There is an explicit check on discarding the nonlinear terms. In \(SU(2)\), put constant bond potentials \(A_1=a\sigma_1\), \(A_2=b\sigma_2\), the other two zero. Their linear lattice curl is zero. With \(u=\eta a\), \(v=\eta b\), the plaquette is the group commutator of \(e^{iu\sigma_1}\) and \(e^{iv\sigma_2}\). Since the Pauli matrices anticommute and square to the identity,
\[
 \tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}
 (e^{iu\sigma_1}e^{iv\sigma_2}e^{-iu\sigma_1}e^{-iv\sigma_2})
          =1-2\sin^2u\sin^2v.
\tag{A8}
\]
For \(0<|u|,|v|<\pi\), excluding zeros of the sines, this differs from one. Thus even arbitrarily small constant potentials have a nonlinear curvature which a factorization through the **linear** curl misses. This does not refute (Q3), which uses the actual plaquette curvature.

For the algebra in (A8), conjugation sends \(\sigma_2\) to \(\cos(2u)\sigma_2-\sin(2u)\sigma_3\). In the product with \(e^{-iv\sigma_2}\), only its scalar component contributes to the half trace. That component is \(\cos^2v+\sin^2v\cos(2u)=1-2\sin^2u\sin^2v\), proving the displayed identity directly.

A completion would need a uniformly bounded, local covariant cochain contraction compatible with the actual determining rows, with the terms in (A7) retained and absorbed at the early constant. The scalar and field inverse statements already proved do not by themselves identify such a contraction. Substituting an arbitrary inverse in (A6) leaves a box/scale constant. Using the desired curvature bound to control the nonflat correction in (A7) is circular.

**Failure at an exact step:** no uniform covariant curl factorization on the actual two-rule constrained domain has been obtained. The two missing pieces are the bounded local right inverse in (A6) and the curvature term in (A7), parts of this one attempted factorization. This is different from the prior straight-potential Taylor, three-real-point, and axial first-difference attempts in B109. It does not prove Q119 or provide a counterexample to it.

### 7.1 New attack 1: fill the response cycles, with their weights

Section 4.4 obtained only an algebraic factorization. This attempt constructs
its coefficients and estimates their sum. It does not invert a Laplacian or
pass from an energy norm to a supremum norm.

**B10 — a quantitative flat curl factorization.** Let \(Q\) be a finite
rectangular cubical lattice of mesh \(h\), with a vertex \(o\). There is no
periodic identification in this lemma. Fields take values in a fixed finite
dimensional normed vector space. Positive bonds suffice; reversed bonds carry
the negative value. Put
\[
 (d_0\lambda)(x,\mu)=\frac{\lambda(x+h\hat\mu)-\lambda(x)}h,
 \qquad
 (d_1A)_{\mu\nu}(x)=
 \frac{A_\nu(x+h\hat\mu)-A_\nu(x)
       -A_\mu(x+h\hat\nu)+A_\mu(x)}h.
\tag{R1}
\]
Let \(KA=\sum_e K_e A(e)\) be a linear map with \(Kd_0=0\), including
potentials at the boundary vertices. Write
\(r_e=|e_--o|_1+h\). Then there are linear maps \(L_f\), indexed by
plaquettes, defining \(\mathcal L F=\sum_f L_fF(f)\), such that
\[
 K=\mathcal L d_1,\qquad
 \sum_f\|L_f\|\le\sum_e r_e\|K_e\|,
 \qquad
 \|KA\|\le\left(\sum_e r_e\|K_e\|\right)\|d_1A\|_\infty.
\tag{R2}
\]

**Proof.** Join \(o\) to each vertex by its coordinate path, taking coordinate
1 first, then 2, and so on. Signed coordinate segments allow \(o\) to be
interior. Their union is a tree. Define \(\lambda(x)\) as the sum of \(hA\)
along that path. For \(e=(x,x+h\hat\mu)\), the difference
\(h(A-d_0\lambda)(e)\) is the sum around the loop formed by the two root
paths and \(e\). Move the additional \(\mu\)-step past the segments in
directions greater than \(\mu\). One move across one lattice square
replaces a path by the other two sides of that square. Its change in the
sum is \(h^2d_1A(f)\), with the orientation sign. Backtracking cancels.
There are at most
\(\sum_{\nu>\mu}|x_\nu-o_\nu|/h\le r_e/h\) such moves. Every square
lies in the coordinate hull of \(o,x,x+h\hat\mu\), hence in \(Q\).
Thus, with integer signed multiplicities \(n_{ef}\),
\[
 (A-d_0\lambda)(e)=h\sum_f n_{ef}d_1A(f),
 \qquad h\sum_f|n_{ef}|\le r_e.
\]
Apply \(K\), use \(Kd_0\lambda=0\), and set
\(L_f=h\sum_e K_e n_{ef}\). The triangle inequality gives (R2).
This constructs the factorization, including its norm. \(\square\)

The moment is uniform on a usual one-scale exponential kernel. In dimension
\(d\), suppose \(0<h\le s\) and
\[
 \|K_e\|\le C h^d s^{-d-1}
       e^{-\delta|e_--o|_\infty/s},\qquad\delta>0.
\tag{R3}
\]
The annulus \(ms\le|e_--o|_\infty<(m+1)s\) contains at most
\(d\,5^d(s/h)^d(m+2)^d\) positive bonds: count each coordinate in its
containing interval, then the \(d\) directions. On it
\(r_e\le(d+1)(m+2)s\). Consequently (B2c) gives
\[
 \sum_e r_e\|K_e\|
 \le C d(d+1)5^d2^{d+1}
       \frac{(d+1)!}{(1-e^{-\delta})^{d+2}}.
\tag{R4}
\]
All powers of \(h,s\) cancel. This proves the displayed constant by hand.
No claim about the actual response kernel is needed for this implication.

At the identity input, a curvature or current response of a gauge-covariant
map annihilates input gradients, provided that its output there is flat.
Indeed an infinitesimal input gauge changes the output by a gauge tangent;
the derivative of its zero curvature or zero current is zero. Thus B10
applies to that linear response on a full box whenever its moment is finite.
It applies to both colour-valued outputs, without selecting their scalar
components separately.

**What this changes.** For a supplied one-scale kernel (R3), the flat
factorization norm left unestimated in (A6) is now supplied: (R2)–(R4)
provide it explicitly. B10 does not assume a derivative bound for an axial
potential. It uses its values only.

**Attempt to continue away from flatness.** First restrict to \(U_r=I\)
and use top-scale units. In exponential coordinates write
\(F(a)=Ka+R(a)\), where \(F\) is one normalized tower output,
\(F(0)=0\), and \(K=DF(0)\). On a fixed analytic ball, Cauchy's
formula bounds \(R(a)\) quadratically. Normalize the input curvature by
\[
 \mathcal C_h(a)=\frac{1}{ih^2}\log\partial(e^{iha})
                   =d_1a+N_h(a).
\tag{R4a}
\]
The four-link product and logarithm series give
\(\|N_h(a)\|_\infty\le C\|a\|_\infty^2\) on the small link chart:
the linear sum is exactly \(ih^2d_1a\), and every remaining product
contains at least two factors \(ha\). The input plaquette bound gives
\(\|\mathcal C_h(a)\|_\infty\le2\epsilon_0\) in that chart. B10's
factor \(\mathcal L\) therefore gives the exact identity
\[
 F(a)=\mathcal L\mathcal C_h(a)+\mathcal E(a),\qquad
 \mathcal E(a):=R(a)-\mathcal L N_h(a).
\tag{R4b}
\]
It is this **corrected** remainder which must be estimated. Bounding
\(R\) alone would miss the cancellation with the nonlinear input curl.
The estimates just proved give only
\(\|\mathcal E(a)\|\le C\epsilon_1^2\). A sufficient new estimate would be
\[
 \|\mathcal E(a)\|\le C\epsilon_1\|\mathcal C_h(a)\|_\infty
\tag{R4c}
\]
on the actual chart, with uniform constants. The linear cycle filling does
not establish (R4c). At a nonflat reference the response to a gauge tangent
also includes the output commutator (A7); it cannot be discarded.

Moreover (R3) is a one-scale hypothesis. A two-rule determining set can
have holes, outward rows and many cell sizes. Its response estimate must
carry the actual cell weights, rather than a fictitious common \(s\).

**Exact failure of this attack:** (R2) closes the flat factorization problem
under its kernel hypotheses, but the corrected nonlinear remainder in
(R4b) has only a quadratic potential bound. The curvature factor in (R4c),
on the actual graded chart, is not proved. This is a new quantitative attack
on Q119, not a claim that its flat restriction was the whole problem.

### 7.2 New attack 2: exact holomorphic division on a tree chart

The first attack loses a square by truncating the nonlinear map. This attempt
keeps that map intact and divides by variables transverse to flat fields.
It proves a finite-chart result, then checks the size of its constant.

**B11 — flat-locus division with a quantitative radius.** Fix a finite graph,
a rooted spanning tree, and a small matrix logarithm chart in
\(SL(N,\mathbb C)\). A bond field is described by its tree transports
\(q_x\), with \(q_o=I\), and its remaining links in tree gauge,
\(e^{ib_e}\). Explicitly,
\[
 W_{xy}=q_x^{-1}Z_{xy}q_y,\qquad
 Z_{xy}=I\text{ on tree bonds},\quad Z_e=e^{ib_e}
       \text{ on the remaining positive bonds}.
\tag{R5}
\]
The inverse coordinates are obtained by multiplying tree links and then
conjugating each remaining link by its two tree transports. This is an
analytic coordinate change wherever its displayed logarithms exist.

Suppose an output \(\mathcal F(q,b)\) is holomorphic for
\(q\in\mathcal Q\), \(\|b\|_\infty<r\), has norm at most \(H\), and
vanishes at \(b=0\) for every \(q\in\mathcal Q\). Then
\[
       \|\mathcal F(q,b)\|\le (H/r)\|b\|_\infty.
\tag{R6}
\]
There is no factor depending on the number of bonds. There is also no
quadratic error in this inequality.

**Proof.** This is the raywise Schwarz argument of SA 4693–4714, applied
to a function vanishing on the flat locus. Credit Sussman and Alpoge, with
Claude and Codex as authors of their text. Fix \(q\) and nonzero \(b\), and put
\(v=b/\|b\|_\infty\). For any norm-one scalar linear functional \(\ell\)
on the finite dimensional output space,
\(f(z)=\ell(\mathcal F(q,zv))\) is holomorphic for \(|z|<r\), bounded
by \(H\), and zero at zero. On \(|z|=r'<r\), the quotient \(f(z)/z\)
has modulus at most \(H/r'\). The maximum principle extends this bound
inside. Let \(r'\uparrow r\), evaluate at \(z=\|b\|_\infty\), then take
the supremum over \(\ell\). The zero case follows directly. This proves
(R6). Alternatively, the exact finite identity
\[
 \mathcal F(q,b)=\sum_e b_e\int_0^1
                   \partial_{b_e}\mathcal F(q,tb)\,dt
\]
expresses the corresponding analytic ideal membership, with scalar
coordinates on each matrix understood. The line argument proves its norm
without an extra coordinate count. \(\square\)

Here is the curvature size of the coordinates in (R5). On a rectangular
cube of side \(R\), use the coordinate tree of B10. Suppose
\[
 W_e=e^{ih a_e}(U_r)_e,\quad (U_r)_e\in SU(N),\quad
 \|a\|_\infty\le\epsilon_1,\quad
 \|\partial W-I\|_\infty\le\epsilon_0h^2.
\tag{R7}
\]
Every link and inverse has norm at most \(e^{h\epsilon_1}\). A coordinate
path has length at most \(dR\). The tree loop associated with one bond is
filled by at most \(dR/h\) elementary squares, as in B10. Use successive
path replacements, without commuting matrix factors. Each replacement
inserts a plaquette, conjugated by the path from the root to that square.
All these paths have length at most a fixed dimensional multiple of \(R\).
An inverted plaquette is the conjugate of the opposite based plaquette; its
norm can instead be bounded by twice the forward defect when that defect
is below \(1/2\). Absorb this fixed factor in \(C_d\). Thus, for
\[
                  u=C_dRh\epsilon_0e^{C_dR\epsilon_1},
\]
the sum of the norms of all inserted defects is at most \(u\). Exact
product telescoping gives \(\|Z_e-I\|\le e^u-1\). If \(u\le1/8\),
the exponential series gives \(e^u-1\le2u\le1/4\). The logarithm series
then gives
\[
             \|b\|_\infty\le4u
                =4C_dRh\epsilon_0e^{C_dR\epsilon_1}.
\tag{R8}
\]
This argument uses no derivative of the tree-gauged potential. It also
allows a real reference with arbitrary unitary link values. Its input
smallness is on the actual plaquettes, not on their linearized curl.

On a full contractible cube, \(b=0\) is exactly the flat locus. Indeed a
flat field has path-independent transport: elementary square moves and
cancellation of reversed links reduce any rectangular-lattice loop to the
constant path. Its tree-gauged links are therefore all identity. Conversely,
(R5) with \(b=0\) is a pure gauge and has identity plaquettes. An actual
curvature or current map that preserves flat fields satisfies B11's
vanishing hypothesis in this chart.

**Attempt to use B11 for Q119.** If the tower functional is bounded and
holomorphic on the entire product chart just stated, (R6)–(R8) give a bound
linear in \(\epsilon_0\), with no \(\epsilon_1^2\) term. This is stronger
than applying Cauchy to a second-order remainder. It shows why the square
in P20 is not, by itself, evidence against Q119.

The quantitative failure remains visible even in the favorable raw-value
chart. Let \(s\) be the top physical scale. Suppose the available chord
radius is \(r=c h/s\), as suggested by a uniform raw-datum ball and
averaging paths of length at most \(s\). Normalize the output as
\((h/s)^{-2}(\partial T-I)\), or as its level-\(n\) current. Then
(R6)–(R8) give
\[
 \|\mathcal F\|\le (4C_dH/c)\,Rs\epsilon_0
                          e^{C_dR\epsilon_1}.
\tag{R9}
\]
Q3 requires \(2B_3s^2\epsilon_0\) for these normalized outputs. The
ratio contains \(R/s\), even after making \(R\epsilon_1\) small. If a
full region \(Z\) supplies the tree, \(R/s\) can be arbitrarily large.
If one instead takes a cover window, that ratio contains its later block
size. Neither can be inserted into the early \(B_3\). Shrinking
\(\epsilon_0\) does not remove this ratio.

The original tower domain is also not the product domain in B11. It is
specified by actual averages, outward rows, local real gauges, and both
tower rules. Fixing all tree transports and varying the chord logarithms
independently need not stay there. A local use of B11 would require a
bounded analytic extension in those variables and compatibility of the
resulting representatives on the overlaps. The common chart for the
*raw determining datum* in `a1-balaban.md` §9.1 is a different statement.
Its existence and (9.1)–(9.2) do not assert this product-chart bound.

**Exact failure of this attack:** finite-chart division is proved, but its
transverse radius and output bound have not been supplied uniformly for

### 7.3 New attack 3: reduce to a real field by polar decomposition

This route would replace the complex input by a canonical unitary field,
apply the real minimal-orbit theorem, and estimate the remaining positive
factor. It first needs the unitary field to have curvature controlled by
\(\epsilon_0\). CA-I `4.22:lem-polar-decomposition`, 77657–77806,
proves polar decomposition and its matrix bounds. It does not assert this
plaquette implication. The following exact example disproves that needed estimate.
It is a failure of this proposed reduction, not of Q119.

**B12 — polar projection can create curvature from a flat small pair.**
For any fine mesh \(0<h\le1\) and positive twist ceiling \(\epsilon_1\),
there is a flat \(SL(2,\mathbb C)\) bond field \(W=e^{iha}I\) with
\(\|a\|,\|\nabla a\|<\epsilon_1\), such that its linkwise unitary polar
part has a nonidentity plaquette. For tower meshes \(h=L^{-p}\), the same
\(W\) obeys (Q1)–(Q2) for every positive \(\epsilon_0\), with real factor \(U_r=I\) and independent
current zero.

**Proof.** Use the operator matrix norm below. Fix \(c_N\ge1\) so that
the colour norm in (Q1) is at most \(c_N\) times that norm. This is a
fixed finite dimensional constant. Put
\[
 H_1=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 H_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 A=e^{tH_1},\quad B=e^{tH_2},
\tag{R10}
\]
where \(0<t<\min\{1/16,\epsilon_1h^2/(32c_N)\}\). These matrices are
positive Hermitian with determinant one. On a lattice containing an
interior square with successive vertices
\(x_0=(0,0),x_1=(h,0),x_2=(h,h),x_3=(0,h)\), set
\(g(x_1)=A\), \(g(x_2)=B\), and \(g=I\) at all other vertices.
The other two coordinates are zero at these named vertices. Define every
oriented bond by
\[
                         W_{xy}=g(x)^{-1}g(y).
\tag{R11}
\]
Every plaquette telescopes to identity. This holds also for squares meeting
the two exceptional vertices in any of the other coordinate planes.

Each bond is a product of at most two exponentials with exponents of norm
\(t\). The exponential series and a product telescoping give
\(\|W_{xy}-I\|\le e^{2t}-1\le4t<1/4\). The logarithm series yields
\(\|\log W_{xy}\|\le8t\). Define
\(a_{xy}=(ih)^{-1}\log W_{xy}\) on positive bonds. Its logarithm is
traceless: the sum of its eigenvalue logarithms is continuous, is zero at
\(t=0\), and its exponential has determinant one throughout this small
chart. Thus \(a\) belongs to the complexified colour algebra. Further,
\[
             \|a\|_\infty\le8t/h<\epsilon_1/(4c_N),
 \qquad \|\nabla a\|_\infty\le16t/h^2<\epsilon_1/(2c_N).
\tag{R12}
\]
The first bound uses \(h\le1\). The second bounds a difference by its two
endpoint norms. Multiplication by \(c_N\) gives the required bounds in
the colour norm of (Q1). With \(U_r=I\), these are precisely the covariant first
differences as well. The real cube-gauge requirement is satisfied with zero
potential. Both input curvatures vanish. Choose any sufficiently large
box or torus containing the square and its stencils, with the required
tower margins. Nothing depends on its volume.

For an invertible matrix \(X\), let
\(\mathcal P(X)=X(X^*X)^{-1/2}\) be its unitary polar factor. Here is the
part of CA-I 77657–77763 used: diagonalize the positive matrix \(X^*X\)
and take its positive square root \(P\). Then \(U=XP^{-1}\) has
\(U^*U=I\). For \(\det X=1\), positivity and \(\det(P)^2=1\)
give \(\det P=\det U=1\). For positive Hermitian \(X\), the positive
square root of \(X^2\) is \(X\), so \(U=I\). Around the specified square,
(R11) has links \(A,A^{-1}B,B^{-1},I\). Its polar projection therefore
has plaquette exactly \(\mathcal P(A^{-1}B)\).

Write \(c=\cosh t\), \(s_t=\sinh t\). Since \(H_i^2=I\) and
\(H_1H_2=-H_2H_1\),
\[
 X=A^{-1}B=c^2I+cs_t(H_2-H_1)-s_t^2H_1H_2,
 \qquad X-X^*=-s_t^2[H_1,H_2]\ne0.
\tag{R13}
\]
If \(\mathcal P(X)=I\), then \(X=(X^*X)^{1/2}\) would be Hermitian,
contradicting (R13). We can quantify the defect. Write \(X=UP\),
\(P=(X^*X)^{1/2}\). Then
\[
 X-X^*=(U-I)P+P(I-U^*),\qquad
 \|P\|=\|X\|\le e^{2t},\qquad
 \|X-X^*\|=2\sinh^2t.
\]
As \(U\) is unitary, \(\|I-U^*\|=\|I-U\|\). Hence
\[
             \|\mathcal P(A^{-1}B)-I\|
                      \ge e^{-2t}\sinh^2t>0.
\tag{R14}
\]
This uses only the displayed two by two identities. The polar factors have
determinant one, so they form an \(SU(2)\) lattice field.
\(\square\)

**Exact failure of this attack:** no constant \(C\), independent of the
ratio of the input radii, can make
\[
       \|\partial\mathcal P(W)-I\|\le C\epsilon_0h^2
\tag{R15}
\]
hold for all the small pairs. Hold \(h,t,\epsilon_1\) fixed as above and
choose \(\epsilon_0<e^{-2t}\sinh^2t/(Ch^2)\). The input still has zero
curvature, whereas (R14) contradicts (R15). Thus the unitary polar field
cannot be passed to the real theorem with Q119's curvature radius.
A bound with an additional twist-square term would not solve that problem.

This example is deliberately a flat *input*, not a nonflat tower output.
Actual averaging is gauge covariant; a pure-gauge datum is the gauge
transform of the identity datum. The continuation of the flat critical
orbit is flat by the covariance identity CMP 102 (181), read in its small
complex neighbourhood (printed pp.307–309), or by analytic continuation of
that identity at real gauges. Thus nothing in B12 contradicts Q3. It
excludes this canonical real-field reduction before any estimate of the
minimizer is invoked.
