## 3. Matrix and averaging lemmas

### Lemma 1. Elementary matrix calculus

Use any submultiplicative matrix norm. If \(s=\sum_j\|X_j\|\le1/4\), then
\[
 \left\|\prod_j e^{X_j}-1-\sum_jX_j\right\|\le s^2,
 \quad \left\|\prod_j e^{X_j}-1\right\|\le2s.                         \tag{1}
\]
For \(\|Z\|\le1/2\),
\[
 \|\log(1+Z)\|\le2\|Z\|,\qquad
 \|\log(1+Z)-Z\|\le\|Z\|^2.                                        \tag{2}
\]
Every derivative of fixed order of these functions is bounded on a fixed smaller ball. Bounds for a convex mean of such functions are independent of the number of summands.

**Proof.** Multiply the absolutely convergent exponential series. The sum of norms of terms of total degree \(r\) is at most \(s^r/r!\), by the multinomial identity. The terms of degree at least two have sum at most \(s^2\sum_{r\ge2}(1/4)^{r-2}/r!<s^2\). The logarithmic series gives (2), since \(1/r\le1/2\) for \(r\ge2\). Termwise differentiation is justified by uniform convergence of the differentiated series on a smaller ball. Its norm majorants are ordinary convergent power series. Convex weights have sum one, so averaging preserves every majorant.

Differentiating the exponential series, with \(X=X(t)\), gives
\[
 {d\over dt}e^X=\int_0^1 e^{(1-s)X}X'e^{sX}\,ds,\qquad
 (e^X)'e^{-X}=g(-\operatorname{ad}_X)X'.                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the coefficient of \(X^aX'X^b\) in the integral is
\(\int_0^1(1-s)^as^b/(a!b!)\,ds=1/(a+b+1)!\), by integration by parts. This is its coefficient in the differentiated series. For small \(X\), \(g(-\operatorname{ad}_X)=1+E\), \(\|E\|<1/2\), so the geometric series gives its inverse. Thus
\[
 {1\over i}{d\over dt}\log(e^{itH}e^{iY})\big|_{t=0}
       =g^{-1}(-i\operatorname{ad}_Y)H.                              \tag{4}
\]
The scalar identity \(g^{-1}(-z)e^z=g^{-1}(z)\) follows directly from the definition of \(g\). These prove the differentiations used in CMP 98 (32)–(41) without importing BCH. □

### Lemma 2. The plain average is covariant and local

For the CMP 98 contours, put \(T_c=U(c)\), where \(c\) denotes its straight fine path, and
\[
 \mathcal M(U)(c)=\exp\left(\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}L^{-d}
                \log\{U(\Gamma_{c,x})T_c^{-1}\}\right)T_c.           \tag{5}
\]
It is gauge covariant wherever the displayed chart is defined. Its depth-\(q\) value on \(c\) reads only \(B^q(c_-)\cup B^q(c_+)\).

**Proof.** Under a gauge \(v\), the loop \(U(\Gamma_{c,x})T_c^{-1}\) is conjugated by \(v(c_-)\). Conjugation commutes term by term with the log and exp series. The remaining \(T_c\) transforms by the two endpoint factors. This proves covariance. Every path in (5) is contained in the two blocks: an internal connector, a translated straight segment, and the reverse internal connector. At the next level, each coarse bond read in these paths reads its two child blocks. Their union is contained in the two parent blocks. Induction proves the claimed support. No small-field membership test is read. □

Equation (5) reconstructs OCR (42), which is garbled at CMP 98 OCR 353–359. The legible prose immediately following (43) and the account's definitions fix the order of the reference transport. This ordering is also checked by covariance and by (A5) below.

### Lemma 3. Local axial gauge from plaquettes

Let a rectangular box have sides at most \(D\), spacing \(h\), and unitary bond field \(U\). Suppose \(\|U(\partial p)-1\|\le f\). The lexicographic tree gauge gives
\[
 \|U^{a}(b)-1\|\le(d-1)(D/h)f.                                    \tag{6}
\]
If the right side is at most \(1/2\) and the links are in the fixed logarithm chart of \(G\), then \(U^a=e^{i h A}\) with
\(\|A\|\le2(d-1)Df/h^2\).

**Proof (port of CA-I 43714–43865).** Set \(a(x)=U(\gamma_x)\), with \(\gamma_x\) the coordinate-ordered path from the smallest corner. For \(b=(x,x+he_\mu)\), the loop \(\gamma_x b\gamma_{x+he_\mu}^{-1}\) can be cancelled by moving its last \(\mu\)-step past the later-coordinate steps. There are at most \((d-1)D/h\) swaps. Each swap changes the holonomy by a conjugated plaquette or its inverse. Unitary conjugation preserves the norm, and
\(\|P_1\cdots P_r-1\|\le\sum_i\|P_i-1\|\) for unitary factors, by telescoping. This proves (6). Tree bonds need no swaps and become 1. Equation (2) proves the logarithm bound. The local logarithm is Hermitian and belongs to the real Lie algebra; for SU(N), diagonalization and continuity of the trace from 1 verify this directly on its identity chart. □

### Lemma 4. First-order averaging, including the symmetric map

Suppose \(U_b=e^{ia_b}\) on the four double blocks of a block plaquette of side \(n\). Let \(\|a\|_\infty\le t/n\), with \(t\) below a fixed dimensional ceiling. For both (5) and the CMP 109 map in CA Definition 1.5,
\[
 \bar U(c)=1+i\langle a\rangle_c+E_c,\quad
 \|E_c\|\le C_d t^2,\qquad
 \langle a\rangle_c=n^{-d}\sum_x\{s_{c_-}(x)+a([x,x'])-s_{c_+}(x')\}.
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]
For CMP 98, \(s_y(x)=a(\Gamma_{y,x})\). For CMP 109, \(s_y(x)\) is the arithmetic mean of these sums over its coordinate permutations. It is the same connector function for every outgoing direction.

**Proof.** Each connector has at most \(dn\) bonds. Each complete path has at most \((2d+1)n\) bonds. Lemma 1 bounds its product by its signed linear bond sum with error \(C_dt^2\). For (5), expanding the relative loop, its log, the averaged exponential, and its reference transport cancels the reference transport's linear term. This leaves (7). Each error is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(t^2\); all site weights sum to one.

For completeness, reconstruct the inner mean from CMP 109 (0.10). Write its inputs as \(e^{X_j}\), \(\|X_j\|\le a\), and its output as \(e^T\). Its equation is
\[
 0=\sum_jw_j\log(e^{X_j}e^{-T})=\bar X-T+R(T),\quad
 \bar X=\sum_jw_jX_j,\quad \sum_jw_j=1.
\]
On \(\|T\|\le2a\), the series from Lemma 1 gives
\(\|R(T)\|\le C a^2\) and \(\|DR(T)\|\le Ca\). To see the second bound, the derivative at \((X,T)=(0,0)\) of \(\log(e^Xe^{-T})+T-X\) is zero. ### Lemma 7. Fundamental equality at every depth

## 4. Constructing the cube gauge without the first-order gauge input

The next argument extends `cp12.md` §4. That section's Lemma 5 already ports the scalar heat-kernel estimates of Sussman and Alpoge (with Claude and Codex as authors of their text), SA BGH2–BGH23. Its proof was read here in full. In particular, we use its bounds for the massive resolvent and its interior estimate (10). These are proved statements in the repository. The source passages SA 12534–12635 and 12753–12795 were also read. They do not supply a nonlinear gauge construction.

The new step below replaces cp12's hypothesis of a given C¹ gauge. A small potential with bounded **additive curl** is enough. The Hodge identity places the derivative on that curl; it does not bound a second resolvent derivative on arbitrary bounded data. This also avoids the multiscale gauge normalizations that the account's target C does not request. Balaban's original proof skeleton remains in §2 for comparison.

Throughout this section put \(h=\eta\le1\) and work in top-scale units. Use cp12's differences \(D_i^+,D_i^-\), \(\nabla\), \(\operatorname{div}\), and \(\Delta=\sum_iD_i^-D_i^+\). For a bond field define
\[
 (dB)_{\nu\mu}=D_\nu^+B_\mu-D_\mu^+B_\nu,
 \qquad (\delta F)_\mu=\sum_\nu D_\nu^-F_{\nu\mu}.
\]
Thus \(\Delta B=\delta dB+\nabla\operatorname{div}B\) by commuting differences. The positive ordinary operator \(\partial^*\partial\) on bonds is \(-\delta d\); signs do not affect its norm. Supremum norms include all components. All local estimates require the indicated difference stencils.

### Lemma 9. A bounded-curl primitive for the divergence

Let \(R=(-\Delta+1)^{-1}\) on bounded functions on \(h\mathbb Z^d\), with \(d=4\) for the application. For a bounded bond field set
\[
 \|B\|_X=\|B\|_\infty+\|dB\|_\infty,
 \quad \|\lambda\|_Y=\|\lambda\|_\infty+\|\nabla\lambda\|_\infty,
 \quad TB=-R\operatorname{div}B.
\]
There is a mesh-independent \(C_H\) such that
\[
 \|TB\|_Y\le C_H\|B\|_X.                                       \tag{20}
\]

**Proof.** The first-difference kernel estimate in cp12 Lemma 5, (9), bounds \(R\operatorname{div}B\) in supremum norm by \(C\|B\|_\infty\), by moving the backward differences onto the convolution kernel. The same estimate applies to \(R\delta F\). The resolvent commutes with every difference: its convolution kernel is translation invariant, and the differences are bounded operators at fixed \(h\). Therefore the exact Hodge identity gives
\[
 \nabla TB=B-RB+R\delta dB.                                     \tag{21}
\]
Indeed \(R\Delta=R-1\). The mass-one resolvent bound controls \(RB\), and the first-difference kernel bound controls \(R\delta dB\). This proves (20). No second-difference kernel norm was used. □

### Lemma 10. A massive gauge from a small potential and its curl

There are mesh-independent constants \(a_*,C_g>0\) such that the following holds. Let \(A\) be a real Lie-algebra-valued bond field on the full lattice, with \(\|A\|_X\le a\le a_*\). Then a real site field \(\lambda\) exists with
\[
 \|\lambda\|_Y\le C_g a,\qquad
 (e^{ihA})^{e^{i\lambda}}=e^{ihB},\qquad
 \|B\|_\infty\le C_g a,\qquad
 \operatorname{div}B=-\lambda.                                 \tag{22}
\]
In particular \(\|\nabla\operatorname{div}B\|_\infty\le C_g a\).

**Proof.** On a fixed small matrix chart let
\[
 \Phi(q,q+v,w)=i^{-1}\log(e^{iq}e^{iw}e^{-i(q+v)}).
\]
Define \(P(q)=\operatorname{Ad}(e^{iq})\), and
\(J(q)=-\partial_v\Phi(q,q+v,0)|_{v=0}\). Both equal the identity at zero. There is an exact expansion
\[
 \Phi(q,q+v,w)=P(q)w-J(q)v+r(q;v,w),                             \tag{23}
\]
with
\[
 |r|+|\partial_qr|\le C|v|(|v|+|w|),\quad
 |\partial_vr|\le C(|v|+|w|),\quad |\partial_wr|\le C|v|.        \tag{24}
\]
To justify the missing \(w^2\) term, observe the exact identity
\(\Phi(q,q,w)=P(q)w\). Subtract it, integrate the derivative in \(v\), and subtract that derivative's value at \((v,w)=(0,0)\). Integrating its bounded derivatives in \(v,w\) proves (24); differentiating the same integral in \(q\) proves the first bound for \(\partial_qr\). All these derivatives are bounded by the convergent series of Lemma 1 on a fixed smaller chart.

Put \(q=\lambda(x)\), \(v=hD_\mu^+\lambda(x)\), \(w=hA_\mu(x)\). Equation (23), divided by \(h\), gives the exact transformed potential
\[
 B=A-\nabla\lambda+N(\lambda,A),
 \quad N_\mu=(P(\lambda)-1)A_\mu-(J(\lambda)-1)D_\mu^+\lambda+E_\mu,
 \quad E_\mu=h^{-1}r(\lambda;hD_\mu^+\lambda,hA_\mu).            \tag{25}
\]
For \(\|\lambda\|_Y,\|\kappa\|_Y\le\rho\), with \(a,\rho\) within fixed chart bounds,
\[
 \|N(\lambda,A)\|_X\le C_N(a+\rho)\rho,
 \quad
 \|N(\lambda,A)-N(\kappa,A)\|_X
       \le C_N(a+\rho)\|\lambda-\kappa\|_Y.                     \tag{26}
\]
Here is the curl estimate in detail. For any site coefficient \(F\),
\[
 \begin{split}
 [d(F A)]_{\nu\mu}(x)={}&F(x)(dA)_{\nu\mu}(x)\ +
 (D_\nu^+F)(x)A_\mu(x+he_\nu)\ -
 (D_\mu^+F)(x)A_\nu(x+he_\mu).
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{27}
\]
For \(F=P(\lambda)-1\), these terms are bounded by \(C\rho\|dA\|+C\rho\|A\|\).
For \(F=J(\lambda)-1\) and \(A\) replaced by \(\nabla\lambda\), the first term vanishes, since \(d\nabla\lambda=0\). The two remaining terms are bounded by \(C\rho^2\). Finally (24) gives
\(\|E\|\le Ch\rho(\rho+a)\); the direct bound
\(\|dE\|\le4h^{-1}\|E\|\) has no mesh loss.

For the difference estimate in (26), write a coefficient difference along a bond as
\[
 D_\nu^+P(\lambda)(x)
 =\int_0^1 DP(\lambda(x)+thD_\nu^+\lambda(x))D_\nu^+\lambda(x)\,dt.
\]
Subtract the corresponding expression for \(\kappa\). Bounded first and second derivatives give
\(C\|\nabla(\lambda-\kappa)\|+C\rho\|\lambda-\kappa\|\), with a harmless additional \(Ch\rho\|\nabla(\lambda-\kappa)\|\). The same formula holds for \(J\). Insert these bounds into (27). For \(E\), (24) gives
\(\|E(\lambda)-E(\kappa)\|\le Ch(a+\rho)\|\lambda-\kappa\|_Y\), so the direct curl bound again cancels \(h\). The value estimates follow from (25). This proves all of (26).

Now solve
\[
 \lambda=T\{A+N(\lambda,A)\}.                                  \tag{28}
\]
Choose \(K\ge2C_H\), then \(a_*\) so small that all chart bounds hold on \(\rho=Ka_*\), and
\(C_HC_N(1+K)a_*\le1/2\). Equations (20) and (26) show that (28) maps the closed \(Y\)-ball of radius \(Ka\) into itself and contracts distances by at most \(1/2\). At fixed mesh the space of bounded fields with bounded first differences is complete: uniform limits preserve the difference relation. Iteration from zero therefore converges, because successive differences form a summable geometric sequence. Its limit solves (28). Every iterate is real and Lie-algebra-valued, and the local logarithm in (23) stays in that same real Lie algebra.

Apply \(-\Delta+1\) to (28). It gives
\((-\Delta+1)\lambda=-\operatorname{div}(A+N)\). Equation (25) consequently gives \(\operatorname{div}B=-\lambda\). Its value bound follows from (25)–(26), and differentiating the divergence identity proves the last assertion. Choose \(C_g\) after \(K,C_N\). □

This extends cp12 Lemma 6: no bound on \(\nabla A\) or \(\operatorname{div}A\) was assumed. The closure norm is \(\|A\|+\|dA\|\). Small curvature supplies that norm for a small logarithmic potential.

### Lemma 11. Current control and the interior regularity upgrade

Inputs: Lemma 10 and cp12 Lemma 5's interior estimate. This ports the bootstrap of cp12 Theorem 7, (17)–(19), while using **Balaban's actual current** from (C1). We do not identify that matrix current with the projected Wilson current used in cp12.

Suppose \(U=e^{ihB}\) on an enlarged cube, and there
\[
 \|B\|\le C_a s,\quad \|\nabla\operatorname{div}B\|\le C_a s,
 \quad \|U(\partial p)-1\|\le C_f s h^2,\quad
 \|D_U^{h*}\partial U\|\le C_J s h^2.                           \tag{29}
\]
The constants are fixed before \(s\). If the enlargement has thickness 6 in these units, and \(s\) is sufficiently small, then on the inner cube
\[
 \|\nabla B\|+\|\Delta B\|+\|\partial^*\partial B\|
       \le C(C_a,C_f,C_J,d)s.                                  \tag{30}
\]
Constants are independent of \(h\le1\) and the cube's size.

**Proof.** Write \(F_{\nu\mu}=h^{-2}i^{-1}\log U(\partial p_{\nu\mu})\) with the orientation that agrees with \((dB)_{\nu\mu}\), and extend it antisymmetrically. Smallness gives \(\|F\|\le2C_f s\). Balaban's operator is the covariant backward divergence of positively oriented plaquette matrices, with the incidence signs in CMP 99a (1.1)–(1.2), OCR 77–100. Expanding those matrices,
\[
 U(\partial p)=1+ih^2F(p)+E(p),\qquad |E(p)|\le C h^4|F|^2.
\]
The divergence annihilates the constant identity. Its norm on bounded arrays is at most \(C_d/h\). Moreover, replacing a covariant backward difference of \(F\) by its ordinary backward difference costs \(C\|B\|\|F\|\): the transport changes by at most \(Ch\|B\|\) before division by \(h\). Consequently, regardless of the overall adjoint sign convention,
\[
 |\delta F|\le h^{-2}|D_U^{h*}\partial U|
           +C\|B\|\|F\|+Ch\|F\|^2.                            \tag{31}
\]
This uses the full matrix bound in (C1), with exactly its factor \(h^2\). It does not require a current equation or a minimizing field.

The four-link logarithm has linear part \(ih^2dB\). Its analytic remainder has zero constant and linear parts. Lemma 1 gives
\[
 |dB-F|\le C\|B\|^2,\qquad
 |\delta(dB-F)|\le C\|B\|\max_{\rm stencil}|\nabla B|.           \tag{32}
\]
For the second bound, match the four arguments at adjacent parallel plaquettes. Each argument has size \(h\|B\|\); each difference has size at most \(h^2\max|\nabla B|\). The derivative of a quadratic remainder is bounded by \(Ch\|B\|\). Its change is therefore at most \(Ch^3\|B\|\max|\nabla B|\). Division by \(h^3\) proves the claim. Negative links are the negatives of the corresponding shifted positive link logarithms, so the same matching applies to them.

Combine (29)–(32) with the exact Hodge identity. On all admissible interior stencils,
\[
 |\Delta B|\le C_0s+C_1s\max_{\rm stencil}|\nabla B|.            \tag{33}
\]
Here \(C_0,C_1\) depend only on the fixed constants in (29) and on \(d\).

For completeness, the absorption does not assume a uniform bound on the last gradient. Fix a centre within distance 1 of the inner cube. Let \(\phi(t)\), \(1\le t\le2\), be the largest first difference on stencils contained in the radius-\(t\) cube about that centre. At every fixed mesh it is finite, bounded by \(2\|B\|/h\). For \(1\le t<t'\le2\), apply cp12 (10) on cubes of radius \((t'-t)/8\) covering those stencils. Equation (33) gives
\[
 \phi(t)\le C_2s\phi(t')+\frac{C_3s}{t'-t}+C_4s.               \tag{34}
\]
If that radius is less than \(64h\), the direct estimate
\(2\|B\|/h\le128\|B\|/[(t'-t)/8]\) gives (34) without its first term. Thus (34) holds for every gap, however small.

Choose \(C_2s\le1/4\). Iterate (34) at \(t_i=2-2^{-i}\). The inverse-gap terms have sum at most
\(2C_3s\sum_{i\ge0}(2C_2s)^i\); the constant terms have sum at most \(C_4s\sum_{i\ge0}(C_2s)^i\). Both converge. The terminal term tends to zero for each fixed \(h\), because \(\phi\) is bounded. Hence \(\phi(1)\le Cs\), with \(C\) independent of \(h\). Moving the centre gives this bound in the required interior neighbourhood. Equation (33) then bounds \(\Delta B\). Finally \(\partial^*\partial B=-\delta dB=-\Delta B+\nabla\operatorname{div}B\), which proves (30). □

The scalar input is also valid in any fixed dimension: in cp12's heat-kernel proof, replace the factor \(T^{-r-2}\) by \(T^{-r-d/2}\) in the weighted Parseval bound, and \(T^2\) by \(T^{d/2}\) in the reciprocal-weight sum. Cauchy–Schwarz cancels those dimension powers and gives the same \(t^{-r/2}\) kernel bound. Choose the weight exponent \(M>N+d/2\). The time integrals and the cutoff proof are then unchanged. Thus the lemmas have constants depending on \(d\), while the application here has \(d=4\).

### Lemma 12. One gauge for all the cubes in target C

Inputs: Lemmas 1, 3 and 9–11, the geometry (C2), and the two regularity assumptions (C1). No minimal-orbit theorem, multiscale propagator theorem, or first-order gauge hypothesis is used.

Put \(r=R_1M_1\), \(M=M^*\), and

The block ratio is the fixed odd integer \(L\ge3\); \(r\ge1\) and \(M\ge r\). Use the \(k\ge1\) setting of §F in which (C2) is written. The finite-mesh case is included below. Since \(\widetilde\square\subset\Omega_{k-1}\) and \(\xi_{k-1}=L^{-1}\), (C1) implies throughout the enclosing cube, on the required interior stencils,

 |U_0(\partial p)-1|<L^2\alpha_0h^2,\qquad
 |D_{U_0}^{h*}\partial U_0|<L^3\alpha_0h^2.                     \tag{36}

Its side is \(M+4r\le5M\). Lemma 3 gives a real axial gauge \(a\) with

 |U_0^a(b)-1|\le5(d-1)L^2M\alpha_0h.
\]
Choose \(s\) small enough that this is at most \(1/6\) and lies in the logarithm chart of the fixed group. The series bound

\]

 \|dA_0\|\le C(L^2\alpha_0+s^2)\le Cs.                         \tag{38}
\]
This is the rough gauge input. No first differences of \(A_0\) were estimated.

The boundary of \(\square_0\) is separated from that of \(\widetilde\square\) by at least

\]

\]

 \|\widetilde A\|\le s,
 \qquad \|d\widetilde A\|\le C(s+\sigma^{-1}s).                 \tag{41}
\]
All fields multiplied by a nonzero cutoff are defined strictly inside the original cube.

\[
 |B'|+|\nabla'\operatorname{div}'B'|\le C s.
\]

After rescaling, their coefficients are \(\sigma^2L^2\alpha_0\) and \(\sigma^3L^3\alpha_0\), respectively. Both are bounded by \(C_L s\). Lemma 11 therefore applies on the rescaled cube and gives, after returning to physical top units,
\[
 |A|,\quad |\nabla A|,\quad
 |\partial^*\partial A|,\quad|\Delta A|\le C_*(d,L)s
       \quad\hbox{on }\square_0.                              \tag{42}
\]
The fixed powers of \(\sigma^{-1}\) are included in \(C_*\). The same constant can include (40). It depends neither on \(M,r,k\) nor on \(h\).

The gauge is defined on all of \(\widetilde\square\), not just on the support of the cutoff. Apply \(e^{i\lambda}\) there to the original field \(U_0^a\). It agrees with the field in (42) wherever \(\chi=1\).

 u^{-1}=e^{i\lambda}a v^{-1},\qquad
 (U_0')^{u^{-1}}=U_0^{e^{i\lambda}a}=e^{ihA}.                   \tag{43}
\]
In the fallback (40), use \(\lambda=0\). This proves (C4). Every \(\square_j\) lies in \(\square_0\), and \(\xi_j\le1\). Multiplying (42) by \(\xi_j,\xi_j^2,\xi_j^3,\xi_j^3\), respectively, proves all four bounds (C5) with one and the same gauge.
