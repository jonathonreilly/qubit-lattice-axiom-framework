### 1.1 Exact mathematical target A: CA-I 24239–24389

This is `4.6:lem-averaging-locality`, credited to Balaban CMP 98 (58)–(63), (77)–(81), (88)–(92), (121)–(125), pp. 26 and 31. The following merely compresses repeated notation in its displays.

Use Balaban's fixed lattice hierarchy \(\Omega\supset\Omega^{(1)}\supset\cdots\supset\Omega^{(k)}\), blocks \(B(y)\), iterated blocks \(B^j(y)\), and contours \(\Gamma_{y,x}\). Put \(R(X)Z=XZX^{-1}\). Let \(V_0,U_0\) be backgrounds. Bond perturbations are on the left. Write \(\mathcal M V=\bar V\) for **the CMP 98 average**, and
\[
 P_y(V_1;x)=(R_{0,y}V_1)(\Gamma_{y,x}),\quad
 m_y(V_1)=\exp\left(\sum_{x\in B(y)}L^{-d}\log P_y(V_1;x)\right),
 \quad \bar R_{0,c}=R((\bar V_0)_c).
\]
All logarithms below are in the common small chart specified in CMP 98.

(a) Relative axiality is
\[
 (R_{0,y}V')(\Gamma_{y,x})
 =\prod_{b\subset\Gamma_{y,x}}R(V_0(\Gamma_{y,b_-}))V'_b=1.
\]
For the configuration \(V=(V_1V_0)^v\), the gauge in the account is fixed by
\[
 (R_{0,y}v)(x)=v(y)P_y(V_1;x),\qquad
 (\overline{R_0v})(y)=1,\qquad v(y)=m_y(V_1)^{-1}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 (\bar V)_c(\bar V_0)_c^{-1}
 =m_{c_-}(V_1)^{-1}(\overline{V_1V_0})_c(\bar V_0)_c^{-1}
       \bar R_{0,c}m_{c_+}(V_1).                                      \tag{A1}
\]
Here, as in the account, an adjoint operator acts on the entire factor immediately following it.

(b) For site functions,
\[
 (\overline{R(V_0)v})(y)=v(y)\exp\left(
 \sum_{x\in B(y)}L^{-d}\log\{v(y)^{-1}R(V_0(\Gamma_{y,x}))v(x)\}\right).
                                                                    \tag{A2}
\]
For \(u\) on \(\Omega\), set \(F_1(u)=\overline{R(U_0)u}\) and
\[
 F_{j+1}(u)=\overline{R(\bar U_0^{\,j})F_j(u)}.
\]
The imposed normalization is \(F_k(u)(y)=1\). Higher contour operations use the chain of block centres \(x_{j+1},x_j,\ldots,x\), as in (77).

(c) Define \(S_{V_0}(V_1)\) by the right side of (A1), let
\(S_1=S_{U_0}(U_1)\), and \(S_{j+1}=S_{\bar U_0^{\,j}}(S_j)\). These are the account's double-bar averages. Write \(\widetilde U_1^{\,j}=\mathcal M^j(U_1U_0)(\mathcal M^jU_0)^{-1}\), and let \(m^{(k)}_y=\overline{R_{0,y}U_1}^{(k)}\) be the recursively averaged contour variable of CMP 98 (82)–(85). Then
\[
 (m^{(k)}_{b_-})^{-1}\widetilde U_1^{\,k}(b)
       R((\bar U_0^{\,k})_b)m^{(k)}_{b_+}=S_k(b).                     \tag{A3}
\]
For each coarse bond \(c\), \(S_k(c)\) reads only the fine bonds contained in
\(B^k(c_-)\cup B^k(c_+)\). The backgrounds read the same set. These constructions use no sequence of small-field domains.

(d) Put \(V_1=e^{iA}\), \(Q(V_0,A,c)=i^{-1}\log S_{V_0}(V_1)(c)\). Then
\[
 Q(V_0,A,c)=L(Q(V_0)A)_c+C(V_0,A,c),\qquad

Here \(|A|<\alpha_1\); CMP 98 Proposition 3 chooses \(C_1=C_1(d)\) first and a small threshold \(c_3(d,L)\le c_2\) for \(\alpha_0,\alpha_1\).

\[
 Y_x=i^{-1}\log V_0(\Gamma_{c,x}(-c)),\quad Y=\sum_xL^{-d}Y_x,
 \quad g(z)=(1-e^{-z})/z,\quad g(0)=1,
\]
\[
 h_x=(R_{0,c_-}A)(\Gamma_{c_-,x}),\quad
 t_x=(R_{0,c_-}A)([x,x']),\quad
 k_x=(R_{0,c_+}A)(\Gamma_{c_+,x'}),\quad a=(R_{0,c_-}A)(c),

## 3. Matrix and averaging lemmas

### Lemma 1. Elementary matrix calculus

Use any submultiplicative matrix norm. If \(s=\sum_j\|X_j\|\le1/4\), then
\[
 \left\|\prod_j e^{X_j}-1-\sum_jX_j\right\|\le s^2,
 \quad \left\|\prod_j e^{X_j}-1\right\|\le2s.                         \tag{1}
\]
For \(\|Z\|\le1/2\),
\[
 \|\log(1+Z)\|\le2\|Z\|,\qquad
 \|\log(1+Z)-Z\|\le\|Z\|^2.                                        \tag{2}
\]
Every derivative of fixed order of these functions is bounded on a fixed smaller ball. Bounds for a convex mean of such functions are independent of the number of summands.

**Proof.** Multiply the absolutely convergent exponential series. The sum of norms of terms of total degree \(r\) is at most \(s^r/r!\), by the multinomial identity. The terms of degree at least two have sum at most \(s^2\sum_{r\ge2}(1/4)^{r-2}/r!<s^2\). The logarithmic series gives (2), since \(1/r\le1/2\) for \(r\ge2\). Termwise differentiation is justified by uniform convergence of the differentiated series on a smaller ball. Its norm majorants are ordinary convergent power series. Convex weights have sum one, so averaging preserves every majorant.

Differentiating the exponential series, with \(X=X(t)\), gives
\[
 {d\over dt}e^X=\int_0^1 e^{(1-s)X}X'e^{sX}\,ds,\qquad
 (e^X)'e^{-X}=g(-\operatorname{ad}_X)X'.                             \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the coefficient of \(X^aX'X^b\) in the integral is
\(\int_0^1(1-s)^as^b/(a!b!)\,ds=1/(a+b+1)!\), by integration by parts. This is its coefficient in the differentiated series. For small \(X\), \(g(-\operatorname{ad}_X)=1+E\), \(\|E\|<1/2\), so the geometric series gives its inverse. Thus
\[
 {1\over i}{d\over dt}\log(e^{itH}e^{iY})\big|_{t=0}
       =g^{-1}(-i\operatorname{ad}_Y)H.                              \tag{4}
\]
The scalar identity \(g^{-1}(-z)e^z=g^{-1}(z)\) follows directly from the definition of \(g\). These prove the differentiations used in CMP 98 (32)–(41) without importing BCH. □

### Lemma 2. The plain average is covariant and local

For the CMP 98 contours, put \(T_c=U(c)\), where \(c\) denotes its straight fine path, and
\[
 \mathcal M(U)(c)=\exp\left(\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}L^{-d}
                \log\{U(\Gamma_{c,x})T_c^{-1}\}\right)T_c.           \tag{5}
\]
It is gauge covariant wherever the displayed chart is defined. Its depth-\(q\) value on \(c\) reads only \(B^q(c_-)\cup B^q(c_+)\).

**Proof.** Under a gauge \(v\), the loop \(U(\Gamma_{c,x})T_c^{-1}\) is conjugated by \(v(c_-)\). Conjugation commutes term by term with the log and exp series. The remaining \(T_c\) transforms by the two endpoint factors. This proves covariance. Every path in (5) is contained in the two blocks: an internal connector, a translated straight segment, and the reverse internal connector. At the next level, each coarse bond read in these paths reads its two child blocks. Their union is contained in the two parent blocks. Induction proves the claimed support. No small-field membership test is read. □

Equation (5) reconstructs OCR (42), which is garbled at CMP 98 OCR 353–359. The legible prose immediately following (43) and the account's definitions fix the order of the reference transport. This ordering is also checked by covariance and by (A5) below.

### Lemma 3. Local axial gauge from plaquettes

Let a rectangular box have sides at most \(D\), spacing \(h\), and unitary bond field \(U\). Suppose \(\|U(\partial p)-1\|\le f\). The lexicographic tree gauge gives
\[
 \|U^{a}(b)-1\|\le(d-1)(D/h)f.                                    \tag{6}
\]
If the right side is at most \(1/2\), then \(U^a=e^{i h A}\) with
\(\|A\|\le2(d-1)Df/h^2\).

**Proof (port of CA-I 43714–43865).** Set \(a(x)=U(\gamma_x)\), with \(\gamma_x\) the coordinate-ordered path from the smallest corner. For \(b=(x,x+he_\mu)\), the loop \(\gamma_x b\gamma_{x+he_\mu}^{-1}\) can be cancelled by moving its last \(\mu\)-step past the later-coordinate steps. There are at most \((d-1)D/h\) swaps. Each swap changes the holonomy by a conjugated plaquette or its inverse. Unitary conjugation preserves the norm, and
\(\|P_1\cdots P_r-1\|\le\sum_i\|P_i-1\|\) for unitary factors, by telescoping. This proves (6). Tree bonds need no swaps and become 1. Equation (2) proves the logarithm bound. The local logarithm is Hermitian and belongs to the real Lie algebra; for SU(N), diagonalization and continuity of the trace from 1 verify this directly on its identity chart. □

### Lemma 4. First-order averaging, including the symmetric map

Suppose \(U_b=e^{ia_b}\) on the four double blocks of a block plaquette of side \(n\). Let \(\|a\|_\infty\le t/n\), with \(t\) below a fixed dimensional ceiling. For both (5) and the CMP 109 map in CA Definition 1.5,
\[
 \bar U(c)=1+i\langle a\rangle_c+E_c,\quad
 \|E_c\|\le C_d t^2,\qquad
 \langle a\rangle_c=n^{-d}\sum_x\{s_{c_-}(x)+a([x,x'])-s_{c_+}(x')\}.
                                                                    \tag{7}
\]
For CMP 98, \(s_y(x)=a(\Gamma_{y,x})\). For CMP 109, \(s_y(x)\) is the arithmetic mean of these sums over its coordinate permutations. It is the same connector function for every outgoing direction.

**Proof.** Each connector has at most \(dn\) bonds. Each complete path has at most \((2d+1)n\) bonds. Lemma 1 bounds its product by its signed linear bond sum with error \(C_dt^2\). For (5), expanding the relative loop, its log, the averaged exponential, and its reference transport cancels the reference transport's linear term. This leaves (7). Each error is bounded by a fixed multiple of \(t^2\); all site weights sum to one.

For completeness, reconstruct the inner mean from CMP 109 (0.10). Write its inputs as \(e^{X_j}\), \(\|X_j\|\le a\), and its output as \(e^T\). Its equation is
\[
 0=\sum_jw_j\log(e^{X_j}e^{-T})=\bar X-T+R(T),\quad
 \bar X=\sum_jw_jX_j,\quad \sum_jw_j=1.
\]
On \(\|T\|\le2a\), the series from Lemma 1 gives
\(\|R(T)\|\le C a^2\) and \(\|DR(T)\|\le Ca\). To see the second bound, the derivative at \((X,T)=(0,0)\) of \(\log(e^Xe^{-T})+T-X\) is zero. Its bounded second derivative gives the bound by integration along the radial segment. The first follows in the same manner from its zero constant and linear terms. Choose \(a\) after \(C\), so \(Ca\le1/4\). The map \(T\mapsto\bar X+R(T)\) preserves the ball of radius \(2a\) and contracts it. Successive substitution converges geometrically to its unique fixed point. For real input it stays in the skew-Hermitian traceless subspace. Its solution satisfies \(T=\bar X+O(a^2)\), uniformly in the number of paths.

Thus the averaged connector of (0.11) has exactly the averaged connector sum as its linear term. Inserting those connectors into (0.12) and repeating the first paragraph proves (7). Reversal gives the inverse connector. Translation of a block translates its family of permutations and preserves all weights. This is the specific transfer needed here, rather than an appeal to universal transfer of Balaban's theorems. The argument follows the P1 proof of CA-I 130714–130779, with the mean's required Taylor property just proved. □

### Lemma 5. Signed plaquette average

With fine spacing \(h\), suppose \(\|U(\partial p)-1\|\le f\) on the local box \(\Delta(P)\). Then, provided \(n^2f\le c_d\),
\[
 \left\|\bar U(\partial P)-1-i n^{-d}\sum_{x\in B(y)}
                    \sum_{p\subset(P)_x}\Phi^{\mathrm{loc}}(p)\right\|
             \le C_d(n^2f)^2.                                      \tag{8}
\]
The same \(C_d\) may be used for its forward and reverse triangle inequalities. Equations (B1)–(B2) hold exactly.

**Proof.** Work in units of one fine bond. The local axial box has side at most \(2n\). Lemma 3 gives dimensionless bond logarithms \(a_b\) with
\(\|a_b\|\le4dnf\). Thus Lemma 4 applies with \(t=4dn^2f\). Multiplying the four coarse links and using (1) yields
\[
 \bar U(\partial P)=1+i\sum_{c\in\partial P}\langle a\rangle_c
                        +O_d((n^2f)^2).                            \tag{9}
\]
For a fixed block offset \(\rho\), each connector sum \(s_z(z+\rho)\) appears once positively and once negatively around the four corners. It cancels. What remains is the four straight sides of the translated square \((P)_{y+\rho}\). Each interior fine edge in the sum of its plaquette boundaries also cancels in pairs. Therefore
\[
 \sum_{c\in\partial P}\langle a\rangle_c
       =n^{-d}\sum_x\sum_{p\subset(P)_x}a(\partial p).               \tag{10}
\]
This ports the full cancellation in CA-I 130522–130620. The argument applies also to the averaged connector sums in Lemma 4, since they are direction independent. There is no commutativity assumption on the matrices: (10) is an identity of additive Lie-algebra sums.

On one fine plaquette, (1)–(2) give
\(\|\Phi(p)-a(\partial p)\|\le C_d n^2f^2\). Indeed the four link logarithms have total norm at most \(16dnf\); the quadratic product and logarithm errors have that squared size. The normalized double sum contains \(n^2\) plaquettes with multiplicity. Its replacement error is at most \(C_dn^4f^2\). Inserting this in (9) proves (8).

For a block with offsets \(0,\ldots,n-1\), the number of pairs \(r,s\) with \(r+s=q\) is \(n-|q-(n-1)|\) for \(0\le q\le2n-2\), and is zero otherwise. The independent choices in the two plaquette directions give the two tents. The other coordinates must belong to the block. Finally each of the \(n^2\) choices of \(s\) fixes \(x=z-s\), which lies in one and only one block. This proves \(\sum_y\omega_y(z)=n^2\) and the regrouping (B1), porting CA-II 123003–123005. □
