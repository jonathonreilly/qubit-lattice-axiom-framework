## 1. The objects

**The class.** CA-I 12949–12967 (Definition 4.5:def-admissible-pairs), with the cube class of
CMP 99b (3.35) taken at side $12\mathfrak M$ as the account states ("the number $O(1)$ may be
taken equal to 12", CA-I 12955–12957; CMP 99b p. 409: "the number $O(1)$ in the condition
(3.35) can be taken as equal to 12"). Write $\ell_q=L^q\eta$, $\eta=L^{-k}$. A *class cube of
index $q$* is a set $\square$ of fine sites with these properties:

- it is a union of level-$q$ big blocks (blocks of side $\mathfrak M\ell_q$ of the level-$q$
  big-block grid), and it is a cube of side $12\mathfrak M\ell_q$;
- $\square\subset B^q(\Lambda_q)\cup B^{q+1}(\Lambda_{q+1})=\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+2}$;
- $\square\cap B^q(\Lambda_q)=\square\cap(\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+1})\neq\emptyset$.

Here $\Omega_{k+1}=\Omega_{k+2}=\emptyset$. A *bond of $\square$* has both end points in
$\square$. (S1) asks, for each class cube, a $G$-valued gauge $u$ on $\square$ with
$U_r^u=e^{i\eta A}$ on the bonds of $\square$, $\ell_q|A|<a_0$ and $\ell_q^2|\nabla A|<a_0$
there. As a set of fine sites, a cube of side $s$ is a product of four runs of $s/\eta$
consecutive sites; two of its sites differ by at most $s-\eta$ in each coordinate.

**Bałaban's restricted class.** CMP 99b p. 396 (page image read): "Here $O(1)$ will mean a
number $\geq 10$. The set $\Lambda_0$ in the above condition means $\Lambda_0=\Omega_0\setminus\Omega_1$,
where $\Omega_0=\{$a union of big blocks of the lattice $T_1$, such that their distances to
$\Omega_1$ are $\le RM\}$." P. 394 also allows "a thinner layer of the big blocks surrounding
$\Omega_1$". The levels $j\ge1$ follow CMP 96 (2.1)–(2.2): each $\Omega_j$ is "a sum of big
blocks" with $(L^j\eta)^{-1}\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})>RM$. No thickness is imposed
on $\Omega_j$, $j\ge1$. So the restricted class is the account's class with one extra rule:
$\Omega_0$ is a neighbourhood of $\Omega_1$ made of level-0 big blocks.

**Carriers and (D).** As in `library/b98p5.md` §3: a row $c$ of height $j\ge1$ is a level-$j$
bond with $c_-\in\Lambda_j$; $E_c=B^j(c_-)\cup B^j(c_+)$, all fine bonds in that double block.
(D): every elementary plaquette $p\subset E_c$ has
$\|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le8\sqrt N L^2a_0(\eta/\ell_j)^2$, with $\|\cdot\|_F$ the
unnormalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm.

## 2. Task (1): the thin finger against (D)

### 2.1 The literal finger does not reach a carrier

Proposition 3.2 of `library/print-inverse.md` puts the finger $F$ in $\Omega_0$ at distance
more than $(R+24)\mathfrak M\eta$ from $\Omega_1$, and changes one bond $b_0$ in its middle.

*Claim.* No carrier $E_c$ of height $j\ge1$ contains a bond of $F$.

*Proof.* Take a row $c$ of height $j\ge1$. Its end point $c_-$ lies in $\Lambda_j\subset\Omega_j$.
$\Omega_j$ is a union of level-$j$ blocks, so $B^j(c_-)\subset\Omega_j\subset\Omega_1$. The
other block $B^j(c_+)$ is a translate by $\ell_j$, so every site of $E_c$ lies within
$\ell^\infty$-distance $\ell_j$ of $\Omega_j$. For $j=1$ this is $L\eta<(R+24)\mathfrak M\eta$.
For $j\ge2$ the gap (CA-I 12717) puts the $\ell_j$-neighbourhood of $\Omega_j$ inside
$\Omega_{j-1}\subset\Omega_1$. In both cases $E_c$ misses $F$. ∎

So at the finger's configuration $U_t$, $U_r=1$ on every carrier bond and (D) holds with
left side zero. The finger breaks the level-0 propagator, not the averaging of rows of height
$\ge1$.

### 2.2 The same mechanism at a level $j\ge1$: Proposition 1

**Proposition 1.** Let $d=4$, $G=SU(N)$ with $N\ge2$, $L\ge3$ odd, $\mathfrak M\ge L$, $R\ge R_1$
and $k\ge1$. Let $w$ be an integer with
\[
 wL\ge26,\qquad w\le11 .
\]
(For $L=3$: $w\in\{9,10,11\}$; for $L\ge27$ every $1\le w\le11$.) Take the fine periodic
lattice of physical side $P$, a multiple of $\mathfrak M$ with $P\ge(w+26)\mathfrak M$
($\ell_k=1$). Let $F$ be a cube of side $w\mathfrak M$ that is a union of $w^4$ level-$k$ big
blocks. Take the tower
\[
 \Omega_0=\dots=\Omega_{k-1}=T_\eta,\qquad \Omega_k=F
\]
(for $k=1$ one may instead take $\Omega_0$ to be the union of level-0 big blocks within
distance $R\mathfrak M\eta$ of $F$). Let $x_0$ be a level-$k$ site with
$x_{0,\mu}-f_\mu\in[w\mathfrak M/2-1,\,w\mathfrak M/2)$ for each $\mu$, where $f_\mu$ is the
least $\mu$-coordinate of $F$. Put $b_0=(x_0,x_0+\eta e_1)$ and
\[
 V=\exp(i\pi H),\quad H=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0,\dots,0),\qquad
 V=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,1,\dots,1)\in SU(N).
\]
Let $U_r(b_0)=V$, $U_r=1$ on every other bond, $\mathfrak a=0$, $J=0$, $U=U_r$. Then:

(a) the tower is admissible (CA-I 12712–12724), and it is in Bałaban's restricted class;

(b) $(U,J)$ satisfies (S1), (S2), (S3) for every $a_0,a_1,a_\gamma>0$;

(c) the row $c_0=(x_0,x_0+\ell_ke_1)$ has height $k$, and the plaquette $p_0$ with corners
$x_0$, $x_0+\eta e_1$, $x_0+\eta e_1+\eta e_2$, $x_0+\eta e_2$ lies in $E_{c_0}$ with
$\|U_r(\partial p_0)-I\|_F=2\sqrt2$ and $|U(\partial p_0)-1|_{\rm op}=2$;

(d) hence (D) fails at $(c_0,p_0)$ whenever $8\sqrt N a_0<2\sqrt2$, and the display
(4.5:eq-admissible-pairs-1) fails at level $k$ whenever $a_0+a_1<1/2$;

(e) the literal straightened map $q_{c_0}=Q_k(U,\eta A,c_0)$ of `library/b98p5.md` (5)–(6) is
not defined at $U$: one of its logarithms has argument $V^{-1}$, outside the identity chart.

*Proof.* (a) Each $\Omega_j$ is a union of level-$j$ big blocks: $T_\eta$ is, as $P$ is a
multiple of $\mathfrak M=\mathfrak M\ell_k$, which is a multiple of $\mathfrak M\ell_j$; $F$ is
by choice. For $j\le k-1$, $\Omega_j^c=\emptyset$, so
$\mathrm{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})=+\infty>R\mathfrak M\ell_j$. There is no gap condition
past $k$. CA-I 12723–12724 admits $\Omega_j=T_\eta$. For $k\ge2$, $\Omega_0=\Omega_1=T_\eta$,
which is Bałaban's $\Omega_0$ for this $\Omega_1$. For $k=1$ the alternative $\Omega_0$ is his
$\Omega_0$ by definition; each site outside it lies in a level-0 big block at distance more than
$R\mathfrak M\eta$ from $F$, so the gap holds strictly.

(b) Since $\mathfrak a=0$ and $J=0$, (S2) and (S3) hold. For (S1) we show that $x_0$ lies in no
class cube. Then $b_0$ is a bond of no class cube, $U_r=1$ on every bond of every class cube,
and $u=1$, $A=0$ satisfy the strict bounds.

- Index $q\le k-2$ ($k\ge2$): $\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+1}=T_\eta\setminus T_\eta=\emptyset$,
  so no class cube has index $q$.
- Index $k$: a class cube would lie in $\Omega_k\setminus\Omega_{k+2}=F$. It has $12\mathfrak
  M/\eta$ sites per coordinate run, and $F$ has $w\mathfrak M/\eta<12\mathfrak M/\eta$. None
  exists.
- Index $k-1$ (or index 0 with the alternative $\Omega_0$ when $k=1$): the cube contains a
  site $z\notin F$. So for some $\mu$, $z_\mu<f_\mu$ or $z_\mu\ge f_\mu+w\mathfrak M$. Two sites
  of the cube differ by at most $12\mathfrak M\ell_{k-1}-\eta=12\mathfrak M/L-\eta$ in each
  coordinate. But
  $\min\{x_{0,\mu}-f_\mu+\eta,\;f_\mu+w\mathfrak M-x_{0,\mu}\}\ge w\mathfrak M/2-1$, and
  $w\mathfrak M/2-1\ge12\mathfrak M/L$ because $\mathfrak M(wL-24)\ge2\mathfrak M\ge2L$. So
  $|x_{0,\mu}-z_\mu|>12\mathfrak M/L-\eta$, and the cube does not contain $x_0$.

$P\ge(w+26)\mathfrak M$ keeps all these coordinate comparisons inside one period.

(c) $x_0$ and $x_0+\ell_ke_1$ are level-$k$ sites of $F$ (the second because
$x_{0,1}-f_1+1<w\mathfrak M-\eta$), so both lie in $\Lambda_k=F$ and $c_0$ is an interior row of
height $k$. The block $B^k(x_0)$ contains every fine site within $(L^k-1)\eta/2\ge\eta$ of
$x_0$ in each coordinate, so $p_0\subset B^k(x_0)\subset E_{c_0}$. Orient
$U(\partial p_0)=U(x_0,x_0+\eta e_1)\,U(x_0+\eta e_1,x_0+\eta e_1+\eta e_2)\,
U(x_0+\eta e_2,x_0+\eta e_1+\eta e_2)^{-1}U(x_0,x_0+\eta e_2)^{-1}=V$. Then
$V-I=\mathrm{diag}(-2,-2,0,\dots,0)$, with Frobenius norm $2\sqrt2$ and operator norm 2.

(d) The right side of (D) is $8\sqrt NL^2a_0L^{-2k}\le8\sqrt Na_0$. The right side of the
display is $4(a_0+a_1)L^{-2k}<2$ when $a_0+a_1<1/2$. Round 1 fixes
$a_*=\rho/(16\sqrt NK_*)$, far below both thresholds.

(e) Write $c^{(m)}=(x_0,x_0+\ell_me_1)$, $1\le m\le k$. Since $x_0$ is a level-$k$ site, it is
a level-$m$ site for every $m$, and $c^{(m)}$ is the first coarse bond of the straight path of
$c^{(m+1)}$. Recursion (6) of `library/b98p5.md` forms $W_m=\mathcal M^m(U)$ and evaluates
$c_0$ from $W_{k-1}$ on the straight path of $c_0$, so it needs $W_1(c^{(1)})$. By
`library/b98.md` (5),
$\mathcal M(U)(c^{(1)})=\exp\big(\sum_{x\in B(x_0)}L^{-d}\log\{U(\Gamma_{c^{(1)},x})T^{-1}\}\big)T$
with $T=U(c^{(1)})$, the product along the straight fine path, whose first bond is $b_0$; so
$T=V$. Take $x=x_0+\eta e_2\in B(x_0)$. Its comb path is the one-step connector
$(x_0,x_0+\eta e_2)$, the straight segment from $x$ to $x+\ell_1e_1$ (transverse coordinate
$x_{0,2}+\eta$, so it avoids $b_0$), and the one-step connector back to $x_0+\ell_1e_1$. None
of these is $b_0$, so $U(\Gamma_{c^{(1)},x})=1$ and the argument of that logarithm is
$V^{-1}$. $V^{-1}-I$ has eigenvalue $-2$, so the series $\sum_n(-1)^{n+1}(X-I)^n/n$ diverges
at $X=V^{-1}$, and $V^{-1}$ has eigenvalue $-1$, on the cut of the principal logarithm. The map
is therefore not defined at $U$ in the chart that (5) and `library/b98p5.md` (7)–(8) use. ∎

**Remark (the reading of $O(1)$).** P. 396 says "$O(1)$ will mean a number $\geq10$", and (3.35)
scales the bound with "a size of $\square$". If the class held cubes of *every* side

## 3. Task (2): where (D) holds

Proposition 1 rules out a proof of (D) on Bałaban's restricted class. This section proves (D) on
two smaller classes. Lemma 3.1 uses the fine plaquette bound that every CMP 109 pair carries;
it does not read the cube class, so it is a new route. Lemma 3.3 is round 1's attack three
(direct plaquette control) with the missing covering step supplied, on thick towers only.

### 3.1 The plaquette clause, and the CMP 109 inclusion

**Definition (PL).** A pair $(U,J)\in\mathfrak S$ with tower $\{\Omega_j\}$ satisfies (PL) with
constant $\alpha$ if its decomposition $U=e^{i\eta\mathfrak a}U_r$ has
\[
 \|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le\sqrt N\,\alpha\,\eta^2
 \qquad\text{for every plaquette $p$ with its four corners in }\Omega_0 .
 \tag{PL}
\]
Here lengths are in the units of CA-I 12667–12668, so $\ell_k=1$ and $\ell_j\le1$.

**Lemma 3.1.** If $(U,J)$ satisfies (PL) with $\alpha\le8L^2a_0$, then (D) holds at every row of
height $j\ge1$.

*Proof.* Let $c$ be a row of height $j\ge1$. Section 2.1 shows that every site of $E_c$ is
within $\ell^\infty$-distance $\ell_j$ of $\Omega_j$. The gap (CA-I 12717), read in any of the
$\ell^1,\ell^2,\ell^\infty$ metrics, gives $\ell^\infty$-distance more than
$R\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}/d\ge\ell_j$ from $\Omega_j$ to $\Omega_{j-1}^c$, once
$R\mathfrak M\ge dL$. So $E_c\subset\Omega_{j-1}\subset\Omega_0$, and every plaquette
$p\subset E_c$ has its corners in $\Omega_0$. By (PL) and $\ell_j\le1$,
$\|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le\sqrt N\alpha\eta^2\le8\sqrt NL^2a_0(\eta/\ell_j)^2$. ∎

**Corollary 3.2 (the account's two entry points satisfy (PL)).**

(i) *CMP 109 inclusion* (CA-I 12996–13018 and its proof 13037–13085). Let $(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)$
satisfy CMP 109 (i)–(iii) on $X$ with parameters $(\alpha_0,\alpha_1,\gamma_0)$ under the
account's ceilings, and let $\Omega_0=X$. With the inclusion's decomposition
($\mathfrak a=A'$, $U_r$ the $G$-valued factor of (i)), CMP 109 (1.11) reads
$|\partial U_r-1|<\alpha_0\xi^2$ on $X$, $\xi=\eta$ (CMP 109 OCR 700–703; quoted at CA-I 13042).
Whether $|\cdot|$ is the operator norm or Bałaban's normalised trace norm (CMP 99b p. 392,
"tr 1 = 1"), $\|\cdot\|_F\le\sqrt N|\cdot|$. So (PL) holds with $\alpha=\alpha_0$. The first
ceiling $c_{12}LMB^{109}\alpha_0\le a_0$, with $c_{12}L\ge2$ (floor (4.5:eq-admissible-pairs-3))
and $M,B^{109}\ge1$, gives $\alpha_0\le a_0\le8L^2a_0$. Hence (D) holds on every pair of the
inclusion, for every admissible tower of $X$, thin or thick.

(ii) *Glued window* (CA-I 4.5:prop-glued-window, 16997–17100). There the source
$(\mathbf U,\mathbf J)\in\mathfrak U$ satisfies (i)–(iii) "on the whole lattice $T_\eta$"
(CA-I 17061–17063), and the product pair is placed in $\mathfrak S$ "with $U_r:=U$" (17090, 17145).
So (1.11) holds for $U_r$ on all of $T_\eta$ with constant $\alpha_0'''=(1+2\beta)\alpha_0$,
and the ceiling $c_{12}LMB^{109}\alpha_0'''\le a_0$ (eq-glued-window-a) gives
$\alpha_0'''\le a_0$. (PL) and (D) follow as in (i). The fine spacing of that space is at most
$\eta$ in level-$k$ units, so $\xi^2\le(\eta/\ell_j)^2$ again.

(iii) *Orbit extension.* (D) is a statement about $U_r$ of a representative in $\mathfrak S$.
Points of $\mathfrak S^{\rm orb}$ are complex gauge transforms of such representatives
(CA-I 12982–12986), so their representatives satisfy (D) when the representative does. Round 1
(statement T, last paragraph) transfers the derivative bounds through the orbit with the factor
$4^4$. ∎

**What 3.1–3.2 give for B98-P5.** On the subclass $\mathfrak S^{\rm pl}$ of pairs with (PL) at
$\alpha\le8L^2a_0$, Lemma 3.1 supplies (D). Then round 1 §3.1 (tree gauge on $E_c$, then the
twist) supplies (11), and round 1 §§2.3–2.4 give statement T. So T holds on

by the other model. I re-did §3.1's two estimates while reading: the coordinate tree from the
lowest corner of the box $E_c$ needs at most $(d-1)\cdot2\ell_j/\eta$ plaquette swaps per bond,
all inside $E_c$, so (D) gives
$\|U_r^g(b)-I\|_F\le2(d-1)(\ell_j/\eta)\cdot8\sqrt NL^2a_0(\eta/\ell_j)^2\le16d\sqrt NL^2a_0\eta/\ell_j$,
which is (18); and (S2) on layers $j-1,j,j+1$ gives $\ell_j\|\mathfrak a\|_F\le L\sqrt Na_1$,
which with (7) gives (19). First failing step none, for these two displays only.

On $\mathfrak S^{\rm pl}$ the stronger input (PL) also makes the cube test unnecessary for (D).
The display (4.5:eq-admissible-pairs-1) of the account then holds for $U_r$ as a consequence of

### 3.2 Thick towers: direct plaquette control with the covering step

This is the closest true statement on a class defined by the cube test alone.

**Definition (TH).** For each $j\ge1$, $\Omega_j$ is a union of cells of a grid $\mathcal G_j$
whose cells are cubes of side $s_j$; $s_j$ is a multiple of $\mathfrak M\ell_j$,
$s_j\ge12\mathfrak M\ell_j$, and $\mathcal G_j$ is aligned with the level-$j$ big-block grid.
(This is (Thick) of `library/print-inverse.md` Proposition 3.1, imposed at levels $j\ge1$
only.)

**Floors.** $\mathfrak M\ge L$, and $R>12d$ (so $R\mathfrak M\ell_i/d>12\mathfrak M\ell_i$ for
every $i$). Both are lower bounds of the kind the account already allows (CA-I 12680–12683).

**Lemma 3.3 (covering).** Assume (TH) and the floors. Let $c$ be a row of height $j\ge1$ with
an end point in $\Lambda_j$. Then $E_c$ lies in one class cube of index $j-1$ or $j$.

*Proof.* Let $y$ be the end point of $c$ in $\Lambda_j$, and $y'$ the other. Blocks of level $j$
refine the big blocks of levels $\ge j$, so $B^j(y)\subset\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$. In
each coordinate the sites of $E_c$ form a run of extent at most $2\ell_j-\eta$.

*Aligned rounding.* Let $I=[\beta_0,\beta_1)$ be an interval of grid points of spacing $g$ with
$\beta_1-\beta_0\ge12g$, and $[e_0,e_1]\subset I$ a run with $e_1-e_0\le11g$. Then some aligned
interval of length $12g$ inside $I$ contains $[e_0,e_1]$. Indeed, let $\gamma$ be the largest
grid point $\le e_0$. If $\gamma+12g\le\beta_1$ take $[\gamma,\gamma+12g)$; it contains $e_1$
since $e_1\le e_0+11g<\gamma+12g$. Otherwise take $[\beta_1-12g,\beta_1)$: its left end is
$\ge\beta_0$, and it is $\le\gamma\le e_0$ because $\gamma+12g>\beta_1$. With $I=\mathbb Z$-wide
(no constraint) the first choice always works.

*Case A: $B^j(y')\not\subset\Omega_j$.* Then $B^j(y')\cap\Omega_j=\emptyset$. Apply the rounding
in each coordinate with $g=\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$ and no outer constraint; this is possible
because $2\ell_j\le11\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$, that is $2L\le11\mathfrak M$. Call the product cube
$\square$; it has side $12\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$, is a union of level-$(j-1)$ big blocks, and
contains $E_c$. Every site of $\square$ is within $\ell^\infty$-distance $12\mathfrak
M\ell_{j-1}$ of $y\in\Omega_j$, hence in $\Omega_{j-1}$ by the gap and $R>12d$. Every site of
$\square$ is within $12\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}<12\mathfrak M\ell_j$ of a site of $B^j(y')$, which
lies outside $\Omega_j$; the gap at level $j$ keeps it out of $\Omega_{j+1}$. So
$\square\subset\Omega_{j-1}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$, and it meets $\Omega_{j-1}\setminus\Omega_j$ in
$B^j(y')$. It is a class cube of index $j-1$.

*Case B: $B^j(y')\subset\Omega_j$.* Then $E_c\subset\Omega_j$. In each coordinate the run of
$E_c$ meets at most two consecutive cell intervals of $\mathcal G_j$, since its extent is below
$s_j$. $E_c$ is a product of runs, so every cell in the product of those intervals meets
$E_c\subset\Omega_j$. Cells are disjoint and $\Omega_j$ is a union of cells, so each such cell
lies in $\Omega_j$. Their union is a box $B_c\subset\Omega_j$ with sides $s_j$ or $2s_j$,
aligned with the big-block grid. Apply the rounding in each coordinate with
$g=\mathfrak M\ell_j$ and $I$ the side of $B_c$ ($2\ell_j\le11\mathfrak M\ell_j$). The product
$\square$ is a cube of side $12\mathfrak M\ell_j$, a union of level-$j$ big blocks, with
$E_c\subset\square\subset B_c\subset\Omega_j$. It contains $y\notin\Omega_{j+1}$, so it meets
$\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$. Every site of $\square$ is within $12\mathfrak M\ell_j$ of $y$,
and $R\mathfrak M\ell_{j+1}/d>12\mathfrak M\ell_j$, so $\square$ misses $\Omega_{j+2}$. It is a
class cube of index $j$. This case covers interior rows and, if the convention keeps them, inward
rows. ∎

**Lemma 3.4 (one plaquette in one gauge).** Let $\square$ be a class cube of index $q$ with its
gauge $u$ from (S1), and $\sqrt Na_0\le1$. For every plaquette $p$ whose four bonds are bonds of
$\square$,
\[
 \|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le(2\sqrt Na_0+2Na_0^2)(\eta/\ell_q)^2\le4\sqrt Na_0(\eta/\ell_q)^2 .
\]

*Proof.* $U_r(\partial p)=u(x)^{-1}U_r^u(\partial p)u(x)$ with $u(x)$ unitary, so the norm is
unchanged by the gauge. Let $p$ have corner $x$ and plane $(\mu,\nu)$. Put $X_1=i\eta A_\mu(x)$,
$X_4=i\eta A_\nu(x)$, $X_2=X_4+\delta_2$, $X_3=X_1+\delta_3$ with
$\delta_2=i\eta^2\nabla_\mu A_\nu(x)$, $\delta_3=i\eta^2\nabla_\nu A_\mu(x)$. The two first
differences compare parallel bonds of $p$, so (S1) bounds them. Then
$U_r^u(\partial p)=e^{X_1}e^{X_2}e^{-X_3}e^{-X_4}$. All $X_i,\delta_i$ are anti-Hermitian, since
a small logarithm of a unitary matrix is. Duhamel's formula
$e^{Y+\delta}-e^Y=\int_0^1e^{s(Y+\delta)}\delta e^{(1-s)Y}ds$ has unitary outer factors, so
replacing $X_2$ by $X_4$ and then $X_3$ by $X_1$ costs at most
$\|\delta_2\|_F+\|\delta_3\|_F$. The two integral identities quoted in round 1 §3.4 give
$\|e^{X_1}e^{X_4}e^{-X_1}e^{-X_4}-I\|_F\le\|[X_1,X_4]\|_F\le2\|X_1\|_{\rm op}\|X_4\|_F$. With
$\|Y\|_{\rm op}\le\|Y\|_F\le\sqrt N|Y|$ for either account norm: $\|\delta_i\|_F<\sqrt N
a_0\eta^2/\ell_q^2$ and $\|X_1\|_{\rm op}\|X_4\|_F<Na_0^2\eta^2/\ell_q^2$. Add. ∎

**Theorem 3.5.** Under (TH), the floors, (S1) and $\sqrt Na_0\le1$, (D) holds at every row of
height $j\ge1$, with the better constant $4\sqrt NL^2a_0$.

*Proof.* Lemma 3.3 gives a class cube $\square\supset E_c$ of index $q\in\{j-1,j\}$. Every
plaquette $p\subset E_c$ has its four bonds in $\square$. Lemma 3.4 and
$(\eta/\ell_q)^2\le L^2(\eta/\ell_j)^2$ finish. ∎

**Remarks.** (1) (TH) is used at levels $j\ge1$ only; level 0 needs nothing, since rows of
height 0 are exactly linear ($q_c=\eta A(c)$, round 1 §3.1). (2) Lemma 3.3 is a geometric
statement and needs no configuration. So under (TH) the whole carrier, not only each plaquette,
lies in one supplied gauge: round 1's attack one also succeeds there, and gives (11) directly with
$s_c\|A_r\|_F\le L\sqrt Na_0$. (3) Proposition 1 shows (TH) cannot simply be dropped: its island
violates (TH) at level $k$ and nothing else.
