## 1. Objects and conventions

**Units and towers.** Lengths are in level-$k$ units (CA-I 12667–12668): $\ell_j=L^{j-k}$,
$\eta=\ell_0=L^{-k}$, $\ell_k=1$. The lattices are nested and the blocks are centred, since $L$ is
odd (CA-I 790–799). A tower is admissible (CA-I 12712–12724) if each $\Omega_j$ is a union of
level-$j$ big blocks (side $\mathfrak M\ell_j$) and $\ell_j^{-1}\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})>R\mathfrak M$,
$R\ge R_1$. $\Omega_j=T_\eta$ is admitted. With each $\Omega_j$ the account associates the bonds that
have **at least one** end point in $\Omega_j$ (CA-I 12721–12722). Put $\Lambda_j$ = the level-$j$
sites of $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$, and $B^j(y)$ = the level-$j$ block of side $\ell_j$ centred at
$y$. Because the big-block grids are nested, $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$ is a union of blocks
$B^j(y)$, $y\in\Lambda_j$. For a site $x$ put $\lambda(x)=\max\{j:x\in\Omega_j\}$; for a plaquette
$p$ with its corners in $\Omega_0$ put $\lambda(p)=$ the least $\lambda$ of its corners.

**The cube test $\mathfrak S_{12}$.** A class cube of index $q$ is a set of fine sites that is a
union of level-$q$ big blocks, lies in $\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+2}$, and meets
$\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+1}$ (CMP 99b p. 396, as CA-I 12949–12957 and 24824–24843 read it). Write
$\mathfrak S_{12}$ for the pairs that satisfy, with cubes of side exactly $12\mathfrak M\ell_q$,
\[
\text{(S1)}\ U=e^{i\eta\mathfrak a}U_r,\ U_r^u=e^{i\eta A},\ \ell_q|A|<a_0,\ \ell_q^2|\nabla A|<a_0\ \text{on }\square;\quad
\text{(S2)}\ N_{U_r}(\mathfrak a)<a_1;\quad \text{(S3)}\ |J|_{(-3)}<a_\gamma
\]
(CA-I 4.5:eq-admissible-pairs-a). This is the account's explicit side-12 specialisation of (S1). The
sentence at CA-I 12955–12957, that the enlarged cube $\square^5$ "is contained in one of the cubes on
which the condition holds", is a further claimed consequence. It is not part of $\mathfrak S_{12}$, and
Prop. 2.2 refutes it at the island. One account detail differs from the printed paper: CMP 99b p. 408
gives $\tilde\square^n$ side $(2+2n)\mathfrak M\ell_j$, so $\tilde\square^5$ has side **12**, while CA-I 12957
says 11. Neither changes any argument here, but CA-I 12957 is not an exact transcription.

**Bałaban's restricted class.** CMP 99b p. 396 (page image): "For each cube $\square$ of this class
there exists a unique index $j$ … such that $\square\subset B^j(\Lambda_j)\cup B^{j+1}(\Lambda_{j+1})$,
$\square\cap B^j(\Lambda_j)\neq\emptyset$, and $\square$ is a union of several big blocks … its size in
the lattice $T_\eta$ is $O(1)ML^j\eta$. Here $O(1)$ will mean a number $\ge10$." The bound in (3.35) is
$O(1)M\alpha_0(L^j\eta)^{-1}$, "where $O(1)M$ is a size of $\square$". The same page fixes
$\Omega_0$ as the level-0 big blocks within $RM$ of $\Omega_1$; p. 394 also allows "a thinner layer".
Levels $j\ge1$ follow CMP 96 (2.1)–(2.4), imported unchanged at p. 393. These passages state no
thickness condition on $\Omega_j$, $j\ge1$. Two readings of the size number exist:
**(R12)** side exactly 12 big blocks (p. 409, "can be taken as equal to 12"; the account's choice);
**(R≥10)** every side $S\mathfrak M\ell_q$ with $S\ge10$, with bound $S\mathfrak M\alpha_0/\ell_q$. The
restricted class differs from $\mathfrak S_{12}$ by the outer-region rule for $\Omega_0$ **and** by
whichever size reading and radius ($O(1)M\alpha_0$ against $a_0$) one adopts.

**Norms.** $|\cdot|$ is the account's matrix norm: normalised Hilbert–Schmidt with
$\operatorname{tr}\mathbf 1=1$ at CA-I 820, the operator norm elsewhere (CA-I 161778–161795).
$\|\cdot\|_F$ is the unnormalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm. For either account norm,
$\|X\|_{\rm op}\le\|X\|_F\le\sqrt N|X|$.

**Carriers, (D) and (11).** A row $c$ of height $j\ge1$ is a level-$j$ bond with $c_-\in\Lambda_j$;
$E_c=B^j(c_-)\cup B^j(c_+)$ with all fine bonds in it; $s_c=\ell_j$ (round 1 §1). The carrier lemma is
\[
\text{(D)}\qquad \|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le 8\sqrt N L^2a_0\,(\eta/\ell_j)^2\quad\text{for every elementary plaquette }p\subset E_c .
\]
Round 1's complete-carrier small-potential bound is
\[
\text{(11)}\qquad U=e^{i\eta B}\ \text{on }E_c\ \text{in one fixed gauge},\qquad s_c\|B\|_{\infty,F}<\rho ,
\]
with a unitary gauge, or a $G^c$-valued gauge with $\|\operatorname{Ad}^{\pm1}\|\le4$ through round 1's
orbit clause (statement T, last paragraph). Round 1 §3.1 proves: (D) for $U_r$ and the twist bound
$s_c\|\operatorname{Ad}_g\mathfrak a\|_F\le L\sqrt Na_1$ on $E_c$ give (11) with $s_c\|B\|_F<\rho/8$ when
$a_0,a_1\le a_*$. Round 2 re-did its two displays (18)–(19), and the gate accepted that re-check.
The constants $a_*,\rho$ are those of round 1 (2).

**The plaquette clause (PL).** A pair with real factor $U_r$ satisfies (PL) with constant $\alpha$ if
\[
\text{(PL)}\qquad \|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le\sqrt N\,\alpha\,(\eta/\ell_{\lambda(p)})^2\quad\text{for every plaquette $p$ with its corners in }\Omega_0 .
\]
$\mathfrak S^{\rm pl}$ is the set of pairs of $\mathfrak S_{12}$ that satisfy (PL) with $\alpha\le8a_0$.

## 2. The cube test against (D)

### 2.1 The literal finger of print-inverse Prop. 3.2 misses every carrier

`library/print-inverse.md` Prop. 3.2 puts its finger $F$ in $\Omega_0$, farther than
$(R+24)\mathfrak M\eta$ from $\Omega_1$, and changes one bond in its middle.

**Lemma 2.1 (carriers lie in the preceding region).** Let $c$ be a row of height $j\ge1$ and
$R\mathfrak M\ge L$. Then every site of $E_c$ lies in $\Omega_{j-1}$, and within distance $\ell_j$ of
$\Omega_j$ in the $\ell^1$, $\ell^2$ and $\ell^\infty$ metrics alike.

*Proof.* $c_-\in\Lambda_j\subset\Omega_j$, and $\Omega_j$ is a union of level-$j$ blocks, so
$B^j(c_-)\subset\Omega_j$. Every site of $B^j(c_+)$ is $z'+\ell_je_\mu$ with $z'\in B^j(c_-)$, so its
distance to $\Omega_j$ is at most $\ell_j$ in each of the three metrics. If such a site were outside
$\Omega_{j-1}$, then $\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_{j-1}^c,\Omega_j)\le\ell_j\le R\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$,
against the strict gap. ∎

At height $j\ge1$ the lemma gives $E_c\subset\Omega_{j-1}\subset\Omega_1$ within distance $\ell_j$ of
$\Omega_j\subset\Omega_1$. For $j=1$ this distance is $L\eta<(R+24)\mathfrak M\eta$. So no carrier of
height $\ge1$ reaches the finger, $U_r=1$ on every carrier bond at the finger's configuration, and
(D) holds there with left side zero. The finger breaks the level-0 propagator input of
print-inverse, not the averaging of rows of height $\ge1$. (Gate §2.1: ADVANCE.)

### 2.2 The same mechanism one level up: the island

**Proposition 2.2.** Let $d=4$, $G=SU(N)$, $N\ge2$, $L\ge3$ odd, $\mathfrak M\ge L$ an admitted
big-block size (a power of $L$), $R\ge R_1$ and $k\ge2$. Take the torus $T_\eta$ with a period $P$, in
level-$k$ units, that is a multiple of $\mathfrak M$ with $P\ge37\mathfrak M$; the admitted periods
$2L^m$ (CA-I 791), read in level-$k$ units, include such $P$ once $m$ is large. Let $F$ be a cube of
side $11\mathfrak M$ that is a union of $11^4$ level-$k$ big blocks:
$F=\prod_\mu\{f_\mu\le x_\mu<f_\mu+11\mathfrak M\}$ as a set of fine sites. Take the tower
\[
\Omega_0=\dots=\Omega_{k-1}=T_\eta,\qquad \Omega_k=F .
\]
Let $x_0$ be the site with $x_{0,\mu}-f_\mu=\tfrac{11\mathfrak M}2-1$ for every $\mu$. It is a level-$k$
site, because $11\mathfrak M$ is odd and the level-$k$ sites of $F$ sit at $f_\mu+\tfrac12+\mathbb Z$.
Put $b_0=(x_0,x_0+\eta e_1)$ and
\[
V=\exp(i\pi\,\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,0,\dots,0))=\mathrm{diag}(-1,-1,1,\dots,1)\in SU(N).
\]
Let $U_r(b_0)=V$, $U_r=1$ on every other bond, $\mathfrak a=0$, $J=0$, $U=U_r$. Then:

(a) the tower is admissible (CA-I 12712–12724), and $\Omega_0=\Omega_1=T_\eta$ obeys Bałaban's
outer-region rule of CMP 99b p. 396;

(b) $(U,J)\in\mathfrak S_{12}$ for every $a_0,a_1,a_\gamma>0$;

(c) $c_0=(x_0,x_0+e_1)$ is a row of height $k$, and the plaquette $p_0$ with corners
$x_0,\ x_0+\eta e_1,\ x_0+\eta e_1+\eta e_2,\ x_0+\eta e_2$ lies in $E_{c_0}$, with
$\|U_r(\partial p_0)-I\|_F=2\sqrt2$, $|U(\partial p_0)-1|_{\rm op}=2$, and $|U(\partial p_0)-1|=2\sqrt{2/N}$
in the normalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm;

(d) (D) fails at $(c_0,p_0)$ whenever $8\sqrt Na_0<2\sqrt2$. The display 4.5:eq-admissible-pairs-1
fails at level $k$ whenever $a_0+a_1<\tfrac12$ in the operator norm, and whenever
$a_0+a_1<\sqrt{2/N}/2=1/\sqrt{2N}$ in the normalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Round 1's
$a_*=\rho/(16\sqrt NK_*)$ is far below all three thresholds;

(e) the literal small-field formula of round 1 (5)–(6) for $q_{c_0}=Q_k(U,\eta A,c_0)$, and the formula
CMP 98 (124) for its linearisation, cannot be used at this pair: one logarithm in each has argument
$V^{-1}$, outside the identity chart. The globally extended real map of CA-I 788–858 remains defined
at this pair; this argument supplies no analytic derivative for that extension.

*Proof.* (a) $T_\eta$ is a union of level-$j$ big blocks for every $j\le k$, because $P$ is a multiple of
$\mathfrak M=\mathfrak M\ell_k$ and hence of $\mathfrak M\ell_j=\mathfrak M L^{j-k}$. $F$ is a union of
level-$k$ big blocks by choice. For $j\le k-1$, $\Omega_j^c=\emptyset$, so the gap holds with distance
$+\infty$; there is no gap condition past $k$ ($\Omega_{k+1}=\emptyset$). CA-I 12723–12724 admits
$\Omega_j=T_\eta$. Since $\Omega_1=T_\eta$, every level-0 big block is at distance $0$ from $\Omega_1$, so
p. 396's rule gives $\Omega_0=T_\eta$.

(b) (S2) and (S3) hold because $\mathfrak a=0$ and $J=0$. For (S1) we show that **neither end point**
of $b_0$ lies in any class cube. Then $b_0$ is a bond of no class cube, under either bond convention
(both end points, or at least one, CA-I 12721–12722), and no first difference read on a class cube
involves $b_0$. On every class cube $U_r=1$, and $u=1$, $A=0$ satisfy the strict bounds.

- Index $q\le k-2$: $\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+1}=T_\eta\setminus T_\eta=\emptyset$, so no class cube has
  index $q$.
- Index $k$: a class cube lies in $\Omega_k\setminus\Omega_{k+2}=F$ and has $12\mathfrak M/\eta$ sites in
  each coordinate run; $F$ has $11\mathfrak M/\eta$. None exists.
- Index $k-1$: a class cube $\square$ has side $12\mathfrak M\ell_{k-1}=12\mathfrak M/L\le4\mathfrak M$,
  and it meets $\Omega_{k-1}\setminus\Omega_k=T_\eta\setminus F$. Let $x$ be an end point of $b_0$. Then
  $x_\mu-f_\mu\in\{\tfrac{11\mathfrak M}2-1,\ \tfrac{11\mathfrak M}2-1+\eta\}$ for every $\mu$. Every site

  $f_\mu+1.5\mathfrak M-1\le z'_\mu\le f_\mu+9.5\mathfrak M-1+\eta$, so $z'\in F$; the bound

  within $12\mathfrak M/L-\eta<4\mathfrak M$ of $x$ in each coordinate, so $\square\subset F$, against
  $\square\not\subset F$. Hence $x\notin\square$.

(c) $x_0\in F=\Omega_k\setminus\Omega_{k+1}$ is a level-$k$ site, so $x_0\in\Lambda_k$. The site
$x_0+e_1$ has $x_1-f_1=\tfrac{11\mathfrak M}2<11\mathfrak M$, so it lies in $F$, and $c_0$ is an interior

(d) The right side of (D) at height $k$ is $8\sqrt NL^2a_0\eta^2=8\sqrt NL^{2-2k}a_0\le8\sqrt Na_0$.
The right side of the display at level $k$ is $4(a_0+a_1)L^{-2k}\le4(a_0+a_1)$. Compare with (c).

site, it is a level-$m$ site for every $m$, and $c^{(m)}$ is the first coarse bond of the straight path

$\mathcal M(W_{k-1})(c_0)$, whose reference transport starts with
$W_{k-1}(c^{(k-1)})=\mathcal M(W_{k-2})(c^{(k-1)})$. Descending, it needs $\mathcal M(U)(c^{(1)})$, with
$W_0=U$. By round 1 (5),
$\mathcal M(U)(c^{(1)})=\exp\big(\sum_{x\in B(x_0)}L^{-d}\log\{P_U(\Gamma_{c^{(1)},x})T^{-1}\}\big)T$, with
$T=P_U(c^{(1)})$ the product along the straight fine path, whose first bond is $b_0$; so $T=V$. Take

### 2.3 Locality

The proof of (b) reads the tower only within coordinate distance $12\mathfrak M\ell_k$ of $x_0$. So the
island works inside any admissible tower, at any level $1\le j\le k$, on the same admitted grids: put
$F$ (side $11\mathfrak M\ell_j$, a union of level-$j$ big blocks) into $\Omega_j$ at a place where
$\Omega_{j-1}$ contains the $(R+12)\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$-neighbourhood of $F$ and the rest of $\Omega_j$
is farther than $24\mathfrak M\ell_j$ from $F$, and scale every length by $\ell_j$. For $R\ge6$ the gap
keeps $\Omega_{j+1}$ out of $F$. Cubes of index $\le j-2$ lie in $\Omega_q\setminus\Omega_{q+2}$, which
misses $\Omega_j\ni x_0$. Cubes of index $j$ near $x_0$ would have to lie in $F$. Cubes of index $j-1$
must contain a site outside $\Omega_j$, hence outside $F$ near $x_0$. (Gate §2.6: ADVANCE. Round 2's
sentence "a finger works the same way" is dropped; it is not needed.)

### 3.3 Local gauge frames give the plaquette clause

**Lemma 3.3 (one plaquette in one gauge).** Let $D$ be a set of fine sites with a $G$-valued gauge $u$
on $D$ such that $U_r^u=e^{i\eta A}$ on the bonds of $D$, with $\ell_D|A|\le r$ and
$\ell_D^2|\nabla A|\le r$ there, and $\sqrt Nr\le1$. For every plaquette $p$ whose four bonds are bonds
of $D$,
\[
\|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le(2\sqrt Nr+2Nr^2)(\eta/\ell_D)^2\le4\sqrt Nr\,(\eta/\ell_D)^2 .
\]

*Proof.* $U_r(\partial p)=u(x)^{-1}U_r^u(\partial p)u(x)$ with $u(x)$ unitary, so the norm does not change.
Let $p$ have corner $x$ and plane $(\mu,\nu)$. Put $X_1=i\eta A_\mu(x)$, $X_4=i\eta A_\nu(x)$,
$X_2=X_4+\delta_2$, $X_3=X_1+\delta_3$, with $\delta_2=i\eta^2\nabla_\mu A_\nu(x)$ and
$\delta_3=i\eta^2\nabla_\nu A_\mu(x)$. These first differences compare parallel bonds of $p$. Then
$U_r^u(\partial p)=e^{X_1}e^{X_2}e^{-X_3}e^{-X_4}$. All $X_i$, $\delta_i$ are anti-Hermitian. Duhamel's
formula $e^{Y+\delta}-e^Y=\int_0^1e^{s(Y+\delta)}\delta e^{(1-s)Y}ds$ has unitary outer factors, so
replacing $X_2$ by $X_4$ and then $X_3$ by $X_1$ costs at most $\|\delta_2\|_F+\|\delta_3\|_F$. The
identities $[e^X,e^Y]=\int_0^1e^{(1-t)X}[X,e^Y]e^{tX}dt$ and
$[X,e^Y]=\int_0^1e^{(1-u)Y}[X,Y]e^{uY}du$ give
$\|e^{X_1}e^{X_4}e^{-X_1}e^{-X_4}-I\|_F\le\|[X_1,X_4]\|_F\le2\|X_1\|_{\rm op}\|X_4\|_F$. With
$\|Y\|_{\rm op}\le\|Y\|_F\le\sqrt N|Y|$: $\|\delta_i\|_F\le\sqrt Nr\eta^2/\ell_D^2$ and
$\|X_1\|_{\rm op}\|X_4\|_F\le Nr^2\eta^2/\ell_D^2$. Add. ∎

(Round 2 Lemma 3.4; gate ADVANCE.) Here $\nabla$ is the ordinary lattice difference, as in CMP 99b
(3.35), where the covariant one is written $\nabla_U$. If a source bounds the covariant difference
instead, the ordinary one exceeds it by at most $2.2\|A_\mu\|_{\rm op}\|A_\nu\|_F\le2.2Nr^2/\ell_D^2$, and
the same proof gives the constant $9$ in place of $4$ when $\sqrt Nr\le1$.

**Lemma 3.4 (frames that cover every plaquette).** Fix $s\in\{0,1\}$. Suppose every plaquette $p$ with
its corners in $\Omega_0$ has its four bonds in some set $D$ as in Lemma 3.3, with
$\ell_D\ge\ell_{\lambda(p)-s}$. Then (PL) holds with $\alpha=4L^{2s}r$.

*Proof.* Lemma 3.3 gives $\|U_r(\partial p)-I\|_F\le4\sqrt Nr(\eta/\ell_D)^2\le\sqrt N\,4L^{2s}r\,(\eta/\ell_{\lambda(p)})^2$. ∎

*Application: Bałaban's blocks $\tilde\Delta(y)$.* CMP 99b p. 397 (page image): "if $y\in\Lambda_j$, then
$\Delta(y)=B^j(y)$, and $\tilde\Delta(y)$ is a cube of the size $2L^j\eta$ on the lattice $T_\eta$, with
center at the point $y$." Read $\tilde\Delta(y)=\{z:|z-y|_\infty\le\ell_j\}$. For a plaquette $p$, choose a
corner $x$ with $\lambda(x)=\lambda(p)=:j$. Then $x\in\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$, which is a union
of blocks $B^j(y)$, $y\in\Lambda_j$; take $y$ with $x\in B^j(y)$. The corners of $p$ lie within
$(\ell_j-\eta)/2+\eta\le\ell_j$ of $y$ in each coordinate, so $p\subset\tilde\Delta(y)$. Hence a family of
gauges on all $\tilde\Delta(y)$, $y\in\mathfrak B=\bigcup_j\Lambda_j$, with $\ell_y|A|\le r$ and
$\ell_y^2|\nabla A|\le r$ (or with the pointwise weight, which is $\ge\ell_{\lambda(p)}$ at the corners of
$p$), satisfies Lemma 3.4 with $s=0$, so (PL) holds with $\alpha=4r$. For $j\ge1$ any reading of
"size $2L^j\eta$" with half-width at least $\ell_j-\eta$ works, since $(\ell_j+\eta)/2\le\ell_j-\eta$ when
$L^j\ge3$; at $j=0$ the closed reading is used.

### 3.5 Thick towers: the closest true statement on a cube-test class

**Definition (TH).** For each $j\ge1$, $\Omega_j$ is a union of cells of a grid $\mathcal G_j$ whose cells
are cubes of side $s_j$; $s_j$ is a multiple of $\mathfrak M\ell_j$, $s_j\ge12\mathfrak M\ell_j$, and
$\mathcal G_j$ is aligned with the admitted level-$j$ big-block grid of the torus (or with its local
lift). **Floors:** $\mathfrak M\ge L$ and $R>12d$, both lower bounds of the kind CA-I 12680–12683 allows.

**Lemma 3.6 (covering).** Assume (TH) and the floors. Let $c$ be a row of height $j\ge1$ with an end
point in $\Lambda_j$. Then $E_c$ lies in one class cube of side $12\mathfrak M\ell_q$, of index $j-1$ or $j$.

*Proof.* Let $y$ be the end point of $c$ in $\Lambda_j$, and $y'$ the other. Level-$j$ blocks refine the
big blocks of levels $\ge j$, so $B^j(y)\subset\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$. In each coordinate the sites
of $E_c$ form a run of extent at most $2\ell_j-\eta$.

*Aligned rounding.* Let $I=[\beta_0,\beta_1)$ be an interval of grid points of spacing $g$ with
$\beta_1-\beta_0\ge12g$, and $[e_0,e_1]\subset I$ a run with $e_1-e_0\le11g$. Some aligned interval of
length $12g$ inside $I$ contains $[e_0,e_1]$. Let $\gamma$ be the largest grid point $\le e_0$. If
$\gamma+12g\le\beta_1$, take $[\gamma,\gamma+12g)$; it contains $e_1\le e_0+11g<\gamma+12g$. Otherwise
take $[\beta_1-12g,\beta_1)$: its left end is $\ge\beta_0$, and it is $\le\gamma\le e_0$ because
$\gamma+12g>\beta_1$. Without an outer interval the first choice always works.

*Case A: $B^j(y')\not\subset\Omega_j$.* Then $B^j(y')\cap\Omega_j=\emptyset$. Round in each coordinate
with $g=\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$ and no outer constraint; this is possible because $2\ell_j\le11\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$,
that is $2L\le11\mathfrak M$. The product cube $\square$ has side $12\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$, is a union of
level-$(j-1)$ big blocks, and contains $E_c$. Every site of $\square$ is within $\ell^\infty$-distance
$12\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}$ of $y\in\Omega_j$, hence in $\Omega_{j-1}$ by the gap and $R>12d$. Every site of
$\square$ is within $12\mathfrak M\ell_{j-1}<12\mathfrak M\ell_j$ of a site of $B^j(y')$, which lies outside
$\Omega_j$; the gap at level $j$ keeps it out of $\Omega_{j+1}$. So
$\square\subset\Omega_{j-1}\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$, and it meets $\Omega_{j-1}\setminus\Omega_j$ in
$B^j(y')$: a class cube of index $j-1$.

*Case B: $B^j(y')\subset\Omega_j$.* Then $E_c\subset\Omega_j$. In each coordinate the run of $E_c$ meets
at most two consecutive cell intervals of $\mathcal G_j$, since its extent is below $s_j$. $E_c$ is a
product of runs, so every cell in the product of those intervals meets $E_c\subset\Omega_j$; cells are
disjoint and $\Omega_j$ is a union of cells, so each such cell lies in $\Omega_j$. Their union is a box
$B_c\subset\Omega_j$ with sides $s_j$ or $2s_j$, aligned with the big-block grid. Round in each
coordinate with $g=\mathfrak M\ell_j$ and $I$ the side of $B_c$. The product $\square$ is a cube of side
$12\mathfrak M\ell_j$, a union of level-$j$ big blocks, with $E_c\subset\square\subset B_c\subset\Omega_j$. It
contains $y\notin\Omega_{j+1}$, so it meets $\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}$. Every site of $\square$ is
within $12\mathfrak M\ell_j$ of $y$, and $R\mathfrak M\ell_{j+1}/d>12\mathfrak M\ell_j$, so $\square$ misses
$\Omega_{j+2}$: a class cube of index $j$. ∎

**Theorem 3.7.** Under (TH), the floors, (S1) on $\mathfrak S_{12}$ and $\sqrt Na_0\le1$, (D) holds at every
row of height $j\ge1$, with the better constant $4\sqrt NL^2a_0$.

*Proof.* Lemma 3.6 gives a class cube $\square\supset E_c$ of index $q\in\{j-1,j\}$, with its (S1) gauge.
Every plaquette $p\subset E_c$ has its four bonds in $\square$. Lemma 3.3 with $\ell_D=\ell_q$, $r=a_0$, and
$(\eta/\ell_q)^2\le L^2(\eta/\ell_j)^2$ finish. ∎

**Remarks.** (1) (TH) is used at levels $j\ge1$ only; rows of height 0 are linear ($q_c=\eta A(c)$,
round 1 §3.1). (2) Lemma 3.6 is geometric. Under (TH) the whole carrier lies in one supplied gauge, so
round 1's first attack also succeeds and gives (11) directly with $s_c\|A_r\|_F\le L\sqrt Na_0$.
(3) The island of Prop. 2.2 shows that the implication cannot be extended to all merely gapped towers.
(TH) is a sufficient condition; it is not proved necessary for an individual tower. (Gate §3.4–3.5:
ADVANCE, with remark (3) revised as asked.)
