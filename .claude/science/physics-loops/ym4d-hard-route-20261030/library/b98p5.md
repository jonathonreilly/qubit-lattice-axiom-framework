### 2.1 The map, its subtraction, and its complete read

CA-I 24323–24332 says:

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal M(W)(c)

       \log[P_W(\Gamma_{c,x})P_W(c)^{-1}]\right)P_W(c),\\

       \log[P_{e^{ia}W}(\Gamma_{y,x})P_W(\Gamma_{y,x})^{-1}]\right),\\

 &=\frac1i\log\left[m_W(a;c_-)^{-1}\mathcal M(e^{ia}W)(c)

 \tag{5}
\end{aligned}
\]
Here $c$ inside $P_W(c)$ means the straight fine path.
Reverse an edge by inverting its whole bond matrix. Do not reverse its two factors separately in the same order.

These are `b98.md` (5), (11)–(13), and CA-I 24261–24342, with the gauge factors written explicitly.

\[
 W_m=\mathcal M^m(U),\quad a_0=\eta A,\quad a_{m+1}=F_{W_m}(a_m),\quad q_c=a_j(c).
 \tag{6}
\]
The identity follows directly from (5):
$\mathcal M(e^{ia}W)=(e^{iF_W(a)}\mathcal M(W))^{m_W(a)}$.
The gauge convention gives $(V^u)^v=V^{vu}$. It therefore fixes the order of every accumulated factor.
Subtracting the derivative of this same recursion at $A=0$ gives (1).
There is no replacement by the plain logarithmic average.

### 2.2 Fixed one-step bounds

SA 8129 says:

> “This establishes the domain before any derivative estimate.”

We follow that order for (5). Use the unnormalised Hilbert–Schmidt norm $\|\cdot\|_F$ in this subsection.
It is submultiplicative. Multiplication by a unitary matrix is isometric.
For $t=\sum_i\|X_i\|_F\le1/4$, expansion by total degree gives
\[
 \left\|\prod_i e^{X_i}-I\right\|_F\le e^t-1\le2t,
 \qquad \|\log(I+H)\|_F\le2\|H\|_F\quad(\|H\|_F\le1/2).
 \tag{7}
\]
The first inequality sums the multinomial series. The second sums
$\sum_{n\ge1}\|H\|_F^n/n$.
These proofs also give uniform convergence of every differentiated series on smaller balls.

On $\|b\|_\infty,\|a\|_\infty<8r$, put $W=e^{ib}$.
There are at most $p$ edges in each path or closed comparison used in (5).
A perturbed edge contributes two exponentials. Apply (7) to their ordered products, then to each logarithm.
Convex weights have sum one. Applying (7) once more to the outer products gives
\[
 \|F_{e^{ib}}(a)\|_\infty<2^{16}pr<1,
 \qquad \|\log m_{e^{ib}}(a)\|_\infty<2^{16}pr<1.
 \tag{8}
\]
For example, a relative path has total exponential length at most $32pr$.
Its logarithm has norm at most $128pr$.
An anchored average has logarithm norm at most $576pr$.
The two site logarithms and the two averaged-field logarithms total at most $1408pr$.
The last product and logarithm cost at most four times that number, below $2^{16}pr$.
All intermediate arguments stay within $1/2$ of the identity.

At $b=a=0$, differentiate the path products.
The two internal path averages cancel against the two derivatives of $m$ in (5).
The remaining operator is
\[
 (S_Lh)(c)=L^{-d}\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}\sum_{b\in[x,x']}h(b).
 \tag{9}
\]
Its row sum is $L$. Its column sum is $L^{1-d}$.
For the column count, fix a parallel fine bond. Over all coarse rows, it occurs at the $L$
possible offsets within a segment of length $L$, each with weight $L^{-d}$.
Nonparallel components contribute zero. Boundary deletion only removes terms.

Apply two scalar Cauchy circles of radius $r$ inside (8).
Each second directional derivative is at most $2r^{-2}$ on the joint $2r$ ball.
Each one-step row has fewer than $n_*$ input bonds.
Each input bond affects fewer than $n_*$ output rows.
Integrate the second derivative from $(0,0)$ to $(b,a)$, then sum these finite entry bounds.
With $\delta=\|b\|_\infty+\|a\|_\infty$, (2) dominates both counts and gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \|D_aF_{e^{ib}}(a)\|_{\rm row}&\le L(1+B_*\delta),\\
 \|D_aF_{e^{ib}}(a)\|_{\rm col}&\le L^{1-d}(1+B_*\delta),\\
 \|\log m_{e^{ib}}(a)\|_\infty&\le B_*\|a\|_\infty.
 \tag{10}
\end{aligned}
\]
The last line integrates the first derivative and uses $m_W(0)=I$.
This supplies the one-step constants in the existing P15 argument without a coordinate count depending on depth.

### 3.1 Why this one lemma would suffice

`b98.md` 302–316 uses the identity

> $(V_1V_0)(\gamma)V_0(\gamma)^{-1}
> =\prod_{b\in\gamma}R(V_0(\gamma_{y,b_-}))V_1(b)$.

The same ordered-product cancellation constructs a coordinate-tree gauge on the rectangular graph $E_c$.
Choose its lowest corner as root. Transport successively in the fixed coordinate order.
Sliding a bond to that tree uses at most $2d\,s_c/\eta$ elementary plaquettes of $E_c$.
Each slide replaces two adjacent coordinate edges by the opposite two edges of one plaquette.
The remaining factors are unitary transports.
The exact identity
\[
 \prod_{h=1}^m V_h-I
  =\sum_{h=1}^m V_1\cdots V_{h-1}(V_h-I)
\]
has unitary factors when applied to these transported plaquettes.
