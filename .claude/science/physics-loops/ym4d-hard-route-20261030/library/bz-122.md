## 1. The native localization is explicit

In 122a, p. 180, (1.19), Balaban defines

\[
 \mathbb B_k^{(n)}(Z)
 =\mathbb B_k^{(n)}\cup\mathbb B_k(Z).
 \tag{1}
\]

Here the union sign denotes the joining operation of **CMP 119 (2.14)**.
It is not ordinary union. Paper III in both bibliographies is CMP 119,
*Convergent renormalization expansions for lattice gauge theories*, 243–285.
The citation to “CMP 122b (2.14)” in I 131725 points to the wrong paper.
122b itself refers to “(2.14) [III]”, for example on pp. 361 and 370.

The parent in (1) is the stage determining set of 122a (1.14)–(1.17).
Equation (1.19) replaces its exterior part
\(\mathbb B_k\cap(\Omega_k\cup Z^c)\)
by \(\mathbb B_k(Z)\cap\Omega_k\).
Equation (1.20) prescribes the localized data from
\(\mathcal M'(Q_k^{s*}V_k)\) on \(Z\cap\Omega_k\), and from
\(V\) on \(Z\cap\Omega_k^c\).
Both domains have been checked against the PDF, p. 180.
In particular, the first domain is not \(Z^c\cap\Omega_k\).

The layer used in item 10 is precisely the layer discussed on p. 188.
After (1.55), a component fails the fluctuation test and leaves the preparation.
Equations (1.56)–(1.58) replace its background-dependent restriction by a localized one.
The forcing in (1.56) lies in an internal boundary layer of width \(2M_1\).
Equation (1.58) calls the resulting function \(\mathbb H_Z^{(j)}\).
Balaban states its analyticity in \(U_k^{(n+1)}\restriction Z\).
This is the exact real-point identification used at I 131740 and 137559–137565.

## 2. The literal cut and its inward collar

Work on one component \(X=X_{\mathfrak a}\) of the class \(Z\).
Balaban decomposes that class componentwise in 122a, p. 177, (1.1).
At the exit of (1.55), the parent is
\(\mathbb B_{\rm par}=\mathbb B_k^{(n+1)}\).
Its localization is (1), restricted to this component.
Write \(\Join\) for Balaban's joining sign in the following displays.
The literal triple is

\[
 (Z_{\rm cut},C,\mathbb B_{\rm par})
 =\bigl(X,X^{\sim-2},\mathbb B_k^{(n+1)}\bigr),
 \tag{2}
\]

where \(\sim\) uses \(M_1\ell_k\)-cubes and \(\ell_m=L^m\eta\).
Thus the core removes two layers **inside** \(X\).
It does not replace the outer cut by that core.

The underlying definition is Balaban, CMP **119** (1988), pp. 256–257,
(2.13)–(2.15). I checked the supplied original PDF as well as its text.
It starts with \(D_0=X\) and chooses the maximal tower

\[
 D_0\supset D_1\supset\cdots\supset D_k,
 \qquad d_\infty(D_m,D_{m-1}^c)\ge M_1\ell_m.
 \tag{3}
\]

The minimal partition \(\mathbf B_k(X)\) has grade \(m\) on
\(D_m\setminus D_{m+1}\), with \(D_{k+1}=\varnothing\).
The source's inclusion \(X^{\sim-2}\subset D_k\) is rebuilt in
Sussman–Alpoge I 147765–147805 and already used in `bz-geom.md`.
Use that result; it is not a new proof here.

The joining definition gives the exact partition identity

\[
 \mathbb B_k^{(n+1)}(X)
 = (\mathbb B_{\rm par}\restriction C)
   \cup\bigl(\mathbf B_k(X)\restriction(X\setminus C)\bigr).
 \tag{4}
\]

This is CMP 119 (2.14), instantiated by 122a (1.19).
Its single-stratum proviso holds at this outer cut for the original preparation.
The boundary of the class component is a boundary of \(\Lambda_k\).
CMP 119, p. 256, places it in \(\Omega_k\) with a \(2MR_k\) collar.
The factor \(\mathbb B_k\cap(\Omega_k\cup Z^c)\) remains unchanged
through 122a (1.14)–(1.17).
Hence the parent has grade \(k\) in the outer \(2M_1\ell_k\) band.
The stated scale ordering \(M>L^2M_1\), \(R_k\ge1\), supplies the margin.
The core boundary follows compatible cube faces; restrictions in (4) retain whole blocks.

There is a useful precise gap calculation.
Let \(P_m\) denote the parent tower, and \(T_m\) the tower of (4).
On \(C\), the minimal tower has grade \(k\).
On \(X\setminus C\), the parent has grade \(k\).
The grade of (4) therefore equals the smaller of the two grades:

\[
 T_m=P_m\cap D_m\quad(0\le m\le k),\qquad T_0=X.
 \tag{5}
\]

This makes the interface agreement in (4) explicit.
Both towers use compatible grids. Their intersection is a union of
\(M_1\ell_m\)-cubes, and of the finer PI big blocks when those grids are matched.
Put \(g_m=d_\infty(P_{m+1},P_m^c)\). De Morgan's identity gives

\[
 d_\infty(T_{m+1},T_m^c)
 \ge\min\{g_m,M_1\ell_{m+1}\}.
 \tag{6}
\]

Indeed \(T_m^c=P_m^c\cup D_m^c\), and
\(T_{m+1}\subset P_{m+1}\cap D_{m+1}\).
Taking distance to either complement proves (6).
The original parent gaps are inputs from the admissible preparation sequence
specified in 122a, p. 179, (1.10)–(1.13), and its stages (1.15)–(1.16).
Equation (6) gives the exact gap transfer; it does not prove parent admission.

For PI's numerical margins, (6) suffices whenever both entries of its minimum
exceed \(R_{\rm PI}M_b\ell_m\).
For \(k\ge1\), the first gap satisfies

\[
 d_\infty(T_1,X^c)\ge M_1L\eta.
 \tag{7}
\]

Thus PI's bare-patch margin follows if \(M_1>2(r_N+2)\).
These comparisons state the needed constant matching explicitly.
They do not change a previously fixed block size or claim a missing floor was proved.
Even granting all these margins leaves the obstruction below.

**Balaban already has an inward buffer.** The core and the places where the
response is evaluated are separated from the outer cut.
The outer edge of this localized partition is nevertheless bare, not grade \(k\).
This confirms `bz-geom.md`'s correction to finding 15 of `c0-sup-check.md`.
A non-bare version of Proposition 4.1 would address a different geometry.

## 3. The requested exterior-read inclusion fails for this cut

**Result: stop (b) for the literal BZ-CUT interface.**
The partition just identified has outer region \(X_{\mathfrak a}\) itself.
It cannot satisfy the requested inclusion of PI's complete touching-plaquette
read in \(X_{\mathfrak a}\).
This refutes that proposed interface, not Balaban's localized layer theorem.
It does not refute the account's item 10 either.

Here is the counterexample in coordinates.
Choose a nonwrapping rectangular class component \(X\), with a face normal to \(e_1\).
These are the original component geometries in 122a, pp. 177 and 179.
Take \(x\) on its last fine-site plane in direction \(e_1\), away from other faces.
Then \(x,x+\eta e_2\in X\), but
\(x+\eta e_1,x+\eta e_1+\eta e_2\notin X\).
The PI field bond and a touching plaquette are

\[
 b=[x,x+\eta e_2],\qquad
 p=[x,x+\eta e_1,x+\eta e_1+\eta e_2,x+\eta e_2].
\]

Their opposite edge is

\[
 b_+=[x+\eta e_1,x+\eta e_1+\eta e_2].
 \tag{8}
\]

The whole-action convention requires every edge of \(p\).
Consequently \(b_+\in E\), while neither endpoint of \(b_+\) belongs to
\(X=X_{\mathfrak a}\). Hence

\[
 E\not\subset X_{\mathfrak a}.
 \tag{9}
\]

This is the outward-edge witness of `c0-sup.md`, section 5, and
`leaf-bz.md`, section 1. Its Hessian calculation is not repeated.
The new fact is (2)–(5): the unbuffered **outer** cut is now the printed
localized construction, rather than an unidentified candidate.
The argument applies to every such proper component at a literal birth call.
It does not depend on the field, test outcome, radii, or the size of the inner gaps.
The whole-torus exception has no boundary and does not cover these components.

No hypothetical bad field is being promoted to a reached-history example.
For any given call with this proper component, (9) is a fact about its sets.
The event that produces the call cannot change (4) or the location of \(b_+\).
Later source maps cannot repair a failure already present at this birth call.
If a different outer domain is intended for an implementation of item 10,
an exact identification with that domain is an additional assertion.
It is not Balaban's printed (1.19).
