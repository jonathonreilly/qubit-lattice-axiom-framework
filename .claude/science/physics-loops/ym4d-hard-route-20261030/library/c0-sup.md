## 3. The exact exterior read

Put `X = Omega_0`. Write `X_eta` for its closed sup-distance `eta` enlargement.
Let `F(X)` be the fine bonds with at least one endpoint in `X`.
Let `P(X)` be the plaquettes containing a bond of `F(X)`.
The coefficient read is

\[
 \mathcal R_X=F(X)\ \cup\!\bigcup_{p\in P(X)}\partial p
                  \ \cup\!\bigcup_{c:\,j(c)\ge1}\mathcal D(c),       \tag{1}
\]

where `D(c)` denotes the full double-block read of a positive-level row.
Include the analogous site-row pillars. They lie in those interior carriers.
All sets in (1) are bond sets, including their endpoints.

**Proposition 3.1.** Every bond of `R_X` is carried by `X_eta`.
The same holds for every local model in `out-pi.md`, section 5.2.
Two backgrounds agreeing on these bonds give identical PI matrices and local matrices.

**Proof.** A plaquette through a field bond has a vertex in `X`.
Each other vertex differs from that vertex by at most `eta` in each coordinate.
Thus every edge of that plaquette lies in `X_eta`.

The matrix `D_U` reads its field bond only.
Transposition and multiplication of finite matrices introduce no new background variables.
Level-zero rows are identities.
Positive-level rows read their double blocks, as proved in `out-pi.md`, sections 4–5.
The gaps place these carriers inside `X`.
This last fact is the existing PI carrier inclusion, not a new row convention.
It follows that `D`, the Hessian, both rows, and all blocks of `M_r` read (1).

A model has outer region `X_tau = X intersect K_0`.
Hence `F(X_tau)` is a subset of `F(X)`.
Its plaquettes lie in `P(X)`.
The model's positive-level carriers stay inside `X` by the same gap argument.
Artificial model boundaries therefore do not enlarge the exterior read.
The model read also lies inside its supplied PI patch
(`out-pi.md:800–834`). This proves every assertion. ∎

Consequently every bond beyond `X_eta` is absent from the PI coefficient matrices.
It is absent from their inverses wherever those inverses exist.
It is also absent from each walk term, since local inversion and assembly introduce no new field reads.
This verifies the local-model qualification left open in the PI-ADMIT check.

There is a minor norm issue. PI also uses differences of zero-extended variables.
It does not create a second exterior coefficient requirement.

**Lemma 3.2.** PI's real-reference norms do not depend on reference links outside `X_eta`.

**Proof.** A nonzero field component has its initial point within `eta` of `X`.
A first difference with two nonzero components uses a link carried by `X_eta`.
With only one nonzero component, its norm is unchanged by unitary transport.
This treats the field gradient. It also treats the first scalar difference.

Expand a second scalar difference into its paths of length at most two.
Scalar variables vanish outside `X`.
If a contributing path uses a link outside `X_eta`, its initial site lies outside `X_eta`.
Every site one step from that initial site is outside `X`.
Only the final, two-step scalar value can then be nonzero.
The expression is that one value, times `eta^-2`, with unitary transport.
Its norm is independent of that transport.
Repeated-direction differences have the same property; their intermediate value vanishes.
All remaining norm terms have no such link dependence.
The physical weights are fixed by the geometry. This proves the lemma. ∎

## 4. A sufficient reduced boundary supplier

The first gap makes each model whose patch crosses `partial X` entirely bare.
This is the existing gap argument in `out-pi.md:763–798,1630`.
Retain all interior admission hypotheses and all other consumer premises.
Here interior hypotheses mean those on patches contained in `X`.
Crossing-patch stencils are instead handled by the filling below.
Set

\[
 \epsilon_r=\tfrac14L^2a_0^*,\qquad
 \epsilon_c=\tfrac14a_1^*.                                      \tag{2}
\]

Call the following **C0-ETA**. It is a reduced supplier within C0-SUP, not a new registry task.
For each reached whole-action PI entry, use its actual pair `U = exp(i eta a) U_r`.
Use the full prescribed parameter and switch domain, including the enlarged discs.
For every crossing PI patch, require one parameter-independent unitary gauge `u` with

\[
 U_r^u(b)=e^{i\eta A(b)},\qquad
 \eta|A(b)|\le\epsilon_r,
 \qquad \eta|a(b)|<\epsilon_c                                  \tag{3}
\]

on all bonds carried by `N(x) intersect X_eta`.
For exterior tiles use the interior anchor of PI, section 5.2.
The reference and twist on overlapping actual reads must be the same pair.
No bound is required on the unused second exterior collar.
The constants in (2) are sufficient margins. No weakest-radius claim is made.
This reduces the required domain and pays for the filling with fixed smallness margins.
It is not an equivalence with C0-SUP at the original maximal radii.

**Proposition 4.1.** C0-ETA suffices for the boundary part of PI and its PI-based consumers.
Interior admission and each consumer's additional hypotheses are retained.
No prescribed parameter domain is reduced.

**Proof.** Work in the single gauge of a boundary model.
Retain `A` and `a^u` on `X_eta`. Set both potentials to zero on unused links.
This filling is local to the estimate. It does not replace an actual coefficient.
Proposition 3.1 proves that every model coefficient remains identical.
The patch contains every required model read.

The model is bare. For every neighbouring pair in the filling,

\[
 \eta^2|\nabla A|\le2\epsilon_r,
 \qquad
 \eta^2|\nabla_{e^{i\eta A}}a^u|<2\epsilon_c .                    \tag{4}
\]

The first inequality is the triangle inequality.
The second uses unitary reference transport and the same triangle inequality.
They also hold at a join to zero.
Thus the filled model satisfies the original comparison bounds with
`L^2 a_0 = 2 epsilon_r` and `a_1 = 2 epsilon_c`.
Both are below the existing ceilings by (2).
PI's coefficient comparison and local Neumann inversion therefore apply with their existing constants
(`out-pi.md:673–757,861–911`).

The reference agrees with the actual reference on the model's one-spacing enlargement.
Lemma 3.2 identifies the corresponding norms.
Conjugating back gives the required bound for the actual local matrix.
Its inverse is fixed by its coefficients, independently of the chosen filling or gauge.
Distinct patch fillings therefore need not be glued.

For an interior model use the original PI proof.
All actual and model coefficients entering a cutoff commutator remain identical to the originals.
The partition, flat bounds, commutator bounds and walk counts are unchanged.
PI's assembly and Schur identifications apply verbatim to these same matrices.
Holomorphy follows on the whole original domain from their holomorphic entries and nonvanishing determinants.
This argument imposes (3) on that domain; it never obtains (3) by shrinking it.

The resulting PI kernels and walk terms are the actual ones.
GW, GW1 and layer uses may consume them with their existing extra row, current and domain premises.
This proves boundary sufficiency, not those extra premises. ∎

This closes the proposed read reduction. It does not yet supply (3) at actual entries.

## 5. New route 1: remove the exterior dependence from the consumer

One might hope that the remaining exterior links cancel when the mixed inverse produces `q_0`.
Then no certificate would be needed there. The following exact calculation rules this out.

Choose a large finite torus and a block-aligned slab `X` with a flat face through zero.
Near that face, `X` is `x_1 <= 0`. Put `Omega_1 = empty`, so every row is bare.
Keep the other face and torus identifications far from the following plaquette.
Let

\[
 b=(0,\eta e_2),\qquad
 b_+=(\eta e_1,\eta e_1+\eta e_2),\qquad H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1).
\]

The bond `b` is a field bond. The bond `b_+` has no endpoint in `X`.
Nevertheless both bound the same plaquette `p`.
Set `U_theta(b_+) = exp(i theta H)` and set every other positive link to one.
Use reversed-link inverses and zero twist.

For sufficiently small real `theta`, these fields are admitted PI fields.
Indeed the identity gauge has one potential of size `|theta|/eta`.
Its scaled first differences have size at most `|theta|`.
All other bounds are the flat bounds.

Here `Q = I`, `Q' = I`, and `R = 0`.
The definitions in `out-pi.md`, section 3.1, therefore give

\[
 q_0=G_0^{-1}=\Delta(U_\theta)+\eta^{-2}I.                       \tag{5}
\]

Take a test field `v(b) = alpha H`, zero on every other field bond.
Of the plaquettes through `b`, only `p` contains `b_+`.
Its Wilson contribution along `tv` is `1 - cos(theta +/- eta t alpha)`.
Its second derivative at zero is `eta^2 alpha^2 cos(theta)`.
At `theta = 0` the same derivative is `eta^2 alpha^2`.
Since `||v||_2^2 = eta^4 alpha^2`,

\[
 \langle v,(q_0(U_\theta)-q_0(1))v\rangle
       =\eta^{-2}(\cos\theta-1)\|v\|_2^2.                       \tag{6}
\]

The right side is nonzero for arbitrarily small nonzero `theta`.
It uses two admitted fields, so singularity is irrelevant to this calculation.
It is also gauge invariant: the plaquette eigenvalues change.
Thus neither zero extension of test fields nor the Schur extraction removes this exterior datum.

The failure is not limited to exact coefficient agreement.
There is a path with every field link fixed to one on which the inverse ceases to exist.
Keep the same slab and bare rows.
For each positive tangential bond in the exterior plane `x_1 = eta`, set its link to `exp(i theta H)`.
Set every other positive link to one.
All these changed bonds have both endpoints outside `X`.
Let `v` now be supported on the crossing normal bond `(0, eta e_1)`, with value `alpha H`.
For each of the three tangential directions there are two incident plaquettes.
Each contains exactly one changed exterior link, so its holonomy is `exp(+/- i theta H)`.
At `theta = pi`, the six Hessian contributions each equal `-eta^2 alpha^2`.
Equation (5) therefore gives

\[
 \langle v,\Delta_a(U_\pi)v\rangle
           =(-6+1)\eta^{-2}\|v\|_2^2<0.                        \tag{6a}
\]

At zero, `Delta_a = d^T d + eta^-2 I` is positive definite.
The finite real symmetric matrix varies continuously with `theta`.
Its least eigenvalue is continuous, since its change is bounded by the matrix-norm change.
It must vanish at an intermediate value. Thus `G_0` fails to exist there.
This uses the continuity argument already used in `out-pi.md`, Proposition 3.3.
Here every field link is held fixed; only non-field exterior links change.
Throughout this path, all field links are one and the twist is zero.
Interior-only field bounds therefore cannot replace the missing exterior control even for existence.
This path still does not identify an entry reached by the construction.

This route proves that the second collar was expendable but the first collar is not.
It fails to supply C0-ETA by deleting all exterior reads.
Equation (6) is an obstruction to that proposed reduction.
It is not a reached-history counterexample to C0-SUP.

This differs from the earlier native-boundary route.
The operator here remains the same whole-action operator throughout.
No inner-action Hessian is substituted.

## 6. New route 2: use the corridor one scale earlier

### 6.1 What the ordinary step geometry proves

Port I 50153–50229, which rewrites Balaban's CMP 119 step construction.

\[
 \Omega_t^c=(Q'_t)^{\sim2}\cup(P'_t)^{\sim4}\cup H_Y^{\sim8}.       \tag{7}
\]

The coarse cell side is `LM check R_t` in level-`k` units.
In physical units write `W_t = LM check R_t ell_k = M check R_t ell_t`, with `ell_j = L^j eta`.
All lengths in the following comparison use these physical units.
This is a construction rule and its proved set identity, not an analytic estimate.
Interpret (7) first on cell indices, then take the corresponding closed geometric unions.

Here is the precise corridor consequence.
It extends the geometric argument beyond the original bare step already treated in `pi-admit.md`.

**Lemma 6.1.** Set `X_t = Omega_t^{~1}`.
Let `x` lie in `X_t`, and let `Q_x` have half-width `q eta < W_t`.
Then every coarse cell meeting `Q_x` has cell-graph distances at least

\[
 7\text{ from }H_Y,\qquad 3\text{ from }P'_t,\qquad
 1\text{ from }Q'_t.                                           \tag{8}
\]

In particular `Q_x` is contained in the preceding small-field region `Lambda_k`.

**Proof.** Coarse cells are adjacent when their closures touch.
By (7), every cell of `Omega_t` has distances at least `9`, `5`, `3` from those sets.
A cell of `X_t` containing `x` is at most one cell from `Omega_t`.
The strict length inequality puts every cell meeting `Q_x` at most one cell from that cell.
The triangle inequality subtracts at most two from the three distances.
This gives (8). Since `Y` is contained in `H_Y`, it also proves the last assertion. ∎

This gives the requested preceding-scale geometric corridor for the ordinary step's `X_t`.
It includes a patch crossing `partial X_t`; crossing that boundary costs no extra layer.
Choose `q >= r_N+2` to include the entire bare patch and its difference padding.
For an ordinary step the required block floor is fixed, independent of the history.

The same distances place the patch inside the domain of the new `P_t` tests.
They also place it outside `P_t'^{~2} union H_Y^{~6}`, where the approximate-fluctuation tests are made.
A failed test cube meeting the patch would put a meeting coarse cell in `P'_t` or `Q'_t`.
Equation (8) excludes that. Thus every such test actually made passes.
This statement does not assert a new covering theorem for all test stencils.
This uses the actual hull definition at I 50168–50171.
It does not turn a test of a coarse field into a test of a different fine field.

## 7. New route 3: reconstruct the collar from the coarse datum

The parent corridor might offer only the tested coarse field and its exact averaging constraint.
Try to recover (3) from those data, without a fine boundary gauge input.
The exact averaging map has invisible fine curvature. The following calculation exhibits it.

Use the account's actual centered contour mean and block map, SA 7943–8000, (QC1)–(QC3).
Their local existence and uniqueness proof is at SA 8002–8053.
These definitions and that proof are ported from Sussman and Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as text authors.
This calculation uses neither a linear substitute for the average nor a large-field Borel extension.

Work first at unit fine spacing on a torus of period divisible by $L$ and greater than $4L+4$.
Let the $L$-periodic real function $f$ on the first coordinate satisfy

\[
 f(0)=1,\qquad f(1)=-1,\qquad f(r)=0\quad(r\not\equiv0,1\pmod L).
                                                                    \tag{10}
\]

Fix $\theta>0$, small enough that every logarithm below lies in the account's analytic chart.
This is a fixed positive choice: all contour phases below have magnitude at most $2\theta$.
Define the fine links by

\[
 U_2(x)=\exp\{i\theta f(x_1)H/L\},\qquad U_\mu(x)=I\quad(\mu\ne2).
                                                                    \tag{11}
\]

Every path transport lies in the same commuting subgroup.
The unique contour mean also lies in it: the exponential of the mean phase solves (QC1).
All contour families translate to identical families under $x\mapsto x+Le_\mu$.
The periodic field is unchanged by that translation.
Thus the two lateral contour means in (QC3) cancel exactly.

For a coarse bond $c=(y,y+Le_2)$, (QC3) becomes

\[
 Z_x(U)=\exp\{i\theta[f(x_1)-f(y_1)]H\},\qquad
 U[c]=\exp\{i\theta f(y_1)H\}.                                  \tag{12}
\]

Every centered block contains one full period of the first coordinate.
Hence $L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(y)}f(x_1)=0$.
The logarithms in (12) are their displayed phases, by the choice of $\theta$.
Substitution in (QC3) gives

\[
 \mathcal M(U)(c)
 =e^{-i\theta f(y_1)H}e^{i\theta f(y_1)H}=I.                     \tag{13}
\]

In every other coarse direction the straight segment is trivial.
The same lateral cancellation gives $Z_x=I$ and the average is again $I$.
Thus the entire coarse field is exactly flat.

The fine plaquette in directions $1,2$ based at $x_1=0$ instead has holonomy

\[
 U(\partial p)=e^{-2i\theta H/L},\qquad
 |U(\partial p)-I|=2\sin(\theta/L)>0.                            \tag{14}
\]

If all four links admitted one gauge with $\eta|A|\le\epsilon_r$,
unitarity and telescoping would give

\[
 |U(\partial p)-I|
 \le\sum_{b\in\partial p}|e^{\pm i\eta A(b)}-I|
 \le4\epsilon_r.                                               \tag{15}
\]

The last inequality follows from $|e^{itH_0}-I|\le|t|\,|H_0|$ for Hermitian $H_0$.
Therefore (3) fails on this plaquette whenever $4\epsilon_r<2\sin(\theta/L)$.
In particular, no fine gauge bound tending to zero with the coarse plaquette bound follows from averaging.
The coarse bound in (13) is zero while (14) stays positive.
Place $\partial X$ through $x_1=0$; this is a plaquette read across that boundary.

There is also an exact complex version of the same obstruction.
Replace $\theta$ by a complex parameter $z$ in the same small chart.
Equations (12)–(13) hold identically throughout that chart.
Use the fixed reference $U_r=I$ and, after restoring spacing $\eta$, the splitting

\[
 a_z(b_2)=\frac{z}{L\eta}f(x_1/\eta)H,\qquad
 a_z(b_\mu)=0\quad(\mu\ne2).                                    \tag{16}
\]

Then $\eta\sup|a_z|=|z|/L$, although the coarse perturbation is identically zero.
The first difference across $f(0),f(1)$ has scaled size $2|z|/L$.
Thus even an exact holomorphic family in the coarse fibre does not bound the fine twist by the coarse twist.
Unlike the old unused-collar example, (14)–(16) concern a genuine Hessian read.
They also satisfy the exact nonlinear averaging constraint.

This is not a counterexample at an actual reached entry.
The fluctuation tests or a regular critical-branch condition may exclude this family.
It is precisely those extra fine-field conditions which this route would have to supply.
SA 7357–7370 already states the correct one-step rigidity estimate.
It has a separate fine-curvature term and a contour-defect term; they are not removed by an exact average.
That conditional rigidity statement is ported, not re-proved here.

The newer proper-background result does not authorize using (13) alone either.
N 35634–35704 constructs a regular critical field from the stated ordinary and mixed tests.
Its depth-zero step prescribes inner bonds.
Its regularity norm explicitly tests inner plaquettes, N 35276–35292.
N 35771–35822 gives the corresponding physical continuation on that native problem.
Those results are retained. They do not identify the protruding plaquette (14) of the whole-action entry.
No substitution of the native Hessian is attempted here.

This third route therefore fails to prove C0-ETA from a preceding coarse test and an exact coarse datum.
Its missing input is control of the fine member selected by the construction on the actual boundary read.
Equations (10)–(16) show why averaging alone cannot provide that input.
