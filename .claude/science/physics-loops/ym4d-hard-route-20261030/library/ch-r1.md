### Attempt B: continue the stationary branch using real uniqueness

I:10725–10733 uses the auxiliary problem's own real minimizer:

> Let $U_0:=U_{p,X}(M^{\cdot}(U))$. This is a real minimiser.

The next lines express the complex auxiliary through the response of this particular \(U_0\).
This suggests working with each native stationary system directly.
The proposal here differs from changing constraint rows or constructing a common contraction ball.
Keep one system fixed. Try to continue its chosen stationary branch through regular solution charts.
Use real uniqueness and real coercivity to prevent an obstruction.
For this test, even grant that its induced raw datum has already continued holomorphically.

The local equations are unambiguous wherever the prescribed coordinate chart exists.
Let \(x\) denote its field coordinates, \(S(x)\) its action, and \(q(x)=b\) its fixed constraints.
If \(Dq\) is onto, constrained stationarity gives
\[
 F(x,\lambda;b)=
 \bigl(DS(x)-Dq(x)^{\mathsf T}\lambda,\ q(x)-b\bigr)=0.
 \tag{6}
\]
Here is the finite-dimensional justification.
Choose a preimage of each coordinate basis vector under \(Dq\).
Extend these choices linearly to a right inverse \(R\).
For every \(v\), the vector \(v-RDqv\) belongs to \(\ker Dq\).
Stationarity annihilates that vector, so \(DSv=(DSR)Dqv\).
Set \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}=DSR\). This proves (6).
The complex version uses bilinear transposes and the continued equations, not minimization of a complex action.

A real gap and a unique real minimizer do not exclude a complex singularity of the selected branch.
The following fixed-constraint calculation proves this, even with a uniform real Hessian gap.
For \(\varepsilon>0\), let
\[
 S_\varepsilon(x,y)=\frac{x^2}{2}+\frac{x^4}{4\varepsilon^2}-xy+y^2,
 \qquad q(x,y)=y=b.                                    \tag{7}
\]
These functions are polynomials. The constraint row is always \((0,1)\).
For real \(x,y,v,w\),
\[
 D^2S_\varepsilon(x,y)[(v,w),(v,w)]
 = (v-w)^2+w^2+3x^2v^2/\varepsilon^2
 \ge \tfrac13(v^2+w^2).                                \tag{8}
\]
Indeed \(v^2\le2(v-w)^2+2w^2\).
Thus the gap is independent of \(\varepsilon\).
On \(y=b\), the stationary equation is
\[
 x+x^3/\varepsilon^2=b.                                \tag{9}
\]
For every real \(b\), its left side is strictly increasing from \(-\infty\) to \(+\infty\).
It has exactly one solution.
The derivative of the restricted action changes from negative to positive there.
Hence that solution is the unique real constrained minimizer.

There is a holomorphic branch \(x(0)=0\) near zero.
For completeness, on \(|x|\le\varepsilon/4\), \(|b|<\varepsilon/8\), iterate
\(T_b(x)=b-x^3/\varepsilon^2\) from zero.
It maps this ball into itself, since
\(\varepsilon/8+\varepsilon/64<\varepsilon/4\).
Its Lipschitz constant is at most \(3/16\), by factoring \(x^3-z^3\).
The successive differences therefore have a summable geometric bound, uniformly in \(b\).
The limit solves (9) and is unique in the ball.
Each iterate is holomorphic.
On a smaller parameter circle, uniform convergence passes its Cauchy integral formula to the limit.
That formula proves holomorphy of the limit.

The branch cannot continue holomorphically to an open disc about zero containing
\[
 b_* = \frac{2i\varepsilon}{3\sqrt3}.
 \tag{10}
\]
To see this without a numerical root calculation, put
\[
 x=i\varepsilon t,\qquad b=i\varepsilon(t-t^3),
 \qquad 0\le t<1/\sqrt3.
\]
The derivative \(1-3t^2\) is positive on this interval.
The root \(i\varepsilon t\) is simple there and agrees with the branch near zero.
If the branch existed on the proposed disc, it would agree along this whole curve.
Here is a direct uniqueness check for that continuation.
At any equality point below \(1/\sqrt3\), factor the difference of the two cubic equations.
The second factor tends to \(1-3t^2\ne0\).
Thus nearby roots that are close to this root must coincide.
The equality set is consequently open and closed along every compact initial subinterval.
It contains zero, so it is the whole interval.
Continuity at (10) would give \(x(b_*)=i\varepsilon/\sqrt3\).
Differentiating (9) at that point gives
\[
 (1+3x(b_*)^2/\varepsilon^2)x'(b_*)=1,
 \qquad 0=1,                                          \tag{11}
\]
a contradiction.

The obstructing datum tends to zero with \(\varepsilon\).
The real gap (8), the constraint row and real uniqueness remain unchanged.
This does not model a CH4-AUX history.
In particular, its higher derivatives contain \(\varepsilon^{-2}\); no claim puts this family inside Bałaban's admissible class.
It refutes the proposed principle that uniform real coercivity and uniqueness alone give the required uniform complex continuation.

**Outcome of B.** Continuing the actual stationary equations could work with quantitative control of those equations.
Real uniqueness cannot supply that control.
I did not exclude the corresponding complex obstructions for the actual reached auxiliary systems.
This attempt does not prove a singularity for any Yang–Mills auxiliary map.

### Attempt C: use stationarity and gauge identities to improve the current order

I:10730–10735 writes the native auxiliary current as

> $\mathbf J_{p,X}=J(U_0)+D^*D\mathcal H_p+F(U_0,\mathcal H_p)$.

This formula retains a linear force term.
I tested whether constrained stationarity or gauge symmetry could cancel that term on the actual data variation.
Such a cancellation would improve the absolute estimate used in A.
This is an algebraic proposal about the physical auxiliary current.
It does not identify that current with a switched reduced current.

First keep every parameter in the variational equation.
On a native stationary chart with multipliers, write the system as
\(D_xS(x,z)=D_xq(x,z)^{\mathsf T}\lambda\),
\(q(x,z)=b(z)\), where \(z\) includes the incoming data and boundary parameters.
Along a differentiable solution curve, the chain rule gives
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}S(x(s),z(s))
 =\lambda^{\mathsf T}\bigl(Db(z)-D_zq(x,z)\bigr)z'
       +D_zS(x,z)z'.                                  \tag{12}
\]
Indeed, differentiate the constraint and substitute its expression for \(D_xq\,x'\) into
\(D_xS\,x'+D_zS\,z'\).
Thus stationarity removes variations tangent to a fixed constraint fibre.
It does not remove a variation of the prescribed raw datum.
Even when \(S\) and \(q\) have no explicit \(z\)-dependence, the term \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}b'\) remains.

An exact SU(2) calculation also rules out a universal quadratic-current cancellation from gauge symmetry.
This calculation is a test of that proposed identity, not an example of a CH localization triple.
Take a graph consisting of one oriented four-edge plaquette.
For \(U_1,\ldots,U_4\in\mathrm{SU}(2)\), put
\[
 P=U_1U_2U_3U_4,\qquad
 S(U)=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}P,
 \qquad W(t)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{it}&0\\0&e^{-it}\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Constrain the based holonomy to be \(P=W(t)\).
Every configuration in this fibre has the same action \(1-\cos t\).
Hence every such configuration minimizes the constrained action.
There is exactly one representative after fixing the three tree edges to the identity:
\[
 U_1=U_2=U_3=I,\qquad U_4=W(t).                         \tag{13}
\]
To verify this, fix the gauge at the first vertex.
Choose its values successively as \(I,U_1,U_1U_2,U_1U_2U_3\).
Under \(U_{ab}\mapsto h_aU_{ab}h_b^{-1}\), the first three edges become identities.
The fourth becomes \(P\).
This also proves uniqueness of (13) in the based gauge orbit.

Restrict to the diagonal subgroup, with oriented edge angles \(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_4\).
Then
\[
 S=1-\cos(\theta_1+\cdots+\theta_4),\qquad
 \frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta_i}=\sin(\theta_1+\cdots+\theta_4).
 \tag{14}
\]
At (13), all four oriented current components therefore equal \(\sin t\).
Their divergence at each vertex is zero: its incoming and outgoing values agree.
This is the gauge identity, verified directly.
The action is also even in \(t\).
Neither fact removes the linear current response.

For \(0<t\le1/2\), one has \(\cos u\ge1-u^2/2\ge7/8\) on \([0,t]\).
Integrating gives \(\sin t\ge7t/8\).
The first inequality follows from
\(1-\cos u=\int_0^u\sin v\,dv\le u^2/2\).
Consequently no fixed \(C\) bounds this current by \(Ct^2\) near zero.
Use the Euclidean operator norm for the following matrix estimate.
The plaquette defect is linear as well, since
\(\|W(t)-I\|\ge|\operatorname{Im}(e^{it}-1)|=\sin t\).
All statements follow from the displayed matrices and finite differentiation.

**Outcome of C.** Gauge identities allow nonzero conserved auxiliary forces.
Stationarity does not cancel the transverse response caused by changing the raw averages.
An improvement from linear to quadratic order would require an additional identity for the actual link-4 datum variation.
I obtained no such identity.
A quantitative linear estimate could still prove (3); this calculation does not disprove it.
