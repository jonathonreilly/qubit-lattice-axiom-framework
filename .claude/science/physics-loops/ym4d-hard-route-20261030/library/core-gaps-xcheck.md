## 3. Lemmas 0–3, checked step by step

**Lemma 0.** If $p\in f$, $a\in A$ and $d(p,a)<g$, a positive-radius
neighbourhood of $p$ is contained in $A^{[g]}\subset B$. Its intersection
with $\operatorname{int}f$ is nonempty and open. Since the grid is finite
on the torus, one can choose a point there off all grid faces. It lies
in the interior of a selected cube of $B$, which must be $f$ by
disjointness of grid interiors. This contradicts $f\not\subset B$.
The same proof works in a sufficiently small local torus chart.
Empty $A$ has distance infinity; there is no exception.

**Lemma 1.** The two-cell enlargement of $e$ lies in $Z_t$ by the step
rule. It is face-connected and contains $e$, so it lies in its component
$K_e$. Apply Lemma 0 to that component. For any other component $X$,
every constituent cell is outside $K_e$, giving $d(e,X)\ge2a_t$.
No lower bound on the distance between arbitrary live components is
assumed. The hole cell $e\subset\Omega_t^c$ is essential.

**Lemma 2.** Inclusion of the step complement in $Z_t$ gives
$\Lambda_t\subset\Omega_t$. For $t\ge2$, R1 puts $Z_{t-1}$ inside
$\widehat Y$; the eight-cell neighbourhood of that hull lies in
$\Omega_t^c$. Applying Lemma 0 to each omitted cell gives
$d(Z_{t-1},\Omega_t)\ge8a_t$. For $t=1$, apply the lemma to
$\Omega_0=\Omega_1^{[a_1]}$, using the level-1 cell grid. Finally
$a_t\ge u_t$. Copying earlier members preserves their established gaps.

**Lemma 3.** I checked h-geo section 2, including the proof of Lemma 2,
rather than importing its earlier check's verdict. Write $h=t_\flat$.
For the strengthened core endpoint here, $D_h=G_h$, not
$\Omega_h^c\cap X$. The hull inclusion $G_h\subset H_{h+1}$ proves the
needed bound for this larger endpoint directly.

The five possible consecutive pairs give:

1. $l=h$: $G_h^{[3a_{h+1}]}\subset H_{h+1}^{[3a_{h+1}]}=D_{h+1}$.
2. $h<l<k_0$: $D_l^{[7a_l+3a_{l+1}]}
   =H_l^{[10a_l+3a_{l+1}]}\subset D_{l+1}$.
3. $l=k_0$: $D_l\subset G_l\subset H_{l+1}$, and
   $D_{l+1}=H_{l+1}^{[u_t]}$. Thus the asserted gap is at least
   $u_t\ge u_{l+1}$. The extra $7a_l$ available in h-geo is unnecessary.
4. $l=k_0+1$: if $l+1=t$ the difference of radii is $2u_t$.
   Otherwise
   $w_{l+1}\le u_t\sum_{r\ge0}L^{-r}=u_tL/(L-1)$.
   Hence the difference is at least $u_t(L-2)/(L-1)\ge u_t/L

   $L^2-3L+1\ge0$ for integer $L\ge3$; the last follows from
   $l+1\le t-1$.
5. $k_0+2\le l<t$: subtracting the two finite sums gives

## 4. Lemma 4: every fate and every new pair

Fix the pre-operation label and all components at once. They are
disjoint as cube regions. On each level, the fate formulas give
$\Lambda_l\subset\Omega_l$: equal filled members for healing, equal
members below the top for islands, equal members in a stopped window,
unchanged old members elsewhere, and empty top $\Lambda_t$ in an
island or drop-out.

F1 is correct: healing deletes the relevant hole, an island deletes
all holes below $t$, and a stop replaces $G_l$ by $D_l\subset G_l$.
The top hole of an island or stop stays unchanged. Thus $Z_l\subset
Z'_l$. F2 is also correct as a cube statement: both the old region
and the operated components are unions of the grid of the target
cube $f$. A target cube outside the old region must belong wholly
to one operated component.

For (G), let $c$ be an $M$-cube of level $l$ in $Z_l$, and $f$ an
$M$-cube of level $l+1$ in $\Omega_{l+1}$.

- If $f$ is old, F1 and the old (G) give the required distance.
  This includes the upper splice after a partial preparation.
- If $f$ is new in $X$, then
  $c\subset Z'_l\subset\Omega_t'^c$ by the old (N).
  Since $u_l$ divides $a_t$, $c$ lies in one level-$t$ cell $e$.
  An interior point of $c$ lies inside $e$; the cell-union property
  of $\Omega_t'^c$ implies $e\subset\Omega_t'^c$.
- If $K_e\ne X$, Lemma 1 gives
  $d(c,f)\ge d(e,X)\ge2a_t\ge u_{l+1}$.
- If $K_e=X$, healing or island formation is impossible for this
  pair, since it leaves no $Z_l$ in $X$ when $l<t$.
  For a stop, newness of $f$ implies
  $h<l+1\le h+m$. Thus $l\ge h$,
  $c\subset D_l$ (using $D_h=Z'_h\cap X$ at the bottom),
  and $f\subset X\setminus D_{l+1}$. Lemma 3 gives the bound.

These cases exhaust the cube pairs and therefore the point pairs.
Positive separation gives $\Omega_{l+1}\subset\Lambda_l$.
Together with the already proved within-level inclusions, it gives (N).

At $m=0$ no member changes. For $0<m<\mathsf N_t$, the lower splice
is Lemma 3 at $h$; interior changed pairs use its later indices;
the upper splice is the old-target case. At $m=\mathsf N_t$ the
last changed target is $\Omega_t\cap X=X\setminus D_t$:
$c\subset D_{t-1}$ and $f\subset X\setminus D_t$ give the last
gap. The top hole is still all of $X$, not $D_t$. This is why the
next ordinary step encloses the right live input.

For $l=0$, R2 fills $\Omega_0$ when a component heals or becomes
an island. A stopped window starts strictly above $h\ge1$ and
cannot change $\Omega_0$ or $\Omega_1$. The same pair analysis applies.
No margin is decremented according to the number of operations.

### Attempted breaking histories

These are symbolic two- and three-operation attempts, not a search
or an enumeration. Each names the obstruction to the attempted failure.

1. **A swallowed mark, then another operation.** At level $s$ create
   a drop-out $A$. At level $t$ a healed component or island $B$
   contains it; at level $r>t$ treat another component. The second
   operation overwrites all entries on $A$ at its old levels and
   removes its mark. Changing the old stopping index therefore cannot
   restore a hole there. A surviving hole cube outside $B$ paired
   with a newly filled cube of $B$ has the fresh $2a_t$ separation.
   The third operation repeats that argument with $2a_r$, not with
   a diminished residue of $2a_t$.
2. **An island inside a later eligible component.** Make $A$ an
   island at $s$, then try to use it inside a class component $B$
   at $t$. If $s\ge t_\flat$, quarantine rejects the candidate.
   If $s<t_\flat$, its entire top hole $A\subset Z_s$ remains;
   the next step encloses it. In $B$ it lies below the preparation
   window, and the transition from $D_{t_\flat}=G_{t_\flat}$ to the
   first prepared box supplies at least $3a_{t_\flat+1}$.
   Its filled lower entries cannot produce a new lower hole.
3. **Two nested maximal drop-outs and a third operation.** Stop
   $A$ after $\mathsf N_s$ stages, then stop a containing $B$ after
   $\mathsf N_t$ stages. Eligibility forces $s<t_\flat$.
   Their affected windows are disjoint; the old mark keeps
   $\Lambda_s\cap A=\emptyset$, and the new one keeps
   $\Lambda_t\cap B=\emptyset$. Reindexing either changes neither
   input top hole. A later containing operation at $r$ requires
   $t<r_\flat$ while $B$ stands. If it stops, its new pairs get
   its own local box bounds. If it heals or becomes an island,
   it overwrites both old windows and removes both marks.
   The proposed short-distance pair is respectively excluded by
   Lemma 3 or by the absence of an internal old hole.

Thus none of these attempts gives cubes with distance below
$u_{l+1}$. Their exclusion is by the identified inequalities and
overwrite rules, not by failure to find a numerical example.

## 5. Lemma 5 and Theorem 6

A mark at level $s$ affects only its own cubes and indices
$(s_\flat,s]$. At index $s$ it never changes $\Lambda_s$.
Consequently every permitted stopping vector has the same top
$Z$; after a new step both new top members are also fixed.
The possible dependence of $\Omega_s$ on $\nu$ for a maximal
current drop-out does not contradict this assertion.

If a standing mark meets a later eligible $X$, its connected region
lies in $Z_s$ and, by the already established determining nesting,
in the current top $Z_t$. Hence it is contained in that component.
Waiting puts $s<t_\flat$. All entries of $X$ at indices at least
$t_\flat$, including the bottom $G_{t_\flat}$, are therefore
unchanged by reindexing older marks. This proves Lemma 5(a).

For (b), copied old entries plus the fixed new entries are precisely
the reindexed label after the step. For (c), extend the final stopping
vector arbitrarily to the old marks. Outside the operated components
the two descriptions agree. Healing and islands overwrite the old
indices and remove swallowed marks, so the extension's values there
do not matter. In a new stopped window no older mark is active;
the two-case rule is exactly Lemma 4 with $m=\nu(X)$. Below that
window the same older marks act on the same old entries.
This also covers a new mark with $\nu=0$.

The induction in Theorem 6 is consequently over step and operation
boundaries, with an operation allowed any vector of partial stage
counts on its disjoint components. There is no need to manufacture
an analytic history with changed tests or weights: Y 62137–62193
fixes the recorded geometry, and that is all the theorem uses.
For sequential processing inside one operation, the maps on disjoint
components commute; an untreated component has stage count zero.

The base is step 1. The early class is empty when depth is insufficient
(Y 63684–63692). A later step uses Lemma 5(b), fixed input $Z$, and
Lemma 2. An operation uses Lemma 5(c), the joint order in section 4,
and Lemma 4. This proves (N) and (G) for all stopping vectors together.

Finally each assertion of Y's display follows separately.

- Divide (G) by $L^j$: $u_{j+1}/L^j=LM$.
- $\Omega_{j+1}$ is a union of level-$j$ $M$-cubes.
  No such cube belonging to it can belong to $Z_j^{[u_j]}$,
  since that would give distance at most $u_j<u_{j+1}$.
  Grid-complement membership therefore gives
  $\Omega_{j+1}\subset\Lambda_j^{\sim-1}$.
- By (N), $\Omega_{j+2}\subset\Omega_{j+1}$.
  The triangle inequality gives
  $d(Z_j^{[u_j]},\Omega_{j+2})\ge u_{j+1}-u_j>0$.
  Thus the intersection is empty even for closed carriers.

These arguments also handle empty members with distance infinity.
They never mix unrelated stopping choices at adjacent levels.

The Remark's equality also holds at the active indices of a retained
prepared nest. There $Z_j\cap X=D_j$, while
$\Omega_{j+1}\cap X=X\setminus D_{j+1}$.
The two concentric boxes have face offset $u_{j+1}$, and
$D_{j+1}$ lies strictly inside $X$. Facing boundary points therefore
give an infimum at most $u_{j+1}$; (G) gives the reverse inequality.
For the transition pair inside that nest, the face offset is
$w_j-w_{j+1}$; this is a local equality, not a larger global gap claim.

## 6. Constants and Corollary 7

The numerical ingredients of Lemmas 0–6 are precisely $L\ge3$,
odd $L$ for alignment, $a_i\ge u_i$, the named nested grids,
and nonwrapping admissible class boxes. The supplied preparation
formula also includes its stated scale-choice restriction on $k_0$.
It is not derived merely from $L\ge3$ and $\check R_i\ge1$.

The lower bound on $\check R_i$ is justified explicitly as follows.
Y 1102–1103 gives $\theta_i\ge\gamma^{-2}>e$ for frames;
Y 1109–1113 defines the rounded logarithmic radius.
For Y's running-coupling formulation, Y 61911 gives $g_i\le\gamma$,
and Y 83084 gives $g_i=x_i^{-1/2}$. Therefore
$x_i\ge\gamma^{-2}>e$ and
$(\log\min_{i\le t}x_i)^r>1$. Y 62001 chooses $\check R_t$ not
below that number. In particular $\check R_t\ge1$.
Being a power of $L$ alone would not establish this. The stage
count interpretation is consistent with, but not needed for, this
direct argument.

In bare units Y 61911 bounds the torus period below by
$2L^{11}a_i$. The class cutoff is at most $100a_i$ along a side.
For $L\ge3$, $L^{11}\ge3^5=243>100$; hence that box fits well
within a half period. This is an integer inequality, not a machine
evaluation. All local prepared-box neighbourhoods used above are
contained in the next box. No global nonwrapping condition is
imposed on an arbitrary live component.

The parameters $\rho,\mu,\theta$ in geo-5, and its larger choices
$M\ge80\mu$ and $L\ge27$, never enter the gap induction reproduced
