## 2. Multiplier matching and layered response

**Lemma 1 (projection and saddle normalization).** Suppose \(\Delta=D^{\mathsf T}D\) is injective on \(\ker Q'\) and \(Q'\) is onto. For any positive diagonal top mass \(a_s\), in any positive diagonal top pairing,
\[
 G_s=(\Delta+Q'^\sharp a_sQ')^{-1},\qquad
 R=I-G_sQ'^\sharp(Q'G_s^2Q'^\sharp)^{-1}Q'G_s
\]
is the fine-orthogonal projection onto \(\Delta\ker Q'\), independent of those choices. For an invertible saddle
\[
 S_K=\begin{pmatrix}X&-K^{\mathsf T}\\K&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
introducing a symmetric row mass \(a_r\) does not change its inverse. Whenever \(G=(X+K^{\mathsf T}a_rK)^{-1}\) and \(\Sigma=(KGK^{\mathsf T})^{-1}\) exist, put \(H=GK^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\), \(T=G-HKG\). Then
\[
 S_K^{-1}(f,b)=(Tf+Hb,-\Sigma KGf+(\Sigma-a_r)b).                 \tag{1}
\]

*Proof.* The scalar quadratic form vanishes only on \(\ker D\cap\ker Q'=0\). The injective map \(G_sQ'^\sharp\) has Gram matrix \(Q'G_s^2Q'^\sharp\). Thus \(I-R\) is its range projection and
\(\operatorname{ran}R=\ker(Q'G_s)=G_s^{-1}\ker Q'=\Delta\ker Q'\).
For (1), \(KH=I\), \(KT=0\). Multiplication gives \(Ka=b\) and \(Xa-K^{\mathsf T}\lambda=f\). Uniqueness gives the same inverse for every admissible mass. For symmetric \(X\), \(\Sigma KG=H^{\mathsf T}\). P82 supplies existence in its system; no inverse is inferred just from notation. \(\square\)

## 3. The profile comparison

**D4 (two sequences and supplied profile hypotheses).** Let \(j_a(p),j_d(p)\) be the largest levels containing the midpoint of \(p\) in the action and determining sequences; put \(n_a=k-j_a\), \(n_d=k-j_d\). Require:

* \(0\le\varphi_l\le1\), \(\varphi_l=0\) off \(\Omega_l^a\), and \(\varphi_l=1\) on \(\Lambda_l^{a,\sim-1}\), with \(\Omega_{l+1}^a\subset\Lambda_l^{a,\sim-1}\).
* The sequences are nested. They differ only in standing drop-out windows \((t_\flat,t_\flat+n(X)]\), where \(t_\flat=t-N_t\ge0\), \(n(X)\le N_t\). There \(\Omega_l^a\cap X=\Omega_l^\circ\cap X\) and \(\Omega_l^d\cap X=X\setminus Z_l''\), with \(Z_l''\subset X\setminus\Omega_l^\circ\). Windows through a point are disjoint, and their lower endpoints belong to no other such window.
* \(x_k(p)=\beta-\sum_{l=1}^kb_l\varphi_l(p)\), \(x_k=\beta-\sum_{l=1}^kb_l\), and \(|b_l|\le B_b=c_0+2c_2+1\). IN1 H-OL/H-SF imply this bound when \(u\le1\); otherwise it is an explicit hypothesis on the complex increments.
* IN1's real frame obeys \(\theta_j\ge X_0>e\), \(\theta_t\le\theta_k+\overline B(k-t)\), and \(N_t=\lceil(\log\min_{i\le t}\theta_i)^r\rceil_{L^{\mathbb N_0}}\), \(r\ge1\).

These are supplied data and geometric hypotheses. *Proof.* Terms with \(l<j_a(p)\) vanish in

without a window \(j_a=j_d\). Rounding and the frame inequality give

Here \((u+v)^r\le2^{r-1}(u^r+v^r)\) and

\((1+y)^{-1}\le(1+y)^{1/r-1}\). This proves H-prof. Its right side is at most

## 4. Fine-mesh regularity in a real gauge

**D5 (local lattice data).** On \(\eta\mathbb Z^4\), set \(D_i^+f(x)=[f(x+\eta e_i)-f(x)]/\eta\), \(D_i^-f(x)=[f(x)-f(x-\eta e_i)]/\eta\), \(\nabla=(D_i^+)_i\), \(\operatorname{div}A=\sum_iD_i^-A_i\), \( (dA)_{\nu\mu}=D_\nu^+A_\mu-D_\mu^+A_\nu\), and \(\Delta_\eta=\sum_iD_i^-D_i^+\le0\). These are SA BGH1's signs. A cube \(Q_s\) has radius \(s\) in maximum norm. Hölder seminorms compare like components at lattice points using physical Euclidean distance. Every stencil is required on the stated enlargement. Fix \(\xi\ge\eta\). On \(Q_{s+8\xi}\) suppose a real field satisfies
\[
 |\partial U-I|\le\varepsilon\eta^2\xi^{-2},\quad
 |J(U)|\le\varepsilon\xi^{-3},\quad
 U^{g_1}=e^{i\eta A_1},\quad
 |A_1|\le a\xi^{-1},\quad|\nabla A_1|\le a\xi^{-2},
 \quad 0\le\varepsilon\le a.                                    \tag{8}
\]
The current is the fine-pairing derivative of IN1's Wilson action:
\[
 J(U)(b)=\eta^{-3}\sum_{p\ni b}\pi(P_{p,b}),\qquad
 \pi(P)=(P-P^*)/(2i).
\]
Here \(P_{p,b}\) starts at \(b_-\) and traverses \(b\) first. Differentiating
\(1-\frac12\operatorname{ReTr}P\) in \(e^{it\eta v}U(b)\) gives
\(\eta\langle v,\pi(P)\rangle\), proving this normalization. The formula uses each of the six unoriented incident plaquettes once.

**Lemma 5 (scalar estimates, SA BGH2–BGH23).** For \(0<\alpha<1\), \(\mu\eta\le1\), the massive resolvent \(R_\mu=(-\Delta_\eta+\mu^2)^{-1}\) on bounded functions satisfies
\[
 \|R_\mu f\|_\infty\le\mu^{-2}\|f\|_\infty,\quad
 \|DR_\mu f\|_\infty\le C\mu^{-1}\|f\|_\infty,\quad
 [DR_\mu f]_\alpha\le C_\alpha\mu^{\alpha-1}\|f\|_\infty.         \tag{9}
\]
If \(R\ge64\eta\), \(\Delta_\eta u=f\) on the full reads of \(Q_{2R}\), then on \(Q_{R/2}\)
\[
 \|Du\|_\infty\le C(R^{-1}\|u\|_\infty+R\|f\|_\infty),\quad
 [Du]_\alpha\le C_\alpha(R^{-1-\alpha}\|u\|_\infty+
                             R^{1-\alpha}\|f\|_\infty).         \tag{10}
\]
Constants are mesh independent.

*Proof.* This carries the source's heat-kernel proof, not a numerical estimate. Define
\(q_T(n)=(2\pi)^{-4}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^4}e^{-T\omega(\theta)}e^{in\cdot\theta}\,d\theta\),
\(\omega=4\sum_i\sin^2(\theta_i/2)\), and \(p_t^\eta(\eta n)=\eta^{-4}q_{t/\eta^2}(n)\). The identity
\(e^{T\Delta_1}=e^{-8T}\sum_{m\ge0}T^m\mathsf A^m/m!\), with \(\mathsf A\) the sum of eight translations, proves positivity and mass one. For an order-\(r\) difference multiplier \(m_r\), product differentiation gives
\[
 |\partial^bm_r|\le C|\theta|^{(r-|b|)_+},\qquad
 \int|\partial^b(m_re^{-T\omega})|^2\le C_{r,b}T^{|b|-r-2}
 \quad(T\ge1).
\]
To see the power, use \(\omega\ge c|\theta|^2\); each first derivative of \(\omega\) contributes \(O(|\theta|)\), higher derivatives are bounded, and substitute \(\theta=T^{-1/2}v\). Parseval then bounds
\(\sum_n(1+|n|^2/T)^M|\delta^rq_T(n)|^2\le CT^{-r-2}\).
For \(M>N+2\), comparison on unit cubes gives the reciprocal-weight tail at most \(CT^2(1+A/\sqrt T)^{-2N}\). Cauchy–Schwarz and physical scaling yield
\[
 \sum_{|x|\ge A}\eta^4|D^rp_t^\eta(x)|
 \le C_{r,N}\begin{cases}
 t^{-r/2}(1+A/\sqrt t)^{-N},&t\ge\eta^2,\\
 \eta^{-r}(1+A/\eta)^{-N},&t\le\eta^2.
 \end{cases}                                                    \tag{11}
\]
For the second line, the periodic multipliers have uniformly bounded derivatives for \(0\le T\le1\), and the same weighted Parseval argument applies.

Set \(G_\mu=\int_0^\infty e^{-\mu^2t}p_t^\eta\,dt\), with convolution measure \(\eta^4\) times counting. Integrating the semigroup derivative proves the resolvent identity, uniqueness on bounded functions, and its value bound. Integrating (11) for \(r=1\) gives the gradient bound. For a displacement \(h\), telescoping and (11) for \(r=1,2\) give the heat-gradient modulus
\(C\min(t^{-1/2},|h|t^{-1})\) above \(\eta^2\), and \(C\eta^{-1}\) below. Splitting the time integral at \(\eta^2,|h|^2,\mu^{-2}\) gives
\(C|h|(1+\log(1/(\mu|h|)))\) for \(\eta\le|h|\le\mu^{-1}\).
The inequality \(x^{1-\alpha}(1+\log(1/x))\le1+[e(1-\alpha)]^{-1}\), and twice the gradient bound for larger \(h\), prove (9).

For \(r\le3\), integrating (11) outside \(|x|\ge cR\), with \(\mu=1/R\), gives
\(\|1_{|x|\ge cR}D^rG_{1/R}\|_1\le C_{r,c}R^{2-r}\):
split at \(\eta^2,R^2\) and choose \(N>\max(0,r-2)\).
Choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi\), one on \(Q_R\), supported in \(Q_{3R/2}\). For \(v=\chi u\),
\[
 v=R_{1/R}\{\chi(-f+R^{-2}u)\}-R_{1/R}[\Delta_\eta,\chi]u.

The first term is bounded by (9). Transfer the commutator to the kernel using
\([\Delta_\eta,\chi]^{\mathsf T}=-[\Delta_\eta,\chi]\).

\]
This is respectively \(C\|u\|_\infty/R\) and \(C\|u\|_\infty/R^2\). All differences of \(\chi\) lie in the separated collar. Telescoping the first derivative in \(Q_{R/2}\) proves its Hölder bound. There \(v=u\), including the required stencils. This proves (10), componentwise as well. \(\square\)

**Lemma 6 (massive gauge).** Under (8), for sufficiently small absolute \(a_*>0\) and \(a\le a_*\), there is a real gauge \(e^{i\lambda}\) after \(g_1\), with
\[
 \|\lambda\|_\infty+\xi\|\nabla\lambda\|_\infty\le C_\lambda a,
 \quad |A_2|\le C_2a/\xi,\quad
 \operatorname{div}A_2=-\xi^{-2}\Psi_1(\lambda)\lambda            \tag{12}
\]
on \(Q'=Q_{s+6\xi}\), where \(\Psi_1(0)=I\). On interior stencils,
\(|\nabla\operatorname{div}A_2|\le C a\xi^{-3}\).

*Proof.* Define the analytic finite-dimensional function
\(\Phi(u,u',w)=i^{-1}\Log(e^{iu}e^{iw}e^{-iu'})\) near zero.
Put \(\Psi_1(u)=-\partial_{u'}\Phi(u,u,0)\),
\(\Psi_2(u)=\partial_w\Phi(u,u,0)=\operatorname{Ad}(e^{iu})\). Then
\[
 \Phi(u,u+v,w)=-\Psi_1(u)v+\Psi_2(u)w+\mathcal R(u;v,w),
\]
where \(\mathcal R=O((|v|+|w|)^2)\),
\(\partial_u\mathcal R=O((|v|+|w|)^2)\), and
\(\partial_{v,w}\mathcal R=O(|v|+|w|)\).
These bounds follow by the convergent exponential/log series and Cauchy on a fixed smaller matrix ball. They are independent of the mesh.

Insert \(u=\lambda(x)\), \(v=\eta\nabla_\nu\lambda(x)\),
\(w=\eta A_{1,\nu}(x)\), and difference the formula backwards.
The exact identity is
\[
 \operatorname{div}A_2=-\Psi_1(\lambda)\Delta_\eta\lambda+
                   \Psi_2(\lambda)\operatorname{div}A_1+S(\lambda).
\]
With stencil maxima, \(V=|\nabla\lambda|+|A_1|\),
\[
 |S|\le CV^2,\quad
 |S(\lambda)-S(\tilde\lambda)|
 \le C\{(V+\tilde V)^2|\lambda-\tilde\lambda|
             +(V+\tilde V)|\nabla(\lambda-\tilde\lambda)|\}.       \tag{13}
\]
Indeed coefficient differences cost \(C|\nabla\lambda|\); each remainder is \(O(\eta^2V^2)\), which cancels the two inverse mesh factors in the divergence. The differentiated remainder bounds prove (13) even without second derivatives of \(\lambda\).

Let \(\mu=\xi^{-1}\) and solve on the full lattice
\[
 \lambda=R_\mu\left(1_{Q'}[-\Psi_1(\lambda)^{-1}
          \{\Psi_2(\lambda)\operatorname{div}A_1+S(\lambda)\}]\right).
                                                                    \tag{14}
\]
The data inside the brackets need \(A_1\) only on the given larger cube. In the complete norm
\(\mathcal N(\lambda)=\max(\|\lambda\|_\infty,\xi\|\nabla\lambda\|_\infty)\),
(9) bounds \(R_\mu\) by \(C_R\xi^2\). On \(\mathcal N\le Ka\), the forcing is at most \(C\xi^{-2}(a+(K+1)^2a^2)\), and its Lipschitz constant at most \(C\xi^{-2}(a+Ka)\). First choose \(K>2C_RC\); then choose \(a_*\) so (14) maps that ball into itself and has contraction constant at most \(1/2\). Include the fixed analytic-chart ceilings in this choice. At fixed \(\eta>0\) the normed space is complete. The map preserves Hermitian traceless fields, giving a real gauge. Its equation gives the negative sign in (12). The Taylor formula gives the bound on \(A_2\); differencing the smooth function \(\Psi_1(\lambda)\lambda\) gives the divergence-gradient bound. No bounded global second-resolvent-derivative norm is used. \(\square\)

**Theorem 7 (fine-mesh Hölder estimate).** There is an absolute \(a_{\rm FH}>0\) such that (8), with \(a\le a_{\rm FH}\), implies a real gauge on \(Q_s\) in which
\[
 |A|\le C_Fa\xi^{-1},\qquad|\nabla A|\le C_Fa\xi^{-2},\qquad
 [\nabla A]_{\alpha,Q_s}\le C_{F,\alpha}a\xi^{-2-\alpha}
 \quad(0<\alpha<1).                                             \tag{FH}
\]
Constants do not depend on \(s,\eta/\xi\) or volume; \(a_{\rm FH}\) is chosen before \(\kappa\).

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 6. For \(P(x;\nu,\mu)\) the oriented plaquette put
\(F_{\nu\mu}=(i\eta^2)^{-1}\Log P(x;\nu,\mu)\);
\(F_{\mu\nu}=-F_{\nu\mu}\). Pairing the positive and negative plaquettes at a bond in the current formula gives
\[
 |\delta F+J|\le C(|A_2||F|+\eta^3|F|^3).                        \tag{16}
\]
Here \((\delta F)_\mu=\sum_\nu D_\nu^-F_{\nu\mu}\). To verify (16), the negative plaquette is the inverse of its positive translate conjugated by one link. Conjugation differs from identity by \(O(\eta|A_2|)\), and
\(\pi(e^{iX})=X+O(|X|^3)\). The factor \(\eta^{-3}\) leaves exactly the displayed two errors.
The four-link logarithm has linear part \(i\eta^2dA_2\) and an analytic quadratic remainder. Differencing that remainder between translated plaquettes gives
\[
 |dA_2-F|\le C|A_2|^2,\qquad
 |\delta(dA_2-F)|\le C|A_2||\nabla A_2|.                         \tag{17}
\]
Indeed the arguments have size \(O(\eta|A_2|)\) and their difference size \(O(\eta^2|\nabla A_2|)\); division by \(\eta^3\) proves the second bound.

Flat differences commute, so
\(\Delta_\eta A_2=\delta dA_2+\nabla\operatorname{div}A_2\).
Real gauges preserve the curvature and current norms, and
\(|F|\le2\varepsilon\xi^{-2}\). Equations (12),(16),(17) therefore give
\[
 |\Delta_\eta A_2|\le C_\Delta(a\xi^{-3}
                   +a\xi^{-1}\max_{\rm stencil}|\nabla A_2|).    \tag{18}
\]
Fix a centre in \(Q_{s+2\xi}\). For \(t\in[\xi,2\xi]\), let \(\phi(t)\) be the largest first difference on contained stencils in \(Q_t\). It is finite at each fixed mesh. For \(t<t'\), apply (10) on radius \(R=(t'-t)/8\) cubes. When \(R<64\eta\), use the direct bound \(2\|A_2\|_\infty/\eta\le128\|A_2\|_\infty/R\) instead. The larger cubes and (18)'s stencil reads fit inside \(Q_{t'}\). Thus
\[
 \phi(t)\le Ca\,\phi(t')+
            C'a\xi^{-1}(t'-t)^{-1}+C''a\xi^{-2}.                \tag{19}
\]
Choose \(Ca\le1/2\). To iterate, take increments
\((1-\tau)\tau^i\xi\), with \(\tau=(1+Ca)/2\). The geometric sums are at most \(12\) for the inverse-increment term and \(2\) for the constant term; the terminal term tends to zero since \(\phi\) is bounded. When \(\xi<128\eta\), all distinct points are at least \(\xi/128\) apart, so the same supremum bound suffices. Choose
\(a_{\rm FH}\le\min(a_*,1,(16C_2)^{-1},(2C)^{-1})\).
The composed gauge is \(g_2g_1\). This proves FH. \(\square\)

## 5. Pointed cancellation and the ordinary window

**D6 (actual pointed Hessian).** At one old scale, on the coarse lattice \(\mathbb Z^4\) with bare mesh \(\eta=1/N\), \(N\) a positive integer, let
\(F_z(B)=\sum_{X\ni z}G(X,\mathcal P(B),z)\).
Assume normal convergence through two derivatives; a flat reference
\(\mathcal P(0)=(1,0)\); gauge covariance of the actual background;
gauge invariance of each member, without fixed colour spectators;
and unit-translation and full hypercubic covariance, including reflections,
of this member at its hypothetical base points. Its actual first response is
\(hB=(HB,d^*dHB)\). Require the absolute moment
\[
 \sum_{x,y,\mu,\nu,a,b}e^{m(|x-z|+|y-z|)}
 \left|\sum_{X\ni z}D^2G(X,0)[h\delta_{x,\mu}^a,
                                  h\delta_{y,\nu}^b]\right|<\infty. \tag{20}
\]
Uniform applications require a uniform bound here. Define \(K_z^{\mu\nu}(x,y)\) as one third of the sum over \(a\) of the inner Hessian in (20) with \(b=a\), and the bulk kernel
\(\pi_{\mu\nu}(u)=\sum_xK_0^{\mu\nu}(x,x+u)\), and
\[
 b[G]=\sum_u\pi_{\mu\nu}(u)u_\mu u_\nu\quad(\mu\ne\nu).
\]
The pointed action is \(A_z=\sum_p h_z(p)A_p\), with
\(h_z(p)=\prod_i(1-|p_i-z_i|)_+\), using plaquette midpoints.
For its normalization additionally require that the **same actual** \(H\)
reproduce affine constant-curl fields modulo gradients, with zero current;
its matrix elements decay sufficiently to extend it to these polynomial
fields. The action convention is \(A(e^{iB})=\frac12|dB|^2+O(B^3)\). The pair argument \(0\) denotes \((1,0)\) in this chart. An affine field is
\(\ell_\mu(x)=a_\mu+\sum_\rho C_{\mu\rho}(x+e_\mu/2-z)_\rho\),
with \(F_{\nu\mu}=C_{\mu\nu}-C_{\nu\mu}\); one choice for a prescribed skew \(F\) is \(C_{\mu\nu}=F_{\nu\mu}/2\).

**Lemma 8 (pointed cancellation; SA PH5–PH23).** Under D6, for every affine field \(\ell\) with constant curl \(F\),
\[
 \tfrac12D^2F_z(0)[\ell,\ell]
       =\tfrac{b[G]}2\sum_{\mu<\nu}\langle F_{\mu\nu},F_{\mu\nu}\rangle,
 \qquad
 D^2(F_z-b[G]A_z\circ\mathcal P)(0)[\ell,\ell']=0.                \tag{21}
\]
The second identity holds for any two affine fields. Pairings are bilinear for complex fields.

*Proof.* Constant colour invariance annihilates the first derivative, so the second chain-rule term vanishes. Rotations by \(\pi\) about colour axes and permutations of those axes make the colour Hessian \(\delta_{ab}K_z\). Commuting mixed derivatives gives its symmetry. Differentiate local gauge invariance in a compactly supported gauge parameter \(\chi\) and a field \(C\): \(D^2F_z(0)[d\chi,C]=0\). Cut off polynomial \(\chi\) at radius \(R\). Its differences have a polynomial majorant independent of \(R\); (20) allows dominated convergence. Thus the Ward identity holds for polynomial arguments, without putting an unbounded field in a nonlinear domain.

Linear \(\chi=x_\mu t_a\) gives separate row and column sums zero. Set
\(T_{\mu\nu,\rho\tau}=\sum_{x,y}K_0^{\mu\nu}(x,y)x_\rho y_\tau\).
Quadratic \(\chi=\frac12x^{\mathsf T}Sx\,t_a\), for arbitrary symmetric \(S\), gives
\[
 T_{\mu\nu,\rho\tau}=-T_{\rho\nu,\mu\tau}
                   =-T_{\mu\tau,\rho\nu}.
\]
Moving starts to bond midpoints changes \(T\) only by zero row/column moments. Reflections then force every label among its four indices to occur an even number of times. The two antisymmetries leave only two equal unordered pairs; permutations make their values equal. Consequently
\[
 T_{\mu\nu,\rho\tau}
   =b(\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\rho\tau}
                          -\delta_{\mu\tau}\delta_{\rho\nu}).
\]
In the bulk second moment the pure \(x^2,y^2\) terms vanish, leaving
\(\sum_u\pi_{\mu\nu}(u)u_\rho u_\tau
=-T_{\mu\nu,\rho\tau}-T_{\mu\nu,\tau\rho}\).
For \(\rho=\mu,\tau=\nu,\mu\ne\nu\), this is \(b\), with no colour factor. Contracting \(T\) with the affine slopes proves the first identity in (21).

If \(\eta=1/N\), the one-dimensional sums of an unshifted tent and a half-mesh-shifted tent, multiplied by \(\eta\), are respectively
\(N^{-1}[1+2\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-j/N)]=1\) and
\(2N^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(1-(j+1/2)/N)=1\).
Their product gives \(\eta^4\sum_{p_{\mu\nu}}h_z(p)=1\).
Affine reproduction therefore makes the action's quadratic jet
\(\frac12\sum_{\mu<\nu}\langle F_{\mu\nu},F_{\mu\nu}\rangle\).
Subtract and polarize. \(\square\)

 \le C_{m,q}D_X^{q+4}\), by counting each distance shell inside a box of side \(C(D_X+t+1)\).
