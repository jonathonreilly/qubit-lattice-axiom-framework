### 2.1 Test the consumers' radii, not just CR's stronger table

I:43402–43419 gives the actual real-factor tests
\[
 T_2=\vartheta_E\alpha_n\eta^2\ell_n^{-2},\qquad
 T_4=\vartheta_E\alpha_n\ell_n^{-3}L^{(n-p)_+}.
 \tag{R3}
\]
For \(n<p\), division of (R2) by (R3) gives respective upper bounds
\[
 2L^2B_3\frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_n}
                 \frac{\ell_p^2}{\vartheta_E},\qquad
 2L^3B_3\frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_n}
                 \frac{\ell_p^2}{\vartheta_E}.
 \tag{R4}
\]
Thus the consumers do not logically require CR's precise constants or its exact pointwise majorant. They require admitted minimizers and (R3), for every prescribed subdomain and slot. This distinction is essential when testing a correction.

For comparison, (R2) divided by CR's below-slot table has the common factor
\[
 \frac{2B_3}{3B_HK_b}\frac{\ell_p^2}{\ell_n}.
 \tag{R5}
\]
At \(p=k\), its scale factor is \(L^{k-n}\). No fixed enlargement of \(B_H\) or \(M\), or common decrease of \(\alpha_k\), absorbs this for unbounded \(k-n\). The coupling cancels in (R5). This disproves that **estimate comparison**, not CR itself.

The weaker test (R4) also has no uniform payment from the stated radius comparison alone. I:42881–42897 gives
\[
 \alpha_m=C_0g_m(\log g_m^{-2})^{q_0},\qquad
 \alpha_k/\alpha_n\le(1+\beta_0)\sqrt{1+k-n}.
 \tag{R6}
\]
For \(n\le k_0\), the guaranteed gain is only \(\vartheta_E\ge\theta_*\). At \(p=k\), (R4) loses all scale decay. Increasing the lower floor on \(N_0\) does not bound \(k-n\) from above.

Here is a hand check that a small coupling ceiling alone cannot repair this scalar comparison. Fix \(T>\max\{1,\gamma^{-2},e^{2q_0}\}\). For a finite depth \(k\), take
\[
 x_m=g_m^{-2}=T+k-m\quad(0\le m\le k).
\]
For \(m\le m'\), the radius ratio is
\[
 \frac{\alpha_{m'}}{\alpha_m}
 =\sqrt{\frac{T+k-m}{T+k-m'}}
   \left(\frac{\log(T+k-m')}{\log(T+k-m)}\right)^{q_0}
 \le\sqrt{1+m'-m}.
 \tag{R7}
\]
Here \(q_0\ge0\), as for the radius exponents used in the account (I:49348–49351 specifies \(q_0>1\)). Every coupling is below \(\gamma\). For fixed \(T\) and \(n\), however,
\[
 \frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_n}
 =\sqrt{\frac{T+k-n}{T}}
   \left(\frac{\log T}{\log(T+k-n)}\right)^{q_0}
 \longrightarrow\infty.
 \tag{R8}
\]
To check the limit, put \(t=\log(T+k-n)\) and use \(e^{t/2}\ge(t/2)^j/j!\) with an integer \(j>q_0\). Thus no finite fixed multiplier of the target radii bounds (R4) on the information (R6). This is a scalar test of the proposed deduction. It does not assert that these radii and a saturating field form an actual RG history. A stronger actual-field estimate could still prove admission.

There is a useful restricted acceptance. If an additional slot restriction gives \(k-p\ge a(k-n)\), for some fixed \(a>0\), then
\[
 \ell_p^2\sqrt{1+k-n}
 \le\sqrt{1+k-n}\,L^{-2a(k-n)}.
 \tag{R9}
\]
This tends to zero; successive-term ratios are eventually less than a fixed number below one. Consequently a sufficiently large early \(N_*\), followed by the usual coupling ceiling, pays (R4) at \(n\le k_0\) in this restricted class. It adds no backward choice: \(a,L,B_3,\theta_*\) are fixed before \(N_*\). But K3 and K4 permit \(p=k\). No such slot restriction is available for all their below-slot readouts. Restricting the consumers would change their definitions.

In contrast, on RB Proposition 2's proved box class, **any fixed smaller target fraction** \(\sigma>0\) can be paid early. RB (12) and its preceding above-slot comparison bound both ratios at \(n<k\) by
\[
 \frac{(1+\beta_0)\max\{2L^3B_3,3L^3B_HK_b\}}{\theta_*}
 W(N_0),\qquad
 W(N)=\max\{L_0^{-2N},\sqrt{1+N}\,L^{-N}\}.
 \tag{R10}
\]
At \(n=k\), where the stated level-\(k\) provision applies, the ratio is at most
\(2L^3B_3/[(1-2\beta)L_0^{2N_0}]\). Both expressions tend to zero. Choose \(N_*\) after \(M,K_b\) to make them at most \(\sigma\), then impose \(N_0\ge N_*\) through the final coupling ceiling (I:42907–42947). This only strengthens one-sided \(\Gamma_1\)–\(\Gamma_3\), with no change of an earlier parameter or birth domain. Thus a fixed fraction such as \(1/16\) is not an additional obstruction on that box class. The obstacle outside is the loss of scale decay in (R4), not the requested numerical fraction.

Here is the exact useful content of ARR §18, with its hand proof retained. Let \(\mathcal R\) contain all bonds read by the local algebras, both layers, all six-plaquette current stencils, every required whole cube and its enlarged gauge domain. Let the actual seed \(U_2\) be consistently defined on \(\mathcal R\), and **assume** its old index-zero cube gauges there. Put
\[
 \delta=\sup_{p\text{ read in }\mathcal R}|\partial U_2(p)-1|,
 \qquad \varepsilon=F_3+F_4,
 \qquad K_*=\max\{4,K_{\rm cur}\},\quad
 K_{\rm cur}=3+18d+2.2K_J .
 \tag{C2}
\]
The nonnegative \(F_s\) must bound the actual local norm
\(N_y^{\mathbb U^{(s-1)}}(t_s\mathbb H_s)\) and the actual current increment by
\(\eta^3|\mathfrak s^\sharp_{\sigma_s}[t_s\mathbb H_s]|\le K_{\rm cur}F_s\), for every parameter and every required segment, on the primitive analytic input classes. The derivative of the second layer is at the preceding partial product, not at an arbitrarily substituted seed. This is ARR (18.2), using I:142279–142281; it is not the assumed collar conclusion I:142287.

Subject to the local-algebra tests at each application, the strongest resulting test is
\[
 \begin{split}
 \delta+4\varepsilon&<f_0(d^t)\alpha_{0,0},\\
 12\delta+K_{\rm cur}\varepsilon&<f_0(d^t)\alpha_{0,0},\\
 \delta&<f_0(d^t)\alpha_{0,0},\\
 1.06\varepsilon&<f_0(d^t)\alpha_{1,0},\\
 1.2\varepsilon&<f_0(d^t)\alpha_{1,0}.
 \end{split}
 \tag{C3}
\]
This includes the full physical seed current. By I:142718–142728, each current stencil in dimension four contains six terms, each a unitary conjugate of a traceless projection of \(\operatorname{Im}\partial U_2\). The projection norm is at most two and \(|\operatorname{Im}W|\le|W-1|\) for unitary \(W\). Thus
\[
 \eta^3|\mathcal J(U_2)|\le12\delta .
 \tag{C4}
\]
No condition on only the plaquettes based at the collar centre would justify (C4); the neighboring plaquettes are included in \(\mathcal R\).

For a concrete uniform choice, ARR (18.4) takes
\[
 \delta\le\frac{\min\{1,\alpha_{0,0}\}}{24},\qquad
 \varepsilon\le\min\left\{\frac1{160},
              \frac{\alpha_{*,0}}{16K_*}\right\},
 \qquad \alpha_{*,0}=\min\{\alpha_{0,0},\alpha_{1,0}\}.
 \tag{C5}
\]
The first left exponential has local norm at most \(1/160<1/40\), initial potential zero, and curvature at most \(1/24<1/10\). The local algebra I:141265–141298 gives, before the second application,
\[
 \eta|A'_3|\le1.06/160<1/80,\qquad
 |\partial\mathbb U^{(3)}-1|
 \le1/24+4/160=1/15<1/10.
\]
Both applications therefore meet the tests. They add at most \(4F_s,1.06F_s,1.2F_s\) to curvature, dimensionless potential and dimensionless gradient. The unitary factor remains \(U_2\). The independent current after the seed is estimated by (C4) and the sum of the stated current increments. Consequently
\[
 \left(\frac{Q_1}{r_1(0)},\frac{Q_3}{r_3(0)},
        \frac{Q_5}{r_5(0)},\frac{Q_6}{r_6(0)},
        \frac{Q_7}{r_7(0)}\right)
 \le\left(\frac5{48},\frac9{16},\frac1{24},
          \frac{53}{3200},\frac3{160}\right).
 \tag{C6}
\]
For example the current ratio is at most \(12/24+1/16=9/16\); the first ratio is at most \(1/24+1/16=5/48\). Every entry is strictly below \(73/20\). The same proof covers omitted or partial layers because (C2) is uniform over the segments. The principal logarithm is one convergent bondwise series, so overlapping local constructions agree. Whole-cube gauges remain those assumed for the unchanged \(U_2\). This proves (C3) and (C6) as **implications**, with no computational step.

This is ARR (11.4)–(11.5) and I:143228–143253.

### 3.4 Why an early ceiling or a new radius does not finish CA

The sufficient lemma has no hidden order problem. In
\[
 L\longrightarrow\mathfrak M\longrightarrow\kappa
 \longrightarrow\kappa_1\longrightarrow M_1
 \longrightarrow M\longrightarrow(q,C)\longrightarrow\gamma,
 \tag{C8}
\]
retain all of I:143355–143425, including the ratio floor \(A_0/A_1\ge c(4,L)\) and one-window scope. The analytic constant \(K_J\), and hence \(K_*\), is fixed before choosing a final ceiling; the numerical fractions in (C5) are absolute. If one had uniform estimates
\[
 \delta/\alpha_{0,0}\le D(t),\qquad
 \varepsilon/\alpha_{*,0}\le E(t),\qquad
 D(t),E(t)\longrightarrow0,
 \tag{C9}
\]
on the actual full read-sets and primitive input classes, where \(t=\log g_k^{-2}\) and the majorants are uniform in all histories and old birth dates, with constants fixed in (C8), one could choose a single early rule for the final ceiling so that the suprema for \(t\ge t_\gamma\) meet (C5), together with the absolute chart tests. This is an acyclic sufficient procedure: no bound is obtained by assuming the admission it is intended to prove. Smaller fixed margins in the preceding chain consumers could be handled in the same way **if** their full normalized estimates were supplied.

But (C9), the seed's cube hypotheses and the primitive-layer admission are not proved for every actual completion. The exact object is the graded-splice minimizer
\(U_2=U_{\mathbf B_2}(V_2(\varpi))\), I:92417–92448 and ARR (15.1), not the still-pinned real minimizer. Its physical current may contain its constraint multiplier; the identity \(J_2=Q^*\lambda_2\) from ARR (12.2) does not bound that multiplier. (C4) bypasses this issue only **after** all seed plaquettes are bounded. The source explicitly imposes additional real-core and perturbed-real-data membership at I:94185–94239. Absolute smallness at \(\gamma^\flat\) is not a bound divided by an old birth radius.

The actual parameter space includes \(\operatorname{supp}\Pi\) as a separate real factor, I:93612–93630. Shrinking a complex polydisc does not change an uncontrolled \(U_2(V_2(\varpi))\) at \(\mathfrak b_2\); it would also omit sources the consumer is required to accept. Enlarging a birth radius would require extending every previously stored analytic term to that larger domain. No such extension follows from a bound on its original domain. Making the domain smaller preserves holomorphy but loses the required full source quantifier. A fixed early change of a radius constant remains possible only with the corresponding uniform input-admission and domain-inclusion proofs; changing the number alone supplies neither.

The positive-level transfer ARR (12.1) has no circular parameter choice **when its admitted-seed hypothesis is true**. The empty-collar proof has no estimate to pay at all. Neither applies to every nonempty actual completion collar. This is the precise difference from the successful correction in [l17c.md](l17c.md): there the weaker supplier's hypotheses were met at every relevant use. Here the missing antecedent is the actual object on its required domain.

## Result

* CR: **still open** — K2/K3 below-slot collar consumers and the mixed K4 consumer need admitted minimizers satisfying the full-domain target tests (R3). RB's strongest outside estimate (R1) retains carrier admission and leaves the unpaid factor (R4); its uniform-cover and induced-datum results do not identify every actual boundary tower. The sharper table (20) is not logically necessary, but no proved replacement covers these consumers.
* CA: **still open** — the nonempty-collar image clause (e4)′, and hence fixed-birth-domain evaluation of stored terms in the correlation/arrest uses, needs CA for every actual completion seed, full parameter domain, segment and whole cube. ARR §18 proves (C3) conditionally; its seed, gauge and layer hypotheses are not universally established. The dated-collar \(1/16\) may be corrected to admission, but this does not discharge CA or the separate chain-containment margins.
