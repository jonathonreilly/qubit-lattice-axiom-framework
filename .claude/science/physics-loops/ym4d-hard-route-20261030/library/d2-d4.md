## 1. Premises and route

Credit: Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text. The source is their [pinned YM4 outline](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex), especially AM6–AM7, ST1–ST15 and GS1–GS13. The arguments below rebuild this transfer by hand.

**D1 is a named premise, not proved here.** It includes ST5–ST6, point-local counterterms, source locality and representation independence. It includes the rooted cube indexing at L4088–4135, not just the weaker ST6 count. All constants are uniform over the permitted cutoff–coupling data and tori. We also use S1g from `am1-port.md`, Result: every periodic Wilson DLR state has all lattice symmetries and reflection positivity. The finite chessboard inequality S1e is rebuilt below in its required complex form.

Write \(a=L^{-K}\), \(a_s=L^{-s}\), \(L\ge3\) odd, and \(M=L^{m_0}\). Tori have side \(\ell=2L^m\), \(m\ge m_0\). Boards have side \(l=L^j\ge a\), with faces through bond midpoints. Every fixed board tiles all sufficiently large tori. Let \(\mathcal O_a(f)=\sum_p f(x(p))a_p\), where \(0\le a_p\le2\), and set
\[
 t_f=\|f\|_\sharp=\max(\|f\|_\infty,c_M\|\nabla f\|_\infty).
\]
Tests are real \(C^1\), with compact radius-\(R\) supports. The letter \(d>0\) denotes support separation; the spatial dimension is four. Zero tests can be removed.

The route is: finite chessboards → arbitrary-state domination → common holomorphic normalizers → D4/G4 → polynomial normalizer loss → D2. Limits in torus size always precede changes of cutoff. No numerical or computer-assisted step enters a proof.

Unsubscripted \(C,c\) depend only on D1, \(L,M,c_M\), and the fixed smooth partition. They are independent of \(n\), cutoff, states and positions.

## 2. Chessboards and domination of every periodic state

Fix a permitted \(\beta\ge0\). A cell function uses only bonds wholly inside the open cell. Our source margins exceed \(2a\), so each source plaquette satisfies this condition. For a bounded cell function \(F\), reflect it into every cell, conjugating odd copies. Set
\[
 p_{l,\ell}(F)=\left\langle\prod_C F^{[C]}\right\rangle_\ell^{1/N},
 \qquad N=(\ell/l)^4.
 \tag{1}
\]
The integral is nonnegative: splitting at opposite faces writes its integrand as \(\Theta\Phi\,\Phi\). Finite Wilson reflection positivity, the input S1d to S1e, applies.

Here is the complex version of S1e. First use one direction with \(q\) slabs, \(q\) even. Transport the profiles to one slab and retain their labels. Divide each occurrence of label \(j\) by \((Z_j^{\rm dis}+\epsilon)^{1/q}\), where \(Z_j^{\rm dis}\ge0\) is its disseminated integral. Among the finitely many labelled fillings, choose one maximizing the absolute normalized integral. Reflection Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(T^2\le T_+T_-\), with both reflected integrals nonnegative and at most the maximum. Thus both also maximize when the maximum is positive. Retain a half starting at a constant run of length \(h\le q/2\). Reflection doubles that run. Once a run contains a half, reflecting that half makes the filling constant. Every constant normalized filling has value at most one. Therefore every filling does. Let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\); zero disseminated integrals are included. Apply this argument in each of the four directions. The coordinate reflections commute, so each original cell receives exponent \(1/N\). Dividing by the unsourced partition function proves
\[
 \left|\left\langle\prod_C F_C\right\rangle_\ell\right|
 \le\prod_Cp_{l,\ell}(F_C).
 \tag{2}
\]
Expanding the disseminated product of \(F+G\), then applying (2) to each term, gives \(p(F+G)\le p(F)+p(G)\). Half the copies are conjugated, hence \(p(zF)=|z|p(F)\), \(p(\bar F)=p(F)\), and \(p(1)=1\). Thus \(p(\operatorname{Re}(e^{-i\phi}F))\le p(F)\).

Fix \(a,\beta,l\) and a periodic DLR state \(\rho\). For bounded real cell \(H\), sum its \(N\) mirror copies in a large cube. S1g gives expectation \(N\rho(H)\). Conditional Jensen and the DLR equation bound this by the largest conditional log exponential moment. Replace the exterior bonds by periodic bonds, adding dummy Haar variables when needed. Only \(C(\ell/a)^3\) boundary plaquettes change. Each action changes by at most \(2\beta\); comparison of both numerator and denominator therefore costs at most \(C\beta(\ell/a)^3\). The source factors use interior cell bonds and stay identical. Consequently
\[
 \rho(H)\le\log p_{l,\ell}(e^H)
             +C\beta l^4a^{-3}\ell^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For bounded real \(G\), take \(H=\log(1+tG)\), \(0<t<(2\|G\|_\infty)^{-1}\). Since \(H\ge tG-Ct^2\|G\|_\infty^2\) and \(p(1+tG)\le1+tp(G)\), (3) gives
\[
 \rho(G)\le\liminf_{k\to\infty}p_{l,\ell_k}(G)
\]
for every compatible \(\ell_k\to\infty\), after taking this limit before \(t\downarrow0\). Rotate a complex \(F\) by the phase of \(\rho(F)\), and use the real-part inequality above. This proves the absolute one-cell bound.

Enclose any finite set of occupied cells inside one larger aligned cell, with side an allowed power of \(L\). Apply the one-cell bound to their product. Disseminate this larger cell on each torus, then apply (2) on the fine board. Each occupied fine-cell profile occurs once per larger cell. Taking the larger-cell root yields
\[
 \boxed{\left|\rho\!\left(\prod_C F_C\right)\right|
       \le\liminf_k\prod_Cp_{l,\ell_k}(F_C).}
 \tag{4}
\]
The aligned larger board exists because powers of the odd integer \(L\) have odd ratios. Its faces can be fine-board faces. The bound holds along every compatible sequence. No perturbed Gibbs state enters this argument.

## 3. Common normalizers, D4 and G4

Write D1's bounds explicitly:
\[
 \frac{|Z_\ell(W)|}{Z_\ell(0)}
 \le\exp\!\left\{\operatorname{Re}\left[U_\ell(W)+\sum_{s,X}e_{\ell,s,X}(W)\right]
                    +(C_++C_-)\ell^4\right\},
 \quad \|W\|_\flat<2r.
 \tag{5}
\]
The source condition means \(W=\sum_i z_if_i\) with \(\sum_i|z_i|t_{f_i}<2r\), including global Lipschitz profiles. Here \(U=\sum_pu_p(W(x(p)))\); all terms are holomorphic and vanish at zero. Each \(e_{s,X}\) depends only on \(W|_{X^+}\), obeys \(|e_{s,X}|\le Ae^{-\kappa m(X)}\), and has \(\operatorname{diam}X^+\le Ca_s(m(X)+1)\). ST6 bounds its weighted count near a radius-\(R\) ball by \(C(R+1)^4a_s^{-4}e^{-ct}\) for \(m(X)\ge t\).

Place \(m\) support balls inside one cell, with margin \(b>2\). For \(w=\sum_i z_if_i\), the disseminated source is \(W^{\rm ch}=\sum_i(z_iH_i^++\bar z_iH_i^-)\). Both real profiles \(H_i^\pm\) vanish near faces and have sharp norm at most \(t_{f_i}\). Thus (5) applies when \(\sum_i|z_i|t_{f_i}<r\).

Retain the point terms and domains whose full dependence regions lie inside a single open cell. Conjugate each retained odd-cell value, then sum and divide by \(N\). Call the result \(E_{C,\ell}(z)\). An odd-cell term becomes \(\overline{g(\bar z)}\), which is holomorphic and has the same real part as \(g(\bar z)\). Representation independence makes each retained term depend only on that cell's source. Thus \(E_{C,\ell}(0)=0\).

A discarded nonzero term sees a support and reaches a face. Therefore \(m(X)\ge cb/a_s-C\). Counting once per support with ST6 gives, per cell,
\[
 B_C=(C_++C_-)l^4+
       CA m(R+1)^4\sum_{s\ge0}a_s^{-4}e^{-c'b/a_s}<\infty,
 \qquad
 p_{l,\ell}(e^{\mathcal O_a(w)})
 \le e^{\operatorname{Re}E_{C,\ell}(z)+B_C}.
 \tag{6}
\]
All point terms with nonzero source are retained. The scale sum converges because its terms decay exponentially in \(L^s\).

At fixed cutoff, D1 bounds the point terms uniformly in \(\ell\). ST6 and the finite sum over \(s\le K\) bound the domain terms. Hence the \(E_{C,\ell}\) are locally bounded. Cauchy estimates give equicontinuity on smaller source balls. Arzelà–Ascoli and a countable compact exhaustion give a locally uniform subsequential limit \(E_C\); Cauchy's formula makes it holomorphic. For any finite list of cells use one subsequence. It depends only on the torus data, so it serves **every state** in (4).

For arbitrary support positions, choose \(l\le C_Ln(R+b+1)\) and an offset giving every support ball this margin. In each coordinate the \(n\) forbidden intervals contain at most \(n(2(R+b)/a+2)\) lattice offsets. An allowed power of \(L\) larger than a fixed multiple of this length leaves an offset. There are at most \(n\) occupied cells. Equations (4) and (6) give
\[
 h_\rho(z):=e^{-E(z)}\rho\!\left(e^{\sum_i z_i\mathcal O_a(f_i)}\right),
 \quad E=\sum_C E_C,\qquad
 h_\rho(0)=1,\quad |h_\rho(z)|\le Q:=e^{\sum_CB_C},
 \quad \sum_i|z_i|t_{f_i}<r.
 \tag{7}
\]

Each observable is bounded at fixed cutoff. Its exponential series therefore converges uniformly on compact parameter sets, proving that \(h_\rho\) is holomorphic. For one test, Cauchy's estimate gives \(|\rho(\mathcal O_a(f))-E_f'(0)|\le Q_Rt_f/r\). Subtract the same estimate for \(\nu\):
\[
 \boxed{|\rho(\mathcal O_a(f))-\nu(\mathcal O_a(f))|
           \le C_1(R)t_f,\qquad C_1(R)=2Q_R/r.}
 \tag{8}
\]
This proves D4. The common \(E_f'(0)\) need not remain bounded. A fixed smooth partition of unity into unit-scale bumps improves \(C_1(R)\) to \(C(R+1)^4\): at most \(C(R+1)^4\) pieces meet the support, and each piece has sharp norm at most \(Ct_f\). Take a compact nonnegative bump positive on a unit cube. Divide each integer translate by the positive periodic sum of all translates; that sum has a positive minimum and bounded derivatives.

For \(n\ge2\), a mixed derivative of a point term vanishes on disjoint supports. A contributing domain must see all supports, so \(m(X)\ge cd/a_s-C\). Cauchy on radii tending to \(r/(nt_{f_i})\), anchored at the first support, gives
\[
 |\partial_1\cdots\partial_n E_C(0)|
 \le D_n(d;R)\prod_i t_{f_i},\qquad
 D_n=CA(R+1)^4\max(1,d^{-4})(n/r)^n.
 \tag{9}
\]
Indeed, split \(\sum_sa_s^{-4}e^{-c'd/a_s}\) at \(a_s\approx\min(d,1)\). The coarse part is geometric. The fine part is at most \(Cd^{-4}\sum_{j\ge0}L^{4j}e^{-c''L^j}\). If several cells are occupied, the full mixed derivative of \(E=\sum_CE_C\) is zero instead.

Cauchy applied to (7), with inactive variables zero, gives
\[
 |\partial_Bh_\rho(0)|\le Q(|B|/r)^{|B|}\prod_{i\in B}t_{f_i}.
\]
Expand \(\log h=\sum_{j\ge1}(-1)^{j-1}(h-1)^j/j\) near zero. For the squarefree coefficient on \(n\) variables, the ordered nonempty blocks give coefficient \((-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!\). Therefore
\[
 |\kappa_n^\rho(\mathcal O_a(f_1),\ldots,\mathcal O_a(f_n))|
 \le\left[D_n+r^{-n}\sum_\pi(|\pi|-1)!Q^{|\pi|}
                      \prod_{B\in\pi}|B|^{|B|}\right]\prod_i t_{f_i}.
 \tag{10}
\]
Only a logarithm near zero was used. This proves G4's cumulant bound, uniformly in cutoff, states and positions. Higher cumulants are unchanged by constant shifts: shifts add only linear terms to the log generating function. Expanding the exponential of that logarithm gives centered moments by partitions, with singleton bound (8) and higher-block bound (10). These statements also hold for separated reflected copies, since isometries preserve the displayed data.

Finally, S1g makes \(\rho(a_p)-\nu(a_p)=\Delta\) independent of the plaquette. Choose a fixed bump \(f\ge1\) on a cube. For sufficiently small \(a\), that cube contains at least \(ca^{-4}\) plaquette centers, by counting lattice points in each coordinate interval. Equation (8) yields \(|\Delta|\le Ca^4\). For the remaining \(a\ge a_0>0\), use \(|\Delta|\le2\le2a_0^{-4}a^4\). This proves D4's plaquette consequence.

## 4. Rooted counting gives a polynomial cell loss

D1 indexes \(X\) by unions of cubes of side \(Ma_s\). A shortest cubical edge tree meeting these cubes has \(m(X)\) edges. For a specified cube \(Q\subset X\), root that tree at a corner of \(Q\). There are at most 16 choices. Traversing each edge twice gives a walk of length \(2m\), with at most \(8^{2m}\) choices. Every cube meeting the tree contains a tree vertex. At most \(16(m+1)\) cubes contain such vertices. Selecting a subset therefore gives
\[
 \#\{X:Q\subset X,\ m(X)=m\}
 \le16\,8^{2m}2^{16(m+1)}\le C e^{\kappa_Dm}.
 \tag{11}
\]
One admissible rate in (11) is \(\kappa_D=2\log8+16\log2\). Use D1's stipulated gap above a valid rooted rate, including any additional domain-type multiplicities. The argument also holds on finite cubical quotients. Summing \(e^{-(\kappa-\kappa_D)m}\) gives an exponentially decreasing tail.

If \(X^+\) sees a radius-\(u\) ball, some cube of \(X\) is within \(CMa_s\) of it. Coordinate interval counts give at most \(C(1+u/a_s)^4\) root choices. Summing instead over every torus cube gives
\[
 \sum_{X:X^+\cap B(u)\ne\varnothing,\ m(X)\ge t}e^{-\kappa m(X)}
 \le C(1+u/a_s)^4e^{-ct},\qquad
 \sum_Xe^{-\kappa m(X)}\le C\ell^4a_s^{-4}.
 \tag{12}
\]
All constants absorb the fixed number of neighboring dependence layers. These are GS2 and GS3. The first bound has no density factor \(a_s^{-4}\) when \(u\ll a_s\).

Let \(v\) have support in a radius-\(\delta\) ball, with margin \(\delta\) inside a side-\(\eta\) cell. Assume \(16a\le\delta\le\eta\le1\). In (6), a discarded domain has \(m(X)\ge c\delta/a_s-C\). Divide its total cost by \(N=(\ell/\eta)^4\). Equation (12) bounds the three scale ranges as follows:
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{range}&\text{cost per scale}&\text{sum}\\ \hline
 a_s\ge\eta&C A(\eta/a_s)^4&C A\sum_{j\ge0}L^{-4j}\\
 \delta\le a_s<\eta&C A&C A[1+\log(\eta/\delta)]\\
 a_s<\delta&C A(1+\delta/a_s)^4e^{-c'\delta/a_s}
       &C A\sum_{j\ge0}(1+L^{j+1})^4e^{-c''L^j}
 \end{array}
 \tag{13}
\]
The first row uses the global count, without a source restriction. The second and third count once per copied support. Both displayed series converge; for the last, \(4j\log L-c''L^j\le-j\) eventually. Indeed \(L^j\ge3^j\ge2j(j-1)\), by the binomial expansion, so \(L^j/j\to\infty\). The stability cost is \((C_++C_-)\eta^4\le C_++C_-\). Point terms remain inside cells. Hence constants \(Q_0\ge1,p\ge0\), fixed before \(n,\eta,\delta\), satisfy
\[
 p_{\eta,\ell}(e^{z\mathcal O_a(v)})
 \le e^{\operatorname{Re}E_{v,\ell}(z)}Q_0(\eta/\delta)^p,
 \qquad |z|<r/t_v.
 \tag{14}
\]
The fixed-cutoff compactness argument of Section 3 supplies common limiting univariate normalizers for any finite list of pieces.

## 5. Product discs, common centering and D2

For \(n\ge2\), put \(\bar d=\min(d,1)\). Choose a power of \(L\) with
\[
 \bar d/(16L)<\eta\le\bar d/16,\qquad \delta=\eta/(64n).
 \tag{15}
\]
First suppose \(a\le\delta/16\). Scale the smooth partition from Section 3 to mesh proportional to \(\delta\), with supports in radius-\(\delta\) balls. It gives \(f_i=\sum_kv_{i,k}\) with
\[
 \#\{k:v_{i,k}\ne0\}\le C(1+R/\delta)^4,
 \qquad t_{v_{i,k}}\le C\delta^{-1}t_{f_i}.
 \tag{16}
\]
The count follows by enclosing the relevant partition centers in a radius-\(R+C\delta\) ball. The norm bound follows from the product rule and the scaled derivative bound \(C/\delta\).

Choose one nonzero piece from each test. In each coordinate a piece's enclosing ball excludes at most \(4\delta/a+2\) link-plane offsets. Thus all pieces exclude at most
\[
 n(4\delta/a+2)\le(33/512)\eta/a<\eta/a
\]
offsets. Choose a remaining offset in each coordinate. Each ball then has margin \(\delta\). Different original tests occupy different cells: a four-dimensional side-\(\eta\) cell has diameter \(2\eta<d\), whereas their supports are \(d\) apart.

Write the selected pieces as \(v_i\), and set \(r_i=r/t_{v_i}\). Equations (14)–(15) give normalizers \(E_i\) and \(Q_i\le Q_0(64n)^p\le Cn^p\). Use (4) on separate cell factors, along their common normalizer subsequence. For every subset \(S\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\) and every periodic state,
\[
 \left|e^{-\sum_{i\in S}E_i(z_i)}
       \rho\!\left(e^{\sum_{i\in S}z_i\mathcal O_a(v_i)}\right)\right|
 \le\prod_{i\in S}Q_i,
 \qquad |z_i|<r_i.
 \tag{17}
\]
This is a genuine product polydisc. Each chessboard factor uses only its own source disc. There is no constraint on \(\sum_i|z_i|t_{v_i}\). Inactive factors equal one, so the bound contains only active \(Q_i\). The same \(E_i\) serve a second state \(\nu\).

Put \(X_i=\mathcal O_a(v_i)-E_i'(0)\). Differentiate (17) once in each active variable and apply product Cauchy:
\[
 |\rho\prod_{i\in S}X_i|\le\prod_{i\in S}Q_i/r_i,
 \qquad |\nu(\mathcal O_a(v_i))-E_i'(0)|\le Q_i/r_i.
 \tag{18}
\]
Only first derivatives of the univariate normalizers occur. Expand the product centered at \(\nu\) over active subsets. Each of its \(2^n\) terms is bounded by \(\prod_iQ_i/r_i\). Summing over the piece choices in (16) proves
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \left|\rho\prod_{i=1}^n[\mathcal O_a(f_i)-\nu(\mathcal O_a(f_i))]\right|
 &\le[C(R+1)^4n^p\delta^{-5}]^n\prod_i t_{f_i}\\
 &\le[C'(R+1)^4]^n n^{(p+5)n}\bar d^{-5n}\prod_i t_{f_i}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{19}
\]
The board may depend on the chosen pieces; all constants are uniform in that choice.

If \(a>\delta/16\), use bounded plaquettes directly. There are at most \(C(1+R/a)^4\) plaquette centers in a radius-\(R\) ball. Therefore
\[
 \|\mathcal O_a(f_i)-\nu(\mathcal O_a(f_i))\|_\infty
 \le C(R+1)^4a^{-4}t_{f_i}
 \le C'(R+1)^4\delta^{-4}t_{f_i}.
\]
Its product is bounded by (19), since \(\delta\le1\) and \(n^p\ge1\). This includes boards finer than the lattice.

Choose the fixed integer \(k=\lceil p\rceil+5\). Since
\(\log n!=\sum_{j=1}^n\log j\ge\int_1^n\log x\,dx\ge n\log n-n\),
we have \(n^{(p+5)n}\le e^{kn}(n!)^k\). Enlarge \(C\) once, including (8)'s polynomial one-point constant, and set \(B(R)=C(R+1)^4\). Then
\[
 \boxed{\left|\rho\prod_{i=1}^n[\mathcal O_a(f_i)-\nu(\mathcal O_a(f_i))]\right|
 \le B(R)^n(n!)^k\max(1,d^{-5n})\prod_i t_{f_i}.}
 \tag{20}
\]
For \(n=1\), (8) proves this with no separation condition and with the maximum replaced by one.

For cumulants of order \(n\ge2\), center at \(\rho\) and use the logarithm partition identity from Section 3. A block \(D\) contributes at most \(B(R)^{|D|}(|D|!)^k\bar d^{-5|D|}\prod_{i\in D}t_{f_i}\). Because \(\prod_D|D|!\le n!\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_\pi(|\pi|-1)!\prod_{D\in\pi}(|D|!)^k
 &\le(n!)^{k-1}\sum_{m=1}^n\frac{n!}{m}\binom{n-1}{m-1}\\
 &\le2^{n-1}(n!)^k.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{21}
\]
For the equality used in the first line, order the \(m\) blocks and choose their positive sizes. There are \(\binom{n-1}{m-1}\) compositions. For each composition, assigning labels and multiplying by block factorials gives \(n!\). Divide by \(m!\) and multiply by \((m-1)!\). This proves (21) without an extra factorial. Hence, writing \(S^T_{n,\rho}[\nu]\) for cumulants of the fields centered at \(\nu\),
\[
 \boxed{|S^T_{n,\rho}[\nu](f_1,\ldots,f_n)|
 \le[2B(R)]^n(n!)^k\max\{1,d^{-5n}\}\prod_i\|f_i\|_\sharp.}
 \tag{22}
\]
The first cumulant is the mean difference (8); higher cumulants do not depend on \(\nu\). This is D2 for every pair of periodic Wilson states, including mixtures.

## 6. Four-dimensional changes and the first missing input

The spatial dimension changes these counts: \(N=(\ell/l)^4\), boundary size \(O((\ell/a)^3)\), eight walk directions, and sixteen cubes per vertex. It changes the local density to \(a_s^{-4}\), the scale sum in (9) to \(d^{-4}\), and the one-point plaquette bound to \(a^4\). In (19), four-dimensional partition counting costs \(\delta^{-4}\), while the sharp norm costs \(\delta^{-1}\). Their product gives \(d^{-5n}\). In three dimensions those two losses would give \(d^{-4n}\). Reflection Cauchy–Schwarz, DLR comparison, holomorphic normalization, and the partition identities otherwise keep the same arguments.

The first insufficient input **if D1 is weakened to ST6 alone** occurs at (13). ST6 gives \(C a_s^{-4}\), instead of a constant, when \(\delta\le a_s<\eta\). Its scale sum can grow as \(\delta^{-4}\). Exponentiation then gives \(e^{C\delta^{-4}}\), which does not supply \(Q_i\le Cn^p\). This is why D1's actual rooted indexing and entropy gap must remain explicit.

With full D1 and the stated lattice inputs, no step of this implication fails. D1 itself remains unproved here. In its upstream floor and upper-bound construction, row 170 and [d1-floor.md](d1-floor.md) identify C1/OH1 first: positivity and exactness of the history representation. This note neither proves nor refutes that input.

## Result

**Conditional on full D1, S1e and S1g, D2, D4 and G4 follow.** Equations (20)–(22) give one fixed \(k=\lceil p\rceil+5\) and \(B(R)=C(R+1)^4\), uniformly in cutoff, states and support positions. Equations (7)–(10) give common cell normalizers, all-state one-point differences and separated cumulant bounds. This supplies the implications required at skeleton S2 and S3, including the D2 premise used by [growth-qf3.md](growth-qf3.md).

First failing step within these named premises: none. First unproved upstream input: D1, beginning with C1/OH1 in the cited construction. Proved by Codex as a research implication; not yet checked by the other model. No audit or scientific status is granted.
