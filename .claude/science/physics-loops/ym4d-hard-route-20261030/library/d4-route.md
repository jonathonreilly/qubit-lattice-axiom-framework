## 4. First step: a stopped factor survives real record extension

This writes out the contraction part of SA 17831–17838 for a drop-out with its Wilson factor attached.
It does not assume complex stage admission.
The one-failed-test restriction is already explained at SA 18178–18186.
Here the comparison also allows any number of added records and normalized auxiliary integrations.

Fix a birth record \(\omega\), stored variables \(\xi\), and support \(S_\omega\).
Write the SA fibre and its nonnegative retained factor as
\[
 K_\xi=\mathfrak K^\sharp(\omega,\xi),\qquad
 E_\xi(e)=e^{-A(\upsilon1_X,U)}K_\omega(\xi;e),\qquad
 m(\xi)=1_{S_\omega}(\xi)\sup_{e\in K_\xi}E_\xi(e).
 \tag{1}
\]
The empty supremum is zero.
The fibre and function remain those fixed at birth.

A **real record extension** keeps the same \(\xi\) and a single real witness \(U\).
It retains these four birth requirements:

1. Every determining average equals its stored value, including the stored bare field.
2. Layer regularity holds. Each test has its recorded value, except the stopping test.
3. Every inner-island read belongs to that island's real birth domain.
4. The complete kept forms, Jacobians and Wilson weights satisfy their birth bounds.

It may add tests, including the stopping failure, and further real variables.
The Wilson floor remains \(\upsilon(p)\ge c\theta_{t(p)}\), with \(t(p)\) from the birth action sequence.
It evaluates the same complete retained factor.
It does not replace that factor by a product of separately optimized pieces.

**Claim.** Such extensions do not increase the birth mass.
This also holds under positive kernels of mass at most one on the added variables.
It holds uniformly in the number of successive extensions.

**Proof.** Forget the added record and variables.
Requirement 1 gives exactly SA's compatibility equations for the same \(U\).
Requirement 2 gives the birth regularity and tests.
Requirements 3–4 give the remaining birth clauses.
Thus the forgotten evaluation belongs to \(K_\xi\).
Adding the stopping failure restricts this set; it does not enlarge it.
Therefore, pointwise on \(S_\omega\),
\[
 0\le E_\xi(e)\le m(\xi).                                    \tag{2}
\]
No comparison of two backgrounds enters this inequality.

Let \(P_\xi\) be any positive kernel of mass at most one, supported on these extensions.
Integrating (2) gives
\[
 1_{S_\omega}(\xi)\int E_\xi(e)\,dP_\xi(e)\le m(\xi).          \tag{3}
\]
A finite composition of these kernels still has mass at most one.
Integrate its last conditional kernel and use its mass bound; repeat backwards.
Hence (3) has the same constant after any finite number of extensions.
Let \(m_{\rm ext}(\xi)\) be the supremum of (3)'s left side over the permitted rules.
Then \(m_{\rm ext}\le m\), with value zero when no extension exists.

Keep the birth reserve \(v_\omega(\xi)\) and measure \(\mu_\omega\) fixed.
Multiplication by \(e^{c v_\omega(\xi)}\) preserves (3).
Every Borel majorant of its right side also majorizes its left side.
Taking integrals and then their infimum gives
\[
 \int^{*} e^{c v_\omega}m_{\rm ext}\,d\mu_\omega
 \le \int^{*} e^{c v_\omega}m\,d\mu_\omega.                    \tag{4}
\]
The unchanged frame average preserves this inequality.
This is the drop-out version of no growth under real record extension. \(\square\)

There is a concrete class of such kernels.
Keep a birth evaluation fixed and form each further read as a specified average of its real field.
Record a binary test \(T\) with weights \(1_{T=0}\) and \(1_{T=1}\).
Their sum is one, so recording all outcomes preserves mass exactly.
Selecting an outcome gives a kernel of mass at most one.
This construction uses no new background solve.
It does not identify the actual remade-shell read with that frozen-field read.

The proof grants no new rarity gain for the stopping failure.
It charges no new component costs to the old reserve.
An unnormalized sum over future histories needs its own estimate.
The birth mass bound still needs the carried-Wilson ledger.
It is not a new free assumption in the island class.
