### The continuation argument itself works

Here is the full finite-lattice argument, conditional on its stated seed and improvement.
It does not require a continuous choice of gauge.

Fix one complete read $`E`$, including every endpoint and difference pair.
Fix $`0<a<\pi/4`$.
For a unitary reference $`V`$, let $`V\in\mathcal A_r(E)`$ mean that one gauge and potentials satisfy

```math
V^u=e^{i\eta A},\qquad
\max\left\{\eta\max_{b\in E}|A(b)|,
\eta^2\max_{(b,b')\in E}|\nabla A(b,b')|\right\}\le r.
```

Let $`V(t)`$ be continuous on $`[0,1]`$ with this fixed read.
Suppose $`V(0)\in\mathcal A_a(E)`$ and

```math
V(t)\in\mathcal A_{2a}(E)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad V(t)\in\mathcal A_a(E).
```

Put $`S=\{t:V(t)\in\mathcal A_{2a}(E)\}`$.
This set is closed.
Indeed, for a convergent sequence in $`S`$, take its witnessing gauges and potentials.
There are finitely many sites and bonds.
The gauges lie in a finite product of compact groups.
The potentials lie in a closed bounded subset of a finite-dimensional real space.
A subsequence therefore converges.
The exponential identities and both non-strict bounds pass to the limit.

The improvement makes $`S`$ relatively open.
At a point of $`S`$, keep a witness with radius $`a`$ fixed.
Each gauged link has its principal logarithm in the open radius-$`\pi`$ chart.
The logarithm is continuous there.
For nearby $`t`$, these logarithms and their finitely many differences remain below $`2a`$.
The same gauge is a witness.
Thus $`S`$ is nonempty, closed and open in the interval, so $`S=[0,1]`$.
The improvement gives the radius-$`a`$ conclusion everywhere.

Here is the quantitative improvement that the proposed shift route would need.
Take a fixed bare box $`R\supset E`$ with all plaquettes used by its comb gauge.
Let $`K_R`$ be its fixed plaquette-count constant from `pi-admit`, section 5.1.
Uniform application requires a bound on $`K_R`$ fixed before the source point and history.
Use the already proved estimates (9)–(11), without rebuilding them.
If on the entire box, with $`h_t`$ real,

```math
V(t)=e^{i\eta h_t}U_*,\qquad
\sup_{P\subset R}|\partial U_*(P)-1|\le\epsilon_*,
```

they give a gauge with radius at most

```math
4K_R\left(\epsilon_*+4\eta\|h_t\|_{\infty,R}\right).
```

For example, sufficient bounds on the radius-$`2a`$ set are

```math
\epsilon_*\le\frac{a}{8K_R},\qquad
\eta\|h_t\|_{\infty,R}\le\frac{a}{32K_R}.
```

Their sum gives radius at most $`a`$.
The same choice keeps the logarithms in the small chart when $`a<1`$.
This estimate needs the actual shift on every link of $`R`$.

### What fixed values imply exactly

This is a bond set, not merely the geometric exterior of an open region.
Fixing a coarse average alone does not put each of its fine bonds in $`F`$.
Suppose the inherited reference is $`U_*`$ and the localized minimizer is $`V`$.
Write their prescribed values on $`F`$ as $`D_*`$ and $`D_Z`$.
At every $`b\in F`$, in the same prescribed endpoint frame,

```math
V(b)U_*(b)^{-1}=D_Z(b)D_*(b)^{-1}.
```

If the right side is in the small logarithm chart, the actual relative shift is

```math
\eta h(b)=-i\log\bigl(D_Z(b)D_*(b)^{-1}\bigr).
```
