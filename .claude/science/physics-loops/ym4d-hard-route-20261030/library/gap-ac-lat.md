## 1. The statement, and what kind of statement it is

Notation of `library/gap-skeleton-max.md` 3.1. Lambda(n, M) = (Z/n)^2 x (Z/M)^2, coordinates (x_1, x_2 | x_3, x_4); the first factor is the fibre, the second the base. For a set S of plaquettes, A_S(U) = sum over p not in S of (1 - (1/2) tr U_p) + sum over p in S of (1 + (1/2) tr U_p), and Z_S = int exp(-beta A_S) dU. For a point c = (a, b) of the fibre, P_c is the column of (1,2)-plaquettes based at (a, b, y), y in the base. c_0 = (n-1, n-1), A^tw = A_{P_{c_0}}, Z_tw = Z_{P_{c_0}}, Z_0 = Z_{empty set}, mu^tw = Z_tw^{-1} exp(-beta A^tw) dU, mu_0 the untwisted measure.

**(AC'-lat).** m(beta, n) is the supremum of the m such that for all M and all gauge-invariant F, G with values in [-1, 1] that depend on the links over base sets X, Y: |mu^tw(FG) - mu^tw(F) mu^tw(G)| <= C(F, G, beta, n) exp(-m dist(X, Y)). Claim: there is beta_1 with inf over n >= 2 of m(beta, n) > 0 for every beta >= beta_1.

Two tools used throughout.

**Flip lemma (proved).** For a set L of links let phi_L multiply U_l by -1 for l in L. It preserves Haar measure, commutes with gauge transformations, and A_S o phi_L = A_{S xor d*L}, where d*L is the set of plaquettes that contain an odd number of links of L. For a != a'' in Z/n and b in Z/n let L be the set of links from (a', b, y) to (a', b+1, y) with a' in the arc a+1, ..., a'' and y in the base. Then d*L = P_{(a,b)} union P_{(a'',b)}.
Proof. -1 is central, so each plaquette trace is multiplied by (-1)^(number of its links in L). A link of L at (a', b, y) lies in the (1,2)-plaquettes based at (a'-1, b, y) and (a', b, y), and in the (2,3)- and (2,4)-plaquettes based at (a', b, y) and (a', b, y - e_j), j = 3, 4. A (2,j)-plaquette based at (a', b, y) contains the two links of L at y and y + e_j: even. A (1,2)-plaquette based at (a', b, y) contains the links of L at a' and a'+1: both for a < a' < a'', one for a' = a and for a' = a''. QED

Consequences (with the same lemma for the direction 2): Z_{P_c} = Z_tw for every column c; Z_{P_c union P_c'} = Z_0 for c != c'; Z_{P_c union P_c' union P_c''} = Z_tw for three different columns. The map W_1 = (flip of the links of direction 2 at x_2 = n-1, x_1 = 0) o (translation by e_1) preserves mu^tw, and on a function whose support does not meet the flipped links it acts as the translation. The same for W_2.

**Twist observable.** tau_c(U) = exp(-beta sum over p in P_c of tr U_p). It is positive, gauge invariant, and exp(-beta A_S) tau_c = exp(-beta A_{S xor P_c}). So mu^tw(tau_c) = Z_0 / Z_tw for c != c_0, and mu^tw(tau_c tau_c') = 1 for different c, c' != c_0.

### 1.1 Lemma A: (AC'-lat) contains the mass gap of the four-dimensional lattice theory at weak coupling

**Lemma A (proved, given the port named in step 1).** Let beta > 0 and m_0 = inf over n >= 2 of m(beta, n) > 0. Then there is a Gibbs measure mu for Wilson's SU(2) action on Z^4 at this beta which is invariant under all translations, reflection positive in the directions 3 and 4, and such that for all bounded gauge-invariant local F, G with F supported in {x_3 <= 0} and G in {x_3 >= t}: |mu(FG) - mu(F) mu(G)| <= sup|F| sup|G| exp(-m_0 t). So the transfer operator of mu in the direction 3, on the gauge-invariant space, has a unique invariant vector and norm at most exp(-m_0) on its complement.

Proof.
1. Fix n. The measures mu^tw on Lambda(n, M), M even, are reflection positive for the reflections in x_3 (the twisted plaquettes lie inside the slices x_3 = const and only change the slice potential V; this is the port of `library/gap-transfer.md` Statement (1) to the slice (Z/n)^2 x Z/M with the potential V + twist, **not rechecked here**; the original is Osterwalder-Seiler). They are invariant under base translations. Let omega_n be a weak limit point as M tends to infinity, as a measure on the links of (Z/n)^2 x Z^2. It is reflection positive in x_3 and invariant under base translations. For every m < m(beta, n) and gauge-invariant local F, G it satisfies the bound of (AC'-lat) with the same constant, because the constant does not depend on M.
2. Constant one. Let H be the Hilbert space of omega_n built from the gauge-invariant local functions of the links in {x_3 >= 0} with (F, G) = omega_n(theta(conj F) G), Omega the class of 1, T the class map of the translation by one step in x_3. T is self-adjoint with norm at most 1 (the Osterwalder-Schrader construction; rebuilt in `library/gap-os-equiv.md`, row 668, not read here). For f = [F] - omega_n(F) Omega and even t, (f, T^t f) = int lambda^t d nu_f(lambda) with nu_f the spectral measure on [-1, 1]. By step 1, (f, T^t f) <= C exp(-m t). If nu_f gave mass q > 0 to {|lambda| >= exp(-m) + eps}, then (f, T^t f) >= q (exp(-m) + eps)^t for even t, a contradiction for large t. So nu_f lives on |lambda| <= exp(-m) for every m < m(beta, n), the vectors f are dense in the complement of Omega, and ||T restricted to that complement|| <= exp(-m(beta, n)) <= exp(-m_0). Hence |omega_n(theta(conj F) tau_t G) - omega_n(conj F) omega_n(G)| <= ||f|| ||g|| exp(-m_0 t) <= sup|F| sup|G| exp(-m_0 t), with no constant that depends on n.
3. Let n tend to infinity. Translate omega_n in the fibre so that the origin is at distance at least n/2 - 1 from the column c_0 and from the seams x_1 = n-1, x_2 = n-1, and read it as a measure on the links of the box of fibre side n/2 around the origin times Z^2. The space of probability measures on SU(2)^(links of Z^4) is weakly compact; take a limit point mu along a subsequence. (a) Gibbs: for a finite box B and a local continuous f, omega_n(f) = omega_n(pi_B f) as soon as no plaquette that meets B lies in P_{c_0}, where pi_B is Wilson's specification, a continuous kernel that depends on finitely many links; pass to the limit. (b) Translations: base translations are exact symmetries. For e_1: omega_n is invariant under W_1, and W_1 acts as the translation on functions supported away from the seam, so mu(f o translation) = mu(f) for local f. The same for e_2. (c) Reflection positivity in x_3 and x_4 and the bound of step 2 are closed conditions on finitely supported functions; they pass to the limit. QED

Reading. For every beta >= beta_1, (AC'-lat) gives a translation-invariant Gibbs state of four-dimensional SU(2) lattice gauge theory with a mass gap at least m_0(beta) in two directions. As far as I know (memory, **unchecked**) no such state is known at any large beta: the gap is proved at strong coupling only. So (AC'-lat) is not a statement about thin tori. It is at least the lattice mass gap at weak coupling, plus a statement about every thin torus. The second part is made exact in 3.3 and 3.4 below.

### 1.2 Lemma G: the Gaussian model of the twist (a filter for every route)

The tree-level content of the twist is: three real massless lattice fields on the fibre with the periodicities (periodic, antiperiodic), (antiperiodic, periodic), (antiperiodic, antiperiodic) (`library/gap-skeleton-max.md` 3.4, Fact 3). Take one of them as a model.

**Model G_n.** phi is a real field on Z^2 x Z^2 with phi(x + n e_1) = -phi(x) and phi(x + n e_2) = phi(x); the measure is the centred Gaussian with covariance (-Delta)^{-1}, Delta the lattice Laplacian of Z^4. It exists because -Delta >= (2 sin(pi/2n))^2 on this space. It has range one, it is reflection positive in the base directions with a positive transfer operator, the field with fibre n is the sector of the field with fibre 3n that changes sign under the translation by n e_1 and is invariant under the translation by n e_2, and for n tending to infinity it converges locally to the massless free field on Z^4.

**Lemma G (proved).** Let mu_n = 2 arcsinh(sin(pi/2n)). In G_n no decay rate above mu_n holds in the base: there are bounded local functions F, G_t at base distance t with covariance at least c t^{-1/2} exp(-mu_n t). Moreover n mu_n < pi for every n, n mu_n tends to pi, and mu_{3n} < 0.55 mu_n for every n >= 2. (The rate mu_n does hold, by Wick's theorem; this half is not used and not written out.)
Proof. Fourier transform in the fibre: momenta k_1 in (2 pi/n)(Z + 1/2), k_2 in (2 pi/n) Z, w_k^2 = 4 sin^2(k_1/2) + 4 sin^2(k_2/2). With q the momentum in x_4 and t the separation in x_3, the covariance of two fields at fibre points u, u' and base separation (t, 0) is (1/n^2) sum_k e^{ik(u-u')} int dq/(2 pi) e^{-mu(k,q)|t|} / (2 sinh mu(k, q)), where 2 cosh mu(k,q) - 2 = w_k^2 + 4 sin^2(q/2) (the one-dimensional lattice Green function). mu(k, q) is minimal at k = (+-pi/n, 0), q = 0, where cosh mu = 1 + 2 sin^2(pi/2n), that is mu = mu_n. Take X = sum_u cos(pi u_1/n) phi(u, 0, 0) and Y the same at the base point (t, 0): Cov(X, Y) = (n^2/2) int dq/(2 pi) e^{-mu(q) t}/(2 sinh mu(q)) with mu(q) = mu((pi/n, 0), q) = mu_n + O(q^2), which is at least c t^{-1/2} e^{-mu_n t}. For the bounded functions sin X, sin Y: E sin X = 0 and E(sin X sin Y) = exp(-(Var X + Var Y)/2) sinh(Cov(X, Y)) >= exp(-Var X) Cov(X, Y). So no rate above mu_n holds. arcsinh x < x and sin y < y give mu_n < pi/n, and the limit is clear. Last claim: mu_{3n} < pi/(3n); sin(pi/2n) >= 1/n and arcsinh x >= x - x^3/6 give mu_n >= (2/n)(1 - 1/(6n^2)) >= (2/n)(23/24) for n >= 2; so mu_{3n}/mu_n < (pi/3)(24/46) < 0.55. QED

Use. G_n has every structural property that routes (i) and (ii) use (reflection positivity in the base, positive transfer operators, range one, the sector embedding of n in 3n, a gap of order pi/n at each n), and the conclusion of (AC'-lat) is false for it: inf_n mu_n = 0. So a proof of (AC'-lat) along (i) or (ii) must use something that G_n does not have. The twist does not give it: G_n is the twist at tree level.

## 2. Route (i): monotonicity in n by reflection positivity in a fibre direction

Hinge of the route. **(SC)** For beta >= beta_1 and n >= 2: m(beta, 3n) >= m(beta, n) - eps(beta, n), with sum over k of eps(beta, 3^k n) < m(beta, n). (A bound of the form m(beta, 3n) >= c m(beta, n) with c < 1 is of no use: it gives m(beta, 3^k n) >= c^k m(beta, n).) The sector statement behind it is true: in the frame where V is trivial, a configuration with fibre n is a field on Z^2 x base with U(x + n e_i) = Gamma_i^{-1} U(x) Gamma_i, and since Gamma_i^3 = -Gamma_i it is a configuration with fibre 3n and the twist that is invariant under the translations by n e_i followed by the conjugation with Gamma_i.

(SC) failed in three ways.

### 2.1 First way: mu^tw is not reflection positive in a fibre direction

Let n >= 4 be even. Let r(x_1) = n - 1 - x_1 on Z/n and let theta be the reflection of configurations that it induces: (theta U)_j(x) = U_j(r x) for j != 1, and (theta U)_1(x) = U_1(r x - e_1)^{-1}. Its two fixed planes x_1 = -1/2 and x_1 = n/2 - 1/2 cut links. theta maps the column P_{c_0} to itself, so A^tw o theta = A^tw and mu^tw is theta-invariant. The positive side is x_1 in {0, ..., n/2 - 1}.

**Lemma B (proved).** (a) For all beta > 0, n >= 2 and M: Z_tw < Z_0. (b) Let c = (0, n-1) and F_t = 1 - t tau_c, t real. F_t is a bounded gauge-invariant function of the links of the positive side, and mu^tw(theta(F_t) F_t) = 1 - 2 t Z_0/Z_tw + t^2, which is negative at t = 1. So mu^tw is not reflection positive with respect to theta.
Proof of (b) from (a). tau_c uses the plaquettes based at x_1 = 0, with corners at x_1 = 0, 1, inside the positive side because n >= 4. theta tau_c = tau_c' with c' = (n-2, n-1), and c, c', c_0 are three different columns. By section 1, mu^tw(tau_c) = mu^tw(tau_c') = Z_0/Z_tw and mu^tw(tau_c tau_c') = 1. QED
Proof of (a). Take x_1 as time. The slice is Sigma = (Z/n) x (Z/M)^2 with coordinates (x_2, x_3, x_4); a configuration is a family of slice configurations u_t in X = SU(2)^(links of Sigma) and of time-like links w_t(x), t in Z/n. Let V(u) be the sum of 1 - (1/2) tr over the plaquettes of Sigma, k(g) = exp(-beta(1 - (1/2) tr g)), and
K(u, v) = int prod_x dw(x) prod over links l = (x, j) of Sigma of k(u(l) w(x + e_j) v(l)^{-1} w(x)^{-1}), T(u, v) = e^{-beta V(u)/2} K(u, v) e^{-beta V(v)/2}.
T is continuous and symmetric on the compact space X x X; let T also denote the integral operator on L^2(X). Integrating the time-like links, Z_0 = int prod_t du_t prod_t T(u_t, u_{t+1}) = Tr T^n (n >= 2; a product of two Hilbert-Schmidt operators with continuous kernels). Let zeta flip the sign of the links of direction 2 of Sigma with x_2 = n-1, and (V_z f)(u) = f(zeta u). The twisted plaquettes P_{c_0} are the time-like plaquettes between the slices n-1 and 0 over exactly these links, so twisting them replaces T(u_{n-1}, u_0) by T(u_{n-1}, zeta u_0), and Z_tw = Tr(T^n V_z). A plaquette of Sigma contains none or two of the flipped links, and in K the signs of u(l) and v(l) cancel, so T(zeta u, zeta v) = T(u, v): V_z is a unitary involution that commutes with T. Hence Z_0 - Z_tw = 2 Tr(T^n Q), Q = (1 - V_z)/2 the projection on the odd functions.
Positivity. With the gauge action (h.v)(l) = h(x) v(l) h(x + e_j)^{-1}, K = int dw C R_w, where C is the convolution operator on the group SU(2)^(links) with the kernel prod_l k(u(l) v(l)^{-1}) and R_w f(v) = f(w.v). k is a class function with character expansion k = sum_j c_j chi_j, c_j = 2(2j+1) e^{-beta} I_{2j+1}(beta)/beta > 0. So C is positive and injective, it commutes with every R_w, and K = C P = P C P with P = int R_w dw the projection on the gauge-invariant functions. Multiplication by e^{-beta V/2} is bounded with bounded inverse and commutes with P. So T >= 0, T vanishes on the complement of Ran P, and (f, T f) > 0 for every non-zero f in Ran P. Q commutes with T and P. The space Ran P intersect Ran Q is not zero: psi(u) = tr of the product of the links of direction 2 along the cycle x_2 = 0, ..., n-1 at a fixed (x_3, x_4) is gauge invariant, not zero, and contains exactly one flipped link, so psi o zeta = -psi. T restricted to this space is compact, self-adjoint, positive and injective, so it has a positive eigenvalue lambda, and Tr(T^n Q) >= lambda^n > 0. QED

(The coefficient formula c_j is recalled, not rederived; only c_j > 0 is used, and it follows from the positivity of the Taylor coefficients of exp((beta/2) tr g) in the characters, since powers of tr g are positive combinations of characters.)

Remark (not proved here). The same computation shows what is reflection positive: Z_0 + Z_tw = 2 Tr(T^n (1 + V_z)/2), so the mixture (Z_0 mu_0 + Z_tw mu^tw)/(Z_0 + Z_tw), the even sector of the untwisted transfer operator, is the natural reflection-positive object in the fibre direction, and Z_0 mu_0 - Z_tw mu^tw is the odd sector. mu^tw alone is their difference. This is Lemma 4's "Z_0 +- Z_n = 2 Tr_{even/odd}" read in a fibre direction. A positivity statement on the mixture says nothing about m(beta, n): the untwisted component has weight above 1/2 by Lemma B(a), and it is the theory that run 1 discarded because it has a transition (1.F).

So the tool named in the brief does not exist for the twisted measure: every chessboard or Schwarz inequality in a fibre direction is an inequality for the untwisted torus or for the mixture.

### 2.2 Second way: where reflection positivity holds, the blocks it compares are not twisted tori

A chessboard estimate with blocks of fibre side n needs a fibre of side 2 k n (an even number of blocks in each direction, so that every block face is one of a pair of antipodal reflection planes). On a fibre of side 3n the three planes x_1 = 0, n, 2n are not of this kind: the plane antipodal to x_1 = 0 is x_1 = 3n/2. On a fibre of side 2kn the lift of a configuration with fibre n and the twist has period 2n without conjugation (Gamma_i^2 = -1 is central), so it lives on the untwisted torus, where reflection positivity holds (Osterwalder-Seiler; here by the proof of Lemma B(a) without V_z). But the configurations that a chessboard estimate produces from a block are the ones invariant under the reflections in the block faces: U_j(x) = U_j(r x). The lift of a twisted configuration is invariant under translation by n followed by conjugation with Gamma_i. The two symmetry groups are different (reflections generate the translations by 2n, with no colour rotation), and their fixed sets are different: a reflection-symmetric configuration restricted to one block is a block with mirror boundary conditions, and its partition function is not Z_tw(n). I found no identity or inequality that relates the two; this is F.2 item 4 of run 1 ("positivity gives no inequality between a sector and the whole") made specific. This way is a failure to find, not a proof of impossibility.

### 2.3 Third way: (SC) is false in the Gaussian model of the twist

By Lemma G, in G_n the decay rate satisfies mu_{3n} < 0.55 mu_n for every n, and mu_n tends to zero. So (SC) fails in G_n at every n, with eps = 0.45 mu_n, whose sum over the tripling sequence exceeds every bound: the sequence loses all of the gap. G_n has the sector embedding of n in 3n, and reflection positivity in the base. (In its antiperiodic fibre direction G_n fails reflection positivity for the same reason as mu^tw: its partition function is Tr(T^n V) with V the parity phi -> -phi, and the covariance of phi at x_1 = a and at its mirror image is negative for n/4 < a < n/2. So G_n shares 2.1 as well.) So no argument that uses only positivity and the sector embedding can prove (SC).

For mu^tw itself the same is expected at weak coupling: for beta >= beta_0(3n) the lowest excitation is one quantum of X with fibre momentum (pi/3n, 0), and m(beta, 3n) is near mu_{3n} < 0.55 mu_n. I did not prove an upper bound on m(beta, 3n): it needs the Gaussian asymptotics of T2 (row `gap-twist-lat`), which give at present only a lower bound. Label: **expected, not proved**. If it holds, (SC) is false for mu^tw whenever beta >= beta_0(3n), and (SC) can only be true for n above a threshold n_c(beta) that grows with beta, which is where the theory is already four-dimensional.

**Verdict on (i).** Not obtained. The positivity that the route names is absent for mu^tw (2.1, proved); where positivity holds it compares mirror blocks, not twisted tori (2.2); and the monotone statement is false in the model that shares all the structure (2.3, proved for G_n). What is left of the route is nothing that I can state as a lemma.

## 3. Route (ii): stability, from one n to cubes of side of order n

Hinge of the route. **(FS)** There are kappa_0 and c > 0 such that, if n m(beta, n) >= kappa_0 for one n, then the hypothesis (D) of the criterion P1-next holds for cubes of side R = c n: the laws induced on the inner half-cube by any two boundary conditions on a cube of side R of Z^4 differ by at most delta_4 in total variation. (P1-next, `gap-ds-gauge`, is another statement and is not attacked here.)

(FS) failed in three ways.

### 3.1 First way: the threshold is above everything the weak-coupling theorem can supply

By Lemma G, the Gaussian model of the twist has n mu_n in (1.9, pi) for every n >= 2 (mu_n >= (2/n)(23/24)), with limit pi, and its limit for n to infinity is the massless free field, for which no cube satisfies anything like (D) and no decay holds. So an implication of the form (FS), if it is proved by means that apply to G_n (range one, reflection positivity in the base, covariance decay in the torus state), must have kappa_0 >= pi.

For mu^tw the hypothesis is then out of reach of T2: T2 gives n m(beta, n) >= c with an absolute c that cannot exceed the tree value n mu_n < pi (if the Gaussian asymptotics hold; **expected, not proved**, as in 2.3). So in the region where anything is proved or looks provable the hypothesis of (FS) is not satisfied, and the first (beta, n) with n m(beta, n) >= kappa_0 > pi lies in the region where the coupling at the scale of the fibre is of order one. (FS) does not cross that region; it starts behind it. What it would give is: (AC'-lat) at beta follows from one n with n m(beta, n) >= kappa_0. The statement "for every beta >= beta_1 there is such an n" is: the dimensionless gap leaves its tree value pi and grows without bound in n. I have no handle on it; the first-order form is the sign of c_1 in n m = pi(1 + c_1/beta + ...), the guiding calculation (i) at the end of Part 4 of run 1, which is perturbative and so cannot reach kappa_0.

G_n is non-compact, so it is not literally in the class of the criterion (compact fibre). The filter is used only for the size of kappa_0, and the three fields of the tree-level twist are what the compact model looks like for beta >= beta_0(n).

### 3.2 Second way: decay in one state without boundary is not a bound over all boundary conditions

m(beta, n) is a property of one measure on a lattice without boundary. (D) is a supremum over all boundary conditions on a cube. Three things separate them, and for none of them do I have an argument.

(a) In the base. Covariance decay in the periodic state does not bound the influence of a boundary condition on a square of the base. For two-dimensional systems with finite fibre the step from "influence of the boundary decays, uniformly in the boundary condition" (weak mixing) to strong mixing in squares is a theorem (Martinelli, Olivieri and Schonmann 1994; memory, **not read**), but its hypothesis is already uniform in the boundary condition, its constants depend on the fibre, and here the fibre is SU(2)^(4 n^2) with n growing.

(b) In the fibre. The cube of (D) has faces in the directions 1 and 2 with arbitrary boundary values. mu^tw closes these directions in one way, by the twist. A boundary value on a fibre face with plaquette defects of size delta, with beta delta large and delta small, moves the minimiser of the action in the whole cube by an amount of order delta times a harmonic profile, while the fluctuations per link are of order beta^{-1/2}; so two such boundary values give laws on the inner half-cube at total variation distance near 1 as long as the cube is below the correlation length. This is the usual reason why (D) can hold only at R above the correlation length (heuristic; the rigorous form is the conclusion of P1-next itself, read backwards: (D) at R gives a rate c/R, so R >= c/m). It shows that (FS) can only be true with kappa_0 large enough that the fibre is already longer than the correlation length, in agreement with 3.1, and that inside the cube the twist plays no role: the argument for (D) would be an argument about the four-dimensional theory with arbitrary boundary values.

(c) The class of (D) is closed under the twist, which is the one exact fact here. **Lemma C (proved).** For a cube Q in Lambda(n, M) with n at least the side of Q plus 4, the specification of mu^tw in Q is conjugate to Wilson's by a flip: there is a set L of links with d*L intersect {plaquettes that meet Q} = P_{c_0} intersect {plaquettes that meet Q}, and the law of mu^tw in Q given the boundary value w is the image under phi_L of Wilson's law in Q given phi_L w. Hence the supremum over pairs of boundary values of the total variation distance on the inner cube is the same for the two specifications. Proof: take for L the links of direction 2 at x_2 = n-1, x_1 = a' for a' from c_0 to a column outside the plaquettes that meet Q, as in the flip lemma; phi_L is a measure-preserving bijection of the configurations in Q and of the boundary values, and exp(-beta A^tw) o phi_L = exp(-beta A_S) with S free of plaquettes that meet Q. QED

### 3.3 Third way: even if (FS) and P1-next hold, the thin tori below the scale R are not covered

P1-next gives decay at rate c/R on lattices that contain the cube with room to spare. With Lemma C it would give m(beta, n) >= c/R for n >= 2R (if P1-next is proved in a form that covers tori and uses only the specification in cubes of side R; **that form is not stated in the brief of P1-next**). It gives nothing for the finitely many n < 2R at the same beta. Each of these is a two-dimensional system with a compact fibre at a coupling that is neither weak (beta < beta_0(n)) nor strong, and for it the question m(beta, n) > 0 is the question of run 1's warning 2.10: no breaking of the cyclic symmetry of order 2n generated by the twisted translation W_i, and no other transition. Nothing in the route addresses it.

**Verdict on (ii).** Not obtained. The route has no starting point (3.1: the threshold is at or above the tree value pi, proved on G_n), its central implication compares one closed lattice with all boundary values of a cube and has no proof and no method (3.2), and its conclusion leaves out the thin tori below the criterion's scale (3.3).

### 3.4 What (ii) leaves, as an exact statement

**Proposition D (conditional; the two hypotheses are P1-next in torus form and are not proved).** Fix beta. Suppose (D) holds for Wilson's specification at beta for one R, and suppose the criterion P1-next holds in this form: (D) at R implies, on every torus with all sides at least 2R and for every action that is conjugate to Wilson's by flips in every cube of side R, the bound |Cov(F, G)| <= C(F, G) exp(-c dist/R) for local F, G, with C independent of the torus. Then m(beta, n) >= c/R for all n >= 2R. So at this beta, (AC'-lat) follows from (D) together with: m(beta, n) > 0 for each of the finitely many n with 2 <= n < 2R.
Proof. Lemma C gives the hypothesis for mu^tw on Lambda(n, M), n, M >= 2R; for M < 2R the distance is bounded and the bound is trivial with a larger constant, which is finite in number of cases. QED

In the other direction, Lemma A: (AC'-lat) at beta gives a four-dimensional Gibbs state with a gap. It does not give (D) or uniqueness (run 1, node N7). So at the level of the lattice the crux of Route T is the finite form (D) of the gap, minus uniqueness, plus the absence of a transition on each thin torus below the scale R(beta). The second part is an obligation that the finite form (D) and Route Z do not have.

## 4. Route (iii): an a priori estimate in beta at fixed n

Frame of run 1, 1.D, on X = SU(2)^(links of Lambda(n, M)) with the metric |X|^2 = -2 tr X^2 on each link, A = A^tw, mu_b = mu^tw at beta = b, L_b = -Delta + b <grad A, grad .> on gauge-invariant functions, gamma(b) = gamma(b; n, M) its lowest non-zero eigenvalue, f_b a gap mode. Lemmas 1 and 2 of 1.D are used as stated there (**not checked here; their check is row 682**).

Hinge of the route. **(TU)** There are b_s > 0 and c such that for all n >= 2, all M and almost every b >= b_s: <f_b (-Delta) f_b> <= (1 + 2 c b) <|grad f_b|^2>. With Lemma 2 it gives gamma(b; n, M) >= gamma(b_s; n, M) exp(-2c(b - b_s)). The route needs two more statements, which I did not prove and which are not the crux: a start, gamma(b_s; n, M) >= gamma_s > 0 for all n, M (expected from the Bakry-Emery bound gamma >= inf (Ric + b Hess A) = 1/2 - C b, since the Ricci curvature of each link is 1/2 in this metric and Hess A is bounded by a constant that does not depend on the lattice; **recalled, not rebuilt**), and an end, m(beta, n) >= g(gamma) > 0 with g independent of n and M (a uniform Poincare inequality for a range-one interaction gives exponential decay; **recalled, not rebuilt**).

(TU) failed in three ways.

### 4.1 First way: the estimate that the frame gives has the volume in it

**Lemma T0 (proved, given Lemma 2 (HF3) of run 1).** For almost every b, |gamma'(b)| <= gamma(b)^{1/2} (<f_b^2 |grad A|^2>/<f_b^2>)^{1/2} <= 6 n M gamma(b)^{1/2}. Hence gamma(b)^{1/2} >= gamma(b_s)^{1/2} - 3 n M (b - b_s).
Proof. (HF3): gamma' = <f <grad f, grad A>>/<f^2>. By the Schwarz inequality |<f <grad f, grad A>>| <= <f^2 |grad A|^2>^{1/2} <|grad f|^2>^{1/2}, and <|grad f|^2> = gamma <f^2>. For one plaquette and a unit tangent vector X at one of its links the derivative of (1/2) tr U_p is (1/2) tr(U' X) with U' in SU(2), and |tr(U' X)| <= (tr U'U'^*)^{1/2} (tr X X^*)^{1/2} = 2^{1/2} 2^{-1/2} = 1. A link lies in 6 plaquettes, so |grad_l A| <= 3, and |grad A|^2 <= 9 times the number 4 n^2 M^2 of links. gamma is locally Lipschitz in b (an infimum of Rayleigh quotients whose b-derivatives are locally bounded), so the bound integrates: d/db gamma^{1/2} >= -3 n M. QED

This is the whole unconditional content of the frame: the gap cannot close faster than linearly in gamma^{1/2}, with a slope of the size of the square root of the volume. (TU) is the statement that the slope is of order gamma^{1/2}, without the volume. The difference is a cancellation: gamma' = (1/2) sum over links l of Cov(f^2, g_l)/<f^2>, g_l the local mean-zero terms of L_b A (HF2), a sum of 4 n^2 M^2 terms of both signs. Bounding the terms one by one with the Schwarz inequality gives |Cov(f^2, g_l)| <= 2 <f^2 |grad phi_l|^2>^{1/2} gamma^{1/2} <f^2>^{1/2} with L phi_l = g_l, and the sum over l is of the order of the volume again unless both f^2 and grad phi_l are known to be spread out and decaying, which is the decay that is to be proved.

### 4.2 Second way: the frame does not see the group, and for U(1) the statement is false

Lemmas 1 and 2, and Lemma T0, hold word for word for U(1) on (Z/M)^4 (and on Lambda(n, M) without the twist). For U(1) the gap is uniform in the volume at small b (weak interaction of circles with gap 1; **recalled**), and at large b in four dimensions the theory has no exponential decay (Guth 1980; Frohlich and Spencer 1982; **recalled, not read**), so gamma(b; M) tends to zero with 1/M there (by the end statement above). Hence for U(1) the inequality (TU) with a constant independent of M fails on a set of b of positive measure. So a proof of (TU) for SU(2) must use, inside the estimate of <f (-Delta) f>, something that is false for U(1).

The twist cannot be that input. By Lemma C the twist is invisible in every cube smaller than the fibre: locally mu^tw and the untwisted SU(2) measure have the same specification. The estimate (TU) is a sum of local terms weighted by f^2. What can enter is only the non-abelian self-interaction, at the scale where the coupling is of order one: this is run 1's 3.6 (the sign of b_0 in Hess phi on a mode of scale R), which is a one-loop heuristic and stops at the correlation length.

### 4.3 Third way: at fixed n the object gamma is not the mass, and (TU) is then a statement about tunnelling

Heuristic, not proved. At fixed n and b to infinity, m(b, n) stays near mu_n (T2), but gamma(b; n, M) should tend to zero like a power of the vortex fugacity exp(-S b): in the chart of row 679 the gauge transformation that brings U to V exp(X) is unique up to the centre, so on configurations that are good along a closed curve C of the base there is a sign eta(C) in {+1, -1} (the holonomy of the centre ambiguity around C), locally constant, changing only when a bad region crosses C. A function of eta(C) for a curve C that encloses of the order of one vortex has variance of order one and Dirichlet energy carried by the event "a bad region sits on C", of probability about (length of C) exp(-S b), with length about exp(S b/2). This is the non-abelian form of run 1's D.2 (iv), where the same was noted for U(1) in d = 2 and denied for SU(2) in d = 2; on the twisted thin torus the Z_2 flux exists and the remark of D.2 (iv) does not protect gamma.

Consequence for the route, if the heuristic is right: gamma(b; n, M) is exponentially small in b at every fixed n while m is not, the gap mode f_b in (TU) is the centre-flux mode and not the lightest particle, and (TU) asks that the second derivative of that mode across the tunnelling region is at most (1 + 2cb) times its first derivative squared, in the mean. The route then proves, at best, a lower bound exp(-2cb) for a tunnelling rate, uniformly in n. This is not absurd (the physical gap in lattice units is also exponentially small in b), but it means that the route has to control the instanton-like configurations at coupling of order one, and that the weak-coupling theorem T2 is of no help to it, because T2 bounds m and not gamma. I have no estimate here at all.

**Verdict on (iii).** Not obtained. The frame gives a volume-dependent bound and no more (4.1, proved); it is blind to the group and the statement is false for U(1) (4.2, on recalled theorems); and at fixed n the quantity it transports is a tunnelling rate, not the mass (4.3, heuristic).

## 5. The counterexample side

The stop rule's second exit is a proof that for some fixed n the family beta -> mu^tw has a point with m(beta, n) = 0. I have none, and I did not look for one numerically (no run was made in this item; the published Monte Carlo studies that run 1 lists in 2.10 saw no transition in the string tension for SU(2)). What can be said exactly:

- A breaking of the symmetry W_i of mu^tw (section 1) at some (beta, n), in the sense that a bounded local gauge-invariant function F has mu^tw(F (F o W_i^k o translation by y)) not tending to mu^tw(F) mu^tw(F o W_i^k) as the base distance |y| grows, uniformly in M, gives m(beta, n) = 0 by definition. On gauge-invariant functions supported away from the seam, W_i is the fibre translation, so the order parameter must be a function that sees the fibre position modulo n or the fibre holonomy: for example tr of the fibre holonomy P_a squared against its W-translates, or the plaquette energy at fibre point u minus its fibre average.
- Both ends are symmetric: for beta small by uniqueness (expected from the Dobrushin bound of `library/gap-zf-box-x.md`, Theorem A, whose lattice is a box with free walls; **not ported to Lambda(n, M) here**), for beta >= beta_0(n) by T2 (open).
- By Proposition D, if (D) holds at beta for some R, a counterexample to (AC'-lat) at this beta can only sit at n < 2R: it would be a transition of a thin torus at a coupling where the four-dimensional theory is already in its confined regime at the scale n. For SU(2) the known scenario (Tanizaki-Unsal, footnote 1, as read by run 1) is at large N only.

## 6. What the three failures have in common

The named lemma is (AC'-lat) itself in the form of a crossing: a lower bound for m(beta, n) on the set of (beta, n) with beta_1 <= beta < beta_0(n), from data outside that set. Route (i) crosses in n at fixed beta, route (ii) crosses in n through a finite-size criterion, route (iii) crosses in beta at fixed n. Each fails at the entrance to the set, and for each there is a model with all the structure the route uses and the opposite conclusion: the Gaussian model of the twist for (i) and (ii) (Lemma G), U(1) for (iii) (4.2).

One structural fact was found that run 1 did not have, and it changes the reading of Route T: **the twist is a boundary condition of the fibre and nothing else.** It is conjugate to the untwisted specification in every cube smaller than the fibre (Lemma C); it destroys reflection positivity in the fibre directions (Lemma B) instead of supplying an inequality; its tree-level effect is the Gaussian model G_n, whose dimensionless gap is the constant pi (Lemma G). So once the fibre is longer than the correlation length the twist has no effect at all, and while it is shorter the twist gives a gap of order 1/n that says nothing about the next n. (AC'-lat) at a fixed beta therefore decomposes (Lemma A, Proposition D) into the four-dimensional gap at beta in a form close to (D), and the absence of a transition on finitely many thin tori. The first part is the common crux of all routes of `library/gap-skeleton-final.md` (section 8: "three statements of one fact"). The second part is special to Route T and is an additional obligation, not a reduction.

The clause of the claim on perturbed actions beta A^tw + W was not treated. The flip lemma, Lemma A and Lemma C extend to it if W is gauge invariant, invariant under the flips phi_L (a function of plaquette-like variables in which each link enters an even number of times, or closed under the flips as a class), and reflection positive in the base; Lemma B(a) needs a transfer-operator form of W in the fibre direction. None of this is written out.
