### 1.1 Plain version

We study the SU(2) lattice gauge theory in four dimensions in a periodic box. Two numbers describe a box of a
fixed physical size: its Casimir free energy D (free energy minus the bulk part) and its lowest energy gap times
its side, z. The lane proved an engine: if one box is "good" (excited states have total weight below 1/5041 at a
time of half the side), every larger box has a gap. The wall: nobody can show that a good box exists at any
physical size once the coupling is of order one. Every tool on file is also true for models with a massless
particle (compact U(1) at weak coupling, a product with a massless factor), where no good box exists. Getting
past the wall means one statement that is false for those models and provable for SU(2). Analogy (marked as
such): a staircase that is proved to go up for ever once one step is found, and no step is found.

### 3.1 Ergodic theory and topological dynamics of the scale shift

**A1. Two incommensurable scalings (Furstenberg 1967; Rudolph 1990, Johnson 1992).**
- Mechanism. A closed subset of the circle that is invariant under x -> 2x and x -> 3x is finite or the whole
  circle (Furstenberg). An ergodic measure invariant under both, with positive entropy for one, is Lebesgue
  (Rudolph; Johnson for multiplicatively independent integers). Proposal of the row: apply two scalings of the
  torus, by 2 and by 3, to the limit set of Theorem CR, and look for the same kind of alternative.
- Exact statement needed, **(RIG-23)**: for the account's trajectory with (BDD-Delta), the limit set built with
  sizes 2^i 3^j has no non-empty closed subset that is invariant under sigma_2 and sigma_3 and minimal.
- Consumer: Theorem CR (4), then (W).

**A2. Invariant measures on the limit set and the entropy form of the scale derivative (Krylov and Bogolyubov
1937; the Gibbs identity log Z = - T <e> + H).**
- Mechanism. A compact flow has an invariant probability measure nu. A function that is differentiable along the
  flow has a derivative of nu-mean zero. For D the derivative is - 4 tau, with tau = H - D: the Shannon entropy H of
  the thermal state of the cube of side b at the time b, minus the Casimir free energy.
- Exact statement needed, **(ENT+)**: every element E of Omega^c of the trajectory has H_E(b) > D_E(b) at every
  scale b. A lattice form that gives it, **(ENT-lat)**: there are c > 0 and L_1 such that, for all large j and all
  integers N >= L_1/a_j with D_j(N) >= d_min/2, N [Delta_j(N, N, N, N) - Delta_j(N + 1, N, N, N)] >= c (7.3).
- Consumer: Omega^c is empty (Section 7.3), then (W) given (BDD-Delta).

**A3. Normalised limits at record scales in branch (II) (a blow-up with normalisation, as in Gidas and Spruck;
projective dynamics of the scale shift).**
- Mechanism. If D^+ is unbounded, divide the torus data by D at a record scale. The quotients are compact too.
  Their limits are "tropical" data: the logarithm of a sum of exponentials becomes a convex function. A Liouville
  statement for these limits is (BDD-Delta).
- Exact statement needed, **(LIOU-II)**: the trajectory has no normalised limit (Section 7.5). A proof must
  exclude, in particular, a limit with constant normalised Casimir free energy (slow growth, for example
  logarithmic).
- Consumer: (BDD-Delta), then Theorems ZD-c and CR.

**A4. One-side scalings as commuting gradient-like maps (Conley's Lyapunov functions; Morse decompositions of a
Z^4 action).**
- Mechanism. By Lemma A, Delta(x)/x_i does not increase when side i is doubled. So each tau_i has a monotone, and
  sigma_c = tau_1 tau_2 tau_3 tau_4. A functional monotone for all four tau_i at once would be constant on recurrent
  orbits and could force a recurrent element to be fixed by each tau_i. An eternal datum fixed by every tau_i is a
  constant, and a constant is critical only if it is log m (Proposition CONST of row 1028).
- Exact statement needed, **(JOINT)**: there is a continuous functional Lambda on the closure of the tau-orbits of
  Omega with Lambda(tau_i E) <= Lambda(E) for i = 1, ..., 4, and with strict inequality unless tau_i E = E.
- Consumer: with a bound D_0 < log 2 in (BDD-Delta) it gives (LIOU).

### 3.2 Optimal transport and entropy

**A5. The Gibbs variational principle for the Casimir free energy, static and dynamic (Lanford and Ruelle;
Follmer; Georgii; in dynamic form Boue and Dupuis 1998, Barashkov and Gubinelli 2020).**
- Mechanism. Tile Z^4 with independent copies of the Gibbs measure mu_N of the torus of side N; call the law nu_N.
  The variational principle gives the exact identity D(N) = 6 beta (2N - 1) N^2 xbar_N - N^4 h(nu_N | beta), with
  xbar_N the mean plaquette of mu_N and h the specific relative entropy of nu_N with respect to the infinite-volume
  specification (derived in Section 5, A5). The dynamic form: F is the value of a stochastic control problem for
  Brownian motion on SU(2)^links, and the product control on 16 blocks is a trial control for the doubled torus.
- Exact statement needed, **(ENT-VP+)**: for one physical size and all large j,
  N^4 h(nu_N | beta_j) >= 6 beta_j (2N - 1) N^2 xbar_N - 6.1 x 10^(-4).
- Consumer: (PHYS-SEED-Delta) of Theorem ZD-c.

**A6. A Riemannian Prekopa-Leindler inequality on products of 3-spheres (Cordero-Erausquin, McCann and
Schmuckenschlager 2001).**
- Mechanism. On a manifold with Ricci curvature at least K the interpolation inequality holds with distortion
  coefficients that favour positive curvature. So a marginal of exp(-V) is log-concave along geodesics of the
  parameter when V is K-semi-convex. SU(2) has K > 0, U(1) has K = 0, a finite group has no geodesics.

### 5.2 The identity of A5 (inequality PROVED by hand; the defect term ARGUED from a recalled theorem)

Let mu_N be the Wilson measure of the torus of side N >= 3 at beta, xbar_N its mean plaquette, and let M be a torus
whose sides are multiples k_i N. Let nu be the law on the links of M that puts an independent copy of mu_N on
each of the k_1 k_2 k_3 k_4 cells (a link belongs to the cell of its tail).

**Lemma SURF.** D(N) <= 6 beta (2N - 1) N^2 xbar_N <= 12 beta N^3.

Proof. Gibbs' inequality on M: F(M) >= - Ent(nu | Haar) - beta E_nu[A_M]. (For a probability density rho against
Haar measure, the integral of rho log(exp(-beta A)/rho) is at most log of the integral of exp(-beta A), by
Jensen.) Entropy: - Ent(nu | Haar) = K [beta E_(mu_N)(A_N) + F(N^4)], K the number of cells, and
E_(mu_N)(A_N) = 6 N^4 (1 - xbar_N). Energy: a plaquette (x; mu, nu) of M has all its links in one cell, as the same
plaquette of the torus of side N, when the cell coordinates x_mu, x_nu are at most N - 2; there are
6 N^2 (N - 1)^2 such plaquettes per cell, each with mean xbar_N. Every other plaquette has a link U that is the
only link of the plaquette in its cell C' (for x_mu = N - 1 the link (x + mu, nu); for x_nu = N - 1 and x_mu <= N - 2
the link (x + nu, mu)). Let y be the tail of U. The law of the copy on C' is invariant under the gauge
transformation h at the site y of its torus. It multiplies U by h on the left and changes no other link of the
plaquette, because the other three links belong to other cells. The Haar mean of h is the zero matrix, so the
plaquette has mean 0. Hence
E_nu[A_M] = K [6 N^4 - 6 N^2 (N - 1)^2 xbar_N], and
F(M)/abs(M) >= F(N^4)/N^4 - 6 beta (2N - 1) xbar_N/N^2. For every torus n, F(n)/abs(n) is at least F(M)/abs(M) with
M = (N n_1, ..., N n_4) (Lemma A). So f >= F(N^4)/N^4 - 6 beta (2N - 1) xbar_N/N^2. Multiply by N^4. QED

times the specific relative entropy of the periodic tiling state with respect to the specification at beta:
D(N) = 6 beta (2N - 1) N^2 xbar_N - N^4 h. So the seed "D(N) < 6.1 x 10^(-4)" says that h equals the mismatch energy
of the cell boundaries per site up to 6.1 x 10^(-4)/N^4. Both terms are of order N^(-1) per site. No tool on record

of the optimal controls of the 16 blocks is admissible for the doubled torus and gives
Phi(N) <= 16 x 6 beta (2N - 1) N^2 xbar_N (ARGUED; it is Lemma SURF again in the Boue-Dupuis form).

- (P2) For integers 3 <= s < t < u: (u - s) Delta(t, B) <= (u - t) Delta(s, B) + (t - s) Delta(u, B). (Holder's
  inequality with theta = (u - t)/(u - s): sum of lambda_r^t <= (sum of lambda_r^s)^theta (sum of lambda_r^u)^(1 - theta);
  the term f t abs(B) is linear in t.)
- (P3) For a quadruple n with least entry mu: Delta(n) <= (abs(n)/mu^4) D(mu). In particular D(a') <= (a'/a)^4 D(a)
  for integers a' >= a >= 3. ((P1) in each direction, from (mu, mu, mu, mu).)
- (P4) For integers 3 <= s < t < t':
  - ((t' - t)/(t - s)) Delta(s, B) <= Delta(t', B) - Delta(t, B) <= ((t' - t)/t) Delta(t, B).
  (Right: (P1). Left: (P2) for (s, t, t') gives (t - s)(Delta(t') - Delta(t)) >= (t' - t)(Delta(t) - Delta(s)), and
  Delta(t) >= 0.)

(P1) and (P3) are on file (`gap-crux-restate.md` L37-44). (P2) and (P4) are the convexity that Lemma K (c) of that
file uses for the ground level; here they are used for Delta itself.

**Rescaling at real sizes.** For an integer N >= 1 and x in (0, infinity)^4 with N x_i > 2 put
Delta^N(x) := Delta(ceil(N x_1), ..., ceil(N x_4)).

**Lemma EQC (equicontinuity in the scale).** Let 0 < m_1 < m_2, K = [m_1, m_2]^4, N m_1 >= 12, and let M bound D(a)
for all integers a with N m_1/2 <= a <= N m_2 + 1. Put C_1 = (2 m_2/m_1)^4. Then for x, y in K:

 (i) 0 <= Delta^N(x) <= C_1 M;  (ii) abs(Delta^N(x) - Delta^N(y)) <= (48 C_1 M/m_1)(abs(x - y)_1 + 4/N).

More exactly, if two integer quadruples n, n' have all entries in [N m_1, N m_2 + 1], then
abs(Delta(n) - Delta(n')) <= (48 C_1 M/(N m_1)) sum over i of abs(n_i - n'_i).

Proof. (i) The entries n_i = ceil(N x_i) lie in [N m_1, N m_2 + 1]. By (P3), Delta(n) <= ((N m_2 + 1)/(N m_1))^4 M
<= C_1 M. (ii) Change one entry at a time; the intermediate quadruples keep their entries in the same interval.
Let the first entries be t < t' and B the common box. Right side of (P4): Delta(t', B) - Delta(t, B) <=
((t' - t)/t) C_1 M <= (t' - t) C_1 M/(N m_1). Left side of (P4) with s = ceil(t/2): t >= 12 gives s >= 3 and
t - s >= (t - 1)/2 >= t/3. The quadruple (s, B) has least entry at least N m_1/2 and all entries at most
N m_2 + 1, so by (P3) Delta(s, B) <= ((N m_2 + 1)/(N m_1/2))^4 M <= 16 C_1 M. So
Delta(t', B) - Delta(t, B) >= - 3 ((t' - t)/t) 16 C_1 M >= - 48 C_1 M (t' - t)/(N m_1). For x, y in K,
abs(ceil(N x_i) - ceil(N y_i)) <= N abs(x_i - y_i) + 1. QED

**Lemma ASC (Arzela-Ascoli with a vanishing step; standard, proof for completeness).** Let K be a compact metric
space and g_i real functions on K with abs(g_i) <= C and abs(g_i(x) - g_i(y)) <= L (d(x, y) + eps_i), eps_i -> 0.
Then a subsequence converges uniformly on K, and the limit is L-Lipschitz.
Proof. Take a countable dense set Q and a diagonal subsequence that converges at every point of Q. For delta > 0
choose a finite delta-net q_1, ..., q_p in Q. For x in K with d(x, q_l) <= delta:
abs(g_i(x) - g_j(x)) <= abs(g_i(q_l) - g_j(q_l)) + 2 L delta + L (eps_i + eps_j). So the subsequence is uniformly
Cauchy. The Lipschitz bound passes to the limit. QED

**Blow-down sequences.** Let (Z_j, u_j) be a family with units. A blow-down sequence is a sequence (j_i, N_i),
j_i -> infinity, N_i integers with N_i/u_(j_i) -> infinity, which is locally bounded: for all 0 < m_1 < m_2 the
upper limit over i of the supremum of D_(j_i)(a) over the integers a in [N_i m_1, N_i m_2] is finite. (For one
model at one coupling the family is constant and only N_i -> infinity matters.) Put Delta_i(x) := Delta_(j_i)(ceil(N_i x)).
BB denotes the set of all limits E of blow-down sequences of the family in the sense of (a) below.

**Lemma FLOW.** (a) Every blow-down sequence has a subsequence along which Delta_i converges, uniformly on
compact subsets of (0, infinity)^4, to a function E >= 0.
(b) E is symmetric and locally Lipschitz. For each side i and each real lambda >= 1,
E(..., lambda x_i, ...) <= lambda E(x). E is convex in each variable.
(c) (Spectral form at real sizes.) For every B in (0, infinity)^3 there are levels e_0(B) <= e_1(B) <= ... in
(-infinity, +infinity], with e_0(B) finite and at most 0, such that exp E(T, B) = sum over r of exp(- T e_r(B)) for
every real T > 0. The levels are determined by E. Along the subsequence of (a), N_i E_r^(j_i)(ceil(N_i B)) tends to
e_r(B) for every r.
(d) If lambda_i -> lambda in (0, infinity), then Delta_(j_i)(ceil(lambda_i N_i x)) tends to E(lambda x), uniformly on
compact sets. So (j_i, ceil(lambda N_i)) is a blow-down sequence with the limit phi_lambda E, where
(phi_lambda E)(x) := E(lambda x). BB is invariant under every phi_lambda, lambda > 0.
(e) BB is closed under convergence that is uniform on compact sets.

Proof. (a) Apply Lemma EQC and Lemma ASC on K_m = [1/m, m]^4 (the hypothesis of EQC holds for large i by local
boundedness on [1/(2m), 2m]) and take a diagonal subsequence over m.
(b) Symmetry is (E1). Lipschitz: Lemma ASC. For lambda >= 1 put t_i = ceil(N_i x_1) <= t'_i = ceil(N_i lambda x_1). By
(P1), Delta_i(lambda x_1, ...) <= (t'_i/t_i) Delta_i(x), and t'_i/t_i -> lambda. Convexity: let a < c, 0 < theta < 1,
b = theta a + (1 - theta) c. For large i the integers s_i = ceil(N_i a) < t_i = ceil(N_i b) < u_i = ceil(N_i c) obey (P2)
with theta_i = (u_i - t_i)/(u_i - s_i) -> theta. Pass to the limit.
(c) Fix B. Write eps_r^(i) := N_i E_r^(j_i)(ceil(N_i B)) and T_i := ceil(N_i T)/N_i, so T <= T_i <= T + 1/N_i and
exp Delta_i(T, B) = sum over r of exp(- T_i eps_r^(i)). Let C(T) bound Delta_i(T, B) for large i. Bounds: the term
r = 0 gives eps_0^(i) >= - C(T)/T; and eps_0^(i) <= 0 because E_0 <= 0 for every datum
(`gap-compact-rigidity-max.md` L143). For Lam >= 0, each level with eps_r^(i) <= Lam adds at least exp(- T_i Lam),
so the number of such levels is at most exp((T + 1) Lam + C(T)).
Take any further subsequence along which eps_r^(i) converges in [- C(1), +infinity] for every r, to e_r (a
diagonal choice; the lower bound is the one above at T = 1). Claim: for every real T' > 0, exp Delta_i(T', B) -> sum over r of exp(- T' e_r). Fix Lam > 0 and
let R be an integer above exp((T'/2 + 1) Lam + C(T'/2)). By the count at the time T'/2, eps_r^(i) > Lam for r >= R
and large i, so e_r >= Lam for r >= R. Tail: sum over r >= R of exp(- T'_i eps_r^(i)) <=
exp(- (T'_i - (T'/2)_i) Lam) exp Delta_i(T'/2, B) <= exp(- (T'/2 - 1/N_i) Lam + C(T'/2)). The same bound holds for the
tail of the limit series (each partial sum is a limit of partial sums). The terms with r < R converge one by one
(a term with e_r = +infinity tends to 0, since T'_i >= T' > 0). So the upper and lower limits of exp Delta_i(T', B)
differ from the limit series by at most 2 exp(- T' Lam/2 + C(T'/2)), for every Lam. This proves the claim, and
exp E(T', B) = sum over r of exp(- T' e_r).
Uniqueness (Lemma UNI). Let G(T) = sum over r of exp(- T e_r) be finite for all T > 0, the e_r non-decreasing in
(-infinity, +infinity]. Then G determines the sequence. Proof: exp(T e_0) G(T) is a sum of non-increasing terms; it
tends to m_0, the number of r with e_r = e_0, as T -> infinity (dominated convergence against T = 1). So
e_0 = - lim (1/T) log G(T) and m_0 are determined. Subtract m_0 exp(- T e_0) and repeat; by the count above, the
levels below any bound are finitely many, so each level is reached after finitely many steps. QED (UNI).
So every convergent further subsequence has the same limit levels, and the levels eps_r^(i) converge along the
whole subsequence of (a). e_0(B) <= 0 as a limit, and it is finite by the lower bound.
(d) The quadruples ceil(lambda_i N_i x) and ceil(N_i lambda x) differ by at most N_i abs(lambda_i - lambda) x_k + 1 in
the entry k. By the second form of Lemma EQC the two values of Delta_(j_i) differ by at most
(48 C_1 M/m_1) sum over k of (abs(lambda_i - lambda) x_k + 1/N_i), which tends to 0 uniformly on compact sets. With
lambda_i = ceil(lambda N_i)/N_i this is the statement for the sequence (j_i, ceil(lambda N_i)); local boundedness is
kept.
(e) Let E_n in BB tend to E. For each n choose a member (j, N) of a blow-down sequence of E_n with j >= n,
N/u_j >= n and abs(Delta_j(ceil(N x)) - E_n(x)) <= 1/n on [1/n, n]^4. These members form a sequence whose rescaled

value of the rescaled function on the diagonal, within 1/n of E_n(b, b, b, b). QED
