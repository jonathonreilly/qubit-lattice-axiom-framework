The centre-neutral symmetric transfer operator is
$$F_+ := P_+ F P_+ = P_+ M C_E P M P_+ = (P_+ M P C_E^{1/2})(C_E^{1/2} P M P_+) \quad \text{on } \mathcal{H}_{0,0},$$
with simple positive top eigenvalue $\lambda_0 = \|F_+\| = \|F\|$ and positive normalized ground state $\psi_0 \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ ($\|\psi_0\|_{L^2(H)} = 1$, $F_+ \psi_0 = \lambda_0 \psi_0$). The physical reversible Markov operator on $L^2(\pi)$ ($d\pi(U) = \psi_0(U)^2 dH(U)$) restricted to the centre-neutral sector is $(P_0 f)(U) = \lambda_0^{-1} \psi_0(U)^{-1} (F_+(\psi_0 f))(U)$.

**Theorem 2.1 (`PROVED`, Haar Electric-Sandwich Isospectrality and Pure-Electric Quadratic Form).** Define the electric-sandwich transfer operator on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0} = P_+ P L^2(H)$ by
$$\widetilde{F}_+ := C_E^{1/2} P_+ P M^2 P P_+ C_E^{1/2}. \tag{2.1}$$
Then:
1. $\widetilde{F}_+$ is a positive, self-adjoint, trace-class operator on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ whose nonzero eigenvalues (counting multiplicity) coincide with those of $F_+$. In particular, $\|\widetilde{F}_+\| = \lambda_0$, its normalized top eigenfunction in $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ is $\phi_{1/2} := \lambda_0^{-1/2} C_E^{1/2} P_+ P M \psi_0$, and for every integer $u \ge 1$ the periodic neutral excited trace of `(N-phys)` is given purely in $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ by
   $$s_+(u; n) = \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{0,0}}\!\left(\left(\frac{\widetilde{F}_+}{\lambda_0}\right)^u\right) - 1. \tag{2.2}$$
2. For every centre-neutral gauge-invariant function $f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$, the physical one-step Markov expectation $\langle f, P_0 f \rangle_\pi$ in $L^2(\pi)$ satisfies the exact product-Haar identity
   $$\langle f, P_0 f \rangle_\pi = \frac{\langle M \psi_0 f, \, C_E (M \psi_0 f) \rangle_{L^2(H)}}{\langle M \psi_0, \, C_E (M \psi_0) \rangle_{L^2(H)}}, \tag{2.3}$$
   where the right-hand side contains only the diagonal Peter–Weyl electric convolution $C_E$ acting on the gauge-invariant, centre-neutral vector $\xi_f := M \psi_0 f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$.

*Proof.* (1) Write $A := C_E^{1/2} P_+ P M: \mathcal{H}_{0,0} \to \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$. Since $C_E^{1/2}$ is Hilbert–Schmidt on $L^2(H)$ and $M, P, P_+$ are bounded self-adjoint operators, $A$ is Hilbert–Schmidt on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ with adjoint $A^* = P_+ P M C_E^{1/2} P_+ P$ on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ (note that $M$ is gauge-invariant and centre-flip invariant, so $M$ commutes with $P$ and $P_+$). Thus $F_+ = A^* A$ and $\widetilde{F}_+ = A A^*$ on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$. For any bounded operator $A$ on a Hilbert space, $A^* A$ and $A A^*$ are positive self-adjoint operators with identical nonzero spectrum and multiplicities, linked by the partial-isometry bijection $v \mapsto \lambda^{-1/2} A v$ on each eigenspace of eigenvalue $\lambda > 0$. Since $C_E^{1/2}$ is Hilbert–Schmidt, both $F_+ = A^* A$ and $\widetilde{F}_+ = A A^*$ are trace-class, and $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{0,0}}(F_+^u) = \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{H}_{0,0}}(\widetilde{F}_+^u)$ for all integers $u \ge 1$. At $\lambda = \lambda_0$, $\phi_{1/2} = \lambda_0^{-1/2} A \psi_0$ has $\|\phi_{1/2}\|_{L^2(H)}^2 = \lambda_0^{-1} \langle \psi_0, A^* A \psi_0 \rangle_{L^2(H)} = 1$ and $\widetilde{F}_+ \phi_{1/2} = \lambda_0^{-1/2} A (A^* A \psi_0) = \lambda_0 \phi_{1/2}$. Dividing by $\lambda_0^u$ yields (2.2).

(2) Since $M$, $\psi_0$, and $f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ are all gauge-invariant and centre-flip invariant, their pointwise product $\xi_f = M \psi_0 f$ satisfies $P_+ P \xi_f = \xi_f$, so $\xi_f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$. By definition of $P_0$ and $d\pi = \psi_0^2 dH$:
$$\langle f, P_0 f \rangle_\pi = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \langle \psi_0 f, \, M C_E P M (\psi_0 f) \rangle_{L^2(H)} = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \langle M \psi_0 f, \, C_E P (M \psi_0 f) \rangle_{L^2(H)} = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \langle M \psi_0 f, \, C_E (M \psi_0 f) \rangle_{L^2(H)}.$$
Setting $f = 1$ (for which $\langle 1, P_0 1 \rangle_\pi = 1$) gives $\lambda_0 = \langle M \psi_0, C_E (M \psi_0) \rangle_{L^2(H)}$, and dividing yields (2.3). $\square$

   where $\mathcal{W}_\Lambda := \{f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0} : \Pi_{\mathbf{j}}(M\psi_0 f) = 0 \text{ for all } \sum_e (d_e^2 - 1) < \Lambda\}$.

*Proof.* (1) By definition of $\nu_M$, $f \in \mathcal{V}_0$ means $\int_X M(U)\psi_0(U) f(U) \, dH(U) = 0$, so $\xi_f = M\psi_0 f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}^\perp$. By Theorem 2.1 (2.3) and Theorem 2.2 (2.5),

where $\|M\|_{L^\infty}^2 = e^{3\beta n^3}$ and
$$c_0(\beta) := \frac{1 - r(\beta)^4}{4}\left(1 - e^{-6\beta}\right) > 0 \qquad \forall \beta > 0. \tag{2.12}$$

*Proof.* Equation (2.10) follows immediately from $0 < C_E \le I$ on $L^2(H)$ and Theorem 2.1 (2.3) at $f = 1$ ($\lambda_0 = \langle M\psi_0, C_E(M\psi_0) \rangle_{L^2(H)}$). $$\|M C_E M\|_{L^2(H) \to L^2(H)} \le \sup_{U \in X} \int_X M(U) C_E(U, V) M(V) \, dH(V) \le \|M\|_{L^\infty} \sup_{U \in X} (C_E M)(U).$$

$$(C_E M)(U) = \mathbb{E}_\xi\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{\beta}{2} \sum_{q \in Q_s} w_q(U \xi)\right)\right],$$
where $\xi = (\xi_e)_{e \in E}$ are i.i.d. random variables on $SU(2)$ with density $h_\beta(\xi_e) = e^{\beta \operatorname{Re} \xi_e} / \int_{SU(2)} e^{\beta \operatorname{Re} z} \, dz$ and $(U\xi)_e = U_e \xi_e$. Since $n$ is even, we may partition the $3n^3$ spatial plaquettes $Q_s$ into $6$ families $Q_{s,1}, \dots, Q_{s,6}$ (two checkerboard sublattices in each of the $3$ spatial coordinate planes) of $|Q_{s,i}| = n^3/2$ plaquettes each, such that within each family $Q_{s,i}$ no two plaquettes share an edge. $$(C_E M)(U) = \mathbb{E}_\xi\!\left[\prod_{i=1}^6 \exp\!\left(\frac{\beta}{2} \sum_{q \in Q_{s,i}} w_q(U\xi)\right)\right] \le \prod_{i=1}^6 \left(\mathbb{E}_\xi\!\left[\prod_{q \in Q_{s,i}} e^{3\beta w_q(U\xi)}\right]\right)^{1/6}.$$
For each fixed $i \in \{1, \dots, 6\}$, the plaquettes $q \in Q_{s,i}$ are pairwise edge-disjoint, so the random variables $\{w_q(U\xi)\}_{q \in Q_{s,i}}$ depend on disjoint subsets of the independent link noises $\{\xi_e\}_{e \in E}$ and are therefore mutually independent. Hence
$$\mathbb{E}_\xi\!\left[\prod_{q \in Q_{s,i}} e^{3\beta w_q(U\xi)}\right] = \prod_{q \in Q_{s,i}} \mathbb{E}_\xi\!\left[e^{3\beta w_q(U\xi)}\right].$$

$$\mu_q(U) := \mathbb{E}_\xi[w_q(U\xi)] = r(\beta)^4 w_q(U) \le r(\beta)^4,$$
since $w_q(U) \in [-1, 1]$ and $r(\beta) \in (0, 1)$. By convexity of $x \mapsto e^{3\beta x}$ on $[-1, 1]$, every $x \in [-1, 1]$ satisfies the chord inequality
$$e^{3\beta x} \le \frac{1+x}{2} e^{3\beta} + \frac{1-x}{2} e^{-3\beta} = \cosh(3\beta) + x \sinh(3\beta).$$
Applying this to $x = w_q(U\xi) \in [-1, 1]$ and taking the expectation over $\xi$ yields
$$\mathbb{E}_\xi\!\left[e^{3\beta w_q(U\xi)}\right] \le \cosh(3\beta) + \mu_q(U) \sinh(3\beta) \le \cosh(3\beta) + r(\beta)^4 \sinh(3\beta) = e^{3\beta} \left[1 - \frac{1 - r(\beta)^4}{2}\left(1 - e^{-6\beta}\right)\right]$$
uniformly in $U \in X$ and $q \in Q_s$. Taking the product over $|Q_{s,i}| = n^3/2$ plaquettes and the geometric mean over $i = 1, \dots, 6$ gives
$$\sup_{U \in X} (C_E M)(U) \le e^{\frac{3}{2}\beta n^3} \left[1 - \frac{1 - r(\beta)^4}{2}\left(1 - e^{-6\beta}\right)\right]^{n^3 / 2} = \|M\|_{L^\infty} \left[1 - \frac{1 - r(\beta)^4}{2}\left(1 - e^{-6\beta}\right)\right]^{n^3 / 2},$$
and multiplying by $\|M\|_{L^\infty}$ and using $1 - y \le e^{-y}$ yields (2.11)–(2.12). $\square$

### 3.4 Exact Tree-Gauge Jacobian Divergence and Global Bessel Bound for Adaptive Root Frames

We now apply Identity STAR and Theorem 3.1 to row 1141's setting (`library/gap-crux2-astra62.md` L139–203): a cell $C$ of $V = R^4$ sites, a spanning tree $T$ of $C$ rooted at $r \in C$, and an adaptive root axis $v(U) \in S^2$ that is a smooth gauge-covariant function of the entire link configuration $U$ ($v(U^g) = \operatorname{Ad}(g_r) v(U)$), transported along $T$ to form the site frame
$$n_x^{T,v}(U) := \operatorname{Ad}\!\left(P_x(U)\right) v(U), \tag{3.9}$$
where $P_x(U) = U_{\gamma_T(x \to r)}$ is the ordered product of links along the unique path in $T$ from $x$ to $r$.

**Theorem 3.3 (`PROVED`, Exact Tree-Gauge Jacobian Divergence Formula and Global Bessel Bound for Adaptive Root Frames).** Let $T$ be any spanning tree of the cell $C$ and let $v(U) \in S^2$ be any smooth gauge-covariant root axis on $SU(2)^E$, defining $n_x^{T,v}(U)$ by (3.9). Then:
1. **(Exact Vanishing of Tree-Edge Frame Defects):** On every tree edge $l \in T$, the frame difference $(D_l n^{T,v})(U) = n_{e^-(l)}^{T,v}(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_l) n_{e^+(l)}^{T,v}(U) \equiv 0$ vanishes identically on $SU(2)^E$, regardless of how $v(U)$ depends on $U$.
2. **(Exact Cancellation of Tree-Link and Outgoing-Link Derivatives in $\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}$):** At every free interior site $z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}$, all derivatives of $v(U)$ along outgoing links $\mathrm{out}(z)$ and incoming tree links $\mathrm{in}(z) \cap T$ cancel identically in the Jacobian divergence $\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z)$. In tree gauge ($U_e = I$ for all $e \in T$), the action current divergence and the Jacobian current divergence at $z$ are given explicitly by
   $$\operatorname{div} j^{T,v}(z) = v(U) \cdot B_z(U), \qquad B_z(U) = \sum_{l \in \mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T} \bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_l)^{-1} - I\bigr) N_l(U), \tag{3.10}$$
   $$\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z) = \sum_{l \in \mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T} \sum_{a=1}^3 \Bigl(\bigl(I - \operatorname{Ad}(U_l)\bigr) R_{(l,a)} v(U)\Bigr)^a. \tag{3.11}$$
   In particular, at every site $z$ whose four incoming links all belong to the tree $T$ ($\mathrm{in}(z) \subset T$), both $\operatorname{div} j^{T,v}(z) \equiv 0$ and $\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z) \equiv 0$ vanish identically for every smooth covariant root axis $v(U)$.
3. **(Global Pairwise-Disjoint Bessel Bound on $\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}$):** Because the sets of incoming chord links $\mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T = \{l \in E(C) \setminus T : e^+(l) = z\}$ are **pairwise disjoint** across all sites $z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}$, the Jacobian current divergence obeys the deterministic global bound
   $$\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} \bigl|\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z)\bigr|^2 \le 48 \sum_{l \in E(C) \setminus T} \sum_{a=1}^3 \bigl|R_{(l,a)} v(U)\bigr|^2 \qquad \forall U \in SU(2)^E. \tag{3.12}$$
4. **(Extensive Chord-Gradient Cost of Jacobian Cancellation on Row 1141's High-Probability Event):** On the event $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{def}}$ of row 1141 eq. (9) (which has Wilson probability $\mathbb{P}_b(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{def}}) \ge 1 - e^{-V}$ for all $b \in [2.3, 2.6]$ and $R \ge R_1$, and on which $\min_{T, \, |v_0|=1} \frac{1}{V} \sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} |v_0 \cdot B_z(U)|^2 \ge c b^2$), every smooth covariant root axis $v(U) \in S^2$ on any tree $T$ satisfies point-by-point on $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{def}}$:

   In particular, any adaptive root axis $v(U)$ whose Jacobian divergence cancels at least half of the signed action defect in $\ell^2(C_{\mathrm{int}})$ (i.e. $\frac{1}{V}\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} |\operatorname{div}(j^{T,v} - d^{T,v})(z)|^2 \le \frac{c}{4} b^2$) must have volume-extensive chord-gradient Dirichlet energy

*Proof.* (1) For any tree edge $l = (x \to y) \in T$, the unique path in $T$ from $x$ to $r$ and the unique path in $T$ from $y$ to $r$ satisfy $P_x(U) = U_l P_y(U)$ (whether $y$ is closer to $r$ than $x$ or vice versa, since $U_l^{-1} = U_{l^{-1}}$). $$(D_l n^{T,v})(U) = \operatorname{Ad}(P_x(U)) v(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_l) \operatorname{Ad}(P_y(U)) v(U) = \operatorname{Ad}(U_l P_y(U)) v(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_l P_y(U)) v(U) = 0$$

$$\bigl|\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z)\bigr|^2 \le 12 \sum_{l \in \mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T} \sum_{a=1}^3 4 \bigl|R_{(l,a)} v(U)\bigr|^2 = 48 \sum_{l \in \mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T} \sum_{a=1}^3 \bigl|R_{(l,a)} v(U)\bigr|^2.$$

Summing over $z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}$ therefore counts each chord link $l \in E(C) \setminus T$ at most once, proving (3.12).

(4) At each configuration $U \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{def}}$, the value $v_0 := v(U) \in S^2$ is a single unit vector in $S^2$ common to all sites $z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}$ in (3.10).
