$$w_0(U) := M(U) \psi_0(U) > 0, \qquad h_0(U) := \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U) = \int_X C_E(U, V) w_0(V) \, dH(V) > 0. \tag{2.1}$$
Then:
1. **(Projection-Free Action of $F_+$ on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$):** For every $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$, the pointwise product $M\psi$ and its electric convolution $C_E(M\psi)$ already lie in $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ ($P_+ P(M\psi) = M\psi$ and $P_+ P C_E(M\psi) = C_E(M\psi)$), so $F_+$ acts on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ without projections as

   $$\frac{d\pi}{dH}(U) = \psi_0(U)^2 = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} w_0(U) \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U), \qquad \frac{M(U)^2}{\lambda_0} = \frac{w_0(U)}{\bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U)} \qquad \forall U \in X. \tag{2.4}$$

$$(P_0 f)(U) = \frac{\bigl(F_+(\psi_0 f)\bigr)(U)}{\lambda_0 \psi_0(U)} = \frac{M(U) \bigl(C_E(M\psi_0 f)\bigr)(U)}{M(U) \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U)} = \frac{\bigl(C_E(w_0 f)\bigr)(U)}{\bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U)},$$
where $M(U)$ cancels identically between numerator and denominator, proving (2.5)–(2.6). $\square$

### 2.2 Exact Dirichlet Form, Conditional-Variance Decay, and Vacuum-Normalized Contraction Without Volume Loss

We now use Theorem 2.1 to express the one-step and two-step Dirichlet forms of $P_0$ and prove a vacuum-normalized contraction bound that never introduces the volume-exponential ratio $\|M\|_{L^\infty}^2 / \lambda_0$:

**Theorem 2.2 (`PROVED`, Exact Pure-Electric Dirichlet Form and Two-Step Conditional-Variance Identity).** For every real function $f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$:
1. **(One-Step Pure-Electric Dirichlet Identity):** The Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}_1(f) := \langle f, (I - P_0) f \rangle_\pi$ of the physical Markov operator $P_0$ in $L^2(\pi)$ equals
   $$\langle f, (I - P_0) f \rangle_\pi = \frac{1}{2\lambda_0} \iint_{X \times X} C_E(U, V) w_0(U) w_0(V) \bigl(f(U) - f(V)\bigr)^2 \, dH(U) dH(V). \tag{2.7}$$
2. **(Two-Step Conditional-Variance and Electric-Ratio Identity):** The two-step norm $\|P_0 f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 = \langle f, P_0^2 f \rangle_\pi$ satisfies the exact identity
   $$\|P_0 f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \int_X \frac{w_0(U)}{\bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U)} \bigl[\bigl(C_E(w_0 f)\bigr)(U)\bigr]^2 \, dH(U) = \frac{1}{\lambda_0^2} \int_X M(U)^2 \bigl[\bigl(C_E(w_0 f)\bigr)(U)\bigr]^2 \, dH(U), \tag{2.8}$$
   and the two-step Dirichlet form $\mathcal{E}_2(f) := \|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 - \|P_0 f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2$ equals the $\pi$-averaged conditional variance of $f$ under the Doob-tilted electric kernel $K_0(U, V) dH(V)$:
   $$\|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 - \|P_0 f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 = \int_X \operatorname{Var}_{K_0(U, \cdot)}(f) \, d\pi(U) = \frac{1}{2} \int_X d\pi(U) \iint_{X \times X} K_0(U, V) K_0(U, V') \bigl(f(V) - f(V')\bigr)^2 \, dH(V) dH(V'). \tag{2.9}$$

$$\|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \int_X w_0(U) (C_E w_0)(U) f(U)^2 \, dH(U) = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \iint_{X \times X} C_E(U, V) w_0(U) w_0(V) f(U)^2 \, dH(U) dH(V),$$
$$\langle f, P_0 f \rangle_\pi = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \int_X w_0(U) f(U) (C_E(w_0 f))(U) \, dH(U) = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \iint_{X \times X} C_E(U, V) w_0(U) w_0(V) f(U) f(V) \, dH(U) dH(V).$$
Symmetrizing in $(U, V)$ using $C_E(U, V) = C_E(V, U)$ and subtracting gives (2.7).
(2) Using $d\pi(U) = \lambda_0^{-1} w_0(U)(C_E w_0)(U) dH(U)$ and $(P_0 f)(U) = (C_E(w_0 f))(U) / (C_E w_0)(U)$,
$$\|P_0 f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \int_X w_0(U) (C_E w_0)(U) \frac{[(C_E(w_0 f))(U)]^2}{[(C_E w_0)(U)]^2} \, dH(U) = \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \int_X \frac{w_0(U)}{(C_E w_0)(U)} [(C_E(w_0 f))(U)]^2 \, dH(U),$$
and substituting $w_0(U)/(C_E w_0)(U) = M(U)^2 / \lambda_0$ from (2.4) gives (2.8). Finally, since $K_0(U, V) dH(V)$ is a probability measure for each fixed $U$ with $\int d\pi(U) K_0(U, V) f(V)^2 dH(V) = \langle 1, P_0(f^2) \rangle_\pi = \|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2$ (by reversibility $\langle 1, P_0(f^2) \rangle_\pi = \langle P_0 1, f^2 \rangle_\pi = \|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2$) and $\int K_0(U, V) f(V) dH(V) = (P_0 f)(U)$, subtracting $\|P_0 f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 = \int [(P_0 f)(U)]^2 d\pi(U)$ gives $\int [K_0(f^2)(U) - (K_0 f(U))^2] d\pi(U) = \int \operatorname{Var}_{K_0(U, \cdot)}(f) d\pi(U)$, which equals the symmetrized double integral in (2.9). $\square$

   $$\bigl|\bigl(P_0 f\bigr)(U)\bigr|^2 \le \|f\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 \int_X \left(\frac{\lambda_0 C_E(U, V)}{\bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U) \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(V)} - 1\right)^2 d\pi(V) \qquad \forall U \in X, \tag{2.12}$$
   and integrating over $d\pi(U)$ bounds the squared operator norm of $P_0$ on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0} \cap \Omega^\perp$ (and the two-step neutral excited trace $s_+(2; n) = \|P_0|_{\mathcal{H}_{0,0} \cap \Omega^\perp}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$) by the vacuum-normalized electric chi-square functional:
   $$s_+(2; n) = \iint_{X \times X} \frac{\overline{C}_E(U, V)^2 M(U)^2 M(V)^2}{\lambda_0^2} \, dH(U) dH(V) - 1 \le \iint_{X \times X} \frac{C_E(U, V)^2 M(U)^2 M(V)^2}{\lambda_0^2} \, dH(U) dH(V) - 1, \tag{2.13}$$
   where $\overline{C}_E(U, V) := (P_+ P C_E)(U, V)$ is the gauge- and centre-flip-averaged electric kernel (which satisfies $(\overline{C}_E(w_0 f))(U) = (C_E(w_0 f))(U)$ for all $f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$) and both expressions use the pointwise cancellation identity $(C_E w_0)(U)^{-2} d\pi(U) = \lambda_0^{-2} M(U)^2 dH(U)$ from (2.3)–(2.4), eliminating the unknown ground state $\psi_0$ from the two-step trace!

*Proof.* (1) Since $\int_X w_0(V) (C_E w_0)(V) f(V) \, dH(V) = \lambda_0 \int_X f(V) \, d\pi(V) = 0$, subtracting $\frac{(C_E w_0)(U)(C_E w_0)(V)}{\lambda_0}$ from $C_E(U,V)$ inside $(C_E(w_0 f))(U) = \int_X C_E(U,V) w_0(V) f(V) \, dH(V)$ adds zero, proving (2.11).
(2) Rewrite (2.11) by multiplying and dividing the integrand by $(C_E w_0)(V) / \lambda_0 > 0$:
$$(P_0 f)(U) = \int_X \left(\frac{\lambda_0 C_E(U, V)}{(C_E w_0)(U)(C_E w_0)(V)} - 1\right) f(V) \, \frac{w_0(V)(C_E w_0)(V)}{\lambda_0} dH(V) = \int_X \left(\frac{\lambda_0 C_E(U, V)}{(C_E w_0)(U)(C_E w_0)(V)} - 1\right) f(V) \, d\pi(V).$$
Applying Cauchy–Schwarz in $L^2(\pi)$ immediately gives (2.12). Furthermore, since $P_+$ and $P$ project orthogonally onto $\mathcal{H}_{0,0} \subset L^2(\pi)$, the Hilbert–Schmidt norm of $P_0|_{\mathcal{H}_{0,0} \cap \Omega^\perp}$ is at most the $L^2(\pi \otimes \pi)$ norm of the integral kernel $\frac{\lambda_0 C_E(U,V)}{(C_E w_0)(U)(C_E w_0)(V)} - 1$. Expanding the square and noting that $\int_X \frac{\lambda_0 C_E(U,V)}{(C_E w_0)(U)(C_E w_0)(V)} d\pi(V) = \frac{1}{(C_E w_0)(U)} \int_X C_E(U,V) w_0(V) dH(V) = 1$ for every $U \in X$, we obtain
$$\iint_{X \times X} \left(\frac{\lambda_0 C_E(U, V)}{(C_E w_0)(U)(C_E w_0)(V)} - 1\right)^2 d\pi(U) d\pi(V) = \iint_{X \times X} \frac{\lambda_0^2 C_E(U, V)^2}{(C_E w_0)(U)^2 (C_E w_0)(V)^2} \, d\pi(U) d\pi(V) - 1.$$
By Theorem 2.1 (2.3) and (2.4), at every $U \in X$,
$$\frac{\lambda_0 \, d\pi(U)}{(C_E w_0)(U)^2} = \frac{w_0(U) (C_E w_0)(U)}{(C_E w_0)(U)^2} dH(U) = \frac{w_0(U)}{(C_E w_0)(U)} dH(U) = \frac{M(U)^2}{\lambda_0} dH(U),$$
where $\psi_0(U)$ cancels out identically! Substituting this at both $U$ and $V$ yields the second equality of (2.13). $\square$

### 2.3 Multi-Step Doob Kernel and the Exact Two-Replica Interior Cross-Ratio Identity for $s_+(2t; n)$

We now extend Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.3 from one step to arbitrary integer times $t \ge 1$, proving that the $t$-step physical transition kernel $p_t(U, V)$ has four-point cross-ratio equal to the **pure interior cylinder partition-function cross-ratio** (with all ground-state densities $\psi_0$, vacuum powers $\lambda_0^t$, and boundary magnetic factors $M(U), M(V)$ cancelling out identically), and that $s_+(2t; n)$ is bounded directly by the chi-square variance of this interior cross-ratio under two independent draws of the stationary two-slice cylinder marginal.

**Theorem 2.4 (`PROVED`, Multi-Step Doob Representation and Exact Two-Replica Interior Cross-Ratio Bound on $s_+(2t; n)$).** For every integer $t \ge 1$, write the $t$-step kernel of $F_+^t$ on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0} = P_+ P L^2(H)$ as
$$F_+^t(U, V) = M(U) K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V) M(V), \qquad K_t^{\mathrm{int}} := P_+ P C_E \bigl(M^2 P_+ P C_E\bigr)^{t-1}, \tag{2.14}$$
where $K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V) > 0$ is the pure interior cylinder kernel with pinned endpoints $U, V \in X$ and no boundary magnetic factors $M(U), M(V)$. Let $p_t(U, V) = \lambda_0^{-t} \psi_0(U)^{-1} F_+^t(U, V) \psi_0(V)^{-1}$ be the $t$-step transition density of $P_0^t$ with respect to $d\pi(V) = \psi_0(V)^2 dH(V)$, and let $d\mathbb{P}_{\pi, t}(U, V) := p_t(U, V) d\pi(U) d\pi(V)$ be the stationary two-slice joint distribution at time separation $t$. Then:
1. **(Multi-Step Pointwise Vacuum Identity and Doob Kernel):** For every integer $t \ge 1$ and every $U \in X$,
   $$\bigl(K_t^{\mathrm{int}} w_0\bigr)(U) = \int_X K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V) w_0(V) \, dH(V) = \lambda_0^t M(U)^{-1} \psi_0(U) = \lambda_0^{t-1} \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U), \tag{2.15}$$
   so $P_0^t$ on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$ is the Doob $w_0$-transform of $K_t^{\mathrm{int}}$:
   $$\bigl(P_0^t f\bigr)(U) = \frac{\bigl(K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(w_0 f)\bigr)(U)}{\bigl(K_t^{\mathrm{int}} w_0\bigr)(U)}, \qquad p_t(U, V) = \frac{K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V)}{\lambda_0^{t-2} \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(U) \bigl(C_E w_0\bigr)(V)} \qquad \forall U, V \in X. \tag{2.16}$$
2. **(Exact Cancellation of $\psi_0, \lambda_0, M$ in the Four-Point Cross-Ratio):** For all $U, U', V, V' \in X$, the four-point cross-ratio of $p_t$ coincides identically with the four-point cross-ratio of the interior cylinder kernel $K_t^{\mathrm{int}}$:
   $$\mathcal{R}_t(U, U'; V, V') := \frac{p_t(U, V) \, p_t(U', V')}{p_t(U, V') \, p_t(U', V)} = \frac{K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V) \, K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U', V')}{K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V') \, K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U', V)}. \tag{2.17}$$
3. **(Exact Representation of $p_t(U, V) - 1$ and Two-Replica Chi-Square Bound on $s_+(2t; n)$):** For every $U, V \in X$,
   $$p_t(U, V) - 1 = \iint_{X \times X} p_t(U, V') \, p_t(U', V) \Bigl(\mathcal{R}_t(U, U'; V, V') - 1\Bigr) \, d\pi(U') d\pi(V'), \tag{2.18}$$
   and consequently the $2t$-step periodic centre-neutral excited trace $s_+(2t; n) = \iint_{X \times X} (p_t(U, V) - 1)^2 d\pi(U) d\pi(V)$ satisfies
   $$s_+(2t; n) \le \mathbb{E}_{(U, V') \otimes (U', V) \sim \mathbb{P}_{\pi, t}^{\otimes 2}}\!\left[\left(\frac{K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V) \, K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U', V')}{K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V') \, K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U', V)} - 1\right)^2\right] = \chi^2\bigl(\mathbb{P}_{\pi, t} \otimes \mathbb{P}_{\pi, t} \,\big\|\, \mathbb{P}_{\pi, t}^{\mathrm{swap}}\bigr), \tag{2.19}$$
   where $\mathbb{P}_{\pi, t}^{\mathrm{swap}}(dU, dV, dU', dV') := p_t(U, V') p_t(U', V) \, d\pi(U) d\pi(V) d\pi(U') d\pi(V')$ is the endpoint-swapped two-replica measure.

*Proof.* (1) By definition of $K_t^{\mathrm{int}}$ and $w_0 = M\psi_0$, $M(U) (K_t^{\mathrm{int}} w_0)(U) = (F_+^t \psi_0)(U) = \lambda_0^t \psi_0(U)$ for every $U \in X$. Dividing by $M(U) > 0$ and comparing with (2.3) gives (2.15). For any $f \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0}$, $(P_0^t f)(U) = \lambda_0^{-t} \psi_0(U)^{-1} (F_+^t(\psi_0 f))(U) = \lambda_0^{-t} \psi_0(U)^{-1} M(U) (K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(w_0 f))(U) = (K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(w_0 f))(U) / (K_t^{\mathrm{int}} w_0)(U)$. Using $dH(V) = \lambda_0 [(C_E w_0)(V)]^{-1} w_0(V)^{-1} d\pi(V)$ from (2.4) yields the formula for $p_t(U, V)$ in (2.16).
(2) In (2.16), $p_t(U, V) = \alpha_t(U) K_t^{\mathrm{int}}(U, V) \alpha_t(V)$ with $\alpha_t(U) := \lambda_0^{-(t-2)/2} [(C_E w_0)(U)]^{-1} > 0$. In the ratio $\frac{p_t(U,V) p_t(U',V')}{p_t(U,V') p_t(U',V)}$, the four factors $\alpha_t(U), \alpha_t(U'), \alpha_t(V), \alpha_t(V')$ appear once each in the numerator and once each in the denominator, cancelling identically to yield (2.17).
(3) Since $\int_X p_t(U, V') d\pi(V') = 1$ and $\int_X p_t(U', V) d\pi(U') = 1$ and $\iint_{X \times X} p_t(U', V') d\pi(U') d\pi(V') = 1$, we have for every $(U, V) \in X \times X$:
$$\begin{aligned}
p_t(U, V) - 1 &= p_t(U, V) \iint_{X \times X} p_t(U', V') \, d\pi(U') d\pi(V') - \left(\int_X p_t(U, V') \, d\pi(V')\right)\left(\int_X p_t(U', V) \, d\pi(U')\right) \\
&= \iint_{X \times X} \bigl[p_t(U, V) p_t(U', V') - p_t(U, V') p_t(U', V)\bigr] \, d\pi(U') d\pi(V') \\
&= \iint_{X \times X} p_t(U, V') p_t(U', V) \bigl[\mathcal{R}_t(U, U'; V, V') - 1\bigr] \, d\pi(U') d\pi(V'),
\end{aligned}$$
proving (2.18). For each fixed $(U, V)$, $\nu_{U,V}(dU', dV') := p_t(U, V') p_t(U', V) d\pi(U') d\pi(V')$ is a product of two probability measures on $X$ (since $p_t(U, \cdot)$ and $p_t(\cdot, V)$ are probability densities relative to $\pi$), hence a probability measure on $X \times X$. Applying Jensen's inequality $(\iint (\mathcal{R}_t - 1) d\nu_{U,V})^2 \le \iint (\mathcal{R}_t - 1)^2 d\nu_{U,V}$ and integrating over $d\pi(U) d\pi(V)$ gives
$$s_+(2t; n) = \iint_{X \times X} \bigl(p_t(U, V) - 1\bigr)^2 \, d\pi(U) d\pi(V) \le \iiiint_{X^4} p_t(U, V') p_t(U', V) \bigl(\mathcal{R}_t(U, U'; V, V') - 1\bigr)^2 \, d\pi(U) d\pi(V) d\pi(U') d\pi(V'),$$
which is (2.19). $\square$

## 3. Structural Reduction of `Lemma O1` (Row 1034): Exact Backward-Plaquette $n_z$-Cancellation, Backward Transport Locking, and Composite-Link Invariance

### 3.1 Exact Cancellation of the Central Frame $n_z(U)$ in Every Backward Plaquette

**Setting (as in Row 1034, `library/gap-sym-reg.md`, and Section 3 of Row 1205).** Let $z$ be a free interior site on $\mathbb{Z}_L^4$ ($L \ge 4$) with homogeneous Wilson coupling $\beta > 0$, incoming links $\mathrm{in}(z) = \{l_1, l_2, l_3, l_4\}$ ($l_\kappa = (z - e_\kappa, \kappa)$), and outgoing links $\mathrm{out}(z) = \{(z, \nu) : \nu = 1, 2, 3, 4\}$. For a gauge-covariant site frame $n_x(U) \in S^2 \subset \mathfrak{su}(2)$ satisfying `(REG)`, recall from Identity STAR (`library/gap-sym-reg.md` L74; row 1205 eq. (3.4)) that the Jacobian-corrected current divergence $\rho_z(U) = \operatorname{div}(d - j)(z)$ satisfies
$$\rho_z(U) = -e^{-A(U)} \sum_{\mu=1}^4 \sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l_\mu, a)}\!\left(e^{A(U)} (D_{l_\mu} n)^a(U)\right) = \sum_{\mu=1}^4 (D_{l_\mu} n)(U) \cdot N_{l_\mu}(U) - \sum_{\mu=1}^4 \sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l_\mu, a)} (D_{l_\mu} n)^a(U), \tag{3.1}$$
where $(D_{l_\mu} n)(U) = n_{z - e_\mu}(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu}) n_z(U)$ and $N_{l_\mu}^a(U) = -R_{(l_\mu, a)} A(U)$.

We first prove that when $\sum_{\mu=1}^4 (D_{l_\mu} n) \cdot N_{l_\mu}$ is expanded over the 18 plaquettes incident to $\mathrm{in}(z)$ (the 12 forward plaquettes $p^+_{\mu\nu} = (z - e_\mu; \mu, \nu)$ and the 6 backward plaquettes $p^-_{\mu\nu} = (z - e_\mu - e_\nu; \mu, \nu)$, $1 \le \mu < \nu \le 4$), **the central frame vector $n_z(U)$ cancels out identically in every one of the 6 backward plaquettes**:

**Theorem 3.1 (`PROVED`, Exact Cancellation of $n_z(U)$ in Every Backward Plaquette and Two-Link Marginal Equation).** For each pair $1 \le \mu < \nu \le 4$, let $\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu} := (z - e_\mu - e_\nu, \mu)$ and $\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu} := (z - e_\mu - e_\nu, \nu)$ be the two backward corner links of $p^-_{\mu\nu} = (z - e_\mu - e_\nu; \mu, \nu)$, and set $X_{\mu\nu} := U_{\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu}}^{-1} U_{\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu}} \in SU(2)$ so that the backward plaquette action term is $A^-_{\mu\nu}(U) = \beta \operatorname{Re}\!\left(U_{l_\mu} X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1}\right)$. Then:
1. **(Pointwise Cancellation of $n_z(U)$ in $p^-_{\mu\nu}$):** The forces of $A^-_{\mu\nu}$ on $l_\mu$ and $l_\nu$ are
   $$N^{p^-_{\mu\nu}}_{l_\mu}(U) = \beta \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu}) \operatorname{Im}\!\left(X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu}\right), \qquad N^{p^-_{\mu\nu}}_{l_\nu}(U) = -\beta \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu}) \operatorname{Im}\!\left(X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu}\right), \tag{3.2}$$
   and their combined contribution to $\sum_{\kappa=1}^4 (D_{l_\kappa} n) \cdot N_{l_\kappa}$ is completely independent of $n_z(U)$:
   $$(D_{l_\mu} n)(U) \cdot N^{p^-_{\mu\nu}}_{l_\mu}(U) + (D_{l_\nu} n)(U) \cdot N^{p^-_{\mu\nu}}_{l_\nu}(U) = \beta \Bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu})^{-1} n_{z - e_\mu}(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu})^{-1} n_{z - e_\nu}(U)\Bigr) \cdot \operatorname{Im}\!\left(U_{\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu}}^{-1} U_{\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu}} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu}\right). \tag{3.3}$$
2. **(Exact Decomposition of $\rho_z(U)$):** Pointwise on $SU(2)^E$,
   $$\begin{aligned}
   \rho_z(U) &= \sum_{\mu=1}^4 \left[ (D_{l_\mu} n)(U) \cdot \operatorname{Im}\!\left(U_{l_\mu} S^+_\mu\right) - \sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l_\mu, a)} (D_{l_\mu} n)^a(U) \right] \\
   &\quad + \beta \sum_{1 \le \mu < \nu \le 4} \Bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu})^{-1} n_{z - e_\mu}(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu})^{-1} n_{z - e_\nu}(U)\Bigr) \cdot \operatorname{Im}\!\left(U_{\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu}}^{-1} U_{\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu}} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu}\right),
   \end{aligned} \tag{3.4}$$
   where $S^+_\mu = \beta \sum_{\nu \ne \mu} U_{(z,\nu)} U_{P_{\mu\nu}}^{-1} U_{Q_{\mu\nu}}^{-1}$ is the forward staple sum of $l_\mu$.
3. **(Two-Link Marginal Conservation Equation Eliminating the Other Two Backward Frames):** Fix any pair $1 \le \mu < \nu \le 4$, and let $A^0_{\mathrm{in}, \mu\nu}$ denote the sum of all plaquette terms in $A(U)$ incident to $\{l_\kappa : \kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}\}$ (namely the 5 backward plaquettes other than $p^-_{\mu\nu}$ and the 6 forward plaquettes $p^+_{\kappa\lambda}$ with $\kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}$). Define the two-link marginal vector fields on $(U_{l_\mu}, U_{l_\nu}) \in SU(2)^2$:
   $$W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \, \alpha}\bigl(U_{l_\mu}, U_{l_\nu}; U_{E \setminus \mathrm{in}(z)}\bigr) := \int_{SU(2)^2} e^{A^0_{\mathrm{in}, \mu\nu}(U)} \bigl(D_{l_\alpha} n\bigr)(U) \prod_{\kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}} dU_{l_\kappa} \qquad (\alpha \in \{\mu, \nu\}), \tag{3.5}$$
   which depend only on the three frames $n_z, n_{z - e_\mu}, n_{z - e_\nu}$ (and not on $n_{z - e_\kappa}$ for $\kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}$). If $\rho_z(U) = 0$ almost everywhere on $\Omega = \Omega' \times SU(2)^{\mathrm{in}(z)}$, then for almost every $(U_{l_\mu}, U_{l_\nu}, U_{E \setminus \mathrm{in}(z)})$:
   $$\begin{aligned}
   &\sum_{\alpha \in \{\mu, \nu\}} \left[ W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \, \alpha} \cdot \operatorname{Im}\!\left(U_{l_\alpha} S^+_\alpha\right) - \sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l_\alpha, a)} \bigl(W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \, \alpha}\bigr)^a \right] \\
   &\quad + \beta \Bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu})^{-1} W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \, \mu} - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu})^{-1} W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \, \nu}\Bigr) \cdot \operatorname{Im}\!\left(U_{\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu}}^{-1} U_{\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu}} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu}\right) = 0.
   \end{aligned} \tag{3.6}$$

*Proof.* (1) Under $U_{l_\mu} \mapsto e^{\varepsilon e_a} U_{l_\mu}$, we have $\left.\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\right|_0 \beta \operatorname{Re}(U_{l_\mu} X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1}) = \beta \operatorname{Re}(e_a U_{l_\mu} X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1}) = -\beta e_a \cdot \operatorname{Im}(U_{l_\mu} X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1})$, so $N^{p^-_{\mu\nu}}_{l_\mu} = \beta \operatorname{Im}(U_{l_\mu} X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1}) = \beta \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu}) \operatorname{Im}(X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu})$ (using $\operatorname{Im}(q h) = \operatorname{Im}(q (h q) q^{-1}) = \operatorname{Ad}(q) \operatorname{Im}(h q)$ for $q = U_{l_\mu} \in SU(2)$). Similarly, under $U_{l_\nu} \mapsto e^{\varepsilon e_a} U_{l_\nu}$, $A^-_{\mu\nu} = \beta \operatorname{Re}(U_{l_\nu} U_{l_\mu}^{-1} X_{\mu\nu}^{-1})$ gives $N^{p^-_{\mu\nu}}_{l_\nu} = \beta \operatorname{Im}(U_{l_\nu} U_{l_\mu}^{-1} X_{\mu\nu}^{-1}) = \beta \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu}) \operatorname{Im}(U_{l_\mu}^{-1} X_{\mu\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\nu}) = -\beta \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu}) \operatorname{Im}(X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu})$ (since $(X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu})^{-1} = U_{l_\mu}^{-1} U_{l_\nu} X_{\mu\nu}^{-1}$ is conjugate to $U_{l_\mu}^{-1} X_{\mu\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\nu}$ by $U_{l_\nu}$ and $\operatorname{Im}(g^{-1}) = -\operatorname{Im}(g)$). Since $\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\alpha})$ is orthogonal on $\mathbb{R}^3$,
$$(D_{l_\alpha} n) \cdot \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\alpha}) w = \bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\alpha})^{-1} D_{l_\alpha} n\bigr) \cdot w = \bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\alpha})^{-1} n_{z - e_\alpha} - n_z\bigr) \cdot w \qquad (\alpha \in \{\mu, \nu\}).$$
Subtracting the $\alpha = \nu$ expression from the $\alpha = \mu$ expression with $w = \beta \operatorname{Im}(X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1} U_{l_\mu})$ cancels $-n_z \cdot w - (-n_z \cdot w) = 0$ identically, yielding (3.3).
(2) Summing (3.3) over all 6 backward plaquettes $1 \le \mu < \nu \le 4$ and adding the 12 forward plaquettes $\sum_{\mu=1}^4 (D_{l_\mu} n) \cdot \operatorname{Im}(U_{l_\mu} S^+_\mu)$ and the divergence term $-\sum_{\mu=1}^4 \sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l_\mu, a)} (D_{l_\mu} n)^a$ in (3.1) yields (3.4).
(3) In Theorem 3.1 (3.1) of row 1205, choose a test function $F(U) = \phi(U_{l_\mu}, U_{l_\nu}) \chi(U_{E \setminus \mathrm{in}(z)})$ independent of $\{U_{l_\kappa} : \kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}\}$. Then $R_{l_\kappa} F \equiv 0$ for $\kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}$, so $\mathbb{E}_b[\rho_z F] = 0$ becomes
$$\mathbb{E}_b\!\left[\sum_{\alpha \in \{\mu, \nu\}} (D_{l_\alpha} n)(U) \cdot R_{l_\alpha}\!\left(\phi(U_{l_\mu}, U_{l_\nu}) \chi(U_{E \setminus \mathrm{in}(z)})\right)\right] = 0.$$
Decompose the full action as $A(U) = A_{\mathrm{ext}}(U_{E \setminus \mathrm{in}(z)}) + \operatorname{Re}(U_{l_\mu} S^+_\mu) + \operatorname{Re}(U_{l_\nu} S^+_\nu) + \beta \operatorname{Re}(U_{l_\mu} X_{\mu\nu} U_{l_\nu}^{-1}) + A^0_{\mathrm{in}, \mu\nu}(U)$, where only $A^0_{\mathrm{in}, \mu\nu}(U)$ depends on $\{U_{l_\kappa} : \kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}\}$. Integrating over $\prod_{\kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}} dU_{l_\kappa}$ first produces $W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \alpha}$ for $\alpha \in \{\mu, \nu\}$, and integrating by parts in $(U_{l_\mu}, U_{l_\nu}) \in SU(2)^2$ (noting that $\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu})^{-1} W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \mu} - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu})^{-1} W^{(2)}_{\mu\nu; \nu} = \int_{SU(2)^2} e^{A^0_{\mathrm{in},\mu\nu}} (\operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu})^{-1} n_{z-e_\mu} - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\nu})^{-1} n_{z-e_\nu}) \prod_{\kappa \notin \{\mu, \nu\}} dU_{l_\kappa}$ because $n_z$ cancels before integration) yields (3.6). $\square$

### 3.2 The Backward Corner Private-Link Locking Theorem

**Theorem 3.2 (`PROVED`, Backward Corner Private-Link Locking Theorem).** Fix $1 \le \mu < \nu \le 4$ on $\mathbb{Z}_L^4$ ($L \ge 4$). Then:
1. **(Exclusivity of $\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu}$ and $\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu}$ Among the 18 Plaquettes at $\mathrm{in}(z)$):** Neither $\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu} = (z - e_\mu - e_\nu, \mu)$ nor $\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu} = (z - e_\mu - e_\nu, \nu)$ appears in $S^+_1, S^+_2, S^+_3, S^+_4$ or in $A^0_{\mathrm{in}, \mu\nu}$. Their *only* appearance anywhere in the 18 plaquettes incident to $\mathrm{in}(z)$ is in the single backward plaquette $p^-_{\mu\nu} = (z - e_\mu - e_\nu; \mu, \nu)$.
2. **(Two-Link Corner Transport Locking):** Suppose $\rho_z(U) = 0$ almost everywhere on $\Omega = \Omega' \times SU(2)^{\mathrm{in}(z)}$.
   - If the three frames $n_z, n_{z - e_\mu}, n_{z - e_\nu}$ do not depend on $U_{\widetilde{P}_{\mu\nu}}$ (or do not depend on $U_{\widetilde{Q}_{\mu\nu}}$), then for almost every configuration in $\Omega$,

### 3.3 Composite-Link Invariance of the Decoupled Single-Link Differential Operator $\mathcal{L}_\mu$

We now look closely at the single-link decoupled differential equation (3.7) of row 1205 (`library/gap-agy-solve-argon.md` Theorem 3.2):

**Theorem 3.3 (`PROVED`, Composite-Link Invariance of $\mathcal{L}_\mu$ and Kernel Constraint on $W^0_{l_\mu}$).** Fix any ordered pair $\mu \ne \nu$ in $\{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ on $\mathbb{Z}_L^4$ ($L \ge 3$). Then:
1. **(Exact Invariance of $A^0_{\mathrm{in},\mu}$, $S^+_\mu$, and $\mathcal{L}_\mu$ under $\Theta^{\mu\nu}_h$):** For every $h \in SU(2)$, $\Theta^{\mu\nu}_h$ preserves product Haar measure on $SU(2)^E$ and leaves $H_{\mu\nu} = U_{Q_{\mu\nu}} U_{P_{\mu\nu}}$, the forward staple sum $S^+_\mu$, the reduced action $A^0_{\mathrm{in},\mu}$, and the differential operator $\mathcal{L}_\mu = \operatorname{div}_{l_\mu} - \operatorname{Im}(U_{l_\mu} S^+_\mu) \cdot$ **identically invariant**.

   $$\sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l_\mu, a)}\!\left(e^{\operatorname{Re}(U_{l_\mu} S^+_\mu)} \bigl(\Delta^{\mu\nu}_h W^0_{l_\mu}\bigr)^a\right) = 0 \qquad \forall U_{l_\mu} \in SU(2). \tag{3.14}$$
   Differentiating (3.14) with respect to $h = e^{\varepsilon e_b}$ ($b \in \{1, 2, 3\}$) at $\varepsilon = 0$, the infinitesimal corner-split generator $X^{\mu\nu}_b := R_{(P_{\mu\nu}, b)} - \bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(U_{Q_{\mu\nu}}) e_b\bigr) \cdot R_{Q_{\mu\nu}}$ satisfies
   $$\mathcal{L}_\mu\bigl(X^{\mu\nu}_b W^0_{l_\mu}\bigr) = 0, \qquad X^{\mu\nu}_b W^0_{l_\mu} = \int_{SU(2)^3} e^{A^0_{\mathrm{in},\mu}(U)} X^{\mu\nu}_b\bigl(n_{z - e_\mu}(U) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_{l_\mu}) n_z(U)\bigr) \prod_{\kappa \ne \mu} dU_{l_\kappa}. \tag{3.15}$$

## 4. Multi-Tree Superposition Bounds for Row 1141 and the Exact Electric-Magnetic Casimir Commutator Identity

### 4.1 Multi-Tree Superposition Extension of Row 1141's Jacobian Divergence Bound

In row 1205 (`library/gap-agy-solve-argon.md` Theorem 3.3), we proved that for any single spanning tree $T$ of a cell $C$ ($|C| = V = R^4$) and any smooth gauge-covariant root axis $v(U) \in S^2$, the Jacobian current divergence $\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z)$ receives zero contribution from all tree edges and outgoing links and obeys the pairwise-disjoint Bessel bound $\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} |\operatorname{div} d^{T,v}(z)|^2 \le 48 \sum_{l \in E(C) \setminus T} \sum_{a=1}^3 |R_{(l,a)} v(U)|^2$. A natural follow-up question is whether a frame can reduce this chord-gradient cost by taking a **linear combination or average over $m$ distinct spanning trees $T_1, \dots, T_m$**. We prove that the gauge-invariant tree cancellation and pairwise-disjoint Bessel bound extend directly to arbitrary multi-tree superpositions:

**Theorem 4.1 (`PROVED`, Multi-Tree Superposition Jacobian Formula and Chord-Gradient Lower Bound).** Let $T_1, \dots, T_m$ be $m \ge 1$ spanning trees of the cell $C$ rooted at $r \in C$, and let $v_1(U), \dots, v_m(U): SU(2)^E \to \mathbb{R}^3$ be smooth gauge-covariant root vector fields ($v_k(U^g) = \operatorname{Ad}(g_r) v_k(U)$). Define the multi-tree superposition site field
$$w_x(U) := \sum_{k=1}^m \operatorname{Ad}\!\left(P_x^{T_k}(U)\right) v_k(U), \tag{4.1}$$
where $P_x^{T_k}(U)$ is the ordered product of links along the unique path in $T_k$ from $x$ to $r$. Then:
1. **(Exact Gauge-Covariant Tree-Edge Cancellation in Each Summand):** At every free interior site $z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}$, the Jacobian current divergence $\operatorname{div} d^w(z)$ of $w_x(U)$ satisfies the gauge-invariant identity
   $$\operatorname{div} d^w(z) = \sum_{k=1}^m \sum_{l \in \mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T_k} \sum_{a=1}^3 \Bigl(\bigl(\operatorname{Ad}(P_{e^-(l)}^{T_k}(U)) - \operatorname{Ad}(U_l P_z^{T_k}(U))\bigr) R_{(l,a)} v_k(U)\Bigr)^a. \tag{4.2}$$
2. **(Global Multi-Tree Bessel Inequality):** Across all free interior sites $z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}$ and for every $U \in SU(2)^E$,
   $$\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} \bigl|\operatorname{div} d^w(z)\bigr|^2 \le 48 m \sum_{k=1}^m \sum_{l \in E(C) \setminus T_k} \sum_{a=1}^3 \bigl|R_{(l,a)} v_k(U)\bigr|^2. \tag{4.3}$$
   If $v_k(U) = \alpha_k v(U)$ is a convex combination ($\alpha_k \ge 0$, $\sum_{k=1}^m \alpha_k = 1$) of a common covariant root axis $v(U) \in S^2$ across $m$ trees $T_1, \dots, T_m$, then by Jensen's inequality (without the factor $m$),
   $$\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} \bigl|\operatorname{div} d^w(z)\bigr|^2 \le 48 \sum_{k=1}^m \alpha_k \sum_{l \in E(C) \setminus T_k} \sum_{a=1}^3 \bigl|R_{(l,a)} v(U)\bigr|^2 \le 48 \sum_{l \in E(C)} \sum_{a=1}^3 \bigl|R_{(l,a)} v(U)\bigr|^2, \tag{4.4}$$
   and on the intersection of row 1141's high-probability event $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{def}}$ with the triangle-inequality bound $\frac{1}{V}\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} |\operatorname{div} j^w(z)|^2 \ge c_w b^2$, cancelling half of the action defect still requires volume-extensive chord-gradient Dirichlet energy $\sum_{k=1}^m \alpha_k \sum_{l \in E(C) \setminus T_k} \sum_{a=1}^3 |R_{(l,a)} v(U)|^2 \ge \frac{c_w}{384} b^2 V$.

*Proof.* (1) As noted in row 1034 (`library/gap-sym-reg.md` L68, L74), Identity STAR is linear in the covariant vector field $w_x(U)$ (without requiring $|w_x(U)| \equiv 1$). Thus $\operatorname{div}(j^w - d^w)(z) = \sum_{k=1}^m \operatorname{div}(j^{T_k, v_k} - d^{T_k, v_k})(z)$. For each $k \in \{1, \dots, m\}$ and each tree edge $l \in T_k$, $P_{e^-(l)}^{T_k}(U) = U_l P_{e^+(l)}^{T_k}(U)$, so $(D_l w^{(k)})(U) \equiv 0$ on $SU(2)^E$. For each incoming chord $l \in \mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T_k$, neither $P_{e^-(l)}^{T_k}(U)$ nor $P_z^{T_k}(U)$ depends on $U_l$, and $\sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(l,a)} (\operatorname{Ad}(U_l) u)^a = \sum_{a=1}^3 (2 e_a \times \operatorname{Ad}(U_l) u) \cdot e_a = 0$ for any $U_l$-independent vector $u = \operatorname{Ad}(P_z^{T_k}(U)) v_k(U)$. Applying the Leibniz rule to $R_{(l,a)} (D_l w^{(k)})^a(U)$ and summing over $k = 1, \dots, m$ yields (4.2).
(2) Since $\operatorname{Ad}(P_{e^-(l)}^{T_k}(U))$ and $\operatorname{Ad}(U_l P_z^{T_k}(U))$ are orthogonal matrices in $SO(3)$, their difference has operator norm at most $2$. For each $k$, the sets $\{\mathrm{in}(z) \setminus T_k\}_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}}$ are pairwise disjoint subsets of $E(C) \setminus T_k$ of cardinality at most $4$ each, so $\sum_{z \in C_{\mathrm{int}}} |\operatorname{div} d^{T_k, v_k}(z)|^2 \le 48 \sum_{l \in E(C) \setminus T_k} \sum_{a=1}^3 |R_{(l,a)} v_k(U)|^2$. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz $|\sum_{k=1}^m X_k|^2 \le m \sum_{k=1}^m |X_k|^2$ (or convexity $|\sum_{k=1}^m \alpha_k X_k|^2 \le \sum_{k=1}^m \alpha_k |X_k|^2$ when $v_k = \alpha_k v$) gives (4.3) and (4.4). $\square$

### 4.2 Exact Non-Abelian Electric-Magnetic Casimir Commutator Identity on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$

We next compute the exact commutator and double commutator (carré du champ) between the product-$S^3$ Laplace–Beltrami Casimir operator $-\Delta_E = \sum_{e \in E} (-\Delta_e) = -\sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 R_{(e,a)}^2$ (whose eigenvalues on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}^\perp$ are $\sum_{e \in E}(d_e^2 - 1) \ge 12$ by Theorem 2.2 of row 1205) and the spatial magnetic log-weight $S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U) := \frac{2}{\beta}\log M(U) = \sum_{q \in Q_s} w_q(U)$:

**Theorem 4.2 (`PROVED`, Exact Electric-Magnetic Casimir Commutator and Carré du Champ Identity on $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}$).** On the periodic spatial cube $\Lambda_3 = (\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^3$ ($n \ge 4$), let $S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U) = \sum_{q \in Q_s} w_q(U)$ ($w_q(U) = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr} U_q$), and for each spatial link $e \in E$ define the spatial plaquette force
$$N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U) := \sum_{q \in Q_s: \, q \ni e} \operatorname{Im}\!\left(U_{q, e}\right) \in \mathbb{R}^3, \qquad \bigl(N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U)\bigr)^a = -R_{(e,a)} S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U), \tag{4.5}$$
where $U_{q,e}$ is the holonomy of the spatial plaquette $q$ based at the tail of $e$ and oriented to start along $e$. Then:
1. **(Eigenfunction Property of $S_{\mathrm{mag}}$ under $-\Delta_E$):** Every elementary spatial plaquette trace $w_q(U)$ is an exact eigenfunction of $-\Delta_E$ in $\mathcal{H}_{0,0}^\perp$ at the minimum Casimir eigenvalue $12$, so
   $$(-\Delta_E S_{\mathrm{mag}})(U) = 12 \, S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U) \qquad \forall U \in X. \tag{4.6}$$
2. **(First and Second Commutators of $-\Delta_E$ and $S_{\mathrm{mag}}$):** For every $\psi \in C^2(X)$,
   $$\bigl([-\Delta_E, S_{\mathrm{mag}}]\psi\bigr)(U) = 12 \, S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U) \psi(U) + 2 \sum_{e \in E} N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U) \cdot R_e \psi(U), \tag{4.7}$$
   and the double commutator (carré du champ) is the nonnegative multiplication operator
   $$\frac{1}{2}\bigl[[-\Delta_E, S_{\mathrm{mag}}], S_{\mathrm{mag}}\bigr](U) = \sum_{e \in E} \bigl|N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U)\bigr|^2. \tag{4.8}$$
3. **(Exact Ground-State Casimir–Force Identity in $L^2(\pi)$):** For any real function $\psi \in \mathcal{H}_{0,0} \cap C^2(X)$,
   $$\int_X \sum_{e \in E} \bigl|N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U)\bigr|^2 \psi(U)^2 \, dH(U) = \bigl\langle S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi, \, (-\Delta_E)(S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi) \bigr\rangle_{L^2(H)} - \bigl\langle S_{\mathrm{mag}}^2 \psi, \, (-\Delta_E)\psi \bigr\rangle_{L^2(H)}, \tag{4.9}$$
   and in particular, in the vacuum state $\psi = \psi_0$ ($d\pi = \psi_0^2 dH$),
   $$\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\sum_{e \in E} \bigl|N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U)\bigr|^2\right] + \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 \int_X \bigl|R_{(e,a)} \psi_0(U)\bigr|^2 S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U)^2 \, dH(U) = 12 \, \mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U)^2\right] - \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 \int_X \bigl|R_{(e,a)}\!\left(S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U) \psi_0(U)\right)\bigr|^2 dH(U) + \mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\sum_{e \in E} \bigl|N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U)\bigr|^2\right],$$
   which simplifies to the exact decomposition of the Casimir energy of $S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi_0$:
   $$\bigl\langle S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi_0, \, (-\Delta_E)(S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi_0) \bigr\rangle_{L^2(H)} = 12 \, \mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U)^2\right] + \sum_{e \in E} \int_X N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e(U) \cdot R_e\!\left(\psi_0(U)^2\right) S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U) \, dH(U) + \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 \int_X S_{\mathrm{mag}}(U)^2 \bigl|R_{(e,a)} \psi_0(U)\bigr|^2 \, dH(U). \tag{4.10}$$

*Proof.* (1) For each spatial plaquette $q \in Q_s$ and each of its $4$ boundary edges $e \in q$, $w_q(U) = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr} U_q$ belongs to the $j_e = 1/2$ ($d_e = 2$) Peter–Weyl subspace on link $e$ and to the $j_{e'} = 0$ ($d_{e'} = 1$) subspace on all $e' \notin q$. Since $-\Delta_e$ acts on spin $j_e$ as multiplication by $d_e^2 - 1$, $(-\Delta_e) w_q = 3 w_q$ for each of the $4$ edges $e \in q$ and $0$ for $e \notin q$, giving $(-\Delta_E) w_q = 12 w_q$ and (4.6) by linearity.
(2) By the Leibniz rule for the right-invariant vector fields $R_{(e,a)}$ on $SU(2)$, $-R_{(e,a)}^2(S_{\mathrm{mag}} \psi) = (-R_{(e,a)}^2 S_{\mathrm{mag}}) \psi - 2 (R_{(e,a)} S_{\mathrm{mag}})(R_{(e,a)} \psi) + S_{\mathrm{mag}}(-R_{(e,a)}^2 \psi)$. Summing over $e \in E$ and $a \in \{1,2,3\}$ and substituting (4.5)–(4.6) gives (4.7). Applying $[-\Delta_E, S_{\mathrm{mag}}]$ a second time to $\psi = 1$ gives $\frac{1}{2}[[-\Delta_E, S_{\mathrm{mag}}], S_{\mathrm{mag}}] = \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 (R_{(e,a)} S_{\mathrm{mag}})^2 = \sum_{e \in E} |N^{\mathrm{sp}}_e|^2$, proving (4.8).
(3) By skew-symmetry of $R_{(e,a)}$ on $L^2(H)$, for any real $\psi \in C^2(X)$,
$$\bigl\langle S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi, \, (-\Delta_E)(S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi) \bigr\rangle_{L^2(H)} = \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 \int_X \bigl(R_{(e,a)}(S_{\mathrm{mag}}\psi)\bigr)^2 \, dH = \sum_{e \in E} \sum_{a=1}^3 \int_X \Bigl[\bigl(R_{(e,a)} S_{\mathrm{mag}}\bigr)^2 \psi^2 + 2 S_{\mathrm{mag}}\bigl(R_{(e,a)} S_{\mathrm{mag}}\bigr) \psi R_{(e,a)}\psi + S_{\mathrm{mag}}^2 \bigl(R_{(e,a)}\psi\bigr)^2\Bigr] dH.$$
Integrating the middle term by parts yields $\sum_{e,a} \int [2 S_{\mathrm{mag}}(R_{(e,a)} S_{\mathrm{mag}})\psi R_{(e,a)}\psi + S_{\mathrm{mag}}^2 (R_{(e,a)}\psi)^2] dH = \langle S_{\mathrm{mag}}^2 \psi, (-\Delta_E)\psi \rangle_{L^2(H)}$, proving (4.9), and integrating the first two terms of the middle expansion by parts using $(-\Delta_E S_{\mathrm{mag}}) = 12 S_{\mathrm{mag}}$ yields (4.10). $\square$
