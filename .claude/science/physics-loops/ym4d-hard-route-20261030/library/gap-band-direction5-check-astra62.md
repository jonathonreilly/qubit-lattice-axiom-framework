## (vi) Slab-front increments — HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION

Use the tensor product of **raw spatial link** Hilbert spaces, embedding the physical density operator $\rho=P_0T^u/Z_+(u)$ by zero outside the gauge-invariant space. $$

$$
These identities fix the sum with its sign. They give no smallness estimate for an individual shift. Even a pure, highly entangled state has $A_k=0$ at every cut, while its reduced states can be very mixed.

1. **Spatial reflection positivity.** For compatible mirrored seams, the Gram/Cauchy–Schwarz step compares the partition function of a reflected pair with the geometric mean of its two doubled halves. The ordinary Wilson Gram form is Sussman–Alpoge AM5, outline 2862–2883. Transferring it to a particular projected, branched seam requires exhibiting the reflected half-integrands. For $n=4$ the curvature at zero exceeds that at two when $a=1$, and is smaller when $a=16$. These moment sequences disprove the deduction from the Gram inequality alone. The ordinary RP input also holds for compact U(1).
2. **Markov property.** Given the interface links, the classical Wilson interiors factor. Moving the seam changes the *weight of the interface data* in the two replica integrals. The first missing line is an order between the averages of these new conditional weights for $R$ and $R^c$. Even collision-entropy strong subadditivity does not follow from conditional independence. Let $B$ be a fair bit, with $A,C$ conditionally independent. At $B=0$ take $A$ fixed and $C$ fair, and at $B=1$ take $A$ fair and $C$ fixed. Then $F_{AB}=F_{BC}=3/8$, $F_B=1/2$, $F_{ABC}=1/4$. The continuum vacuum entropy arguments associated with Casini and Huerta do not supply this lattice thermal Rényi-2 inequality.
3. **Positive transfer.** On two replicas $F(R)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho^{\otimes2}S_R)$. For the next layer $L$, $S_{R\cup L}-S_R=S_R(S_L-I)$. The swaps commute, but for nonempty $R,L$ this difference has both signs. Positive $\rho$ therefore cannot be used for a Loewner-order step. Adjoining an independent mixed system decreases purity, whereas adjoining the other half of a pure entangled pair increases it. Both examples use positive density operators. A Wilson-specific estimate of the seam matrix elements would be additional information.
