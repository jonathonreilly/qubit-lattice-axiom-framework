## A block map keeping one center sign on each coarse link

**GUIDE.** The single-link disintegration used here is the SU(2)/SO(3) center split of Claude Fable, `gap-crux-restate.md` §4.4, which credits Mack and Petkova for the center method. The stack sums in `gap-solve16-astra62.md` (14)–(18) and `gap-solve17-astra62.md` (24) concern a specified twist in one orientation. Here the retained variables are links of a four-dimensional coarse torus. The allowed plaquette signs are all the coboundaries on that torus. This keeps the interactions in the interior of each fine box as well as those on its faces.

**PROVED (exact step).** On a periodic four-dimensional cubical grid take an unnormalized positive measure $a(dy)$ and bounded real plaquette coefficients $J_p(y)$, with strictly positive, finite mass for the following integral. For independent fair link signs $s_e$ write
$$
 Z=\int a(dy)\;\mathbb E_s\exp\!\left(\sum_p J_p(y)(ds)_p\right),
 \qquad (ds)_p=\prod_{e\subset\partial p}s_e.                     \tag{1}
$$
For the Wilson convention $q(U)=\mathrm{tr}(U)/2$, $e^{\beta(q-1)}$, choose one measurable lift of each SO(3) link with positive real part off the equator. Haar disintegrates into Haar on SO(3) and the fair center sign by invariance under multiplication by $-1$. Use $J_p=\beta q(\widetilde U_p)$, $a=e^{-\beta |\mathcal P|}$ times the product of quotient Haar laws, where $|\mathcal P|$ counts all fine plaquettes. A change of section is absorbed by a change of signs.

**PROVED (construction and proof).** Tile by blocks of $b^4$ sites, $b\ge2$ dividing the period, with at least three coarse sites per coordinate. For each coarse edge $E$, the fine links across its interface form a bundle of $b^3$ links. Pick the same relative pivot in corresponding bundles. Write $s_e=\rho_e t_E$ on that bundle, setting $\rho_{\rm pivot}=1$, and $s_e=\rho_e$ on links within blocks. This is a bijection of sign arrays and carries product fair measure to product fair measure on all free $\rho_e,t_E$. A plaquette $p$ crosses zero, one or two cuts. With one cut its two crossing links use the same $t_E$, which squares to one. With two cuts the four links belong to the four bundles on the boundary of a coarse plaquette $P$. Denote the latter set of fine plaquettes by $S(P)$. Fixing the two crossing coordinates leaves $b^2$ free choices of transverse position, so $|S(P)|=b^2$. These sets are disjoint. Put
$$
 a'(dy,d\rho)=a(dy)\,\nu(d\rho)\,
    \exp\!\left(\sum_{p\notin\cup_P S(P)}J_p(y)(d\rho)_p\right),
 \qquad J'_P(y,\rho)=\sum_{p\in S(P)}J_p(y)(d\rho)_p ,                 \tag{2}
$$
where $(d\rho)_p$ denotes the four-edge product of the relative signs and $\nu$ is their product law. Substitution gives (1) on the coarse grid with data $a',J'$ and signs $t$. The identity is for the *unnormalized* mass, including all original Wilson scalars. It applies to each torus separately and allows arbitrary dependence among the coefficients through $y$.

**PROVED (iteration and the signed packet).** Use aligned tilings of compatible sides and iterate (2). After steps of total linear factor $B=\prod_j b_j$, the fine faces contributing to a given surviving coefficient are exactly the $B^2$ original faces at the two cuts with the $B\times B$ array of transverse positions. At a merge the child arrays tile the new transverse array, and every center sign dropped from the retained link is multiplied into its relative sign. This proves, by induction, the pointwise identity on the original Wilson configurations
$$
               J^{(B)}_P(dt)_P=\beta\sum_{p\in S_B(P)}q(U_p).       \tag{3}
$$
Here $(dt)_P$ is the retained coarse plaquette sign. Factor-$L$ steps work on the account's odd-$L$ grids. Formula (2) maps every input of the form (1) back into that class. In particular it holds at order-one bare Wilson coupling. It keeps a growing environment whose dimension and mass still require control. The identity assigns no running coupling to this environment.

**PROVED (how many original link angles the sign interaction reads).** Equivalently disintegrate the original links crossing each $B$-block face only by their single simultaneous sign flip. Take a pivot at a transverse corner. Fix the orbit of the exposed links under this flip. The exposed links are those with at least one transverse coordinate at an endpoint of its $B$-interval. There are $h=B^3-(B-2)^3$ on each face, including the pivot. Choose the representative by the sign of the pivot's real part, with a measurable choice at ties. Apply the same flip on the remaining links of the face. Haar invariance splits off a fair sign $t_E$. Products on zero- and single-crossing plaquettes are independent of all $t_E$, while all links on a double crossing are exposed. After integrating every unobserved variable the sign law still has exactly the coefficients (2), now measurable in the exposed orbits alone. On a torus of $v$ coarse sites these orbits use $4vh$ of the $4vB^4$ fine links. The marginal measure on the orbits absorbs the other interactions and generally depends on the torus.

**PROVED (sharp fieldwise amplification).** With exposed representatives equal to the identity, every coefficient of the sign theory is $B^2\beta$. In a sufficiently small open neighbourhood of this profile all coefficients exceed $(1-\delta)B^2\beta$ for any prescribed $0<\delta<1$, using the section continuous near the identity. This event has positive probability on every fixed finite torus because the Wilson density is strictly positive and all the finitely many open angular caps have positive Haar measure. Thus even integrating all but the exposed fraction $h/B^4$ cannot give a uniform pointwise reduction of the center couplings for this map. The effect of this observation on the *mean* mass requires a separate estimate.

## What the actual Wilson law does to the coarse signs

**GUIDE.** Use the directional deletion inequality of Codex, row 1167, `gap-solve12-astra62.md` (1), also row 1149 (23). **PROVED (simultaneous estimate).** On the torus of $v=n^4$ coarse sites let $X_P=J^{(B)}_P(dt)_P$. For any specified collection $H$ of coarse plaquettes and any $0\le u<d_\beta$,
$$
 \Pr_\beta\{X_P\le u B^2\text{ for every }P\in H\}
       \le \exp[-(d_\beta-u) B^2\max_i h_i],
 \qquad h_i=|\{P\in H:i\parallel P\}|,\quad \max_i h_i\ge |H|/2.       \tag{4}
$$
Here $i\parallel P$ means that $i$ is one of its two directions. Select its maximizing direction. Sum (3) over the corresponding disjoint packets, apply exponential Markov to the negative of that sum and apply the quoted deletion estimate on their union. Each face counts in two directions. In particular, with probability at least $1-6v e^{-B^2d_\beta}$ every packet is strictly positive in the original fine field. For $0<u<d_\beta$ the same union bound shows that, outside a set of probability at most $6v e^{-(d_\beta-u)B^2}$, every magnitude exceeds $uB^2$ and every coefficient sign agrees with the measured coarse curvature.

**PROVED (one angular integral for the periodic mass up to a controlled error).** On the coarse torus let $C=\mathrm{im}(d:C^1(\mathbb F_2)\to C^2(\mathbb F_2))$, written as sign arrays. Its dimension is $r=3v-3$. There are $4v$ links and $2^{v+3}$ flat link fields. After specifying the four periods one has $2^{v-1}$ choices of a vertex gradient, by path transport from a root. Thus a fair link array induces the uniform distribution on $C$. Let $J=J^{(B)}$ and $a_B$ be its unnormalized environmental measure in (2). Define
$$
 M_0(T)=\int a_B(dy)\;e^{\sum_P |J_P|}\,
   {\bf1}_{\{J_P\ne0\ \forall P,\;\operatorname{sgn}J\in C\}} .
$$
Each environment that passes this indicator admits exactly one coarse *curvature* making every $X_P>0$. Summing over the fine Wilson configurations with that condition gives the exact identity
$$
       2^{-r}M_0(T)= Z(T)(1-p_T),\qquad
       0\le p_T=\Pr_\beta\{\min_P X_P\le0\}
                   \le 6v A(\beta)^{-B^2}.                              \tag{5}
$$
This handles ties without a convention for their signs. It keeps all zero- and single-crossing plaquettes in $a_B$. The unrestricted center sum in (1) remains the exact, mass-preserving version.

**PROVED (uniformity on the first-passage tail).** Fix both physical tori. With $B=L^K$ the two coarse volumes stay fixed while $B^2d_\beta\to\infty$ uniformly over admitted data. One may even fix $B=L^j$ once and for all with $B^2\lambda_\sharp>4\log L$. Then for $K\ge j$ each coarse volume is a fixed constant times $L^{4K}/B^4$. For every $0<\eta<1$, $d_\beta\ge(1-\eta)\beta+\log m_\eta$, where $m_\eta>0$ is the Haar mass of $q>1-\eta$. Choose $\eta$ to keep $(1-\eta)B^2\lambda_\sharp>4\log L$ and use $\beta\ge g^{-2}+\lambda_\sharp K-c_5$ from CA-I 56439–56458, 56530–56547, on the account's stated premises. This establishes uniform *sign summation* for the bare data. The environmental integral still contains the continuous fields.

**PROVED (the term END must cancel).** Put $m=L^4$ and $D(F)=m\log F(T_\ell)-\log F(T_{L\ell})$. For either choice of $B$ just made, (5) gives
$$
             D(Z)=D(M_0)+3(m-1)\log 2+o(1).                              \tag{6}
$$
Indeed $mr_\ell-r_{L\ell}=-3(m-1)$, and the error in the two logarithms is bounded by $(m+1)[-\log(1-\max_T p_T)]$ once the maximum is below one. For any common local normalization per coarse site its logarithm cancels. The required upper bound on $D(M_0)$ must therefore be lower than the CH threshold by $3(m-1)\log 2$ in the cutoff limsup. **GUIDE.** A positive local weight can replace the indicator in (5) if every coarse plaquette is first given its own auxiliary sign. The resulting mass includes twists. **PROVED (signed directional deletion).** Write $Z_\tau$ for the fine Wilson mass with $q_p$ replaced by $\tau_p q_p$, for any deterministic signs. In the convention $e^{\beta q}$, a deletion replaces a factor by $1$. If the deleted faces $D$ share a direction, then
$$
                      Z_{\tau,\,\mathrm{del}\,D}\le A(\beta)^{-|D|} Z_0,    \tag{7}
$$
where $Z_0$ here and below denotes ordinary, untwisted Wilson mass. With the common original bulk scalar $e^{-\beta |\mathcal P|}$ kept on both sides the same inequality holds. To prove it take the shared direction as time of period $s$. Normalize the electric convolutions by $A$. Write $C$ for their homogeneous product restricted to the vertex-gauge-invariant space. GL3 gives $C\ge0$ and the one-bond normalized operator is the identity on constants. In one temporal layer, a sign on a surviving electric plaquette translates its spatial link by the center element $-1$. A deleted plaquette projects that link onto constants. All these translations and projections commute with $C$ and with vertex-gauge averaging. Their product $U_t$ is a contraction. Thus the electric operator in that layer is $C^{1/2}U_t C^{1/2}$, with one factor $A$ lost per deletion. If $D_t$ is multiplication by the positive signed *spatial* Boltzmann weight, put $H_t=C^{1/2}D_t C^{1/2}\ge0$. The layer formula and cyclicity give the normalized trace $\operatorname{Tr}\prod_t H_t U_t$. Trace Hölder bounds its absolute value by $\prod_t(\operatorname{Tr} H_t^s)^{1/s}$, using the ideal norm inequality for each contraction. These operators are trace class on every finite spatial lattice by GL3 and boundedness of the multipliers. After restoring the homogeneous scalars each trace in this geometric mean is a real Wilson partition with the spatial sign pattern of one layer copied throughout time and no temporal signs or deletions. Apply the same inequality in a new coordinate with no deletions. Copying a slice cannot reintroduce an orientation whose signs have all been removed. After using all coordinates every term is $Z_0$. ## A robust end class with large center couplings

**GUIDE.** Equation (4) directs the end test toward large packet magnitudes. Equation (6) fixes the required normalization. **PROVED (sufficient endpoint).** On each of $T_n,T_{Ln}$ take independent fair link signs $t_E$, independent fair plaquette signs $y_P$, and independent site variables $w_x$ of a common probability law.  {\cal Z}(T)=c^v\,\mathbb E\!\left[

$$

The functions at a plaquette, cube or site may read variables with base sites within sup distance one of its base site, in addition to its displayed boundary product. Assume finite, real $j_P$ and real $u_x$. Require $j_P\ge j_*$, $g_D(+,w)=1$, $0\le g_D(-,w)\le G$ with $G\ge1$, and $|u_x|\le e_*$. **PROVED (defects in the joint integral).** For fixed $t$, substitute $z=y\,dt$. This is a bijection on *all* plaquette arrays preserving fair product measure, and $dy=dz$.
