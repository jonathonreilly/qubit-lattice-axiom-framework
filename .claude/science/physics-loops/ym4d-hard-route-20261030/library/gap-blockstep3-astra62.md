## Keeping the continuous coarse link

**GUIDE.** A center flip cancels from every fine plaquette that crosses just one cut. A common SU(2) rotation leaves a quadratic function of the coarse link on those plaquettes. Keeping that function gives access to the angular integral. I first keep it exactly, then integrate it on an interface. This extends row 1345's blocking construction. The straight-path Haar estimate in row 1145 already concerns a different choice of continuous coarse transport. [Straight paths, §1](gap-solve2-astra62.md)

**PROVED (a closed algebra of conditional actions).** On a four-torus tiled by \(B^4\)-site boxes, orient each fine and coarse link positively in its coordinate direction. Assume \(B\ge2\) divides the side lengths and the coarse lattice has at least three sites in each direction. Each interface \(E\) has \(B^3\) crossing links. Pick a pivot, keep \(v_E\in SU(2)\) on that pivot, and write all crossing links there as \(u_e=v_E r_e\), with \(r_{\rm pivot}=1\). Left Haar invariance, conditional on the pivot, gives independent Haar measures for all free \(r_e\) and for \(v_E\). Let \(I_x\) be all links inside box \(x\). Give \(I_x\) its normalized open-box Wilson law, with weight \(\exp(\beta\sum_{p\ {\rm inside}\ x}q(U_p))\) and partition function \(z_B\). Write \(q(U)=\operatorname{tr}(U)/2\). There are \(v\) coarse sites. The full mass in the convention \(\exp(\beta(q-1))\) is exactly
\[
 Z(T)=c_B^v\,{\mathbb E}_{I,r}\int
 \exp\left(\sum_E v_E^{t}Q_E v_E+
           \sum_P T_P[v_{E_1},v_{E_2},v_{E_3},v_{E_4}]\right)
                  \prod_E dv_E,\qquad
 c_B=e^{-6\beta B^4}z_B .                                      \tag{1}
\]
Here each \(v_E\) is also its real unit quaternion column. The \(I_x\) are independent copies of the same law. The free relative links are independent Haar, also independent of the boxes. In particular \(c_B\) is **the same number on both tori**. No torus normalization is part of the reference probability in (1).

**PROVED (derivation and sizes).** A single-cut plaquette contains two links of the same bundle. Their other factors are fixed at given \(I,r\). Quaternion multiplication is bilinear, and inversion on the sphere is quaternion conjugation, a real linear map. Its trace is therefore a real quadratic in \(v_E\). Symmetrize its matrix and sum over the \(3(B-1)B^2\) transverse bonds of this interface to get \(Q_E\). A double-cut plaquette contains one link from each edge of a coarse square. Its trace is four-linear in those four quaternions. Summing the \(B^2\) such traces gives \(T_P\). Inverses and orientations are included in this tensor. Since each trace has modulus at most one on the unit spheres,
\[
 \|Q_E\|_{\rm op}\le 3\beta(B-1)B^2,\qquad
 \sup_{|v_i|=1}|T_P[v_1,v_2,v_3,v_4]|\le\beta B^2.             \tag{2}
\]
The first coefficient reads its two incident boxes and its own interface. The second reads the relative links on its four interfaces. Internal plaquettes and the Wilson scalar account for everything else in (1).

**PROVED (iteration).** Allow a positive finite environmental measure and arbitrary real symmetric edge matrices and four-linear plaquette tensors in (1). At the next blocking apply the same change of variables to the current coarse links. An old edge quadratic becomes an environmental constant on an internal edge and another edge quadratic on a crossing edge. An old plaquette tensor gives a constant, an edge quadratic, or a new four-linear tensor according to the number of its cuts. Right multiplication by a fixed relative quaternion and inversion of a unit quaternion are orthogonal real linear maps. The degree assertions therefore hold for the whole class. Exponentiate the constants into the new environment. Fubini preserves each mass, including every accumulated scalar. For an original Wilson input the direct construction (1) is available at every aligned total block factor. The number of real coefficients multiplying the retained variables is bounded by ten per coarse link and \(4^4\) per plaquette. This count concerns the conditional action. The joint law of its coefficients and the open-box boundary fields still depends on \(B\).

## Integrating a retained rotation with the full Wilson action present

**PROVED (conditional angular integral).** In (1), fix the environment and every retained rotation other than \(v_E\). The complete dependence on \(v_E=u\) is \(u^t Q u+h\cdot u\). The six coarse plaquettes incident to \(E\) give \(h\), since each reads \(E\) exactly once. For \(B=2\), \(0\le\beta\le 7/3\), (2) gives \(\|Q\|+|h|\le (12+24)\beta\le84\). The shared rotation can be integrated exactly as the function
\[
 H(Q,h)=\int_{S^3}e^{u^t Q u+h\cdot u}\,du.                    \tag{3}
\]
All plaquettes, including double crossings, are present. For a set of edges no two of which belong to the same square, these integrals factor conditionally. Substituting their exact values preserves each unnormalized mass.

**PROVED (finite polynomial for (3)).** Let \(D(t)=\det(1-2tQ)\), \(P(t)=h^t\operatorname{adj}(1-2tQ)h\) and set
\[
 C_j(t)=D(t)^{-1/2}\frac1{j!}\left(\frac{P(t)}{2D(t)}\right)^j,\qquad
 H_M=\sum_{k+2j\le M}
 \frac{[t^k]C_j(t)}{2^{k+j}(k+j+1)!}.                         \tag{4}
\]
Take \(k,j\ge0\) in the sum. Complete the square for a standard four-dimensional Gaussian \(Y\) in the generating function of \(tY^tQY+s\,h\cdot Y\). Its \(s^{2j}\) coefficient is \(C_j(t)\), and its odd coefficients vanish. Writing \(Y=Ru\) separates a uniform \(u\) from its radius. The moment \(\mathbb E R^{2(k+j)}=2^{k+j}(k+j+1)!\) follows by radial integration. Thus (4) integrates the Taylor polynomial of degree \(M\) in the **whole exponent** of (3). With \(M=512\) the relative error is at most
\[
 e^{168}\frac{84^{513}}{513!}
 \le e^{168}(84e/513)^{513}<2^{-261}.
\]
Here \(H\ge e^{-84}\), \(e<3\), \(e^2<8\), and the factorial bound follows by integrating \(\log x\) from 1 to 513. The interval is uniform even for correlated external quaternions.

**PROVED (arithmetic count).** The degrees of \(D,P\) are at most four and three. Compute the first row from \(2D C'_0=-D'C_0,\ C_0(0)=1\), then use \(2jD C_j=P C_{j-1}\). Since \(D(0)=1\), each next coefficient uses at most nine earlier terms with known scalar multipliers. Fewer than \(513\cdot257\) coefficients and 100 rational operations per coefficient suffice, including the accumulation in (4). Direct \(4\)-by-\(4\) determinants, the factorials and the small-degree polynomials fit within the remaining slack of \(2\cdot10^7\) operations. This is an arithmetic circuit for a polynomial in the supplied entries. Bit cost depends on their encoding. The error estimate above is a hand proof. No numerical evaluation or Lean check is used.

**OPEN.** Equation (3) integrates the common SU(2) rotation. The relative angles also enter the action. Applying (3) on a lattice of overlapping plaquettes produces new functions of the neighboring fields and retains their normalizers. To test the size of those effects I next integrate all the crossing angles on one face.

## A finite neutral calculation and its size under blocking

**PROVED (a uniform norm on the unit cube face).** For \(B=2\), let \(K^{\rm inv}\) be (5) compressed to separately gauge-invariant endpoint functions; equivalently use the invariant restriction on both sides. Let \(\Pi\) be the constant projection on this space. At \(0\le\beta\le7/3\),
\[
          \|K^{\rm inv}-\Pi\|_{\rm HS}^2<1/32.                      \tag{7}
\]
To see the multiplicities in (6), the edge Peter–Weyl block is the tensor product of its representation spaces at all edge ends, dualizing incoming ends. At a trivalent vertex the invariant subspace has dimension one exactly when the three integer labels satisfy the triangle inequalities and have even sum, and zero otherwise. Indeed \(\chi_a\chi_b=\sum_{c=|a-b|,\,{\rm step}\,2}^{a+b}\chi_c\) by multiplying the finite geometric sums for SU(2) characters on a diagonal element. Character orthogonality gives the claimed invariant dimension; SU(2) characters are real. This is the trivalent spin basis also used in the repository's cube calculations. Thus the squared norm in (7) is the sum over admissible nonzero labellings of the twelve edges of \(\prod_b r_{j_b}^2\). [Earlier cube basis](gap-descent-n-trend-astra.md)

**PROVED (counting without evaluating the matrix).** By the ratio bounds above, \(r_j^2\le (1/4)^j\). A labelling on the trivalent graph is covered by unoriented nonbacktracking circuits. At a vertex pair the \(j_1,j_2,j_3\) strands in groups of \((j_1+j_2-j_3)/2\) between edges 1 and 2 and the two analogous counts. Match ends along each edge and follow the strands. Erasing starting points gives cyclically nonbacktracking walks. Break a repeated primitive walk into copies. Conversely the multiset of walks recovers the edge counts, so choosing one such decomposition for each labelling gives an injection into multisets of unoriented primitive circuits. Let \(H\) be the directed-edge nonbacktracking adjacency matrix. At \(t=1/4\) its row sum times \(t\) is \(1/2\). The multiset series is \(\det(I-tH)^{-1/2}\): the expansion \(\sum_{k\ge1} t^k\operatorname{tr}H^k/k\) counts oriented primitive circuits with their repeated traversals and weights \(1/j\) on repetition \(j\). Reversal pairs the two orientations. A cyclic nonbacktracking word cannot equal its reverse up to rotation: the resulting reflection of its cyclic polygon fixes an edge with reversed orientation or a vertex with immediate backtracking. Dividing by two and exponentiating proves the formula.

**PROVED.** For completeness, let \(S,T\) be the vertex-by-directed-edge start and terminal incidence matrices and \(J\) edge reversal. Then \(H=T^t S-J\). Use \((I+tJ)^{-1}=(I-tJ)/(1-t^2)\) and \(\det(I-XY)=\det(I-YX)\) to obtain the Ihara–Bass determinant
\[
 \det(I-tH)=(1-t^2)^{12-8}\det(I-tA+2t^2 I),
\]
where \(A=ST^t\) is cube adjacency and \(SJT^t=3I\). On functions of the three cube bits, the eight product sign characters have adjacency eigenvalues \(3,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1,-3\). At \(t=1/4\) this gives

       (15/16)^4(45/64)(77/64)^3>(32/33)^2 .
\]
The multiset sum excluding the empty collection is consequently less than \(1/32\). This proves (7). Keeping the six elementary-square fundamental characters explicitly leaves squared Hilbert–Schmidt error less than \(1/32-6/256=1/128\): remove their six distinct monomials \(t^4\) from the *majorant* before bounding the remainder.

**PROVED (the projection measured by a torus mass).** On \(\mathcal H=L^2(G^{E(\Gamma)})\) let \(P\) be vertex gauge averaging, \(C\) the normalized \(\beta\) electric convolution and \(M\) multiplication by the square root of the spatial Wilson weight. A common positive scalar per time step can be omitted throughout this comparison. With time period \(s\), \(T=MCPM\ge0\). Replacing the gauge integration on one time cut by projection \(P'\le P\) commuting with \(C\) gives the mass \({\rm Tr}(T^{s-1}MCP'M)\). Its ratio to \({\rm Tr}\,T^s\) equals \({\rm Tr}(\rho P')\), where \(\rho\) is \(PC^{1/2}MT^{s-1}MC^{1/2}P\) divided by its trace. This follows by cyclicity and \(P^2=P\). For an electric spin event use \(P'=P Q\) with \(Q\) its joint edge spectral projection. These projections commute. This constructs a probability law of the edge spins on a cut of the **ordinary periodic Wilson torus**.

**PROVED (the cost of splitting a face).** Partition the spatial bonds into the bonds of one open face graph \(F\) used in (5) and the other bonds. Integrating independent vertex gauges on the two parts gives \(P_{\rm split}\), the projection onto functions invariant on each part separately. Its range is inside \(P\), and it commutes with \(C\). It describes the proposed gluing in which one projects (5) to neutral separately on \(F\): the bonds inside and outside \(F\) now have independent copies of the temporal links at their common sites. At a rim site of \(F\) with just one coordinate at an endpoint, exactly one incident spatial bond lies outside \(F\). Independent gauge invariance on that part averages one end of this single bond over all SU(2). It sets its spin to zero. Thus \(P_{\rm split}\) is bounded above by the joint spin-zero projection on any collection of these outward bonds, restricted to the physical space. For a large \(B\) we can mark \(r\) of order \(B^2\) on one side of the rim. For instance take the other two coordinates in \(\{1,4,7,\ldots\}\cap[1,B-3]\). With each marked electric plaquette take the unit three-cube extending it one positive step along the first of these two axes. The chosen cubes have disjoint face sets, and each contains exactly one marked electric plaquette. The estimates below apply to any such selection.

**PROVED (a local angular price).** Write \(z(x)=e^{-x}A_0(x)\). Define \(W(\boldsymbol\theta)\) as the ratio to the ordinary torus mass obtained by rotating each marked electric plaquette by an independent, uniformly conjugated element of class angle \(\theta_i\in[0,\pi]\). Set \(a_i=1-\cos\theta_i\). Then for every \(\beta>0\),
\[
 e^{-\beta\sum_i a_i}\ \le W(\boldsymbol\theta)
 \ \le \min\{1,(6\,2^{3/2})^r e^{-(\beta/144)\sum_i a_i}\}.       \tag{9}
\]
For the lower bound, in the ordinary Wilson expectation the changed factor at \(p\) is the class average of \(\exp[\beta(q(U_p V_i)-q(U_p))]\). The average of \(V_i\) is \(\cos\theta_i\) times the identity. Jensen and \(q(U_p)\le1\) give the bound for each factor, with all other fields arbitrary.

**PROVED (comparison used for the upper bound).** A class rotation of a normalized electric plaquette multiplies its spin-\(j/2\) convolution eigenvalue by \(\chi_j(\theta)/(j+1)\in[-1,1]\). Replacing \(\beta\) by \(\beta/2\) additionally multiplies it by \(r_j(\beta/2)/r_j(\beta)\le1\). For the latter monotonicity, \(I_{n+1}(x)/I_n(x)\) is the tilted mean of \(u\) on \([-1,1]\) with density proportional to \(e^{xu}(1-u^2)^{n-1/2}\). Integrating the even powers gives a constant times \(x^{-n}I_n(x)\) for its normalizer; differentiation and the Bessel series give the ratio. The mean has derivative equal to its variance. Multiply successive ratios to get monotonicity of \(r_j\).

**PROVED.** Consider any patterns of independent class rotations, and replacements \(\beta\to\beta/2\) on a set \(D\) of faces all parallel to one common coordinate direction. Take that direction as time. In each layer the normalized electric operator has the form \(C^{1/2}V_t C^{1/2}\) with a contraction commuting with the gauge projection; this follows from the preceding multipliers. The spatial weight \(D_t\) is positive, including its spatially rotated plaquettes. **PROVED (positive minorant).** Uniformly for \(0\le\beta\le7/3\) and \(-1\le q\le1\) there is an explicit degree-ten polynomial \(P_\beta(q)>0\) with rational coefficients at rational \(\beta\) for which

**PROVED (exact discretization of the core).** Each fine link is incident to six plaquettes, and \(q(U_p)\) is linear in the real quaternion components of each link when the others are fixed. **PROVED (the full unnormalized equality).** For plaquettes \(p,p'\) declare an adjacency if a plaquette \(q\) shares a link with both, allowing \(q=p\) or \(q=p'\).               \prod_{p\in C} R_\beta(q(U_p))\,d\mu^{E_C}}
      {\int \prod_{q\in N_C} P_\beta(q(U_q))\,d\nu^{E_C}}.        \tag{14}

**PROVED.** With \({\cal P},{\cal E}\) the fine plaquettes and links, the Wilson mass is

It follows that \(J_s=Z_{2s}/[A_0(\beta)^{12} Z_s^2]=m_{2s-1}/m_{s-1}^2\) is strictly increasing in \(s\). Indeed \(J_{s+1}/J_s\) is the product of ratios at indices \(2s,2s-1\) divided by the squared ratio at \(s-1\). This example permits a different scalar at every scale. To compute that scalar from a fixed-size rule one must carry the changing boundary state or bound its contribution.

## Carrying the angular discretization through the cutoff limit

**PROVED (a finite-state Wilson trajectory with the same seed).** There is an explicit version of (15) *on every late bare datum*, with an error in the two log masses that tends to zero even if one drops the whole angular correction. Fix the two admissible physical sides and the account's block factor \(L\). For each admitted \((K,\beta)\) set
\[
 d_K=\lceil12\beta\rceil+8(K+1)\lceil\log_2 L\rceil,\qquad
 P_{K,\beta}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{d_K}\frac{[\beta(1+q)]^j}{j!}.
\]
Write the Wilson weight as \(e^{\beta(q-1)}=e^{-2\beta}P_{K,\beta}(q)[1+R(q)]\). For \(0\le y=\beta(1+q)\le2\beta\) the first omitted term is at most \([2e\beta/(d_K+1)]^{d_K+1}\le2^{-(d_K+1)}\), and successive terms have ratio below \(1/2\). As the polynomial is at least one,
\[
                 0\le R(q)\le2^{-d_K}\le L^{-8(K+1)}.             \tag{18}
\]
Use phase indices modulo \(2(3d_K+1)\) of equal parity and \(\lceil(3d_K+1)/2\rceil\) Gaussian radial nodes. As above this integrates all real quaternion polynomials of degree \(6d_K\). Use the identical rule on the two tori. Define \(Z^{\rm fin}\) to be their finite-state partition with the displayed polynomial and the full scalar \(e^{-2\beta|{\cal P}|}\). Formula (14) with (18) retains the original masses *exactly* when desired.

**PROVED (uniform two-volume bound).** Put \(D(Z)=L^4\log Z(T_\ell)-\log Z(T_{L\ell})\). \[
 \big|D(Z)-D(Z^{\rm fin})\big|

With a strict margin for the discrete cores, keeping their positive residuals supplies exact masses and preserves a strict margin at all sufficiently late cutoffs.

**PROVED (cost of this particular reduction).** At fixed \(g,L\), the source flow bounds give \(d_K=O(K)\). The local alphabet in this construction has \(O(K^3)\) points. This scaling is necessary up to constants for a scheme requiring *both* uniform pointwise relative polynomial accuracy on the bare plaquette interval and cubature exactness for all polynomials of degree \(6d\). The Chebyshev bound after (15) forces \(d\ge cK\) on the admitted tail. Exact cubature to degree \(6d\) needs at least \(\sum_{j=0}^{3d}(j+1)^2\) points. Indeed the matrix entries of the degree-\(j\) symmetric power of the fundamental representation are polynomials of degree \(j\) and are linearly independent across these Peter–Weyl spaces. Fewer points would annihilate a nonzero linear combination of their evaluations, in the sense that the polynomial vanishes at every node. Its squared modulus, of degree at most \(6d\), has positive Haar integral and zero cubature sum. Thus even optimal exact point sets for this choice of approximation do not bound the number of local states independently of \(K\). This is a restriction on explicit quadrature state counts; it is not a lower bound on a symbolic proof.
