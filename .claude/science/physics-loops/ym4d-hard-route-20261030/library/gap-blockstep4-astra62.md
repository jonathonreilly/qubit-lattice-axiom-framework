**PROVED.** Let \(w_P(0),w_P(1)\) be the two weights, \(M_P=\max w_P\), \(m_P=\min w_P>0\). Choose \(\eta_P\) attaining the maximum, with either choice at equality. Then
\[
 w_P(k)=m_P+(M_P-m_P){\bf1}_{k=\eta_P}.
\]
Open a set \(A\) of plaquettes by taking the second term there. Over the fair link and twist bits, the mass is exactly
\[
 Z_+(T)=\int a_B(dy)\sum_A
 \prod_{P\notin A}m_P\prod_{P\in A}(M_P-m_P)
 \;2^{-\operatorname{rank}D_A}\,
 {\bf1}_{\{\eta_A\in\operatorname{im}D_A\}}.                         \tag{2}
\]

**PROVED (local composition rule).** At fixed \(y,A\), split the columns of a group of rows into interior bits \(i\) and boundary bits \(b\), retaining \(h\) as shared columns. The table on \(b\), before its average, is its scalar weight times

 2^{-\operatorname{rank}D_i}{\bf1}_{\{\eta-D_b b\in\operatorname{im}D_i\}}.      \tag{3}
\]
Eliminate the interior columns by row operations to obtain affine equations solely on \(b\). \[
 2^{-3Nt}\int a_B(dy)

      \prod_{j=0}^{t-1}\lambda^{\,|d_s b_j|+|\pi b_j|}.
\]

 1\le \frac{Y_\lambda(t)}{Z_+(t)}

\]

This uses no estimate of the ratios of the eight allowed temporal sectors to each other. At fixed admissible \(m\) and \(3\le t\le m/512\), take \(B=L^K\) on the first-passage tail with \(4\mid mB\). Thus the right side of (19) tends to one on both tori. This use of a large *bare* coupling imposes no artificial small-defect requirement on a homogeneous order-one block action.

Here the subscripts on \(T\) are the fine time lengths. All three errors vanish uniformly at fixed mesh. ## Keeping the angular strip normalization as a local factor

**PROVED (a finite-memory formula for the entire conditional mass).** There is a way to eliminate a colour of Wilson columns in (17) which also estimates every strip and seam prefactor. It uses positive scalar quotients on the exterior. Fix the exterior at fine period \(T\) and repeat it periodically, including all twists. For one column let \(W_{i,j}\) be the positive open integral on sites \(i-j+1,\ldots,i\) with all \(j\) magnetic site factors and the \(j-1\) intervening electric kernels. Put \(W_{i,0}=1\). For integers \(T\ge h\ge3\), define a scalar on the exterior of just \(h\) successive sites by
\[
        g_{i,h}=W_{i,h}/W_{i-1,h-1}.
\]
If \(Z_{\rm col}(T)\) is the closed column integral, then
\[
 \left|\log Z_{\rm col}(T)-\sum_{i\bmod T}\log g_{i,h}\right|
       \le 2\log(1+\delta_T)+ T\log(1+\delta_{h-2}).                         \tag{21}
\]
The error is uniform over the magnetic factors, the temporal links and the twist. Each \(g_{i,h}\) can vary substantially with the exterior. It carries the conditional normalization itself.

**PROVED (loss of memory of an open normalization).** For \(r> h\) integrate the extra past of \(W_{i,r}\) up to its attachment at \(i-h+1\). This multiplies the first site of the common path on \(i-h+1,\ldots,i-1\) by a positive function \(f\). Appending site \(i\) amounts to weighting its last site by \(g(x)=\int k_{i-1}(x,y)D_i(y)\,dy\). Under the normalized common path law,
\[
 \frac{W_{i,r}/W_{i-1,r-1}}{g_{i,h}}=\frac{\mathbb E(fg)}{\mathbb E f\,\mathbb E g}.
\]
Its endpoints are separated by \(h-2\) electric steps. (17) bounds their cross ratios by \(1+\delta_{h-2}\), also after any separate weighting of the endpoints. Integrating the cross-ratio inequality against an independent pair of endpoint variables gives the same bound in both directions on the displayed ratio. In particular *all* longer pasts give the same error bound without a factor proportional to the length of the past.

**PROVED (closing the time circle).** Use the \(T\)-step open kernel \(K_T(x,y)\), including the initial site weight, all intermediate site weights and no final site weight. Identifying its endpoints and integrating gives \(Z_{\rm col}(T)\). By (17) it lies pointwise between \(l(x)r(y)/(1+\delta_T)\) and \((1+\delta_T)l(x)r(y)\) for positive functions \(l,r\); multiplication by the initial site weight preserves the bound. Write \(s_0=\int l(x)r(x)\,dx\). Closing \(p\) copies of \(K_T\) and dividing the logarithm by \(p\) gives a number within \(\log(1+\delta_T)\) of \(\log s_0\), for every \(p\ge1\). Restoring the last kernel to the open mass \(W_{pT,pT}\) multiplies it by a number between the positive infimum and the supremum of that kernel. The difference of the open and closed logarithms is therefore bounded independently of \(p\). Telescope the open mass as the product of its successive one-site increments. After the first \(h-1\) sites, the preceding bound on longer pasts compares each increment with \(g_{i,h}\). Divide by \(p\) and take \(p\to\infty\); the finitely many initial increments and the final closure cost disappear. Comparing to the bound at \(p=1\) proves (21). The repeated exterior is used only in this proof for *one* fixed column environment. The Wilson integrations at the two periods in (20) still use their own unrestricted environments.

**PROVED (a block step with a controlled scalar remainder).** Apply (21) simultaneously to the \(n^3/2\) disjoint conditional columns of (17). At fixed exterior replace each whole column integral by \(\prod_i g_{i,h}\) and retain their true ratio as a positive remainder for exact transport. Its log modulus is bounded by \(n^3[2\log(1+\delta_T)+T\log(1+\delta_{h-2})]/2\). Positivity preserves the same log-error bound if the remainder is dropped and the exterior and twists integrated. At fixed order-one coupling choose \(h=C\log n\) with sufficiently large \(C\), and \(T=\lfloor n/512\rfloor\) or twice this. The bound tends uniformly to zero. The remaining action is then a product of known positive scalar functions of finite temporal windows and bounded transverse range, with all normalizers of this colour included. At fixed \(h=16385\), rational coupling and supplied rational exterior quaternions, the core version of each \(g_{i,h}\) requires fewer than \(2^{49}\) rational arithmetic operations by the two open contractions counted after (17). Their endpoints are integrated; no spectral oracle is involved. Constant \(h\) here gives a per-site error and does not imply a cutoff-uniform total error. The logarithmic choice gives vanishing total error but has growing window length. Equation (21) also applies to a fixed group of \(k\) adjacent links with \(R\) replaced by \(R^k\).

**PROVED (fixed range and a small interaction tail, simultaneously).** The errors of the fixed-window rule can also be kept as *local* interactions with decreasing norms. For \(j\ge4\) put \(\phi_{i,j}=\log(g_{i,j}/g_{i,j-1})\) on the periodically extended exterior. The open-path argument gives
\[
 |\phi_{i,j}|\le\log(1+\delta_{j-3})\le 2^{17}e^{-j/256}
                    \quad (R\le256).
\]
For \(H=16385\), in any torus of period \(T>2H\), keep the window \(g_{i,H}\) and the factors \(e^{\phi_{i,j}}\) for \(H<j\le\lfloor T/2\rfloor\). Their product at site \(i\) is exactly \(g_{i,\lfloor T/2\rfloor}\). Equation (21) bounds the total log cost of the remaining winding term by \(2\log(1+\delta_T)+T\log(1+\delta_{\lfloor T/2\rfloor-2})\) per column. This tends to zero exponentially in \(T\), times a polynomial, also after multiplying by \(n^3/2\) on the chosen pair of periods. For the retained tail one has the following absolute estimate on the unscaled fine lattice:
\[
 \sup_{\text{exterior link }a}
 \sum_{\text{windows }(i,j)\text{ reading }a,\ j>H}
                      e^{j/1024}\|\phi_{i,j}\|_\infty
                        <2^{-18}.                                           \tag{22}
\]
The sum includes all columns of the chosen colour. An exterior spatial link participates in at most four of their magnetic plaquettes on a slice, a temporal link in at most two of their electric plaquettes. Thus at most \(8(j+1)\) windows of length \(j\) can read a given link. For \(d=3/1024\) one has \((1-e^{-d})^{-1}<1+1/d<2^9\), and \(e^{-dH}<e^{-48}<2^{-64}\) since \(e^3>16\). Now \(\sum_{j>H}(j+1)e^{-dj}\le e^{-dH}[(H+1)2^9+2^{18}]<2^{25-64}\). This proves (22) with room to spare. No smallness assumption on a finite-window quotient \(g_{i,H}\) enters the estimate. In this form the fixed one-column computation within the arithmetic budget leaves a small, explicitly normed family of longer interactions and a controlled winding term. A separate stability or convergence argument is needed to carry such local perturbations through the remaining exterior integral.

## A neutral end class stable under the strip tail

**PROVED (stability theorem).** There is a stricter version of (10) which absorbs the entire family of long strip interactions using a hand bound. Take the independent-link model of (10) on sides of length at least 16, with
\[
        \|h_p\|_6\le 2^{-30},\qquad \|v_p\|_6\le 1/128,\qquad \theta=2^{-17}.
\]
It includes \(h_p=0,\ v_p=e^{-2J}\) with \(J\ge3\). Permit extra positive factors \(e^{\phi_W(y,z)}\) depending on the auxiliary links and center curvatures in a rectangular window \(W\). For the strip application its spatial vertex set has size \(2\cdot3\cdot3=18\), with time length \(j\), and \(S_W\) denotes those vertices. Count all oriented links and plaquettes read by their base vertices; require their full geometry to lie in this rectangle. Use one spatial direction of columns. At both periods allow \(H< j\le\lfloor T/2\rfloor\), with the same rule for each common window under the cover. The sufficient tail condition is
\[
  \sup_a\ \sum_{W:\,a\in S_W}
           e^{3\theta\,18j}\,\|e^{\phi_W}-1\|_\infty < 2^{-19},               \tag{23}
\]
where \(a\) ranges over vertices. Then, with \(j_0=\lfloor\min(T,m)/8\rfloor\) and \(v=Tm^3\),
\[
       |2\log Z_+(T)-\log Z_+(2T)| \le 8v\,e^{-\theta j_0}.                  \tag{24}
\]
Field-independent factors must lift with the periods as in (11). The inequality gives any strict neutral endpoint margin on sufficiently large common coarse cubes with \(T=\lfloor m/512\rfloor\).

**PROVED (absolute convergence with long windows).** In the expansion for (11), in addition to \(F\) and \(X\) select a set of windows on which to take \(e^{\phi_W}-1\). For fixed \(F\) the curvature input to these functions is \(z=1_F\). Use their supremum norms and integrate the original factors by the six-fold Hölder argument of (10). Give each marked plaquette and each surface plaquette a degree of 16, and a window of length \(j\) a degree of \(18j\). These degrees add to give the exponent of the analytic marking variable. Enlarge the support of a plaquette with base \(b\) to the 16 vertices in \(b+\{0,1\}^4\). These supports include its auxiliary reads, and they overlap whenever two plaquettes occur on one three-cube. Window supports include all their reads. Join labels with overlapping supports. Closure and coefficients, including a character of the three periods, factor on distinct components. In particular a tail factor in one component can depend on the realised surface in that component without coupling it to surfaces in the other components.

**PROVED.** Decompose each connected term into pieces as for (12): a minimal closed surface of area \(k\) has weight bound \(128^{-k}\), degree \(16k\); a singleton plaquette mark has bound \(2^{-30}\), degree 16; a window has bound \(\|e^{\phi_W}-1\|_\infty\), degree \(18j\). Write \(L\) for the degree of a piece, which bounds its support size. A vertex belongs to at most 96 enlarged plaquette supports. With each weight multiplied by \(e^{3\theta L}\), the sum of the first two kinds through a given vertex is at most
\[
 96\left[2^{-30}e^{48\theta}+
       \frac{(5e^{48\theta}/128)^6}{5(1-5e^{48\theta}/128)}\right]<2^{-19}.
\]
Indeed \(e^{48\theta}<4/3\), \(5e^{48\theta}/128<1/16\), and the circuit count in (12) applies. Including (23) gives a total less than \(\theta/2\). To bound connected terms through a fixed vertex, root a deterministic spanning tree of their pieces at a piece through that vertex. For a parent with support size at most \(L\), the sum over *unordered distinct* child trees touching its support is bounded by \(e^{L\theta}\) if the child sum through a vertex is at most \(\theta\). This follows by expanding the product of \(1+\text{weight}\) over all eligible child trees; distinct pieces in an actual configuration have distinct labelled roots. Overlaps and repeated anchors in the bound cause no loss of terms. The set of pieces recovers \(F,X\) and the selected windows. Inducting on tree height, the rooted connected sum with weights multiplied by \(e^{2\theta L}\) is therefore at most \(\theta/2\).

**PROVED (volume and topology).** Allow only configurations whose supports lie in a given vertex set \(D\). In the quotient by the partition on \(D\) with one vertex removed, a term using that vertex has a unique connected component there. The remainder lives outside its support. Deletion at smaller sets has log modulus cost at most \(\theta\) per removed vertex by induction. On the analytic disk of radius \(e^\theta\), the marking and this deletion cost use together at most \(e^{2\theta L}\) per piece. The connected sum just bounded puts the quotient within \(\theta/2\) of one; its analytic logarithm from zero thus has modulus less than \(\theta\). Give each underlying plaquette or window its own marking variable before making the single-degree substitution. **PROVED (the actual strip tail passes (23)).** In the specified band even the core has \(R<128\): \(e<11/4\) from its Taylor series, \(e^{2/3}<2\), and \(2(11/4)^4(1+13\,2^{-20})<128\).        36\cdot2^{15}\sum_{j>H}j\,e^{-7j/1024}<2^{-90}.

For the last inequality the geometric denominator is bounded by \(2^8\) after inversion, and \(e^{-7H/1024}<e^{-111}<2^{-148}\) by \(e^3>16\). These estimates leave a strict margin. The actual magnetic factors and temporal links read by a window of an eliminated column fit in the indicated \(2\times3\times3\) spatial tube across \(j\) slices. The scalar quotients also have the local curvature interpretation required above. Give each remaining fine link its projective representative and center bit and, if necessary, restore dummy fair bits on the eliminated links. **OPEN (the short factors).** The remaining Wilson plaquettes and the \(g_{i,H}\) are positive, often strongly varying functions of the same exterior links. **PROVED (a short angular mode the fixed window must retain).** There is an explicit conditional test of \(g_{i,H}\) in the ordinary Wilson band. Take its temporal links and spatial connectors to be the identity. In slices before \(i-1\) choose the four distinct parallel neighbours of the column in two opposite pairs, so the four magnetic exponents sum to zero. At slices \(i-1,i\) set all four neighbours to \(u,v\in {\rm SU}(2)\), respectively. This is realizable on a spatial side at least five. Use the trivial twist. Write \(f_b(g)=e^{bq(g)}/A(b)\). The exact quotient as a function of these two exterior inputs is
\[
       g_{i,H}(u,v)=A(\beta)A(4\beta)\,
             (f_{4\beta}*f_\beta*f_{4\beta})(u v^{-1}).                       \tag{25}
\]
The zero-field prefix leaves Haar at slice \(i-1\). The denominator supplies \(A(4\beta)\) there; in the numerator the two magnetic weights and the last electric link give the three convolutions. The common prefix scalars cancel. Spatial Wilson factors independent of the eliminated column are elsewhere in the remaining exterior law. Equation (25) tests the actual scalar produced by (21), uniformly in \(H\ge2\).

**PROVED.** Even after averaging \(u\) and \(v\) over their center signs separately, the positive normalized kernel in (25) has an adjoint multiplier \(r_2(4\beta)^2 r_2(\beta)>1/35\), with multiplicity nine; the constant eigenvalue is one. Here \(r_j(b)=I_{j+1}(b)/I_1(b)\) as in row 1341 (28), from the one-link multipliers of Sussman and Alpoge, with Claude and Codex. To see the bound, \(I_4/I_3\le\beta/8\) by the positive series, so \(I_3/I_2=\beta/(6+\beta I_4/I_3)>2/7\) in the band. Together with (16)'s \(r_1>2/5\) this gives \(r_2(\beta)>4/35\). The recurrence also gives \(r_2(b)=1-4r_1(b)/b>1-4/b\ge1/2\) at \(b=4\beta\ge8\). Center averaging keeps the whole twice-spin-two space. Consequently any separated rank-at-most-nine kernel on \(u,v\) misses the normalized even kernel in operator norm by more than \(1/35\): its kernel meets the ten-dimensional space of constants and adjoint coefficients. A pointwise error of at most \(1/35\) *relative to the true even kernel* would imply operator error at most \(1/35\), by Cauchy–Schwarz under that doubly stochastic kernel and then Haar integration. Thus a uniformly accurate product of two one-slice scalar weights fails even on this restricted family, including after the extra center averages. This is an adjacent-time mode of the exterior law. Its size neither measures nor rules out the decay of the fully integrated winding term.

## Outstanding block induction

**OPEN.** The unresolved implication is uniform in the fine cutoff. Starting with the polynomial trajectory in (1), including its residual for exact transport, one needs a sequence of block maps whose unnormalized measures on the two periods have the required common scalar bookkeeping. One must show that their exterior-dependent short interactions enter an end class such as (23)–(24), with a common strict margin on one admissible physical side. An alternative is to bound the positive angular integral in (20) at both periods. The first colour in (21)–(23) controls the temporal tail at homogeneous order-one Wilson coupling. The short factors still interact across the remaining spatial links, and the induced action at a first-passage scale need not have the original Wilson form. Neither admission of the whole cutoff trajectory to a fixed order-one block class nor closure of such a class under another spatial merge has been obtained. The bounds (16) and (25) specify interactions that uniform scalar approximations would lose.

The latter rational-operation bounds do not bound bit lengths for arbitrary rational exteriors. No integral was evaluated by a large run, no Lean certificate was checked, and no complete one-block certificate for the outstanding implication is specified. RESULT — OPEN. At order-one Wilson coupling one colour of spatial links admits an exact mass-preserving strip elimination with a finite-window angular factor, a tail satisfying (23), and an exponentially bounded winding term. The cutoff-uniform block step carrying the interacting short factors of the Wilson trajectory into the neutral end class remains open. Neither (N-phys), the periodic (SEED-L), nor the Jaffe–Witten mass gap follows here.
