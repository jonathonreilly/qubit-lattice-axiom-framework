**The crux, in one paragraph.** At an order-one coupling (bare beta_W about 2.3 to 2.6; in truth the Sussman-Alpoge first-passage action carried a few more block steps), one must show that the odd electric-flux sector of a time slice has exponentially small weight: Q_F <= C a^p eta_*^(a/2), with a rate bounded in physical units. ## PHASE 2. Diverge (unjudged)

Working remark used below (ARGUED, mine, from (E16)-(E17) at t = a): p = Tr(P_- L^a)/Tr L^a is at most (lambda_0^-/lambda_0^+)^a times a multiplicity factor. ### Batch B (off-axis and "dumb" on purpose), ideas 11-20

11. **Error correction.** Read the twist class as the logical bit of a four-dimensional toric code, and the SO(3) part of the field as the noise record. (E17) says the code is above threshold: the class cannot be decoded. Prove non-decodability by channel degradation: show the SO(3) record is a degraded copy of a record already known to be useless.
12. **Modular bootstrap for the torus.** Axis exchange is the lattice's modular symmetry. 13. **Escape from subgroups (additive combinatorics).** The proper closed subgroups of SU(2) are U(1), its normalizer and the finite groups: exactly the rival models. This is rigorous, size-uniform, true for every connected group and false for finite groups. Dumb use: make the consumer tori flat enough that Q_F < 1/192 is free.

Needs: a lower bound in the mean; the worst case is still negative at beta 2.3 (off-diagonal Hessian blocks of size beta/4 each). Opens: an averaged criterion (G6) and the link to diamagnetism: G_U is short-ranged exactly when the background has no covariantly constant frame.

20. **Roaming Dirac sheet.** Kernel: the position of a sign sheet is free, but a thick vortex is not a sheet. A coupling of the two laws needs mixing faster than the size, which needs the gap. Circular. Opens: nothing.

Opens: idea 33; one local family containing nu_ce, the full sum and (in one sector) Wilson.

Needs: nothing hard. It is a toy: the mixed planes are at strong coupling, so it is not near the continuum limit. Opens: a second solvable corner from which a pattern (idea 32) can be anchored.

Opens: with C-periodic conditions the U(1) rival fails only through its photon, that is through (Z-UNB); a cleaner statement of the U(1) test, no route.

### 3c. Kernels of ideas 29-42

A second reading note first (READ `library/gap-means-max.md` L144-226): Theorem R says that (ES), two numbers of the even sector (eps = S_ev(a; a^3) and the vacuum-energy defect delta = a(8 e(a^3) - e((2a)^3)), both at most 1e-7), gives two of the three seeds whatever the pattern, hence the gap. So the pattern need not be chosen. The flux enters only through delta. And delta is a thermal quantity: with e(b) = b f + C(b), 2 e(a) - e(2a) = 2 C(a) - C(2a) is the thermal pressure, per unit length, of an infinite tube of cross-section (c, d) at temperature 1/a (ARGUED, mine). So delta small says: every excitation of the tube, glue or winding flux, is heavy against 1/a.

29. **Dichotomy.** Kernel: Theorem R already is the dichotomy inside gapped phases, and it shows the pattern is irrelevant for the gap. A dichotomy "gap or massless vector" needs a monotone parameter; none is proved for SU(2) (GKS fails on non-orientable surfaces at least, Corollary NS0). Opens: nothing beyond Theorem R.

But the killing property needs a non-degenerate S-matrix, and Rep SU(2) is symmetric: the mechanism disappears as k grows. Needs: a uniform bound in k, which is the crux. Opens: the remark that in SU(2) any rigidity of a dense half-integer net must come from signs in recoupling, since weights alone (a positive loop gas above its percolation point) give parity 1/2. This matches Corollary NS0.

Needs: a consumer with a vacuum multiplet, and the account redone for a mixed action. Opens: the difficulty of the flux half depends on the regularization; and the two halves have different rivals. Frozen finite groups are gapped, so they bind only the flux half.
32. **Pattern selection.** Kernel: superseded by the reading note above. (ES) gives the gap for every pattern. Drop.
33. **Global forms.** Kernel: periodic, full, kappa are SU(2), SO(3)+, SO(3)- (ARGUED; the sign (-1)^Pf(k) is the discrete theta term). Theorem R is 't Hooft's classification with constants. Needs: nothing. Opens: vocabulary; the centre-Villain family of idea 21 holds all three forms.

Needs: non-percolation of active surfaces, false at beta 2.3 (activity 0.88). It is the thin-vortex mechanism. Opens: a blocked version would need a centre projection on blocks, which is a gauge choice.
35. **Zero conditional mean.** Kernel (PROVED, two lines): the centre flip across a closed cocycle is a symmetry of the Z_2 conditional law for every environment, so E[g | SO(3) data] = 0 for odd g, and the norm of g is the mean Z_2-fibre variance. ### 3d. Second-generation ideas (each with its parents)

G1. **Transverse reflection positivity fuses two flux tubes** (from 3, 4, 27, 36, and the fusion input (F-h) of `gap-es1-opus.md`). The lane says (F-h) needs a cluster estimate in twisted sectors. Reflection in a direction transverse to the flux gives the missing locality for free: for a centre-odd F built in one half-box, <F theta F> >= <F>^2. With F = O(0) O(t), O a winding-line operator, the left side is the propagator of the pair "line and mirror line", an even state; the right side is the square of the one-line propagator. So the lightest even excitation weighs at most two flux tubes. The lane tried reflection with global twists, where two equal twists cancel (`gap-es1-opus.md` L232); two local lines do not cancel.
G2. **Witten Laplacian on the orbit space, with a convex-paramagnetic split** (from 18, 5, 36, 38, 14). The Langevin gap equals the bottom of the Witten Laplacian on exact one-forms, W1 = -L + Ric + beta Hess S, a matrix Schrodinger operator. Split Hess(-x_p)(xi, xi) = x_p |Xi_p|^2 + (1/2) sum_(e<e') <F_p, [xi_e, xi_e']>: a covariant-curl term, non-negative on positive plaquettes, and a paramagnetic commutator term, absent for U(1). Dobrushin-type criteria pay for the off-diagonal part of the curl term; here only the paramagnetic term and negative plaquettes cost. The quotient by the gauge group adds the O'Neill term. This is an exact continuous-time form of the annealed condition that failed in discrete time (row 852: a schedule may repeat one jump; Langevin has no schedule).
G3. **Lasso martingale** (from 13, 17). Area law if the conditional mean matrix of the n-th lasso, given the earlier lassos of the surface, is a contraction that does not depend on the previous lasso. A restatement, kept as a diagnostic: it names memory between neighbouring lassos as the whole difference between area and perimeter.
G4. **(ES) is a low-temperature equation of state** (from 29, 31, 32 and the reading of Theorem R). eps is the thermal sum of the even sector of the cube at temperature 1/a; delta is the thermal pressure of the tubes at temperature 1/a. (ES) says the theory at T = 1/a, about a third of the believed deconfinement temperature for a m about 16 (GUIDE, recalled lattice numbers), has pressure below 1e-7 in units of T^4 a^... . Rivals of (ES) are gapless models only. Frozen Q_8 and 2T satisfy (ES) (they are gapped; their pattern is the electric one). So the Q_8/2T test binds only routes that pass through the confining pattern, such as (E17).
G5. **Two rivals, two inputs** (from 4, 6, 31, G4). Against U(1): positive Ricci curvature of S^3 and the absence of fractional Z_2 charge; both are zeroth-order facts. Against frozen finite groups: connectedness, used only to exclude long-range order of winding lines. Do not look for one input with both properties (W15); look for two lemmas.
G6. **The flux half is one order-one statement, given the glue half** (from G1, G4, 37). On the symmetric torus a^4 let h(r) be the correlation of two parallel winding lines at transverse distance r. Fusion in both transverse readings gives h(r)^2 <= h(s)^2 + e^(-m r) for r < s, with m the even gap of the box: h^2 is a plateau plus an exponential. Either the plateau is there (winding lines have long-range order: the electric pattern) or h decays at rate m/2. So the flux half is: h(a/2) <= h(a/4)/sqrt 2. In words: a lower bound on the deconfinement temperature, T_c >= 1/a at the seed size, with a factor of about 7 of room (GUIDE).
G7. **The 2D O(4) twin** (from 16, 22, 14, 1). The SU(2) principal chiral model in two dimensions with the antiperiodic twist s -> -s has every feature of the crux: asymptotic freedom, a twist in a connected group, XY as the Coulomb rival (Kosterlitz-Thouless phase), spins in a finite subgroup of S^3 as the frozen rivals, Dobrushin ceilings at order one, and two dimensions marginal for the twist as four are for the gauge twist. Run Theorem S, Theorem R and the fusion argument there. Extra tools exist only there: correlation inequalities for at most four components, and a Jensen twist bound that works because the symmetry is global.
G8. **Three dimensions through the slab, and a warning** (from 22, 1, 23, Durhuus-Frohlich). In three dimensions the slab model is a two-dimensional O(4)-type model, expected massive at every coupling, so the layer route has no flat-background ceiling there. Warning (ARGUED): the flat background is not the worst. For equal rough slices U' = U the slab energy is maximal exactly at V = +1 and V = -1 and the continuous symmetry is broken down to Z_2 by the background, so the slab can order like an Ising model, also in two dimensions. A non-abelian diamagnetic inequality in the form "flat is worst" is false. Mean-field theory for this pinned model gives threshold exactly 2/3, the lane's Dobrushin number.
G9. **Twist as colour-rotated periodicity** (from 15, 24, 37). Z(twist) equals the partition function with the standard weight on every plaquette and the gluing U(x + L_1 e_1) = sigma_1 U(x) sigma_1, U(x + L_2 e_2) = sigma_2 U(x) sigma_2 (PROVED by the twist-eater change of variables). No stack. The two partition functions are fixed-point integrals of two Z_2 x Z_2 actions on the doubled torus that agree on every gauge-invariant local function. The deficit is then the difference of two "orbifold" partition functions.
G10. **Planted centre with a positive doubled current** (from 2, 41, 34, 16). Where the centre couplings have one sign (small beta_F in a mixed action, or after removing Z_2 monopoles), the twist ratio is exactly a non-percolation probability of a doubled plaquette current. Gives a clean percolation picture of the thin-vortex regime and of its end.
G11. **A consumer with a vacuum multiplet** (from 31, 33, 21). Rewrite Theorem S and Theorem R for a transfer operator with a known finite quasi-degenerate vacuum multiplet (the SO(3)+ form). Then the regularization of idea 31 makes the flux half a Z_2 expansion, uniform along the continuum limit, and the crux is the glue half alone.
G12. **Motional narrowing for the tangent flow** (from G2, 9). The paramagnetic operator has entries of fixed size and directions that rotate with the links. The link relaxation rate (Ric plus the staple curvature) sets a correlation time. Ask for a rigorous second-order bound: effective coupling at most (size)^2 x (correlation time), a Kato-type bound for the Feynman-Kac matrix cocycle.
G13. **Low spins only see a few moments; use 2I as a finite twin** (from 6, 31, G4). At beta 2.3 to 2.6 the binary icosahedral model is in the SU(2)-like phase (GUIDE, row 838) and its configuration space is finite. Finite-range, finite-spin systems have constructive finite-size criteria (Dobrushin-Shlosman). This is not a proof route under the no-computation rule; it is a twin where any conjectured inequality can be refuted exactly on small tori.
G14. **Half-box creation as a frame bound** (from G1, 26, 35). The missing step of G1 at trace level: smeared winding lines in the inner half-box capture a polynomial fraction of the odd thermal trace, Tr(L^(a/2) G) >= a^(-p) Tr(L^(a/2) P_-), G = sum_x |W_x Omega><W_x Omega|. Equivalent to: the conditional expectation of the lowest odd wave functions on half the box is not small.
G15. **Thermal pressure by a tube transfer** (from G4, 37, 12). delta is a pressure of an infinite tube. Read it with the tube axis as time: delta is then a ratio of top eigenvalues of transfer operators on boxes (a, c, d) and (2a, c, d). Ask for an operator inequality between the doubled-box operator and the tensor square of the single-box one in the even sector; fusion (G1) is the statement that the difference lives on two-tube states.
G16. **S1 as the anchor of a twin statement at weak coupling** (from 10, 15, G6). On the twisted thin torus the lines winding the thin directions are provably ordered or disordered by a weak-coupling computation. That gives a rigorous value of the plateau ratio of G6 at small size. The crux is then how the ratio moves with the size: one monotone function of one variable, h(a/2)/h(a/4), between a computed start and a wanted end.

### 3e. Two more second-generation ideas, found while settling G1 and G6

G17. **Fusion by squaring: no locality needed** (from G1, 4, 26, 35, and the Dirichlet-form reading of idea 5). The ground-state transform P = Omega^(-1) (L/lambda_0) Omega of a positive transfer operator is a Markov operator, reversible for mu = Omega^2. If g is the top eigenfunction of a flux sector, P g = theta g, then by Jensen P(g^2) >= (P g)^2 = theta^2 g^2 pointwise. But g^2 - 1 is centre-even and orthogonal to the vacuum. So the even sector has a state of "energy" at most twice the flux energy, up to a factor that depends only on the fourth moment kappa = Int g^4 d mu:

  1 - theta^2 >= (1 - 1/kappa)(1 - rho), rho = lambda_1^+/lambda_0 the second even eigenvalue ratio.

  In continuous time (a diffusion generator) the exact identity <g^2 - 1, H (g^2 - 1)> = (4/3) E kappa gives m <= (4/3) E kappa/(kappa - 1), with equality for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (kappa = 3: m = 2E). So "two flux tubes make an even state" is a two-line consequence of positivity. The lane's rows 751, 752, 757 say (F-h) needs a cluster estimate in the twisted sectors. It does not: it needs one number, the kurtosis of the flux ground state. kappa near 1 means g is nearly a sign function, which is spontaneous breaking of the centre symmetry: the light branch of 't Hooft's dichotomy. Details and the proof are in Phase 4.
G18. **A window for the kurtosis** (from G17, G6). The same inequality read backwards: a flux state lighter than half the even gap must be flat-topped, kappa <= m/(m - 2E) in the continuous-time form. So between "light" and "heavier than m/2" the ground state would have to be more and more like a sign function without being degenerate with the vacuum. The flux half becomes: exclude that window. It is a regularity statement about one function, not a confinement statement.

### 4c. Top 1 attempted: fusion by squaring

**Setting.** X = SU(2)^E with normalized Haar measure dU. L is a self-adjoint integral operator on L^2(dU) with a continuous, symmetric, strictly positive kernel L(U, U'). The lane's operators T_(B,m) are of this kind: the kernel is an integral of exponentials of plaquette terms, with or without sign flips (READ `gap-means-max.md` L150, (TM-tw); the strict positivity of the kernel is ARGUED from that form, not read line by line). A finite group Gamma of flips acts on X, preserves dU and commutes with L. Sectors are the characters chi of Gamma.

**Step 1 (ground-state transform; PROVED, standard).** By Jentzsch's theorem the top eigenvalue lambda_0 is simple and its eigenfunction Omega is continuous and strictly positive; it is Gamma-invariant. Put mu = Omega^2 dU (a probability measure after normalization) and (P f)(U) = (lambda_0 Omega(U))^(-1) Int L(U, U') Omega(U') f(U') dU'. Then P has a positive kernel, P 1 = 1, and mu(dU) P(U, dU') is symmetric. So P is a Markov operator, self-adjoint on L^2(mu). The map f -> Omega f is unitary from L^2(mu) to L^2(dU) and carries P to L/lambda_0. Sectors are preserved.

**Step 2 (Lemma SQ; PROVED).** Let g be real, P g = theta g, Int g^2 d mu = 1, kappa := Int g^4 d mu finite. Then for every integer t >= 1: <g^2, P^t g^2> >= theta^(2t) kappa.
Proof. P^t is a Markov operator. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the probability measure P^t(U, .): (P^t g)^2 <= P^t(g^2) pointwise. The left side is theta^(2t) g^2. Multiply by g^2 >= 0 and integrate against mu. QED

**Step 3 (Theorem SQ; PROVED).** Let chi != 0 be a sector, Psi its top eigenvector (norm 1 in L^2(dU)), lambda_chi its eigenvalue, g = Psi/Omega, kappa = Int Psi^4/Omega^2 dU. Let lambda_1^+ be the second largest eigenvalue of L in the trivial sector. Then for every t >= 1:

  (lambda_chi/lambda_0)^(2t) <= 1/kappa + (1 - 1/kappa)(lambda_1^+/lambda_0)^t.     (SQ)

Proof. g is continuous (Psi = L Psi/lambda_chi is continuous; Omega is bounded below), so kappa is finite. g^2 is Gamma-invariant because chi takes values +-1. Put h = g^2 - 1. Then <h, 1> = 0, so h lies in the trivial sector, orthogonal to the vacuum, and <h, P^t h> <= (lambda_1^+/lambda_0)^t ||h||^2 = (lambda_1^+/lambda_0)^t (kappa - 1). Also <g^2, P^t g^2> = 1 + <h, P^t h> because P^t 1 = 1. Lemma SQ gives theta^(2t) kappa <= 1 + rho^t (kappa - 1). QED

With E = -log(lambda_chi/lambda_0) and m = -log(lambda_1^+/lambda_0), t = 1: 1 - e^(-2E) >= (1 - 1/kappa)(1 - e^(-m)), so E >= (1/2)(1 - 1/kappa)(1 - e^(-m)). No reflection positivity, no locality, no clustering is used. The statement holds for every group and every positive-kernel model.

**Step 4 (diffusion form; PROVED for a diffusion generator).** If H = -L_diff >= 0 is a self-adjoint diffusion generator on L^2(mu) (chain rule L phi(f) = phi'(f) L f + phi''(f) Gamma(f)), H g = E g, Int g^2 = 1, then Int Gamma(g^2) d mu = 4 Int g^2 Gamma(g) = (4/3) Int Gamma(g^3, g) = (4/3) <g^3, H g> = (4/3) E kappa. Hence m <= (4/3) E kappa/(kappa - 1). For the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck generator and g = x: E = 1, kappa = 3, and the bound is m <= 2, attained by the second Hermite polynomial.
