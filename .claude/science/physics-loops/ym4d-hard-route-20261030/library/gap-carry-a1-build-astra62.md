**PROVED by hand, on the stationary profiles (8d).** Let the old slab be one nonempty interval of cells on the transverse circle. The complementary slab is nonempty. Use the small regular branch from row 1569. Fix its odd block size $L\ge3$ and an aligned period large enough for the double blocks. Set the last coarse spacing to one. The determining intervals $J_m$ have width $h_m=1/L$ on the old side and $h_m=1$ on the coarse side. At bare spacing $\eta\le1$, the horizontal links are $\exp(iu_iH)$ with $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$. The other links are the identity. The prescribed mean of $u_i/\eta$ is $-\tau$ on every old interval and zero elsewhere. The reference profile has $\tau=0$. In (8d) this is the B profile and the flat A profile, respectively.

Let $\mathcal B$ contain the first and last old intervals, counted once if they coincide. For any bare site $i$ in interval $m$, define the signed horizontal Wilson force with unit plaquette weight by
$$
 I_m=\frac{h_m}{\eta^3}
 \left[\sin(u_{i+1}-u_i)-\sin(u_i-u_{i-1})\right].
 \tag{1}
$$
The sign inside the brackets is that of the negative action derivative for $1-\cos$. The factor $h_m/\eta^3$ is part of the definition. On the stationary solutions the value is independent of $i$ in that interval. For $\tau\ne0$,
$$
 \max_{m\in\mathcal B}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau) I_m\ \ge\ |\tau|/3,
 \qquad \max_m |I_m|\ \le\ 15|\tau| .
 \tag{2}
$$
At $\tau=0$ all forces vanish. The constants work for both meshes, every slab width, and every period with the two embedded cuts. Equation (2) holds at finite amplitude on the branch specified below.

**Proof.** Write $r_e$ for the flux at each interface between determining intervals. Let $\phi_e$ be the piecewise linear hat with value one at $e$ and zero at the other interfaces. Put $p(t)=\sum_e\phi_e(t)r_e$. The exact stationary solution of row 1569 (2) has $\sin(u_{i+1}-u_i)=\eta^2p(t_{i+1/2})$. Since $p$ is affine on $J_m$, (1) gives $I_m=r_{\mathrm{right}(m)}-r_{\mathrm{left}(m)}$. Both plaquettes at the cut enter this equation.

Let $d_e=f_{m+1}-f_m$ be the jump of the prescribed mean at $e$. Write $b$ for the vector that is $-1$ at entry into the old slab, $+1$ at exit and zero elsewhere. Thus $d=\tau b$. Use row 1569 (3) before division by $H_e=(h_m+h_{m+1})/2$. Sum over bare faces $t$. With $T_\eta(v)=\eta^{-2}\arcsin(\eta^2v)$ that equation is
$$
 d_e=\eta\sum_t\phi_e(t)T_\eta(p(t))=(M r)_e,
 \qquad M_{ef}=\eta\sum_t\phi_e(t)\phi_f(t)
             \int_0^1 T_\eta'(s p(t))\,ds .
 \tag{3}
$$
This is an exact secant matrix for the given solution. Fix $\|r\|_\infty\le1/4$ before choosing the mesh. Row 1569 obtains this branch from $6\max_e |d_e|/H_e\le1/4$. For instance $|\tau|\le1/48$ suffices for this inequality at either interface. The regular-chart and history tests still apply at their own radii.

The derivative in (3) lies between $1$ and $4/\sqrt{15}<6/5$. With the integral replaced by one, the matrix has row sum $H_e$, diagonal at least $2H_e/3$, and off-diagonal sum at most $H_e/3$ by row 1569 (4). Each entry is nonnegative. The hats meet only at adjacent intervals. Thus $M$ has diagonal $D_e\ge2H_e/3$, off-diagonal row sum at most $(3/5)D_e$, and row sum at most $6H_e/5$. It is symmetric and strictly diagonally dominant with positive diagonal, hence positive definite. Also $M\preceq(6/5)I$ since $H_e\le1$. Both spectral bounds follow by bounding each cross term with $2|vw|\le v^2+w^2$.

Take $R=\max_e|r_e|$. At an unforced face ($d_e=0$), (3) gives $|r_e|\le3R/5$. For $\tau\ne0$, a maximizing face is therefore one of the two interfaces of the old slab. At that face the diagonal term dominates, so $r_e$ has the sign of $d_e$. The row-sum bound gives $R\ge5|\tau|/6$. Now suppose the slab has two or more intervals. Its next face towards its interior is unforced. The first old interval at the maximizing face therefore has
$$
 \operatorname{sgn}(\tau) I_m\ge R-3R/5\ge|\tau|/3 .
$$
For a single old interval its flux difference is $b^{\mathsf T}r$. Equation (3) gives $b^{\mathsf T}r=\tau b^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}b$. Since $\|b\|_2^2=2$, it has the required sign and magnitude at least $5|\tau|/3$. For the upper bound, at a maximizing face we have $|d_e|\ge(2/5)D_eR$. ### Which variation sees the force

 \tag{4}
