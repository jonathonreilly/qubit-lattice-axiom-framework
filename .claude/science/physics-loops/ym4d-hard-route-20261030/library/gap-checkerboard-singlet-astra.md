## Step 3 result: the exact port for (a)

**Lemma DEC — PROVED (hand argument; not independently checked).** Let
G be a finite nearest-neighbor subgraph of Z^2, with no boundary pins.
For each edge e={x,y}, introduce an S^3 variable v_e with product Haar
reference measure. Put

\[
 d\widehat\mu\ \propto
 \exp\!\left[b\sum_{e=\{x,y\}}
       (\rho_x\cdot v_e+v_e\cdot\rho_y)\right]
 \prod_x d\omega(\rho_x)\prod_e d\omega(v_e).
 \tag{DEC1}
\]

Its marginal on the rho variables is exactly the law in (a), including
the normalization. Indeed the already established GH2-0 Haar integral gives

\[
 \int_{S^3}e^{b(\rho+\rho')\cdot v}\,d\omega(v)
 =\omega\bigl(b|\rho+\rho'|\bigr)
 =f_b(\rho\cdot\rho').                         \tag{DEC2}
\]

At fixed rho the edge integrals factor. Fubini applies since these are
bounded positive functions on a finite compact product. Thus both the
partition function and every original-spin numerator agree exactly.
This uses the prior Haar integral; it does not repeat its derivation.

The expanded graph is an actual square-lattice subgraph: place rho_(i,j)
at (2i,2j), the horizontal v at (2i+1,2j), and the vertical v at
(2i,2j+1). Retain exactly the two half-edges of each original edge.
Odd-odd sites and any extra bonds can be omitted or given zero strength.
Consequently every present edge has strength b, with all others zero.
Free boundaries remain free. An n-by-m torus becomes a 2n-by-2m torus
with that same periodic pattern, so the source's even-period requirement
is met, including when n or m is odd. Every unoriented original bond is
counted once; for a small torus with parallel-edge conventions use its
distinct midpoints. Self-loop factors are constants and can be removed.

For F supported on A, put
`L_F=||F||_infty+max_{x in A} Lip_x(F)`, using round S^3 distance
with other coordinates fixed (the maximum over an empty support is zero).
Define its lift by ignoring every v_e. Then

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_{\mu}(F,G)
 =\operatorname{Cov}_{\widehat\mu}(F,G),\qquad
 L_{\widehat F}=L_F,\quad |\widehat A|=|A|,
 \quad d(\widehat A,\widehat A')=2d(A,A').       \tag{DEC3}
\]

The distance is the ambient maximum distance, periodically interpreted
on a torus, exactly as in OpenAI §3. Each original coordinate retains
the same round metric; the individual Lipschitz constants in auxiliary
coordinates are zero. Thus, **given OpenAI Theorem 3.1**, on every free
subgraph and on every expanded torus with sides at least C Xi(b),

\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu}(F,G)|
 \le C(1+b+|A|+|A'|+2d)^{20}L_FL_G
               e^{-2cd/\Xi(b)}.                \tag{DEC4}
\]

This is a port of the full observable theorem, not merely its spin
two-point corollary. It includes invariant local observables.

**Why the literal substitution is unnecessary, and would be wrong.**
Write V(t)=log f_b(t). With the source's quaternion bond t+s and a
one-axis edge difference delta, the correct contact is

\[
 D^2[-V(t)]=\{V'(t)t-V''(t)(s\cdot a)^2\}\delta^2.
 \tag{DEC5}
\]

The first derivative is V'(t)(s dot a) delta. Directly replacing `w_e`
by V'(t) in (3.6) loses the second term of (DEC5), and differentiating
the current also differentiates V'. For example the convergent series
at t=-1 gives

\[
 f_b(t)=1+\frac{b^2}{4}(1+t)
          +\frac{b^4}{48}(1+t)^2+O((1+t)^3),
 \qquad V''(-1)=-\frac{b^4}{48}<0.             \tag{DEC6}
\]

By continuity the omitted term is nonzero on suitable interior bonds.
This refutes that *literal identity*, not a possible generalized-current

For coupling differentiation use b_1,b_2 on the two half-edges if needed:

\[
 f_{b_1,b_2}(t)=\omega(\sqrt{b_1^2+b_2^2+2b_1b_2t}),
 \quad
 \partial_{b_1}\log f_{b_1,b_2}(t)

 \tag{DEC7}
\]

## Step 4 result: all torus sizes and the precise conclusion for (a)

A bounded-diameter torus is handled by the trivial covariance bound.

**Lemma STRIP — PROVED (hand argument; not independently checked).**
Fix b>0 and an integer W. On any unpinned rectangular O(4) torus with
one side w<=W and arbitrary strengths in [0,b], there is exponential
mixing with constants depending only on b,W and support sizes, uniformly
in the other side. It holds also with missing bonds.

Regard a column as one state X in (S^3)^w. Put all within-column factors
in its positive reference measure. The factor connecting consecutive
columns is K_j(X,Y), with

\[
 e^{-bw}\le K_j(X,Y)\le e^{bw}.                 \tag{ST1}
\]

Condition on the end columns of any interval. Its left-to-right
transition has the form

\[
 P_j(X,dY)=\frac{K_j(X,Y)h_j(Y)\nu_j(dY)}
                    {\int K_j(X,Z)h_j(Z)\nu_j(dZ)}
 \ \ge\ e^{-2bW}\,Q_j(dY),                    \tag{ST2}
\]

where h_j is the positive right-boundary message and Q_j is the
probability measure proportional to h_j nu_j, independent of X.
All messages are finite positive integrals on compact spaces. Thus two
such chains with the same right boundary but different left boundaries
can be coupled at each step using their common Q_j part, of mass at
least epsilon=exp(-2bW). Once equal, use identical transitions afterwards.
The probability of remaining different after k steps is at most
q^k, q=1-epsilon<1. This is a coupling proof of the bound, not a
simulation. Reverse the chain to treat a change of the right boundary.
Changing both ends costs the sum of the two bounds.

Let I,J be the column supports of F,G. If I and J are disjoint, condition
on all J columns. The remaining intervals are independent. An interval
containing an I column has two conditioned ends, and every I column
is at cyclic column distance at least D=dist(I,J) from J. Couple two
choices of those ends as above. In each interval the chance of a
disagreement on any required I column is at most 2q^D: for each end
start with the nearest required column, after which a successful coupling
persists. There are at most |I| such intervals. The product coupling and
the union bound give

\[
 \operatorname{osc}\mathbb E[F\mid X_J]
       \le4\|F\|_\infty |I|q^D,
 \quad
 |\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)|
       \le4\|F\|_\infty\|G\|_\infty |I|q^D.    \tag{ST3}
\]

For ambient maximum distance d>W, every pair of support sites must be
separated by at least d in the long direction, since their short-direction
periodic distance is at most W/2. Hence D>=d and I,J are disjoint.
For d<=W use `|Cov|<=2||F|| ||G||` and enlarge the fixed prefactor by
exp((-log q)W). This proves the assertion for all d, including short
cycles. No unbounded-width pin theorem was used.

Apply STRIP to the expanded torus with
W=ceil(C Xi(b))+4, using the threshold constant of OpenAI Theorem 3.1.
If both sides exceed its threshold use (DEC4); otherwise use STRIP.
Very small simple tori are treated with the same column argument, or
with zero-padded width four after deleting constant self-loops. All widths
below the threshold are bounded by this single W.

\[
 M_\kappa:=\mathbb E[y_1y_2y_3y_4]
       =\frac{I_3(\kappa)}{27 I_1(\kappa)}>0.    \tag{NA1}
\]

To verify it without any enumeration, note that y(q^{-1})=y(q), and
that normalized Haar convolution obeys

\[
 (y*y)(h)=\int y(g)y(g^{-1}h)\,dg=\frac{y(h)}3.
 \tag{NA2}
\]

Indeed R(g)e is uniform on S^2; its coordinate second moments are
delta_ab/3 by rotations and the identity sum_a v_a^2=1. Expanding the
two matrix coefficients gives (NA2). Set r_1=q_1, r_2=q_2,
r_3=q_3^{-1}, r_4=q_4^{-1}. Three applications of (NA2), with the
product H=r_1 r_2 r_3 r_4 fixed, give the convolution density y(H)/27.
The normalized central convolution by exp(kappa Re H) has adjoint
(spin-one) eigenvalue I_3(kappa)/I_1(kappa). This is exactly GL3 of
Sussman–Alpoge, outline 2210–2231, with its constant e^{-kappa}
canceled and its trivial eigenvalue divided out. Equivalently centrality
makes the mean of R(H) scalar and the adjoint character computes that
scalar. The positive Bessel series in the same source makes the ratio
strictly positive for kappa>0. These steps prove (NA1).

Now take the increasing functions on [-1,1]^4

\[
 F=y_1+y_2-y_1y_2,\qquad G=y_3+y_4+y_3y_4.
 \tag{NA3}
\]

Each of their nonzero individual derivatives is 1+y or 1-y, hence
nonnegative. Their means vanish, and all terms in FG except the
four-variable product vanish. Therefore

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}(F,G)
      =-M_\kappa=-\frac{I_3(\kappa)}{27 I_1(\kappa)}<0.
 \tag{NA4}
\]

For the alpha order, use -F(cos 2alpha) and -G(cos 2alpha). They are
increasing in their respective alpha coordinates and have the same
negative covariance. Also every cross pair of alpha coordinates is
independent. Thus the right side of an attempted (3.63) for these
functions would be zero while the absolute covariance is positive.

These test functions concern the **intermediate amplitude law**.
They are not claimed to be corner singlets, and their same-site
covariance is not a failure of spatial mixing. This is precisely a
counterexample to the proposed association supplier, within the requested
unpinned P4 parameter class.

### Attempt 2: change to conditional ferromagnetic signs — FAILS

**Lemma P4-SIGN — PROVED (hand counterexample; not independently checked).**
Write q_i=tau_i v_i, where tau_i is an independent fair sign in the
Haar reference measure and v_i is in the hemisphere Re v_i>=0.
The equator is Haar-null. Conditional on the v_i, the actual one-site
P4 sign law is

\[
 p(\tau\mid v)=\frac{\exp(J\tau_1\tau_2\tau_3\tau_4)}
                         {16\cosh J},
 \qquad J=\kappa\operatorname{Re}
             (v_1v_2v_3^{-1}v_4^{-1}).          \tag{SG1}
\]

There is an open set of positive measure with all v_i close to 1,
so J>0. At fixed tau_3=1, tau_4=-1, the remaining pair has weight
exp(-J tau_1 tau_2). Its lattice-condition ratio is

\[
 \frac{p(+,+)p(-,-)}{p(+,-)p(-,+)}=e^{-4J}<1.   \tag{SG2}
\]

Thus this sign decomposition produces an antiferromagnetic conditional
pair. It cannot supply the nonnegative pair-coupling Ising/FK step of
OpenAI §3.11.1 or the FK conditional law used by DHY Theorem 3.6.
For the full four-sign law, the increasing functions (NA3), with y_i
replaced by tau_i, have covariance -tanh J<0 as well.

The obstruction is not confined to a conditioning on a null endpoint of
the source's coordinate decomposition. Fix the absolute values of all
four real quaternion components, with scalar magnitude close to 1 and
each imaginary magnitude small and strictly positive; fix the imaginary
signs and leave the four scalar signs free. At the limiting magnitudes
(1,0,0,0) their lattice-condition ratio is e^{-4kappa}. The finitely
many weights are continuous in the magnitudes. The strict violation
therefore persists on an open set of interior magnitudes of positive
measure. This is also a failure of the natural coordinate-sign version
of the attempted conditional-ferromagnet argument.

This does **not** refute Ginibre's cosine-character inequality. In
particular a positive four-angle cosine term on an abelian circle is
within the larger character class of DHY (2.41). What fails here is the
nonnegative conditional **pair-Ising** structure needed to pass to
arbitrary local observables.

### Attempt 3: remove the on-site term by the same auxiliary-spin port — FAILS

**Lemma P4-LIFT — PROVED for the following specified representation.**
No unpinned finite network of ordinary untwisted O(4) pair bonds with
independent Haar auxiliary spins has the one-site P4 density as its
marginal on the four original q_i terminals when kappa>0.

Every such network, and every marginal of it, is invariant under a
common O(4) rotation of all terminal spins: perform that rotation on
the auxiliary variables as well. This remains true for arbitrary
nonnegative strengths and any graph. The P4 density lacks that symmetry.
For the unit quaternions (1,i,j,k),

\[
 \operatorname{Re}(1\,i\,j^{-1}k^{-1})=-1.
\]

Common left multiplication by i is an O(4) isometry and sends the
four terminals to (i,-1,k,-j), for which

\[
 \operatorname{Re}(i\,(-1)\,k^{-1}(-j)^{-1})=+1.
\]

The two positive densities differ by exp(2kappa). Since they are
continuous, this is a failure on neighborhoods, not a null-set
representative issue. It contradicts the necessary common-rotation
invariance. The restricted representation is therefore impossible.

This rules out the direct same-terminal auxiliary O(4) network that
solved (a). It does not rule out nonlinear terminal maps, additional
constraints, non-Haar site laws, or a different representation with a
separately proved mixing theorem.

### What remains of the singlet problem

At one site the corner singlet is x_p=Re H, as P4-ORB says. At several
sites there are more singlets. For example take q_1(u)=q_4(u)=a(u),
q_2(u)=q_3(u)=1: all x_p(u)=1, while the corner-invariant bond energy
Re(a(u)a(v)^{-1}) varies. This realizes the correction in row 1079;
one may not replace the multi-site singlet algebra by the scalar
x_p field alone.

Lemma RIG concerns corner rotations, under which an actual singlet is
fixed. It consequently supplies no variation of x_p or of these bond
singlets. That is the existing limitation in row 1053, not an additional
failed route claimed as new. The three attempts above instead attack
the specific unproved arbitrary-observable supplier: its proposed
amplitude association, its replacement by ferromagnetic conditional
signs, and its replacement by the exact auxiliary-spin mechanism of
(a). Each has a written first failure. **P4-MIX for kappa>0 remains OPEN.**

## Step 6 result: positive beta_L in GH2

Use the GH2_n law of row 1076 §6.2, with its independent Haar references:

\[
 d\mu\ \propto\ e^{\beta_L\sum_p\operatorname{Re}a_p}
 \prod_{i=1}^n
  \exp\!\left[b\sum_{e=uv}
       \operatorname{Re}(\Phi_i(u)^{-1}a_e\Phi_i(v))\right]
       da\,d\Phi .                             \tag{GH1}
\]

At beta_L=0 the edge integrations in GH2-0 are independent; n=2 then
has the exact port (DEC1). At positive beta_L, the plaquette factor
couples the four longitudinal links around each face. Edge-by-edge
Haar integration no longer yields the product (DEC2). Positivity of
its character expansion does not restore independence of these links.

There is a concrete failure of extending the same association machinery,
not just a missing name for the measure.

**Lemma GH-NA — PROVED at the following scope (not independently checked).**
Fix n>=1 and beta_L=kappa>0. On one free longitudinal plaquette, the
unitary-gauge link-amplitude law is not associated for all sufficiently
small positive common Higgs strength b.

Set Phi_1=1 by the Haar-preserving local gauge change
`a'_uv=Phi_1(u)^(-1) a_uv Phi_1(v)`,
`rho_i(u)=Phi_1(u)^(-1) Phi_i(u)` for i>=2, and integrate the redundant
Phi_1 variables. At b=0 the four a' links have exactly the single-site
P4 law used in (NA1)–(NA4), while the rho variables are independent
Haar. Let F,G be (NA3) formed from these four oriented links, with
y(a^{-1})=y(a). Then

\[
 \mathbb E_0F=\mathbb E_0G=0,\quad
 \operatorname{Cov}_0(F,G)=-\delta,\qquad
 \delta=\frac{I_3(\kappa)}{27I_1(\kappa)}>0.
 \tag{GH2}
\]

The b-perturbation has density e^{bS}/E_0 e^{bS}, with |S|<=M=4n:
there are four edges and n Higgs terms per edge, each bounded by one.
Hence

\[
 \int\left|\frac{d\mu_b}{d\mu_0}-1\right|d\mu_0
          \le\epsilon_b:=e^{2Mb}-1.
\]

Since ||F||_infty,||G||_infty<=3 and their unperturbed means vanish,

\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_b(F,G)-\operatorname{Cov}_0(F,G)|
             \le9\epsilon_b+9\epsilon_b^2.     \tag{GH3}
\]

Choose

\[
 0<b\le\frac{\log(1+\delta/36)}{8n}.           \tag{GH4}
\]

Then epsilon_b<=delta/36 and 0<delta<=1/27, the latter also following
from (NA1) as a convolution coefficient of a bounded function. The
right side of (GH3) is less than delta/2, so Cov_b(F,G)<-delta/2.
The same increasing-coordinate functions therefore refute association
at strictly positive b. This includes n=2 and n=4. The argument is a
bounded-density estimate with an explicit constant choice, not a
floating-point test. It does not establish failure of association at
the window b in [2.3,2.6], and does not refute GH2-MIX anywhere.

For every b, the remaining singlet obstruction can also be stated
without changing the measure. Given a, the Higgs species are the fixed
one-sided twisted O(4) systems in the repaired CB-CH statement. Their
annealed gauge marginal is

\[
 d\nu(a)=\frac1Z e^{\beta_L\sum_p\operatorname{Re}a_p}
                 \prod_{i=1}^n Z_i(a)\,da.     \tag{GH5}
\]

For arbitrary observables, total covariance retains both terms

\[
 \operatorname{Cov}_\mu(F,G)
 =\mathbb E_\nu\operatorname{Cov}(F,G\mid a)
    +\operatorname{Cov}_\nu
        (\mathbb E[F\mid a],\mathbb E[G\mid a]). \tag{GH6}
\]

If F and G are two local plaquette traces of a, the first term is zero
and the second is the entire desired covariance. These are actual
gauge-invariant, far-end-singlet observables of (GH1). A charged estimate
for a Higgs species says nothing about this term. Calling its decay an
annealed-gauge lemma would only restate this part of GH2-MIX; it is not
an additional proved reduction or a progress count.

Thus the port in (a), the repaired CB-CH implication, and Lemma RIG do
not close (c). The first unprovided input remains the singlet covariance
bound for the actual coupled law, already present on pure a-plaquette
observables in (GH5). **GH2-MIX at positive beta_L is OPEN.**
