#### 8.2 Proof of (a) and (b)

*The small block.* In 4D, the block z = c + (1, 1, 1, 0) meets the ring of c in exactly one edge of the 4-cube: the l links (x, x + e_4) with relative coordinates x = (l, l, l, s), s from 0 to l - 1 (anchors with three coordinates equal to l; links in directions 1, 2, 3 leave the cube). Every plaquette of c that contains such a link lies in a 2-face (two of its transverse coordinates equal l), so it is ring-only, with weight 1/4. There are 3 per link (planes (mu, 4), base x - e_mu), 3l in all. Each has one link in z and three in other blocks. No core plaquette contains an edge link. In 2D, the corner x = lc + (l, l) is the only site of block c + (1, 1) on the ring of c (for the other offsets, the corner diagonal to the owned one). It has no core neighbour. It meets c only through the ring bonds (x - e_1, x), (x - e_2, x), each of weight 1/2 in c.

*The exact cost.* Take T = {z}. Since D_c is the maximum over T, D_c is at least Delta_T^+. In 4D, with t_p = tr U_p/2,

    Delta_T = sum over the 3l edge plaquettes of (beta/4) [t_p(omega) + t_p(eta) - t_p(omega^T) - t_p(eta^T)],

with no core term. In 2D, with d = (omega - eta)/2, Delta_T = 2b d_x . (d_{x - e_1} + d_{x - e_2}), the swap-bond form of row 1099 at half coupling.

*The mean.* Any Gibbs state of the Wilson specification is invariant under local gauge transformations (row 691 3.1), so rho is too, and (omega, eta) has the law of (g.omega, h.eta) for independent i.i.d. Haar g, h. Z_c is invariant under a gauge transformation of all sites of the closed cube (change variables in the core Haar integral). Apply g^{-1} to the exchanged configuration (Z_c and every t_p are unchanged): t_p(omega^T) has the law of t_p evaluated on the configuration equal to omega off the edge and to k.eta on the edge, with k = g^{-1} h i.i.d. Haar and independent of (omega, eta). In an edge plaquette the site x of the edge link enters this holonomy through k_x exactly once. Given everything else, the holonomy is P k_x Q with k_x Haar, so its trace has mean 0. The same holds for eta^T. Hence E[Delta_T] = 3l (beta/4) 2 Pbar = (3/2) beta l Pbar, exactly. In 2D, independence and O(4) invariance give E[d_x . d_y] = G_1/2, so E[Delta_T] = 2b G_1.

*From the mean to p_c.* abs(Delta_T) is at most M = 3l (beta/4) 4 = 3 beta l (4D), and at most 4b (2D). On [0, M], 1 - e^{-u} is at least u (1 - e^{-M})/M (concavity). So p_c = 1 - e^{-D_c} is at least (1 - e^{-M}) Delta_T^+/M, and E[p_c] is at least (1 - e^{-M}) E[Delta_T]/M. This is (a) and (b), since E[Delta_T]/M = Pbar/2 and G_1/2. QED (a), (b).

#### 8.3 Proof of (c): lower bounds on Pbar and G_1 (PROVED; the Bessel values are hand-checkable from four series terms; a short script (copy in `runs/gap-conv-in-law-check-opus/scripts/bounds.py`) confirmed them and found the best trial, a guide only)

Write z(a) = 2 I_1(a)/a, the Haar mean of e^{a tr U/2} on SU(2) (equal to the uniform mean of e^{a sigma.e} on S^3), r(a) = I_2(a)/I_1(a), and KL(a) = a r(a) - log z(a), the relative entropy of the tilted law e^{a tr U/2}/z(a) to Haar.

- Convexity. Put Z_N(beta) = the Haar integral of exp(beta sum_p t_p) on the torus with N sites (the same Gibbs law as the Wilson action). log Z_N(beta) is convex with log Z_N(0) = 0, so its derivative is at least log Z_N(beta)/beta. On the cubic torus all 6N plaquettes have the same mean (lattice symmetry), so 6N Pbar is at least log Z_N/beta. For the sheet, 2N G_1 is at least log Z_N/b.
- Gauge fixing. Fix the links of a maximal tree to 1 (all temporal links but one per temporal circle, and a spatial tree of one time slice). For gauge-invariant integrands this is exact (the standard axial-gauge identity; Adhikari-Cao Lemma 2.14 is the finite-group form). Jensen with the product trial e^{a tr U/2}/z(a) on the 3N + 1 free links gives log Z_N/N at least beta (3 r^2 + 3 r^4) - 3 KL(a) - 3 beta (r^2 - r^4)/L - KL(a)/N, for the cubic torus of side L. Here (mu, 4) plaquettes off the cut slice have at most two free links, mean at least r^2; all other plaquettes have at most four, mean at least r^4 (the trial mean of a product of k independent free links is r^k 1).
- So Pbar is at least (r^2 + r^4)/2 - KL(a)/(2 beta) - (r^2 - r^4)/(2L), and G_1 is at least r^2 - KL(a)/(2b) (product trial on all sites, no gauge step). Both bounds increase with the coupling, so the value at 2.3 holds on the whole window.
- At a = 2: I_1(2) = 1.5906, I_2(2) = 0.6889, r = 0.4331, z = 1.5906, KL = 0.4020. At beta = b = 2.3: Pbar is at least 0.1114 - 0.0874 - 0.0038 = 0.020 (L at least 20), and G_1 is at least 0.1876 - 0.0874 = 0.100. The best trial (a near 9.5 and 7.5) gives Pbar at least 0.14 (as L grows) and G_1 at least 0.248.
- Hence E[p_c] is at least 0.010 (4D) and 0.050 (2D) on the window. QED (c).

#### 8.4 Size of the cost, typical pairs, and the junction size (the brief's estimate)

- Guide values. Published Monte Carlo plaquettes for 4D SU(2) at beta from 2.3 to 2.6 are about 0.60 to 0.67 (recalled, not opened; guide). Row 1099's guide run gives G_1 about 0.64 (b = 2.3) and 0.68 (b = 2.5). With these, E[p_c] is at least 0.30 (4D) and 0.32 (2D). The mean far-edge cost (3/2) beta l Pbar is about 2l in 4D. It grows linearly in l with no core term.
- Typical pairs (ARGUED). In 4D, Delta_T on the edge is a sum of 3l bounded plaquette terms. Near beta = 2.4 the 4D SU(2) correlation length is of order one lattice unit (lightest glueball mass near 1 in lattice units; recalled, guide). In 2D the corner cost is one bounded term and needs no concentration; there the sheet's correlation length is about 20 to 50 (the Hasenfratz-Maggiore-Niedermayer formula as recorded at `library/gap-cuttyp-max.md` L157; guide). If these terms decorrelate, Delta_T concentrates near its mean (about 2l), and p_c is close to 1 - e^{-2l} or above for most pairs. So p_c is near 1 for typical pairs. Proposition J needs only the mean.
- The large junctions. For any T, the same randomization shows that every mixed ring plaquette (links in T and outside T) has Haar holonomy in omega^T and eta^T. So the ring-only part of Delta_T has mean exactly 2 beta sum over mixed ring plaquettes of w_p Pbar (PROVED). For T a large block (a 3-face piece), the mixed ring plaquettes lie along 2-dimensional junctions, about c l^{d-2} of them. So the ring part has mean of order beta l^{d-2} Pbar, as the brief expected. The core part of Delta_T is not signed by any argument I have; it adds the frustrated interior near the junction (ARGUED nonnegative in mean, not proved). Proposition J avoids it by using the edge, where it is absent.
- Where H-swap can hold. At small beta the leading strong-coupling value Pbar near beta/4 (argued) gives E[p_c] at least about (3/8) beta^2 l. So H-swap needs beta below about 0.033 at l = 2, shrinking like l^{-1/2}; the 2D junctions of the large blocks push this to beta of order 1/l (argued). For the sheet, G_1 near b/4 gives E[p_c] at least about b^2/2, so b below about 0.2. With the nonvacuity of L278, H-swap holds in a strong-coupling neighbourhood whose size shrinks with l, and (argued) only there.

#### 8.5 Comparison with Dobrushin-type criteria and row 691 Theorem W

- Dobrushin's single-site condition for 4D SU(2): one link sees 18 links through its 6 staples, each with an influence of order beta/2, so it holds for beta below about 0.1 (argued). For the 2D O(4) sheet, below about b = 0.25 (argued). Disagreement percolation (van den Berg-Maes) is of the same strong-coupling kind. H-swap's domain is inside, or of the order of, Dobrushin's. Averaging in law gains nothing here, because typical pairs pay the junction cost.
- Theorem W's (W) is an influence criterion. It changes one boundary link, couples, and counts disagreements, with the sum normalized by the cube volume (sum of k_z at most delta N_R). An effect of order one per boundary link is allowed at large R. So the junction mechanism does not exclude (W) at weak coupling; whether (W) holds at the window remains the lane's OPEN question. H-swap's defect is per cell and unnormalized: it is a gluing free energy, paid in full by every typical pair, and it grows with l.
- Why Adhikari-Cao work at weak coupling and this does not: for a finite group the weak-coupling vacuum is rigid. After gauge fixing, two independent samples agree outside rare excitations, so the swap is free there. For SU(2) and O(4), two independent samples always differ by an order-one relative frame (the Haar twist of 8.2). Even an optimally aligned exchange leaves Gaussian fluctuations whose gluing cost is of order one per junction element at every coupling (argued; the scale-invariant Dirichlet-to-Neumann estimate).
- With row 1119's Lemma TS2: (H-two) in total variation already contains one-scale core-ring mixing, and H-swap is false at the window by Proposition J. So neither two-sample hypothesis in law is a usable supplier at the window couplings.

#### 8.6 A different ownership, or a smoothed exchange

- Ownership in the theorem's own convention: the offset is forced (Step 5), and every admissible offset (or the reflected upper-end anchor convention) gives a far edge in 4D or a corner in 2D. Proposition J covers all of them.
- Any other localized partition (thick rings, other block shapes). **Lemma JI (PROVED).** For a ring block z and N_z the cells c with z in S_c, E_{rho x rho}[sum over N_z of Delta_z(c)] = 2 (I + L), where I = E D(pi_z(. given the other blocks) || nu_z) is the mutual information of block z and the other ring blocks, and L = E D(nu_z || pi_z(. given the other blocks)) is its reverse ("lautum") form. Proof: the sum of F_c over N_z is minus the log of the ring-specification density pi_z plus a term of the other blocks only; the normalizers cancel in Delta; given the other blocks of omega, omega_z has law pi_z and eta_z has law nu_z; the chain rule for relative entropy does the rest. For a gauge theory, take one mixed plaquette whose links in z form one path; data processing on the pair (path in z, path outside z), both Haar by gauge invariance at an end site, gives I at least D(plaquette law || Haar), which is at least sup_t (t Pbar - log z(t)). At Pbar = 0.6 this is 0.83 (guide value of Pbar). Blocks must have bounded diameter for the path count: a single infinite block makes p_c = 0 but joins U and V always. So no partition makes the mean exchange cost small at the window. For general partitions, the step from this mean to failure for typical pairs is ARGUED (it needs a bound on abs(Delta) or concentration).
- Smoothed exchange (ARGUED). The cancellation (4.10)-(4.11) needs an exact measure-preserving involution of the pair that negates F(omega) - F(eta) on U and fixes the G difference on V. Taking F and G to be indicators shows that such a map is the swap on U and the identity on V. A relative rotation of one replica preserves the reference but negates nothing. A one-site remark (PROVED, standard topology): the swap of S^3 x S^3 (the pair of O(4) spins, or of SU(2) links) has degree (-1)^9 = -1, so it is not homotopic to the identity. Two homeomorphisms that are uniformly closer than the convexity radius are homotopic, so in any chain of site homeomorphisms from the swap (on U) to the identity (on V) some neighbouring pair of sites carries maps that are far apart for some configurations; a "smooth" exchange keeps an order-one jump somewhere. A continuous twist family of spin-wave type, spread over an annulus of radii r, R in 2D, costs a Dirichlet energy of order b pi^3/log(R/r). That is below eps_0 = 1/50 at b = 2.3 only for log(R/r) of order 3500, and such twists give power-law bounds, not exponential ones (the McBryan-Spencer mechanism). In 4D a relative gauge alignment is free, but it does not change the gauge-invariant mismatch of two independent replicas (mixed plaquette traces, and holonomies beyond the correlation length). So no smoothed exchange avoids the junction cost at the window.

It gives no progress on the Wilson window or on the O(4) sheets at b in [2.3, 2.6]. This refutes a hypothesis; it says nothing against mixing, which is not claimed either way.
