## 2. Ranked attack

**GUIDE — Rank 1, extract independent commutators at a time cut.** A Haar commutator is $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ for independent Haar $a,b$. Insert a fresh pair for each spatial link at the output of a slab. Decompose the slab transition into two positive parts. One part, of probability at least $p$, must support *all* these kicks together. The rest of the law may be arbitrary. On that branch the auxiliary links have joint product Haar marginal. The part of a boundary observable carried by any nontrivial SU(2) representation then loses a fixed fraction of its norm. The output receives only the links after these independent kicks. This is a proposal of type K-one for the spectral side of the wall.

**PROVED — Choice of coordinates.** Write $H$ for product Haar on $X=\mathrm{SU}(2)^E$. Fix the product of round unit three-sphere metrics. Choose a map $S$ sending $H$ to $\pi=\rho H$ as follows (Moser's volume-form construction). Solve $\Delta u=1-\rho$ with zero Haar mean, using the nonpositive Laplacian. The right side is smooth with zero integral. On this finite product the inverse on mean-zero smooth functions is given by dividing the rapidly decaying Peter–Weyl coefficients by their nonzero Laplace eigenvalues. Flow for $0\le s\le1$ with velocity $\nabla u/(1-s+s\rho)$. The density $1-s+s\rho$ solves the continuity equation and starts at one. The smooth flow exists on the compact product and its time-one map is $S$. Positivity and smoothness of $\psi$ follow from the finite Wilson Perron equation with its smooth strictly positive gauge-averaged kernel. Vertex gauges and centre flips are isometries preserving $\rho$. Uniqueness of the zero-mean solution and of the flow makes $S$ equivariant. Locality and cutoff-uniform derivative estimates for this change of coordinates would require additional bounds.

**OPEN — First statement (A).** Fix the Wilson trajectory $(a_j,\beta_j)$ and the admitted dyadic physical lengths $\mathcal P$ of row 720. Seek $t>0$, $L_0\in\mathcal P$, $0<p\le1$ and $\delta>0$. For every $L=2^h L_0$ with integer $h\ge0$, require the following for every $j\ge j_0(L)$. Take $a=a_j$, $n=L/a\in4\mathbb N$ with $n/4\ge3$, and $k=\lfloor t/a\rfloor\ge1$. Define $j_k(x,y)$ as the joint Haar density of the two stationary slices in coordinates $S^{-1}$. Explicitly, if $T^k(U,V)$ is the unnormalized transfer kernel relative to $H$, then $j_k(x,y)=T^k(Sx,Sy)/[\lambda_0^k\psi(Sx)\psi(Sy)]$. Both marginals are one. Let $W_e$ convolve link $e$ with a Haar commutator, $W_E=\prod_e W_e$, and $q_G=\|W_E\|_{L^2_0(H)}$. Seek a probability kernel $r(x,dz)$ with $H r=H$ such that, for $H$-almost every $x$, as measures in $y$,

$$
       j_k(x,y)H(dy)\ \ge\ p\,(rW_E)(x,dy),\qquad p(1-q_G)\ge\delta .          \tag{A}
$$

The kernel $r$ may depend on the actual box and cutoff. The same $t,p,\delta$ must work for them all. Multiplying separate linkwise acceptance probabilities does not furnish the joint weight.

**PROVED — Consumer, conditional on (A).** The residual divided by $1-p$ is a Haar-stationary Markov kernel when $p<1$. Such kernels preserve means and contract $L^2$ by Jensen. The commutator multiplier on spin $j$ is $(2j+1)^{-2}$, by the Frobenius identity used in §3. Tensoring gives $\|W_E\|_{L^2_0(H)}=1/4$ for any nonempty finite $E$. Hence $\|P_0^k\|_{0}\le 1-3p/4$ also on the physical subspace. At fixed $L$, the lower cutoff limit of $na\,m_{\rm phys}$ is at least $(L/t)[-\log(1-3p/4)]$. This grows along dyadic $L$ and supplies Z-UNB. For the one-size version seek a single admitted $L,t,p$ for which (A) holds for all $j\ge j_0(L)$ and $(L/t)[-\log(1-3p/4)]>2\limsup_{j\to\infty}\Phi_{4,j}(n/4)+30$. Both formulations leave the free-energy input visible. The all-size argument needs BDD-Delta for the brief's wall.

**PROVED — Abelian and product comparisons.** In compact U(1) the commutator is the identity almost surely, so $q_G=1$ even on a nonconstant gauge-invariant loop. The positive margin in (A) fails. For a product, an SU(2)-only kick fixes $1\otimes f$ for every mean-zero spectator vector $f$. If the entrance, exit or noise is redesigned to treat *both* factors with a positive margin, restrict the resulting slab norm bound to those same vectors. The product transfer there is exactly the spectator transfer. Along boxes where its lowest physical energy tends to zero, its time-$t$ norm tends to one, violating the bound $1-\delta$. This is the product test of the brief and covers a massless spectator with no compact-group presentation.

**PROVED — Frozen comparison.** On a finite group every one-dimensional character equals one on every commutator. The quaternion group $Q_8$ has quotient $Q_8/\{\pm1\}\simeq C_2^2$. The binary tetrahedral group has quotient $2T/Q_8\simeq C_3$ (the rotations cyclically permuting the three coordinate axes supply the cosets). In either case a nontrivial quotient character around a closed loop is gauge invariant, Haar-centred and fixed by every kick. This also has a spin-two test. On harmonic quadratics in three coordinates, $xy$ transforms by a nontrivial sign under the diagonal half-turns of $Q_8$. For $2T$, $x^2+\omega y^2+\omega^2 z^2$, with $\omega^3=1$, $\omega\ne1$, is fixed by those half-turns and acquires a nontrivial cube root under a cyclic permutation. Both lines are commutator-fixed after restriction. On the full SU(2) spin-two space the multiplier is $1/25$.

## 3. Attempt on (A)

**PROVED — The inverse used in the test.** Use Frobenius's commutator identity in row 778, (6), checked in row 780. For an irreducible unitary $D$ of dimension $d$, averaging first in $a$ and then in $b$ by Schur orthogonality gives $\mathbb E D([a,b])=I/d^2$. On the unit $S^3$ the spin-$j$ matrix entries have Laplace eigenvalue $-4j(j+1)=1-d^2$, from the quadratic SU(2) Casimir with unit quaternion generators. The two multipliers identify $W=(1-\Delta)^{-1}$. On the product put $D_E=\prod_e(1-\Delta_e)$. Peter–Weyl expansion gives $D_E W_E=I$ on smooth test functions, hence also on distributions by duality.

**PROVED — The measure inequality in (A) has an equivalent sign test.** One can look for a density $0\le g(x,y)\le j_k(x,y)$ with both Haar marginals equal to $p$, such that $D_{E,y}g$ is a positive measure. Indeed, given (A), take $g\,H(dx)H(dy)=pH(dx)(rW_E)(x,dy)$, which is dominated by the slab density. Applying $D_{E,y}$ recovers $pH(dx)r(x,dy)$. Conversely the positive derivative has the same two marginals, since $D_E1=1$ and Haar integration kills Laplacians. Disintegrate it in $x$, divide by $p$ and apply $W_E$ to obtain (A). This is a positivity test on the joint law. A spectral estimate or a one-link marginal need not pass it.

**PROVED — A sharp one-step obstruction.** For the normalized single temporal link put $h_\beta(g)=e^{\beta x}/\int e^{\beta x}\,dg$, $x=\tfrac12\mathrm{tr}\,g$. The round radial Laplacian is $(1-x^2)\partial_x^2-3x\partial_x$, from the spherical density proportional to $\sin^2\theta$ with $x=\cos\theta$. Hence

$$
  (1-\Delta)h_\beta=h_\beta[1+3\beta x-\beta^2(1-x^2)].
$$

At $x=-1$ the bracket is $1-3\beta$. For $0<\beta\le1/3$ it increases throughout $[-1,1]$, since its derivative is $3\beta+2\beta^2x>0$. Haar integration gives $\int(1-\Delta)h_\beta\,dg=1$. Thus the electric-link law is a convolution of a probability measure with the Haar commutator law exactly when $0\le\beta\le1/3$. Beyond $1/3$ the unique deconvolution is negative on an open set. This threshold concerns this particular factorization.

**PROVED — One step fails even for partial extraction on many physical plaquettes.** Turn off spatial plaquette weights, retain Gauss projection, and take bare $23/10\le\beta\le13/5$. Here $\psi=1$, $S$ is the identity. Write $a_d=I_d(\beta)/I_1(\beta)$ for the normalized electric multiplier of a dimension-$d$ representation. GL3–4 and the Bessel recurrence give $a_2=1/(4/\beta+I_3/I_2)>2/5$, using $4/\beta<2$ and $I_3/I_2\le\beta/6<1/2$. Choose $N$ edge-disjoint spatial plaquettes in an increasing sequence of tori. Write $w=\chi_{1/2}/2$. For an input slice $x$ with $w(x_i)>\eta$ on those plaquettes, their outgoing Wilson traces have means $a_2^4 w(x_i)$. Such an open set has positive Haar measure for every $\eta<1$. The electric link increments are independent before the final gauge projection. The projection leaves every function of the plaquette traces invariant, so the traces retain that conditional independence.

**PROVED — Bound on the extracted weight.** Choose $\eta<1$ with $b=\eta a_2^4-4^{-4}>0$. Given *any* entrance slice $z$ of the proposed commutator branch, the outgoing plaquette traces are independent, bounded by one in absolute value, and have means $4^{-4}w(z_i)\le4^{-4}$. Let $A$ be the event that their empirical mean is at most $4^{-4}+b/2$. Under the Wilson electric step, on the input set above, $\Pr(A)\le e_N=4/(Nb^2)$ by Chebyshev. Under the commutator branch $\Pr(A)\ge1-e_N$ even after averaging over an arbitrary correlated entrance $z$. Domination forces $p\le e_N/(1-e_N)$ for $e_N<1$. Increasing $N$ rules out any volume-independent positive $p$ for this one-step construction. The test uses gauge-invariant observables. It explains why separate local acceptance probabilities cannot be used as the slab probability.

**PROVED — Twelve electric steps pass for every box.** In the same model with spatial weights turned off, take $0\le\beta\le13/5$. For $d\ge2$, GL4 gives $a_d\le(13/20)^{d-1}$ by successive ratios (at zero use the Haar limit). The character expansion of $(1-\Delta)h_\beta^{*12}$ has constant one. The absolute value of its nonconstant part is at most

$$
 \sum_{d\ge2}d^4 a_d^{12}
 \ <\ \sum_{d\ge2}(16/81)^{d-1}=16/65.
$$

Indeed $|\chi_j|\le d$, $d\le2^{d-1}$, $13/20<2/3$, and $(2/3)^3<1/3$. The deconvolved density is strictly positive and integrates to one. Tensor the corresponding probability kernels, and average over the gauge group at the entrance. Central convolutions commute with gauge projection. This yields (A) with $p=1$ for twelve *electric* time steps on every finite spatial torus. Twelve is a sufficient hand bound. It uses the bare temporal weight at the displayed coupling. The stopped effective action requires its own estimate.

**PROVED — What works for the interacting Wilson slab at fixed cutoff and box.** In the fixed Haar coordinates the one-step density $j_1$ is smooth, symmetric and strictly positive. Put $h=\min j_1>0$. For $h<1$ subtract the constant kernel $h$ and divide by $1-h$. Jensen applied to the resulting bistochastic kernel gives a mean-zero operator norm at most one. Thus the original norm is at most $1-h$. Set $A_1=\sup_x\|j_1(x,\cdot)-1\|_2$ and $B_1=\sup_y\|D_{E,y}(j_1(\cdot,y)-1)\|_2$. Both are finite by smoothness on the fixed compact product. In the identity $J_k=J_1J_1^{k-2}J_1$ for the slice operators, subtract the constant projection and put the derivatives on the final kernel. Cauchy–Schwarz yields

$$
        |D_{E,y}j_k(x,y)-1|\le A_1 B_1(1-h)^{k-2}\quad(k\ge2).
$$

For large enough $k$, $g=j_k$ passes the sign test with $p=1$. The case $h=1$ is the constant kernel. No spectral assumption is used here. The bound depends on the whole finite box and on the cutoff.

**PROVED — First failing line in the Wilson calculation.** Propagating the electric sign through the spatial half-weight $M$ would require positivity after applying $1-\Delta_e$ to products with $M$. This already fails on the constant test. In a periodic spatial cube of side at least four, take all links equal to $I$ except $U_e=-I$. Each of the four incident spatial plaquettes has $w=-1$ and zero link gradient there. Since $\Delta_e w=-3w$,

$$
        (1-\Delta_e)M=(1-6\beta)M
$$

at this configuration, since $M$ is proportional to $\exp[(\beta/2)\sum_p w(U_p)]$. The expression is negative in a neighbourhood for $\beta>1/6$. In general the product rule also gives $-2\nabla_e M\cdot\nabla_e f$. Neither positivity of the transfer nor its Haar commutator multipliers bounds that signed term. The example refutes passing the sign through one multiplier. Integration through the whole slab can restore positivity, as the fixed-box argument shows.

**OPEN — Where the attempt stops.** On the actual trajectory, extract a subdensity with both marginal masses $p$ and positive $D_{E,y}g$ at $k\le t/a$, with $t,p$ fixed as in (A). The fixed-box estimate gives no such control of $h,A_1,B_1$. The one-step local identity has the wrong sign in the Wilson window. The twelve-step calculation integrates no magnetic plaquettes. A proof must bound their *integrated* signed contribution, including the Perron density in the actual boundary coordinates, or construct the subdensity directly. The free-energy side of the seed stays open.
