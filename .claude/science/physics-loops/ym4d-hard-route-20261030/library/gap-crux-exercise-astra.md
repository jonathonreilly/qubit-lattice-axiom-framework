## Step 1. The wall twice

**Reading — plain version.** We want to prove that the scalar observables of
four-dimensional SU(2) Yang–Mills theory have a positive minimum excitation
energy. This would exclude arbitrarily slow physical fluctuations. The lane
can propagate a sufficiently quiet finite periodic box to all larger boxes.
It has not proved that such a box is quiet when the interaction is no longer
weak. Two quantities must be small: the total excited-state weight and the
change in vacuum energy when the box is doubled. Neither has been bounded
uniformly as the lattice spacing vanishes. Getting past the wall means
proving those bounds, or proving a different sufficient estimate for the
same continuum scalar theory. Changing the theory or assuming its gap does
not do that.

**DEF — precise target.** Use (GAP) verbatim from
`gap-skeleton-final.md:29`: for a canonical component sigma of the stated
volume-first continuum limit, with vacuum Omega and nonnegative Hamiltonian
H, ker H = C Omega and spec(H) is contained in {0} union [m,infinity) for
some m > 0. The cyclic algebra is generated by tr(F²), not the asserted
whole gauge theory. Its existence and limit identifications remain named
inputs, as `gap-seed-limit.md:183–204` explains.

**DEF — the present sufficient wall.** To avoid confusing spacing with box
size, write h for the spacing and n for the integer side. At the prescribed
bare coupling beta_h, let e_h(B)=log lambda_0(B), and

    epsilon_h(n) = Tr[P_0 (T_(n³)/lambda_0(n³))^n] - 1,
    delta_h(n)   = n [8 e_h(n³) - e_h((2n)³)].

Theorem R's ES is epsilon_h(n) <= 10^(-7) and delta_h(n) <= 10^(-7),
with n >= 3 (`gap-means-max.md:208–216`). Its physical version requires a
finite L_* independent of h and, for every sufficiently small admitted h,
an n_h with h n_h <= L_* and both inequalities. It gives a physical lower
gap bound greater than 0.65/L_*, conditional on the stated continuum
passage. A single beta, a side n independent of h, or n_h h tending to
infinity does not establish this version.

**THM, inherited checked statement.** Theorem R and R4–R5 hold by the check
in `gap-means-max-check.md:47–67,202–241`. The existential equivalence there
is between ES and the existence of *one of* the three sector seeds.
The sentence “equivalent to each” in the final skeleton, §13.19, cannot mean
that all three seeds hold: R(b) explicitly makes one fail at the seed size.
No choice of that stronger reading is made here.

**SUPPLIED — scope of success.** A written proof of an actual cutoff-uniform

## Step 3. Reason from the inventory

### 3.1 Immediate consequences

**Argued, from I1, I5, I9.** A proof may bound the spectral measures of a
total class of physical scalar vectors instead of a trace over every even
state. If each such vector has no spectral weight in (0,m), density removes
that interval from the scalar Hamiltonian. This is the spectral content
already used by Proposition P'. No new sufficiency theorem is claimed.

**Argued, from I5, I10.** Vacuum subtraction and normalization cannot produce
the missing spectral estimate. Multiplying a cube's transfer operator by a
positive scalar leaves every E_j and S_ev unchanged. An extensive change
of log lambda_0 also cancels from delta. A volume-dependent nonextensive
change can alter delta but would change the finite-volume theory, and is
not a proof of ES for Wilson's measure.

### 3.2 The least any solution needs

**Reading, from I1–I2, I9, I14.** At least one estimate must exclude a fixed
positive-energy interval in the actual scalar limit. Decay without a rate,
a gap on each finite graph, control on a constrained Gaussian fibre, and
agreement at short distances do not exclude that interval. A successful
hypothesis must fail in a massless comparison in its claimed class; the
abstract implication need not use noncommutativity. The model-specific
verification must supply the difference.

### 3.3 A smaller object, and a different frustration-free object

**Construction, argued from I3 and I16.** At finite cutoff write
mu(dU)=rho(U)dU with rho strictly positive. In L²(mu), let E_B be conditional
expectation given all links outside a block B. Then E_B is an orthogonal
projection. Set

    A = sum_B (I - E_B).

Every term is positive, has norm at most one, and annihilates 1. Thus A is
exactly frustration free. It is an auxiliary relaxation operator on the
*four-dimensional Euclidean configuration law*. It is not -log T in
physical time. After conjugation f -> sqrt(rho) f into product Haar space,
each term is local to B and the plaquettes meeting B: outside-only action
factors cancel in the conditional density. This construction does not need
the physical Hamiltonian to be frustration free. It should be compared
with the earlier record before any claim of novelty.

**Open question generated here.** Can a physical-block version of A have a
uniform local spectral certificate, and can its gap control normalized
physical correlations without a cutoff-dependent sup-norm prefactor?
The second part is a bridge to attempt, not an assumed identification.

**Argued — toy test, from the same definitions.** Take two spins s,t in
{−1,1} with probability proportional to exp(Jst), J >= 0. Write c=tanh J.
For A=(I-E_s)+(I-E_t), conditional averaging gives E_s s=c t and E_t t=c s.
Consequently

    A(s+t)=(1-c)(s+t),   A(s-t)=(1+c)(s-t),
    A(st-c)=2(st-c),     A1=0.

These four linearly independent vectors span the function space. The gap
is exactly 1-tanh J, positive at finite J but tending to zero. Thus exact
frustration freeness, strict positivity and a compact configuration space
supply no uniform gap. This is a hand calculation, not a run. It does not
refute the conditional-expectation route for SU(2).

**Argued — scope toy, from I1 and I5.** On C² tensor C² set
H=diag(0,1) tensor I + I tensor diag(0,h), vacuum |0,0>, and observed algebra
M_2 tensor I. Its cyclic observed sector has gap 1. The full thermal excess
at time L is exp(-L)+exp(-Lh)+exp(-L(1+h)); at fixed L it stays above a
number tending to 1 as h tends to zero. A trace criterion is strictly more
information than an observed-sector gap in this abstract class. This is
not a Wilson counterexample, nor a model with the crossing identities of R.
It establishes no failure of ES in SU(2).

### 3.4 Mechanisms and separate failures

**Reading, from I3–I16.** The problem separates into physical spectral
exclusion, state counting, vacuum-energy additivity, and identification of
the constructed limit. ES handles the first through the next two. A
conditional-expectation parent would handle it through spatial locality
and relaxation. A deformed action could handle it through stability and a
limit-identification theorem. These trades change the open estimate; they
do not make any of it automatic.

### 3.5 Make the requirement less strong where the consumer permits it

**Reading, from I1, I8–I11.** The consumer need not know the flux phase,
every boundary state, every excited state, or every bare coupling. It needs
the actual selected continuum component and its scalar total class, with a
positive physical rate. A proof on a single ultraviolet trajectory is enough
for that trajectory. Replacing all periodic boxes by walls is permissible
only with a same-limit or direct reconstruction bridge. Dropping uniformity
in the cutoff loses the physical rate and is not an acceptable weakening.

### 8.1 A: an analytic physical-block certificate, then its missing supplier

#### Contract and exact finite statement

**Definition.** Fix a finite four-dimensional torus with spacing h and Wilson
law `mu = rho dHaar`, beta finite. For a set B of oriented links, `E_B`
is conditional expectation given all links outside B in `L²(mu)` and
`a_B = I-E_B`. A block cover has parameters (d,K,r) if:

1. it covers every link;
2. its interaction graph joins B,C whenever a plaquette meets both (including
   overlap), has maximum degree at most d, and has a coloring with K colors;
3. each block together with the links in its touching plaquettes has lattice
   `l_infinity` diameter at most r.

Blocks of one color do not interact. The graph may contain extra edges.
The constants refer to blocks, not to the number of microscopic links inside
one block. No bounded local Hilbert-space dimension is assumed.

**Open input PA.** For every interacting pair B,C, and every exterior
configuration tau outside `B union C`, let `E_B^tau,E_C^tau` be the two
conditional projections in the law on the union. Require

    || E_B^tau E_C^tau - E_(B union C)^tau || <= eta < 1/d.       (PA)

Here `E_(B union C)^tau` is projection onto constants in that fibre. The
bound must hold uniformly in torus size, location, and h at one fixed
physical block size. Its norm includes all L² functions, not just Wilson
loops. This is a precise, strong all-boundary input, not an asserted
consequence of the account. Essential-supremum versions would suffice for
the operator proof; the stated every-tau form avoids choosing null-set
versions and matches the owner's wall-box question.

**Lemma A1, argued.** Under PA, the auxiliary operator
`A=sum_B a_B` satisfies

    A² >= (1-d eta) A,
    ker A = constants.                                         (A1)

*Proof.* Strict positivity on a finite compact configuration space makes
`mu` equivalent to product Haar, with finite positive density bounds. If a
function is unchanged by both E_B and E_C, it is measurable outside each
set. In product Haar the coordinate conditional projections commute, so
Fubini shows it is measurable outside their union. Equivalence of measures
preserves that statement modulo null sets. Hence their intersection
projection is `R=E_(B union C)`. The same argument over the cover gives
`ker A = constants`.

For two arbitrary orthogonal projections P,Q with intersection R, put
`k=||PQ-R||`. On `ker R`, consider the map
`Jf=(Pf,Qf)` into `ran(P-R) direct-sum ran(Q-R)`. The diagonal blocks of
`JJ*` are identity and the off-diagonal blocks have norm at most k. Thus
`||P+Q||_(ker R) = ||J*J|| <= 1+k`. Consequently
`S=(I-P)+(I-Q)` is zero on `ran R` and is at least `(1-k)I` on its
orthogonal complement. Functional calculus gives `S² >= (1-k)S`.
Equivalently,

    (I-P)(I-Q)+(I-Q)(I-P) >= -k[(I-P)+(I-Q)].

Disintegrating over the exterior gives this inequality with k<=eta for
B,C. For noninteracting blocks, conditional independence given the exterior
makes E_B and E_C commute, so their anticommutator is nonnegative. Expand
`A²=sum_B a_B + sum_(B<C){a_B,a_C}`; each a_B is charged at most d times.
This proves A1. Bounded self-adjoint functional calculus gives
`spec A subset {0} union [1-d eta,infinity)`. No eigenvalue computation or
compactness of the operator was used. End proof.

**Reading — credit.** Two-projection geometry and FF finite-size estimates
are standard; this proof is a deliberately elementary implementation of
B7, not a novelty claim. Unlike a direct invocation of a spin-chain theorem,
it states the actual Wilson conditional operators and their boundary norm.

#### From this auxiliary gap to physical correlations

**Lemma A2, argued, using the credited detectability argument.** Write
`gamma=1-d eta>0`, take d>=1, and let `D` be the product of the K layers
of E_B. Then on mean-zero functions

    ||D|| <= q := (1+gamma/d²)^(-1/2) < 1.                       (A2)

For completeness, the Anshu–Arad–Vidick finite-sum argument is as follows.
Order the projections, put `f_j=E_j f_(j-1)`, `v=Df`, and
`loss_j=||(I-E_j)f_(j-1)||`. To move `I-E_i` through the product until it
hits E_i, only noncommuting factors contribute. The triangle inequality
therefore bounds `||(I-E_i)v||` by a sum of at most d losses. Squaring,
then summing i, charges each loss at most d times:

    <v,Av> <= d² sum_j loss_j²
            = d² (||f||²-||v||²).

Mean zero is preserved. Combine with `<v,Av> >= gamma ||v||²` to obtain A2.
All operators are bounded projections; the argument applies to this
infinite-dimensional L² space without a finite-spin approximation.

**Lemma A3, argued.** For bounded local functions F,G whose supports are
at lattice distance greater than `k K r`,

    Cov_mu(F,G) = <F-mu F, D^k(G-mu G)>_mu,
    |Cov_mu(F,G)| <= q^k sqrt(Var_mu F Var_mu G).                (A3)

Complex functions use the conjugate in the inner product. The first identity
is worth proving: E_B fixes a function if B misses its support; otherwise
locality of the Wilson conditional density enlarges that support by at most
r. Within one color, no update can propagate dependence into another update
of that color, since the blocks do not interact. Thus one layer enlarges the
support by at most r. In kK layers, every update which changes the evolving
G is disjoint from the support of F. For such an update, self-adjointness and
`E_B F=F` preserve its inner product with F. All remaining updates fix G.
Induction proves the identity. A2 and Cauchy–Schwarz prove the inequality.

This is spatial decay in the original four-dimensional Euclidean measure.
No auxiliary-time eigenvalue has been equated with a physical-time energy.
The physical exponent is obtained by separating F and G in a Euclidean time
direction, using locality to convert layers into distance.

**Explicit cover, argued.** Choose an integer ell>=12. In each periodic
coordinate, partition the sites into intervals of lengths s_i in
`[ell,2ell]`, with at least twelve intervals; this is possible for every
side N>=12 ell by distributing the remainder after division by ell. At
interval endpoint x_i use the integer interval

    [ x_i-floor(2 s_(i-1)/3), x_i+floor(2 s_i/3) ].

Adjacent intervals overlap: their overlap length is at least
`s_i/3-2 >=2`. Intervals two indices apart are separated by at least
`(s_i+s_(i+1))/3 >=8` before harmless endpoint rounding. Take products in
four coordinates and include every oriented link whose base lies in the
product. They cover all links. If two block indices differ by at least two
in some coordinate, no plaquette meets both blocks. Hence

    d <= 3^4-1 = 80,    K <= 81,    r <= 3 ell+2.

The last bound includes the one-plaquette collar. The degree bound gives a
coloring by the elementary greedy algorithm; no torus divisibility or
bipartite coloring is assumed. Arbitrarily large tori, not a selected
arithmetic subsequence of volumes, are covered.

**Conditional conclusion A4, argued.** Set `ell_h=ceil(R/h)` with fixed
R>0 and h<=R/12. If PA holds on these covers with `eta<=1/160` for all
sufficiently small h and all sufficiently large tori, then gamma>=1/2 and

    q <= (1+1/12800)^(-1/2),
    m_h >= -log(q)/(81(3 ell_h+2)h)
        >= log(1+1/12800)/(648 R) =: m_*>0.                    (A4)

Take `k=floor((distance-1)/(Kr))` in A3. Floors and finite support widths
change a prefactor, not the exponential rate. Continuous local functions
pass to any volume-first periodic limit by weak convergence. For a centered
positive-half scalar test F, reflection and translation express its OS
matrix element as such a covariance. At each fixed h its spectral measure
therefore has no mass in `[0,m_h)`: if it put positive mass in
`[0,m_h-epsilon]`, its nonnegative Laplace transform could not be bounded
by a fixed multiple of `exp(-m_h t)` for all large t. Density of the local
test class gives the lattice scalar spectral exclusion and a simple vacuum.

Now use the *already available* Lemma L/Proposition P' of
`gap-seed-limit.md:182–238`, including reflected norm convergence, totality
and time continuity. The lattice spectral exclusion has first become a
norm-one semigroup bound; it can pass to the specified scalar continuum
component with rate m_*. A cutoff-dependent sup norm of a renormalized
observable was not passed through a limit. Nontriviality/existence of that
component remains the caller's conditional account input.

This conditional GAP implication does not estimate either ES number and
does not prove ES from a physical gap. It deliberately avoids the extra
state-counting obligation already separated by M5-H. Its open premise PA
is stronger than the consumer and could fail even where the consumer holds.

#### Actual attempts to prove PA for Wilson's law

**Attempt A.i: direct change of density, argued.** On one union patch with
fixed exterior let nu be product Haar and `dmu=rho dnu`, with
`0<a<=rho<=b`. Conditional variance is a variational minimum, so

    E_mu Var_mu(f | outside B)
       = inf_g E_mu |f-g(outside B)|²
       >= a inf_g E_nu |f-g(outside B)|².

Sum this for B,C. Their product-Haar projections commute, cover the patch,
and have common kernel constants, so their sum has gap at least one.
Also `Var_mu f <= b Var_nu f`. Thus their Wilson two-block sum has gap at
least `a/b >= exp(-osc S)`. For the conditional Wilson action,

    osc S <= 2 beta N_p,
    gap(a_B+a_C) >= exp(-2 beta N_p),                           (A5)

where N_p counts plaquettes touching the patch. PA would require the much
stronger lower bound `gap(a_B+a_C) >= 1-1/160`. At fixed physical size,
N_p grows with the number of microscopic plaquettes and beta does not tend
to zero. A5's certified lower bound then tends to zero. This attempt does
not supply PA. It proves no upper bound on the true patch gap and is not a
counterexample to PA. Subtracting an exterior-only constant from S changes
neither its oscillation nor the normalized measure.

**Attempt A.ii: use the account's convex fibre, reading and argument.** The
existing `convex-maximal-correlation.md` would control the angle if its
pointwise convex-product-domain Hessian hypotheses held after integrating
the overlap. They are not hypotheses of the full conditional Wilson law.
Gauge directions are flat at a stationary configuration; on a torus even
constant commuting infinitesimal connections have zero quadratic curl.
A small-field, gauge-fixed constrained fibre removes different modes.
The outline itself distinguishes that fibre's gap from a physical gap
(SA 766–779). Covering the whole group configuration space with that fibre
would require cuts, their denominators, and all remaining histories. No
bound for their joint effect on PA was found in the indexed source ranges.
The convex-fibre estimate therefore cannot simply be inserted into A1.

**Attempt A.iii: make exceptional histories rare, argued stress test.** A
small exceptional probability does not by itself control maximal correlation.
For `0<epsilon<1/4`, put on {0,1}² the strictly positive law

    p_11=epsilon,   p_10=p_01=epsilon²,
    p_00=1-epsilon-2epsilon².

Both marginals have `p=epsilon+epsilon²`. The correlation of the two
indicators is

    c = (epsilon-p²)/(p(1-p)),
    1-c = epsilon/[(1+epsilon)(1-epsilon-epsilon²)] -> 0.

Yet the probability of leaving (0,0) is `epsilon+2epsilon² ->0`.
Maximal correlation is at least c. Thus an exceptional-set probability,
without a bound on how L² functions concentrate there, cannot supply PA.
This quantifies the rare-event warning already in
`convex-maximal-correlation.md:55`; it is not a new SU(2) counterexample.
No rare-history estimate of the account is refuted by this different model.

**Outcome A, argued/OPEN.** A1–A4 give a hand-written sufficient implication
in an existing approach family. All three elementary suppliers fail to
verify its Wilson premise: the density estimate is too weak, the convex
estimate has the wrong domain, and rarity alone has the wrong norm.
**The physical wall has not moved.** PA is an explicit remaining input,
not a proof of it. A simpler all-boundary W hypothesis was already available;
no dominance or practical superiority of PA over W or ES is claimed.


### 8.2 B: change the action, with its identification cost exposed

**Finite-action scope, reading/argued.** The account's theorem read here is
for its Wilson trajectory, not a universality theorem for arbitrary lattice
actions (SA 66–151, 248–274; CA-I 2702–2712). The lane's W criterion is
for compact fibres and positive local specifications. Lemma L/P' is an
OS/spectral implication with its stated convergence and totality hypotheses.
Those abstract implications are not restricted to the Wilson formula.
The finite transfer and centre-crossing identities behind R use the positive
transfer input TM/TM-c (`gap-crux-restate.md:31–35`), not the perturbative
Wilson beta function. A different action can retain those identities while
losing the account's continuum construction.

For example, a smooth strictly positive central plaquette weight with
nonnegative character coefficients gives a nonnegative convolution operator
by Peter–Weyl. Products over temporal plaquettes, positive multiplication
by spatial weights, and gauge projection give the same positive transfer
construction. Twisting by the central element still replaces w(U) by w(-U).
These statements cover positive Wilson mixtures and the positive
fundamental–adjoint weights below. They do **not** automatically cover an
improved action with arbitrary signed rectangle terms; its temporal
reflection positivity must be supplied separately. A heat-kernel action is
another familiar positive-character candidate, but no equality of its 4D
continuum limit with the requested Wilson datum is imported. Each use of R
still requires its other input hypotheses and admissibility.

**Lemma B1, argued — exact linkwise changes are too small a class.** Let
`F:SU(2)->SU(2)` obey the original independent endpoint gauge law

    F(g U k^-1) = g F(U) k^-1   for all g,k,U in SU(2).

Then `F(U)=U z` with `z` central, hence `z=I` or `z=-I`. If F fixes I,
F is the identity.

*Proof.* Taking U=I and k=I gives `F(g)=gF(I)`. Taking U=I and g=k
shows F(I) commutes with every element. The centre of SU(2) is {I,-I},
as follows by commuting with the diagonal subgroup and with the matrix
interchanging its two eigenlines. End proof.

This stops an exact gauge-equivariant, independent-link “straightening”
that would change the coupling. Link-dependent central translations can
change background plaquette signs; they do not change the strength or shape
of the one-plaquette weight. Maps using neighbouring links, gauge fixing,
extra fields, or a different gauge action are outside the lemma. PR 10114's
SOFTHARD P2 already warns against extending an equivariant selector through
a degenerate mean; B1 is a separate small-domain classification, not a
no-go for blocking or for Lüscher's configuration-dependent maps.

**Lemma B2, argued — a quantitative comparison price.** On the same finite
configuration space let

    dnu = exp(-Delta) dmu / mu(exp(-Delta)),
    osc Delta <= omega.

Then `exp(-omega) <= dnu/dmu <= exp(omega)`. Consequently every bounded
F satisfies

    |nu F-mu F| <= (exp(omega)-1) ||F||_infinity.               (B2)

The conditional-variance minimization in A.i also gives a gap comparison
`gap_nu >= exp(-omega) gap_mu` for the same block-update family. To prove
these statements, bound the normalizing integral between the extrema of
`exp(-Delta)` and then minimize the two equivalent quadratic integrals.
The ratio of the extrema of the normalized density is at most exp(omega),
so the gap loss is exp(-omega), not exp(-2 omega).

If per-plaquette action differences, after constants are removed, are
bounded in absolute value by epsilon_h, then a region with N_p plaquettes
has `omega <= 2 N_p epsilon_h`. For a fixed physical four-volume,
`epsilon_h=o(h^4)` is a sufficient sup-norm condition for B2 to vanish.
It is **not necessary** for universality. Nor does this finite-volume
statement interchange the account's volume-first and cutoff limits; a
local comparison uniform in the exterior or another limit-identification
theorem would still be required. Equality of characteristic functionals of
the actual renormalized scalar tests is the relevant readout, not agreement
of two bare quadratic Taylor coefficients.

**An actual positive-action test, argued.** Put
`x=(1/2)tr U`, `a=1-x`, and
`b=1-(1/3)chi_1(U)=(4/3)(1-x²)`, using `chi_1=4x²-1`.
For fixed kappa>0 and beta>8 kappa/3, consider the admissible plaquette action

    S'_p = (beta-8 kappa/3) a + kappa b
         = beta a - (4 kappa/3) a².                            (B3)

Both couplings in the first expression are positive. Expanding its weight
as powers of the fundamental and adjoint characters gives nonnegative
character coefficients, since tensor-product multiplicities are
nonnegative. Its weight is strictly positive. At U=I the extra term is
quartic in the Lie-algebra coordinate, so its quadratic classical
normalization agrees with Wilson's.

Nevertheless on an even-sided periodic four-torus,

    osc(S'-S) = (16 kappa/3) N_p.                              (B4)

The pointwise bound follows from `0<=a<=2`. It is attained: all links I
make every a zero; the central links
`U_mu(x)=(-1)^(sum_(nu<mu) x_nu) I` make every plaquette -I and every a=2.
Even side lengths make this assignment periodic. Thus B4 is an exact hand
calculation, not an extrapolation from a perturbative expansion.

This is a concrete failure of the proposed *uniform density-oscillation
verification*, even within a positive transfer class with matching classical
quadratic terms. It does not establish different continuum limits; such
actions may still be universal by a proof using much less than sup-norm
closeness. That proof is absent from the cited account and is not supplied
here. Choosing this action just because it might be easier moves the hard
obligation to dynamics **and** to identification.

**Larger/deformed theories, reading.** Thin twisted compactifications,
Higgs fields, supersymmetric parents and large N can supply additional
parameters. The relevant limit must remove that parameter while retaining
a common positive physical lower bound and the same scalar datum. Analytic
finite-volume dependence alone is insufficient: `diag(0,t,1)` is an analytic
positive family for t>0, but its gap tends to zero as t decreases to zero.
That toy is not a Yang–Mills counterexample. It fixes what a continuity
argument must bound. AC' and the large-N-to-two passage remain open rather
than being supplied by a choice of action.

**Outcome B, argued/OPEN.** B1 stops the independent-link equivariant
subclass; B2–B4 stop a particular attempted shortcut from classical action
matching to uniform measure comparison. They do not rule out other actions
or a useful deformation. No alternative action with a proved gap and a
proved identification with the requested Wilson continuum theory emerged.
This is a bounded negative result for the attempted verification, not a
physical no-go or a solution of the wall.

### 8.3 C: grow the box, accounting for new modes and all losses

**The physical FF issue, argued with scope.** Consider the continuous-time-type
candidate `K+V`, with `K=sum_e c_e(-Delta_e)`, c_e>0, and V a positive sum
of Wilson plaquette potentials. The electric kinetic term has constant
functions as its zero modes before gauge restriction. The magnetic
plaquette potential vanishes only on a Haar-null flat set; multiplication by
it has no nonzero L² kernel. Thus those raw positive terms have no common
zero vector. Subtracting the total ground energy does not make those same
terms individually positive with a common kernel. This observation concerns
that Hamiltonian presentation, not a derivation of locality for `-log T` of
the isotropic Wilson transfer operator. The latter locality was not supplied
by the account. A ground-state transform can make Dirichlet terms vanish
on constants, but their coefficients involve the unknown ground state and
need not be local. B7's Euclidean parent avoids this issue exactly, at the
price PA already exposed.

**Lemma C1, argued — what a relative approximation would buy.** On a common
Hilbert space with common vacuum projection P suppose, as quadratic forms,

    H_0 >= gamma (I-P),   H_0 P=H P=0,
    |<f,(H-H_0)f>| <= epsilon <f,H_0 f>,   epsilon<1.

Then `H >= (1-epsilon) gamma (I-P)`, by direct substitution. These are
form inequalities on the whole form domain. A small vacuum expectation
of frustration is not this hypothesis. If instead one has a bounded
perturbation of norm at most delta and a simple isolated vacuum, the
min–max comparison gives a gap of at least `gamma-2 delta` when this is
positive; local errors whose total norm grows with the number of terms do
not give a uniform delta for free.

**Lemma C2, argued — the loss in an induction is real.** If valid full-space
gap comparisons at successive steps give
`gamma_(j+1) >= (1-epsilon_j) gamma_j`, then

    gamma_N >= gamma_0 product_(j<N)(1-epsilon_j).              (C2)

For `0<=epsilon_j<=1/2` and `sum epsilon_j<=C`, the product is at least
`exp(-2C)`; integrate `1/(1-x)<=2` on `[0,epsilon_j]` to prove the logarithmic
bound. If a proof pays a fixed factor q<1 at each of `N_h>=c/h` steps, its

**New degrees of freedom cannot be omitted, argued counterexample.** On n
qubits set

    H_n = sum_(j=1)^n 2^(-j) Q_j,
    Q_j=|1><1| on qubit j.

It is exactly frustration free and has a unique product vacuum. Its gap is

#### GC1. A gauge corner forces a large full-L² angle

**Lemma GC1, argued.** In the cover of 8.1 there is an interacting pair B,C
and an elementary plaquette p such that

    ||E_B E_C-E_(B union C)|| >= mu_beta(x_p),
    x_p=(1/2)tr U_p.                                          (GC1)

This holds for the actual periodic Wilson measure on each sufficiently large
cubic torus, with no small-field approximation.

*Geometry.* Choose C one coarse index to the right of B in coordinate 1
and one index below B in coordinate 2, with the same other two indices.
Let v have first coordinate the left endpoint of C's interval and second
coordinate the lower endpoint of B's interval. Choose its other coordinates
strictly inside the common intervals. Neighbour overlaps of length at least
two guarantee that

    v belongs to B and C;
    z=v-e_1 belongs to B only;
    w=v-e_2 belongs to C only;
    u=v-e_1-e_2 belongs to neither.

The block rule assigns links by their bases. Therefore the links
`U:z->v` and `V:w->v` are respectively B-only and C-only, whereas
`A_0:u->w` and `B_0:u->z` are exterior to the union. Every link incident
to v is inside the union: incoming links in directions 1,2 are U,V;
those in directions 3,4 lie in the common interior, and all outgoing links
have base v. A gauge rotation at v thus leaves the exterior fixed.

*Conditional calculation.* Fix the exterior tau and write
`T=B_0^-1 A_0`, which is now fixed. Let

    f=x(U),   g=x(TV),   x(W)=(1/2)tr W.

A gauge rotation k at v sends both U and V to their right translates by
k^-1. The conditional Wilson law is invariant under it. Hence each of U
and TV is Haar marginally, and f,g have conditional means zero and
conditional squared norms 1/4. The elementary SU(2) Haar identity

    integral x(R k^-1) x(S k^-1) dk = (1/4) x(R S^-1)

follows from matrix-coefficient orthogonality in dimension two. The right
side is unchanged if its argument is inverted. Cyclicity of the trace then
gives

    E_tau(fg) = (1/4) E_tau x(U^-1 T V)
             = (1/4) E_tau x_p.

Here `U_p=A_0 V U^-1 B_0^-1`. We have `E_C f=f`, `E_B g=g`, and
`E_(B union C)f=E_(B union C)g=0`, both conditionally and in the full law.
Thus the two-projection angle is at least the absolute value of this
conditional correlation coefficient. Equivalently, in the full law f,g
have norms 1/2 and
`<g,(E_B E_C-E_(B union C))f>=mu_beta(x_p)/4`.
Cauchy–Schwarz proves GC1. The exterior-dependent T is allowed because
its links lie outside both blocks. End proof.

#### GC2. The required plaquette mean is uniformly close to one

**Lemma GC2, argued.** For beta>=1 on any cubic four-torus with side at
least three,

    1-mu_beta(x_p)
       <= [log beta + 8 + (2/3)log 24]/beta.                   (GC2)

*Proof.* Write `A=sum_p(1-x_p)`, `Z(beta)=integral exp(-beta A)dHaar`,
`N_e=4N^4` and `N_p=6N^4`. On SU(2), the normalized Haar volume of the
angle ball of radius delta<=1 is
`(2/pi) integral_0^delta sin²(theta)dtheta >= delta³/24`:
use `sin(theta)>=theta/2` and pi<=4. Restrict every link to that ball.
Bi-invariance and the triangle inequality bound a plaquette angle by
4 delta, so `1-x_p<=8 delta²`. Choosing `delta=beta^(-1/2)` gives

    -log Z(beta)
       <= N_e[log 24+(3/2)log beta]+8N_p.

The function `F(beta)=-log Z(beta)` is concave, `F(0)=0`, and
`F'(beta)=mu_beta(A)`, since `F''=-Var_beta(A)<=0`. Therefore
`beta mu_beta(A)<=F(beta)`. Divide by beta N_p and use translation and
axis-permutation symmetry of the cubic torus to identify each plaquette
mean. This is GC2, uniformly in N. End proof.

The estimate is deliberately crude. It uses neither a perturbative series
nor a conjectured continuum mass, and it is valid even when N increases
arbitrarily fast with beta.
