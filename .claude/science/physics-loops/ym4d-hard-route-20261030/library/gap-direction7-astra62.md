### A test on one spatial face

**PROVED, given the checked fixed-graph limit of rows 1375/1376.** Fix the release witnesses and *any* allowed $\ell$. Put $v=u/2=n/1024$, $F=P_0T$, and write $k_v$ for the nonnegative kernel of $F^v$. Let
$$
 p_K(x,y)=k_v(x,y)^2/Z_+(2v),\qquad \mu_K=\mathcal L_{p_K}(x).
$$
For fixed $R<\infty$, let $\mathcal M_R(n)$ consist of normalized densities on the **raw spatial links**, against product Haar, that are products of finitely many nonnegative measurable factors. Each factor reads a set of links of diameter at most $R$ in the spatial link graph. Links sharing a vertex are adjacent. The factors can vary with position and cutoff, with arbitrary strength and arity. Take finite integrable products with positive normalizer. Then
$$
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\ \sup_{q\in\mathcal M_R(n)}
       \operatorname{Aff}(\mu_K,q)=0.                                      \tag{1}
$$
Here $\operatorname{Aff}(p,q)=\int\sqrt{dp\,dq}$. Consequently the same limit is zero for $\operatorname{Aff}(p_K,q\otimes q)$, by Cauchy–Schwarz upon integrating out the second face. In particular it is zero for the spatial Wilson density used as the trial in row 1600, Attack 6, even with a freely chosen coupling. This last statement tests that trial at the *required physical period*.

**PROVED (conditional affinity loss).** Translate and rescale the two conditional one-colour normals to unit variances. Let $g(x,y)$ be their joint density, with correlation $r$, and let $A$ be the integral operator with kernel $\sqrt g$. The affinity to a product density is at most $\|A\|$, by taking the two unit square roots as test vectors.  \|A\|^4\le\operatorname{Tr}[(AA^*)^2]
 =\mathbb E\exp[-r^2(X-X')^2/(4(1-r^2))]=\sqrt{1-r^2}.

The equality for the trace follows by integrating first over the two $Y$ variables. The last equality uses $X-X'\sim N(0,2)$. **PROVED (amplification and order of limits).** Fix a positive integer $m$. Place $m$ translates of this same patch in a line, at separation $D$ much larger than $B+R$. Join successive observed graphs by simple paths, each meeting its two patches only at its endpoints. Keep only those path edges in the gaps. Take a spanning tree consisting of the patch trees and connectors. Retain all raw tree links, all observed based chords and the $2m$ withheld based chords. At fixed $m,D$ this is a finite connected graph in a contractible box on all sufficiently large tori. The raw links on this graph are recoverable from the retained data. For any candidate $q$, no factor can contain two holes. Every other argument of a factor meeting a hole is observed. Thus, conditional on $S$, the $2m$ individual holes are independent under $q$. Given $S$, passing to based, scaled chords only changes each individual hole by its own invertible map. This argument also covers trials which break gauge invariance. Keeping the tree variables matters for those trials.

In the Gaussian limit, connectors traversed out and back cancel in the linear current. Each chord is consequently a translate of a circulation in a *single* patch. Cross-patch covariances tend to zero as $D\to\infty$. Indeed their Fourier integrands are translates in position of the product of two finite-current symbols times $1/\sqrt{h(h+4)}$. This is integrable in three dimensions. Its oscillatory Fourier coefficients tend to zero by approximation in $L^1$ by trigonometric polynomials. Each patch covariance is positive definite. At fixed $m$ the full Gaussian laws therefore approach $m$ independent copies of the patch law in TV because the covariance determinants and inverses converge, and the densities have a common integrable Gaussian envelope for all sufficiently large $D$. Give the independent-patch comparison the same independent Haar tree variables as the Wilson graph. They change neither distance. For each $D$ the two laws agree on the entire independent tree factor.

Against this independent-patch law, condition on all observed coordinates, including the raw tree. Its conditional law on the holes is a product of $m$ correlated Gaussian pairs. The trial conditional law is a product on the $2m$ individual holes. The preceding bound gives conditional affinity at most $\gamma^m$. Integrating against the geometric mean of the two observation marginals keeps the bound, by Cauchy–Schwarz. Trial conditionals outside their marginal support contribute zero. The limiting Gaussian is on all of Euclidean chord space. Extend the trial density by zero off its principal charts.

Projecting probability measures to the retained data can only increase affinity, by conditional Cauchy–Schwarz. Replacing a probability law $p$ by $p'$ changes its affinity against *every* trial by at most $\|\sqrt p-\sqrt{p'}\|_2\le\sqrt{2\|p-p'\|_{\rm TV}}$. First let $K\to\infty$ at fixed $m,D$ using row 1375. Then let $D\to\infty$ at fixed $m$ using the Gaussian comparison. This gives $\limsup_K\sup_q\operatorname{Aff}(\mu_K,q)\le\gamma^m$ for every $m$. Taking $m\to\infty$ proves (1). No estimate for a graph growing with $K$ or independence of distant Wilson patches at a fixed cutoff was used. There is no claimed rate in volume.

**PROVED (scope).** If a trial is a mixture of at most $J$ such densities, its affinity is at most $\sqrt{J}\sup_{q\in\mathcal M_R}\operatorname{Aff}(\mu_K,q)$. This follows from $\sqrt{\sum_j a_jq_j}\le\sum_j\sqrt{a_jq_j}$ and $\sum_j\sqrt{a_j}\le\sqrt J$. Thus finitely many global labels of uniformly bounded cardinality do not repair the trial. Growing spatial range, an increasing number of labels, and integrating an auxiliary field all evade the conditional-independence step. Even a fixed-depth hidden slab need not be Markov on its *observed* face. The theorem says nothing adverse about such a slab.

 J_t(X,Y)=\int\prod_{e=(i,j)}
   \frac{\exp[t\,w(X_e^{-1}g_iY_e g_j^{-1})]}{z(t)}\,dg,\qquad
 w(U)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}U,\quad z(t)=\int e^{tw(U)}dU .              \tag{4}

 D_K(t)=\log

$$
The reading $w_{j,e}$ is the plaquette trace in (4). My target, on a single $\ell,g_0$ and infinitely many $K$, is

### What the first Wilson calculation gives

**PROVED (first nonzero coefficient).** At fixed cutoff, give the ends $X,Y$ of one free piece the probability law $S(X,Y)\,dX\,dY/G(v)$. For each *unoriented* spatial plaquette $p$ let $w_p(X)$ be its normalized trace. In this law put
$$
 m_p=\mathbb E\,w_p(X)=\mathbb E\,w_p(Y),\qquad
 C_{pq}=\mathbb E[w_p(X)w_q(Y)]-m_pm_q.                                  \tag{8}
$$
The matrix is symmetric by time reflection. The signed joining cost satisfies
$$
 D_K(t)=\frac{t^8}{64^2}\sum_{p,q}C_{pq}^2+O_K(t^{10})
                    \qquad(t\longrightarrow0).                             \tag{9}
$$
In particular it reads a genuine gauge-invariant correlation at separation $v$ at each positive coupling. This differs from the zero vertex-gauge response in row 1605.

To prove it, first expand a *single* join at fixed ends. On a forest of selected edges the dressed link variables $X_e^{-1}g_iY_e g_j^{-1}$ have independent Haar laws. Integrating a leaf gauge first proves this for arbitrary functions of the selected dressed links. Thus every coefficient up to degree four agrees with the product normalizer, except for four distinct edges forming a square. These are the only cycles of that length on the present torus. Reverse edges as needed using invariance of the SU(2) trace under inversion. On the oriented square the four normalized traces contribute a factor $2^{-4}$, and the four vertex integrals contribute $2^{-4}$ by $\int g_{ab}\overline{g_{cd}}\,dg=\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}/2$. The remaining index contractions trace the two holonomies separately, giving $4w_p(X)w_p(Y)$. There is one term per square because the four distinct-edge orderings cancel the factorial. The torus is bipartite. Multiplying the vertex gauges on one sublattice by the central minus identity reverses every link trace, so the integrated kernel is even in $t$. It follows that
$$
 J_t(X,Y)=1+(t^4/64)U(X,Y)+O_K(t^6),\qquad
 U(X,Y)=\sum_p w_p(X)w_p(Y).
$$
All Taylor expansions here are finite-volume expansions of analytic integrals near zero, uniform in the pinned group elements at each fixed cutoff. No uniform radius or remainder in $K$ is asserted.

At $t=0$ the six pieces are independent. Each isolated join has the same one-join expectation in A and B because it meets the ends of two *different*, identical pieces. Subtract the four logarithms of these one-join expectations in each arrangement. Expanding the logarithm of the remaining expectation, the first possible term uses two distinct joins $i,j$ and has coefficient $64^{-2}\sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Cov}(U_i,U_j)$ at degree eight. Independence kills all pairs supported on disjoint pieces. In A the two joins of the ring share *both* pieces. Their covariance is $\sum_{pq}(C_{pq}^2+2m_pm_q C_{pq})$ by (8) and independence of the pieces. In B there are two pairs that share exactly one piece, each contributing $\sum_{pq}m_pm_q C_{pq}$. Subtraction proves (9). For $\beta_K>0$, $C_{pp}=\langle w_p-m_p,S(w_p-m_p)\rangle/G(v)>0$. Indeed the one-link Wilson convolution has strictly positive coefficients in every spin by GL3 (outline 2195–2231, index chunk 02, 88–91). Its finite tensor product is injective and positive. Vertex projection preserves this on gauge-invariant vectors, as does sandwiching the transfer by its strictly positive spatial half-weights. Thus $S$ is strictly positive as a quadratic form there, and $w_p-m_p$ is a nonconstant gauge-invariant function. The elementary Schur integral is standard. The new calculation is the cancellation of the shared-one-piece terms between these two arrangements.
