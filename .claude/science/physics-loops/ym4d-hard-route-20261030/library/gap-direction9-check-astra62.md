## Break-it estimate at a fixed physical wavelength

Use the spatial adjoint Laplacian of row 1283, equations (1)–(4). Its quadratic form is the sum over spatial bonds xy of |sₓ − Ad(Yₓᵧ)sᵧ|². Check 1289, step 5, verifies both sides of its spectral count. [Row 1283](gap-solve32-astra62.md) · [Check 1289](gap-solve32-check-opus.md)

For a Wilson slice Y in an ordinary periodic n⁴ box define

$$
D_n(u)=\frac{1}{3n^3}\operatorname{Tr}\mathbf1_{[0,u]}(L_Y).
$$

Fix release parameters L, M, g₀. Choose A larger than the square of the geometric constant in 1283 (1). Choose ℓ divisible by 1024 so large that even ℓ/√A is above the chosen weak range. Set n = ℓ Lᴷ and u = A/n². Thus u in physical units is the fixed squared inverse length A/ℓ². Here is Λ, with 0 < c < C independent of K:

$$
c n^3\log n\ \le\ -\log \mathbb E_{\beta_K}D_n(A/n^2)\ \le\ C n^3\log n
\qquad(K\ge K_0).
$$

**Clauses 1–2, short reading — HOLDS.** This gives both an upper bound and a positive lower bound on the mean number of rare low modes after normalization. It probes the whole slice. In compact U(1) the adjoint connection is trivial. The scalar constant section gives Dₙ ≥ n⁻³ under its rank-one normalization, so the left inequality fails, at weak coupling as required. Dimension enters through the packing count. A spatial block with radius of order u⁻¹ᐟ² has order u⁻³ᐟ² disjoint test cells. In five spacetime dimensions that count is of order u⁻². Specifically, on the same n, β_K comparison sequence the two-plaquette test still has private links with at most eight staples per link and tilt at most 8β_K. The Poincaré estimate in four spatial coordinates still costs O(r²). The block probability estimate below then gives E Dₙ ≤ exp(−c n⁴ log n) with four-dimensional spatial normalization and A chosen large enough for both packings. This violates the right inequality with n³. The dimension test needs no assertion about a 5D physical gap.

**Clause 3 — HOLDS for the transfer comparison in check 1556(iii).** Tensor the transfer with a decoupled centre-neutral two-state factor with eigenvalue ratios 1 and exp(−1/n²). Normalizing the product law cancels that factor from the distribution of Y. It leaves Λ exactly as before. In the transfer test there are now at least two weights at time w with ratio at least exp(−w/n²) between the second and the top. Even the best scalar times a rank-one fit in the normalization of Stage 0 has squared error at least

$$
\frac{e^{-2w/n^2}}{1+e^{-2w/n^2}} .
$$

Indeed, for a positive operator B the optimal unit vector is a top vector, and minimizing over the scalar normalization gives squared error 1 − ‖B‖²/‖B‖²_HS. For 1 ≤ w ≤ n/1024 the lower bound tends uniformly to 1/2, above (2/5)². Thus even exact knowledge of this gauge-slice statistic cannot force the seed by steps valid for a neutral spectator. This is an information test on the enlarged transfer class. Pure Wilson could use an additional estimate coupling the slice data to its transfer.

**Clause 4 — HOLDS, including the estimate itself.** Here is the first probability estimate used for the difficult side. In a disjoint cell let q₁,q₂ be the two based plaquette holonomies of 1283 (2). Let D be the minimum, over unit colour vectors, of the sum of squared displacements by Ad(q₁), Ad(q₂). Then

$$
\Pr(D<v\mid\text{outside the cell})\le C_1(1+\beta_K)v.
$$

On |Im q₁| ≥ |Im q₂| the Gram bound gives D ≥ 2|Im q₂ × ŵ₁|², where ŵ₁ is the unit direction of Im q₁. Ignore the null zero case. Fix the first plaquette and expose a private link of the second. The event requires a tube of squared transverse radius at most v/2 under a one-link Haar tilt of size at most 6β_K. Use the cap bound of 1141 (3)–(4), then interchange the plaquettes for the other ordering. This is the estimate checked in 1289, lines 325–334.

To see the size, put v = B u for a fixed sufficiently large B and r = ⌊κ/√u⌋ with sufficiently small fixed κ, as in 1283 (3). Enough good cells put the block spectrum above u. Conditioning successively, the probability of a bad block with M disjoint cells is at most 2ᴹ[C₁(1+β_K)v]ᴹᐟ². Here M ≥ c₂u⁻³ᐟ². Neumann bracketing gives the same upper bound in order for the expected spectral fraction. For the reverse bound, 1283 (1) gives

$$
\mathbb E D_n(u)\ge c_3 u^{3/2}\exp[-C_3 u^{-3/2}(1+\beta_K+\log(1/u))].
$$

Its witness is a box of side ⌈4π/√u⌉ with all internal links in a Haar cap of radius √(u/48), tested by a Dirichlet sine vector. Check 1453, BND-1, gives β_K ≤ W′+2BK for a fixed release calibration (here B is the release bound). Hence β_K = O(log n). Substitution gives both sides of Λ. This is a specialization of the existing hand estimates, with no new certificate or physics assumption.
