## 7. Direct spectral check

For a direct check using only fixed lattice translations, take any finite linear combination $F$ of positive-time tuples and set $v_j=Q_j[\iota_jF]$, $v=Q[F]$, where $Q$ projects off $\Omega$. In the first-passage length unit, each $t=2m$, $m\in\mathbb N$, is exactly an even lattice time, since $a_j=B^{-K_j}$. By self-adjointness and P-ev(c),

$$
 u_j(\iota_jF,\iota_jF;2m)
 =\|T_j^{m B^{K_j}}v_j\|^2
 \le e^{-2mM_*}\|v_j\|^2.
$$

For **fixed** $m$, both the left side and the zero-time norm on the right are finite sums of fixed separated moments and products of such moments. Their limits give

$$
 \langle v,e^{-2mH}v\rangle\le e^{-2mM_*}\|v\|^2.
$$

For $0<\delta<M_*$ the spectral theorem now gives

$$
 \|\mathbf 1_{[0,M_*-\delta]}(H)v\|^2\le e^{-2m\delta}\|v\|^2.
$$

Let $m\to\infty$ and then $\delta\downarrow0$. The classes $Q[F]$ are dense in $\Omega^\perp$, so the spectral projection of $[0,M_*)$ vanishes on that complement. Since $H\Omega=0$, both conclusions follow. Once the compact scalar Hilbert space and its identified translation semigroup are given, fixed-tuple convergence at these common times suffices for the spectral inequality. The per-order uniform estimate in §4 verifies the stronger moving-time convergence actually requested by P′-ev.
