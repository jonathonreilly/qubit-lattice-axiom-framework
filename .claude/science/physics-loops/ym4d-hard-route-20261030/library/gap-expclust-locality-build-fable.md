## 2. Setting and the result in one paragraph

Fix a cutoff index $j$. The lattice has spacing $\varepsilon_j=L^{-K(j)}$. The state $\nu_j$ is the infinite-volume Wilson $SU(2)$ state at $\beta_{K(j)}$. $X_p$ is the energy of face $p$. At fixed $j$ the energies are bounded: $|X_p|\le b_j$. Distances are physical maximum-norm distances. Write
$$
e_j(R)=\sup_p\|X_p-\mathbb E[X_p\mid\mathcal B_R(p)]\|_2 .
$$
Estimate (2) of row 1707 at $j$ is $e_j(R)\le A_je^{-\kappa R}$ for all $R\ge0$. The requirement is (2) at arbitrarily late $j$ with one $\kappa>0$.

**Result.** I did not prove (2) for $SU(2)$. I did not prove an obstruction for $SU(2)$. I proved five structural statements (sections 3 to 7). Together they show this. ## 3. Lemma V (PROVED): the bounded range is void

**Statement.** Let $R_0\ge0$, $\kappa>0$ and $j$ be arbitrary. For every map of the class (any map with $|X_p|\le b_j$), $e_j(R)\le b_je^{\kappa R_0}e^{-\kappa R}$ for all $0\le R\le R_0$.

**Proof.** A conditional mean is the $L^2$ projection, so $e_j(R)\le\sup_p\|X_p\|_2\le b_j$. For $R\le R_0$, $b_j\le b_je^{\kappa R_0}e^{-\kappa R}$. QED

**Corollary V1 (PROVED).** (2) holds at $j$ with rate $\kappa$ for some finite $A_j$ if and only if there is $R_0$ with $e_j(R)\le A'e^{-\kappa R}$ for $R\ge R_0$. So (2) at $j$ implies $\limsup_{R\to\infty}R^{-1}\log e_j(R)\le-\kappa$, and this limsup bound implies (2) at $j$ for each smaller rate $\kappa'<\kappa$.

**Corollary V2 (PROVED).** In lattice units, (2) at $j$ says: the fixed law $\nu_j$ on $\mathbb Z^4$ at coupling $\beta_{K(j)}$ has lattice locality rate at least $\kappa L^{-K(j)}$. The prefactor is free, so no uniformity in $j$ is asked and no estimate at lattice distances below $R_0L^{K(j)}$ is asked.

**Use.** The brief names the partial result "(2) at the scales inside the account's range". If the range is a bounded physical distance, this partial result is true for every bounded field and has no content. Rung LO1, first alternative, is void. All the content of (2) is at $R\to\infty$ at fixed $j$. That is the regime at and beyond the scale where the effective coupling is of order one.

## 4. Lemma G (PROVED): a larger class and the rate $2\kappa/3$

**Class $\mathcal C$.** Let $I$ be a countable set. Give each $i\in I$ a position $x_i\in\mathbb R^4$. Let $(\xi_i)_{i\in I}$ be independent random variables in any measurable spaces. Let $X_p=\Phi_p(\xi)$ have joint law equal to the face energy law of $\nu_j$. Let $\mathcal B_R(p)$ be generated by the $\xi_i$ with $x_i$ within distance $R$ of $p$. The sequential quantile map of row 1707 is in $\mathcal C$ (one uniform at each face). A map that first samples the link field, or block variables at several scales, and then reads the energies is also in $\mathcal C$.

**Statement.** Let a map in $\mathcal C$ satisfy (2) at $j$. Let $F$ and $G$ be polynomials in finitely many $X_p$, with supports $S_F$, $S_G$ at distance $t>0$. Then there is $C=C(F,G,A_j,b_j)$ with
$$
|\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)|\le C\,e^{-2\kappa t/3}.
$$
So ExpClusters holds with $m=2\kappa/3$. Row 1707 has $m=\kappa/3$.

**Proof.** Step 1 (row 1707, the telescoping): for a polynomial $F$ and the $\sigma$-field $\mathcal F_R$ of the $\xi_i$ within $R$ of $S_F$, $\|F-\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_R]\|_2\le c_Fe^{-\kappa R}$. Define $\mathcal G_S$ and $c_G$ in the same way. Step 2: if $R+S<t$ then $\mathcal F_R$ and $\mathcal G_S$ are independent. Then $\mathbb E[\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_R]\mid\mathcal G_S]=\mathbb EF$, so
$$
\|\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal G_S]-\mathbb EF\|_2=\|\mathbb E[F-\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_R]\mid\mathcal G_S]\|_2\le c_Fe^{-\kappa R}.
$$
Let $R\uparrow t-S$: the bound is $c_Fe^{-\kappa(t-S)}$. The same holds with $F$ and $G$ exchanged. Step 3: write $F_R=\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal F_R]$, $G_S=\mathbb E[G\mid\mathcal G_S]$ with $R+S<t$. Then
$$
\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)=\operatorname{Cov}(F,G_S)+\operatorname{Cov}(F_R,G-G_S)+\operatorname{Cov}(F-F_R,G-G_S).
$$
First term: $\operatorname{Cov}(F,G_S)=\mathbb E[(\mathbb E[F\mid\mathcal G_S]-\mathbb EF)G_S]$, at most $c_F\|G\|_2e^{-\kappa(t-S)}$. Second term: $\mathbb E[G_S\mid\mathcal F_R]=\mathbb EG$, so it equals $\mathbb E[(F_R-\mathbb EF)(\mathbb E[G\mid\mathcal F_R]-\mathbb EG)]$, at most $\|F\|_2c_Ge^{-\kappa(t-R)}$. Third term: at most $c_Fc_Ge^{-\kappa(R+S)}$. Take $R=S\uparrow t/3$. The reflection, the translations, the finite sums and the finitely many early times are handled as in row 1707. QED

**Remark.** The constant 2/3 is the gain. It does not change what is open.

## 5. Lemma N (PROVED): what (2) forces

**N1 (any map in $\mathcal C$).** (2) at $j$ gives exponential decay of all truncated two-set correlations of face energies, in every direction, at rate $2\kappa/3$ (Lemma G). So (2) is at least as strong as ExpClusters and it adds the space directions.

**N2 (the sequential map; the first face).** Let $p_0$ be the first face of the order. $X_{p_0}$ is a function of its own uniform $U_0$ alone. For every face $p$ at distance $d$ from $p_0$,
$$
|\operatorname{Cov}(X_p,X_{p_0})|\le A_jb_je^{-\kappa d}.
$$
**Proof.** For $R<d$ the ball $\mathcal B_R(p)$ omits $U_0$, so $\mathbb E[\mathbb E[X_p\mid\mathcal B_R(p)]\mid U_0]=\mathbb EX_p$ and $\|\mathbb E[X_p\mid U_0]-\mathbb EX_p\|_2\le e_j(R)$. Then $\operatorname{Cov}(X_p,X_{p_0})=\mathbb E[(\mathbb E[X_p\mid U_0]-\mathbb EX_p)X_{p_0}]$. Let $R\uparrow d$. QED

**Use.** Row 1707 proposed the response to a resample of the first face as the cheapest rejection test (rung LO2). N2 shows what this test is: by the same projection, the response dominates $\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E[X_p\mid X_{p_0}])$. So a bound on the first remote influence that is good enough for (2) already contains the exponential decay, at rate $\kappa$, of the two-point function of one plaquette energy with all others. That function is the scalar glueball correlator. Rung LO2 is not below the crux.

**N3 (rate ceiling).** Let $\mu_j$ be the physical decay rate of $\operatorname{Cov}(X_p,X_{p_0})$ in the slowest direction. Then $\kappa\le\mu_j$ for the sequential map and $\kappa\le3\mu_j/2$ for every map in $\mathcal C$.

## 6. Theorem GM (PROVED, with two standard facts CITED): the Gaussian case is settled

This is the model in which the class can be computed. It serves as the free twin.

**Setting.** $\phi$ is the centred Gaussian field on $\mathbb Z^d$ with precision $m^2-\Delta$ ($\Delta$ the nearest-neighbour Laplacian); $m>0$, or $m=0$ and $d\ge3$. Take **any** enumeration of $\mathbb Z^d$ in which each site has finitely many predecessors. Write $S_q$ for the set of sites up to and including $q$. The sequential quantile map is $\phi_q=\mathbb E[\phi_q\mid\phi_{<q}]+\sigma_qW_q$, $W_q=\Phi^{-1}(U_q)$, with $\sigma_q^2$ the conditional variance. Distances are lattice maximum-norm distances.

**GM1 (exact formula).** $\phi_p=\sum_{q\le p}L_{pq}W_q$ with $L_{pp}=\sigma_p$ and, for $q<p$ in the order,
$$
L_{pq}=\sigma_q\,H_{S_q}(p,q),
$$
where $H_S(p,\cdot)$ is the law of the first point of $S$ visited by the simple random walk from $p$ that is killed at rate $m^2/(2d+m^2)$ per step.

**Proof.** CITED (standard Markov property): for finite $S$ and $p\notin S$, $\mathbb E[\phi_p\mid\phi_S]=\sum_{r\in S}H_S(p,r)\phi_r$, and the residual is independent of $\phi_S$. $W_q$ is a function of $\phi_{S_q}$, is independent of $\phi_r$ for $r<q$, and $\operatorname{Cov}(\phi_q,W_q)=\sigma_q$. So $L_{pq}=\operatorname{Cov}(\phi_p,W_q)=\sum_rH_{S_q}(p,r)\operatorname{Cov}(\phi_r,W_q)=H_{S_q}(p,q)\sigma_q$. Each $\phi_p$ is a finite linear combination of the $W_q$, $q\le p$. QED

Guide (exact rationals, `python3 -I`, fractions): on the $3\times4$ torus, $m^2=1/3$ and $m^2=2$, a random order, all 66 pairs $q<p$ satisfy $\operatorname{Cov}(\phi_p,\iota_q)/\sigma_q^2=H_{S_q}(p,q)$ exactly, and every row of $H$ is nonnegative with sum below 1. The script is not in the repository. It is a guide and proves nothing.

**GM2 (massive: (2) holds, every order).** Let $\cosh\mu=1+m^2/2$. Then $H_S(p,q)\le e^{-\mu|p-q|_\infty}$ and
$$
\|\phi_p-\mathbb E[\phi_p\mid W_q:\ |q-p|_\infty\le R]\|_2^2=\sum_{q\le p,\ |q-p|_\infty>R}L_{pq}^2
\le G_m(0)\sum_{n>R}2d(2n+1)^{d-1}e^{-2\mu n}.
$$
**Proof.** The function $u(x)=e^{\mu x_1}$ satisfies $\sum_{y\sim x}u(y)=(2d+m^2)u(x)$. So $u$ at the killed walk (zero after killing) is a nonnegative martingale. By Fatou at the hitting time of $q$, $P_p(\hbox{reach }q\hbox{ alive})\,u(q)\le u(p)$. Choose the axis and its sign so that $q_1-p_1=|p-q|_\infty$. $H_S(p,q)$ is at most this probability. $\sigma_q^2\le\operatorname{Var}\phi_q=G_m(0)$. The sphere of radius $n$ has at most $2d(2n+1)^{d-1}$ sites. The error is the tail sum because the $W_q$ are independent standard Gaussians. QED

**GM3 (sharpness).** CITED (standard): $G_m(ne_1)$ decays at exactly the rate $\mu$. By N2 (here directly: the term $q=p_0$ of the tail sum is $G_m(p-p_0)^2/G_m(0)$), no sequential order has lattice rate above $\mu$. So the best rate of the sequential class is exactly $\mu$, for every order. In physical units with $m=m_{\rm ph}\varepsilon$ the rate $\mu/\varepsilon$ tends to $m_{\rm ph}$, and in $d=4$ the prefactor is bounded as $\varepsilon\to0$.

**GM4 (massless: no map).** For $m=0$, $d\ge3$, CITED: $G_0(x)\sim c_d|x|^{2-d}$. By N1 no map in $\mathcal C$ satisfies an exponential bound for $\phi$. The same holds for $\phi^2$ by Wick, as in row 1707.

**GM5 (energies as squares).** For $m>0$ let $X_p=\phi_p^2$ and use the map of GM1 for $\phi$. It is in $\mathcal C$. With $\phi_p=a+b$, $a$ measurable on the ball and $b$ an independent centred Gaussian, $X_p-\mathbb E[X_p\mid\mathcal B_R]=2ab+b^2-\mathbb Eb^2$, so the error is at most $2\|a\|_2\|b\|_2+\sqrt2\,\mathbb Eb^2\le Ce^{-\mu R}$. The energy-only quantile map is not needed.

**What GM shows and does not show.** For a Markov Gaussian field, locality of the sequential map is the same as the mass, the order is irrelevant, and the mechanism is the sub-probability $\sum_rH\le2d/(2d+m^2)<1$ of one conditioning step. This is the contraction that a Dobrushin argument uses. The Wilson energy field is neither Gaussian nor Markov. At $\beta_K$ one conditioning step has no such contraction: a face is nearly fixed by the other five faces of a cube (Bianchi) (ARGUED). GM gives no estimate for the Wilson law.
