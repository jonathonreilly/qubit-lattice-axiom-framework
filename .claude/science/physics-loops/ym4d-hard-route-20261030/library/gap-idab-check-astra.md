For GS, a second way to see the crucial step avoids the note's inverse
formula. By R, \(\operatorname{ran}R=\Delta\ker Q'\). Choose
\(\lambda\in\ker Q'\) with \(\Delta\lambda=-RD^*A\). Then
\(RD^*(A+D\lambda)=0\). QD preserves the datum and \(dD=0\) preserves
the Wilson energy. Positivity gives the opposite inequality. This checks
the exact gauge-removal step, including the gauge kernel, without assuming
that the Wilson Hessian itself is invertible.

For PS, the seemingly stronger simultaneous lifting condition also holds:
extend each coarse \(\mu\) constantly over its complete fine block. Both
its value at the centre and its block mean equal \(\mu\). Thus both plain
and straightened rows take \(D\lambda\) to \(\bar D\mu\). The value
functions are quadratic: project the affine fibre's curvature image onto
zero in a finite-dimensional space. This also proves existence and
continuity of their minima without a strict gauge-fixed gap.

For QD, use the actual formula, not the prose description of its sign.
At identity the derivative of the block factor is
\(s(A)(y)=L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(y)}\overline A(y,x)\), and straightening is
\(Q^\sharp=K+\bar D s\). A gradient gives
\(s(D\lambda)=q\lambda-r\lambda\), where \(r\) evaluates at the centre.
Since \(KD\lambda=\bar D r\lambda\),
\(Q^\sharp D=\bar Dq\). At identity the straightened row is exactly the
straight-segment row. Composing its telescoping identity gives the mean
over the nested block at depth \(k\). In the physical conventions of
outline 10139, the fine derivative contains \(\eta^{-1}\), the starting
logarithm contains \(\eta\), and the coarse derivative contains
\(\xi_k^{-1}=1\). These factors give the same intertwining identity.
