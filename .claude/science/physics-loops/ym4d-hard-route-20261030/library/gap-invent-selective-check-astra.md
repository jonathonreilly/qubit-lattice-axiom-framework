## First verdicts: exact statements

Here `R` means a line of `gap-invent-selective-max.md` at the source pin. HOLDS refers only to the statement with its displayed hypotheses. A failing inference does not refute its conclusion without a counterexample.

1. **WN(i), R138–144: HOLDS.** For either traversal of a geometric link, its translated factor starts with the positive endpoint exponential and ends with the negative endpoint exponential. Cyclically moving the final exponential to the start of the trace leaves `F_departure-F_arrival` at each corner. This also checks the backward-link sign required by W1(a).

2. **WN(ii), R140,144: HOLDS.** Each unpinned factor undergoes fixed left and right Haar translations. Pinned factors are unchanged. The numerator integral is exactly the original partition function. Compactness and smoothness justify both derivatives without a limiting argument.

3. **WN(iii), R140–144: HOLDS.** Read “two corners” as two distinct corners. Independently write the second derivative as the sum of single-insertion terms `-beta_p x_p theta_i^2` and double-insertion terms. At a free corner `i`, local gauge averaging conjugates the one `c` insertion there while transporting all intervening links. Its mean is zero: the adjoint representation has no invariant vector. The other insertion is at another vertex. Thus its average vanishes, including when the other vertex is pinned. An elementary plaquette has four distinct vertices under the side-length assumption. The differentiated normalization gives the claimed positive score variance. This is a second-moment identity, not independence of corners or a statement about all higher moments.

4. **WN1, R146–150: HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION.** Say “an unpinned cubic torus,” or explicitly assume a common plaquette mean. R138 allows unequal side lengths; constant coupling alone does not supply rotations exchanging unequal periodic directions. On a general unpinned rectangular torus retain `E x_p` inside the sum. A link in direction `k` occurs `k-1` times as a second-link square and `4-k` times as a third-link square. Replace the mixed dummy indices in R150 by these. With the common-mean assumption the displayed formula and constant 3 hold. For completeness the difference squares are at most `6 ||f||_2^2`, so the full form lies between `3 beta xbar ||f||_2^2` and `9 beta xbar ||f||_2^2`. Here `xbar>=0`: if `S=sum_p x_p`, then `(log Z)'=E S`, `(log Z)''=Var(S)>=0`, and `E_0 S=0`; cubic torus symmetries give the common mean. Zero coupling causes no exception. “No cheap direction” covers these deterministic left translations, not a pinned law or field-dependent axes.

5. **WN2, R152–156: HOLDS.** The nonzero corners must be pairwise distinct sites across the *whole* corner field, exactly as stated. Average the score at each such site to get `W'(0)=0`. 6. **WN3, R158–162: HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION.** Make the same unpinned cubic-torus assumption, now for both `E x_p` and `E(1-x_p^2)`. Orient all six plaquettes to traverse the selected link first. ## First verdicts: load-bearing extensions

12. **The extension of WN to every pinned law: FAILS, first at R179** (also R299's unqualified “every”). There is an explicit counterexample within C1's class. Take one square, pin three links to the identity, and integrate its remaining link `U` with weight `exp(beta tr(U)/2)`, `beta>0`. Put `F=1` at both ends of this free link and zero on all other link-ends. Then `tau_t U=e^(tc) U e^(-tc)`, so `X=0` pointwise. Its two nonzero corners have squared sum 2. The proposed diagonal formula would give `2 beta E x>0`. Positivity follows by pairing `x` and `-x` in the one-plaquette integral: `x sinh(beta x)>0` for `x!=0`. Neither endpoint is a free *site*, so WN(iii) correctly excludes this example. R179 drops that hypothesis. The same exclusion prevents using WN to discard the entire pin-uniform C1 class.

13. **R166's corner graph: HOLDS for the unpinned full lattice; its final exclusion FAILS at R166.** There are eight link-ends at a site, grouped in four opposite pairs. Distinct-direction pairs give `6·4=24` edges, namely `K_(2,2,2,2)`. Deterministic endpoint values therefore have a site-local *quadratic* cost modulo a site-constant gauge direction. This proves no spatial gradient gain from that quadratic form. It does not prove a bound on every fourth-order family identity: products of gauge-invariant local squares at different sites need not have zero covariance. The proposed family identity in R103 and R166 was never written. Correct replacement: “The direct quadratic form supplies no lattice-gradient estimate; no fourth-order selective estimate is established.”

14. **C2's curvature identity: HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION; its unconditional rejection FAILS at R192.** A sheet with one twisted corner per distinct free site satisfies WN2. On the common-mean torus its curvature is `beta rho A`. To contradict a proposed `-Z''/Z<=delta_A`, one must prove `beta rho A>delta_A` in the same volumes. Neither `rho>0` nor the needed uniformity is proved. At one finite area, positive `rho` alone need not violate an unspecified positive `delta`. R200, R204 and R299 must not call C2 an exact refutation. Replacement: “If a volume-uniform positive lower bound on rho holds, a bound with delta_A/A tending to zero fails at t=0 for this sheet.” This neither proves nor endorses a noncentral interpolation route.

15. **R164's rho assertions: HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION to open estimates.** The small-beta character argument and the large-beta free-field count are unproved here; the latter also needs an order of volume and coupling limits. The proposed condition `E(1-x)<3/(2 beta)` is not by itself sufficient. For example, the moment data `beta=3`, `x=3/5` have deficit `2/5<1/2` but `x-(beta/3)(1-x^2)=-1/25`. This tests the claimed moment implication, not the Wilson measure. R285's stronger sufficient condition *does* hold: writing `d=E(1-x)`, `1-x^2<=2(1-x)` gives `rho>=1-(1+2 beta/3)d>0` if `d<3/(3+2 beta)`. Use that condition consistently. Its supply remains open.

Item (2) is false on its unrestricted pinned class by item 12 above. Item (3a) holds only with WN2's distinct-free-site hypotheses and the extra rho estimate; (3b) describes a possible boundary response, not a classification theorem. Item (4) adds unsupported claims about frames, their decay scale and the necessity of blocking. Neither WN nor TWIST rules out field-dependent changes of variables; C8 itself is one. Correct replacement: “Gauge-orbit boundary changes give no response. Deterministic translations on the WN class have the displayed quadratic cost. The constructions tried here give no proved selective step.” R183's “only global symmetry” must distinguish physical symmetries from redundant gauge actions and symmetries of a chosen representation. No new symmetry classification is proved here.

17. **JEN's scaling and necessity claim: FAILS as stated, first at R214.** Its first finite-eta upper bound holds with the pinned-site terms *retained*. The next sentence replaces those terms by a common bulk `xbar` and a corner-square norm without justification. Pinned vertices are precisely where the free-site averaging is unavailable. The claim that “nonzero curl” yields a lower bound of order `beta eta^2 R` also lacks a normalization away from gauge directions.

   A direct counterfamily makes the latter problem concrete. Take identity boundary links on a cube of side R. Choose a bounded vertex function `phi_R`, varying on scale R, and extend it to all vertices. On boundary links set `a_l=phi_R(x)-phi_R(y)+epsilon_R b_l`, where `b` is supported on one boundary link, and choose `phi_R` with nonzero gradient of size `1/R`. Then `a` has size `1/R` and nonzero curl for every `epsilon_R>0`. All twists are about `c`. Translate the integrated links by the gauge transformation `exp(eta phi_R c)`. After the gauge change the only remaining twist is the one boundary link with coefficient `epsilon_R`. This is an allowed JEN trial field: only the action of each pinned translation matters, and all identity pins commute with `c`.

   At identity boundary data the expectation of the linear single-insertion term vanishes under common conjugation. Each of at most six affected plaquettes has mean change `(cos(eta epsilon_R)-1) E x_p`. Thus the Jensen bound is at most `6 beta [1-cos(eta epsilon_R)] <= 3 beta eta^2 epsilon_R^2`. Take `epsilon_R=R^(-2)`. It is incompatible with any positive lower constant times `beta eta^2 R` under the written conditions. This does not refute such scaling for a specified normalized transverse profile; that narrower problem is not solved in R214. Even a lower bound on the *best Jensen upper bound* would not lower-bound the exact free-energy response, whose covariance can cancel it. R218's “cannot” and “needs the account” therefore do not follow.

18. **K8/refilling, R258: FAILS as the stated lattice implication.** TWIST establishes invariance, not an action gap, an entropy bound, or a large-field activity. In particular, sample any smooth boundary gauge map and prescribe the pure-gauge links `u_xy=Omega_x Omega_y^(-1)`. Extend its vertex values to the interior. Every plaquette of this lattice filling is the identity, so its Wilson action is zero, regardless of the degree assigned to a chosen continuum boundary interpolation. Excluding such a filling requires additional smoothness or admissibility conditions on the interpolation; none is stated in R258. A continuum winding obstruction and a positive lattice activity price are different assertions. Replace R258 by: “Refilling requires an explicitly specified admissible class and an action/entropy estimate. TWIST alone neither obstructs nor constructs such an admissible refilling.” No `exp(-c beta_k)` price or value of c is established.
