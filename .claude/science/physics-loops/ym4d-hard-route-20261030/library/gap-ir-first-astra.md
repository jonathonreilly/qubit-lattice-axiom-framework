## Step 2 result: V4 with its identification and consumer

Fix a physical reference length a_0 and a blocking factor L. Let X_k(T)
be the SU(2) link configuration space on a fixed physical torus T, at
a_k = a_0 L^{-k}. Write B_k:X_{k+1}(T) -> X_k(T) for the actual map,
and B_{k,N}=B_k ... B_{N-1}. A proposed V4 family is a collection of
probability laws mu_{k,T}, for every k and every required T, with

    (B_k)_* mu_{k+1,T} = mu_{k,T}.                         (1)

There are four distinct requirements.

1. **IR:** mu_{0,T} has a specified cluster expansion or mixing bound,
   uniform in T, with a strictly positive rate in physical units.
2. **UV:** all finer laws have the actual small-field/large-field
   decomposition, regular background domains, Balaban coupling ceiling,
   running-coupling normalization and trajectory comparison bounds required
   by the account. A small-field support condition alone is not this package.
3. **Reconstruction and transfer:** the continuum limits have the required
   Euclidean/OS properties; exponential decay reaches a dense set of physical
   observables with a common positive exponent. Decay on the pullback of
   X_0 alone is insufficient.
4. **Identification:** the reconstructed observables agree with the chosen
   pure SU(2) Yang–Mills construction, with its scale and source normalization.
   If only the account's action-density correlations are identified, the
   conclusion concerns that scalar sector. The full gauge-invariant theory
   needs additional observables and their compatibility (SA 31860–31869).

In the UV clause the constants and admissibility witnesses must be fixed
coherently across depths and the required tori. CA-I 4136–4198, Theorem 4.3,
requires a family of effective actions with the inductive analyticity,
Euclidean, localization and coupling-increment properties, with gamma
chosen after the other constants. It states forward preservation of those
properties by Balaban's small-field integral; it does not state inverse
existence or classification. CA-I 49495–49511, located through the
4.12–4.19 index, additionally separates actual background admission from
the free-current operator theorem's implication. A small value assigned
to a coupling does not supply either witness.

There are consequently two readings to keep distinct. Membership in the
inductive domains is a constraint on an action and its representation.
Being obtained from the specified Wilson runs, with matched sources and
renormalized parameter, is an additional formation/identification condition.
If the phrase "account's trajectory" already imposes that formation, it
does not constitute a theorem classifying other domain-admitted laws.
Even then the observable and limit requirements in (6) below must be stated.

**ARGUED conditional chain, with an elementary final implication proved
below:** (1) + IR + UV + transfer + identification -> exponential clustering
of the identified continuum theory -> its spectral mass gap. The consumer
is the local continuum-gap branch of `parts/gap.md`, as distinguished in

Here is the precise last implication. Suppose OS reconstruction has a unique

    <v, exp(-t H) v> <= C_v exp(-m t),  t >= 0,             (2)

with one m>0. For 0<r<m its spectral measure satisfies
exp(-rt)||1_[0,r](H)v||^2 <= C_v exp(-mt). Taking t to infinity
gives zero. A countable sequence r increasing to m gives
1_[0,m)(H)v=0. This bounded projection vanishes on a dense set and hence
on the whole vacuum complement. Thus Spec(H) is contained in {0} union
[m,infinity). **PROVED as an implication.** Neither density nor (2) is
supplied by merely choosing mu_0.

For example, the exact covariance identity under a lift is

Coarse clustering bounds the second term only when the conditional means

Consistency in k is finite algebra. Existence and identification of limits

## Step 3 result: unrestricted preimages exist; admission is a different question

**PROVED, using the account's explicit lift.** Let S_k:X_k -> X_{k+1}
be its crossing-bond lift, with B_k S_k = id (SA 7127,7215–7234).
For every probability mu_0, recursively set mu_{k+1}=(S_k)_*mu_k.
For every bounded Borel f,

    integral f(B_k U) dmu_{k+1}(U)
      = integral f(B_k S_k V) dmu_k(V) = integral f(V) dmu_k(V).

This proves (1) at every step. It also gives a measure on the full inverse
limit: push mu_0 forward by V -> (V,S_0 V,S_1 S_0 V,...).
The coordinate maps are Borel; their countable product is measurable.
No compactness or selection assumption is hidden in this construction.

The geometric section is already in Sussman–Alpoge. In that section all
intrablock links are identity. Every straight segment between corresponding
points of neighbouring blocks crosses their common face once and carries
the same coarse link. Thus its closed comparison transport is identity;
both logarithmic rules give zero and the fallback gives that same coarse
link. This verifies the stated section against the actual three-rule B,
including coarse fields outside its analytic domain. It is not an inverse
on all fine configurations.

If mu_k is gauge invariant, averaging (S_k)_*mu_k over the compact fine
gauge group gives a gauge-invariant lift. Covariance of B and invariance
of mu_k show that its pushforward is still mu_k. This repair alone gives
neither reflection positivity nor the required action.

**FAILS for these lifts: Wilson/UV admission.** Choose an elementary
plaquette wholly inside a block (L>=3). Its holonomy under S_k is exactly
identity, also after gauge averaging. Under a finite-coupling Wilson law
this event has probability zero: condition on the other links of that
plaquette, and its remaining link has a strictly positive continuous
density relative to nonatomic SU(2) Haar measure. Thus the lifted law is
singular relative to Wilson measure. It has no Wilson-type fluctuation
density on these interior plaquettes.

There is also a direct small-field diagnostic. A fine plaquette at the
intersection of two block faces carries precisely the corresponding coarse
plaquette holonomy. Under repeated sections it remains so. If

    mu_0{||V_p-1|| > delta} = c > 0,

then at every depth k a corresponding p_k has that same probability c.
Consequently these lifts cannot obey a UV estimate, uniform over plaquettes,
of Pr(||U_p-1||>epsilon_k) -> 0 with epsilon_k -> 0. Choosing mu_0 in
a clustering domain supplies no such estimate. The all-flat special case
evades this diagnostic but has no nontrivial Wilson fluctuations either.

This is a failure of the explicit section construction, not a proof that
every lift fails. The mere many-to-one property would not have established
surjectivity; the account's section is what establishes it.

**Which constraint is missing?** Write D_k for the class of full effective
laws satisfying the actual account hypotheses at level k, including the
specified norm constants and source/trajectory comparisons. The relevant
question is whether mu_k belongs to B_{k*}(D_{k+1}), and whether choices can
be made compatibly for all levels. Surjectivity of B on configurations says
nothing about this restricted image. Even nonempty, unrelated finite-depth
admissible sets do not give an infinite admitted tower without a closedness
or compatible-selection argument. No such argument is supplied here.

Thus unrestricted existence is **PROVED**; existence in D_k at all scales,
with a prescribed clustered mu_0, remains **OPEN**.

## Step 4 result: a proved gap model, and the theorem that would repair it

### 4.1 Coarse reweighting preserves every conditional refinement law

**PROVED.** This statement applies to the actual B whenever a reference
projective family nu_k has been supplied. Let h:X_0 -> (0,infinity) be Borel,
with integral h dnu_0=1 and 0<c<=h<=C<infinity. Define

    dmu_k(U) = h(B_{0,k}U) dnu_k(U),   B_{0,0}=id.         (5)

Then mu_k are probabilities satisfying (1), and mu_0=h nu_0. Indeed, for
bounded Borel f on X_k, projectivity of nu gives

    integral f(B_k U) h(B_{0,k+1}U) dnu_{k+1}(U)
      = integral f(V) h(B_{0,k}V) dnu_k(V).

For j<k, take a regular conditional kernel K_{j,k}(V,dU) of nu_k given
B_{j,k}U=V. These spaces are finite products of compact groups and hence
standard Borel. Since B_{0,k}=B_{0,j} B_{j,k}, disintegration gives

    mu_k(dU) = integral h(B_{0,j}V) nu_j(dV) K_{j,k}(V,dU).

Thus the **same** K_{j,k} is a conditional kernel for mu_k given mu_j.
All conditional fluctuation bounds phrased entirely inside these fibres
are identical. Also c nu_k(A)<=mu_k(A)<=C nu_k(A) for every event A.
In particular uniform small-field probability estimates survive with a
constant factor. If h is nonconstant modulo nu_0, the coarse laws differ.
If the reference laws are gauge invariant, taking a bounded nonconstant
gauge-invariant h preserves that invariance.

There is an elementary nonempty example, requiring no Yang–Mills existence
assumption. On each site of a finite spatial torus let Y be a fair sign,
and Z_j independent uniforms on [-g_j,g_j], j>=1, with
g_j=(H+c_0 j)^(-1/2), H>1 and c_0>0. X_k records Y,Z_1,...,Z_k
at each site; B deletes the last layer. All laws are finite product laws.
At one site take h(Y)=1+epsilon Y, 0<|epsilon|<1. Then (5) changes
E Y from 0 to epsilon, leaves every conditional Z-law unchanged, and has
bounded density ratio. To preserve spatial translation invariance, instead
use the product of these h's over all sites. For each fixed torus it is
positive and normalized. Both families have exact spatial independence,
hence clustered coarse laws. Their local UV increments obey |Z_j|<=g_j,
and j g_j^2 -> 1/c_0, identically in both families. This last equality is
a prescribed numerical running schedule, **not** a computed Yang–Mills
beta function. The product density ratio need not be volume uniform;
no volume-uniform comparison is inferred from it.

The example can keep the coarse law fixed as well: leave Y fair and
replace the first-layer density by

    (1 + epsilon z/g_1)/(2g_1),  -g_1 <= z <= g_1,

at every site. Keep every j>=2 layer unchanged. Projection still gives
the same mu_0. Normalization follows from oddness; positivity follows
from |epsilon|<1; direct integration gives E Z_1=epsilon g_1/3.
Two choices of epsilon yield different projective families with the same
coarse law, the same UV tail and the same small-increment bounds.

**What the model refutes.** Conditional UV estimates, a vanishing running
scale and projectivity alone do not classify a family. This is a
counterexample to that implication, with finite integrals as its proof.
It is not an SU(2) Wilson model and does not purport to satisfy all D_k.

For actual B, (5) exposes the missing constraint directly. Relative to nu_k
the new action is changed by -log h(B_{0,k}U). A coarse interaction may
therefore read a fixed physical region, spanning order L^k fine cells.
Boundedness of h does not control an exponentially weighted interaction
norm on those fine cells. For a representation assigning one nonzero
activity of size a to a polymer of diameter d_k, its contribution to such
a norm is a exp(kappa d_k); that representation fails a uniform bound
when d_k grows. This observation is **not** a proof that every alternative
local representation fails. Locality, the permitted relevant parameters,
source matching and actual trajectory formation must all be checked.
They are precisely what the conditional-kernel calculation does not supply.

### 4.2 When backward contraction really does give uniqueness

**PROVED.** Suppose D_k are spaces of admissible **full laws** with metrics
d_k, and B_{k*} sends D_{k+1} to D_k. Suppose q_k>=0 and, for any two admitted laws,

    d_k(B_{k*}rho,B_{k*}sigma) <= q_k d_{k+1}(rho,sigma).

For two projective families suppose d_N(mu_N,nu_N)<=M_N and, for every
fixed k, M_N product_(j=k)^(N-1) q_j -> 0 as N->infinity.
Iteration gives d_k(mu_k,nu_k) <= M_N product q_j; taking the limit gives
d_k=0, hence mu_k=nu_k for every k. In particular uniform q_k<=q<1
and bounded M_N suffice. If only irrelevant coordinates are contracted,
this proof identifies only those coordinates, and only after all other
coordinates and their forcing have been matched.

Two exact models show why the qualifiers cannot be dropped.

- Strict contraction at each step need not have a vanishing product.
  For delta>0, on [-delta,delta] let q_k=exp(-2^(-k-1)) and B_k x=q_k x.
  For any |a|<=delta/e set x_k=a exp(1-2^(-k)). Then B_k x_{k+1}=x_k
  and |x_k|<=delta. Distinct a give distinct bounded ancient families,
  although every B_k is a strict contraction. Indeed the product from k
  to N-1 is exp(-2^(-k)+2^(-N)), with positive limit exp(-2^(-k)).
  Interpreting x as the Dirac law at x, with distance |x-y|, makes this
  also an example of projective probability laws with those contractions.
- Even uniform contraction of **all designated irrelevant coordinates**
  leaves other coordinates. Let

      R(x,t,r) = (x-1,L^2 t,q r),  0<q<1.

  For x_k=H+k, t_k=t_0 L^(-2k), r_k=0, one has
  R(x_{k+1},t_{k+1},r_{k+1})=(x_k,t_k,r_k).
  Every t_0 in [0,delta] stays small and tends to zero in the UV;
  g_k=x_k^(-1/2) has the same prescribed running for every t_0.
  The irrelevant coordinate contracts uniformly, yet t_0 is undetermined.
  Here t is a relevant mass-squared coordinate. Gauge invariance forbids
  simply importing a scalar mass term as a pure Yang–Mills counterexample.
  The model refutes a classification argument that never proves its list
  of allowed coordinates complete. It does not refute such a completeness
  theorem if one is supplied.

Therefore the Opus backward-expansion intuition is correct under the
quantitative full-law hypothesis just proved. Those hypotheses are not the
fixed-torus uniqueness statement, and are not established by the cited
passages. No contraction estimate for a few polymer coordinates is silently
promoted to this metric estimate on all compatible laws.

## Step 5 result: controls and kill tests

### Compact U(1) and a free fine theory

There are two different claims to test. A fine theory can have a free
**ultraviolet leading term** and a massive infrared limit; leading matching
alone decides neither. If the identified **whole limit** is the free photon,
an honest coarse observable that detects its low momenta cannot cluster
exponentially. Arbitrary lifts can evade that statement only by failing
identification or by observing too small an algebra.

Here is a hand control for the second claim. In Euclidean free Maxwell
theory in four dimensions, in Feynman gauge,

    <A_mu(x) A_nu(0)> = delta_(mu,nu)/(4 pi^2 |x|^2).

For the gauge-invariant curvature F_12, at x=t e_4, direct differentiation
gives

    <F_12(t e_4) F_12(0)>
      = -(partial_1^2+partial_2^2)(1/(4 pi^2 |x|^2))
      = 1/(pi^2 t^4).                                    (7)

Let phi be smooth, nonnegative, compactly supported, with integral one,
and let X_t be F_12 smeared with phi translated by t e_4. These Gaussian
variables have a common finite variance v. Differentiating the explicit
kernel bounds its first derivative by C t^(-5) on the bounded smearing
region for sufficiently large t. Integrating this bound gives
C(t):=Cov(X_t,X_0)=pi^(-2)t^(-4)+O(t^(-5)). The elementary Gaussian
characteristic function now gives

    Cov(cos X_t, cos X_0) = exp(-v)(cosh C(t)-1)
                         >= exp(-v) C(t)^2/2.

For all sufficiently large t this is at least c t^(-8), c>0. It cannot be
bounded by C exp(-m t) for any m>0. **PROVED for this free Gaussian model.**
The same argument applies when the coarse linear readout is another compact
smearing with nonzero integral: it retains the leading term in (7).

Thus a claim that this physical free theory is obtained from a fully
exponentially clustered coarse algebra detecting these readouts **FAILS**.
If B discards the readouts, transfer to the full physical algebra fails
instead. This is not an assertion that the nonlinear compact-group B is
globally its linearization at finite coupling. For compact Villain U(1),
the lane separately uses the weak-coupling massless result of Guth and
Fröhlich–Spencer (the theorem-grade range in its PT control). That result
is a cited input here, not re-proved or extended to order-one Wilson
coupling. None of (1), (5), or the lifting argument implies a U(1) gap.
