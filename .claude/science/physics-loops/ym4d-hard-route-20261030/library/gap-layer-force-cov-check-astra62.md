**HOLDS for the unweighted harmonic shell, including the half-open outer boundary.** Use a spatial torus of dimension $d$, $4\mid n$ as on the test trajectory, and quotient the real heights by constant shifts (equivalently set their mean to zero). Completing the square in lines 192–197 gives
$$
 \operatorname{Cov}(F_{x,i},F_{y,j}\mid X,Y)=\beta P_{ij}(y-x),

$$
where $d_0$ is the forward difference. The background changes the force mean only. In the finite Fourier transform put $h_j(k)=e^{ik_j}-1$, $\lambda(k)=\sum_j|h_j(k)|^2$. Up to the Fourier phase convention the multiplier is $h_i\bar h_j/\lambda$, with value zero at $k=0$. Summing $P_{11}(y)$ over the transverse plane retains only $(k_1,0,\ldots,0)$. Inverting $1_{k_1\ne0}$ gives $\mathbf1_{y_1=0}-1/n$. Exactly $n/2$ ring sites satisfy $n/4\le |y_1|_{\rm per}<n/2$ when $4\mid n$. **Torus-kernel bound, lines 206–209 — HOLDS.** Here is the finite-period step missing from the citation. For $r=|y|_{\infty,\rm per}\ge1$,
$$
 |P_{ij}(y)|\le C_d(1+r)^{-d}
$$
uniformly in $n$. Use the numerator and derivative estimate (16) in the Riesz proof identified above, or differentiate $h_i\bar h_j/\lambda$ using $\lambda\asymp |k|^2$ in the Brillouin cell. Derivatives of order $m$ are bounded by $C_m |k|^{-m}$ off zero. Apply the outline's finite-grid split with mesh $\delta=2\pi/n$, integer $M=d+1$, and $R=\max(r^{-1},2M\delta)$. Since $r\le n/2$, $R\asymp_d r^{-1}$. For bounded $r$ the multiplier norm gives the result after increasing $C_d$. Otherwise cut off smoothly at radii $R,2R$ inside the frequency cell. The low part of the normalized Fourier sum is at most $C_d R^d$ by counting grid points. For the high part $H$, the $M$-th coordinate difference is at most $C\delta^M(R+|k|_{\rm per})^{-M}$ by the iterated fundamental theorem of calculus. The shift in that formula is at most $M\delta\le R/2$. The normalized sum of the last power is at most $C R^{d-M}$ by summing on dyadic annuli (each has at most $C(n2^jR)^d$ points). Sum by parts $M$ times on the periodic grid in a direction with $|y_i|_{\rm per}=r$. The phase divisor is $|e^{i\delta y_i}-1|\ge 4r/n$. Thus the high part costs at most $Cr^{-M}R^{d-M}$, as required. In particular the bound holds *on* the antipodal planes. Direct absolute summation of infinite-lattice $|x|^{-d}$ images would give a divergent majorant.

Inside the counted slab, removing transverse antipodal planes discards at most $(d-1)n^{d-1}$ terms, each at most $C_d n^{-d}$. The actual **unweighted half-open sum is at least $\beta(1/2-C_d/n)$**. In the claimed arbitrary-dimensional extension, on a ring of length $4m+1$ even the closed sum is exactly $2m\beta/(4m+1)$. Coordinate permutation symmetry on the half-open shell $B$ therefore gives $\sum_{y\in B}|P_{11}(y)|\ge |B|/(d n^d)$, bounded away from zero.
