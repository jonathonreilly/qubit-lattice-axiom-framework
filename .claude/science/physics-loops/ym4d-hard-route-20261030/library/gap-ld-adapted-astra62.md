## First lemma. Protected hot plaquettes force the plain factors locally — proved by hand

Fix a coarse bond, an oriented longitudinal axis $\mu$, fine period $mL$ and $m\ge2$. Write $J=\mathtt{baseDepth}$ and $s=\mathtt{threshold}$. All bounds are for the unconditioned Wilson input at $x=\beta>0$. `CutsLumpsBasesAndContours.lean` 37–61, 73–104 and `LumpsOnATorus.lean` 55–78 are the source definitions. Unit mesh requires $1\le J$, $45056J<L$, odd $L\ge2^{40}$ and $0<s<1$. In the constructed parameter assignment the extra bound $2^{10}L(N(0)+1)s\le1$ gives $s\le 1/(1024 L)$. The kernel uses this same threshold even when it is evaluated below the proof's floor.

1. **Nonempty base sets.** They factor by cell. In a noncrowded cell there are $k<J$ nearby lumps. A collar has at most $d=2^{15}-1+2(2^{12}+2)=40963$ sites along any coordinate. Form a separated cover by joining boxes at sup distance at most two, taking their rectangular hull with compatible periodic lifts. If a box has $v$ original collars, each side has at most $v(d+1)-1$ sites. In a merge the projections have gap at most one missing site, so the two side lengths plus one suffice. The bound is less than $L$ at every merge and compatible lifts exist by lifting one pair of nearby points coordinate by coordinate. This terminates at a separated cover. The least-cover predicate forces every returned excluded box to be contained in a box of this cover. If no least cover exists the definition returns the empty set. The excluded projections use at most $k(d+1)<L$ sites per coordinate. Choose an allowed coordinate independently on each of the four length-$L$ cell intervals. Empty and crowded cells have the forced centre. Consequently every fine field has at least one base choice. The factors are normalized Haar densities by quaternion rotation, so integrating first over $V$ gives the original Wilson law as the fine marginal of the normalized joint law. The base conditional given $W$ before observing $V$ is uniform on the product of these sets.

2. **A posterior estimate uniform in the exterior.** In *each* grid lump of a given cell choose one interior elementary plaquette on two axes different from $\mu$, with a chosen edge at distance at least two from the boundary of its lump. There are $N\ge (L/2^{15})^4$ such lumps by the grid partition, all sides being smaller than $2^{15}$ and at least $2^{14}$. The chosen edges are plaquette-disjoint. Resampling them changes only fine plaquettes with all corners in that cell. All the test plaquettes survive any later changes of $\mu$-links. Under Wilson conditional on the other links, the test variables are independent. A chosen edge has density proportional to $\exp(\kappa\,\operatorname{Re}(u g^{-1}))$, $0\le\kappa\le6\beta$, by the six linear Wilson staples.

   One lower bound on the conditional probability of a hot test is $p_1=1/(1+e^{12\beta})$. The two antipodal links cannot both put its plaquette in the open chordal ball of radius $s<1$ at $1$, and their Wilson density ratio lies in $[e^{-12\beta},e^{12\beta}]$. For the single-plaquette convention here $w_\beta(t)w_\beta(-t)=e^{-2\beta}$. The staple tilt gives the displayed antipodal ratio directly. A second lower bound is $p_2=1-4[s\sqrt{1+6\beta}]^3$. Use $p=\max(p_1,p_2)$ below. Indeed Haar chord radius $r$ on $S^3$ has density $(2/\pi)r^2\sqrt{1-r^2/4}$ for $0<r<2$. The normalizer is at least $e^{\kappa-1/2}$ times the Haar mass of the cap of radius $(1+\kappa)^{-1/2}$ at the tilt maximum. The Haar masses of the two caps have ratio at most $2[s\sqrt{1+\kappa}]^3$. This bound is uniform in the centre of the cold cap.

   For fixed $V$, the adapted posterior changes the conditional Wilson probability of any event of these test links by a factor at most $e^{20000x}$. To see this, changing the test links can affect nearby-lump lists only for the at most $5^4$ coarse sites within two cells of the changed cell. All paths stay in their two endpoint cells apart from the bond joining them, since each within-cell coordinate displacement has length $<L\le mL/2$. At most $8\cdot5^4$ coarse factors have an endpoint in the affected set. Drop these factors temporarily. The average of the remaining product over the *unchanged* exterior base sets is then independent of the test links. The local base averages have mass one by part 1. Restoring the factors costs a ratio at most $e^{4x}$ per factor since each lies in $[e^{-2x},e^{2x}]$. This proves the claimed pairwise oscillation, including changes of bases or filters. It also bounds the normalized conditional density ratio.

   If $T$ is the number of hot tests in the cell, $\Pr_{\rm Wilson}(T<J\mid{\rm exterior})\le e^J \mathbb E e^{-T}\le e^{J-pN/2}$ since $1-e^{-1}>1/2$. Therefore under the full posterior at *every* $V$ the probability of failure of that cell's tests is at most
   $$
   b=\min\{1,\exp(20000x+J-pN/2)\}.
   $$
   Every hot test forces a different nearby hot lump at its own cell. On the complementary event that cell is crowded under **every** change of longitudinal links. This is an event measurable off all those links. It does not condition any plaquette of the selected longitudinal word to be cold.

The small-threshold bound gives $p\ge1/2$ throughout $0<\beta\le\sqrt L/3$. The antipodal bound alone gives $p\ge (2L^{3/2})^{-1}$ for all unit-mesh thresholds if $\beta\le(\log L)/8$. In particular it covers order-one coupling without the calibrated choice of threshold.

## A bound on the release's slope, when it exists — proved by hand

The slope is a signed infinite-volume sum. A bound on one diagonal Hessian entry does not bound that sum. Here it is possible to bound the noncontact colour correlations themselves. The argument uses the independent rotations of the **coarse gauge frames** at $V=\mathbf1$. It makes no mixing assumption on gauge-invariant fine Wilson observables.

**A local moment with other bonds observed.** In (1) refine the choice of a line. For two different transverse triples select one with the greatest number $j$ of nonzero coordinates. A leg of the other contour can meet its longitudinal bonds only if its transverse triple extends the selected triple by setting additional coordinates to nonzero values. Maximality and distinctness exclude this. If all the selected triple's nonzero axes precede $\mu$ in its own leg, the two end legs fuse with its central run as before. This includes trailing axes of zero displacement. Otherwise the central run is untouched. A contour on any affected coarse bond using the line now fixes at least those $j$ coordinates, so the one-line oscillation is at most $64x/L^j$. The fraction with both triples having at most two nonzero entries is at most $9/L^2$, and with both having at most one is at most $9/L^4$. Equal triples cost $L^{-3}$ and every remaining pair has $j\ge1$. Using $e^u-1\le2u$ for $0\le u\le64x/L<1/2$ gives the sharper bound
$$
 M_c(V)\le v=\frac{3+256x}{L^3},\qquad a=x\sqrt v\le4L^{-3/4}. \tag{3}
$$
Indeed the three oscillation errors sum to at most $128x(L^{-3}+9L^{-4}+9L^{-5})$. The equal-triple, Wilson-exponential and crowd-failure terms each cost at most $L^{-3}$.

The same bound holds after tilting the posterior by any nonnegative bounded product $F$ of functions of the **norms** of means at bonds whose endpoints all have coarse sup distance greater than eight from the endpoints of $c$ (provided the tilt has positive mass). To check this, the test links for a protected cell are within three cells of an endpoint of $c$. Changing them changes hot lumps only inside that protected cell and base laws only within two more cells. None of the factors of $F$, their endpoint base laws, or their contour lists change. The conditional ratio argument with the unaffected base average therefore holds with $F$ inside that average. The same is true in the longitudinal resampling on $G$. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz under each successive tilt gives, for any such pairwise separated bonds,

 \mathbb E_{\rm post}\prod_{i=1}^k x\|\bar W_{b}(e_i)\|\le a^k. \tag{4}
$$

Split each such factor into the constant $e^{-t_e}$ and its nonnegative remainder, where $t_e=x\|M_e\|$. Mark a bond occupied when its remainder is used. Given the spins and $W,b$, occupancies are independent with probabilities at most $1-e^{-2t_e}\le2t_e$. Given the occupied graph and $W,b$, simultaneous left rotation of all spins on any one connected component preserves the weight. For different bonds $e,f$, its product with $Y_f$ averages to zero unless an occupied path joins their endpoint sets. In the disconnected case rotate together all components meeting $e$ by a common Haar element. This conjugates its quaternion, killing its imaginary mean, and leaves $Y_f$ fixed. The off-diagonal Hessian of $\log D'$ is $x^2\mathbb E(Y_eY_f)$ by LD-1 polarized. Conditional on a connection, use $|xY_e|\le t_e$ and $|xY_f|\le t_f$.

After $i\le k$ such steps the new site is within $13i$ of the start and still at least $r-2-13i\ge23$ from the target endpoint when $k>0$. since $2Da\le 2^{24}L^{-3/4}\le1/64$. Here $K_m$ is the mixed derivative of the finite-volume log density ratio at the trivial field. ## An actual-readout counterexample at the strong-coupling end

**Choice of parameters.** Take $x=\beta=L^{-200}$ and use a convergent expansion uniformly in the coarse volume. At zero fine coupling the interface links are independent Haar variables. Folding fine paths modulo $L$ rules out short fundamental surfaces through order eight. Two copies of a transporter on each of four bonds leave an adjoint plaquette with a strictly positive Gram coefficient. This permits a comparison with the release's *witnessed* readout, including its sign. This family tends to zero in inverse coupling. Equations (2) and (6) apply at order-one and increasing inverse couplings.

**Taylor coefficient of the plain image — proved.** Take $m\ge11$. Through order eight in $x=\beta$ the entire field-dependent part of the *plain* log image is
$$
 \lambda\sum_P\chi_{\rm ad}(V_P),\qquad
 \lambda=B_L\beta^8,\quad B_L\ge 2^{-60}L^{-32},\quad
 \chi_{\rm ad}(g)=4(\operatorname{Re}g)^2-1. \tag{7}
$$
Here $P$ runs once through unoriented coarse plaquettes. The following parity argument concerns Taylor coefficients at zero, where all fine links are independent Haar variables. It does not use a truncated measure as a substitute for the Wilson law.

At total order at most eight, let $q$ count contour occurrences and $p$ the number of fine Wilson plaquette occurrences, with $p+q\le8$. Each power in a plain bond numerator contributes its number of paths, and the reciprocal partition function contributes pairs of paths via powers of $\|M\|^2$. Thus the parity of occurrences on each coarse bond equals the parity of its degree in $V$ on that bond. A necessary condition for a nonzero Haar monomial is that the fine path chain modulo two is the boundary of the $p$ fine plaquettes counted with multiplicity. This follows by negating any fine link with odd multiplicity. The centre-odd coarse bonds form an even-valence graph by coarse gauge invariance (equivalently project the fine boundary to coarse cells). With $p=0$ every coarse interface has even parity by the fine crossing-link signs.

Suppose the odd graph is nonempty, so $p\ge1$ and $q\le7$. Folding each fine coordinate modulo $L$ cancels the two translated end legs of every plain contour as mod-two chains. Each central run becomes a whole axis circle. In any direction distinct transverse triples give disjoint circles of $L$ links that paths in other directions cannot cover after folding. The folded boundary of $p$ plaquettes has at most $4p<L$ links. Consequently every transverse triple in each direction occurs an even number of times, which gives even $q\le6$. A nonempty even graph with at most six distinct bonds on this torus is either a square or a simple six-cycle. Indeed a decomposition into two edge-disjoint cycles would require eight edges, and cycles of length at most six lift to the ordinary bipartite lattice. For a square, at most one extra pair of paths is available, on a single bond. Choose the other square direction if necessary. For a six-cycle, at least one used direction occurs exactly twice, and all six paths are used. We have therefore found a direction with precisely two occurrences, on distinct parallel bonds crossing the *same coarse coordinate hyperplane* in opposite cycle senses (there is just one forward and one backward step in this direction on the lifted cycle). Their transverse fine offsets agree by folding. On the fine dual hyperplane halfway between these coarse layers, the paths cross at exactly two points whose transverse torus distance is at least $L$ because their coarse transverse cells differ and their offsets agree. End legs remain inside cells. Intersecting the fine plaquette chain with this hyperplane gives a transverse edge chain with just those two odd boundary sites, of length at most $p$. It must contain a path between them. This contradicts $p<L$ and excludes every centre-odd term through order eight.

In the centre-even sector a nontrivial coarse bond harmonic requires degree at least two. Project each bond polynomial onto its constant and nonconstant parts under Haar on that bond. These projections commute with endpoint rotations and gauge averaging. A graph of nonconstant bonds with a leaf vanishes under Haar gauge averaging at that leaf. Thus at least four distinct bonds are necessary, and equality at order eight requires the four edges of one elementary square, two powers on each, and $p=0$. The degree-two nonconstant part on one bond is the adjoint harmonic. There is no field-dependent term of lower order, so taking the logarithm preserves this first coefficient up to additive constants.

Here is its sign and size. Write $R(Q)u=Q u\bar Q$ on imaginary quaternions. Then $\operatorname{tr}R(Q)=4(\operatorname{Re}Q)^2-\|Q\|^2$. Since $\int(\operatorname{Re}g)^2dg=1/4$, the degree-two bond term is $\operatorname{tr}(R(M_e)R(V_e)^T)/8$. There are $n_c=24L^4$ contours. Conditional on the internal links in each cell, a contour $i$ is $A_{+,i}U_t A_{-,i}^{-1}$, where $U_t$ is its single interface link at transverse triple $t$, and $A_{\pm,i}$ are within-cell paths from the centre to the two faces in their respective cells. For $i,j$ with the same $t$, the loop $A_{\pm,i}A_{\pm,j}^{-1}$ **does not depend on which face is used** in a given cell. The two paths from their offsets to that face lie on the same longitudinal line. The segment out to the face and back reduces to the segment between the two offsets. Let $v_y^{\mu,ij}$ be the imaginary part of this loop in cell $y$. Independent Haar integration of the crossing links gives
$$
 \mathbb E[R(M_{(y,z)})\mid\text{interiors}]
   = n_c^{-2}\sum_{i,j:\ t_i=t_j}v_y^{\mu,ij}(v_z^{\mu,ij})^T.
$$
Distinct crossing links give zero. For a shared link, use $\int U Q U^{-1}dU=\operatorname{Re}Q$ and $\operatorname{Re}(A_i^{-1}u A_j)=\langle\operatorname{Im}(A_i A_j^{-1}),u\rangle$. The real part on the output cancels on summing the ordered pairs. Distinct square interfaces use independent links.

For directions $\mu,\nu$, index the within-cell features by ordered pairs $\alpha,\gamma$ and set $C_{\alpha\gamma}=\mathbb E[v^{\mu,\alpha}\cdot v^{\nu,\gamma}]$ in one Haar cell. The colour matrix for this pair is $C_{\alpha\gamma}I/3$ by conjugation invariance at the centre. The four independent cell integrals therefore contract the four $R(V_e)$ matrices around the square, transposing the reversed bonds, with scalar
$$
 B_L=(8\cdot3)^{-4}n_c^{-8}\operatorname{tr}[(CC^T)^2].
$$
Explicitly the feature sum is $\sum_{\alpha,\alpha',\gamma,\gamma'}C_{\alpha\gamma}C_{\alpha'\gamma}C_{\alpha'\gamma'}C_{\alpha\gamma'}$. Permuting axes gives the same scalar on every square. Take offset one in each of the two axes orthogonal to $\mu,\nu$ and zero in the others. Use the two orders exchanging those transverse axes, in both features, to obtain the same elementary plaquette loop based at the centre. Its Haar holonomy has $\mathbb E|\operatorname{Im}U_P|^2=3/4$. Thus the trace is at least $(3/4)^4$ by its diagonal square at this feature. This proves (7).

**Uniform remainder and the actual adapted slope — proved.** Convergence uniform in coarse volume lets us use (7) in the release's slope. Use the disc $|\beta|\le r_0=2^{-80}L^{-4}$. Use one independent reference variable per coarse cell, consisting of all fine links starting there and one uniform scalar in $[0,1]$. Enumerating its allowed sites relative to the centre and using this scalar chooses a uniform admissible base. This realizes the product base average exactly. Group the fine Wilson factors whose plaquettes start in $y$ and the four outgoing kernel factors at $y$ into $1+u_y$. For the plain map omit the base selection. Each variable $u_y$ reads only cells at coarse sup distance at most five because the two endpoint lists read hot lumps within two cells, whose corners test plaquettes in at most one further layer. The torus geometry of this rule stabilizes on every finite patch as $m\to\infty$. In detail, the greedy hull constructed above is itself a least cover in a sufficiently large torus. In any separated cover, touching (distance at most two) current boxes must be in the same cover box if both already lie in cover boxes. Their coordinate hull lies there too: intervals of length $<L$ containing the pair must use the same short periodic lifts for $m\ge3$. Induction gives refinement of every cover. Two least separated covers refine each other and have disjoint nonempty members, hence coincide. All the merges are consequently determined by the nearby seeds and take place in a fixed bounded patch for large $m$.

Allow complex rotations of at most two indicated coarse bonds in unit colours, each parameter of modulus at most $1/4$. The analytic continuation of $\operatorname{Re}(M_e V_e^{-1})$ has modulus at most $e^{1/2}<2$, by the power series of the unitary-matrix exponential with the real quaternion trace extended complex linearly. The log of each normalizer has modulus at most $4r_0$, since $|\mathcal Z-1|\le e^{r_0}-1$. There are $6L^4$ fine plaquettes of energy at most two in a cell. Consequently $|u_y|\le Q=2^8L^4r_0=2^{-72}$ throughout this domain.

Expand the product into occupied cell sets and split them into connected sets $S$ for adjacency at sup distance at most ten. Disjoint, nonadjacent sets use independent reference variables. The exact polymer activities are $w(S)=\mathbb E\prod_{y\in S}u_y$ and satisfy $|w(S)|\le Q^{|S|}$. Here is the explicit summability check for the logarithm. With $d=21^4<2^{18}$, the number of size-$h$ connected sets containing a specified site is at most $d^{2h-2}$, by a traversal of a spanning tree. For activities inflated by $e^{3|S|}$ the incompatibility sum satisfies
$$
 \sum_{T\not\sim S} Q^{|T|} e^{4|T|}
 \le |S|\,d\sum_{h\ge1} d^{2h-2}(Qe^4)^h\le |S|.
$$
In the logarithm the coefficient of a list of polymers is the alternating sum over connected subgraphs of its incompatibility graph divided by its list factorial. This follows by exponentiating the connected-graph series and splitting graphs into components. Toggling the least edge whose ends are joined by smaller edges cancels cyclic contributions, giving the spanning-tree majorant. For a fixed polymer $S$ iteration of the exponential of the child sum bounds the rooted tree sum by $e^{|S|}$ at every height. Thus the absolute sum of clusters meeting any fixed site, inflated by $e^{3n}$ for total polymer size $n$ with multiplicity, is at most $\sum_{h\ge1}d^{2h-2}(Qe^4)^h<1$. The connected series is a holomorphic logarithm by exponentiating its normally convergent series from zero. This is the hard-core tree argument of Kotecký–Preiss in the form proved in SA outline 1223–1347, applied here to the two actual local integrals.

The full and plain activities differ by at most $2|S|Q^{|S|}e^{-L^2}$. Indeed at reference Haar a cell has fewer than $J$ hot witnesses with probability at most $\exp(J-N/4)$ for every $s<1$ (antipodal bound at zero coupling). With probability at least $1-5\exp(J-N/4)\ge1-e^{-L^2}$ all five cells of a star are crowded. Its two local factors then agree exactly. Telescoping the products proves the activity bound. Telescoping a cluster product costs at most $2n e^{-L^2}$ times its common absolute majorant. This argument compares its analytic integrands at complex $\beta$ using reference Haar, without treating a complex weight as a probability.

A derivative on a bond with tail $y$ kills clusters missing $y$. For two tails at distance $h$ a contributing cluster also has $10n\ge h$. Cauchy's bound in the rotation parameters costs at most $32$ for a second derivative. The spare cluster weight absorbs $2n$ in the difference. Cauchy's bound in $\beta$ gives a geometric-series remainder after order eight for $0<\beta\le r_0/2$. In particular, for either a single-direction Hessian or a noncontact mixed Hessian, the error of replacing the *adapted log density* by its plain order-eight field term (7) is bounded by
$$
 2^8 e^{-h/10}\big[(\beta/r_0)^9+e^{-L^2}\big], \tag{8}
$$
where $h=0$ for a single bond. These bounds hold at real coarse fields, uniformly in $m\ge11$. For each fixed displacement all bounded-size clusters stabilize as the torus grows. Normal convergence gives the Hessian limit on the *whole* sequence $2L^q$. Constant conjugation makes the limiting bilinear form a scalar times the colour inner product. The finite-volume gauge and torus isometry conditions in `IsGaugeTotalAt` follow by lifting the symmetries. A coarse gauge transformation acts by that rotation throughout each fine cell, and a signed permutation with coarse translation carries the symmetric grid, the unique least cover (or its empty fallback), the base sets and the permutation-indexed contours to the corresponding sets. Reversing a bond interchanges the two legs with the same order. Haar and Wilson are invariant. Nonempty bases give positivity, and compact differentiation gives the required smoothness. The cluster bound gives exponential decay for all unit-colour pairs, including the contact, and absolute convergence of the signed second moment. These facts supply the release's `HasNextCoupling` witness. Its scalar is unique by coefficientwise uniqueness of limits on the nontrivial side filter and uniqueness of the sum.

For (7), opposite parallel bonds of a $(0,1)$ plaquette at displacement $\pm\hat0$ each have mixed log Hessian $+8\lambda$ at the identity, since $\chi_{\rm ad}(e^{uX})=1+2\cos(2u)$. These are the only contributing displacements with $z_0\ne0$. The signed readout is therefore $8\lambda$ up to (8) summed with $z_0^2/2$. Grouping the sup norm in intervals $[10j,10j+9]$ gives $\sum_z z_0^2e^{-|z|_\infty/10}<2^{42}$, using $\sum(j+1)^6 e^{-j}\le 6!\sum\binom{j+6}6 2^{-j}=6!2^7$. At $\beta=L^{-200}$ both the single-bond and readout errors are less than $2^{-12}\lambda$. For instance the polynomial error divided by $\lambda$ is bounded by $2^{52+720+60}L^{-132}$. The other term is at most $2^{112}L^{1632}e^{-L^2}$. Each is below $2^{-14}$ for $L\ge2^{40}$ (use $\log L\le\sqrt L$ for the exponential). In particular
$$
  7\lambda < \mathtt{nextCoupling}(L,\beta,D') <9\lambda. \tag{9}
$$
This estimate concerns the witness branch itself. The fallback plays no role in (9).

**Typical-field comparison — proved.** Use normalized $D'(V)\,dV$. Orient and base each of the six plaquettes at a fixed bond $c$ so that its holonomy reads $G_p=V_c S_p=t_p+\mathbf u_p$. Conjugation, cyclic permutation and inversion preserve the character. With
$$
 A_0=\sum_p t_p^2,\qquad T=\sum_p\mathbf u_p\mathbf u_p^T,
$$
the single-bond action Hessian is the real symmetric form
$$
 H_c(V)=8\lambda(A_0 I-T)+E_c(V),\qquad
 \|E_c\|_{\rm op}<2^{-12}\lambda. \tag{10}
$$
The bound follows from (8) in every unit direction. In fact $\|E_c\|_{\rm op}/\lambda\le L^{-1}$ throughout this family by the same arithmetic. Each $G_p$ contains a different link parallel and opposite to $c$, absent from the other five plaquettes. Under product coarse Haar the six $G_p$ are therefore independent Haar even with the other links fixed. Under the actual image, condition first on $W,b$ and those other links. The six link densities are independent and each is at least $e^{-2\beta}$ relative to Haar. Thus every Haar event of the six holonomies retains at least $e^{-12\beta}$ times its Haar probability.

- **Fixed bond and fixed colour.** For $X=i$ set $S=\sum_p(t_p^2-u_{p,1}^2)$. Under Haar its law is symmetric. A summand has variance $1/6$, since rotation and $(\sum_{j=0}^3q_j^2)^2=1$ give $\mathbb E q_0^4=1/8$ and $\mathbb E q_0^2q_1^2=1/24$. Its fourth moment is at most $1/6$ since its magnitude is at most one. Hence $\mathbb E S^2=1$, $\mathbb E S^4\le7/2$ and Cauchy–Schwarz on $\{|S|\ge1/2\}$ gives $\Pr(S\le-1/2)\ge9/112$. Under $D'$ this event has probability greater than $1/16$. There $H_{c,i}<-3\lambda$ while the actual readout exceeds $7\lambda$. This rules out a positive lower bound with probability tending to one at a preassigned bond and colour.
- **A zero direction at a fixed bond.** Put $A_j=\sum_p u_{p,j}^2$ for $j=1,2$. Under Haar the three numbers $A_0,A_1,A_2$ are exchangeable with no ties. With probability $1/3$, $A_0$ is between the other two. For either difference, conditioning on five plaquettes gives $\Pr(|A_0-A_j|<d)\le2\sqrt{2d}$. For the remaining Haar quaternion $(q_0,q_j)$ its squared radius is uniform on $[0,1]$ and independent of the uniform angle $\phi$ (the two orthogonal coordinate-plane polar variables on $S^3$ have area element proportional to $d(r^2)\,d\phi\,d\psi$). Its difference of squares is $r^2\cos 2\phi$. Split at $|\cos2\phi|=\sqrt{2d}$ and use $2\arcsin h/\pi\le h$. With $d=1/2048$ both margins hold on Haar probability at least $5/24$. On image probability greater than $1/6$, (10) thus has opposite signs in directions $i,j$. Some unit direction in their plane has **exactly zero** directional second derivative. Its absolute ratio to the positive readout is zero. This statement concerns a quadratic form on a direction.
- **Signed upper bound on the whole matrix at a fixed bond.** Place the six holonomies within chord $1/8$ of $+i,-i,+j,-j,+k,-k$, respectively. Here $A_0\le6/64$ and $T\succeq(2-12/8)I$, since each outer product differs from its target by at most $2/8$ in operator norm. Equation (10) gives $H_c\preceq-3\lambda I$ throughout an event of probability at least $e^{-12\beta}(1/(1536\pi))^6$, uniformly in $L,m$.
- **Entire Hessian on a typical growing coarse field.** Take the release volume $m=2L$. Prescribe the six target holonomies $(1/2,\sqrt3\,u/2)$ for $u=+i,-i,+j,-j,+k,-k$, in a fixed order. There $A_0 I=T=3I/2$. If each holonomy is within chord $\eta=L^{-1/8}/16$ of its target, the change in $A_0$ and in $T$ has size at most $12\eta$ each. By (9)–(10),
$$
 \frac{\|H_c(V)\|_{\rm op}}{x'}\le \frac{192\eta+L^{-1}}{7}.
$$
The local event has probability at least $p_L=e^{-12\beta}(\eta^3/(3\pi))^6$ at each tested star, by the Haar chord density. Take stars in one fixed direction with tails four sites apart on the grid in each coordinate, leaving the last incomplete interval at the torus seam. There are at least $\lfloor m/4\rfloor^4$ disjoint stars. Given $W,b$ they use disjoint sets of independent output links. Hence with probability at least $1-\exp[-\lfloor m/4\rfloor^4p_L]\to1$ the coarse field has a bond obeying this **absolute operator-norm** bound, which tends to zero. The tested bond here may depend on $V$. The three preceding estimates have a bond fixed in advance.

For clarity about typicality, at a *fixed* bond the absolute operator norm in this same family does not tend to zero relative to $x'$. In fact $\|H_c\|_{\rm op}\le\epsilon x'$ requires $|A_0-A_1|\le (9\epsilon+L^{-1})/8$. Each conditional coarse link density is at most $e^{2\beta}$ times Haar, so the small-interval estimate above bounds this event's probability by $2e^{12\beta}\sqrt{(9\epsilon+L^{-1})/4}$. It tends to zero for $\epsilon\to0$. The three distinct conclusions are a failure of a positive lower matrix bound at a typical fixed bond, a signed upper matrix bound on a fixed-probability local event, and a vanishing absolute operator norm at a bond of a typical growing field.

The construction applies to any sequence of admissible odd sides tending to infinity. For example $L=(2^{14}+1)(2k+1)\ge2^{40}$ admits a reflection-symmetric partition into $2k+1$ consecutive intervals of length $2^{14}+1$ in every coordinate. Taking $J=625$ satisfies the unit-mesh depth bound. The proofs also apply to the parameter choices of the release whenever they satisfy the stated hypotheses. It works even if the unit-mesh threshold is only known to lie in $(0,1)$. The small coefficient (7) and the zero directional derivatives come from the real adjoint harmonic on typical group elements. Comparing to an input coupling or substituting the fallback would miss this effect.

## Fixed order-one coupling and a second step

**A sign test uniform in the coarse volume — proved.** At fixed admissible $L,x$ in (3) set $M=a+a^2$ and $\omega=\pi^2a/2$. The single-bond Hessian has norm at most $M$ by LD-1. The action on one $S^3$ has oscillation at most $\omega$, by integrating its upper Hessian bound $a$ along a geodesic of length at most $\pi$ from a minimum. Refresh only this bond with Haar and leave the coarse exterior at its image marginal. The original density against this refreshed law is between $e^{-\omega}$ and $e^\omega$. Under the refreshed law the mean of $H_{c,X}$ is zero for every fixed unit $X$, by twice differentiating Haar translation invariance with the exterior held fixed. Thus, if $x'>0$, at every coarse volume
$$
 \Pr_{D'}(H_{c,X}<d x')\ge e^{-\omega}\frac{d x'}{M+d x'}\qquad(d>0).
$$
This precludes a lower bound $H\ge d x'$ with probability tending to one in *volume* at fixed $L,x$. This last compact-group observation applies to other smooth densities too. Its constant can shrink with $L$ in the small-witness branch. The missing order-one estimate in the scale limit is the signed size of that branch relative to the local response. Equation (6) bounds the sum of absolute values in the slope series. Its signed sum can be much smaller.

There is also a signed vanishing-fraction statement when the side $L$ and a positive actual readout are held fixed and only $m=2L^q$ grows. Let $\nu_m$ be the refreshed law, $h=H_{c,X}$ and $I=\limsup_m\mathbb E_{\nu_m}|h|$. If $I>0$, along a subsequence $\mathbb E_{\nu_m}h^-\ge I/4$ by the zero mean. The event $h\le-I/8$ then has image probability at least $e^{-\omega}I/(8M)$ there. If $I=0$, put $\epsilon_m=m^{-1}+\sqrt{\mathbb E_{\nu_m}|h|/x'}$. Markov's inequality gives $|h|\le\epsilon_m x'$ with image probability tending to one. The first alternative supplies a strictly negative signed upper bound and the second an absolute vanishing ratio. The fraction in the first alternative may depend on $L$. Equations (7)–(10) are what give a comparison uniform as $L$ also grows in the counterexample.

**Second-step input — proved implication.** For a second kernel coupling $y=x'>0$ in these ranges, the incoming one-link conditional density is between $e^{-\omega}$ and $e^\omega$ times Haar at every exterior. A protected test plaquette therefore has conditional hot probability at least $e^{-\omega}/2$ for any $s<1$. The lower bound survives exposing earlier plaquettes, since each chosen edge appears in just its own test. Iterating conditional expectations bounds $\mathbb E e^{-T}$ by $(1-(1-e^{-1})e^{-\omega}/2)^N$. The local-base comparison in the first lemma then costs $e^{20000 y}$ as before. For a surviving longitudinal word, integration of even *one* singly traversed link under the incoming law gives norm at most $e^\omega-1$, by subtracting its zero Haar integral. On the protected event the posterior perturbation for this link is at most $e^{64y/L^j}-1$, with $j$ the number of nonzero transverse coordinates. For $y\le\sqrt L/3$, summing over the three values of $j$ as in (3) bounds the second-step posterior squared mean by
$$
 L^{-3}+(e^\omega-1)+128y(L^{-3}+9L^{-4}+9L^{-5})
 +4802\exp(20000y+J-e^{-\omega}N/4).
$$
A second-step comparison to its own coupling needs the signed mixed response and its limiting sum for this new input. The six-staple product law supplied the length-$L$ gain only at the Wilson step. In the counterexample the leading input interaction for the next calculation is (7).
