## 1. Theorem 1: Action Sum Rule, Continuous-Time Equivalence, and Discrete-Time Strict Incomparability of `(MAG-def)` and `(VIR-down)`

Define the thermal **magnetic action deficit** condition at rate $c_M > 0$ on a cold point $(t, \beta)$:
$$\mathcal{M}(t; \beta) \;\ge\; c_M\, W_t(\beta) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad t a_0^\sigma(\beta) - A_t^\sigma(\beta) \;\ge\; c_M\, t \bar{E}_t(\beta) \quad (\text{at integer } t). \tag{MAG-def}$$
Recall the exact lattice action sum rule / anisotropic split (`ON FILE`, row 1618 Theorem 4(1), row 1638 Theorem 1(1)):
$$\frac{1}{t}\partial_\beta G(t; \beta) = \mathcal{E}(t; \beta) + \mathcal{M}(t; \beta) = \frac{1}{\beta}\mathcal{A}(t; \beta) + 2\mathcal{M}(t; \beta). \tag{1.1}$$
This theorem settles the exact logical relationship between `(MAG-def)`, the action sum rule (1.1), and `(VIR-down)` ($\frac{1}{t}\partial_\beta G(t;\beta) \ge \kappa_0 W_t(\beta)$): in continuous-time (Kogut–Susskind) gauge theory `(MAG-def)` is algebraically equivalent to `(VIR-down)` up to $1/\beta$, whereas in discrete-time positive transfer operators $T(\beta_\tau, \beta_\sigma) = M_{\beta_\sigma} C_{\beta_\tau} M_{\beta_\sigma}$ (even with zero hidden states, $J = I$, at arbitrarily cold points, and with exact heat-kernel or $\mathbb{Z}_2$-Wilson kinetic kernels), `(MAG-def)` and `(VIR-down)` are **strictly incomparable**.

### 1.2 Proof of Theorem 1

#### Proof of Part 1
Since $V \ge 0$ is bounded and $K \ge 0$ has compact resolvent, $H(\beta_\tau, \beta_\sigma) = \frac{1}{\beta_\tau} K + \beta_\sigma V$ is a real-analytic self-adjoint family of Kato type (A) on $(0, \infty)^2$ with common domain $\operatorname{Dom}(K)$ and compact resolvent. Every eigenfunction $\psi \in \operatorname{ran} F_m$ lies in $\operatorname{Dom}(K)$. At $\beta_\tau = \beta_\sigma = \beta$, $\partial_{\beta_\tau} H = -\frac{1}{\beta^2} K$ and $\partial_{\beta_\sigma} H = V$, so first-order perturbation on the simple ground state $\Omega$ gives $\partial_{\beta_\tau} E_0 = -\frac{1}{\beta^2}\langle K \rangle_\Omega$ and $\partial_{\beta_\sigma} E_0 = \langle V \rangle_\Omega$, while compression to $F_m$ gives
$$b_m^{(E)} = -F_m\bigl(\partial_{\beta_\tau}(H - E_0 I)\bigr)F_m = \frac{1}{\beta^2} F_m(K - \langle K \rangle_\Omega)F_m, \qquad b_m^{(M)} = -F_m\bigl(\partial_{\beta_\sigma}(H - E_0 I)\bigr)F_m = F_m(\langle V \rangle_\Omega - V)F_m.$$
Multiplying $b_m^{(E)} - b_m^{(M)}$ by $\beta$ yields
$$a_m^{\mathrm{aniso}} = \beta\bigl(b_m^{(E)} - b_m^{(M)}\bigr) = F_m\!\left(\frac{1}{\beta} K + \beta V - \left(\frac{1}{\beta}\langle K \rangle_\Omega + \beta\langle V \rangle_\Omega\right) I\right)\!F_m = F_m(H - E_0 I)F_m = m F_m,$$
since $H F_m = (E_0 + m) F_m$. Adding and subtracting gives $b_m^{(E)} = \frac{m}{\beta} F_m + b_m^{(M)}$ and $b_m = b_m^{(E)} + b_m^{(M)} = \frac{m}{\beta} F_m + 2 b_m^{(M)}$, proving (1.2). For any unit $\psi \in \operatorname{ran} F_m$, $0 \le \langle V \rangle_\psi \le \|V\|$ and $0 \le \frac{1}{\beta}\langle K \rangle_\psi \le E_0 + m$, so $|b_\psi^{(M)}| \le \|V\|$ and $|b_\psi^{(E)}| \le (E_0 + m)/\beta$. For $\beta' \in [\beta/2, 2\beta]$, $H(\beta') \ge \frac{1}{2\beta} K$, so min–max gives $E_i(\beta') \ge \frac{1}{2\beta}\mu_i(K)$ and $\operatorname{Tr}((H(\beta') + I) e^{-t H(\beta')}) \le C_{t,\beta} \operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\frac{t}{4\beta} K}) < \infty$ uniformly in $\beta'$. Hence $G^{\mathrm{KS}}$, $W_t^{\mathrm{KS}}$, $\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{KS}}$, and $\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{KS}}$ converge absolutely for every $t > 0$. Moreover, applying the elementary convexity inequality $e^{-t y} - e^{-t x} \ge -t(y - x)e^{-t x}$ pairwise to the eigenbases $\{\psi_i, E_i\}$ of $H(\beta)$ and $\{\phi_j, \widetilde{E}_j\}$ of $H(\beta')$ (all in $\operatorname{Dom}(K)$) yields the two-sided Klein bound $-t\sum_i \langle \psi_i, (H(\beta') - H(\beta))\psi_i\rangle e^{-t E_i} \le \operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t H(\beta')} - e^{-t H(\beta)}) \le -t\sum_j \langle \phi_j, (H(\beta') - H(\beta))\phi_j\rangle e^{-t \widetilde{E}_j}$. Dividing by $\beta' - \beta \to 0$ (using strong resolvent convergence on bounded spectral windows and the uniform tail $\sum_{E_j > M} (E_j + 1) e^{-t E_j} \to 0$) justifies $\partial_\beta \operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t H(\beta)}) = -t\sum_{i \ge 0}\langle \psi_i, (\partial_\beta H(\beta))\psi_i\rangle e^{-t E_i(\beta)}$; differentiating $1 + G^{\mathrm{KS}}(t; \beta) = e^{t E_0(\beta)}\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t H(\beta)})$ via the product rule (adding $t\partial_\beta E_0(\beta)\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-t H(\beta)})$ and multiplying by $e^{t E_0(\beta)}$) together with (1.2) yields (1.3). $\square$

#### Proof of Part 2
Fix $\beta_0 > 0$ and $c_M > 0$. Define $f(x) := \frac{1 + e^{-x}}{2}$ and $h(x) := \frac{x e^{-x}}{1 + e^{-x}} = \frac{x}{e^x + 1}$ for $x > 0$. - $0 < \log(1 + e^{-6}) < e^{-6} < 0.0025$;
- hence $\gamma_* := \log\frac{1 + e^{-2}}{1 + e^{-6}} \in (0.1235, 0.1354)$;

Choose $\delta := \min\!\left(\frac{1}{20},\; \frac{1}{30(1 + c_M \beta_0)}\right) \in (0, 0.05]$, and put

$$V := \operatorname{diag}(v_A,\; v_{\mathrm{wall}},\; 0,\; v_{\mathrm{wall}}), \qquad B_0 := \begin{pmatrix} w_A & -w_A & 0 & 0 \\ -w_A & w_A & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & w_B & -w_B \\ 0 & 0 & -w_B & w_B \end{pmatrix}, \qquad B_\varepsilon := B_0 + \varepsilon(4 I_4 - \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^T),$$

For every $\varepsilon > 0$, $B_\varepsilon$ is the graph Laplacian of the complete graph $K_4$ with strictly positive edge weights, so $e^{-\frac{1}{2\beta_\tau} B_\varepsilon}$ has strictly positive entries (as the transition semigroup of an irreducible continuous-time Markov chain on $K_4$), and $T^{(v_{\mathrm{wall}}, \varepsilon)}$ is symmetric, positive definite, with strictly positive entries.

Its eigenvalues are explicit radicals:
$$\lambda_\pm(v, w) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1 + q}{2}(a + \eta) \pm \sqrt{\frac{(1 + q)^2}{4}(a - \eta)^2 + (1 - q)^2 a \eta}\right), \qquad \lambda_+ \lambda_- = q a \eta.$$

Differentiating the explicit radical formula for $\lambda_+$ in $\beta_\tau$ and $\beta_\sigma$ gives, as $v_{\mathrm{wall}} \to \infty$:
$$\lambda_+(v, w; \beta_0, \beta_0) \longrightarrow a f(w/\beta_0) = e^{-\beta_0 v} f(w/\beta_0),$$
$$\left.\partial_{\beta_\sigma}\log \lambda_+(v, w)\right|_{\beta_0, \beta_0} \longrightarrow -v, \qquad \left.\partial_{\beta_\tau}\log \lambda_+(v, w)\right|_{\beta_0, \beta_0} \longrightarrow \frac{1}{\beta_0} h(w/\beta_0),$$
while $\lambda_-(v, w; \beta_0, \beta_0) = q a \eta / \lambda_+ \le 4 e^{-\beta_0 v_{\mathrm{wall}}}$. Applying this to block $A$ ($v = v_A, w = 2\beta_0$) and block $B$ ($v = 0, w = 6\beta_0$):

#### Proof of Part 2 (Exact virial theorem for $\mathcal{L}_d$)
Let $\phi \in \operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_d)$ be any real unit eigenfunction with $\mathcal{L}_d \phi = \varepsilon \phi$ (since $\mathcal{L}_d$ is real, real and imaginary parts are eigenfunctions separately). Since $V_{\mathrm{quart}}$ is a smooth polynomial, elliptic regularity gives $\phi \in C^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{3d})$, solving $-\frac{1}{2}\Delta \phi + V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi = \varepsilon \phi$ pointwise with $\phi \in \operatorname{Dom}(q)$, i.e. $\int (|\nabla\phi|^2 + V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi^2 + \phi^2)\,dx < \infty$. Fix a smooth cutoff $\chi \in C_c^\infty([0, \infty))$ with $0 \le \chi \le 1$, $\chi = 1$ on $[0, 1]$, and $\chi = 0$ on $[2, \infty)$. For $R \ge 1$, put $X(x) := \chi(|x|/R)\, x \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{3d}; \mathbb{R}^{3d})$ and $A_R \phi := X \cdot \nabla\phi + \frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{div} X)\phi$. Integrating by parts on the compact support $\{|x| \le 2R\}$:
- $\int (A_R \phi)\,\phi = \frac{1}{2}\int \operatorname{div}(X \phi^2) = 0$, so $\int (A_R \phi)(\varepsilon \phi) = 0$.
- For the potential term, since $V_{\mathrm{quart}}$ is homogeneous of degree $4$ ($x \cdot \nabla V_{\mathrm{quart}} = 4 V_{\mathrm{quart}}$):
  $$\int (A_R \phi)(V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi) = \frac{1}{2}\int \bigl(\operatorname{div}(X \phi^2)\bigr) V_{\mathrm{quart}} = -\frac{1}{2}\int \phi^2 (X \cdot \nabla V_{\mathrm{quart}}) = -2\int \chi(|x|/R) V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi^2.$$
- For the kinetic term, integrating $-\Delta \phi$ by parts and then integrating $\frac{1}{2} X \cdot \nabla(|\nabla\phi|^2) = -\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{div} X)|\nabla\phi|^2$ (which cancels $+\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{div} X)|\nabla\phi|^2$) and $\frac{1}{4}\nabla(\phi^2) \cdot \nabla(\operatorname{div} X) = -\frac{1}{4}\phi^2 \Delta(\operatorname{div} X)$ gives
  $$\int (A_R \phi)\bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\phi\bigr) = \frac{1}{2}\int \sum_{a,b=1}^{3d} (\partial_a X_b)(\partial_a \phi)(\partial_b \phi) - \frac{1}{8}\int \phi^2 \Delta(\operatorname{div} X).$$
Adding the two integrals yields $0$. Note that $\partial_a X_b(x) = \chi(|x|/R)\delta_{ab} + \frac{x_a x_b}{R|x|}\chi'(|x|/R)$, where the second term vanishes on $\{|x| \le R\} \cup \{|x| \ge 2R\}$ and is bounded in operator norm by $2\|\chi'\|_\infty \mathbf{1}_{\{R \le |x| \le 2R\}}$, and $|\Delta(\operatorname{div} X)| \le C_\chi R^{-2} \mathbf{1}_{\{R \le |x| \le 2R\}}$. Sending $R \to \infty$, dominated convergence (using $\int (|\nabla\phi|^2 + V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi^2 + \phi^2) < \infty$) yields $\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|^2 - 2\langle \phi, V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi\rangle = 0$. Together with $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|^2 + \langle \phi, V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi\rangle = 3\langle \phi, V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi\rangle$, this proves (2.5). $\square$

#### Proof of Part 3 (Griffiths' lemma + reverse Fatou)
First recall the elementary **Griffiths convexity lemma**: if differentiable convex functions $h_n : I \to \mathbb{R}$ on an open interval $I$ converge pointwise to $h : I \to \mathbb{R}$ and $h$ is differentiable at $s_0 \in I$, then for every sequence $s_n \to s_0$, $\lim_{n\to\infty} h_n'(s_n) = h'(s_0)$. Indeed, for any $\delta > 0$ with $[s_0 - \delta, s_0 + \delta] \subset I$ and all large $n$ with $|s_n - s_0| \le \delta/2$, convexity gives
$$\frac{h_n(s_0 - \delta/2) - h_n(s_0 - \delta)}{\delta/2} \le h_n'(s_0 - \delta/2) \le h_n'(s_n) \le h_n'(s_0 + \delta/2) \le \frac{h_n(s_0 + \delta) - h_n(s_0 + \delta/2)}{\delta/2}.$$
Taking $n \to \infty$ and then $\delta \downarrow 0$ yields $h'(s_0) \le \liminf h_n'(s_n) \le \limsup h_n'(s_n) \le h'(s_0)$.

Fix $\tau > 0$ and put $t_B := \lceil \tau B^{1/3} \rceil$. For $\sigma > 0$, define
$$f_B(\sigma) := \log \operatorname{Tr}_{H_0^{(1)}}\!\left(T_{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B)^{t_B}\right), \qquad g_B(\sigma) := t_B \log \lambda_0^{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B).$$
Expanding the trace in $H_0^{(1)}$ using $P_0 = 2^{-d}\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^d} C_z$ and $P_{\mathrm{conj}}$ as a multiple Haar integral over $(q^{(0)}, \dots, q^{(t_B-1)}) \in (SU(2)^d)^{t_B}$ and temporal gauge links $(g^{(0)}, \dots, g^{(t_B-1)}) \in SU(2)^{t_B}$ gives
$$e^{f_B(\sigma)} = \frac{1}{2^d} \sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^d} \int \left[\prod_{\tau'=0}^{t_B-1}\prod_{i=1}^d c_B^{\mathrm{prob}}\!\left(g^{(\tau')} q_i^{(\tau'+1)}(g^{(\tau')})^{-1} (q_i^{(\tau')})^{-1}(-1)^{\delta_{\tau', t_B-1} z_i}\right)\right] e^{-\sigma B \sum_{\tau'=0}^{t_B-1} V(q^{(\tau')})}\,d\mu.$$
Because $c_B^{\mathrm{prob}} > 0$ everywhere and is independent of $\sigma$, the integrand is strictly positive and depends on $\sigma$ purely through $e^{-\sigma B S_\sigma}$. By Hölder's inequality, $f_B$ is smooth and convex on $(0, \infty)$. Likewise, $\log \lambda_0^{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B) = \lim_{m\to\infty} \frac{1}{m}\log\operatorname{Tr}_{H_0^{(1)}}(T_{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B)^m)$ is a pointwise limit of convex functions, so $g_B$ (and $B^{1/3}\log\lambda_0^{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B)$) is smooth and convex on $(0, \infty)$.

By (2.4), on $(0, \infty)$ we have the pointwise limits
$$B^{1/3}\log\lambda_0^{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B) \longrightarrow -\sigma^{1/3}\varepsilon_{0,d}, \qquad f_B(\sigma) \longrightarrow f(\sigma) := \log\!\left(\sum_{k \ge 0} e^{-\tau \sigma^{1/3}\varepsilon_{k,d}}\right), \qquad g_B(\sigma) \longrightarrow g(\sigma) := -\tau \sigma^{1/3}\varepsilon_{0,d}.$$
By the polynomial counting bound $N(A) \le C(1+A)^{9d/2}$ inherited from (8), $f$ and $g$ are smooth on $(0, \infty)$ with termwise differentiation valid. Applying Griffiths' lemma at $\sigma = 1$:
- From $B^{1/3}\log\lambda_0^{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B) \to -\sigma^{1/3}\varepsilon_{0,d}$: since $\partial_\sigma \log\lambda_0^{\mathrm{el}}(B, \sigma B)|_{\sigma=1} = -B\langle \Omega_B, V \Omega_B\rangle$, we obtain
  $$\lim_{B \to \infty} B^{4/3} \langle \Omega_B, V \Omega_B \rangle = \frac{1}{3}\,\varepsilon_{0, d}. \tag{2.8}$$
- From $f_B \to f$ and $g_B \to g$: since $f_B'(1) - g_B'(1) = \partial_\sigma \log(1 + G_B(t_B; B, \sigma B))|_{\sigma=1} = B(t_B a_0^\sigma(B) - A_{t_B}^\sigma(B))$,
  $$\lim_{B \to \infty} B\bigl(t_B a_0^\sigma(B) - A_{t_B}^\sigma(B)\bigr) = f'(1) - g'(1) = -\frac{\tau}{3}\,\frac{\sum_{k \ge 1}(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d}) e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d})}}{1 + \sum_{k \ge 1} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d})}}. \tag{2.9}$$
- For the thermal energy denominator, for $B > (4/\tau)^3$ define $\phi_B(s) := \log\bigl(1 + G_B(s B^{1/3}; B, B)\bigr) = \log\bigl(\sum_{k \ge 0} r_k(B)^{s B^{1/3}}\bigr)$ on the open interval $I_\tau := (\tau/2, 2\tau)$ (where $s B^{1/3} > 2$, so $\operatorname{Tr}(T_{\mathrm{el}}) < \infty$ ensures absolute convergence and termwise differentiation). By Hölder's inequality in $s$, $\phi_B$ is smooth and convex on $I_\tau$ and converges pointwise by the exact counting/trace tail of Part 1 to $\phi(s) := \log\bigl(\sum_{k \ge 0} e^{-s(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d})}\bigr)$. Applying Griffiths' lemma along $s_B := t_B B^{-1/3} \to \tau \in I_\tau$ yields
  $$\lim_{B \to \infty} t_B \bar{E}_{t_B}(B) = \lim_{B \to \infty} \bigl(-s_B \phi_B'(s_B)\bigr) = -\tau \phi'(\tau) = \tau\,\frac{\sum_{k \ge 1}(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d}) e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d})}}{1 + \sum_{k \ge 1} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d} - \varepsilon_{0,d})}} > 0. \tag{2.10}$$
Dividing (2.9) by (2.10) proves (2.6).

Finally, to deduce the levelwise limit (2.7) uniformly over all unit eigenvectors $\psi_{k, B}$ at level $k \ge 1$: fix any sequence of orthonormal eigenbases $\{\psi_{k, B}\}_{k \ge 1}$ of $T_{\mathrm{el}}(B, B)$ on $\Omega_B^\perp$, and set
$$a_{B, k} := B^{4/3} B_M(\psi_{k, B})\, r_k(B)^{t_B}, \qquad a_k := -\frac{1}{3}(\varepsilon_{k, d} - \varepsilon_{0, d})\, e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k, d} - \varepsilon_{0, d})}.$$
1. *Individual upper bound (Fatou + virial):* For any fixed $k \ge 1$ and any subsequence $B_j \to \infty$, by Part 1's strong $L^2$-precompactness there is a further subsequence along which $U_{h_j}\psi_{k, B_j} \to \phi_k$ strongly in $L^2(\mathbb{R}^{3d})^{SO(3)}$ (and hence a.e. after a further subsequence), where $\|\phi_k\| = 1$. Testing against any $\eta \in C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{3d})^{SO(3)}$ via Part 1's generator limit gives $\langle \mathcal{L}_d \eta, \phi_k \rangle = \varepsilon_{k, d}\langle \eta, \phi_k \rangle$; since $C_c^\infty(\mathbb{R}^{3d})^{SO(3)}$ is an operator core of $\mathcal{L}_d$, $\phi_k \in \operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_d)$ with $\mathcal{L}_d \phi_k = \varepsilon_{k, d}\phi_k$. Since $U_{h_j}$ is isometric (absorbing the Haar density square root) and $B_j^{4/3} V(\exp(h_j x))\mathbf{1}_{\{\max_i |h_j x_i| < \pi/2\}} \ge 0$ converges pointwise to $V_{\mathrm{quart}}(x)$ on $\mathbb{R}^{3d}$, Fatou's lemma and (2.5) give $\liminf_{j\to\infty} B_j^{4/3} \langle \psi_{k, B_j}, V \psi_{k, B_j} \rangle \ge \langle \phi_k, V_{\mathrm{quart}}\phi_k \rangle = \frac{1}{3}\varepsilon_{k, d}$. Combining with (2.8) and $r_k(B)^{t_B} \to e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k, d} - \varepsilon_{0, d})}$ yields
   $$\limsup_{B \to \infty} a_{B, k} \le a_k \qquad (\forall k \ge 1). \tag{2.11}$$
2. *Uniform tail upper bound:* Since $V \ge 0$ and $\varepsilon_{0, d} > 0$, (2.8) gives $a_{B, k} \le B^{4/3}\langle \Omega_B, V \Omega_B \rangle r_k(B)^{t_B} \le \varepsilon_{0, d}\, r_k(B)^{t_B}$ for all $k \ge 1$ and all sufficiently large $B$. Since $r_k(B)^{t_B} \to e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d}-\varepsilon_{0,d})}$ termwise and $\sum_{k \ge 1} r_k(B)^{t_B} \to \sum_{k \ge 1} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d}-\varepsilon_{0,d})} < \infty$ by (2.4), for every $K_0 \ge 1$ we have $\lim_{B\to\infty} \sum_{k > K_0} r_k(B)^{t_B} = \sum_{k > K_0} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d}-\varepsilon_{0,d})}$, hence $\limsup_{B\to\infty} \sum_{k > K_0} a_{B, k} \le \varepsilon_{0, d} \sum_{k > K_0} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d}-\varepsilon_{0,d})}$.
3. *Sum limit:* By (2.4) and (2.9),
   $$\sum_{k \ge 1} a_{B, k} = B^{4/3}\mathcal{M}_B(t_B; B) = \frac{B^{1/3}}{t_B}\bigl(1 + G_B(t_B; B)\bigr)\, B\bigl(t_B a_0^\sigma(B) - A_{t_B}^\sigma(B)\bigr) \longrightarrow \sum_{k \ge 1} a_k.$$
Therefore, for every fixed $k_0 \ge 1$ and every $K_0 \ge k_0$,
$$\liminf_{B \to \infty} a_{B, k_0} = \lim_{B \to \infty}\sum_{k \ge 1} a_{B, k} - \limsup_{B \to \infty}\sum_{k \neq k_0} a_{B, k} \ge a_{k_0} + \sum_{k > K_0} a_k - \varepsilon_{0, d}\sum_{k > K_0} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,d}-\varepsilon_{0,d})}.$$
Sending $K_0 \to \infty$ gives $\liminf_{B \to \infty} a_{B, k_0} \ge a_{k_0}$, which with (2.11) forces $\lim_{B \to \infty} a_{B, k_0} = a_{k_0}$. Dividing by $r_{k_0}(B)^{t_B} \to e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k_0, d} - \varepsilon_{0, d})} > 0$ and using $B^{1/3} m_{k_0}(B) \to \varepsilon_{k_0, d} - \varepsilon_{0, d} > 0$ (from (2.3)) proves (2.7). Because the argument holds for every choice of orthonormal eigenbases $\{\psi_{k, B}\}$ (and each eigenvalue $\varepsilon_{k_0, d}$ has finite multiplicity by compact resolvent), convergence is uniform over all unit eigenvectors in every excited eigenspace. $\square$

#### Proof of Part 4
Immediate from Parts 1 and 3: for $\tau$ large enough that $\sum_{k \ge 1} e^{-\tau(\varepsilon_{k,3} - \varepsilon_{0,3})} < 4/21$, $T_{\mathrm{el}}(B, B)$ is cold at $t_B = \lceil \tau B^{1/3}\rceil$ for all large $B$ (with purity $\to (1 + G(2\tau))/(1 + G(\tau))^2$, arbitrarily close to $1$ as $\tau \to \infty$), while (2.6)–(2.7) force $\mathcal{M}_B(t_B) < 0$ and $B_M(\psi_{k, B}) < 0$. $\square$

## 3. Theorem 3: Exact All-$\beta$, Non-Tail Structural Identities on Actual Wilson Tori

Every identity in this section holds on actual finite Wilson tori $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{D-1} \times (\mathbb{Z}/t)$ at **every** coupling $\beta > 0$ (neither a tail truncation nor a strong-coupling bound). By Lemma ALG (row 1553), exact universal identities hold in 4D $SU(2)$, 5D $SU(2)$, and 4D $U(1)$ and are therefore structural representations, not selective inputs by themselves.

### 3.1 Statement of Theorem 3 (`PROVED`)

Fix spatial side $n \ge 3$, group $G \in \{SU(2), U(1)\}$, spacetime dimension $D \ge 3$ ($D - 1$ spatial directions, $|E| = (D-1)n^{D-1}$, $|\mathcal{P}| = \binom{D-1}{2} n^{D-1}$), and $\beta > 0$. Realize $G$ as the unit sphere $S^{c_G} \subset \mathbb{R}^{c_G + 1}$, with $c_{SU(2)} = 3$ and $c_{U(1)} = 1$, and equip $G$ with its round unit-sphere Laplace–Beltrami operator $\Delta_e$ and gradient $\nabla_e$ ($\Delta_{S^{c_G}}(u \cdot v) = -c_G (u \cdot v)$). Put $\kappa_{SU(2)}(k) := k(k+2)$ ($k = 2j \in \mathbb{N}_0$) and $\kappa_{U(1)}(k) := k^2$ ($k \in \mathbb{Z}$).

1. **(Exact 4D $S_4$ anisotropy–Casimir reduction at $t = n$, and 5D $S_5$ contrast)** Let $G_{\mathrm{all}}(t; \beta_\tau, \beta_\sigma) := \operatorname{Tr}_{H_{\mathrm{inv}}}(T(\beta_\tau, \beta_\sigma)^t)/\lambda_0(\beta_\tau, \beta_\sigma)^t - 1$ be the untwisted gauge-invariant thermal sum ($z = 0$), with temporal and spatial mean actions $A_t^{\tau, 0}$ and $A_t^{\sigma, 0}$, and let $a_0^\tau(\beta; n) := -\partial_{\beta_\tau}\log\lambda_0|_{(\beta,\beta)}$, $a_0^\sigma(\beta; n) := -\partial_{\beta_\sigma}\log\lambda_0|_{(\beta,\beta)}$.
   - In **4D ($D = 4$, $3$ temporal planes $= 3$ spatial planes)** at symmetric period $t = n$: $A_n^{\tau, 0}(\beta) = A_n^{\sigma, 0}(\beta)$ identically, so the entire untwisted thermal anisotropy equals the **vacuum Casimir anisotropy**:
     $$\frac{1}{\beta}\left.\partial_\xi \log\bigl(1 + G_{\mathrm{all}}(n; \xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)\bigr)\right|_{\xi=1} = n\bigl(a_0^\tau(\beta; n) - a_0^\sigma(\beta; n)\bigr) = -\frac{n}{\beta}\left.\partial_\xi \log \lambda_0(\xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta; n)\right|_{\xi=1}, \tag{3.1}$$
     $$n a_0(\beta) - A_n^0(\beta) = 2\bigl(n a_0^\sigma(\beta) - A_n^{\sigma, 0}(\beta)\bigr) + n\bigl(a_0^\tau(\beta) - a_0^\sigma(\beta)\bigr). \tag{3.2}$$
     (The same exact cancellation $A_n^{\tau, \mathrm{sym}} = A_n^{\sigma, \mathrm{sym}}$ holds for the $S_4$-invariant $64$-twist average $Z_{\mathrm{sym}}(n) := \frac{1}{64}\sum_{(z, m) \in \mathbb{Z}_2^6} Z^{(z, m)}(n)$.) On the centre-neutral $4$-torus $Z_+(n) = \frac{1}{8}\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^3} Z^{(z, 0)}(n)$, the untwisted sector $z = 0$ cancels identically in $A_n^{\sigma, +} - A_n^{\tau, +}$:
     $$\frac{1}{\beta}\left.\partial_\xi \log\bigl(1 + G(n; \xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)\bigr)\right|_{\xi=1} = n\bigl(a_0^\tau(\beta; n) - a_0^\sigma(\beta; n)\bigr) + \frac{1}{8 Z_+(n)}\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^3 \setminus \{0\}} Z^{(z, 0)}(n)\bigl(A_n^{\sigma, (z,0)} - A_n^{\tau, (z,0)}\bigr). \tag{3.3}$$
   - In **5D ($D = 5$, $4$ temporal planes vs $6$ spatial planes)** at $t = n$: $A_n^{\sigma, 0}(\beta) = \frac{3}{2} A_n^{\tau, 0}(\beta)$, so the $S_5$-invariant curve is $(\beta_\tau, \beta_\sigma) = (\xi^{3/2}\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)$:
     $$\frac{1}{\beta}\left.\partial_\xi \log\bigl(1 + G_{\mathrm{all}}^{(5)}(n; \xi^{3/2}\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)\bigr)\right|_{\xi=1} = n\left(\frac{3}{2} a_0^{\tau, (5)}(\beta; n) - a_0^{\sigma, (5)}(\beta; n)\right), \tag{3.4}$$
     whereas along $(\xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)$ an extensive bulk term $\frac{1}{2} A_n^{\tau, 0} = \Theta(n^5)$ does not cancel.

2. **(Exact purely magnetic Schwinger–Dyson identity)** For every integer $t \ge 2$, let $Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta)$ (resp. $Q_t^{\mathrm{spat}}(\beta)$) be the neutral-ensemble expectation of $\sum_{\ell_0 \text{ temp}} |\nabla_{\ell_0} S^z|^2$ (resp. $\sum_{\ell_i \text{ spat}} |\nabla_{\ell_i} S^z|^2$) on $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{D-1} \times (\mathbb{Z}/t)$. The per-slab vacuum limits $q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}} := \lim_{t\to\infty} Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}/t$ and $q_\infty^{\mathrm{spat}} := \lim_{t\to\infty} Q_t^{\mathrm{spat}}/t$ exist, and for every $t \ge 2$ and $\beta > 0$:
   $$t a_0^\tau(\beta) - A_t^\tau(\beta) = \frac{\beta}{2 c_G}\bigl(Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta) - t q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta)\bigr), \tag{3.5}$$
   $$t a_0^\sigma(\beta) - A_t^\sigma(\beta) = \frac{\beta}{4 c_G}\Bigl(\bigl(Q_t^{\mathrm{spat}}(\beta) - t q_\infty^{\mathrm{spat}}(\beta)\bigr) - \bigl(Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta) - t q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta)\bigr)\Bigr), \tag{3.6}$$
   $$\left.\partial_\xi \log\bigl(1 + G(t; \xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)\bigr)\right|_{\xi=1} = \frac{\beta^2}{4 c_G}\Bigl(3\bigl(Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta) - t q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}}(\beta)\bigr) - \bigl(Q_t^{\mathrm{spat}}(\beta) - t q_\infty^{\mathrm{spat}}(\beta)\bigr)\Bigr). \tag{3.7}$$
   (Adding (3.5) and (3.6) recovers the full force identity $t a_0 - A_t = \frac{\beta}{4 c_G}(Q_t - t q_\infty)$, `ON FILE` in rows 913, 934, 1618 Remark 4.1, and 1619 §3.3.)

3. **(Exact single-eigenstate intrinsic Casimir–gradient and spatial-plaquette identities on $G^{E(B)}$)** Let $\psi = \Omega f \in H_0$ be any real unit eigenvector of $R_\beta$ with eigenvalue $\lambda > 0$ and electric-placement unit vector $w_\psi := \lambda^{-1/2} C_\beta^{1/2} M_\beta \psi$. Then $\psi \in C^\infty(G^{E(B)})$, and:
   - *(Intrinsic Casimir–gradient identity)*
     $$\mathbb{E}_{w_\psi}\!\left[\sum_{e \in E(B)} \kappa_G(k_e)\right] = \|\nabla \psi\|^2 - \frac{\beta^2}{4}\langle \psi, |\nabla V|^2 \psi \rangle, \tag{3.8}$$
     $$\Delta_\psi \mathbb{E}_w\!\left[\sum_{e \in E(B)} \kappa_G(k_e)\right] = \int |\nabla f|^2\,d\pi - \operatorname{Cov}_\pi\!\left(f^2,\; \frac{\Delta \Omega}{\Omega} + \frac{\beta^2}{4}|\nabla V|^2\right). \tag{3.9}$$
     In particular, for the heat-kernel transfer $T_{\mathrm{HK}}(\beta) = P_0 M_\beta e^{\frac{1}{2\beta}\Delta} M_\beta P_0$, the exact electric slope is $b_\psi^{(E), \mathrm{HK}} = \frac{1}{2\beta^2}(\|\nabla\psi\|^2 - \|\nabla\Omega\|^2) - \frac{1}{8}(\langle |\nabla V|^2\rangle_\psi - \langle |\nabla V|^2\rangle_\Omega)$.
   - *(Single-eigenstate spatial-plaquette identity)*
     $$4 c_G\bigl(|\mathcal{P}| - \langle \psi, V \psi \rangle\bigr) = \beta \langle \psi, |\nabla V|^2 \psi \rangle - \frac{2}{\lambda}\bigl\langle M_\beta(\nabla V)\psi,\; \nabla C_\beta(M_\beta \psi)\bigr\rangle, \tag{3.10}$$
     and subtracting (3.10) at $\psi = \Omega$ expresses $4 c_G B_M(\psi)$ as the state-minus-vacuum difference of the right-hand side.

### 3.2 Proof of Theorem 3

#### Proof of Part 1
On the untwisted isotropic $D$-torus $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^D$, the hypercubic symmetry group $S_D$ acts transitively on the $\binom{D}{2}$ plaquette orientations and preserves the Haar measure and isotropic Wilson action. Hence $\mathbb{E}_n^0[1 - W_p]$ takes a common value $p_n(\beta)$ on every plaquette $p$. Since there are $(D-1) n^D$ temporal plaquettes and $\binom{D-1}{2} n^D$ spatial plaquettes, $A_n^{\tau, 0} = (D-1) n^D p_n(\beta)$ and $A_n^{\sigma, 0} = \binom{D-1}{2} n^D p_n(\beta) = \frac{D-2}{2} A_n^{\tau, 0}$.
- For $D = 4$: $A_n^{\tau, 0} = A_n^{\sigma, 0}$. By Lemma D1 (`ON FILE`, check 1613) applied separately to $\beta_\tau$ and $\beta_\sigma$, $\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_\xi \log(1 + G_{\mathrm{all}}(n; \xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta))|_{\xi=1} = (n a_0^\tau - A_n^{\tau, 0}) - (n a_0^\sigma - A_n^{\sigma, 0}) = n(a_0^\tau - a_0^\sigma)$, giving (3.1), and adding $2(n a_0^\sigma - A_n^{\sigma, 0})$ gives (3.2). The same $S_4$ permutation invariance holds for the uniform average over all $2^6 = 64$ twist sectors $(z, m) \in \mathbb{Z}_2^6$. For $Z_+(n) = \frac{1}{8}\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^3} Z^{(z, 0)}(n)$, $A_n^{\sigma, +} - A_n^{\tau, +} = \frac{1}{8 Z_+(n)}\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^3} Z^{(z, 0)}(n)(A_n^{\sigma, (z,0)} - A_n^{\tau, (z,0)})$ drops the $z = 0$ summand identically, giving (3.3).
- For $D = 5$: $A_n^{\sigma, 0} = \frac{3}{2} A_n^{\tau, 0}$, so $\frac{1}{\beta}\partial_\xi \log(1 + G_{\mathrm{all}}^{(5)}(n; \xi^{3/2}\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta))|_{\xi=1} = \frac{3}{2}(n a_0^{\tau, (5)} - A_n^{\tau, 0}) - (n a_0^{\sigma, (5)} - A_n^{\sigma, 0}) = n(\frac{3}{2} a_0^{\tau, (5)} - a_0^{\sigma, (5)})$, proving (3.4). $\square$

#### Proof of Part 2
Because $n \ge 3$ and $t \ge 2$, the four links bounding every plaquette $p$ of $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^{D-1} \times (\mathbb{Z}/t)$ are pairwise distinct. For any link $\ell$ and twist $z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^{D-1}$, holding all other links fixed, each plaquette $p \ni \ell$ has $W_p = u_\ell \cdot v_{p, \ell}$ for a unit vector $v_{p, \ell} \in S^{c_G}$ (the staple of $p$ around $\ell$). Hence $S^z = -u_\ell \cdot \Sigma_\ell^z + \text{const}$ with $\Sigma_\ell^z := \sum_{p \ni \ell} \epsilon_p^z v_{p, \ell}$, and $\Delta_\ell S^z = +c_G \sum_{p \ni \ell} \epsilon_p^z W_p$. Integrating the exact divergence $\Delta_\ell(e^{-\beta S^z}) = (-\beta \Delta_\ell S^z + \beta^2 |\nabla_\ell S^z|^2) e^{-\beta S^z}$ over the closed sphere $S^{c_G}$ gives
$$c_G \sum_{p \ni \ell} \mathbb{E}_t^z[\epsilon_p^z W_p] = \beta\,\mathbb{E}_t^z\bigl[|\nabla_\ell S^z|^2\bigr] \qquad (\forall \ell,\ \forall z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^{D-1}). \tag{3.11}$$
Summing (3.11) over all temporal links $\ell_0$ counts every temporal plaquette twice and no spatial plaquette:
$$2 c_G \bigl(|\mathcal{P}_\tau| - A_t^{\tau, z}\bigr) = \beta \sum_{\ell_0 \text{ temp}} \mathbb{E}_t^z\bigl[|\nabla_{\ell_0} S^z|^2\bigr].$$
Summing (3.11) over all spatial links $\ell_i$ counts every temporal plaquette twice and every spatial plaquette four times ($\epsilon_p^z = +1$ on $\mathcal{P}_\sigma$):
$$2 c_G\bigl(|\mathcal{P}_\tau| - A_t^{\tau, z}\bigr) + 4 c_G\bigl(|\mathcal{P}_\sigma| - A_t^{\sigma, z}\bigr) = \beta \sum_{\ell_i \text{ spat}} \mathbb{E}_t^z\bigl[|\nabla_{\ell_i} S^z|^2\bigr].$$
Averaging both equations over $z$ with weights $Z^z(t)/\sum_{z'} Z^{z'}(t)$ (each $Z^z(t) = \int e^{-\beta S^z}\,d\mu > 0$) gives
$$A_t^\tau = |\mathcal{P}_\tau| - \frac{\beta}{2 c_G} Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}, \qquad A_t^\sigma = |\mathcal{P}_\sigma| - \frac{\beta}{4 c_G}\bigl(Q_t^{\mathrm{spat}} - Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}\bigr).$$
Since $|\mathcal{P}_\tau| = t(D-1)n^{D-1}$, $|\mathcal{P}_\sigma| = t\binom{D-1}{2} n^{D-1}$, and $A_t^\tau/t \to a_0^\tau$, $A_t^\sigma/t \to a_0^\sigma$ as $t \to \infty$ (check 1613, row 1618 Theorem 4), the limits $q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}} = \lim_{t\to\infty} Q_t^{\mathrm{temp}}/t$ and $q_\infty^{\mathrm{spat}} = \lim_{t\to\infty} Q_t^{\mathrm{spat}}/t$ exist and satisfy $a_0^\tau = (D-1)n^{D-1} - \frac{\beta}{2 c_G} q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}}$ and $a_0^\sigma = \binom{D-1}{2} n^{D-1} - \frac{\beta}{4 c_G}(q_\infty^{\mathrm{spat}} - q_\infty^{\mathrm{temp}})$. Subtracting $A_t^\tau$ from $t a_0^\tau$ and $A_t^\sigma$ from $t a_0^\sigma$ gives (3.5) and (3.6); taking $\beta$ times their difference gives (3.7). $\square$

#### Proof of Part 3
Since $c_\beta$ is a $C^\infty$ class function on $G$, the link-convolution operator $C_\beta = \bigotimes_{e \in E(B)} C_{e, \beta}$ commutes with every spatial gauge transformation (hence with $P_{\mathrm{inv}}$) and with $P_0$, as does $M_\beta$.
