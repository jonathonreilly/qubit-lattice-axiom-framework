## Phase 3. Exact division by thinned noise

**Lemma 1 (hand proof).** Write $\mathcal W=(1-\Delta)^{-1}$ for the Haar commutator convolution and $Q_u=(1-u\Delta)^{-1}$ for $u>0$. For a continuous strictly positive joint density $F$ relative to product probability Haar, division on edge $e$ by $K_p=(1-p)I+p\mathcal W$, $0<p<1$, has a positive-measure residual if and only if
$$
                      F\ \ge\ p Q_{1-p,e}F.                                  \tag{1}
$$
The unique residual density is $(F-pQ_{1-p,e}F)/(1-p)$. Indeed $K_p=(1-(1-p)\Delta)/(1-\Delta)$ in that coordinate. The claimed inverse follows on each matrix coefficient, and these determine finite measures. The resolvent is a probability convolution, as seen by integrating the group heat semigroup at time $(1-p)t$ against $e^{-t}dt$. Thus the formula is continuous and preserves total mass. A strict inequality gives a strictly positive density. The endpoint $p=1$ is the earlier differential test. [Row 1132, §3](gap-council-op-astra.md)

**Lemma 2 (simultaneous positive residual, hand proof).** On any finite ordinary four-dimensional Wilson torus with nondegenerate plaquettes and coupling $\beta\ge0$, choose *any* central probability $N$ on the link group. Put $K=(1-p)I+pN$, using convolution on densities. The normalized Wilson law has a strictly positive joint residual after independent $K$'s are removed from **every fine link at once** whenever
$$
                       0<p\le p_0(\beta):=\frac{e^{-12\beta}}{16\cdot19^2}. \tag{2}
$$
This also holds with any prescribed plaquette centre signs, hence for their positively weighted neutral mixture. The constants are independent of all periods. The proof applies unchanged to any product of positive plaquette factors whose individual log oscillations are at most $2\beta$.

For the proof let $F$ be the original density, and $J=K^{-1}-I$. The geometric series for $(I-p(I-N))^{-1}$ is absolutely convergent as a convolution of signed measures. It gives $\|J\|_{\rm TV}\le 2p/(1-2p)\le4p$ under (2). Form a graph on links, joining two when they share a plaquette. Its degree is at most $D=18$. For fixed configuration $x$, and a subset $A$ of links, set $v_A=(\prod_{e\in A}J_e)F(x)/F(x)$, with $v_\varnothing=1$. Changing the links in $A$ changes the log of $F$ by at most $12\beta |A|$. Therefore $|v_A|\le q^{|A|}$ with $q=4p e^{12\beta}\le1/[4(D+1)^2]$. For disconnected $A$, the ratio of the changed to the original weight factors over its connected components, since no plaquette meets two of them. Integration against the signed product gives the same factorization of $v_A$.

For $\Lambda$ a subset of the links, let $Z_\Lambda=\sum_{A\subseteq\Lambda}v_A$. We prove $Z_\Lambda/Z_{\Lambda-e}\ge1-\epsilon>0$ for every $e\in\Lambda$, with $\epsilon=1/[2(D+1)]$. Start with $Z_\varnothing=1$. Sorting by the occupied component $C$ of $e$ gives
$$
 Z_\Lambda=Z_{\Lambda-e}+\sum_{e\in C\subseteq\Lambda\,\mathrm{connected}}
                  v_C Z_{\Lambda\setminus N[C]},
$$
where $N[C]$ is the closed graph neighbourhood. By induction, deleting at most $(D+1)|C|$ vertices from $\Lambda-e$ increases the ratio of the smaller to the larger partition sum by at most $(1-\epsilon)^{-(D+1)|C|}\le 2^{|C|}$, by Bernoulli's inequality. There are at most $D^{2(k-1)}$ connected $k$-sets through $e$. Fix an order of neighbours and record the depth-first traversal of a chosen spanning tree. Its $2(k-1)$ moves recover the vertex set. The absolute relative correction is at most $2q/(1-2D^2q)\le4q\le\epsilon$. This proves the induction. At the full link set $E$, the residual $F Z_E=(\prod_e K_e^{-1})F$ is positive and integrates to one. This expansion is in the freely specified thinning probability. It requires no smallness of the Wilson coupling. This adapts the deletion induction of row 1135, which expands in the action at tiny coupling for full differential deconvolution. Here the connected sets are changed links and the expansion parameter is the thinning at arbitrary coupling. [Row 1135, local sign proof](gap-slab-a-astra.md) · [Tensor endpoint counting in row 1418](gap-rg-step-sizing-astra62.md)

**Straight products.** Let $D_b$ keep the products along disjoint straight coarse edges of length $b$ dividing the periods. If independent central random multipliers are applied to fine links before $D_b$, conjugate each multiplier through the preceding residual transports. Conditional on all residual links, their conjugates are again independent with their original laws. The coarse density therefore has independent factors $K^{*b}$ on *every* retained link, with a positive residual given by $D_b$ of the fine residual. The proof holds at every such $b$ with the same fine residual and is consistent under composition of straight products. In fact write the original density as a single product noise applied to a residual law $R$. Set $R_j=(\bigotimes K)*D_{2^j}R$, so $R_0$ is the original law. Then $D_2R_j=(\bigotimes K)*R_{j+1}$ on each available grid. This supplies one fresh copy per link per doubling with a positive residual at every step. For the Haar word, spin $j$ receives $(1-p+p/(2j+1)^2)^b$. This proves actual divisibility by a nonzero amount where row 1434 rules out every positive independent heat time.

## Six coarse plaquettes bound the weight of a commutator

**Theorem 3 (hand proof).** Fix an integer block length $b\ge1$, put $m=b^2$, and use the straight-product map. Consider any periodic four-dimensional Wilson rectangle with periods divisible by $b$ and at least $3b$. Use either the ordinary law or the positive normalized sum defining the zero-spatial-twist electric-neutral trace. In the latter choose a coarse temporal edge whose time interval misses the common twist seam. Write $F_b$ for the joint coarse law. All bounds below are uniform in the periods and in the choice of such an edge.

Suppose $F_b$ is obtained from a positive residual law by changing this one temporal edge as follows. With a fixed probability $p$ multiply its value by $[A,B]$, independently choosing whether to make the change. Conditional on the residual, $A,B$ are independent with densities proportional to $e^{\lambda_A u\cdot A}$, $e^{\lambda_B v\cdot B}$ in unit-quaternion coordinates. The unit vectors $u,v$ may depend on the residual, and the nonnegative concentrations obey $\lambda_A+\lambda_B\le \ell_\beta$ there. Left or right multiplication, fixed inversions, or conjugation of the word by the residual are allowed. Other independent link multipliers can be absorbed into the residual. For both Haar take $\ell_\beta=0$. Along any $\beta\to\infty$, with $\log0=-\infty$,
$$
 \limsup\frac{\log p}{\beta}
 \ \le\ \min\!\left(0,\ \limsup\frac{\ell_\beta}{\beta}
                -6m[1-\cos(\pi/m)]\right).                                  \tag{3}
$$
Even if the word occurs only on a dominated positive branch of mass $p$ and the other branch has an arbitrary law, a strict negative second entry in (3) implies $p\le C e^{-c\beta}$ with $c>0$. For this more general branch the exponent in (3) itself need not follow.

**The Wilson probability bound.** Set $x(g)=\operatorname{tr}(g)/2$, $a(g)=1-x(g)$, $z(\beta)=\int e^{-\beta a(g)}dg$, with normalized Haar throughout. For any set $D$ of distinct fine *temporal* plaquettes off the seam,
$$
              \mathbb E\exp\!\left(\beta\sum_{f\in D}a(U_f)\right)
                  \ \le\ z(\beta)^{-|D|}.                                    \tag{4}
$$
For ordinary Wilson this is already row 1149 (23). Rows 1155/1156 give the related half-coupling estimate. For the neutral extension use the same marked-layer comparison with the original time direction. In a temporal slice normalize each electric convolution to have constant multiplier one. At a removed plaquette it becomes the Haar projection. This is below the original normalized convolution in positive-operator order, since the latter has constant multiplier one and all other multipliers nonnegative by (GL3). Tensoring the positive factors, compressing to site-gauge invariants and multiplying on both sides by the same spatial half-weight preserve the order. Call the resulting layers $0\le T_s\le T$. They commute with the electric centre flips since the single-bond convolutions are central and the spatial action is invariant. In the neutral case, put the twists on a common temporal seam. Their average inserts the orthogonal projection $P_0$. Restriction to its range preserves the order. For time period $N_t$, Schatten Hölder bounds the absolute trace of the ordered product by $\prod_s(\operatorname{Tr} T_s^{N_t})^{1/N_t}\le\operatorname{Tr} T^{N_t}$ on the chosen space, by eigenvalue monotonicity. The ratio of the discarded single-bond normalizers is $1/z(\beta)$ per deleted plaquette. This proves (4), without commuting different layers. For $\beta\ge1$, the Haar ball of angle $\beta^{-1/2}$ gives $z(\beta)\ge [8e^{-1/2}/(3\pi^3)]\beta^{-3/2}$, using $1-\cos t\le t^2/2$ and $\sin t\ge 2t/\pi$ on that ball. Let $d(g,h)=\arccos x(gh^{-1})$ be round geodesic distance. A coarse face of angle at least $r\in[0,\pi]$ costs at least
$$
                 A_m(r)=m[1-\cos(r/m)]
$$
in the sum of $a$ on its $m$ fine faces. Peel unit squares off the rectangle in rows. At each attachment the new boundary word is the old boundary word times a conjugate of the unit face word, up to a choice of orientation and base point, by cancellation of the common boundary path. Thus the sum of fine angles dominates the coarse angle by the bi-invariant triangle inequality. Reduce the fine angles, if necessary, to have sum $r$. Averaging any pair preserves their sum and decreases the sum of their costs, since $\cos u+\cos v=2\cos((u+v)/2)\cos((u-v)/2)\le2\cos((u+v)/2)$ for $u+v\le\pi$. Orient the tested temporal edge as $U$, and let $S_i$, $1\le i\le6$, be the other three coarse links composed from the same start to the same end around each incident face. All six angles are $d(U,S_i)$. The six tiled surfaces have distinct fine plaquettes.  \Pr(G^c)\le 6z(\beta)^{-m}e^{-\beta A_m(\epsilon/2)},\qquad
 \Pr(E)\le z(\beta)^{-6m}e^{-6\beta A_m(\pi-2\epsilon)}.                    \tag{5}
$$
Indeed, if all six angles are at most $\epsilon/2$, their staples are pairwise within $\epsilon$. These estimates concern events in the integrated Wilson measure.

**Cost of the proposed noise.** Write $c(\lambda)=\int e^{\lambda x(g)}dg\le e^\lambda$. Independent sign changes of $A$ and $B$ preserve their commutator. Averaging the four signs replaces each numerator by a hyperbolic cosine, at least one. On $G$, placing the changed edge within $\epsilon$ of $-S_1$ forces $E$. Crucially, $G$ depends only on the other coarse links.        p\,\Pr(G)\, v_\epsilon e^{-\ell_\beta}/2\le\Pr(E).                  \tag{6}
$$
For fixed $\epsilon$, $\Pr(G)\to1$ uniformly by (5). Take upper logarithmic limits using (5)–(6), then $\epsilon\downarrow0$, proving (3). With an arbitrary complementary branch, the mass of the word branch on $G$ is at least $p-\Pr(G^c)$. Use that quantity instead of $p\Pr(G)$ in (6). If $\limsup\ell_\beta/\beta<6A_m(\pi)$, choose $\epsilon$ small enough for the strict margin at $\pi-2\epsilon$. Both terms in the resulting upper bound on $p$ are then exponentially small by (5). uniformly over the indicated tori, including tori varying with $\beta$. Equation (2) supplies the lower probability and (3) on one temporal edge supplies the upper. ## An obstruction inside the order-one band

**Proposition 4 (hand proof).** On the ordinary or zero-spatial-twist electric-neutral torus, take a nonwrapping *spatial* coarse square of block length two with distinct boundary edges and periods at least six. At every $\beta\ge2$, independent thinned Haar commutators on its four boundary links with any positive joint residual must satisfy
$$
               \prod_{e\in\partial C}(1-3p_e/4)
                      \ \ge\ [I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta)]^4 .
                                                                            \tag{8}
$$
In particular a common $p$ must be at most $16/21$ throughout $[2,513/256]$. This rejects full independent words in the band. A common small thinning is possible there by (2).

For $w=e^{\beta x}$ put $c=\int w$, $r=c'/c=I_2/I_1$. The convolution with $xw-rw$ is positive as an operator. Its eigenvalue at degree $h$ is $c\,\partial_\beta[I_{h+1}/I_1]\ge0$, by (GL3). For monotonicity, row 1149 (16) identifies each neighbouring ratio $I_{j+1}/I_j$ with the mean of $t$ in the exponential tilt of $(1-t^2)^{j-1/2}dt$ on $[-1,1]$. Differentiating gives its variance. The quotient for degree $h$ is their product. Insert this one-bond inequality in the transfer sandwich of row 1434 to get $\mathbb E x(U_{1,1})\ge r$.

Use the checked rectangle inequality of rows 1434/1436 twice, first in one direction of the square and then in the other. It gives $\mathbb E x(U_{2,2})\ge [\mathbb E x(U_{1,1})]^4$. Here is the extra centre check for the **fundamental** spatial loop. When an original spatial direction $i$ becomes transfer time, the only electric stack is $0i$. It can be moved to the new seam by flipping original time links, which leaves the spatial loop untouched. The remaining stacks are spatial potentials in the new transfer and independent of the new time. Averaging the $0i$ seam inserts the projection for a flat flip of links parallel to the original time direction. The open spatial path in the other original spatial direction uses no such links. Multiplication by its transporter therefore intertwines this projection on the scalar and charged spaces. In each static magnetic sector the positive transfer thus gives the same probability weights $\lambda_i^N |J_{ki}|^2/\operatorname{Tr}(T_{\rm sc}^N)$ in the open-line moment formula as in check 1436, with new time period $N$, the scalar eigenvalues on the projected space and the charged matrix elements of the path isometry $J$.
