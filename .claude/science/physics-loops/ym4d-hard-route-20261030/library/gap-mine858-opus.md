### 2.1 Two readings of my own that change the ranking (ARGUED here, not checked)

(a) **The route M sign points the wrong way.** r858's conjecture (L330) is d log R/d beta <= 0 at a fixed lattice: R falls with beta. R(0) = 1, and the seed needs R close to 1 at LARGE beta. A decreasing R therefore gives only R(beta) <= R(beta') for beta' <= beta. That is an upper bound on R, a lower bound on the deficit, which is the free direction (R <= 1 already holds by reflection positivity). r858 also expects the sign to hold for Z_2 and U(1) Villain (Griffiths). So as a carrier it fails the U(1) test too. Its only usable reading is "R rises with the physical size along a scaling trajectory" (L328). That is (MON') / (AF-step) / (MONO-Delta), which rows 859 and 878 and gap-phys-seed.md L351-384 already have. Row 874 makes the matching point for row 871's interpolation: monotonicity is needed only for the converse (HAC L142).

(b) **The free end of E6 is group-blind.** At beta_Sigma = 0 the integrand is d log R/d beta_Sigma = -2 E_cut[H], with H = sum over Sigma of (1/2) tr U_p, in the ensemble with the sheet removed. Take a surface S with boundary dp that avoids Sigma. Then c = S + p is a closed 2-cycle that meets the closed dual surface Sigma* once. So c does not bound, and it has the class of the (1,i) plane. Hence every such S has area at least n_1 n_i - 1, and E_cut[tr U_p] is a plane-wrapping loop: area-law small for confined SU(2). It is ALSO small for U(1) in the Coulomb phase. The flux Phi through p spreads over the rest of the plane at a cost of about beta Phi^2/(2 n_1 n_i), so with the compact flux sum Phi is close to uniform mod 2 pi, and E_cut[cos Phi] is small (Gaussian plus theta-function heuristic). So the free end does not separate SU(2) from U(1). U(1)'s order-one deficit (about beta pi^2/2 on a symmetric box, r858 F3) must build up at intermediate beta_Sigma. The content of E6 is the sheet response over the whole interval: the (C2) kind, which check 874 found to restate (E17) or be stronger.
