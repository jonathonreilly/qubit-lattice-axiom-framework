## The Siciak argument

**HOLDS — line 83, for one period with the other three fixed at admissible real lattice sizes.** For a positive transfer at real coupling, use the half-plane of trace convergence and write $F(z)=Z_+(z)/\lambda_0^z=1+\sum_{a\ge1}x_a^z$. The sum converges normally to the right of any real time of convergence. If $s(u)\le q<1$, then $|F(z)-1|\le s(\Re z)\le q$ for $\Re z\ge u$. The logarithm defined by the series at 1 is holomorphic for $\Re z>u$ with $|\log F|\le-\log(1-q)$. For an unprojected partition function this argument needs the **full** excited trace.

**HOLDS — line 83, the angle in the ideal cross theorem.** Suppose each $D_j$ is a right half-plane and $A_j$ is a nondegenerate real interval inside it. Assume a single compatible, locally bounded function on the four sets $D_j\times\prod_{k\ne j} A_k$ (with coordinates placed in their original order), holomorphic in the active variable for every real choice of the other three. Taking the classical cross theorem as input gives a holomorphic extension to $\sum_j h^*_{A_j,D_j}(z_j)<1$, where $h^*_{A,D}$ is the regularized supremum of subharmonic $v\le1$ in $D$ with $v\le0$ on $A$. For the full positive ray in $\Re z>0$, $\log z=x+iy$ maps the domain to $|y|<\pi/2$. The extremal function is $2|y|/\pi$, harmonic on either side of the real axis.

Here is the endpoint check for a long finite interval. Center it at $\ell$ on the log scale, so $A=[\ell e^{-R},\ell e^R]$. In the upper rectangle $|x|\le R$, $0\le y\le\pi/2$, use $\log(z/\ell)=x+iy$. The excess over $2y/\pi$ is bounded above on its boundary by the positive harmonic function

$$
 \frac{\cosh x\,\cos(y-\pi/4)}{\cosh R\,\cos(\pi/4)} .
