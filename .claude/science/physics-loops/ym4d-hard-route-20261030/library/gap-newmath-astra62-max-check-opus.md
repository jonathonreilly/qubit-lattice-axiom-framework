## Step 3. The double pole of (16): signal of the law, or algebra and dimension?

Comparison models used by the file: 4D compact U(1) (l.441, B = 0) and 5D SU(2) at beta = O(log n) (l.441, Pr(M = 1) -> 0). These are (g1) and (g2) of row 1553.

The pole has three ingredients, and none of them reads the law at a physical scale.
1. **Where it is (y_K -> pi/2).** This comes only from E(1 - |w_p|) = O(delta_K), a one-plaquette ultraviolet statement (Stage 7). Drop the mask (B = w_p): the same argument puts a pole near i pi/2 in weak 4D U(1) and in weak 5D SU(2) (ARGUED; the convexity proof of Stage 7 does not use the group or the dimension).
2. **That it is uncancelled.** The numerator is a Hilbert-Schmidt norm, positive for every real self-adjoint slice insertion at beta > 0 (reflection positivity). So every real zero of the short trace gives a double pole, in every model. The pole is a zero of a one-period quantity; it reads nothing about the ratio of the two periods. Its residue over P_K is O(delta_K) -> 0, and R_K(0) = P_K is not estimated.
3. **Why it disappears in the comparison models.** Only the mask M does this. By the density bound of Stage 4, the law of M is, up to factors tending to one, the product-Haar law of the d spatial windings at m base points: three windings in 4D (F_3(a) >= c'a^2, so M -> 1), four in 5D (F_4(a) ~ kappa a^3 log(1/a), so M -> 0), and M = 0 identically for an abelian group. This is a property of Haar measure on SU(2)^d. It holds at every beta = O(log n), including fixed strong coupling.
