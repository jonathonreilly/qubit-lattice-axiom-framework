\[

**Proof.** For the mask in row 1656 take \(m=\lfloor n/4\rfloor\), \(a=m^{-2/5}\). The remaining private links of that row still contain at least \(n-O(1)\) pivots per circuit. Different measured slices are separated by at least two time steps. Given their exterior, which now includes the local holonomies, the joint winding law is within the multiplicative factors \((1\pm\eta)^{3km}\) of independent Haar, where
\(\eta=\sum_{j\ge2}j^2\exp(-(j-1)(n-O(1))/(1+6\beta))\).
This is the translated one-link convolution estimate of rows 1145 and 1656, at each temporal twist. These factors tend to one. Consequently the conditional probability that any mask vanishes is \(O(e^{-c m^{1/5}})\), at the physical and shorter periods. Write \(F_r(w)=\cosh(rw)/\cosh r\). Consider any sequence of strengths, passing to a subsequence where \(r/\beta\to s\in[0,\infty]\). If \(s=0\), \(0\le1-F_r(w)\le r(1-w)\), so its product has mean tending to one. If \(0<s<\infty\), then
\[
 F_r(w)=\frac{e^{-r(1-w)}+e^{-r(1+w)}}{1+e^{-2r}} .
\]
The second term tends to zero in probability. By Stage 2 and boundedness the product mean tends to \((\mathbb E e^{-sH_*})^k=(1+s/2)^{-3k/2}\), by the three scalar Gaussian integrals.

If \(r/\beta\to\infty\), expand the product into its \(2^k\) choices of centre. Near a given centre \(\epsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}\) use \(h_j=\epsilon_j\exp(t_j/\sqrt r)\) in the term with \(e^{-r(1-\epsilon_j w_j)}\), over the full logarithm ball \(|t_j|<\pi\sqrt r\). The subsequence test proves uniformity. Taking the long expectation divided by the square of the short one proves (1).
