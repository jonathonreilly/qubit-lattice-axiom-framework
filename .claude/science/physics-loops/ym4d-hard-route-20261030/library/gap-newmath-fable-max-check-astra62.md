**PROVED (Stage 3.3, lines 196–205).** Group the six plane bits into three complementary pairs. In a pair, empty and singleton selections have generating polynomial $a=1+2x$, and a double selection has weight $b=x^2$. Pfaffian parity is the parity of the number of double selections. Even parity gives $a^3+3ab^2=1+6x+12x^2+8x^3+3x^4+6x^5$. Odd parity gives $3a^2b+b^3=3x^2+12x^3+12x^4+x^6$. Drop the empty twist from the first polynomial, subtract the second and subtract 7. The result is
$$
D=-7+6x+9x^2-4x^3-9x^4+6x^5-x^6 .
$$
For a separate hand multiplication put $Q=x^4-4x^3+8x+7$. The terms of $(-1+2x-x^2)Q$ in ascending order are
$$
(-7-8x+4x^3-x^4)+(14x+16x^2-8x^4+2x^5)
+(-7x^2-8x^3+4x^5-x^6).

They sum to exactly $D$. On $[0,1]$, $x^4-4x^3$ decreases from 0 to −3 by its derivative $4x^2(x-3)$. Thus $Q\ge4$, and equality in $D\le0$ occurs only at 1.

## 6. Must the mass term cancel exactly? — FAILS as a Wilson necessity claim

**PROVED (the conditional calculation, lines 244–254).** For one specified response and wavelength $R$, suppose the direct term is $C_R+T_R$ and the variance is $V_R=T_R+G_R$, with $T_R\ge0,G_R>0$. Then the true stiffness is $C_R-G_R$. Substitution of the lower estimate $L_R=\theta V_R$, with $0<\theta<1$ fixed, certifies a strict improvement on $C_R$ precisely if
$$
                     (1-\theta)T_R<\theta G_R .
$$
If $T_R/G_R\to\infty$, for each fixed $\theta<1$ there is an $R_\theta$ beyond which this bound certifies no improvement. No precise growth law is needed. The threshold can depend on the retained fraction and model constants. Having proved $L_R\ge\theta V_R$ alone does not imply that the actual estimator loses a fixed fraction.

**PROVED (counterexample to the word “exactly”).** For any lower estimate $L_R=V_R-e_R$, the upper bound is $C_R-G_R+e_R$. Any $0\le e_R\le(1-\eta)G_R$ with fixed $0<\eta<1$ preserves a gain of at least $\eta G_R$. For example, when $T_R\ge G_R$, retain $T_R-G_R/2$ of the mass contribution and all of $G_R$. The positive mass residual $G_R/2$ is never exactly cancelled, and half the gain survives at every scale. An exact Ward cancellation is sufficient; an error controlled on the gain scale is sufficient too. Failure of this particular scalar upper bound to show a gain says nothing about the true stiffness.

**ARGUED (what would need proof).** The first missing Wilson step is the common decomposition for **the same** pin law, wavelength-normalized profile and stiffness. Section 4.1 initially has a last-layer direct term, with its alleged mass penalty in a trial Fisher *denominator*. It subsequently writes that penalty as an additive $T_R$ in the direct term and the variance. There is no identity making that switch. To prove the fixed-fraction obstruction for a named family, specify the transfer, pins, extension, coupling/cutoff range and scale normalization; prove the decomposition and $G_R>0$, and estimate $T_R/G_R$ from below uniformly on the required scales. For the particular rate claimed one would need, for instance, a lower bound on the mass term and an upper bound on the gain with ratio $\gtrsim R^2/\log R$, with any chart, boundary, orbital and remainder terms controlled. To exclude a *class* of estimators one must also show that every estimator in that class really loses at least the specified fraction, or specify that this is its defining restriction. Gauge invariance alone supplies none of those quantitative comparisons. The O(3) preliminary length in rows 1007/1009 has a different exponent budget; it does not measure a fraction of a 4D mass-cancelling variance.
