## S. Setting and properties

S.1. **Time set.** $\mathbb T$ is $\mathbb R$ (continuum) or $a\mathbb Z$ with $a>0$ (lattice). $\mathbb T_+=\mathbb T\cap[0,\infty)$ and $2\mathbb T_+=\{2t:t\in\mathbb T_+\}$.

S.2. **Data.** A *time-reflection datum* $\mathfrak D=(\mathcal V,b,\tau,1)$ consists of
- a complex vector space $\mathcal V$ (the observables at nonnegative time);
- a map $b:\mathcal V\times\mathcal V\to\mathbb C$, antilinear in the first and linear in the second slot (the reflected pairing, "$\langle\Theta\bar F\,G\rangle$");
- linear maps $\tau_t:\mathcal V\to\mathcal V$, $t\in\mathbb T_+$, with $\tau_0=\mathrm{id}$, $\tau_s\tau_t=\tau_{s+t}$ (translation forward in time);
- an element $1\in\mathcal V$ with $\tau_t1=1$ and $b(1,1)=1$.

The **truncated two-point function** is $u(F,G;t):=b(F,\tau_tG)-b(F,1)\,b(1,G)$.

S.3. **Properties.** Each statement below lists which of these it uses, and no others.
- **(P1) reflection positivity:** $b(F,F)\ge0$ for all $F\in\mathcal V$.
- **(P2) time symmetry:** $b(F,\tau_tG)=b(\tau_tF,G)$ for all $F,G$ and $t\in\mathbb T_+$.
- **(P3) time growth:** for each $F$ there are $C_F,k_F\ge0$ with $b(\tau_tF,\tau_tF)\le C_F(1+t)^{k_F}$ for all $t\in\mathbb T_+$.
- **(P4) time continuity** ($\mathbb T=\mathbb R$ only): $b(F,\tau_sF)\to b(F,F)$ as $s\downarrow0$, for each $F$.

S.4. **Instance (M): a measure.** Let $(\Phi,\Sigma,\mu)$ be a probability space: lattice fields, or distributions. Let $\alpha_t$ ($t\in\mathbb T$) be a group of bimeasurable bijections of $\Phi$ preserving $\mu$ (time translations), and $\theta$ a bimeasurable involution preserving $\mu$ (time mirror), with $\theta\alpha_t\theta=\alpha_{-t}$. For a measurable $F$ put $(\tau_tF)(\phi)=F(\alpha_{-t}\phi)$ and $(\Theta F)(\phi)=F(\theta\phi)$; then $\Theta\tau_t=\tau_{-t}\Theta$. Let $\Sigma_+\subset\Sigma$ be a sub-$\sigma$-algebra (fields at times $\ge0$) with $\tau_t L^2(\Sigma_+)\subset L^2(\Sigma_+)$ for $t\ge0$. Put $\mathcal V=L^2(\Phi,\Sigma_+,\mu)$, $b(F,G)=\mathbb E_\mu[\overline{\Theta F}\,G]$, $1$ the constant. Then:
- (P2) holds: by invariance of $\mu$ under $\alpha_t$, $b(\tau_tF,G)=\mathbb E[\overline{\tau_{-t}\Theta F}\,G]=\mathbb E[\overline{\Theta F}\,\tau_tG]$.
- (P3) holds with $C_F=\|F\|_2^2$, $k_F=0$: Cauchy-Schwarz and invariance give $|b(\tau_tF,\tau_tF)|\le\|\Theta\tau_tF\|_2\|\tau_tF\|_2=\|F\|_2^2$.
- (P4) holds if $\|\tau_sF-F\|_2\to0$ as $s\downarrow0$, since $|b(F,\tau_sF)-b(F,F)|\le\|F\|_2\|\tau_sF-F\|_2$.
- (P1) is the hypothesis "$\mu$ is reflection positive for $\theta$". For SU(2) Wilson measures on $a\mathbb Z^4$ it holds at site and link mirrors (`library/cl1.md` Lemma 3; `gap-transfer.md` section 1 for the torus with a finite time circle).
- $u(F,G;t)=\mathbb E[\overline{\Theta F}\,\tau_tG]-\mathbb E[\overline{\Theta F}]\,\mathbb E[G]$, the usual truncated function of the pair $(\overline{\Theta F},G)$ at time distance $t$.

S.5. **Instance (S): Schwinger functions.** $\mathcal V$ is the span of separated tuples supported in $\{x_4>0\}$ (with the empty tuple $1$), $b(F,G)=\sigma(\theta\bar F\cdot G)$, as in `library/cl1.md` (16). There (P1) is its Lemma 3, (P2) is translation invariance with $\theta\tau_t=\tau_{-t}\theta$, (P3) is its position-independent bound (4) (with $k_F=0$), and (P4) is its equations (6)-(7). So statement (1) in this instance is CL1 Lemma 5.

## A. Three general lemmas (proved here)

Throughout, $\mathcal K$ is a complex Hilbert space, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ antilinear in the first slot.

**Lemma A1 (Hermitian form, Cauchy-Schwarz). Uses (P1) only.** For all $F,G\in\mathcal V$: $b(F,G)=\overline{b(G,F)}$ and $|b(F,G)|^2\le b(F,F)\,b(G,G)$.

Proof. A1.1. Put $q(F)=b(F,F)$, real by (P1). Expanding, $q(F+zG)=q(F)+z\,b(F,G)+\bar z\,b(G,F)+|z|^2q(G)$. Summing with weights $i^{-k}$ over $z=i^k$, $k=0,1,2,3$: $\sum_ki^{-k}q(F+i^kG)=4b(F,G)$, because $\sum_ki^{-k}=0$, $\sum_ki^{-k}i^k=4$, $\sum_ki^{-k}i^{-k}=\sum_k(-1)^k=0$.
A1.2. So $\overline{b(G,F)}=\frac14\sum_ki^{k}q(G+i^kF)$. Since $q(cX)=|c|^2q(X)$, $q(G+i^kF)=q(i^k(F+i^{-k}G))=q(F+i^{-k}G)$. Substituting $j=-k$ mod 4 gives $\overline{b(G,F)}=\frac14\sum_ji^{-j}q(F+i^jG)=b(F,G)$.
A1.3. By A1.1-A1.2, $0\le q(F+zG)=q(F)+2\operatorname{Re}(z\,b(F,G))+|z|^2q(G)$. If $q(G)=0$, take $z=-s\overline{b(F,G)}$, $s>0$: $0\le q(F)-2s|b(F,G)|^2$ for all $s$, so $b(F,G)=0$. If $q(G)>0$, take $z=-\overline{b(F,G)}/q(G)$: $0\le q(F)-|b(F,G)|^2/q(G)$. ∎

**Lemma A2 (log-convexity of a self-adjoint semigroup).** Let $T_t$ ($t\in\mathbb T_+$) be bounded self-adjoint operators on $\mathcal K$ with $T_0=1$, $T_sT_t=T_{s+t}$. Let $\psi\in\mathcal K$, $h\in\mathbb T_+$ with $h>0$, $\rho\ge0$, $C<\infty$, and suppose $\|T_{kh}\psi\|\le C\rho^k$ for infinitely many $k\in\mathbb N$. Then $\|T_h\psi\|\le\rho\|\psi\|$.

Proof. A2.1. Put $g_k=\|T_{kh}\psi\|^2$, $k\ge0$. For $k\ge1$, self-adjointness and the semigroup law give $g_k=\langle\psi,T_{2kh}\psi\rangle=\langle T_{(k-1)h}\psi,T_{(k+1)h}\psi\rangle$, so by Cauchy-Schwarz $g_k^2\le g_{k-1}g_{k+1}$.
A2.2. If $\psi=0$ or $g_1=0$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise every $g_k>0$: if $j\ge2$ were the least index with $g_j=0$, then $g_{j-1}^2\le g_{j-2}g_j=0$, against minimality.
A2.3. Then $r_k=g_k/g_{k-1}$ satisfies $r_k\le r_{k+1}$ (divide A2.1 by $g_{k-1}g_k$). So $g_k=g_0r_1r_2\cdots r_k\ge g_0r_1^k$, and $r_1\le(g_k/g_0)^{1/k}\le(C^2\rho^{2k}/g_0)^{1/k}$ for the infinitely many $k$ of the hypothesis. The right side tends to $\rho^2$. So $g_1\le\rho^2g_0$. ∎

**Lemma A3 (facts on self-adjoint operators).** Definitions: an operator $A$ is a linear map from a subspace $D(A)\subset\mathcal K$ to $\mathcal K$. If $D(A)$ is dense, $D(A^*)$ is the set of $\chi$ for which some $\eta$ satisfies $\langle\chi,A\varphi\rangle=\langle\eta,\varphi\rangle$ for all $\varphi\in D(A)$, and $A^*\chi=\eta$ ($\eta$ is unique by density). $A$ is self-adjoint if $D(A^*)=D(A)$ and $A^*=A$. The resolvent set $\rho(A)$ is the set of $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ for which $A-\lambda:D(A)\to\mathcal K$ is bijective with a bounded inverse; $\operatorname{spec}(A)=\mathbb C\setminus\rho(A)$. "$A\ge c$" means $\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle\ge c\|\varphi\|^2$ for $\varphi\in D(A)$. Let $A$ be self-adjoint and $\mathcal K\ne\{0\}$.
(a) $A$ is closed: $\varphi_n\to\varphi$, $A\varphi_n\to y$ imply $\varphi\in D(A)$, $A\varphi=y$.
(b) If $\lambda\in\mathbb C$, $c>0$, $\|(A-\lambda)\varphi\|\ge c\|\varphi\|$ for all $\varphi\in D(A)$, and $\ker(A-\bar\lambda)=\{0\}$, then $\lambda\in\rho(A)$ and $\|(A-\lambda)^{-1}\|\le1/c$.
(c) If $\mu:=\inf\{\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle:\varphi\in D(A),\|\varphi\|=1\}>-\infty$, then $\mu\in\operatorname{spec}(A)$.
(d) If $A=B$ is bounded with $D(B)=\mathcal K$: $\|B\|=\sup_{\|\varphi\|=1}|\langle\varphi,B\varphi\rangle|$; and for $r\ge0$, $\operatorname{spec}(B)\subset[-r,r]$ if and only if $\|B\|\le r$. In particular $\operatorname{spec}(B)$ is a nonempty subset of $[-\|B\|,\|B\|]$.
(e) For $B$ as in (d) and a real polynomial $q$: $\|q(B)\|\le\sup_{x\in\operatorname{spec}(B)}|q(x)|$.
(f) If $A\ge0$, then $-1\in\rho(A)$; $R:=(A+1)^{-1}$ is bounded, self-adjoint, $\langle g,Rg\rangle\ge0$, $\|R\|\le1$; $\ker A=\ker(R-1)$; and for $\lambda\ne-1$: $\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(A)$ if and only if $(1+\lambda)^{-1}\in\operatorname{spec}(R)$.

Proof. A3.1. (a): The set $\{(\chi,A^*\chi):\chi\in D(A^*)\}$ is closed in $\mathcal K\times\mathcal K$, since each condition $\langle\chi,A\varphi\rangle=\langle\eta,\varphi\rangle$ is closed in $(\chi,\eta)$. It is the graph of $A$.
A3.2. (b): The inequality gives injectivity. Range closed: if $(A-\lambda)\varphi_n\to y$, then $(\varphi_n)$ is Cauchy, $\varphi_n\to\varphi$, $A\varphi_n\to y+\lambda\varphi$, so by (a) $(A-\lambda)\varphi=y$. Range dense: if $y\perp(A-\lambda)D(A)$ then $\langle y,A\varphi\rangle=\langle\bar\lambda y,\varphi\rangle$ for all $\varphi\in D(A)$, so $y\in D(A^*)=D(A)$ with $Ay=\bar\lambda y$, hence $y=0$. So $A-\lambda$ is bijective; the inequality bounds the inverse by $1/c$.
A3.3. (c): $\mu$ is real. Suppose $\mu\in\rho(A)$, $S:=(A-\mu)^{-1}$. For $x=(A-\mu)\varphi$, $y=(A-\mu)\chi$: $\langle x,Sy\rangle=\langle(A-\mu)\varphi,\chi\rangle=\langle\varphi,(A-\mu)\chi\rangle=\langle Sx,y\rangle$ ($A$ symmetric, $\mu$ real), and $\langle x,Sx\rangle=\langle\varphi,(A-\mu)\varphi\rangle\ge0$. So $S$ is a bounded positive form and Cauchy-Schwarz $|\langle x,Sy\rangle|^2\le\langle x,Sx\rangle\langle y,Sy\rangle$ holds (A1.3 for the form $(x,y)\mapsto\langle x,Sy\rangle$). Take unit $\varphi_n\in D(A)$ with $\langle\varphi_n,(A-\mu)\varphi_n\rangle\to0$ and $y_n=(A-\mu)\varphi_n$, so $Sy_n=\varphi_n$. Then $1=|\langle\varphi_n,Sy_n\rangle|^2\le\langle\varphi_n,S\varphi_n\rangle\langle y_n,Sy_n\rangle\le\|S\|\langle\varphi_n,(A-\mu)\varphi_n\rangle\to0$. Contradiction.
A3.4. (d), first claim: let $s$ be the supremum. $s\le\|B\|$. For unit $x,y$ choose $|\omega|=1$ with $\langle x,B\omega y\rangle\ge0$ and put $y'=\omega y$. Since $\langle y',Bx\rangle=\overline{\langle x,By'\rangle}$: $4\operatorname{Re}\langle x,By'\rangle=\langle x+y',B(x+y')\rangle-\langle x-y',B(x-y')\rangle\le s(\|x+y'\|^2+\|x-y'\|^2)=4s$. So $|\langle x,By\rangle|\le s$; with $x=By/\|By\|$, $\|By\|\le s$.
A3.5. (d), second claim. If $\|B\|\le r$ and $\lambda\notin[-r,r]$: for real $\lambda$, $\|(B-\lambda)\varphi\|\ge(|\lambda|-\|B\|)\|\varphi\|$; for non-real $\lambda$, $\|(B-\lambda)\varphi\|\|\varphi\|\ge|\operatorname{Im}\langle\varphi,(B-\lambda)\varphi\rangle|=|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|\|\varphi\|^2$. The same bounds hold at $\bar\lambda$, so (b) gives $\lambda\in\rho(B)$. Conversely, if $\operatorname{spec}(B)\subset[-r,r]$: by (c) for $B$ and for $-B$ (note $\operatorname{spec}(-B)=-\operatorname{spec}(B)$), the infimum and the supremum of $\langle\varphi,B\varphi\rangle$ over unit $\varphi$ lie in $[-r,r]$, so $\|B\|\le r$ by A3.4. Nonempty: (c) puts the infimum in the spectrum.
A3.6. (e): $q(B)$ is bounded self-adjoint. Let $M=\sup_{\operatorname{spec}(B)}|q|$, finite by (d). Claim $\operatorname{spec}(q(B))\subset q(\operatorname{spec}(B))\subset[-M,M]$; then (d) gives $\|q(B)\|\le M$. If $q\equiv c$ is constant, $q(B)-\nu=(c-\nu)1$ is invertible for $\nu\ne c$, and $c\in q(\operatorname{spec}B)$ because $\operatorname{spec}B\ne\emptyset$. If $\deg q=k\ge1$ and $\nu\notin q(\operatorname{spec}B)$, write $q(x)-\nu=c\prod_{i=1}^k(x-z_i)$, $c\ne0$. No $z_i$ lies in $\operatorname{spec}B$, since $q(z_i)=\nu$. So $q(B)-\nu=c\prod_i(B-z_i)$ is a product of bijections with bounded inverses.
A3.7. (f): $\|(A+1)\varphi\|\|\varphi\|\ge\langle\varphi,(A+1)\varphi\rangle\ge\|\varphi\|^2$, and the same for $\bar\lambda=-1$, so (b) gives $-1\in\rho(A)$, $\|R\|\le1$. Self-adjointness and positivity of $R$: as for $S$ in A3.3, with $\mu=-1$. Kernel: $A\varphi=0$ iff $(A+1)\varphi=\varphi$ iff $\varphi=R\varphi$ (and then $\varphi\in RD=D(A)$). Correspondence: for $\varphi\in D(A)$, $(1-(1+\lambda)R)(A+1)\varphi=(A+1)\varphi-(1+\lambda)\varphi=(A-\lambda)\varphi$. Since $A+1:D(A)\to\mathcal K$ is a bijection with bounded inverse $R$, $A-\lambda$ is a bijection with bounded inverse iff $1-(1+\lambda)R$ is one; the inverses are related by $(A-\lambda)^{-1}=R(1-(1+\lambda)R)^{-1}$ and $(1-(1+\lambda)R)^{-1}=1+(1+\lambda)(A-\lambda)^{-1}$ (check: $(1-(1+\lambda)R)(1+(1+\lambda)(A-\lambda)^{-1})=1+(1+\lambda)[(A-\lambda)^{-1}-R-(1+\lambda)R(A-\lambda)^{-1}]$, and the bracket is $R[(A+1)-(A-\lambda)-(1+\lambda)](A-\lambda)^{-1}=0$; the other order is the same computation). Finally $1-(1+\lambda)R=(1+\lambda)((1+\lambda)^{-1}-R)$. ∎

## 1. Statement (1): Hilbert space, semigroup, Hamiltonian, vacuum

**Statement (1).** Let $\mathfrak D=(\mathcal V,b,\tau,1)$ be a datum (S.2).
(1a) *Uses (P1).* $\mathcal N=\{F:b(F,F)=0\}$ is a subspace and equals $\{F:b(G,F)=0\ \forall G\}$. $\langle[F],[G]\rangle:=b(F,G)$ is a well-defined inner product on $\mathcal V/\mathcal N$. Let $\mathcal H$ be the completion and $[F]$ the class of $F$. The vacuum $\Omega:=[1]$ has $\|\Omega\|=1$, and $\langle\Omega,[G]\rangle=b(1,G)$.
(1b) *Uses (P1), (P2), (P3).* For $t\in\mathbb T_+$, $\|[\tau_tF]\|\le\|[F]\|$, so $\tau_t\mathcal N\subset\mathcal N$ and $[F]\mapsto[\tau_tF]$ extends to a bounded operator $T_t$ on $\mathcal H$. Each $T_t$ is self-adjoint with $\|T_t\|\le1$; $T_0=1$, $T_sT_t=T_{s+t}$, $T_t\Omega=\Omega$, $T_{2t}=T_t^*T_t\ge0$, and $\langle[F],T_t[G]\rangle=b(F,\tau_tG)$. With $Q:=1-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$: $QT_t=T_tQ$ and
$$u(F,G;t)=\langle Q[F],T_tQ[G]\rangle,\qquad u(F,F;0)=\|Q[F]\|^2=b(F,F)-|b(1,F)|^2 .$$
(1c) *Uses (P1)-(P4), $\mathbb T=\mathbb R$.* $t\mapsto T_t\psi$ is continuous on $[0,\infty)$ for each $\psi$. Define $D(H)$ as the set of $\psi$ for which $\lim_{h\downarrow0}h^{-1}(\psi-T_h\psi)$ exists, and $H\psi$ as the limit. Then: $H$ is self-adjoint and $H\ge0$; $H\Omega=0$; $T_tD(H)\subset D(H)$ with $HT_t\varphi=T_tH\varphi$ and $\frac{d}{dt}T_t\varphi=-HT_t\varphi$ for $\varphi\in D(H)$; $\ker H=\{\psi:T_t\psi=\psi\ \forall t\ge0\}$; and $(T_t)$ is the only family of operators with $\|S_t\|\le1$, $S_0=1$, $S_sS_t=S_{s+t}$, $t\mapsto S_t\psi$ continuous, whose generator (defined in the same way) is $H$. We write $T_t=e^{-tH}$.
(1d) *Lattice, $\mathbb T=a\mathbb Z$, uses (P1)-(P3).* $T:=T_a$ is a self-adjoint contraction with $T_{na}=T^n$ and $T^2\ge0$. No Hamiltonian is defined here; the gap is stated for $T$ (Lemma E, E3). If $T\ge0$ and $\ker T=0$, then $-a^{-1}\log T$ is defined by functional calculus; this file does not use it.

**Proof.**
1.1. (1a): by Lemma A1, $F\in\mathcal N$ iff $b(G,F)=0$ for all $G$ (one direction is A1.3, the other takes $G=F$). This set is a subspace. If $F-F',G-G'\in\mathcal N$ then $b(F,G)=b(F',G')$, so the form is well defined; it is Hermitian (A1.2), positive, and definite on the quotient. $\|\Omega\|^2=b(1,1)=1$.
1.2. (1b), the contraction. Fix $F$ and put $a(t)=\|[\tau_tF]\|=b(\tau_tF,\tau_tF)^{1/2}$ (real by (P1)). (P2) with $G=\tau_tF$ gives $b(\tau_tF,\tau_tF)=b(F,\tau_{2t}F)$. Lemma A1 gives $a(t)^2\le a(0)a(2t)$.
1.3. If $a(0)=0$ then $a(t)=0$. Otherwise iterate 1.2 $r$ times: $a(t)\le a(0)^{1-2^{-r}}a(2^rt)^{2^{-r}}$. By (P3), $a(2^rt)^{2^{-r}}\le\big(C_F(1+2^rt)^{k_F}\big)^{2^{-r-1}}$, whose logarithm $2^{-r-1}(\log C_F+k_F\log(1+2^rt))$ tends to $0$ (if $C_F=0$, $a(2^rt)=0$ and then $a(t)=0$). Letting $r\to\infty$: $a(t)\le a(0)$. This is the argument of `cl1.md` (18).
1.4. So $\tau_t\mathcal N\subset\mathcal N$ and $[F]\mapsto[\tau_tF]$ is a well-defined linear contraction on the dense subspace $\mathcal V/\mathcal N$; it extends uniquely to $T_t$ with $\|T_t\|\le1$. $\langle[F],T_t[G]\rangle=b(F,\tau_tG)=b(\tau_tF,G)=\langle T_t[F],[G]\rangle$ by (P2); both sides are continuous in each argument, so $T_t$ is self-adjoint. The semigroup law, $T_0=1$ and $T_t\Omega=\Omega$ hold on classes and pass to the closure. $T_{2t}=T_tT_t=T_t^*T_t$.
1.5. $Q$ and $T_t$: $T_tQ\psi=T_t\psi-\langle\Omega,\psi\rangle\Omega$ and $QT_t\psi=T_t\psi-\langle\Omega,T_t\psi\rangle\Omega=T_t\psi-\langle T_t\Omega,\psi\rangle\Omega$, equal. Then $\langle Q[F],T_tQ[G]\rangle=\langle[F],T_tQ[G]\rangle=\langle[F],T_t[G]\rangle-\langle\Omega,[G]\rangle\langle[F],\Omega\rangle=b(F,\tau_tG)-b(1,G)\,b(F,1)$, using $Q^2=Q=Q^*$ and $\langle[F],\Omega\rangle=b(F,1)$.
1.6. (1c), continuity. $\|T_s[F]-[F]\|^2=b(F,\tau_{2s}F)-2\operatorname{Re}b(F,\tau_sF)+b(F,F)\to0$ as $s\downarrow0$ by (P4) (first term by 1.2). For $\psi\in\mathcal H$ and $\varepsilon>0$ pick $[F]$ with $\|\psi-[F]\|<\varepsilon$; then $\|T_s\psi-\psi\|\le2\varepsilon+\|T_s[F]-[F]\|$. So $T_s\psi\to\psi$. At $t>0$: $\|T_{t+h}\psi-T_t\psi\|\le\|T_h\psi-\psi\|$ and, for $0<h\le t$, $\|T_{t-h}\psi-T_t\psi\|=\|T_{t-h}(\psi-T_h\psi)\|\le\|\psi-T_h\psi\|$.
1.7. Generator calculus. Let $\varphi\in D(H)$, $t\ge0$. $h^{-1}(T_t\varphi-T_hT_t\varphi)=T_th^{-1}(\varphi-T_h\varphi)\to T_tH\varphi$, so $T_t\varphi\in D(H)$, $HT_t\varphi=T_tH\varphi$. The right difference quotient $h^{-1}(T_{t+h}\varphi-T_t\varphi)=-T_th^{-1}(\varphi-T_h\varphi)\to-T_tH\varphi$. For $t>0$ the left one is $-T_{t-h}h^{-1}(\varphi-T_h\varphi)=-T_{t-h}H\varphi-T_{t-h}[h^{-1}(\varphi-T_h\varphi)-H\varphi]$; the first term tends to $-T_tH\varphi$ by 1.6 and the second has norm at most $\|h^{-1}(\varphi-T_h\varphi)-H\varphi\|\to0$. So $\frac d{dt}T_t\varphi=-T_tH\varphi$, continuous in $t$.
1.8. The resolvent. For $\psi\in\mathcal H$, $s\mapsto e^{-s}T_s\psi$ is continuous with norm $\le e^{-s}\|\psi\|$. Its Riemann integrals over $[0,N]$ exist (uniform continuity on $[0,N]$, completeness) and converge as $N\to\infty$ (the tails have norm $\le e^{-N}\|\psi\|$). Put $R\psi=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}T_s\psi\,ds$. $R$ is linear, $\|R\|\le1$, and $\langle\chi,R\psi\rangle=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}\langle\chi,T_s\psi\rangle ds$ (the inner product passes through Riemann sums and limits). Hence $R$ is self-adjoint ($\langle\chi,T_s\psi\rangle=\langle T_s\chi,\psi\rangle$). Two vectors with the same inner products with every $\chi$ are equal; this is how the identities below are checked.
1.9. $R\psi\in D(H)$ and $HR\psi=\psi-R\psi$. Substituting $u=s+h$ in the scalar integrals: $T_hR\psi=e^h\int_h^\infty e^{-u}T_u\psi\,du$. So $h^{-1}(R\psi-T_hR\psi)=h^{-1}\int_0^he^{-u}T_u\psi\,du-h^{-1}(e^h-1)\int_h^\infty e^{-u}T_u\psi\,du$. The first term tends to $\psi$, since its distance to $\psi$ is at most $\sup_{0\le u\le h}\|e^{-u}T_u\psi-\psi\|$. The second tends to $R\psi$.
1.10. $RH\varphi=\varphi-R\varphi$ for $\varphi\in D(H)$. For $\chi\in\mathcal H$ the function $g(s)=\langle\chi,T_s\varphi\rangle$ is $C^1$ with $g'(s)=-\langle\chi,T_sH\varphi\rangle$ (1.7). Integration by parts on $[0,N]$ gives $\int_0^Ne^{-s}g'=e^{-N}g(N)-g(0)+\int_0^Ne^{-s}g$. As $N\to\infty$ ($|g|\le\|\chi\|\|\varphi\|$): $-\langle\chi,RH\varphi\rangle=-\langle\chi,\varphi\rangle+\langle\chi,R\varphi\rangle$.
1.11. By 1.9-1.10, $1+H:D(H)\to\mathcal H$ is a bijection with inverse $R$, and $D(H)=R\mathcal H$. $D(H)$ is dense: if $\chi\perp R\mathcal H$ then $\langle R\chi,\psi\rangle=\langle\chi,R\psi\rangle=0$ for all $\psi$, so $R\chi=0$ and $\chi=(1+H)R\chi=0$.
1.12. Self-adjoint. Symmetric: for $\chi,\varphi\in D(H)$, $\langle\chi,H\varphi\rangle=\lim\langle\chi,h^{-1}(\varphi-T_h\varphi)\rangle=\lim\langle h^{-1}(\chi-T_h\chi),\varphi\rangle=\langle H\chi,\varphi\rangle$. So $D(H)\subset D(H^*)$ and $H^*=H$ there. If $\chi\in D(H^*)$ with $\langle\chi,H\varphi\rangle=\langle\eta,\varphi\rangle$ for all $\varphi\in D(H)$, then $\langle\chi,(1+H)\varphi\rangle=\langle\chi+\eta,\varphi\rangle$; with $\varphi=R\psi$ this reads $\langle\chi,\psi\rangle=\langle R(\chi+\eta),\psi\rangle$ for all $\psi$. So $\chi=R(\chi+\eta)\in D(H)$, and $H\chi=(\chi+\eta)-\chi=\eta$.
1.13. $H\ge0$: $\langle\varphi,H\varphi\rangle=\lim h^{-1}(\|\varphi\|^2-\langle\varphi,T_h\varphi\rangle)\ge0$ since $\|T_h\|\le1$. $H\Omega=0$ since $T_h\Omega=\Omega$. Kernel: if $T_t\psi=\psi$ for all $t$, then $\psi\in D(H)$ and $H\psi=0$. If $H\psi=0$, then $\frac d{dt}T_t\psi=-T_tH\psi=0$, so for every $\chi$ the scalar $\langle\chi,T_t\psi\rangle$ is constant (mean value theorem on real and imaginary parts) and $T_t\psi=\psi$.
1.14. Uniqueness. Let $(S_t)$ be as in (1c) with generator $H$. By 1.7 applied to $S$, $S_s\varphi\in D(H)$ and $\frac d{ds}S_s\varphi=-HS_s\varphi$ for $\varphi\in D(H)$. Fix $t>0$ and put $w(s)=T_{t-s}S_s\varphi$, $0\le s\le t$. For $0<s<t$ and small $h$ of either sign, $h^{-1}(w(s+h)-w(s))=T_{t-s-h}\,h^{-1}(S_{s+h}\varphi-S_s\varphi)+h^{-1}(T_{t-s-h}-T_{t-s})S_s\varphi$. The first term tends to $-T_{t-s}HS_s\varphi$ (the operators $T_r$ are uniformly bounded and strongly continuous, and the vectors converge). The second tends to $+T_{t-s}HS_s\varphi$ by 1.7 at the point $t-s$ with $S_s\varphi\in D(H)$. So $w'=0$ on $(0,t)$; $w$ is continuous on $[0,t]$; hence $w(0)=w(t)$, i.e. $T_t\varphi=S_t\varphi$. By density of $D(H)$ and boundedness, $T_t=S_t$.
1.15. (1d): 1.1-1.5 hold for $\mathbb T=a\mathbb Z$ unchanged; $T_{na}=T^n$ by the semigroup law and $T^2=T^*T\ge0$. For the SU(2) Wilson transfer operator, $\mathsf T_P=\hat S^*\hat S\ge0$ (`gap-transfer.md` step 3.3, as used in its 6.1). ∎

## E. Lemma E: the gap as a semigroup bound and as a spectral statement

Fix a datum with (P1)-(P3), its $\mathcal H,\Omega,T_t,Q$ from (1), and $m>0$. Define:
- **(V)** $\{\psi\in\mathcal H:T_t\psi=\psi\ \forall t\in\mathbb T_+\}=\mathbb C\Omega$ (the vacuum is the only invariant vector);
- **(G$_m$)** $\|T_t\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|\psi\|$ for all $\psi\in\Omega^\perp$ and $t\in\mathbb T_+$ (semigroup form of the gap).

For $\mathbb T=\mathbb R$ with (P4), let $H'$ be $H$ restricted to $D(H')=D(H)\cap\Omega^\perp$, an operator on the Hilbert space $\Omega^\perp$.

**Lemma E.**
(E1) (G$_m$) implies (V).
(E2) $\mathbb T=\mathbb R$, (P4). $H'$ is self-adjoint on $\Omega^\perp$, and the following are equivalent: (i) (G$_m$); (ii) $\langle\varphi,H\varphi\rangle\ge m\|\varphi\|^2$ for $\varphi\in D(H')$; (iii) $\operatorname{spec}(H')\subset[m,\infty)$; (iv) (V) and $\operatorname{spec}(H)\subset\{0\}\cup[m,\infty)$. Form (iv) is the hypothesis of row item (2).
(E3) $\mathbb T=a\mathbb Z$, $T=T_a$. (G$_m$) $\iff$ $\|T|_{\Omega^\perp}\|\le e^{-ma}$ $\iff$ $\operatorname{spec}(T|_{\Omega^\perp})\subset[-e^{-ma},e^{-ma}]$.

**Proof.**
E.1. $T_t\Omega^\perp\subset\Omega^\perp$ by 1.5. If $T_t\psi=\psi$ for all $t$, then $Q\psi$ is invariant and in $\Omega^\perp$, so $\|Q\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|Q\psi\|$ for $t>0$ in $\mathbb T_+$, so $Q\psi=0$. This is (E1).
E.2. $H'$ maps into $\Omega^\perp$: $\langle\Omega,H\varphi\rangle=\langle H\Omega,\varphi\rangle=0$. Since $\Omega\in D(H)$, $QD(H)\subset D(H)$, so $D(H)=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus D(H')$ and $D(H')=QD(H)$ is dense in $\Omega^\perp$. $H'$ is symmetric. If $\chi,\eta\in\Omega^\perp$ and $\langle\chi,H'\varphi\rangle=\langle\eta,\varphi\rangle$ for $\varphi\in D(H')$, then for $\varphi=c\Omega+\varphi'\in D(H)$: $\langle\chi,H\varphi\rangle=\langle\chi,H'\varphi'\rangle=\langle\eta,\varphi'\rangle=\langle\eta,\varphi\rangle$. So $\chi\in D(H)\cap\Omega^\perp$ with $H\chi=\eta$. Hence $H'$ is self-adjoint.
E.3. (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii). For $\varphi\in D(H')$: $\langle\varphi,T_h\varphi\rangle=\|T_{h/2}\varphi\|^2\le e^{-mh}\|\varphi\|^2$, so $\langle\varphi,H\varphi\rangle=\lim_{h\downarrow0}h^{-1}(\|\varphi\|^2-\langle\varphi,T_h\varphi\rangle)\ge\lim h^{-1}(1-e^{-mh})\|\varphi\|^2=m\|\varphi\|^2$.
E.4. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii). For real $\lambda<m$: $\|(H'-\lambda)\varphi\|\|\varphi\|\ge\langle\varphi,(H'-\lambda)\varphi\rangle\ge(m-\lambda)\|\varphi\|^2$. For non-real $\lambda$: $\|(H'-\lambda)\varphi\|\|\varphi\|\ge|\operatorname{Im}\langle\varphi,(H'-\lambda)\varphi\rangle|=|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|\|\varphi\|^2$. Both bounds hold at $\bar\lambda$ too, so Lemma A3(b) on $\Omega^\perp$ gives $\lambda\in\rho(H')$.
E.5. (iii)$\Rightarrow$(ii). $H'\ge0$ (1.13). If $\Omega^\perp=\{0\}$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise by A3(c) the infimum $\mu$ of $\langle\varphi,H'\varphi\rangle$ over unit $\varphi\in D(H')$ lies in $\operatorname{spec}(H')\subset[m,\infty)$.
E.6. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i). Let $\varphi\in D(H')$ and $f(t)=\|T_t\varphi\|^2$. By 1.7, $T_t\varphi\in D(H)\cap\Omega^\perp$ and $\frac d{dt}T_t\varphi=-HT_t\varphi$. Expanding $\langle w(t+h),w(t+h)\rangle-\langle w(t),w(t)\rangle$ for $w(t)=T_t\varphi$ gives $f'(t)=-2\langle T_t\varphi,HT_t\varphi\rangle\le-2mf(t)$. So $(e^{2mt}f)'\le0$ and $f(t)\le e^{-2mt}f(0)$ (mean value theorem). Both sides of $\|T_t\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|\psi\|$ are continuous in $\psi$, and $D(H')$ is dense in $\Omega^\perp$ (E.2).
E.7. (iii)$\Rightarrow$(iv). If $T_t\psi=\psi$ for all $t$, then $H\psi=0$ (1.13), $HQ\psi=0$, so $Q\psi\in\ker H'=\{0\}$ ($0\in\rho(H')$ as $m>0$): (V). For $\lambda\notin\{0\}\cup[m,\infty)$, $H-\lambda$ acts as $-\lambda$ on $\mathbb C\Omega$ and as $H'-\lambda$ on $D(H')$; the map $\psi\mapsto-\lambda^{-1}\langle\Omega,\psi\rangle\Omega+(H'-\lambda)^{-1}Q\psi$ is a bounded two-sided inverse.
E.8. (iv)$\Rightarrow$(iii); this excludes a non-eigenvalue spectral point of $H'$ at $0$. Let $R=(H+1)^{-1}$ (A3(f)) and $\alpha=(1+m)^{-1}<1$. Claim: $\operatorname{spec}(R)\subset[0,\alpha]\cup\{1\}$. By A3(d), $\operatorname{spec}(R)\subset[-1,1]$. For $\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(R)$, $\lambda\ne0$, A3(f) gives $\lambda^{-1}-1\in\operatorname{spec}(H)\subset\{0\}\cup[m,\infty)$; so $\lambda=1$ or $0<\lambda\le\alpha$ (a negative $\lambda$ would give $\lambda^{-1}-1<-1$).
E.9. For $k,j\ge0$, A3(e) with $q(x)=x^k-x^{k+j}$ gives $\|R^k-R^{k+j}\|\le\sup_{[0,\alpha]\cup\{1\}}|x^k(1-x^j)|\le\alpha^k$. So $R^k$ converges in norm to a bounded self-adjoint $P$ with $P^2=\lim R^{2k}=P$ and $RP=\lim R^{k+1}=P$. Likewise $\|R-R^{k+1}\|\le\sup_{[0,\alpha]\cup\{1\}}|x(1-x^k)|\le\alpha$, so $\|R(1-P)\|\le\alpha$.
E.10. $RP=P$ gives $P\mathcal H\subset\ker(R-1)=\ker H$ (A3(f)), and $\ker H$ is the set of invariant vectors (1.13), which is $\mathbb C\Omega$ by (V). $R\Omega=\Omega$ because $(H+1)\Omega=\Omega$, so $P\Omega=\Omega$. Hence $P=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ and $1-P=Q$.
E.11. $R$ maps $\Omega^\perp$ into $D(H')$, and $R':=R|_{\Omega^\perp}$ inverts $H'+1$ on $\Omega^\perp$. By E.9-E.10, $\|R'\|\le\alpha$, so $\operatorname{spec}(R')\subset[-\alpha,\alpha]$ (A3(d)). $H'\ge0$ gives $\operatorname{spec}(H')\subset[0,\infty)$ (E.4 with $m=0$). For $\lambda\in\operatorname{spec}(H')$, A3(f) applied to $H'$ gives $(1+\lambda)^{-1}\in\operatorname{spec}(R')$, so $(1+\lambda)^{-1}\le\alpha$, i.e. $\lambda\ge m$.
E.12. (E3): (G$_m$) at $t=a$ is the norm bound. Conversely $\Omega^\perp$ is $T$-invariant, so $\|T^n\psi\|\le\|T|_{\Omega^\perp}\|^n\|\psi\|$. The last equivalence is A3(d) for the bounded self-adjoint $T|_{\Omega^\perp}$. ∎

## 2. Statement (2): a gap gives exponential decay

**Statement (2).** *Uses (P1), (P2), (P3) and (G$_m$).* For $\mathbb T=\mathbb R$ with (P4), (G$_m$) may be replaced by the row's hypothesis "(V) and $\operatorname{spec}(H)\subset\{0\}\cup[m,\infty)$" (Lemma E2, (iv)$\Rightarrow$(i)); for $\mathbb T=a\mathbb Z$, by $\operatorname{spec}(T|_{\Omega^\perp})\subset[-e^{-ma},e^{-ma}]$ (E3).
(2a) For all $F,G\in\mathcal V$ and $t\in\mathbb T_+$:
$$|u(F,G;t)|\le e^{-mt}\,\|Q[F]\|\,\|Q[G]\|\le e^{-mt}\,b(F,F)^{1/2}\,b(G,G)^{1/2},\qquad\|Q[F]\|^2=u(F,F;0)=b(F,F)-|b(1,F)|^2 .$$
(2b) (*monotonicity*) For each $F$, $t\mapsto e^{mt}u(F,F;t)$ is nonnegative and nonincreasing on $2\mathbb T_+$ (on all of $\mathbb R_+$ if $\mathbb T=\mathbb R$).
(2c) Instance (M). Put $\Sigma_-=\{\theta(E):E\in\Sigma_+\}$. For $A\in L^2(\Sigma_-)$, $B\in L^2(\Sigma_+)$, $t\in\mathbb T_+$:
$$\big|\mathbb E[A\,\tau_tB]-\mathbb E[A]\,\mathbb E[B]\big|\le e^{-mt}\,\sigma_A\sigma_B\le e^{-mt}\|A\|_2\|B\|_2,\qquad\sigma_A^2=\mathbb E|A|^2-|\mathbb E A|^2 .$$
By invariance of $\mu$ the same holds for $\tau_sA$ and $\tau_{s+t}B$, any $s\in\mathbb T$: observables separated by a time slab of width $t$.

**Proof.**
2.1. (2a): By 1.5, $u(F,G;t)=\langle Q[F],T_tQ[G]\rangle$. $Q[G]\in\Omega^\perp$, so $\|T_tQ[G]\|\le e^{-mt}\|Q[G]\|$, and Cauchy-Schwarz gives the first bound. $\|Q[F]\|\le\|[F]\|=b(F,F)^{1/2}$.
2.2. (2b): for $\sigma\le\sigma'$ in $\mathbb T_+$, $u(F,F;2\sigma)=\|T_\sigma Q[F]\|^2$ and $u(F,F;2\sigma')=\|T_{\sigma'-\sigma}T_\sigma Q[F]\|^2\le e^{-2m(\sigma'-\sigma)}u(F,F;2\sigma)$, because $T_\sigma Q[F]\in\Omega^\perp$.
2.3. (2c): $F:=\overline{\Theta A}$ is $\Sigma_+$-measurable (for a Borel set $U$, $F^{-1}(\bar U)=\theta^{-1}(A^{-1}(U))$ and $A^{-1}(U)=\theta(E)$ with $E\in\Sigma_+$, $\theta$ an involution) and $\|F\|_2=\|A\|_2$ ($\theta$ preserves $\mu$). $\overline{\Theta F}=A$. So $b(F,\tau_tB)=\mathbb E[A\tau_tB]$, $b(F,1)=\mathbb E[A]$, $b(1,B)=\mathbb E[B]$, and (2a) gives the bound with $\|Q[F]\|\|Q[B]\|$. Finally $\|Q[F]\|^2=\mathbb E[A\,\overline{\Theta A}]-|\mathbb E A|^2\le\mathbb E|A|^2-|\mathbb EA|^2$ by Cauchy-Schwarz and $\|\Theta A\|_2=\|A\|_2$; likewise $\|Q[B]\|^2\le\sigma_B^2$. ∎

**Remark 2.4.** The constant is $\|Q[F]\|\|Q[G]\|$: no growth, clustering or rotation property enters. In the lattice compact case this is `gap-transfer.md` (6b) and the finite-dimensional lemma (B) of `cluster-mass-gap-adjoint-repair` (0.3).

## 3. Statement (3): exponential decay on a total class gives the gap

**Statement (3).** *Uses (P1), (P2), (P3) only;* (P4) only to restate the conclusion with $H$. Let $m>0$ and $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal V$ (any subset). Hypothesis **(X$_m^{\mathcal C}$)**: for each $F\in\mathcal C$ there are $C_F<\infty$ and $t_F\ge0$ with
$$u(F,F;t)\le C_Fe^{-mt}\qquad\text{for all }t\in2\mathbb T_+,\ t\ge t_F .$$
Let $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}$ be the closed linear span of $\{Q[F]:F\in\mathcal C\}$ in $\Omega^\perp$.
(3a) $\|T_t\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|\psi\|$ for all $\psi\in\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}$, $t\in\mathbb T_+$. Hence $|u(F,G;t)|\le e^{-mt}\|Q[F]\|\,\|Q[G]\|$ for every $F\in\mathcal V$, $G\in\operatorname{span}\mathcal C$, $t\in\mathbb T_+$.
(3b) If $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}=\Omega^\perp$, equivalently if $\{\Omega\}\cup\{[F]:F\in\mathcal C\}$ is total in $\mathcal H$, then (G$_m$) holds. So the vacuum is the only invariant vector (E1); for $\mathbb T=\mathbb R$ with (P4), $\ker H=\mathbb C\Omega$ and $\operatorname{spec}(H)\cap(0,m)=\emptyset$, indeed $\operatorname{spec}(H|_{\Omega^\perp})\subset[m,\infty)$ (E2); for $\mathbb T=a\mathbb Z$, $\operatorname{spec}(T|_{\Omega^\perp})\subset[-e^{-ma},e^{-ma}]$ (E3).
(3c) *How large the class must be.* (i) Sufficient: $\{\Omega\}\cup\{[F]:F\in\mathcal C\}$ total in $\mathcal H$. Only diagonal pairs $(F,F)$ are needed, only at even times and only from some time on; the constants $C_F$, $t_F$ may depend on $F$ in any way; $\mathcal C$ need not be a vector space, nor closed under products or translations. One factor in $\operatorname{span}\mathcal C$ then bounds every pair (3a). (ii) Necessary: if (G$_m$) holds, every $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal V$ satisfies (X$_m^{\mathcal C}$) with $C_F=\|Q[F]\|^2$, $t_F=0$ (by (2a)). So (G$_m$) holds if and only if some class with $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}=\Omega^\perp$ satisfies (X$_m^{\mathcal C}$). (iii) Nothing beyond (3a) follows when $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}\ne\Omega^\perp$: Example (3d).
(3d) *Example: a smaller class does not suffice.* There is a datum (a stationary reversible Markov chain with four states) and an infinite class $\mathcal C_B$ such that $u(F,F;t)=0$ for all $F\in\mathcal C_B$ and $t\ge1$, so (X$_\mu^{\mathcal C_B}$) holds for every $\mu>0$, while (V) fails: an invariant unit vector is orthogonal to $\Omega$ and $\operatorname{spec}(T|_{\Omega^\perp})=\{0,1\}$. In a second member of the same family the theory has a gap, but the class decays at a strictly larger rate than the gap.

**Proof of (3a)-(3c).**
3.1. For $F\in\mathcal C$ and $\sigma\in\mathbb T_+$: $\|T_\sigma Q[F]\|^2=\langle Q[F],T_{2\sigma}Q[F]\rangle=u(F,F;2\sigma)$ (1.4, 1.5). So $\|T_\sigma Q[F]\|\le C_F^{1/2}e^{-m\sigma}$ for $2\sigma\ge t_F$.
3.2. For $\psi=\sum_{i=1}^kc_iQ[F_i]$, $F_i\in\mathcal C$: $\|T_\sigma\psi\|\le\sum_i|c_i|\|T_\sigma Q[F_i]\|\le C_\psi e^{-m\sigma}$ for $2\sigma\ge\max_it_{F_i}$, with $C_\psi=\sum_i|c_i|C_{F_i}^{1/2}$.
3.3. Fix $h\in\mathbb T_+$, $h>0$. By 3.2, $\|T_{kh}\psi\|\le C_\psi\rho^k$ with $\rho=e^{-mh}$ for all large $k$. Lemma A2 gives $\|T_h\psi\|\le e^{-mh}\|\psi\|$. For $h=0$ the bound is trivial.
3.4. Both sides of $\|T_h\psi\|\le e^{-mh}\|\psi\|$ are continuous in $\psi$, so the bound holds on $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}$. For the pair bound: $|u(F,G;t)|=|\langle Q[F],T_tQ[G]\rangle|\le\|Q[F]\|\|T_tQ[G]\|$ with $Q[G]\in\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}$.
3.5. (3b): (G$_m$) is 3.4 with $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}=\Omega^\perp$; the rest is Lemma E. Equivalence of the two density conditions: $[F]=Q[F]+b(1,F)\Omega$, so $\operatorname{span}(\{\Omega\}\cup[\mathcal C])=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus\operatorname{span}Q[\mathcal C]$, which is dense in $\mathcal H=\mathbb C\Omega\oplus\Omega^\perp$ iff $\operatorname{span}Q[\mathcal C]$ is dense in $\Omega^\perp$.
3.6. (3c)(ii): (2a) with $G=F$. ∎

**Instance (K): stationary reversible Markov chains (used in (3d) and (6)).** Let $S$ be a finite set, $\pi$ a probability on $S$ with $\pi(x)>0$, and $P$ a stochastic matrix ($P(x,y)\ge0$, $\sum_yP(x,y)=1$) with $\pi(x)P(x,y)=\pi(y)P(y,x)$. Time is $\mathbb Z$.
- K.1. Path weights. For integers $r\le s$ put $w(x_r,\dots,x_s)=\pi(x_r)\prod_{j=r}^{s-1}P(x_j,x_{j+1})$. Summing $x_s$ (row sums 1) or $x_r$ ($\sum_x\pi(x)P(x,y)=\pi(y)$, from reversibility) gives the weight of the shorter path; so expectations $\mathbb E[F]=\sum w\,F$ of functions of finitely many times do not depend on the window used. No measure on $S^{\mathbb Z}$ is needed.
- K.2. $\mathcal V$: functions $F(x_0,\dots,x_n)$, $n\ge0$ (a function of fewer variables is one of more). $(\tau_tF)(x_0,\dots,x_{n+t})=F(x_t,\dots,x_{t+n})$. $\Theta F$ is $F$ evaluated at $(x_0,x_{-1},\dots,x_{-n})$. $b(F,G)=\mathbb E[\overline{\Theta F}\,G]$, $1$ the constant.
- K.3. Claim: $b(F,G)=\sum_x\pi(x)\overline{f_F(x)}f_G(x)$ with $f_F(x)=\sum_{x_1,\dots,x_n}\prod_{j=1}^nP(x_{j-1},x_j)F(x_0,\dots,x_n)|_{x_0=x}$. Proof: by reversibility applied $n'$ times, $w(x_{-n'},\dots,x_n)=\pi(x_0)\prod_{j=1}^{n'}P(x_{-j+1},x_{-j})\prod_{j=1}^nP(x_{j-1},x_j)$; summing the negative-time variables against $\overline{F(x_0,x_{-1},\dots,x_{-n'})}$ gives $\overline{f_F(x_0)}$, and the positive-time ones give $f_G(x_0)$.
- K.4. Hence (P1) holds; $[F]\mapsto f_F$ is an isometry of $\mathcal H$ onto $L^2(S,\pi)$ (onto: $F=f(x_0)$ has $f_F=f$); $f_{\tau_tF}=P^tf_F$ (sum $x_1,\dots,x_t$ first); so $T_t\cong P^t$, $\Omega\cong1$. $P$ is self-adjoint on $L^2(\pi)$ by reversibility, which gives (P2). $|Pf|^2\le P|f|^2$ pointwise (Cauchy-Schwarz with the weights $P(x,\cdot)$) and $\sum_x\pi(x)(P|f|^2)(x)=\sum\pi|f|^2$, so $\|P\|\le1$ and (P3) holds with $k_F=0$.

**Proof of (3d).**
3.7. $S=\{-1,1\}^2$, $x=(x^A,x^B)$, $\pi$ uniform, $P((x^A,x^B),(y^A,y^B))=P_p(x^A,y^A)P_q(x^B,y^B)$ with $P_r(s,s)=1-r$, $P_r(s,-s)=r$, $0\le p,q\le\frac12$. $P$ is symmetric, so $\pi$ uniform is reversible. The functions $1$, $\mathsf a(x)=x^A$, $\mathsf b(x)=x^B$, $\mathsf{ab}$ are orthonormal in $L^2(\pi)$ (four sign functions, each non-constant one of mean zero, products of distinct ones again non-constant). $(P\mathsf a)(x)=\sum_{y^A}P_p(x^A,y^A)y^A=(1-p)x^A-px^A=(1-2p)\mathsf a(x)$; likewise $P\mathsf b=(1-2q)\mathsf b$, $P\mathsf{ab}=(1-2p)(1-2q)\mathsf{ab}$.
3.8. $\mathcal C_B$: all $F\in\mathcal V$ that depend only on $(x_0^B,\dots,x_n^B)$. In $f_F$ the sums over the $A$-components give $\prod\sum_{y^A}P_p=1$, so $f_F$ depends on $x^B$ only: $f_F\in\operatorname{span}\{1,\mathsf b\}$, $Qf_F=c_F\mathsf b$, and $u(F,F;t)=|c_F|^2(1-2q)^t$. $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C_B}=\mathbb C\mathsf b$ ($F=x_0^B$ gives $\mathsf b$).
3.9. $p=0$, $q=\frac12$: $u(F,F;t)=0$ for $t\ge1$ and all $F\in\mathcal C_B$, so (X$_\mu^{\mathcal C_B}$) holds for every $\mu>0$. But $P\mathsf a=\mathsf a$ with $\mathsf a\perp1$: (V) fails. The spectrum of $P$ on $1^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{\mathsf a,\mathsf b,\mathsf{ab}\}$ is $\{1,0,0\}$.
3.10. $0<p<q\le\frac12$: on $1^\perp$, $P$ is diagonal with entries $1-2p>1-2q\ge0$ and $(1-2p)(1-2q)\le1-2p$, so $\|P|_{1^\perp}\|=1-2p$ and the gap (E3) is exactly $m=-\log(1-2p)$. The class $\mathcal C_B$ decays at the rate $-\log(1-2q)>m$ ($=\infty$ if $q=\frac12$). ∎

**Remark 3.11.** The repository's (L3) of `ym/crossover-reduction` (0.3) is (3b) as reported; `gap-transfer.md` (7a) is (3b) for the compact Wilson transfer operator, and its Remark 7.7 (the constants) is the trivial case $\mathcal C=\{1\}$ of (3d). Polynomial decay on a total class gives nothing: the Sussman-Alpoge example $d\mu(E)=Ee^{-E}dE$ (0.4) has $u(t)=(1+t)^{-2}$ and spectral weight in every interval $(0,\varepsilon)$.

## 4. Statement (4): rotations give the bound in every direction

**Setting (R).** $d\ge2$. Continuum: $\mathfrak G$ is the Euclidean group of maps $x\mapsto Rx+v$, $R\in\mathrm O(d)$, $v\in\mathbb R^d$. Lattice: $\mathfrak G$ is the group of maps $x\mapsto Rx+v$ with $R$ a signed permutation matrix and $v\in a\mathbb Z^d$. $(\Phi,\Sigma,\mu)$ is a probability space with bimeasurable $\mu$-preserving bijections $\alpha_g$ ($g\in\mathfrak G$), $\alpha_{gh}=\alpha_g\alpha_h$. Put $g\cdot F=F\circ\alpha_g^{-1}$; then $(gh)\cdot F=g\cdot(h\cdot F)$, $g\cdot(FG)=(g\cdot F)(g\cdot G)$, $\mathbb E[g\cdot F]=\mathbb E[F]$. For each closed $X\subset\mathbb R^d$ there is a sub-$\sigma$-algebra $\Sigma_X$ (fields in $X$), with $\Sigma_X\subset\Sigma_Y$ for $X\subset Y$, and $g\cdot L^2(\Sigma_X)=L^2(\Sigma_{gX})$. Time is the direction $e_d$; $\tau_t$ is the translation by $te_d$, $\theta$ the reflection $x_d\mapsto-x_d$ (an element of $\mathfrak G$ in both cases), $\Sigma_+=\Sigma_{\{x_d\ge0\}}$. This is instance (M) with $\Theta F=\theta\cdot F$.

**Statement (4).** *Uses:* (P1) at the mirror $\theta$ only; invariance of $\mu$ under the translations and under one element of $\mathfrak G$ that turns the chosen direction into $e_d$; the covariance of the localization; (G$_m$) for the time direction. (P2), (P3) hold automatically (S.4). No reflection positivity at other mirrors is used.
(4a) Continuum. For a unit vector $e$, closed $X\subset\{x\cdot e\le0\}$ and $Y\subset\{x\cdot e\ge0\}$, $A\in L^2(\Sigma_X)$, $B\in L^2(\Sigma_Y)$ and $s\ge0$, with $\tau_{se}$ the translation by $se$:
$$\big|\mathbb E[A\,(\tau_{se}\cdot B)]-\mathbb E[A]\,\mathbb E[B]\big|\le e^{-ms}\sigma_A\sigma_B .$$
(4b) Continuum, balls. If $X,Y$ lie in the closed ball of radius $r$ about $0$ and $|v|\ge2r$, then $|\mathbb E[A(\tau_v\cdot B)]-\mathbb E[A]\mathbb E[B]|\le e^{-m(|v|-2r)}\sigma_A\sigma_B$.
(4c) Lattice. (4a) holds for $e\in\{\pm e_1,\dots,\pm e_d\}$ and $s\in a\mathbb Z_+$. If $X,Y\subset[-r,r]^d$ with $r\in a\mathbb Z_+$, and $v\in a\mathbb Z^d$ has $|v|_\infty\ge2r$, the bound of (4b) holds with $|v|_\infty$ in place of $|v|$. Since $|v|_\infty\ge|v|/\sqrt d$, the rate in the Euclidean distance is at least $m/\sqrt d$; the hypotheses give no more.
(4d) Schwinger functions. If instead $\mathfrak G$ acts on the test tuples and preserves the Schwinger functional, the same steps give the bound with the constant $\|Q[R\cdot F]\|$ of the rotated tuple. That is the reflection form at the rotated mirror; it is not $b(F,F)$, and a bound uniform over $R$ needs a moment bound per ball (T0's (c)(2), as `gap-skeleton.md` 1.2 says). In the measure instance the constant $\sigma_A$ is invariant under $\mathfrak G$ and no such bound is needed.

**Proof.**
4.1. (4a). If $e=e_d$ put $R=1$; otherwise let $w=e-e_d$ and $R=1-2ww^{\mathsf T}/|w|^2$, an orthogonal reflection with $Re=e_d$ (direct check: $w^{\mathsf T}e=1-e_d\cdot e=|w|^2/2$). Let $g$ be the motion $x\mapsto Rx$.
4.2. $A':=g\cdot A\in L^2(\Sigma_{RX})$ and $RX\subset\{x_d\le0\}$, because $(Rx)\cdot e_d=(Rx)\cdot(Re)=x\cdot e$. Likewise $B':=g\cdot B\in L^2(\Sigma_{RY})$ with $RY\subset\{x_d\ge0\}$.
4.3. As motions, $g\circ\tau_{se}=\tau_{sRe}\circ g=\tau_{se_d}\circ g$. So $g\cdot(\tau_{se}\cdot B)=\tau_{se_d}\cdot B'$, and by invariance $\mathbb E[A(\tau_{se}\cdot B)]=\mathbb E[A'(\tau_{se_d}\cdot B')]$, $\mathbb E[A]=\mathbb E[A']$, $\mathbb E[B]=\mathbb E[B']$, $\sigma_{A'}=\sigma_A$, $\sigma_{B'}=\sigma_B$.
4.4. $\theta\cdot A'\in L^2(\Sigma_{\theta RX})$ and $\theta RX\subset\{x_d\ge0\}$, so $F:=\overline{\theta\cdot A'}\in\mathcal V$ and $\overline{\Theta F}=A'$. Now 2.3 applies verbatim with $(A',B')$ in place of $(A,B)$.
4.5. (4b). Let $e=v/|v|$. Put $A''=\tau_{-re}\cdot A$, supported in $X-re\subset\{x\cdot e\le0\}$, and $B''=\tau_{re}\cdot B$, supported in $Y+re\subset\{x\cdot e\ge0\}$. Since $\tau_{-re}\tau_v=\tau_{(|v|-2r)e}\tau_{re}$, invariance under $\tau_{-re}$ gives $\mathbb E[A(\tau_v\cdot B)]=\mathbb E[A''(\tau_{(|v|-2r)e}\cdot B'')]$. Apply (4a) with $s=|v|-2r$; $\sigma$ is translation invariant.
4.6. (4c). For $e=\pm e_i$ the motion $R$ of 4.1 is a signed permutation (a coordinate swap, possibly with a sign), so 4.1-4.4 hold in the lattice group. For the box form, take $i$ with $|v_i|=|v|_\infty$, say $v_i>0$ (else use $-e_i$). $B':=\tau_{v-v_ie_i}\cdot B$ is supported in $Y+v-v_ie_i\subset\{|x_i|\le r\}$, and $\tau_v\cdot B=\tau_{v_ie_i}\cdot B'$. Then 4.5 with $e=e_i$, the lattice shifts $\pm re_i$ and $s=v_i-2r\in a\mathbb Z_+$. ∎

## 5. Statement (5): the gap passes to limits

**Setting.** For $n\in\mathbb N$ let $\mathfrak D_n=(\mathcal V_n,b_n,\tau^{(n)},1_n)$ be data on time sets $\mathbb T_n$ (each $\mathbb R$ or $a_n\mathbb Z$; lattice spacing $a_n\to0$, volume $\to\infty$, or both), with (P1)-(P3), and $\mathcal H_n,\Omega_n,Q_n,T^{(n)}_t,u_n$ from (1). Let $\mathfrak D_\infty=(\mathcal V_\infty,b_\infty,\tau^{(\infty)},1_\infty)$ on $\mathbb T_\infty$ be the limit datum, with (P1)-(P3). Let $\mathcal C\subset\mathcal V_\infty$ be a fixed class and $\iota_n:\mathcal C\to\mathcal V_n$ maps (the version of $F$ in the $n$-th theory). How $\mathfrak D_\infty$ is built is not part of the statement; in the usual construction $b_\infty$ is the pointwise limit of $b_n\circ(\iota_n\times\iota_n)$, and then (P1) and (P2) pass to the limit, while (P3) for $\mathfrak D_\infty$ needs a bound uniform in $n$ (e.g. the bound $\|F\|_2^2$ of S.4 with converging $L^2$ norms).

**Hypotheses.**
- **(H1) rate.** There are $m_n>0$ with $\liminf_nm_n\ge m>0$ and closed subspaces $\mathcal S_n\subset\Omega_n^\perp$ with $T^{(n)}_t\mathcal S_n\subset\mathcal S_n$, $Q_n[\iota_nF]\in\mathcal S_n$ for $F\in\mathcal C$, and $\|T^{(n)}_t\psi\|\le e^{-m_nt}\|\psi\|$ for $\psi\in\mathcal S_n$, $t\in\mathbb T_{n,+}$. (With $\mathcal S_n=\Omega_n^\perp$ this is (G$_{m_n}$); a smaller $\mathcal S_n$ is the gap on the sector of the class.)
- **(H2) convergence.** For each $F\in\mathcal C$ and $t\in2\mathbb T_{\infty,+}$ there are $t_n\in2\mathbb T_{n,+}$ with $t_n\to t$ and $u_n(\iota_nF,\iota_nF;t_n)\to u_\infty(F,F;t)$.

**Statement (5).** *Uses (P1)-(P3) for every $\mathfrak D_n$ and for $\mathfrak D_\infty$, (H1), (H2).*
(5a) For $F\in\mathcal C$ and $s\le t$ in $2\mathbb T_{\infty,+}$: $0\le u_\infty(F,F;t)\le e^{-m(t-s)}u_\infty(F,F;s)$. In particular $u_\infty(F,F;t)\le\|Q_\infty[F]\|^2e^{-mt}$.
(5b) Hence $\mathfrak D_\infty$ satisfies (X$_m^{\mathcal C}$) with $C_F=\|Q_\infty[F]\|^2$, and by (3a): $\|T^{(\infty)}_t\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|\psi\|$ on $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}$ and $|u_\infty(F,G;t)|\le e^{-mt}\|Q_\infty[F]\|\|Q_\infty[G]\|$ for $F\in\mathcal V_\infty$, $G\in\operatorname{span}\mathcal C$.
(5c) If moreover $\{\Omega_\infty\}\cup\{[F]_\infty:F\in\mathcal C\}$ is total in $\mathcal H_\infty$, then $\mathfrak D_\infty$ satisfies (G$_m$): by (3b) and Lemma E the limit has a unique invariant vector and, with (P4) for $\mathfrak D_\infty$, $\operatorname{spec}(H_\infty|_{\Omega_\infty^\perp})\subset[m,\infty)$. The limit theory has a gap at least $m$.
(5d) *Which uniformity is used.* Only $\liminf_nm_n\ge m$: one rate for the whole sequence. Not used: any bound on constants; uniformity of the convergence in $t$ or in $F$; convergence of off-diagonal pairs; totality of $\iota_n(\mathcal C)$ in $\mathcal H_n$; (P4) for the members. The row's hypothesis "constants bounded along the sequence" is a consequence, not an input: by (2a) the member constant can be taken to be $\|Q_n[\iota_nF]\|^2=u_n(\iota_nF,\iota_nF;0)$, which converges by (H2) at $t=0$. If the convergence at $t=0$ fails (for instance a contact term makes $\|Q_n[\iota_nF]\|\to\infty$) but (H2) holds at all $t>0$, then (5a) with any $s>0$ still gives the rate $m$ with the constant $e^{ms}u_\infty(F,F;s)$.

**Proof.**
5.1. (Monotonicity in the member.) Let $\psi_n=Q_n[\iota_nF]\in\mathcal S_n$ and $\sigma\le\sigma'$ in $\mathbb T_{n,+}$. By 1.5, $u_n(\iota_nF,\iota_nF;2\sigma)=\|T^{(n)}_\sigma\psi_n\|^2$, and since $T^{(n)}_\sigma\psi_n\in\mathcal S_n$: $u_n(\iota_nF,\iota_nF;2\sigma')=\|T^{(n)}_{\sigma'-\sigma}T^{(n)}_\sigma\psi_n\|^2\le e^{-2m_n(\sigma'-\sigma)}u_n(\iota_nF,\iota_nF;2\sigma)$. This is (2b) on the sector.
5.2. Let $s<t$ in $2\mathbb T_{\infty,+}$ and take $s_n,t_n$ from (H2). For large $n$, $s_n<t_n$, and both lie in $2\mathbb T_{n,+}$, so 5.1 gives $u_n(t_n)\le e^{-m_n(t_n-s_n)}u_n(s_n)$ (arguments $\iota_nF,\iota_nF$ suppressed). Given $\varepsilon>0$, for large $n$: $m_n\ge m-\varepsilon$ and $t_n-s_n\ge t-s-\varepsilon$, so $e^{-m_n(t_n-s_n)}\le e^{-(m-\varepsilon)(t-s-\varepsilon)}$. Letting $n\to\infty$ and then $\varepsilon\to0$: $u_\infty(F,F;t)\le e^{-m(t-s)}u_\infty(F,F;s)$. The case $s=t$ is trivial.
5.3. Nonnegativity and the case $s=0$: $u_\infty(F,F;t)=\|T^{(\infty)}_{t/2}Q_\infty[F]\|^2\ge0$ and $u_\infty(F,F;0)=\|Q_\infty[F]\|^2$ (1.5 for $\mathfrak D_\infty$).
5.4. (5b) is (5a) inserted in (3a) with $t_F=0$; (5c) is (3b). ∎

**Remark 5.5 (a test).** (5a) is a necessary condition that a computed or estimated limit must pass: if $e^{mt}u_\infty(F,F;t)$ increases somewhere on $2\mathbb T_+$ for one $F$ of the class, then no sequence of reflection-positive members with rate $\ge m$ on a sector containing the class converges to it in the sense of (H2).

## 6. Statement (6): two counterexamples

**Statement (6).**
(6a) *Gaps bounded below, limit without a gap.* Under the hypotheses of (5) with a class that is total in the limit, this cannot happen: (5c). In particular unbounded constants never cause it when the members are reflection positive (5d). Two explicit sequences show which hypothesis must break:
- (6a-1) *The class changes.* Reflection-positive members, all with a positive gap, whose rate on the sector of a class $\mathcal C_A$ is $\log2$ for every $n$; the reflected pairings of all observables converge; the limit has an invariant vector orthogonal to the vacuum. The uniform rate holds on a class that is not total in the limit, and the class that is total in the limit has no uniform rate.
- (6a-2) *The constants are not bounded, members not reflection positive.* Stationary Gaussian sequences $X^{(n)}$ whose truncated two-point functions $c_n(t)$ satisfy $|c_n(t)|\le C_ne^{-\mu|t|}$ for every $\mu>0$, with $C_n=e^{\mu n}$, and converge pointwise to $c(t)=r^{|t|}$, which decays only at the rate $-\log r$. Neither the site nor the link mirror is reflection positive for them.
(6b) *Finite volumes going to infinity with gaps going to $0$.* A reflection-positive lattice field on the spatial torus $\mathbb Z/N$ with gap exactly $m_N=-2\log\cos(\pi/N)$, so $0<m_N\le2\pi^2/N^2$ for $N\ge4$.

**Proof of (6a-1).**
6.1. Members: instance (K), the four-state chain of 3.7 with $p=\frac14$ and $q=q_n$, $0<q_n<\frac14$, $q_n\to0$. Limit: the same chain with $q=0$. All data share $\mathcal V$ (functions of finitely many nonnegative times) and $\iota_n=\mathrm{id}$.
6.2. Convergence: for $F,G\in\mathcal V$, $b_n(F,\tau_tG)=\sum_x\frac14\overline{f_F(x)}(P_n^tf_G)(x)$ with $f_F$ from K.3 for $P_n$; each is a polynomial in $q_n$, so it converges to the value at $q=0$. So (H2) holds for every $F$, and the limit pairing is that of the $q=0$ chain.
6.3. Members: by 3.7, the eigenvalues of $P_n$ on $1^\perp$ are $\frac12$, $1-2q_n$, $\frac12(1-2q_n)$; the largest is $1-2q_n$. So member $n$ has the gap $m_n=-\log(1-2q_n)>0$, and $m_n\to0$.
6.4. The class $\mathcal C_A$ of functions of the $A$-components only has, as in 3.8, $Q[F]\in\mathbb C\mathsf a$; $\mathcal S:=\mathbb C\mathsf a$ is $P_n$-invariant with $\|P_n\mathsf a\|=\frac12$. So (H1) holds for $\mathcal C_A$ with $m_n=\log2$ for all $n$, and (5) gives the rate $\log2$ on $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C_A}=\mathbb C\mathsf a$ in the limit, correctly.
6.5. Limit: $P_\infty\mathsf b=\mathsf b$, $\mathsf b\perp1$: (V) fails. The class $\mathcal C_A$ is not total in $\mathcal H_\infty$ (it misses $\mathsf b$ and $\mathsf{ab}$), and the total class $\mathcal V$ has no rate uniform in $n$ (6.3).

**Proof of (6a-2).**
6.6. Fix $0<r<1$, $n\ge3$. Put $c_n(t)=r^{|t|}(1-|t|/n)_+$ for $t\in\mathbb Z$. Then $c_n(t)=0$ for $|t|\ge n$, so $|c_n(t)|\le e^{\mu n}e^{-\mu|t|}$ for every $\mu>0$; and $c_n(t)\to r^{|t|}$ for each $t$.
6.7. Positive definiteness, by explicit factorization. For integers $s,t$: $r^{|s-t|}=(1-r^2)\sum_{k\le\min(s,t)}r^{s-k}r^{t-k}$ (geometric series: the sum is $r^{s+t-2\min(s,t)}/(1-r^2)$). And $(1-|s-t|/n)_+=\frac1n\#\{j\in\mathbb Z:\max(s,t)\le j\le\min(s,t)+n-1\}$. So $c_n(s-t)=\sum_{k,j}w(s;k,j)w(t;k,j)$ with $w(s;k,j)=\sqrt{(1-r^2)/n}\;r^{s-k}\,\mathbf 1[k\le s\le j\le s+n-1]$, and $\sum_{k,j}w(s;k,j)^2=c_n(0)=1<\infty$.
6.8. Realization: with $(\xi_{k,j})_{(k,j)\in\mathbb Z^2}$ independent standard Gaussians, $X^{(n)}_s=\sum_{k,j}w(s;k,j)\xi_{k,j}$ converges in $L^2$ (orthonormal terms, square-summable coefficients) and $\mathbb E[X^{(n)}_sX^{(n)}_t]=c_n(s-t)$. Since $w(s+h;k,j)=w(s;k-h,j-h)$, shifting time relabels the i.i.d. family, so the law is stationary. The existence of a countable independent family is cited (product measure; **not rebuilt**). The counterexample to the statement "pointwise convergence plus decay with unbounded constants gives decay in the limit" needs only the numbers $c_n$, $c$ of 6.6 and the positive definiteness of 6.7.
6.9. Not reflection positive. For a linear observable $F=\sum_{s\ge0}z_sX_s$ the site mirror gives $b(F,F)=\sum_{s,t\ge0}\bar z_sz_tc_n(s+t)$, and the link mirror (between times $0$ and $1$) gives $\sum\bar z_sz_tc_n(s+t+1)$. The $2\times2$ minors on $\{0,1\}$ are $c_n(0)c_n(2)-c_n(1)^2=r^2[(1-\frac2n)-(1-\frac1n)^2]=-r^2/n^2<0$ and $c_n(1)c_n(3)-c_n(2)^2=r^4[(1-\frac1n)(1-\frac3n)-(1-\frac2n)^2]=-r^4/n^2<0$. So (P1) fails at both mirrors, consistent with (5).

**Proof of (6b).**
6.10. Instance (K) with $S=\mathbb Z/N$, $N\ge3$, $\pi$ uniform, $P(x,x)=\frac12$, $P(x,x\pm1)=\frac14$; $P$ is symmetric, so reversible. Read $X_t$ as the position of one particle on the spatial torus, i.e. the lattice field $n_t(x)=\mathbf 1[X_t=x]$ with $\sum_xn_t(x)=1$.
6.11. $e_k(x)=e^{2\pi ikx/N}$, $k=0,\dots,N-1$, form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\pi)$ ($\frac1N\sum_xe^{2\pi i(k-l)x/N}=\delta_{kl}$, and there are $N$ of them). $(Pe_k)(x)=\frac12e_k(x)+\frac14(e_k(x+1)+e_k(x-1))=\frac12(1+\cos\frac{2\pi k}N)e_k(x)=\cos^2(\frac{\pi k}N)e_k(x)$.
6.12. On $1^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{e_k:k\ne0\}$ the eigenvalues are $\cos^2(\pi k/N)\in[0,1)$, the largest $\cos^2(\pi/N)$ ($k=1$). So $\|P|_{1^\perp}\|=\cos^2(\pi/N)$ and by (E3) the gap is exactly $m_N=-2\log\cos(\pi/N)>0$.
6.13. Bound: $\cos x\ge1-x^2/2$, and for $0\le y\le\frac12$, $-\log(1-y)=\sum_ky^k/k\le y/(1-y)\le2y$. With $x=\pi/N\le1$ ($N\ge4$): $-\log\cos x\le x^2$, so $m_N\le2\pi^2/N^2\to0$.
6.14. The local field $n(0)$ sees it: $f=\mathbf 1_{\{0\}}$ has $\langle e_k,f\rangle_\pi=1/N$, so $u(t)=N^{-2}\sum_{k\ne0}\cos^{2t}(\pi k/N)\ge2N^{-2}e^{-m_Nt}$. ∎

**Remark 6.15.** For the free massless lattice field on $(\mathbb Z/N)^3\times\mathbb Z$ the same mechanism (lowest nonzero momentum $2\pi/N$) gives a gap of order $1/N$. This file does not prove that example: the lower bound (a positive gap on the full Fock space in each volume) needs the spectral analysis of Gaussian transfer operators, which is not rebuilt here. Example (6b) has the same content with every step finite.

## 7. Statement (7): finite spatial volume

**Setting.** $d=4$. The *cylinder* $C_\ell=T_\ell^3\times\mathbb T$ has a spatial torus of side $\ell$ and an infinite time axis. The *torus* $T_\ell^3\times(\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z)$ has a periodic time of period $L$ ($L=\ell$ for the cubic tori of T0). A datum on the cylinder is instance (M) with fields on $C_\ell$, $\Sigma_+$ the fields at times $\ge0$, the time mirror and the time translations.

**Statement (7).**
(7a) On the cylinder, (1), (2), (3) and (5) hold unchanged: their proofs use the time mirror, the time translations and (P1)-(P4), never the spatial geometry. In particular, at each $\ell$, a gap $m_\ell$ in the semigroup form (G$_{m_\ell}$) is equivalent to decay at the rate $m_\ell$ on a total class with constants that may depend on the observables ((2), (3)). For SU(2) Wilson at fixed spacing this is `gap-transfer.md` (6)-(7), and the cylinder measure is its slab family (8).
(7b) (4) fails on the cylinder. No element of the symmetry group of $C_\ell$ maps $e_4$ to a spatial direction, so the time bound gives no spatial bound. Moreover a spatial shift by $\ell e_1$ is the identity on $C_\ell$, so $\mathbb E[A(\tau_{\ell e_1}\cdot B)]-\mathbb E[A]\mathbb E[B]=\mathbb E[AB]-\mathbb E[A]\mathbb E[B]$: spatial correlations are $\ell$-periodic and do not tend to the product.
(7c) On the torus with periodic time, (1) does not hold as stated: the reflected pairing $b(F,\tau_tG)$ exists only while the supports fit in half a period, and the torus expectations are thermal. At fixed lattice spacing, `gap-transfer.md` (6a) gives the exact comparison $|\langle A_0B_t\rangle_L-\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty|\le2\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j^{L-t}$, the sum running over the excited eigenvalue ratios of the transfer operator with multiplicity. So a statement on the cubic tori transfers to the cylinder only together with a bound on that sum, i.e. on the thermal weight of the excited states at time $L-t$. A version in physical units, uniform in the spacing, is **not proved** here.
(7d) *The statement in $\ell$ that gives the infinite-volume gap.* Let $\ell_j\to\infty$, let $\mathfrak D_j$ be cylinder data on $C_{\ell_j}$ with (P1)-(P3), let $\mathcal C$ be a class of observables localized in one fixed bounded region $W$ (for large $j$, $W$ embeds in $C_{\ell_j}$; $\iota_j$ is that embedding), and let $\mathfrak D_\infty$ be the infinite-volume datum. Suppose:
- (U$_\ell$) there is $m>0$ such that for every $j$, $\|T^{(j)}_t\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|\psi\|$ for $t\ge0$ and $\psi$ in a closed $T^{(j)}$-invariant $\mathcal S_j\subset\Omega_j^\perp$ that contains $Q_j[\iota_jF]$, $F\in\mathcal C$ (the cylinder gap on the sector of the class, with $m_{\ell_j}\ge m$);
- the diagonal reflected truncated functions converge, as in (H2);
- $\{\Omega_\infty\}\cup[\mathcal C]_\infty$ is total in $\mathcal H_\infty$.

Then $\mathfrak D_\infty$ has a unique invariant vector and the gap (G$_m$); with (P4), $\operatorname{spec}(H_\infty|_{\Omega_\infty^\perp})\subset[m,\infty)$. This is (5c). What must be uniform in $\ell$ is the rate $m$ only. The constants need not be uniform in $\ell$ (5d): the bound of (2) carries the constant $\|Q_j[\iota_jF]\|^2$, which converges by the convergence hypothesis. A bound uniform in $\ell$ on the moments of observables in $W$ is what usually gives the convergence (compactness); it is an input of the existence of the limit, not of the transfer of the gap.
(7e) *The time direction alone suffices for the gap*, because (5) uses only the time mirror and time translations, and the conclusion is a statement about $H_\infty$, the generator of time translations. Decay in every direction of the limit is a second step: (4) applied to $\mathfrak D_\infty$, which needs the limit's invariance under rotations (or the hypercubic group) that turn a spatial direction into time. That invariance is a property of the limit, not of the cylinders, and no spatial rate at finite $\ell$ is used or needed. It is not automatic: a volume limit of cylinders treats time differently from space, and its invariance under exchange of time and space is a separate statement (`gap-skeleton.md` 1.4, (J2)). With only hypercubic invariance of the limit the rate in Euclidean distance is $m/2$ (4c with $d=4$).
(7f) *Contrapositive.* If the infinite-volume datum has an invariant vector orthogonal to $\Omega_\infty$ in the closed span of $Q_\infty[\mathcal C]$, for a class whose diagonal reflected functions converge, then no rate $m>0$ holds uniformly along the sequence on sectors containing the class: the cylinder gaps on those sectors tend to $0$ along every such sequence.

**Proof.**
7.1. (7a): read the proofs of (1)-(3), (5): each step uses only $b$, $\tau_t$ ($t$ in the time set), $1$, and (P1)-(P4). The spatial side enters only through the datum.
7.2. (7b): a motion $x\mapsto Rx+v$ of $\mathbb R^4$ descends to $C_\ell$ only if $R$ maps the period lattice $\ell\mathbb Z^3\times\{0\}$ onto itself. Then $R$ preserves its span $\mathbb R^3\times\{0\}$ and, being orthogonal, the orthogonal complement $\mathbb Re_4$; so $Re_4=\pm e_4$. The identity under $\tau_{\ell e_1}$ is periodicity.
7.3. (7c): the inequality is `gap-transfer.md` (6a) as proved there (its 6.2-6.3), with $T$ its time period; nothing new is claimed.
7.4. (7d): apply (5) with $\mathfrak D_n=\mathfrak D_j$, $m_j=m$, $\mathcal S_n=\mathcal S_j$; (5c) gives the conclusion; (5d) gives the remark on constants.
7.5. (7e): (5) and Lemma E use only the time structure. (4) needs the rotation invariance of $\mathfrak D_\infty$ as stated there.
7.6. (7f): if (U$_\ell$) held with some $m>0$, (5b) would give $\|T^{(\infty)}_t\psi\|\le e^{-mt}\|\psi\|$ on $\mathcal K_{\mathcal C}$, which contains the invariant vector $\psi\ne0$; contradiction. The same holds along every subsequence. ∎

## 8. Differences from `gap-skeleton.md` section 1

8.1. (G-inf-T) of the skeleton (1.2) asks for decay for every $F\in\mathfrak P_+$. Statement (3) shows that one total class suffices, with diagonal pairs only, at even times, from some time on, and with constants that may depend on the observable. The conclusions agree.
8.2. The skeleton's step "(S) $\Rightarrow$ (D) in every direction ... needs ... the per-ball moment bound (c)(2)" (1.2). This holds for the Schwinger-function formulation (4d). In a measure formulation the constant is $\sigma_A$, invariant under the motions, and no moment bound is needed (4a). The skeleton's rate $m/2$ with $W_4$ only agrees with (4c).
8.3. The skeleton's (J) (1.4) says the decay form of (G-ell) "has the constant $\mu(\theta\bar F\cdot F)$, which is bounded by (c)(2)", and uses this to pass to the limit. By (5) the passage needs no bound on the constant: convergence of the diagonal reflected functions and one common rate suffice (5d). (c)(2) is still needed, but for the existence of the limit $S^\sharp$, not for the gap.
8.4. The skeleton's "(G-ell) lattice form implies the continuum form" (1.3) is (5) with $\mathcal S_n$ the cyclic sector minus the vacuum. The conclusion "$\ker H_\ell=\mathbb C\Omega$" needs the class to be total in the continuum cylinder space (5c); there the space is generated by the same tuples, so totality holds by construction.
8.5. The skeleton's (U) (1.4) is (U$_\ell$) of (7d), stated on the sector of the class. Only the rate has to be uniform.
8.6. The skeleton's direction "(T) $\Rightarrow$ (S)" (1.2) is a sketch through spectral measures. Statement (3) proves it with Lemma A2 and no spectral theorem.
