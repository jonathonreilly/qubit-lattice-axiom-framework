## What a Wilson tiling measures

**PROVED (a commutator in the proposed tiling).** Use two embedded, edge-disjoint coarse square loops on a fixed straight spatial grid. Join their base points by a path using neither square's edges, and write $A=H_1$, $B=P H_2P^{-1}$. The observable in check 1279 is $D=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(ABA^{-1}B^{-1})$. In the physical-mesh limit of the straight-grid estimate cited above, its mean tends to $3/4$. Indeed in product Haar the square holonomies are independent Haar, even conditional on $P$. Write $A=a+u$, $B=b+v$ as unit quaternions. Then $AB-BA=2u\times v$, so $D=2|u\times v|^2$ by the chord-distance identity on the unit sphere. Haar is uniform on $S^3$, hence $\mathbb E uu^t=\mathbb E vv^t=I_3/4$. Independence gives $2(9/16-3/16)=3/4$. Since $0\le D\le2$, the limiting inferior of $\Pr(D\ge1/4)$ is at least $2/7$. Disjoint pairs with disjoint stems are independent in the limiting product-Haar law. These conclusions describe bare paths with specified transport, at fixed physical geometry. The same source's equation (4) removes low physical energy from the centered straight-grid observable.

**GUIDE.** One could still try to obtain a slow variable by using all parallel paths through a block, combining several directions, or attaching observables of its faces before measuring a commutator. The following estimate allows these operations simultaneously for a specified family of paths.

**PROVED (all columns of a tiled slice).** On an ordinary periodic Wilson spatial slice of even side $n$, let $h\mid n$. In each of its three directions partition *every fine coordinate line* into straight segments of $h$ bonds, starting at multiples of $h$. This gives $M=3n^3/h$ segment transports $B=(B_1,\ldots,B_M)$. In particular the transverse spacing of parallel paths may be one fine unit. Let $m=\lfloor(h-2)/8\rfloor\ge1$. A deterministic choice of at least $m$ private bonds in each segment has no two chosen bonds on one space-time plaquette.

**PROVED (choice of bonds).** Use positive orientations and take fine coordinates in $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$. Drop the first and last bond of each segment. Select a remaining bond in direction $i$ when the sum of the three coordinates of its initial vertex is $2(i-1)\pmod 8$. Each run of eight eligible starts gives one bond. Opposite parallel edges of a plaquette have starts of opposite parity, including across a periodic face since $n$ is even. On a nonwrapping plaquette the starts of perpendicular edges have coordinate sums differing by at most one, whereas different selected directions require distinct even residues. On a plaquette wrapping in one of its directions the bonds in that direction were dropped. A temporal plaquette has at most one spatial bond on the selected slice for time periods at least three. This proves the assertion even where segments in different directions meet.

**PROVED (conditional law).** Call the unselected spatial bonds of that slice $X$, and condition on *all* unselected bonds of the space-time cylinder. The selected bonds are then independent. The Wilson one-link staples have tilt of length at most $6\beta$. Equation (2) of `gap-solve2-astra62.md`, 17–32, based on the character identity (GL3) of Sussman–Alpoge, outline 2197–2231, gives a conditional transport density within $\eta=\sum_{d\ge2}d^2\exp[-(d-1)m/(1+6\beta)]$ of Haar in sup norm, separately for each segment. Distinct segments use disjoint selected bonds. Thus for $\eta<1$, the conditional joint density $p$ relative to product Haar $\nu$ satisfies
$$
 (1-\eta)^M\le p\le(1+\eta)^M,\qquad
 \|p-1\|_\infty\le\delta=(1+\eta)^M-1.                       \tag{1}
$$
The all-direction coloring for full windings and its application to charged eigenfunctions are already in `gap-solve9-astra62.md`, 342–360, equations (23)–(24). Here the paths are cut into block segments. The next estimate keeps the unselected bonds as dynamical arguments and applies also in the neutral sector.

**PROVED (which part of a dressed block variable can have low energy).** For a gauge-invariant slice function $F=F(X,B)$ set
$$
 A F(X)=\int F(X,b)\,d\nu(b),\qquad R=F-AF.
$$
Open segment transports transform at their two endpoints. Product Haar invariance therefore makes $AF$ gauge invariant. It also preserves centre-flip invariance when $F$ has it. Take $H=-\log(T/\lambda_0)$ in lattice units for the **magnetic sandwich** Wilson transfer, with normalized vacuum $\Omega$. For $\delta<1$ and any $E\ge0$,
$$
 \|{\bf1}_{[0,E]}(H) R\Omega\|
 \le e^E\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{1-\delta}}\|F\Omega\|.             \tag{2}
$$
In particular (2) controls the error of replacing $F$ by $AF$ before taking its low-energy projection, uniformly over the choice of $F$.

**PROVED (proof of (2)).** For bounded $F$ and a bounded gauge-invariant test $Y$ of the *entire* next slice, $X$ and $Y$ are known to the conditioning in (1). As $\nu R=0$, the absolute conditional mean of $R$ is at most $\delta(\int|R|^2d\nu)^{1/2}$. Haar orthogonal projection gives $\int|R|^2d\nu\le\int|F|^2d\nu$. The conditional Wilson second moment of $F$ is at least $(1-\delta)\int|F|^2d\nu$. Cauchy–Schwarz now gives on every sufficiently long periodic cylinder
$$
 |\mathbb E(\overline{R_0}Y_1)|\le
 \frac{\delta}{\sqrt{1-\delta}}(\mathbb E|F_0|^2)^{1/2}(\mathbb E|Y_1|^2)^{1/2}.
$$
Take the infinite-time limit at fixed spatial lattice. The elementary trace limit uses the simple positive top eigenvector of the magnetic sandwich and gives $\langle R\Omega,e^{-H}Y\Omega\rangle$ on the left. The slice norm is $L^2(\Omega^2dU)$. Because $\Omega>0$, the bounded tests $Y\Omega$ are dense in the gauge-invariant Hilbert space. Duality gives the norm bound on $e^{-H}R\Omega$ and spectral calculus gives (2). Truncation extends it to square-integrable $F$ by (1). The Wilson transfer construction is due to K. Osterwalder, E. Seiler and M. Lüscher, as rebuilt in `gap-transfer.md`, §§4, 6–7. For the trace see also Sussman–Alpoge (GL8), outline 2313–2335.

**PROVED (cutoff and scope).** Set $E=a_j E_{\rm phys}$ with fixed $E_{\rm phys}$. At fixed commensurate physical sides $L$ and $\ell>0$, take $n=L/a_j$, $h=\ell/a_j$. For $\beta_j=O(\log n)$, $M=3n^3/h$ grows polynomially while $\eta\le C\exp[-m/(1+6\beta_j)]$ once $m/(1+6\beta_j)\ge1$. Hence $\delta\to0$. The growth bound on $\beta_j$ follows *given* the first-passage drift and mesh of Sussman–Alpoge, outline 6844–6861, 20781–20801. If $F$ is a function of several directions, of many paths with endpoint connectors in $X$, or of axes chosen from those data, the part $F-AF$ obeys (2). In a dressing whose Haar average is a function of the face fields, this estimate leaves precisely that average to be tested for physical spectral weight. It gives no bound on general fluctuations inside a segment with specified total transport.

## Two ways to couple the periodic-block oscillators

**GUIDE.** Another reading of the owner's proposal keeps the Lie-algebra zero modes themselves as degrees of freedom. One precise test is to start with the checked Hamiltonian on each block and charge a nonnegative energy for mismatches across adjacent faces. The test below computes an excitation *above the coupled vacuum*. An inequality on the unshifted Hamiltonian would leave the extensive vacuum energy undetermined.

**PROVED (independent and exactly constrained arrays).** Write $r\ge2$ for the number of colour vectors per block, $d=3r$, and $V(c)=\sum_{i<j}|c_i\times c_j|^2$. Take $M=N^3$ blocks of side $\ell$. The one-block operator is
$$
 \ell H_1=-\tfrac{g^2}{2}\Delta_c+\tfrac1{2g^2}V(c).
$$
For independent blocks restricted to a singlet in *each* block, every excited tensor eigenstate places at least one block above its vacuum. A one-block excited singlet attains the resulting one-block lower bound. With only a simultaneous singlet projection, charged excitations may couple to singlets across blocks, so this product-of-singlets argument gives no equality for that projection.

**PROVED (constraining the coordinates before quantization).** In a common colour frame fix $c_x=c$ for all blocks, with the metric induced by the product kinetic energy. In the normalized common coordinate $Q=\sqrt M\,c$, the Euclidean kinetic metric is $dQ^2$ and $\sum_xV(c_x)=M^{-1}V(Q)$. The dilation $Q=M^{1/6}g^{2/3}q$ gives the simultaneous-singlet gap
$$
 \Delta_{\rm common}=\frac{\kappa_r g^{2/3}}{\ell M^{1/3}}
                    =\frac{\kappa_r g^{2/3}}{L},\qquad L=N\ell.             \tag{3}
$$
Here $\kappa_r$ is the singlet gap of $K_r=(-\Delta+V)/2$ from row 1273. All $r$ vectors survive and may be mutually noncommuting. Locking the *tuples* across distinct blocks differs from making the directions in one tuple parallel. The constrained submanifold has product Lebesgue measure zero. A spectral comparison with the unconstrained quantum system therefore requires a separate argument.

**PROVED (finite spring stiffness tending to infinity).** Fix a finite connected undirected graph of $M\ge2$ blocks, $g,\ell>0$. Give each unordered edge the energy $\sigma|c_x-c_y|^2/(2g^2\ell)$ for $\sigma>0$, using the same frame at each end. Take the Friedrichs Hamiltonian on the simultaneous colour singlets. If $\Delta_{M,\sigma}$ is its first excitation above its own vacuum, then
$$
 \lim_{\sigma g^{-4/3}\to\infty}\frac{\ell}{g^{2/3}}\Delta_{M,\sigma}
     =M^{-1/3}\kappa_r .                                                 \tag{4}
$$
Only the displayed combination of $\sigma,g$ enters the rescaled problem, so (4) also covers $g\downarrow0$ at fixed $\sigma$. The graph and $r$ are held fixed in the limit. This is a coupled oscillator theorem. In a Wilson tiling independent block frames would require parallel transport across interfaces, and the plaquette potential and Gauss law give other terms.

**PROVED (the common and relative coordinates).** Dilate $c_x=g^{2/3}y_x$, and divide energies by $g^{2/3}/\ell$. The resulting operator is
$$
 K_t=\tfrac12\sum_x[-\Delta_{y_x}+V(y_x)]
          +\tfrac t2\sum_{x\sim y}|y_x-y_y|^2,\qquad t=\sigma g^{-4/3},
$$
where each edge occurs once. It dominates the sum of independent compact-resolvent oscillators, so it too has compact resolvent. Make an orthogonal change to $Q=M^{-1/2}\sum_xy_x$ and the orthogonal complement. In the latter rescale to $z_x=t^{1/4}(y_x-Q/\sqrt M)$, so $\sum_xz_x=0$. Let $F_\perp=(-\Delta_z+\sum_{x\sim y}|z_x-z_y|^2)/2$ in that Euclidean subspace. If $0=\lambda_1<\lambda_2\le\cdots\le\lambda_M$ are the graph Laplacian eigenvalues, diagonalization gives a unique invariant Gaussian ground vector $\phi$, energy $e_*=\tfrac d2\sum_{k=2}^M\sqrt{\lambda_k}$, and gap $\gamma=\sqrt{\lambda_2}>0$. Exactly, after the unitary changes of variables,
$$
 K_t-\sqrt t\,e_*=-\tfrac12\Delta_Q+\sqrt t(F_\perp-e_*)+W_t,qquad
 W_t=\tfrac12\sum_x V(Q/\sqrt M+t^{-1/4}z_x).                           \tag{5}
$$

**PROVED (uniform confinement of the common coordinate at fixed graph).** Put $s_r=\sqrt{(r-1)/2}$. The checked inequality $K_r\ge s_r\sum_i|y_i|$ gives
$(-b^2\Delta_y+V(y))/2\ge b s_r\sum_i|y_i|$ for $b>0$, by the dilation $y=b^{1/3}w$. At fixed $z$ split $-\Delta_Q/2$ equally among the $M$ terms of $W_t$. In the $x$th term use $y=Q/\sqrt M+t^{-1/4}z_x$. Its kinetic coefficient is then $1/(2M^2)$, so $b=1/M$. Summing and using $\sum_xz_x=0$ yields
$$
 -\tfrac12\Delta_Q+W_t\ \ge\ \frac{s_r}{M}\sum_{x,i}|Q_i/\sqrt M+t^{-1/4}z_{x,i}|
                  \ \ge\ \frac{s_r}{\sqrt M}\sum_i|Q_i|.                 \tag{6}
$$
The shifts may have any size. This bound prevents low states from escaping to infinity along the commuting valleys as $t$ grows.

**PROVED (spectral limit with multiplicities).** The limiting operator in the Gaussian relative ground space is $K_{\rm c}=(-\Delta_Q+M^{-1}V(Q))/2$. For its upper eigenvalue bounds take compactly supported smooth simultaneous singlets $v(Q)$ and trial vectors $v(Q)\phi(z)$. Such $v$ form a core in the singlet form domain by cutoff, smoothing and group averaging. In (5) the relative term vanishes, and the potential expectation converges to that of $V(Q)/(2M)$ by polynomial expansion and finite Gaussian moments. This is uniform on any fixed finite-dimensional trial space. The min–max principle gives the upper limit for each eigenvalue.

**PROVED (lower eigenvalue bounds).** If unit vectors $u_t$ have bounded expectation of (5) for $t\to\infty$, then their $Q$ derivatives are bounded and their squared norm orthogonal to $\phi$ in $z$ is at most $C/(\gamma\sqrt t)$. By (6) their tails at $|Q|>R$ have squared norm at most $C\sqrt M/(s_r R)$. The coefficients $v_t(Q)=\langle\phi,u_t(Q,\cdot)\rangle$ inherit the derivative and tail bounds. Rellich compactness on a ball, followed by this tail bound, gives a subsequence $u_t\to v(Q)\phi(z)$ strongly in $L^2$. The limit is a singlet if the sequence is. The $Q$ kinetic energy is lower semicontinuous. On each compact $(Q,z)$ set, $W_t\to V(Q)/(2M)$ uniformly. Its nonnegativity permits exhaustion by such sets in the lower bound for the energy. Hence the limiting form of $v$ is at most the limiting inferior of (5). Apply this to the span of the first $k$ orthonormal eigenvectors along a subsequence realizing the lower limit of the $k$th level. The upper bound already proved bounds all their energies. Pass to a common subsequence for the $k$ vectors. Their limits remain orthonormal, and every unit linear combination has limiting form at most that lower limit. Min–max proves the reverse eigenvalue bound. The dilation $Q=M^{1/6}q$ gives $K_{\rm c}\simeq M^{-1/3}K_r$. Subtracting the first two singlet levels cancels $\sqrt t e_*$ and proves (4).

**PROVED (failure of a volume-independent fraction of the block gap).** For any proposed fixed fraction $b>0$, first choose an integer $N$ with $1/N<b$, and then use (4) on the fixed $N^3$ grid. At sufficiently large but finite $t$ its singlet gap is less than $b\kappa_r g^{2/3}/\ell$. This also holds with $\sigma=1$ and $g$ sufficiently small depending on $N$. No interchange of the large-graph and large-stiffness limits is used.

**PROVED (opposite answers to the one-box thermal test).** Fix $g,\ell,r$ with $\kappa_r g^{2/3}<512\log2$. In this paragraph the thermal time is the physical time $L/512$. For independent blocks the excited trace on simultaneous singlets tends to zero as $N\to\infty$ at fixed $g,\ell,r$. To see the level-count bound, the full one-block operator obeys $2K_r\ge-\Delta/2+s_r\sum_i|y_i|$. The operator on the right has a finite heat trace at every positive time. To verify this, divide $\mathbb R^d$ into unit cubes, use Neumann bracketing and on each cube replace the potential by its infimum. The free Neumann unit-cube trace is a finite product of Gaussian sums, and the sum over cubes of the exponentials of the negative infima converges. Min–max gives the required heat trace for $K_r$. Write $s(v)$ for its full vacuum-subtracted trace of excitations at dimensionless time $v$. For $v\ge1$ it is at most $s(1)e^{-(v-1)d_r}$, with full one-block gap $d_r>0$. The simultaneous-singlet trace excess is bounded above by $(1+s(Ng^{2/3}/512))^{N^3}-1$, which tends to zero.

**PROVED.** Choose a finite $N$ large enough that the independent array passes the $1/2$ test strictly. Keep the same $N,g,\ell,r$. In the sufficiently stiff array one singlet excitation alone contributes $\exp[-L\Delta_{M,\sigma}/512]>1/2$ by (4). Both behaviors also occur at strictly positive finite stiffness. As $t\downarrow0$, min–max gives convergence of each singlet level to its value at zero by using compactly supported smooth singlet trials for the upper bound and positivity of the spring for the lower bound. The ground level stays bounded, and the heat trace at $t=0$ gives a summable dominator for the normalized weights for small $t$. Thus the strict pass persists for small positive $t$. This comparison holds at the same local coupling and with the same quartic operator on every block. It disproves a thermal carry based solely on the one-block theorem and positivity of the interaction that joins neighboring tuples.

## Where the neutral trace goes under the Wilson block measurement

**PROVED (a trace estimate including multiplicities).** Return to the actual positive Wilson transfer in (2). In the vacuum slice law $\pi=\Omega^2 dU$ let $P=T/\lambda_0$ in ground-state coordinates, so $P(f)=\Omega^{-1}(T/\lambda_0)(\Omega f)$. Let $\mathcal H_0$ be the gauge-invariant, centre-invariant subspace in these coordinates, including the vacuum $1$. Write $Z=(X,B)$ and $P_D$ for conditional expectation onto the slice data $D=X$ or $Z$ under $\pi$. Both data sets transform under site gauges and centre flips, so these projections commute with projection to $\mathcal H_0$. For $k\ge1$ put
$$
 \Theta_D=\|P_D P^k|_{\mathcal H_0}\|_{\rm HS}^2,\qquad
 J=\|(I-P_Z)P^k|_{\mathcal H_0}\|_{\rm HS}^2 .
$$
The Hilbert–Schmidt norms are finite at fixed lattice, since $T$ is trace class. Whenever $\delta<1$, the trace at time $2k$ satisfies
$$
 \Theta_X+J\ \le\ \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H_0}P^{2k}
       \ \le\ \left(1+\frac{4\delta^2}{1-\delta}\right)\Theta_X+J .       \tag{7}
$$
There is no factor counting the number of excited states in the relative error. The full-trace mixing identity on the centre quotient is Lemma G3 of Claude's `gap-glue-max.md`, 122–125. Here the calculation concerns the additional trace captured by the directional measurements even when their unselected links are revealed.

**PROVED (conditional-density proof).** Observe $Y$, the complete gauge and centre orbit at time $k$, in the stationary vacuum chain. Denote its law by $\pi_Y$. For $D=X$ or $Z$ let $q_D(d,y)$ be the density of $(D_0,Y)$ relative to $\pi_D\otimes\pi_Y$. The kernel mapping $f(Y)$ to $\mathbb E(f(Y)\mid D_0)$ is $q_D$ and equals the restriction of $P_D P^k$ under the identification $L^2(\pi_Y)=\mathcal H_0$. Hence $\Theta_D=\int q_D^2\,d\pi_D\,d\pi_Y$. Existence follows also by projecting the continuous full finite-lattice transition kernel. For clarity, (1) passes to the vacuum cylinder as inequalities of measures against arbitrary nonnegative tests of $(X,B,Y)$. One can first take continuous tests, use the finite-cylinder weak limit (averaging site gauges on each observed slice gives the same integral), and then extend by measure domination for the joint law and its $(X,Y)$ marginal. Consequently the densities $p_{x,y}$ of $B\mid X=x,Y=y$ and $p_x$ of $B\mid X=x$, both relative to $\nu$, lie in $[1-\delta,1+\delta]$ almost everywhere in their conditioning laws. Disintegration gives
$$
 \Theta_Z=\int q_X(x,y)^2
   \left(1+\int\frac{(p_{x,y}-p_x)^2}{p_x}\,d\nu\right)d\pi_X(x)\,d\pi_Y(y).
$$
This follows from $q_Z=q_X p_{x,y}/p_x$ and the fact that both densities integrate to one. The inner extra integral is bounded by $4\delta^2/(1-\delta)$. Orthogonality gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal H_0}P^{2k}=\Theta_Z+J$, proving (7).

**PROVED (an omitted neutral variable in the Wilson law).** Take $h$ large enough to have two selected bonds in one segment. On an elementary spatial plaquette through the first bond, the other three bonds lie in $X$ by the coloring. Its normalized trace is neutral. Vary the first selected matrix while changing a second selected matrix of the same segment to preserve its total transport. The fine-plaquette trace varies and $(X,B)$ stays fixed. This is also a statement in $L^2(\pi)$. In product Haar coordinates one can use the segment product in place of its last selected bond, leaving independent Haar coordinates on the other selected bonds at fixed $Z$. The same conditional laws for $\pi$ have strictly positive densities because the finite-volume ground function is continuous and positive. The conditional variance of the plaquette trace at fixed $Z$ is positive. Thus $I-P_Z$ has nonzero neutral range. For $\beta>0$ the single-link electric convolution has strictly positive Fourier multipliers in (GL3). The magnetic factors are strictly positive and gauge projection acts as the identity on the gauge-invariant subspace after commuting it to the end. It follows that $P^k$ is injective with dense range on $\mathcal H_0$. In particular $J>0$ at finite cutoff. This argument supplies no lower bound on $J$ uniform in $k,n$.

**OPEN (the remaining Wilson estimate).** At $2k$ of order $n/512$, (1) and (7) make the additional trace from the segment transports vanish relative to $\Theta_X$ on the physical mesh. The two uncontrolled contributions to the excited trace are $\Theta_X-1$, from the unselected fields, and $J$, from the full slices after their projection onto $(X,B)$ is removed. A bound on their sum with a strict margin below $1/2$ at one admissible physical side, uniform in the cutoff, would suffice in (7). Disjoint block gaps, the positive probability of a transported commutator, and the oscillator limit (4) supply neither quantity. One can take $2k=2\lfloor n/1024\rfloor$ for the sufficiency statement for all large $n$.

## Letting the transports between blocks fluctuate

**GUIDE.** Equation (4) tests common-frame stitching. To test whether the energy itself selects the transports, give each edge of the same graph a compact link $U_{xy}$ and use $R_{xy}=\operatorname{Ad}U_{xy}$ to compare its endpoints. The rotation axes of individual blocks are now gauge dependent. The following bound concerns a specified loop product in the *dynamical* reduced model, uniformly in the size of its graph.

**PROVED (an explicitly gauged array).** Let the finite connected simple graph have maximum degree at most $b$, $b\ge1$, with unit edge weights. Use the dimensionless variables of (5) and the Hamiltonian
$$
 \mathcal K_t=\tfrac12\sum_x[-\Delta_{y_x}+V(y_x)]
       +\tfrac t2\sum_{xy,i}|y_{x,i}-R_{xy}y_{y,i}|^2
       +\alpha\sum_e(-\Delta_{U_e}) .                                  \tag{8}
$$
Each undirected edge appears once. Here $\alpha>0$ is fixed, each link has Haar measure with the round bi-invariant metric, and states are invariant under site gauges. Set $D_R$ on one colour vector per site by $\langle z,D_Rz\rangle=\sum_{xy}|z_x-R_{xy}z_y|^2$, and let $D_I$ denote the same operator with identity transports on the $3M$-dimensional space. Put $e_I=(r/2)\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{D_I}$. For $t\ge1$ and the ground energy $E(t)$ of (8),
$$
 0\le\frac{E(t)/\sqrt t-e_I}{M}\le C_{b,r}(1+\alpha)t^{-1/6}.           \tag{9}
$$
For any collection $\mathcal C$ of distinct directed, rooted closed walks of a fixed length $s\ge1$ in the graph, the ground-state expectation satisfies
$$
 \frac1M\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal C}\langle 3-\operatorname{Tr}R_\gamma\rangle
       \le C_{b,r,s}(1+\alpha)t^{-1/6}.                                \tag{10}
$$
In particular one may take every elementary square with a chosen orientation and root in a cubic tiling. At finite graph size, compactness follows because the form dominates the sum of the compact-resolvent onsite oscillators and compact-group Laplacians, whose product eigenbasis has only finitely many levels below any finite energy.

**PROVED (energy cost for holonomy).** At fixed $U$ the harmonic part in $y$ has bottom $\sqrt t\,e(R)$, $e(R)=(r/2)\operatorname{Tr}\sqrt{D_R}$. Diagonalizing $D_R$ gives a free coordinate of energy infimum zero for each zero eigenvalue. Thus (8) dominates this multiplication operator as a form. The positivity comparison for a closed walk is the same heat-kernel method used for flat torus phases in `gap-solve23-astra62.md`, 84–88. Here it gives, for arbitrary transports,
$$
 e(R)-e_I\ge
 \frac{r\,\Gamma(s-1/2)}{4\sqrt\pi\ s!\, b^{s-1/2}}
 \sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal C}(3-\operatorname{Tr}R_\gamma).              \tag{11}
$$
Indeed $bI-D_R$ has nonnegative scalar stays $b-\deg x$ and one orthogonal transport per edge step. Expand $e^{-uD_R}=e^{-bu}\exp(u(bI-D_R))$. In the trace each closed walk has a nonnegative scalar weight times its rotation trace, at most three. In the untwisted trace the corresponding trace is three.
