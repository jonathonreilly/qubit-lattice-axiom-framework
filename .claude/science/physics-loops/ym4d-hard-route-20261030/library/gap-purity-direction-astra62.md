**GUESSED — An estimate on a specified sampling rule.** Order the plaquettes by increasing maximum norm of their basepoint in lattice units. Break ties in lexicographic order, then in fixed orientation order. Give each face an independent uniform variable. In order, take its energy $X_p$ from the inverse conditional distribution under $\nu_j$ given the earlier energies. Let $\mathcal B_R(p)$ contain the uniforms on faces within physical maximum-norm distance $R$ of $p$. At arbitrarily late $j$, ask for a common $\kappa>0$ and finite $A_j$ such that
$$
\sup_p\|X_p-\mathbb E[X_p\mid\mathcal B_R(p)]\|_2
   \le A_j e^{-\kappa R}\qquad(R\ge0). \tag{2}
$$
The open Wilson estimate is the error from discarding the distant uniforms of this particular map.

**PROVED — Payment into Stage 2.** Induction on the finite sampling prefixes gives the exact joint face law. At each fixed $j$ the energies are bounded. Replace each factor of a local polynomial by its conditional mean on its own ball. Telescoping the product bounds the $L^2$ error by a finite constant times $A_j e^{-\kappa R}$. Finite sums, smears and deterministic centre subtractions preserve the bound. Let $b_{Z,j}$ cover the time extent of the reflected and translated supports. At $t>b_{Z,j}$ take $R=(t-b_{Z,j})/3$. The two approximations use disjoint uniforms, so their covariance is zero. Translation keeps the coefficient and support bounds. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $|U_j(Z,t)|\le C_{Z,j}e^{-\kappa t/3}$. Enlarge the constant for the finitely many earlier odd times. This proves ExpClusters with $m=\kappa/3$. A spectator absent from the measured fields drops out of this proof. **PROVED — Strictness and the cheapest rejection.** In a stationary scalar comparison put one independent variable, equally likely 0 or 2, on each time plane. Give every site in the plane that same value. Local tests on disjoint time intervals are independent, so every fixed test has zero truncated correlation for all sufficiently late times. At arbitrary spatial distance on one plane the covariance is one. The disjoint-ball proof in space rules out (2) for *any* encoding. This comparison is anisotropic. For the prescribed Wilson encoding, resample just the first face’s uniform. When the ball about $p$ misses that face,
$$
\tfrac12\,\mathbb E|X_p-X_p'|^2
 =\mathbb E\operatorname{Var}(X_p\mid\hbox{all other uniforms})
 \le \|X_p-\mathbb E[X_p\mid\mathcal B_R(p)]\|_2^2 .
$$
The equality follows by conditional independence of the two choices. The inequality follows by conditioning on more variables. A root-mean-square response that decays slower than every physical exponential excludes (2) at that $j$. To exclude an unbounded subsequence one needs failures on all sufficiently late eligible $j$. A small response at the first face alone leaves the combined error from all distant uniforms open.
