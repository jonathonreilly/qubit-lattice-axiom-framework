## Part 2. Three ways to test the architecture on the Wilson law

**ARGUED.** I fixed three objects before opening the transfer rows or the registry. They are (i) a family of singlet pairings on the six spatial ends at a vertex, tested in two replicas, (ii) the law of a product of commutators of two transverse loops, and (iii) the two possible orientations of three based SU(2) loops with given scalar and pair traces. Each gives a specific measurement different from the original trace quotient. The full cutoff set and the size of the box stay those of the consumer. Independent Haar variables appear only in a solvable toy model or a specified reference comparison.

### The common measurement

**PROVED (trace identities).** Work at one of the stated cutoffs. Write $A=P_0T^u$, $Z=\operatorname{Tr}A$ and $\rho=A/Z$. A spatial centre cut meets each contractible plaquette evenly. In the temporal kernel its two simultaneous signs cancel. Thus each cut commutes with $T$. Averaging the eight elements of that group gives a self-adjoint projection. Since $u$ is even, $B=P_0T^{u/2}$ satisfies $B^*B=BB^*=A$. On a finite lattice $B$ has a continuous positive kernel on the compact slice with Haar probability $dx$. This follows by composition and finite averaging from the half-weights and the positive temporal integral in `Transfer.lean` 548–564, 584–589, 666–671. In particular $0<Z=\int\|B(\cdot,x)\|^2dx<\infty$ by Parseval applied to the Hilbert–Schmidt kernel. This is the basis trace in the definition. Null spaces off the physical sector contribute zero.

**PROVED.** The eigenvalues $p_i$ of $\rho$ are nonnegative and sum to one. Hence

$$
 \operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^2\rho)=\sum_{i<j}p_ip_j
 =\tfrac12(1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2).
$$

The target is equivalent to a two-copy antisymmetric weight at most $84/625$. Another exact reading uses the half slab. Put $v_x=B(\cdot,x)/\|B(\cdot,x)\|$, $R_x=|v_x\rangle\langle v_x|$ and $d\pi(x)=\|B(\cdot,x)\|^2dx/Z$. Integration gives $\mathbb E_\pi R_x=BB^*/Z=\rho$ and $\|R_x\|_{\rm HS}=1$. Thus the variance of $R_x$ is $1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2$. For a reversible proposal $Q$ on $\pi$ with scalar Poincaré gap $\gamma>0$, put

$$
 D_Q=\int [1-|\langle v_x,v_y\rangle|^2]\,\pi(dx)Q(x,dy).
 \qquad 1-\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2\le D_Q/\gamma. \tag{1}
$$

Indeed the integrand is $\|R_x-R_y\|_{\rm HS}^2/2$. Apply the scalar inequality coordinate by coordinate in a real orthonormal basis of self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt operators and sum the nonnegative terms. These are bookkeeping identities and a conditional bound. To estimate either side one needs a specified family under the Wilson law.

**ARGUED (scales used below).** Fix an integer $s$ beyond the physical range controlled by the account and choose, for example, $\ell=1024 b s$ with an integer $b\ge 2$ for packing the marked squares. It is independent of the release block factor $L$. A square of physical side $s$ then has side $sL^k$ bonds and fits inside lattice time $u=2bsL^k$. No change of $\beta_k$ is allowed. Numbers such as $1/16$ below are proposed error budgets, chosen with slack against $84/625$ or $168/625$. They are not measured constants.

### A. A family of six-end singlets for the antisymmetric trace

**ARGUED (object and two bounds).** Use three based spatial square loops with side $sL^k$. At their common corner take directions $(+1,+2),(-2,+3),(-3,-1)$, with the signs denoting coordinate directions. The three loops have disjoint edges and use the six incident ends. Under slice Haar their holonomies are independent because each loop contains a private Haar link. Multiplication by its other fixed links preserves Haar. The remaining gauge action at the base conjugates all three. All loops are contractible, hence neutral. Let $W$ be the space of simultaneous-conjugation-invariant fundamental matrix coefficients, one fundamental matrix on each loop. The upper estimate costs only positive two-vector Gram minors of the Wilson matrix on $W$. The competing lower estimate is an exact frame identity on a five-dimensional space. Testing one pairing alone sees only one direction.

**PROVED (first estimate, proof in Part 4, Lemma A).** There are 15 unit routing vectors $v_i$ in $W$. With $A_W$ the compression of $A$, put

$$
 D_{ij}(A)=\langle v_i,A v_i\rangle\langle v_j,A v_j\rangle-
             |\langle v_i,A v_j\rangle|^2 .
$$

Then $D_{ij}\ge0$ and $\sum_{i<j}D_{ij}=9\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^2 A_W)$. In particular this counts rank in the actual slice inner product. It needs no integer spacing in any Wilson trace.

**GUESSED (Wilson test).** For a fixed $s,\ell$ as above, at infinitely many calibrated $k$, on every cube and every translate of the marked triple, test

$$
 \sum_{i<j}D_{ij}(A)\ \le\ \frac9{16}\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}A_W\bigr)^2. \tag{2}
$$

Zero trace gives a vacuous test and must be recorded. The measurement also records $\operatorname{Tr}A_W/Z$. The comparison cancels two powers of the same local intensity, including their linear logarithmic cost. It asks whether an *occupied* six-end channel loses information through a physical slab. ### C. The two orientations of three loops

**PROVED (discrete object).** Write the three petal holonomies as unit quaternions $U_i=(a_i,v_i)$ with $v_i\in\mathbb R^3$. Simultaneous conjugation rotates all $v_i$ by the same proper rotation. Scalar traces give $a_i$. Pair traces give $v_i\cdot v_j=a_i a_j-\tfrac12\operatorname{Tr}(U_iU_j)$. Write $G=(v_i\cdot v_j)$ and $w=\det(v_1,v_2,v_3)$. Then $w^2=\det G$. When $\det G>0$ the given scalar and pair traces leave precisely two conjugation orbits. The unique orthogonal map between two triples of the given Gram matrix has determinant fixed by their signs, and SU(2) covers $SO(3)$. Reflection of $v_3$ in the span of $v_1,v_2$ exchanges the signs. A private link in the third petal makes this a Haar-preserving involution of the slice. Replace that link by the third holonomy as coordinate and keep the other links fixed. Use gauge-fixed coordinates based at the common vertex. The reflection is equivariant for the residual rotations. All three petals are neutral. On the degenerate set use the identity.

**ARGUED (analogue of the lift).** Use the two-element orbit of this involution as a fibre. Keep its quotient coordinate $z$ and all remaining links. Write $x=(z,\pm)$ on a two-element orbit. Slice Haar gives its two points equal conditional weight. Along successive time cuts, hold the quotient path $h$ fixed and sum over the interior signs. The exact result is a product of positive $2\times2$ Wilson kernel matrices $K_i$, including the conditional Haar factors. This is a finite sum for a genuine part of the integral, with no change of action. The $P_0$-averaged kernel also composes on the neutral sector. Fubini then integrates over $h$. The sign exists because there are three independent spatial planes at a $3+1$ dimensional corner and three imaginary quaternion coordinates. For U(1) all three vectors are collinear and the fibre has one point. This test is void on its photon states.

**PROVED (first estimate, Lemma C).** For a strictly positive $2\times2$ matrix $K=(k_{ij})$ put

$$
 q(K)=\tanh\!\left(\tfrac14\left|\log\frac{k_{++}k_{--}}{k_{+-}k_{-+}}\right|\right).
$$

Positive row and column factors cancel exactly. For a fixed path $H_h=K_1\cdots K_m$, $q(H_h)\le\prod_i q(K_i)$. Thus a path can lose orientation at every cut while arbitrarily large endpoint normalizations cancel from its contraction rate. ## Part 4. Ranked measurements and their first lemmas

### 1. C — Compare endpoint biases on the two orientation sheets

**ARGUED.** This ranks first because a term in the actual Wilson action gives the coupling of the two signs explicitly. The first open estimate bounds the spread of endpoint weights after conditioning. It can fail even when every individual sign path contracts. It needs no estimate of the full spectrum to formulate or refute.

**PROVED, Lemma C (a sign path in the Wilson slab).** Fix a four-dimensional periodic Wilson cube, a half slab of integer thickness $r=u/2\ge1$, three petals as in A, and one private spatial edge $e$ of the third petal. Number the $r+1$ cuts from 1. Keep all spatial links other than $e$ at each cut. From $e$ keep the scalar part of the third loop holonomy and its vector projected into the plane of the first two vectors. Fix all temporal links along the path. One may also fix the neutral centre twist and sum over it at the end. Put

$$
 h_i=\frac{|\det(v_{1,i},v_{2,i},v_{3,i})|}{|v_{1,i}\times v_{2,i}|},\qquad 0\le h_i\le1 .
$$

Work off the degenerate orbits, which have slice Haar measure zero by independence and absolute continuity. For any such quotient history the positive sign transfer is a product. Write $J_i=\tfrac14\log[(K_i)_{++}(K_i)_{--}/((K_i)_{+-}(K_i)_{-+})]$ at the $i$th fine step. Then

$$
 |J_i|\le \beta h_i h_{i+1},\qquad
 q(K_1\cdots K_r)\le \prod_{i=1}^r|\tanh J_i|
 \le \exp\!\left[-2\sum_{i=1}^r e^{-2\beta h_i h_{i+1}}\right].                 \tag{4}
$$

In particular the observable $\sum_i e^{-2\beta h_i h_{i+1}}\ge\log 4$ suffices for $q\le1/16$ on that history.

**PROVED (Wilson calculation).** With the other links fixed, trading $U_e$ for the third loop holonomy is a left and right multiplication, possibly with inversion. Each is an isometry of the unit quaternion sphere. Reflection changes the vector part by length $2h_i$. Write $U_i^+,U_i^-$ for the corresponding two values of the **private link**. Every spatial term in the action sees a single time. Exactly one temporal plaquette from cut $i$ to cut $i+1$ reads this edge at both cuts. Its factor is $\exp(\beta\, U_i^\sigma\cdot O_i U_{i+1}^\tau)$, with an orthogonal $O_i$ fixed by the two temporal links and conventions. Thus $4J_i=\beta(U_i^+-U_i^-)\cdot O_i(U_{i+1}^+-U_{i+1}^-)$. All one-cut factors cancel. In three spatial dimensions four spatial plaquettes meet $e$. The magnitude of their combined contribution to half the one-cut log-weight difference is at most $4\beta h_i$. In four spatial dimensions the corresponding count is six. Haar disintegration assigns the two signs weight $1/2$ before action weights. The centre cut can be placed off the marked petals. Summing the sign paths therefore uses the stated matrices with positive weights.

**PROVED (matrix calculation).** For any strictly positive $K=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ the map on log odds is $f_K(s)=\log[(ae^s+b)/(ce^s+d)]$. Set $x=e^s$. The absolute derivative is $|ad-bc|/(acx+ad+bc+bd/x)$. Minimizing the denominator gives

$$
 \sup_s |f'_K(s)|=\frac{|ad-bc|}{(\sqrt{ad}+\sqrt{bc})^2}
 =\tanh\!\left(\frac{|\log(ad/bc)|}{4}\right).
$$

Since $f_{KL}=f_K\circ f_L$, the constants multiply as upper bounds. For $t>0$, put $z=e^{-2t}$. The derivative of $\log((1+z)/(1-z))$ is $2/(1-z^2)\ge2$ on $[0,1)$, so $\tanh t\le\exp(-2e^{-2t})$. The case $t=0$ has zero contraction. This proves (4).

**PROVED, Lemma C (integration with exceptional paths).** Fix an initial two-point orbit $z$. For every terminal quotient $z'$ use the same positive measure on interior histories in both initial rows. Let $H_h$ be their positive $2\times2$ product and let $l_h$ be the mean of its two terminal log row ratios. Suppose $0\le q_0<1$, $\delta\ge0$, $0\le\eta<1$. For each $z'$ take a good set on which $q(H_h)\le q_0$ and $|l_h-l(z)|\le\delta$ for one common real $l(z)$. At almost every terminal state $y=(z',t)$ suppose the omitted paths carry at most fraction $\eta$ of the full integral of $(H_h)_{\sigma t}$, **for each** $\sigma$. Then, for the normalized actual columns,

$$
 1-|\langle v_{z,+},v_{z,-}\rangle|^2
 \le \tanh^2(D/2),\qquad
 D=2\delta+4\operatorname{artanh}q_0+2\log(1/(1-\eta)).                 \tag{5}
$$

For $\delta=1/16,\ q_0=1/16,\ \eta=1/64$ the right hand side is at most $1/16$.

**PROVED (proof).** The difference of the two terminal log ratios of $H_h$ has magnitude $4\operatorname{artanh}q(H_h)$. Each good ratio lies between $\exp(l(z)-\delta-2\operatorname{artanh}q_0)$ and $\exp(l(z)+\delta+2\operatorname{artanh}q_0)$. A ratio of positive integrals over the good set is a weighted mean of these ratios. If the actual bad fractions at $y$ are $\eta_+(y),\eta_-(y)$, restoring the bad paths multiplies the good ratio by $(1-\eta_-(y))/(1-\eta_+(y))$. Thus $r(y)=B(z,+;y)/B(z,-;y)$ lies in $[a,b]$ with $\log(b/a)\le D$. Symmetry of $B$ identifies its rows and columns. Under the probability proportional to $B(z,-;y)^2dy$, the squared overlap is $(\mathbb E r)^2/\mathbb E r^2$. With $m=\mathbb E r$, the bound $(r-a)(r-b)\le0$ gives $\mathbb E r^2\le(a+b)m-ab$. The square $((a+b)m-2ab)^2\ge0$ gives $(\mathbb E r)^2/\mathbb E r^2\ge4ab/(a+b)^2$. This proves (5). For the numbers stated, $\operatorname{artanh}(1/16)\le1/15$ and $\log(64/63)\le1/63$, hence $D\le1/8+4/15+2/63<1/2$ and $\tanh(D/2)\le1/4$.

**PROVED (why this is distinct from purity).** Take two equally weighted products $aa^\mathsf{T}$ and $bb^\mathsf{T}$ with $a=(1,t), b=(t,1)$ and $0<t<1$. Each has $q=0$. Their sum has diagonal $1+t^2$ and off-diagonal $2t$. Its two normalized rows become orthogonal as $t\downarrow0$. Correlation of endpoint biases across histories can undo arbitrarily good contraction. Conversely take a product of the passing binary toy and an unseen identity of rank $M$. Its orientation statistics agree with the binary toy at every cut, while its purity is $65/(81M)$. The same conclusion holds with a strictly positive spectator kernel approaching the identity in fixed finite dimension. These examples separate the measurement from the seed.

**GUESSED (first physical decision).** Test (4) and the common-centre and two bad-mass bounds of (5) on a half slab of length $u/2$, at a fixed physical $s$ and infinitely many common calibrated cutoffs. A useful first falsifier is a substantial *terminal-weighted* population of paths with disjoint intervals for $l_h$. The whole-slice version of the test would lose its separate meaning. ### 2. A — Measure the minors of a complete singlet frame and its translates

**ARGUED.** This ranks second. The sign test measures one fibre. The frame test sees every pair in an explicitly embedded five-dimensional sector. Its first limitation has an exact count. **PROVED, Lemma A.** Let $V=\mathbb C^2$ with its usual SU(2) action. For each of the 15 perfect matchings of six labelled ends, put a normalized singlet $(e_+\otimes e_- - e_-\otimes e_+)/\sqrt2$ on its three pairs, in any fixed orientation. Write $v_i$ for the resulting unit vectors. On $W_0=(V^{\otimes6})^{\rm SU(2)}$,

$$
 \dim W_0=5,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^{15}|v_i\rangle\langle v_i|=3I_{W_0}.
$$

For every positive bounded $A$ on any Hilbert space containing this sector isometrically, its compression $A_W$ obeys (2)'s exact readout $\sum_{i<j}D_{ij}=9\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^2 A_W)$. Nonnegativity of $D_{ij}$ holds even when the two vectors are dependent.

**PROVED (dimension and normalization).** Let $H,E,F$ be the weight, raising and lowering matrices on $V$, with $H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$, $Ee_-=e_+$, $Ee_+=0$, $F=E^*$. Sum each generator over the six tensor positions. Then $[E,F]=H$. Weight zero has dimension $\binom63=20$ and weight two has dimension $\binom62=15$. On weight two, $\|Fw\|^2=\|Ew\|^2+2\|w\|^2$. Thus $F$ is injective there and $E$ maps weight zero onto weight two. On weight zero $\|Ev\|=\|Fv\|$, so the kernel on that weight is killed by both. These generators span the complexified Lie algebra. Since SU(2) is connected, this kernel is precisely $W_0$, of dimension five.

**PROVED.** Superpose two matchings. Their union consists of $c$ alternating cycles, counting a doubled edge as one cycle. Each cycle allows two assignments of opposite basis labels on its adjacent ends. Flipping them changes the sign of an even number of singlet factors in the inner product. The $2^c$ assignments therefore have the same sign and the absolute overlap is $2^{c-3}$. Relative to one fixed matching, one matching coincides, $3\cdot2=6$ share exactly one edge, and the other eight share none. The corresponding $c$ are $3,2,1$. For the frame operator $S$, $\operatorname{Tr}S=15$ and $\operatorname{Tr}S^2=15(1+6/4+8/16)=45$. Thus $\|S-3I\|_{\rm HS}^2=45-90+45=0$. In particular, write $t=\operatorname{Tr}A_W>0$. If two orthonormal vectors in $W$ have diagonal readings at least $t/3$ and an absolute cross-reading at most $t/6$, their minor is at least $t^2/12$. Completing this pair to a basis gives $\sum D_{ij}\ge9t^2/12>9t^2/16$. This is an explicit competing lower bound that would refute (2) without any integer-valued observable.

**PROVED (physical embedding and price of translations).** Let $n>4h$, with $h=sL^k$ a positive integer, and fix the three oriented square petals of side $h$. Schur averaging gives $\int U_{ij}\overline{U_{kl}}\,dU=\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}/2$ by averaging $U|j\rangle\langle l|U^*$ and using its trace. The products of three $\sqrt2 U_{ij}$, one per petal, are consequently orthonormal over the three independent Haar holonomies. Simultaneous conjugation acts on three copies of $V\otimes V^*$. The determinant form gives a unitary equivariant identification $V^*\simeq V$. This embeds $W_0$ as the claimed neutral $W$ on the actual slice. In this paragraph write $W_x$ for the translate to vertex $x$ and $P_x$ for its orthogonal projector. The union of the three boundaries has precisely one vertex of degree six, namely $x$. Distinct translates have different edge sets with the same cardinality. An edge belonging to just one set occurs there once in a fundamental factor. Haar integration over it gives zero, while every function from the other packet is independent of it. Hence $W_x\perp W_y$ for $x\ne y$. With $P=\sum_xP_x$ and any positive trace-one translation-invariant $\rho$,

$$
 \operatorname{Tr}(P_x\rho)=n^{-3}\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho)\le n^{-3}.          \tag{6}
$$

This applies to the periodic Wilson operator. Translating a centre cut gives the same operator on the gauge-invariant space because the two cut actions differ by a vertex gauge on the strip between them. Equivalently, gauge project first.

**PROVED (what a successful family would measure).** On $P$ use the same orthonormal basis at each vertex, transported by translation. The compression commutes with translations. The finite Fourier transform of the vertex variable diagonalizes it into positive $5\times5$ matrices $M_p$, one for every torus momentum, normalized so $\sum_p\operatorname{Tr}M_p=\operatorname{Tr}P\rho$. Consequently

$$
 \operatorname{Tr}\wedge^2(P\rho P)=
 \sum_p\operatorname{Tr}(\wedge^2 M_p)
 +\sum_{p<q}\operatorname{Tr}M_p\,\operatorname{Tr}M_q.                         \tag{7}
$$

This follows by multiplying eigenvalues within a block and between each two blocks. For the unnormalized spatial blocks $C(z)=P_0^{\rm pet}\rho P_z$ in the transported bases, where $P_0^{\rm pet}$ means the packet at the origin, $M_0=\sum_z C(z)$ and $\operatorname{Tr}P\rho=n^3\operatorname{Tr}C(0)$. Thus a zero-momentum coverage test can use measured off-site matrix elements, with no inference about momentum from a single local expectation.

**GUESSED (first physical decision).** Test (2) and record the full-family intensity $n^3\operatorname{Tr}(P_x\rho)$. **PROVED (separation).** On $W$ the choice $A_W=I$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}\wedge^2 A_W/(\operatorname{Tr}A_W)^2=2/5>1/16$. Add one orthogonal eigenvector of arbitrarily large weight. The group-theoretic dimension five prices the readout. It does not force Wilson to occupy it.

**ARGUED.** The proposal on $\pi$ ranks third. The one-box character test is unusually tractable, but prior Wilson estimates show *why* it can pass. It detects the high-frequency randomness of bare transports. The remaining experiment compares how often a move changes the boundary configuration with how much it changes the normalized half-slab column. There are two independent costs in that question.

**CITED (toy inputs on file).** Row 778, gap-vortex-astra.md, (6)–(7), gives the Haar commutator multiplier $d^{-2}I$ and Haar density. On $L^2({\rm SU}(2))$ the spin-$j$ block has $d^2$ matrix coefficients, $d=2j+1=1,2,\ldots$, as in the Peter–Weyl convention of Sussman–Alpoge, outline GL3, 2197–2213. The multipliers of independent convolution steps multiply. These inputs are credited to the older work.

**PROVED (toy constants).** Three fresh handles on one coordinate give a positive transfer with vacuum eigenvalue 1. **PROVED, Lemma B (the finite Wilson word test, using the cited segment bound).** In a four-dimensional periodic Wilson spacetime box at any $\beta>0$, take square loops in complementary $01$ and $23$ planes with a common base, each of side $h\ge4$. Keep them inside the conditioning box, away from any wrap. Suppose

$$
 h-2\ \ge\ (1+6\beta)\log 512 .                                                 \tag{8}
$$

For every mixture of the eight temporal centre twists, and almost every exterior $\mathcal F$, the commutator law satisfies

$$
 \sum_{d\ge2}d^2\,|d^{-1}\mathbb E_{\mathcal F}\chi_d([X,Y])-d^{-2}|^2
 \le 2\big[(1+1/64)^2-1\big]^2<1/256.                                          \tag{9}
$$

The same statement holds for each complementary choice of axes. It quantifies a finite-length submeasurement. Its input is the established one-segment estimate, gap-solve2-astra62.md (1)–(2), 17–32, also checked in gap-owner-tiling-check-opus.md, 101–104. In particular row 1286 already treated the fundamental mean for two spatial loops.

**PROVED (derivation from that input).** Choose the $h-2$ interior bonds of a far side of each square. Within a straight side no plaquette uses two such collinear edges. The two far sides are separated by more than one lattice unit and the two loops share no edge. Thus no plaquette uses two selected edges. Conditional on all other links and the twist, their Wilson factors are independent with single-link tilt at most $6\beta$. A twist only changes the sign of a staple. Each loop density relative to Haar differs from one in sup norm by at most $\eta=\sum_{d\ge2}d^2 t^{d-1}$, $t=\exp[-(h-2)/(1+6\beta)]$, by the cited estimate. Under (8), $t\le1/512$. Since $d^2\le4^{d-1}$, $\eta\le4t/(1-4t)\le1/127<1/64$. The two conditional densities multiply. The joint law is within a factor $1\pm\Delta$ of product Haar, $\Delta=(1+1/64)^2-1$. Pushing this measure inequality through the word map and averaging the unobserved links preserves it relative to the Haar-word law $\mu$. Its density $w$ is central. Its character coefficients are $d\cdot d^{-2}=1/d$, giving $\|w\|_2^2=\sum_{d\ge1}d^{-2}\le2$ by the integral test. For completeness the square-summable character series defines an $L^2$ function with these coefficients. Character orthogonality and density of class polynomials then identify its measure with $\mu$. Thus the new density relative to Haar differs from $w$ in $L^2$ by at most $\sqrt2\Delta$. Taking its character coefficients gives (9) by Bessel's inequality, even before imposing centrality. All bounds hold after conditioning only on the exterior. This step uses only the marginal domination of the word law. No estimates were multiplied for dependent Wilson boxes.

**CITED / PROVED (the original calibrated cutoffs).** In the outline's first-passage convention RF25 implies $\beta\le g^{-2}+B_\sharp(k+1)$ under its construction hypotheses (Sussman and Alpoge, 2026, outline 6853–6861, 20781–20801, index chunks 06 and 19). **PROVED, Lemma B (cost of accepting a physical move).** Make the proposal precise as follows. In tree coordinates fix the entrance slice $x$ and draw the other fields of one neutral periodic cylinder of period $u$, conditional on this slice. Its slice marginal is $\pi(dx)=A(x,x)dx/Z$. **PROVED.** Under (8) even a *fixed entrance* and fixed stem give an increment law between $(1-\Delta)\mu$ and $(1+\Delta)\mu$, with $\mu$ the Haar-word law and $\Delta=(1+1/64)^2-1$. Indeed one can condition on everything except the pivots, apply the two measure inequalities in the proof of (9), conjugate by the now fixed stem, and average back. $$
 (1-\Delta)\mathcal E_{Q_{\rm ref}}(F)
 \le\mathcal E_{Q}(F)\le(1+\Delta)\mathcal E_{Q_{\rm ref}}(F),\qquad
 \mathcal E_Q(F)=\tfrac12\int\|F(x)-F(y)\|^2\,d(\pi Q).                    \tag{10}

This applies to the gap comparison and to the field $F(x)=R_x$. Thus the bare-word replacement costs a known factor. It still leaves the actual Wilson target measure in the acceptance step.

**PROVED (absolute rate fails).** Let $\alpha_e$ be the equilibrium mean probability that the move on $e$ is accepted and changes the coordinate, conditional on choosing $e$. Put $\alpha_*=\min_e\alpha_e$. For $\beta\ge1$ set

$$
 b_\beta=\frac{48+4\log(6\pi)+6\log\beta+\log 8}{\beta}.
$$

For every such cube satisfying (8) and every $r>0$, the scalar gap on the physical neutral configuration quotient satisfies

 N\gamma\le2\alpha_*,\qquad
 \alpha_e\le(1+\Delta)\mu\{g:|g-1|\le2r\}+4b_\beta/r^2 .                   \tag{11}

Here distances are Euclidean chord distances of unit quaternions. In particular $N\gamma\le14\sqrt{b_\beta}$ once $b_\beta\le1$. Along any sequence with $\beta\to\infty$ satisfying (8), $N\gamma\to0$. The absolute lower budget in Part 2 therefore fails on this regime.

**PROVED (proof of (11)).** The plaquette concentration argument is on file in gap-newmath-astra62-max.md, proof of the small-area estimates, 197–201. An explicit constant for this neutral mixture follows by keeping the untwisted sector alone in a lower bound on its partition function. Write $V=n^3u$ and $S$ for the sum of the $6V$ nonnegative signed plaquette deficits. Under normalized Haar, a link of angle at most $\beta^{-1/2}$ has probability at least $(6\pi)^{-1}\beta^{-3/2}$, from the angular density $(2/\pi)\sin^2 t$ and $\sin t\ge t/2$ on $[0,1]$. Constrain all $4V$ links this way. In the untwisted sector each plaquette has chord at most $4/\sqrt\beta$ and deficit at most $8/\beta$. **PROVED.** Take a spatial plaquette through chord $e$. Changing just $e$ by $g$ changes $U_p$ by chord distance $|g-1|$. If its initial and final plaquettes both lie within $r$ of the identity, then $|g-1|\le2r$. The accepted joint measure at fixed $e$ has both marginals bounded above by $\pi$. **PROVED (why the relative test adds a real hypothesis).** Even with purity one, the ratio $\gamma/\alpha_*$ need not have a lower bound for an accepted Haar-word proposal. One contractible SU(2) loop coordinate suffices. Spatial centre cuts act trivially on it and the example is invariant under conjugation. Take $Q_{\rm ref}$ with $\pi$ uniform on the union of two equal small chord balls of radius $\varepsilon$ about $1$ and $-1$. Take a rank-one half kernel $B(x,y)=\sqrt{\pi(x)\pi(y)}$, writing $\pi$ for its Haar density. Any proposal inside the union is accepted. The ball label has variance $1/4$. The Haar-word density is $w(t)=(\pi-t)/(2\sin t)$ at group angle $t$ (row 778, (7)). Near the antipode it is bounded, whereas on a ball of radius $2\varepsilon$ about the identity it is bounded below by $c/\varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon$. The volume of either ball is of order $\varepsilon^3$ by the same angular formula. For every start the probability of entering the other ball is at most $C\varepsilon^3$, while the probability of staying in its own ball is at least $c'\varepsilon^2$. Hence $\gamma/\alpha_*\le C'\varepsilon$ by the ball label's Rayleigh quotient. If strict positivity on the whole group is desired, give the complement total mass $\varepsilon^6$ with constant positive density there. Reversibility bounds all accepted flux to that complement by $\varepsilon^6$. The same orders and the rank-one construction persist. These are comparisons of positive thermal kernels. They do not assert that Wilson has two such wells. Conversely a good word law on one coordinate coexists with an independent spectator of arbitrary rank. In U(1) the move is identically stationary and the test with positive *off-diagonal* acceptance is void.

### Decision on the physical claim

**ARGUED (comparison theories and gate).** All three tests use a non-abelian representation or orbit at a stated physical size. In weak 4D compact U(1), A has one routing at the fixed charges, C has one orientation, and B cannot change any charge. The same group obstructions occur in the strong-coupling U(1) twin. They distinguish the available operation. Distinguishing phases requires the physical inequalities. In 5D SU(2) one can embed the same three loops or the same two planes. The single-link staple bound becomes $8\beta$ and the private edge has six spatial staples. Neither change forbids (2), (4) or a near-Haar bare-word estimate. The finite lemmas alone thus fail the 5D and gapped-twin discrimination tests. A product with a neutral massless spectator can keep all three local readouts. The missing coverage, endpoint drift or full-space mixing cost detects where the argument would have to see it. The moves use $sL^k$ bonds at physical side $s$. Sending $\beta$ to infinity at fixed lattice size loses (8) and can make (4)'s bound ineffective. These are distinct requirements of the direction gate, gap-direction9-fable.md §4 and its check, gap-direction9-check-astra62.md, conclusion.

**ARGUED (ranking).** First measure whether a common endpoint bias survives integration over the two orientation sheets. A positive or negative answer there has a direct interpretation by (4)–(5). Second measure the colour packet together with its translation and momentum weights. Equation (6) rules out treating one local packet as representative of the trace. Third measure the cost of transverse-word moves per accepted change. The absolute-rate proposal fails by (11). Equation (12) specifies the surviving relative test. The elementary word-law signal already has a short-distance explanation in (8)–(9). None of these measurements has delivered a cutoff-uniform infrared bound with the required coverage. No passing cutoff, (PUR-io), or mass gap has been proved in this note.
