$$
   F(t)=\int_{SU(2)}e^{t\,\operatorname{tr}(U)/2}\,dU.
$$

$$
 F^{(2n)}(0)=4^{-n}C_n,\qquad C_n=\binom{2n}{n}/(n+1).
$$

A second derivation of this input is GL3 at spin zero, $F(t)=2I_1(t)/t$. Its Taylor coefficient is $1/(4^n n!(n+1)!)$ at degree $2n$, giving the same derivative. ## From the signed sum to zeros

**Read — proved in the exposition using cited analytic number theory.** Put $G(y)=\sum_r\Lambda(r)(y/r-1)_+-y\log y+(1+\gamma)y$. The Euler product gives the following identity for the Mellin transform of this same statistic.

$$
 \int_1^\infty G(y)y^{-s-1}\,dy
 =-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{s(s-1)\zeta(s)}
   -\frac{1}{(s-1)^2}+\frac{1+\gamma}{s-1}.
$$

The subtractions cancel both terms of the principal part at $1$. A zero $\rho$ of multiplicity $m_\rho$ gives a simple pole of residue $-m_\rho/[\rho(\rho-1)]$. Inverting gives the absolutely convergent nontrivial-zero sum with terms $-y^\rho/[\rho(\rho-1)]$. The smoothed prime statistic is centered and can have either sign.
