### T1. The one-sided criterion (Landau; on windows). Explainer I.1, II.1.

Tool (read). A transform of an eventually non-negative function has its first singularity on the real axis. So an upper bound G(x) <= x^theta on windows [Y, Y^(3/2)], plus the absence of real singularities, excludes all singularities right of theta.

Transfer statement 1 (time variable). For the neutral transfer operator, s(u) = sum over i > 0 of r_i^u is the Laplace transform of a positive measure. So an upper bound s(u) < 1 at ONE u gives the gap r_1 <= s(u)^(1/u). **On file**: Fact PUR (rows 1373, 1461; check 1465), Proposition PER (c) of row 1303, reading (R1) there. Transfer statement 2 (coupling variable). "A function of beta that controls the gap has eventually non-negative Taylor coefficients at beta = 0; so its first singularity is real; so real-analyticity on [0, beta*] gives a zero-free disc of radius beta*, which is the missing input of Theorem ZB." **Closed here for log Z^z and log Z_+ (Lemma PRG and item (ii) below); not decided for P_K (item (iii)):**

**Lemma PRG (proved here; elementary; not on file: grep for Pringsheim and Vivanti finds nothing).** Let h(beta) = sum c_k beta^k have radius of convergence R with 0 < R < infinity, and let h extend analytically to a neighbourhood of the real point beta = R. Then c_k < 0 for infinitely many k, and c_k > 0 for infinitely many k.

Proof. Suppose c_k >= 0 for all k >= k_0. Subtract the polynomial of the first k_0 terms; this changes neither R nor the analyticity at R. So take all c_k >= 0. The disc abs(beta - R/2) <= R/2 lies in the closed disc of radius R and meets its boundary only at R, where h is analytic. So the Taylor series of h at R/2 has radius rho > R/2. Its coefficients are h^(j)(R/2)/j! = sum over k >= j of binom(k, j) c_k (R/2)^(k-j) >= 0. Take a real x with R < x < R/2 + rho. Then sum over j of [sum over k of binom(k, j) c_k (R/2)^(k-j)] (x - R/2)^j converges, and all terms are non-negative, so the double sum may be reordered: it equals sum over k of c_k (R/2 + x - R/2)^k = sum c_k x^k. So the series of h converges at x > R. This contradicts the radius R. The same argument for -h gives the second claim. QED (This is the Vivanti-Pringsheim theorem in contrapositive form; the proof above is complete, so nothing is taken from memory.)

Consequence, in four items.

(i) PROVED from Lemma PRG. Let h be real-analytic on a neighbourhood of [0, infinity). On a finite torus log Z^z, log Z_+ and P_K are such functions, because Z^z(beta) and Z_+(v; beta) = Tr(P_0 T^v) are positive for real beta. If the Taylor coefficients of h at 0 are eventually of one sign, the radius of convergence is infinite: h is entire.

(ii) PROVED given one classical theorem that is RECALLED and not rebuilt here (Hadamard: an entire function of order at most 1 without zeros is the exponential of a polynomial of degree at most 1). Z^z and Z_+ are Laplace transforms of the law of the total action. That law has compact support and is not a point mass. So abs(Z(beta)) <= e^(c abs(beta)), and the order is at most 1. If Z had no zero, then Z = e^(c beta) since Z(0) = 1, so (log Z)'' = 0, so the action would have variance zero under the Gibbs law at real beta. That is false for a continuous non-constant action and a measure of full support. So Z^z and Z_+ have complex zeros; log Z^z and log Z_+ are not entire; by (i) their Taylor coefficients change sign infinitely often. Transfer statement 2 is closed for them.

(iii) NOT DECIDED here. For P_K = Z_+(2u)/Z_+(u)^2, eventual positivity would force P_K to be entire: every zero of Z_+(u; .) would be a zero of Z_+(2u; .) of at least twice the order. I do not expect this (GUESSED) and did not exclude it.

(iv) In infinite volume (i) applies to the free energy density wherever it is real-analytic on the positive axis. Eventual positivity with a finite radius then needs a singularity at a real coupling: a transition, of the kind that compact U(1) in d = 4 and SU(2) in d = 5 have on the real axis (RECALLED; filter (t1), (t2)). So where the hypothesis of the tool can hold with a finite radius, the model is one that the filter must reject.

## 5. Verdict

**Outcome (B). No tool of the Alpoge-Claude proof, as the explainer presents it, transfers to the crux as a new, selective lemma.** In plain words, for the owner's question: the RH proof wins its sign from whole numbers.
