## Which first angular moment actually responds

**PROVED.** In the genuine Wilson law there is an exact answer at finite volume for the lowest responding degree in every term after a tree reveal. Fix *any* spatial spanning tree and *any* order of its chords. For all but three of the $2n^3+1$ chords, the conditional degree-one moments of 1367 (4) differ for almost every history. On the other three, every odd moment is identical under the two laws and the degree-two moments differ for almost every history. The exceptional chords are precisely those whose fundamental cycle first adds a new independent winding class modulo two. This statement holds at every $\beta>0$ and finite $w\ge1$ on the indicated tori. It specifies a degree and supplies no cutoff-uniform lower bound on its amplitude.

**PROVED (strict positivity of the squared kernel).** Use the raw-face kernel $B=P_0T^w/\|P_0T^w\|_{\rm HS}$ of 1367, with positive Haar-unit vacuum $\Omega$ and leading eigenvalue $\sigma$. Its restriction to the neutral gauge-invariant functions is positive and injective. Indeed the central electric convolution has a strictly positive multiplier in every representation at $\beta>0$, the magnetic half-weight is bounded away from zero on a finite cube, and these operations preserve the neutral gauge-invariant space. This is the injectivity used in 1178 for the strict loss upon retaining only elementary plaquette traces. The integral operator with kernel $J(x,y)=B(x,y)^2$ satisfies
$$
    \int f(x)J(x,y)f(y)\,dx\,dy
        \ \ge\ \sigma\langle \Omega f,B(\Omega f)\rangle>0                  \tag{18}
$$
for any real nonzero neutral $f\in L^2(dx)$. Indeed $B-\sigma\Omega\otimes\Omega$ and $B$ are positive kernels. Their pointwise product is positive by the Schur product theorem. One can see this by taking Gram vectors for each kernel and tensoring their two vectors at each point. For a positive continuous integral kernel on this compact slice, finite point matrices are positive by testing with normalized Haar measures on shrinking neighborhoods. Subtracting the leading term then gives (18). The last inequality uses $\Omega>0$ and injectivity. In particular $\mathbb E_J[f(Y)\mid X]$ cannot vanish almost surely for such an $f$.

**PROVED (a neutral test at each step).** For chord $i$ let $H_i$ be its fundamental based holonomy, $t_i=\operatorname{Re}H_i$, and $a_i\in H_1(\mathbb T^3;\mathbb F_2)$ its winding class. After the tree has been revealed, $t_i$ is linear in the four quaternion coordinates of the new link, with unit coefficient norm. A sheet flip $g$ acts on $t_i$ by $(-1)^{\langle g,a_i\rangle}$. If $a_i$ belongs to the span of the previous chord classes, express it as the sum of at most three of them and take $F_i=t_i\prod_{j\in J_i}t_j$ with that choice of $J_i$. For $a_i=0$ use just $t_i$. Otherwise, when $a_i$ increases the span, take $F_i=t_i^2$. In both cases $|F_i|\le1$ and $F_i$ is gauge and centre invariant. The linear multiplier $\prod_{j\in J_i}t_j$ is nonzero almost surely. Each earlier trace is zero on a Haar-null set by integrating its own chord, and the one-face marginal $l$ has a strictly positive density.

Let $b_i=\mathbb E_l[F_i(Y)\mid Y_{<i}]$, and put $f_i=F_i-b_i$. The sigma-field of already revealed links is preserved by the gauge and centre actions. Invariance of $F_i$ and $l$ makes $b_i$, extended to the full slice, invariant up to null sets. Thus $f_i$ is neutral. It is nonzero in $L^2(l)$ since the next link has an everywhere positive conditional density and the indicated linear or squared-linear function has positive conditional variance. The measures $l$ and Haar are equivalent at finite volume. If the conditional moments through the specified degree agreed almost surely, then $\mathbb E_J[F_i\mid X,Y_{<i}]=b_i$ since all the coefficients of its link polynomial are already known. This would give $\mathbb E_J[f_i(Y)\mid X]=0$, contradicting (18).

For a span-increasing chord choose a sheet flip pairing to zero with every previous class and to one with $a_i$. Compose it with the vertex signs obtained by propagating along the tree to restore every tree edge. The resulting deterministic sign cochain is $+1$ on the whole prefix and $-1$ on the new chord. It acts on $Y$ alone by a symmetry of both $J$ and $Q$. Both next-link laws are then even, which proves the odd-degree assertion. The fundamental cycles generate the three spatial winding classes, so exactly three steps increase their span.

To obtain the almost-every-history assertion, the conditional moment differences are real analytic functions of the raw history. Before normalization their densities and moment integrals are compact Haar integrals of exponentials of polynomials, with a finite sum for the seams. The holomorphic extensions in the finitely many ambient boundary variables are bounded on each compact set before integration. The real denominators are strictly positive. On the connected product of three-spheres a nonidentically-zero real analytic function has a null zero set. Locally, each zero has finite order and belongs to a regular zero hypersurface of a derivative of one lower order. A countable chart cover and the countable derivative choices suffice. The preceding contradiction shows that at least one component is nonidentically zero. In the second case the constant part of $t_i^2$ has zero difference, so degree two itself responds. ## The log trace as a sum of conditional angular terms

**PROVED.** There is also a positive sum for the *logarithm of the desired neutral trace* on the same cube. The coefficients are low conditional angular moments in the positive vacuum process, and the error has a uniform additive bound. Use the positive neutral transfer $F$, vacuum $\Omega$, eigenvalue $\lambda$, and $\pi(dx)=\Omega(x)^2\,dx$. At separation $w$ put
$$
 K(x,y)=\frac{F^w(x,y)}{\lambda^w\Omega(x)\Omega(y)} .
$$
The vacuum pair has density $K$ against $\pi\otimes\pi$. It integrates to one in either variable. The squared norm $\int K^2\,d\pi^2=1+s(2w)$ is exactly the vacuum Pearson identity of 1178 (9). Its squared-density law is precisely the thermal $J$ used in 1367. [Vacuum Pearson identity](gap-solve18-astra62.md)

Reveal $x$ and then the first $i$ links of $y$. Let $L_i=\mathbb E_{\pi^2}[K\mid x,y_{\le i}]$ be the prefix likelihood, $H_i=\mathbb E_{\pi^2}L_i^2$, and $\nu_{i-1}$ the history law proportional to $L_{i-1}^2\,d\pi(x)\,d\pi_{<i}(h)$ where $h=y_{<i}$. In this section $p_i^*(v\mid x,h)$ is the next-link Haar density under $K\,d\pi^2$, and $q_i^*(v\mid h)$ is that under $\pi$. Factoring the two prefix probabilities gives $L_i=L_{i-1}p_i^*/q_i^*$. Hence
$$
 \kappa_i=\mathbb E_{\nu_{i-1}}\int\frac{(p_i^*-q_i^*)^2}{q_i^*}\,dv,
 \qquad \frac{H_i}{H_{i-1}}=1+\kappa_i,\qquad
             \log(1+s(2w))=\sum_i\log(1+\kappa_i).                         \tag{22}
$$
Indeed $\int(p_i^*)^2/q_i^*=1+\int(p_i^*-q_i^*)^2/q_i^*$ at every history, $H_0=1$, and $H_m=1+s(2w)$. Each history law in (22) is normalized before integrating the next-link influence. The fully revealed history law is $J$.

**PROVED (the vacuum one-link estimate).** The two densities in (22) are mixtures of $g_h$ with $|h|\le6\beta$, as in the thermal case. For $M\ge2$ sandwich the operator-norm spectral remainder for $M-2$ steps between two one-step kernel rows, whose $L^2$ norms are uniformly bounded at fixed volume. All prefix densities and next-link conditionals converge since their limiting denominators are strictly positive. At finite $M$ the conditional densities are mixtures by the six-plaquette Wilson rule. The probability mixing measures on the closed parameter ball form a weakly compact set, and $g_h(v)$ is jointly continuous there. Thus the mixtures persist in the limit. Marginalizing the first endpoint gives the assertion for $q_i^*$ as well. In particular $q_i^*\ge e^{-12\beta}$ uniformly.

**PROVED (an additive error on the log trace).** Let $d+1$ be the least integer at least $\max\{24e\beta,(30(1+\beta)+20\log n)/\log2\}$. Set $p_{i,d}=\Pi_d p_i^*$ and $q_{i,d}=\Pi_d q_i^*$ using the Haar projection of 1367 (5). For all large $n$ in the band, $q_{i,d}>0$ everywhere and

          \frac{(p_{i,d}-q_{i,d})^2}{q_{i,d}}\,dv,\qquad
 \left|\log(1+s(2w))-\sum_i\log(1+\kappa_{i,d})\right|\le C n^{-5}.      \tag{23}
$$
There is no clipping or renormalization in this formula. The positive Taylor approximation in 1367 (5) has uniform error at most $e^{6\beta}2^{-(d+1)}$ before Haar projection. The norm of $\Pi_d$ from $L^2$ to $L^\infty$ equals the square root of its dimension, since its reproducing kernel has constant diagonal by rotation invariance and the integral of that diagonal is the dimension. This dimension is $\sum_{k=0}^d(k+1)^2$. For any of the mixtures, subtract the approximating polynomial before projecting. The resulting uniform projection error is at most $(1+\sqrt{\dim\Pi_d})e^{6\beta}2^{-(d+1)}\le n^{-8}e^{-12\beta}$ for large $n$. Thus $|q_{i,d}/q_i^*-1|\le n^{-8}$.

Keep the exact denominator $q_i^*$ for a moment.
