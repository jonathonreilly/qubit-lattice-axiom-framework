## What one more observed link costs on the neutral circle

**PROVED (exact retained fraction).** Fix one finite lattice and write $Z_t=\operatorname{Tr}F^t$. Let $X,Y$ be the two faces of the projected $2w$ circle. Their Haar density is $J(x,y)=F^w(x,y)^2/Z_{2w}$. This uses both independently projected arcs as in row 1367. Reveal any ordered list of the $m=3n^3$ links of $Y$. With $h=Y_{\le i}$ write $j_i(x,h)$ for the marginal density of $(X,h)$, $l(x)$ for the density of $X$, and put
$$
 A_i(x,x')=\int\sqrt{j_i(x,h)j_i(x',h)}\,dh,\qquad r_i=\|A_i\|_{\rm op}.
 \tag{1}
$$
Each $A_i$ is the Gram operator of a Hilbert–Schmidt kernel of squared norm one. In particular $A_i\ge0$, $\operatorname{Tr}A_i=1$ and $A_i(x,x)=l(x)$. Its kernel is continuous and strictly positive. At the empty and full prefixes the operators are
$$
 A_0=|\sqrt l\rangle\langle\sqrt l|,\qquad
 A_m=F^{2w}/Z_{2w},\qquad
 r_0=1,\qquad r_m=(1+s(2w))^{-1}.
 \tag{2}
$$
For the second identity use the positive square root **of the pointwise density**, $\sqrt J=F^w/\sqrt{Z_{2w}}$, and integrate the shared face. These statements use no estimate on any continuum limit.

**PROVED (one-link decrement).** With $h=Y_{<i}$ let $p_{x,h}(v)$ be the thermal conditional density of the next link. Define its affinity at two opposite-face values by
$$
 c_i(x,x';h)=\int\sqrt{p_{x,h}(v)p_{x',h}(v)}\,dv .
$$
Then $0<c_i\le1$. In (1) the step $A_{i-1}\mapsto A_i$ inserts $c_i$ under the $h$ integral. This gives $A_i\le A_{i-1}$ pointwise. In the Rayleigh quotient of a nonnegative kernel, replacing a vector by its modulus can only increase the quotient, so $r_i\le r_{i-1}$. In particular *every* prefix above the target fraction $21/25$ has all its other eigenvalues summing to at most $4/25$. This separation follows from trace one at the prefix. It requires no spectral-gap hypothesis on the unknown Wilson trajectory.

**PROVED (finite copies, positive increments).** For any integer $k\ge2$ set $C_{i,k}=\operatorname{Tr}A_i^k$ and $G_{i,k}=-k^{-1}\log C_{i,k}$. Form a probability law on $x_1,\ldots,x_k$ and $k$ histories $h_1,\ldots,h_k$ with density
$$
 \frac1{C_{i-1,k}}\prod_{a=1}^k
 \sqrt{j_{i-1}(x_a,h_a)j_{i-1}(x_{a+1},h_a)},\qquad x_{k+1}=x_1 .
$$
Integration of each history gives its kernel $A_{i-1}$, and closure gives the displayed normalizer. All the marginals in this formula are integrals of the finite projected $2w$ circle. Factoring the conditional densities gives
$$
 G_{i,k}-G_{i-1,k}
  =-\frac1k\log\mathbb E_{i-1,k}\prod_{a=1}^k c_i(x_a,x_{a+1};h_a)\ \ge0 ,
 \qquad C_{m,k}=Z_{2kw}/Z_{2w}^k .                              \tag{3}
$$
In particular $G_{0,k}=0$. The increments consider the *same* history under the two endpoint values. Comparing endpoints after drawing unrelated link histories would change (3).

**PROVED (sharp error at the seed threshold).** Put $q=21/25$, $\theta=-\log q$, $g_i=-\log r_i$. Uniformly in the lattice, every prefix and every spectrum,
$$
 0\le\min(\theta,g_i)-\min(\theta,G_{i,k})
      \le \epsilon_k=\frac1k\log\!\left(1+(4/21)^k\right).       \tag{4}
$$
Indeed $C_{i,k}\ge r_i^k$. For $r_i\ge q$ the other eigenvalues have $k$th powers summing to at most $(1-r_i)^k$, by positivity. For $r_i\le q$ all eigenvalues sum to one and none exceeds $q$. If the largest is at least $1/2$, its $k$th power plus the $k$th power of its complement increases up to $q^k+(1-q)^k$. If the largest is below $1/2$, the sum is at most $2^{1-k}$, by bounding each power by its eigenvalue times $2^{1-k}$. This proves (4), with equality in its error bound for the two weights $q,1-q$. For example $k=\max(2,\lceil5\log n/\log(21/4)\rceil)$ gives $\epsilon_k\le n^{-5}/k$. Any fixed strict margin below $\theta$ can instead be tested with fixed $k$ chosen for that margin. Clipping in (4) makes the error uniform at the crossing.

**PROVED (one-link angular data suffice for these increments).** In the conditional projected Wilson circle $p_{x,h}$ is a mixture of $e^{b\cdot v}/\int e^{b\cdot u}du$ with $|b|\le6\beta$, by the six incident plaquettes. A degree $d=O_{B_0}(\log n)$ suffices because the positive Taylor bound of 1367 (5) is $e^{6\beta}2^{-(d+1)}$ if $d+1\ge24e\beta$. Projection costs at most $1+\sqrt{\sum_{j=0}^d(j+1)^2}$ in this estimate by the constant diagonal of the Haar projection. Take, for instance, $d+1$ the least integer at least $\max\{24e\beta,(30(1+\beta)+22\log n)/\log2\}$. Every $p\ge e^{-12\beta}$. Consequently replacing $p$ by $\Pi_dp$ in each $c_i$ changes it by relative error at most $n^{-9}$. The product in (3) changes by factors between $(1-n^{-9})^k$ and $(1+n^{-9})^k$, also after expectation. The logarithm divided by $k$ costs at most $2n^{-9}$ at each link. The entire sum changes by at most $6n^{-6}$ with its exact finite-circle history laws. In particular, a strict cutoff upper limit for the sum below the threshold suffices when the replica order tends to infinity.

$$

**PROVED (all placements, including a fixed exterior).** Let $S$ be any deterministic subgraph of the near face with $d\ge1$ raw spatial links, $d<b/4$.             \mathcal L_+(D_S)\otimes\mathcal L_+(E_S)\big)

 \mathbb E D_{\rm KL}(\mathcal L_+((Y_B)_B\mid C)\Vert\textstyle\bigotimes_B R_B)

**PROVED (what a change of reveal order can cover).** Consider a direct application of (11) to a raw link, or a block of raw links, with every earlier raw link required to lie inside its chosen cube of side at most $s$, or in its permitted exterior. **PROVED (a bound on all the unpaid terms at the physical time, from earlier work).** Write $g_+(t)=\log(1+s(t))$ and $g_{\rm ord}(t)$ for the full periodic vacuum-normalized log trace at time $t$.
