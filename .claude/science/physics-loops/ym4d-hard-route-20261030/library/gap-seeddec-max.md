### Step 3a (PROVED). Setting, relative fillings, and the patching lemma

**Setting.** T = (Z/L)^3 with its cubical cells (sites, edges, plaquettes, cubes). Chains have Z_2 coefficients. gamma is a 1-cycle (every site has even gamma-degree). A *box* is a product of three cyclic intervals of n_1, n_2, n_3 sites, 2 <= n_i <= L - 1, with all cells whose sites lie in it; it is a cubical 3-ball. dN is the union of its six faces. A cell of N is *interior* if it is not contained in dN. C_i(N, dN) is spanned by the interior i-cells, and d_rel is the boundary followed by dropping non-interior cells.

Standard facts (S), each provable by a sweep along one coordinate: H_1(N, dN) = 0, H_2(N, dN) = 0, H_3(N, dN) = Z_2 generated by [N] = all cubes of N; and H_1(N) = 0 (a cycle inside a box is a boundary inside the box).

**Definitions.** gamma_N = the interior edges of N that lie in gamma. It is a relative cycle (all six edges at an interior site are interior). A *relative filling* of gamma in N is D in C_2(N, dN) with d_rel D = gamma_N. One exists by (S). If N' is a box inside N, interior cells of N' are interior cells of N, and D|N' (the plaquettes of D interior to N') is a relative filling of gamma in N' (the four plaquettes at an interior edge of N' are interior to N').

**Lemma A (transition chains).** If D, D' are relative fillings of gamma in the same box N, there is a 3-chain W of N with D + D' = d_rel W. The only other one is W + [N].
*Proof.* D + D' is a relative 2-cycle; H_2(N, dN) = 0 gives W; two choices differ by a relative 3-cycle, so by 0 or [N]. QED

**Lemma B (patching).** Let U be a family of boxes, D_a a relative filling of gamma in N_a for each a in U. For all a, b in U with a common cube let W_ab = W_ba be a 3-chain of N_a n N_b with d_rel W_ab = (D_a + D_b)|(N_a n N_b), and W_aa = 0. Suppose the cocycle condition: for all a, b, c in U and every cube q in N_a n N_b n N_c, W_ab(q) + W_bc(q) + W_ca(q) = 0. Let R be the set of edges that are interior to at least one box of U. Then there is a 2-chain D on T with (dD)(e) = gamma(e) for every e in R.
*Proof.* For each cube q inside some box choose a(q) in U with q in N_a(q). For a in U and q in N_a put V_a(q) = W_(a,a(q))(q). For q in N_a n N_b the cocycle condition for (a, b, a(q)) gives V_a(q) + V_b(q) = W_ab(q). Put D'_a = D_a + d_rel V_a, a relative filling in N_a. If a plaquette P is interior to N_a and to N_b, it is interior to the box N_a n N_b (a face of the intersection lies in a face of N_a or of N_b), both cubes at P lie in N_a n N_b, and D'_a(P) + D'_b(P) = (D_a + D_b)(P) + (dW_ab)(P) = 0. So D(P) := D'_a(P) for any a with P interior to N_a (and D(P) = 0 if there is none) is well defined. If e is interior to N_a, its four plaquettes are interior to N_a, so (dD)(e) = (d_rel D'_a)(e) = gamma(e). QED

**Corollary C (localisation).** In Lemma B, gamma + dD is a cycle homologous to gamma and supported off R. If every connected component of it (edges joined through shared sites) lies in a box, then gamma is a boundary.
*Proof.* A component of a cycle is a cycle; inside a box it is a boundary by (S). QED

Remark (where the class lives). Without the cocycle condition, c(a, b, c) = W_ab + W_bc + W_ca is 0 or the whole triple overlap, a Cech 2-cocycle with values in H_3(overlap, boundary) = Z_2, and its class is the Poincare dual of [gamma]. Lemma B is the statement that a vanishing cocycle gives a global filling. ARGUED (not needed below).

Otherwise N is *unclean*. Cleanness of N depends only on gamma_N, the interior edges of N. (27 core-boxes B, N included; so any two fillings in M(N) differ by a small chain.)

### Step 3c (PROVED). The hierarchy and (TOP_j)

**Levels.** g_j = 10^j g. Let J >= 1 and let L be a multiple of 4 g_J. B_j = { N^j_x = g_j x + [0, 2 g_j - 1]^3 : x in (Z/(L/g_j))^3 }. The class of N^j_x is x mod 2, one of 8. The boxes of one class sigma form a tiling T^j_sigma of T by site-blocks of side 2 g_j, cut by link-bisecting planes at 2 g_j Z + sigma_i g_j - 1/2. Distinct boxes of one class are disjoint.

**Definition (bad).** A box of B_0 is bad_0 if it is unclean. For j >= 0, a box Q of B_(j+1) is bad_(j+1) if there are two distinct boxes of B_j, of the same class, both inside Q, both bad_j, whose indices differ by at most 8 in each coordinate.

**Lemma G (geometry of one level).** (a) Two boxes of B_j share a site iff their indices differ by at most 1 in each coordinate; two distinct boxes that share a site have different classes. (b) The union of a connected family of n boxes of B_j lies in a box with at most (n+1) g_j sites per side (if (n+1) g_j <= L - 1). (c) A set that lies in a box with at most g_(j+1) + 1 sites per side lies in a box of B_(j+1). (d) A connected family of 9 distinct boxes of B_j contains two distinct boxes of the same class whose indices differ by at most 8 in each coordinate, and its union lies in a box of B_(j+1).
*Proof.* (a) One coordinate at a time: [g x, g x + 2g - 1] meets [g y, g y + 2g - 1] iff |x - y| <= 1 (cyclic, L/g_j >= 4); indices that differ by at most 1 everywhere and agree mod 2 are equal. (b) Add the boxes along a spanning tree; each new box shares a site with an old one, so each side of the bounding box grows by at most g_j. (c) For an interval [p, p + w - 1] with w <= g_(j+1) + 1 take x = floor(p / g_(j+1)). (d) Nine boxes, eight classes; two members are joined inside the family by at most 8 steps, each step moves each index by at most 1; by (b) the union has at most 10 g_j = g_(j+1) sites per side; apply (c). QED

**Lemma H (hierarchy).** Let 1 <= j <= J. If F is a connected family of bad_0 boxes whose union lies in no box with 9 g_(j-1) sites per side, then every member of F lies inside a bad_j box.
*Proof.* Induction on j. j = 1: by (b), F has at least 9 members. Given N_0 in F take a connected subfamily of exactly 9 members containing N_0. By (d) it lies in a box Q of B_1 and has two distinct members of one class with index offsets at most 8. Both are bad_0, so Q is bad_1. Step j -> j+1: the union of F lies in no box with 9 g_j sites per side, so in none with 9 g_(j-1); by induction each N in F lies in a bad_j box Q(N). The set Q = {Q(N)} is connected (N, N' share a site, so Q(N), Q(N') share it), and its union contains the union of F. By (b), Q has at least 9 members. Take a connected subfamily of 9 containing Q(N_0); by (d) it lies in a box Q' of B_(j+1) and has two distinct same-class members with offsets at most 8, both bad_j. So Q' is bad_(j+1) and contains N_0. QED

**Theorem TOP (this is (TOP_j), exact form).** Let g >= 2, J >= 1, L a multiple of 4 g_J, M any filling rule, gamma a Z_2 1-cycle on T = (Z/L)^3 with [gamma] != 0. Then for every j = 0, ..., J some box of B_j is bad_j. By definition every bad_j box, j >= 1, contains two disjoint bad_(j-1) boxes of one tiling T^(j-1)_sigma; so a non-trivial class forces two separated bad sub-boxes at every level.
*Proof.* Lemma T1 gives a connected family F of bad_0 boxes whose union lies in no box at all; boxes have at most L - 1 sites per side and 9 g_(j-1) <= L - 1. Apply Lemma H. QED

#### 7.1 The exact law of the parity cycle, and its sign structure

#### 7.3 A local property that would make the bad event rare, and the barrier it meets

**Property (FN_eps), filling noise.** For a box N: on an extension of the probability space there is a random relative filling D_0 of gamma in N with P(S inside D_0) <= eps^|S| for every set S of interior plaquettes. (Local stochastic noise on a primitive of the cycle. For an exact perimeter law it holds with eps = p, by 7.2. Deconfined models fail it for every eps < 1/2 small enough to matter: Proposition SEL with Proposition RIG below.)

**Lemma HC (half coverage; PROVED).** Let D be a minimal relative filling of gamma in a box B and D_0 any relative filling of gamma in B. Every edge-connected component Sigma of D + D_0 satisfies |Sigma n D_0| >= |Sigma|/2.
*Proof.* D + D_0 is a relative 2-cycle. The plaquettes of it at one interior edge are edge-connected through that edge, so each component Sigma is a relative 2-cycle, and D + Sigma is a relative filling. Minimality: |D| <= |D + Sigma| = |D| + |Sigma| - 2 |Sigma n D|. So |Sigma n D| <= |Sigma|/2, and the rest of Sigma lies in D_0. QED

**Lemma PB (one wall; PROVED given (FN_eps)).** For an interior plaquette P of B inside N and n >= 1: the probability that, for some minimal D, the component of D + D_0|B through P has exactly n plaquettes is at most a_n (2 sqrt(eps))^n, where a_n <= 144^(n-1) counts the edge-connected sets of n interior plaquettes containing P.
*Proof.* Union over the sets Sigma. By Lemma HC at least n/2 plaquettes of Sigma lie in D_0; there are at most 2^n choices of them and each has probability at most eps^(n/2). A plaquette shares an edge with at most 12 others; a connected set of n is covered by a closed walk of 2(n-1) steps. QED

(2) Either one wall component has at least c g^2 plaquettes (relative isoperimetry in a core-box), which Lemma PB makes exponentially rare in g^2; or many small components enclose a quarter of a core-box, which the joint bound P(S inside D_0) <= eps^|S| over their disjoint union makes rare in g^3. I did not write (2) to proof level. With the classical count of closed surfaces (3^n, recalled) in place of 144^(n-1) the condition is eps < 1/36.

**The filling-Peierls barrier (PROVED arithmetic from Lemma PB's condition; the density is GUIDE).** A filling has at least |gamma_N|/4 plaquettes. So (FN_eps) forces eps >= (1 - O(1/g)) rho/4, rho the density of half-integer edges. The Peierls sum of Lemma PB converges only if 2 c sqrt(eps) < 1. Together: rho < 1/c^2, which is 4.8e-5 with c = 144 and 1/9 with c = 3. At beta 2.3, rho = 0.33 (row 860, GUIDE), so eps >= 0.08 and 2 c sqrt(eps) >= 1.7 even with c = 3. So no Peierls argument on minimal-filling walls proves the seed at the lattice scale in the window. A proof must use that a wall through a filling of density 0.1 gains at most 0.1 of its area on average, with a large-deviation bound that beats the wall entropy; that is the content of the ordered phase of the +-J model beyond the Peierls regime.

#### 7.4 Where the attack stops

The seed (in either form, at a fixed coupling in the window) follows from two statements. Neither is reached by a tool I know.

- **(S1), the SU(2) input.** A noise model for the one-slice law: (FN_eps) for boxes up to the consumer's size with eps = eps(beta) below the decoding threshold, or any other statement that the 't Hooft functional T of 7.2 is that of a short-range, low-density random surface (a local perimeter law, not only a perimeter bound). The proved product bounds on label events are worst-case and give 0.58 to 0.87 per edge (rows 860, 867); the chessboard estimate turns (S1) into bulk free energies of periodic constrained ensembles, for which nothing is proved at order one; by 7.1(c) no bound that conditions on a typical gauge field is available; no correlation inequality of Ginibre type is known for T in SU(2). U(1) Coulomb and frozen 2T, Z_2 fail (S1) (Proposition SEL), so any proof must use the non-abelian low spins quantitatively. OPEN.
- **(S2), group-blind.** Rigidity of minimal fillings (the "clean" property) under an eps-sparse filling noise for eps near 0.12 to 0.17: the zero-temperature ferromagnetic phase of the three-dimensional +-J Ising model, with stability under a change of the free boundary, for dependent disorder. Known by Peierls for eps below 1/36 at best (Lemma PB); believed true up to about 0.22 for independent disorder (recalled numerics); not proved there. OPEN, and outside gauge theory. Changing the rule M does not remove it: every decoder of loop-like syndromes has its proved threshold in the Peierls range (recalled), and Theorem TOP holds for every rule.
- **Lowering the density first** would bring (S2) into the Peierls range: a coarse cycle on blocks of a few xi with noise below 1/36. But a coarse cycle with the same class is exactly the Cech cocycle of local fillings (Step 3a, Remark), so building it is the decoding problem again; and face parities are 't Hooft loops with a perimeter law (F2), so no linear block map of the labels does it. I found no reflection-positive coarse electric variable. OPEN.
- **What a known tool does reach.** At strong coupling rho is small, strand counting applies and the seed holds; this adds nothing to Theorem F's range (ARGUED, not written). The topological half (Theorem TOP, Lemma T1, Proposition OUT) is proved at every coupling and for every group.

So the route stops at the same place as (SEED-FP): an order-one statement about the bare measure at a few correlation lengths. It changes the form of the seed three times over: from a twist ratio on the whole torus to the probability of one event in a ball (SEED-DEC-U); from there to a noise model (S1) plus a decoder threshold (S2). It adds one cost that (SEED-FP) does not have: (S2) is an open problem of disordered systems. And it is a fixed-coupling route (7.2).
