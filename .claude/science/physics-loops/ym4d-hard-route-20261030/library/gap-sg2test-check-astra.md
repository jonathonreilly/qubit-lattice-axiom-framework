## Hand checks

**Lemma LB: HOLDS at population level.** Disintegrate the exact equilibrium slab by its orbit under one common spatial gauge transformation on all t >= 1. Only the first temporal coupling changes along that orbit. Thus E[X | orbit] = <v,Ebar(u,w1)>. For a centered proxy vector Y, Cauchy–Schwarz applied to b.Y gives (b.c)^2 <= Var(Xbar) b^T G b. Optimize b. Use the Moore–Penrose inverse if G is singular. When EX=0, the uncentered version with c=E[YX], G=E[YY^T] also follows by the L2 projection inequality. This includes the code's multi-slice proxies: the same frame acts on every slice, so sums of corresponding links and their norm divisions are equivariant. These are population inequalities, not confidence bounds for fitted estimates.

**Frame-lag monotonicity: HOLDS with exact equilibrium input and even L.** The implemented sweep is P0 P1 P0, where P0 and P1 are the conditional-expectation projections updating each parity. It is a positive self-adjoint contraction, since <f,P0 P1 P0 f>=||P1 P0 f||^2. Conditional on the slab orbit Q, write f=X-E[X|Q]. Then

    A_n = Var(Xbar) + E_Q <f,K_Q^n f>,

so A_n decreases to Var(Xbar). Finite-box full-support heat baths supply ergodicity. This applies to expectations under the finite slab, not automatically to Monte Carlo averages or the vacuum law. A plateau at sampled lags is not a bound on the remaining tail. This uses row 951's correction of row 950's factorization; no new claim to that prior correction is made.

**Proposition TW: HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION.** Take even L, as in all jobs. Moving `exp(phi k/L)` into one seam is the gauge change `h(x)=exp(x_d phi k/L)`. Its commuting factors cancel on each plaquette. The constant stabilizer contains `exp(alpha k)`, which forces Ebar into the k-axis. The winding traces on the two explicit abelian patterns differ by `4 sin(4 theta e_p) sin(phi)`; thus they are gauge-inequivalent when the stated product is nonzero. This proves inequivalence of configurations, not existence or positive limiting weight of ordered phases.

Reflection of d followed by conjugation by i fixes the datum. On a reversed link the third component of E changes sign; conjugation supplies another sign. Reindexing the staggered weight then gives X3 -> -X3, also for d=x. Thus EX3=0 in the exact symmetric law. For horizontality, use

    div_u xi(x) = sum_i [xi_i(x) - Ad_(u_i(x-i)^*) xi_i(x-i)].

Every link of either twisted datum commutes with k. For the stated xi, the x contribution is `2(-1)^(x+y) k/sqrt(2L^3)` and the y contribution is its negative. Hence the divergence vanishes and `||xi||=1`. The x-twisted x-links are **not central**, contrary to lines 225–226 and the explanation in 4.1; commutation with k is the sufficient fact. It follows, with the law specified, that `Var(phi_(u,z))=beta^2 Var(<v,Ebar>)`. At population level this passes a genuine SG2 violation to SG-step. It does not turn finite-size growth into such a violation.

**Heat-bath kernels: HOLDS on the reported even boxes when the SU(2) staple norm is at least 1e-12.** A change of one slab link changes the exponent by `<Delta U,S>`, where S is exactly the two spatial staples in each transverse direction, weighted beta (beta/2 at t=T), plus the adjacent temporal links weighted beta. At fixed direction and parity these links do not interact, so the simultaneous updates are Gibbs updates. The frame exponent gives the outgoing term `u_i g_(x+i) w_i^*` and incoming term `u_i(x-i)^* g_(x-i) w_i(x-i)`, as implemented. The U(1) phase formulas are the corresponding scalar cosines. Even L is essential to both checkerboards; odd L is not an implemented valid extension. Below the SU(2) norm floor the code is not the stated exact kernel; see defect 6.

For the SU(2) rejection sampler, its proposed r=`lam2` has density proportional to `sqrt(r) exp(-2 kappa r)`. The acceptance probability is `sqrt(1-r)` on [0,1]. Therefore a0=1-2r has density `sqrt(1-a0^2) exp(kappa a0)`; a uniform imaginary direction and multiplication by the unit staple give the desired conditional law. This derivation checks the normalization independently of the controls. The zero-staple defect is treated below.

**Proxy implementation: HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION.** `proxy` sums over **all L^3 base sites**, divided by `sqrt(L^3)`; 1.4 instead prints the z=0 plane sum divided by L. These are generally different regressors. Both are admissible in LB, so the computed bound remains a valid population construction after naming the actual proxy. `proxy_sm` averages corresponding links on slices 1–3 before normalization. A common gauge transformation preserves the quaternion norm and rotates all these links equivariantly. The holonomy and plaquette then transform by conjugation at the same base site. Their imaginary dot product is invariant. Independent gauge changes on different slices would require temporal parallel transports; the temporal-gauge residual orbit here uses one common transformation, so none are missing. The numerical norm floor also preserves equivariance, though it is not literal unit normalization at a zero norm.
