## 3. Strong coupling with spatial plaquettes present

**PROVED (a uniform sign in the original links).** For $0\le\beta\le10^{-6}$, every $k\ge1$ and every admitted periodic cube, the physical transition from *each* $U$ has density $b_k(U,V)=T^k(U,V)\psi(V)/(\lambda^k\psi(U))$ relative to product Haar in the original output $V$, with
$$
                      D_{E,V} b_k(U,V)>0 .
$$
Thus a full independent commutator on every original output link can be extracted with probability one. Its entrance marginal is $D_E\rho\,dH$. This distinction matters for (A).

**PROVED (local calculation).** Expand the $k$-step integral and substitute the Perron equation once for the terminal $\psi(V)$. The endpoint half-weights then give $M(V)^2$. Freeze the previous and the added next slice and all temporal gauge links. Up to positive factors independent of $V$, the density is
$$
 B(V)=\prod_{q\in Q} e^{\beta w_q(V)}.
$$
There are four spatial faces and two temporal faces incident to each output edge. Each face reads at most four output edges, once each. The temporal faces may read only one. The same representation works at $k=1$. All frozen variables are integrated against positive weights. Derivatives in $V$ pass through these finite-dimensional compact integrals.

**PROVED (size of a connected contribution).** Put $b_q=e^{\beta w_q}-1$ and join two faces when they share an output edge. This graph has degree at most 20. For any nonempty connected set $C$ of faces set
$$
 z_C=D_{E(C)}\prod_{q\in C} b_q,
$$
where $E(C)$ is the set of output edges read by $C$. At each $V$, $|z_C|\le b^{|C|}$ with $b=e^{256\beta}-1$. Indeed expand every $b_q$ in powers $h_q\ge1$. In the resulting product of normalized traces, edge $e$ occurs $d_e=\sum_{q\ni e}h_q$ times, including inverse occurrences. One invariant unit derivative inserts a generator of operator norm one, up to sign, at one occurrence. Two derivatives give at most $d_e^2$ terms for each of the three generators. The same count applies after differentiating other edges, since the inserted matrices have norm one. The absolute value of each product of normalized traces is at most one. Hence all edge operators cost at most
$$
 \prod_e(1+3d_e^2)\le 4^{\sum_e d_e}\le 256^{\sum_q h_q}.
$$
Here $1+3d^2\le4^d$ for integers $d\ge0$, by induction starting at $d=1$. The exponential series now gives the bound and justifies termwise differentiation.

**PROVED (sign in arbitrary volume).** For $A\subseteq Q$, let $Z_A=D_E\prod_{q\in A}(1+b_q)$. On expanding in selected faces, disjoint connected components have disjoint edge sets. Their weights therefore multiply *after* applying $D_E$. This gives a sum over compatible connected sets with weights $z_C$. For $q\in A$,
$$
 Z_A= Z_{A\setminus\{q\}}+
 \sum_{C\subseteq A\,\mathrm{connected},\ q\in C}
 z_C Z_{A\setminus N[C]},
$$
where $N[C]$ deletes $C$ and its neighbours. Induct on $|A|$ to prove $Z_A/Z_{A\setminus\{q\}}\ge41/42$ for every deletion. Start with $Z_\varnothing=1$. For $|C|=m$, deleting at most $21m$ vertices from the smaller denominator bounds the ratio in the sum by $(42/41)^{21m}\le2^m$. The last inequality follows from $(1-1/42)^{21}\ge1-21/42$. There are at most $20^{2(m-1)}$ connected sets of size $m$ containing $q$. A deterministic traversal of a rooted spanning tree takes $2(m-1)$ neighbour choices and recovers the set of visited vertices. The relative correction thus has modulus at most $2b/(1-800b)$. For $\beta\le10^{-6}$, $b\le512\beta\le1/1600$, using $e^x\le(1-x)^{-1}$ for $0\le x<1$. The correction is at most $1/400<1/42$. This closes the induction. Integrating the positive result gives the asserted sign. Integration against the stationary input gives $D_E\rho$. The inverse identity of row 1132 supplies the stated entrance law.

**PROVED (what this does to the requested sign).** In the prescribed chart the density to differentiate is $j_k(x,y)=[b_k(Sx,V)/\rho(V)]_{V=Sy}$. The calculation above differentiates $b_k$ in $V$. ## 4. Fixed physical time and the upper bound on the extracted mass

**PROVED (a volume bound using only physical Wilson integrals).** Fix $t$, $k=\lfloor t/a\rfloor\ge1$ and $23/10\le\beta\le13/5$. Choose $N$ edge-disjoint elementary plaquettes in the spatial cube. One can take $N=n^3/4$ for even $n$ by choosing one $12$-plaquette per $2\times2$ square in each plane of fixed third coordinate. Pick independent fair centre signs $\sigma_e$ on *all* input links and let $c_i=\prod_{e\in q_i}\sigma_e$. Run the actual Perron-normalized Wilson slab from $U_e=\sigma_e I$. For definiteness, predict $d_i(V)=\operatorname{sign}w_{q_i}(V)$ from the physical output, breaking ties at $+1$. Any other joint decoder using $V$ is allowed. Put $B_N=N^{-1}\sum_i1_{d_i=c_i}$ and
$$
 s_4=\frac{1+(1-2/\pi)^4}{2},\qquad
 M_N=2^{-|E|}\sum_\sigma\int
 1_{B_N\le s_4+u}\,
 \frac{T^k(\sigma I,V)\psi(V)}{\lambda^k\psi(\sigma I)}\,dH(V).
$$
For $0<u<1-s_4$ and $4Nu^2>1$,
$$
 p_*\ \le\ \min\left(1,\frac{M_N}{1-(4Nu^2)^{-1}}\right).                 \tag{1}
$$
In particular, if the Wilson mean of $B_N$ is at least $s_4+2u$ and its variance is $v_N$, the numerator is at most $v_N/u^2$. Recovery of at least 51 percent of the bits with probability tending to one would force $p_*\to0$ on that sequence. Indeed $\pi<22/7$ gives $(1-2/\pi)^4<(4/11)^4<1/50$, so $s_4<51/100$. This tests a volume-growing family on the *full* Wilson law. The right side contains no Moser map, unknown entrance, or derivative of the slab.

**PROVED (why singular input data are allowed).** Transposing the domination gives $J_k\ge p W_E r^*$, since $J_k$ is reversible in Haar coordinates and $W_E$ is symmetric. Here $r^*$ is the conditional in the other direction of the coupling $H r$. For every continuous nonnegative test the left side is continuous in the starting point. On the right, convolution of a bounded measurable function with an $L^1$ kernel is continuous because translations act continuously in $L^1$. Domination therefore extends to every starting point and then to every Borel test by measure order. Global SU(2) conjugation commutes with the entire Moser flow. Each central configuration stays in its fixed-point set $\{\pm I\}^E$, which is discrete. Thus $S(\sigma I)=\sigma I$. Conditional on the extracted branch, the experiment seen by any physical decoder consists of independently noisy signs followed by the channel $r^*$ and the map $S$. Giving the decoder all noisy links can only improve its performance.

**PROVED (the noisy-sign experiment).** For a class angle $0<\theta<\pi$, the Haar commutator density is $h(\theta)=(\pi-\theta)/(2\sin\theta)$. Indeed the Frobenius multipliers give the character series $\sum_{d\ge1}\sin(d\theta)/(d\sin\theta)$ in $L^2(H)$ since $\sum d^{-2}<\infty$. Taking the imaginary part of $-\log(1-r e^{i\theta})$ and letting $r\uparrow1$ sums it. Under a fair input sign the output angle has density $\tfrac12\sin\theta$ with respect to $d\theta$. The posterior sign mean is $1-2\theta/\pi$, whose mean absolute value is $1-2/\pi$ by integration. Given all noisy links, the disjoint parity bits are independent. The best success probability for bit $i$ is $(1+\prod_{e\in q_i}|1-2\theta_e/\pi|)/2$. Even for a joint randomized decoder, condition on its predictions and on the noisy links. Couple each bit’s success indicator below an independent Bernoulli with this optimal conditional parameter. After averaging, the optimal indicators are independent with common mean $s_4$. Thus the extracted branch’s number of successes is dominated by $\operatorname{Bin}(N,s_4)$. Chebyshev gives the denominator of (1). Multiplying by its branch mass proves (1).

**PROVED (branch comparison).** Conditional on an arbitrary commutator entrance $z$, freeze three increments on a plaquette. ## 5. Locality of the specified Moser map

**PROVED (intermediate times).** At every fixed $\beta>0$ and $0<s<1$, the Moser map $S_s$ fails the following weak form of uniform locality. One cannot approximate every $w_q\circ S_s$ in Haar $L^2$, to an arbitrarily small common error, by functions of links within a common radius of $q$ on all cubes.

**PROVED (separation of the two slice laws).** Let $F=\sum_q w_q$ on a spatial cube, $|Q_s|=3n^3$. Keep the electric coupling fixed at $\beta$ and normalize its constant eigenvalue to one. Vary the spatial coupling $\gamma$ and call the Perron root $\lambda(\gamma)$. The electric transfer $C_E P$ dominates the constant projection in quadratic-form order by its nonnegative multipliers. Sandwiching gives $\lambda(\gamma)\ge\int e^{\gamma F}dH$ and $\lambda(0)=1$. The function $\log\lambda(\gamma)$ is convex as the limit of $l^{-1}\log\langle1,T_\gamma^l1\rangle$, each a log integral of exponentials of affine functions. Differentiating the simple Perron eigenvalue gives $(\log\lambda)'(\beta)=\int F\,d\pi$, since $T'_\gamma=(FT_\gamma+T_\gamma F)/2$.

**PROVED.** Select one $12$-plaquette at the corner of each $4^3$ spatial cell. Bias Haar by the product of $e^{\gamma w_q}$ on these plaquettes, normalizing each by $I(\gamma)=\int e^{\gamma w}dH_{\mathrm{SU}(2)}$. Every unselected plaquette contains an untouched Haar link, so its biased mean is zero. Jensen on their sum gives $\lambda(\gamma)\ge I(\gamma)^{n^3/64}$. Symmetry and $\int w^2=1/4$ give $I(\beta)\ge1+\beta^2/8$. Convexity now gives
$$
 \int (F/|Q_s|)\,d\pi\ \ge\ c_\beta=
 \frac{\log(1+\beta^2/8)}{192\beta}>0,\qquad \int F\,dH=0.
$$
The image of Haar at time $s$ is $(1-s)H+s\pi$. The variance of $F/|Q_s|$ in this mixture is at least $s(1-s)c_\beta^2$. If the approximations existed, clip them to $[-1,1]$. The average of the radius-$R$ approximants has variance $O((R+1)^3/n^3)$ by independence on disjoint Haar supports. The $L^2$ error of the average is at most the common error. First let $n$ grow and then send that error to zero. This contradicts the variance lower bound. At $s=1$ the mixture term vanishes.

**PROVED (the endpoint reads distant plaquettes).** Take two elementary spatial plaquettes $Q,R$ separated by at least three lattice units. Write $f=w_Q$, $h=w_R$, $g=h^2-1/4$, and let $S_\beta=S_1$ for the actual isotropic Wilson transfer. In each finite cube,
$$
 A_{QR}(\beta):=\int f(S_\beta y)\,g(y)\,dH(y)
        =-\frac{\beta^3}{25344}+O_n(\beta^4).                         \tag{2}
$$
The coefficient is independent of the separation. In particular a single finite coding radius on an interval of sufficiently small bare couplings is impossible.

**PROVED (which terms enter).** Up to order three the density $\rho$ has the Taylor coefficients of $e^{\beta F}/\int e^{\beta F}dH$. Indeed $M\psi$ is constant plus $O_n(\beta)$. Gauss projection removes every block whose support has a vertex of degree one. Each remaining nonempty support contains a cycle and hence at least four edges. The normalized electric multiplier is $O_n(\beta^4)$ on those blocks by GL3–4. The Perron equation then gives $\psi/M=\mathrm{constant}+O_n(\beta^5)$. Smooth finite-volume Taylor coefficients also follow directly from the analytic kernel. To project onto $g=\chi_1(U_R)/4$, each of the four edges of $R$ must carry at least two fundamental factors. Invariant derivatives and the inverse product Laplacian preserve each edge’s representation space. At order at most three, one face must read an edge of $Q$ to move $f$. The other two must both be $R$. If the near face is $Q'$, its contribution after averaging the near variables contains $\int\nabla f\cdot\nabla w_{Q'}\,dH$, zero unless $Q'=Q$ by Haar orthogonality. Terms of lower order vanish. Thus the coefficient can be computed with density proportional to $e^{\alpha f+\gamma h}$, keeping precisely $\alpha\gamma^2$.

**PROVED (calculation of the coefficient).** Put $\lambda_f=12$ and $\eta=32$. The per-edge Casimir eigenvalues are 3 in the fundamental and 8 in spin one. Hence $-\Delta f=\lambda_f f$, $-\Delta h=\lambda_f h$, and $-\Delta g=\eta g$. Set $B=e^{\gamma h}/\int e^{\gamma h}$. At $\alpha=0$ only the $R$ variables move. Write their base flow as $Y_s$. The first variation of the endpoint displacement in the $Q$ variables is $\nabla f$ times
$$
 I_\gamma=\int_0^1\frac{[(\lambda_f-\Delta_R)^{-1}B](Y_s)}
                                  {1-s+sB(Y_s)}\,ds .
$$
Indeed the first variation of the Poisson source is $fB$, and the base velocity in $Q$ vanishes identically. Put $\ell=\lambda_f$. Use $B=1+\gamma h+\gamma^2g/2+O(\gamma^3)$ and $h(Y_s)=h+s\gamma|\nabla h|^2/\ell+O(\gamma^2)$. The resolvent sends $1,h,g$ to $1/\ell,h/(2\ell),g/(\ell+\eta)$ respectively. The first-order coefficient in the ratio integrates to zero. The integrated second-order coefficient is
$$
 \left(\frac1{2(\ell+\eta)}-\frac1{4\ell}\right)g
       -\frac{|\nabla h|^2}{12\ell^2}+\frac{h^2}{12\ell}
 =\left(\frac1{88}-\frac1{48}+\frac1{108}\right)g=-g/4752 .
$$
Here $|\nabla h|^2=\ell h^2-\eta g/2$ by applying $\Delta$ to $h^2$. Pair the displacement with $\nabla f$, then integrate against $g$. The two factors are $\int|\nabla f|^2=3$ and $\int g^2=1/16$. This gives (2).

**PROVED.** The inverse of $-\Delta$ on a mean-zero function analytic in this tube has supremum bound $Cb$ times its tube supremum. Integrate its heat evolution over positive time. ## 6. The free-energy side

**PROVED (a uniform physical gap does not bound the torus excess in general).** The counterexample has a fixed number of SU(2) variables per site, nearest-neighbour weights, hypercubic symmetry and a simple finite-volume vacuum. At each site take four spins $u^\mu$, one for each direction. A spin of type $\mu$ interacts only with the next spin of that type in direction $\mu$, with the SU(2) heat kernel $C_a=\exp[(ma/3)\Delta]$, $m>0$. Integrate normalized Haar. Each bond kernel is symmetric, pointwise positive, and a positive operator. Reflection positivity across a time cut follows by factoring its crossing kernels with $C_a^{1/2}$ and keeping the uncrossed weights in the two reflected halves. Permuting axes permutes the four spin types. These are local spin variables with a different action from Wilson plaquettes.

**PROVED (spectrum and pressure).** A closed line of $b$ bonds contributes
$$
 Z_{\rm line}(b)=\operatorname{Tr} C_a^b
   = 1+\sum_{d\ge2}d^2\exp[-(ma/3)b(d^2-1)] .
$$
In any chosen time direction the time-parallel lines have tensor-product transfer $C_a^{\otimes n^3}$. All perpendicular variables contribute a rank-one factor with kernel $A(V)A(V')$, where $A$ is the square root of their within-slice weight. Its Perron eigenfunction is $A$. The ground-state transform therefore redraws the perpendicular slice independently of the input with density $A^2/\int A^2$. Thus the nonzero transfer spectrum of each cube has a simple vacuum and exact physical gap $m$ for every $a,n$. Counting four families of $n^3$ closed lines gives $F_a(n)=4n^3\log Z_{\rm line}(n)$. At each fixed $a$, $F_a(n)/n^4\to0$, so its bulk pressure is zero. At fixed $L=na$,
$$
 D_a(n)\ \ge\ 4(L/a)^3\log(1+4e^{-mL})\ \longrightarrow\ +\infty .
$$
This uses the cutoff limit in the definition of $D^+$, at precisely the symmetric torus. Even the first excited multiplet on each line provides the divergence.

**PROVED (this comparison also passes the commutator sign test).** Here $\rho$ depends only on perpendicular spins. The product Poisson equation and the prescribed Moser flow leave the time-parallel variables untouched and use only perpendicular variables for the other coordinates. In Haar coordinates the perpendicular output consequently has constant transition density one. For a time-parallel spin at physical time $s$, the nonconstant part of the deconvolved heat density is bounded in sup norm by $\sum_{d\ge2}d^4e^{-(m/3)s(d^2-1)}$, since the character of a dimension-$d$ representation has absolute value at most $d$. The series tends to zero as $s\to\infty$ by domination by a convergent Gaussian series at any positive time. Choose $t$ so the bound at $t/2$ is below one. Then for every $a\le t/2$, $s=a\lfloor t/a\rfloor\ge t/2$ and each deconvolution is positive of mass one. Tensoring gives $p=1$ on the full slice. Thus even the specified form of extraction need not control the free energy in a local, hypercubic compact-spin family.

**OPEN (Wilson-specific implication).** The gap controls the Boltzmann factor of each excited state. The Wilson transfer still needs a uniform bound on the *number* of relevant states, or on the smaller-torus excess that pays for it. The distinction already appears in `prior/gap-lit-casimir.md`, lines 14–32, and row 1032’s Lemma CHAIN-4 identifies four nonnegative thermal contributions to $D$. Row 720, Corollaries Z1, Z2 and Theorem ZD-c give a sufficient extra input. Suppose the physical cube gaps are at least $m_0>0$ in lower cutoff limit on a tail and $\liminf_{L\to\infty,\ L\in\mathcal P}D^+(L)/L<m_0/64$. Then choose $L$ large with $4Lm_0>256D^+(L)+30$. At side $4L$ the strict margin passes to all sufficiently fine cutoffs. Corollary Z1 gives the seed and ZD-c gives (BDD-Delta). The spin example shows why some control beyond the spectral lower bound is necessary for a general theorem. The exact Wilson implication with only the gap postulated remains open.
