**HOLDS — lines 483–489, 514–520 and 572–573.** For $S=\sum_{p\cap V(Q)\ne\varnothing}(1-\operatorname{ReTr}U_p/2)$ with the prescribed exterior and every $d\ge314$, each minimizer has at least one noncentral tangential link on the lower face. 1. **HOLDS, lines 491–499.** Put $n=3d-1$, $w=2d-1$, and $\Phi=Q\cap\{x_3=1\}$. Suppose the links in directions 1 and 2 based in $\Phi$ are central. The square immediately below each such link has its other three links prescribed. It costs two for a mismatch with the prescribed parallel link $u_\mu(x)$ and zero for a match. Write $D$ for the mismatches. There are $f=n(w-1)^2$ internal face squares of type $(1,2)$. The prescribed parallel products on their lower translates are $-1$. A face square therefore costs two unless an odd number of its edges belong to $D$. Each mismatched edge can reverse the parity of at most two squares. The two lists of plaquettes are disjoint. If $2|D|\ge f$, the lower squares alone pay $f$. Otherwise the two lists pay at least $2|D|+2(f-2|D|)\ge f$.
2. **HOLDS, lines 500–507.** In the circle generated by a unit imaginary quaternion $J$, put $\theta=\pi/4$, $s=(-1)^{x_1+x_2}$ and choose face links $W_0=1$, $W_1=e^{s\theta J}$, $W_2=(-1)^{x_1}e^{-s\theta J}$. A $(1,2)$ product is $-e^{4s\theta J}=1$. A product involving direction 0 is one by independence of $x_0$. Thus on the entire face plane one may integrate along lattice paths to write $W_\mu(x)=g(x)g(x+e_\mu)^{-1}$, since elementary square moves and backtracking leave the path product unchanged. Extend $g$ by one off the plane. Use this pure gauge on all links based in $Q$, and the specified exterior everywhere else.
3. **HOLDS, lines 506–511, counting positive links by base.** Every square crossing from $x_3=0$ to $1$ that sees a free link has precisely one, its upper tangential link. Its total contribution over the face is $2(1-1/\sqrt2)nw^2$, including zero in direction 0. For every other square meeting $V(Q)$, nonzero cost needs an exterior link differing from the pure gauge. Such a link has **base** on $x_3=1$ outside $\Phi$, at horizontal sup distance at most one from $\Phi$. The crossing squares account for level zero. Off levels zero and one both fields assign the identity to every exterior link. The enlarged face minus the face has $(n+2)(w+2)^2-nw^2=32d^2+2$ sites. At each base there are four positive links, each in at most six squares of cost at most two. Overcounting gives
   $S_{\rm trial}\le B=(2-\sqrt2)nw^2+1536d^2+96$.

## Persistence along the tuning

**FAILS as an inference — lines 356–361, 415–422, 579–582 and 594–602.** The first unsupported step is the asserted stiffness of the *coupled* face. In fact the attribution at lines 416–418 to plaquettes wholly on the free face has a counterexample. Start with the flat $W$ in NC. Choose $h_t\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, equal to one on $Q_-$, equal to a rotation taking $J$ to $-J$ on $Q_+$, with the change across $Q_0$. At a face site of split coordinate $t=x_0$, replace a tangential link by
$$
W'_\mu(x)=h_t W_\mu(x) h_{t+\mathbf1_{\mu=0}}^{-1}.
$$
The two staggered readings now point in opposite directions. All wholly free face plaquettes still have product one, by cancellation around the square. In particular the varying split-direction links $W'_0=h_t h_{t+1}^{-1}$ can absorb the twist inside those squares. This changes the actual boundary readings and is allowed as a trial configuration. The changed boundary readings distinguish the two configurations modulo $G_Q$. Extending the endpoint transformation to all free links preserves the trace of every wholly free plaquette. Plaquettes touching the prescribed exterior can cost energy. For example, the lower $(0,3)$ pinning square costs $1-\operatorname{ReTr}(h_t h_{t+1}^{-1})/2$. Neither this construction nor a twist computed with other links held fixed bounds the effective stiffness below after integration. Annealing preserves the global symmetry but supplies no sign, size or range estimate for the induced face interactions.

Here is a separate Wilson example showing exactly how little NC implies about $\rho$. Fix any $d\ge314$ and finite $m$. At every positive $\beta_W$ the minimizers of $\beta_WS$ are the same. At sufficiently small positive $\beta_W$ the normalized density $f$ relative to product Haar $\lambda$ satisfies $\|f-1\|_\infty\le1/4$ uniformly in the exteriors and shapes at these fixed $d,m$. Indeed $0\le S\le2P$ for at most $P\le24(md-1)^4$ plaquettes, so $e^{-2\beta_W P}\le f\le e^{2\beta_WP}$. For disjoint admissible $X,Y$, put $\epsilon=\|f-1\|_\infty$. Their Haar $L^2$ norms are at most $(1-\epsilon)^{-1/2}$ and $|\lambda X|\le\epsilon\|X\|_{2,\lambda}$ by centering under $f\lambda$, likewise for $Y$. Product Haar factorizes $\lambda(XY)$. Hence
$$
|\nu(XY)|\le\frac{\epsilon+\epsilon^2}{1-\epsilon}\le\frac5{12}<\frac12.
$$
Thus the same classical NC conclusion coexists with a slab constant below $1/2$ at positive coupling. This example concerns the inference from minimizers alone. Weak-coupling persistence would require control of thermal fluctuations, the relative orientation of the two readings, and the growth of the box simultaneously.
