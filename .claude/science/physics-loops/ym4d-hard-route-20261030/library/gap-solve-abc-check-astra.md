## Row 1190 · route verdicts

**Free-boundary induction — FAILS as a general no-go (C113–124, 218).** The decoupling observation holds when the deleted interactions separate the *link interaction graph*. Each surviving plaquette belongs to one component, and product Haar then factors. Reintroducing the interactions changes the law on the separating links. A crossover story for one field does not verify small covariances at $d$ in *every* free box and for *every* field in the proposed class. Nor do the pinned or unbounded-field examples test precisely that premise. Row 1011, `gap-pinned-cell-gauge-max.md` 429–432, explicitly distinguishes these classes.

There is even a natural interpretation of “all normalized covariances” under which the recursion **holds**. In each free-volume law suppose the maximal correlation between arbitrary sets at graph distance at least $d$ is at most $\epsilon<1$. Normalize by the two standard deviations and allow every bounded test of each set. For sets $A,B$ at distance at least $2d$, let $S$ be the shell at distance $d$ from $A$. It separates $A$ and $B$ and is at least $d$ from both. Conditioned on its links the two sides are independent, by factorization of the Gibbs density. For centered $f(A)$, the test $h=\mathbb E[f\mid S]$ gives $\|h\|_2^2=\mathbb E(fh)\le\epsilon\|f\|_2\|h\|_2$. Applying the same argument to $g(B)$ bounds its conditional mean. Cauchy–Schwarz on the shell now gives $|\mathbb E(fg)|\le\epsilon^2\|f\|_2\|g\|_2$. The argument is uniform in the allowed fields and uses no bound at fixed boundary pins. This strong premise concerns arbitrary sets in the *same* free law. A weaker test on small free subboxes or on local sup-norm-bounded observables would need a comparison with the exterior law. C119–123 establishes neither a Wilson counterexample nor a general impossibility for that weaker test.

**Multivariate half-plane — HOLDS AFTER CORRECTION, by a cube counterexample (C139–150, 159–173, 205–206, 220).** The deduction at C166 fails. The corrected versions of Theorems Z and ZB give a lower bound on a lattice mass, conditional on transport, strong coupling, zero-freeness of the appropriate partition functions, and a domain of the required shape. The periodic theorem uses the zero-flux partition function. The free-wall theorem uses its own trace. Nonvanishing of separate summands need not imply nonvanishing of their sector average. See `gap-zero-transport.md` §§5–6 and the domain correction in `gap-zero-transport-check.md` §§3, 5. The claimed harmonic-measure power is consistent with the ideal domain. The map $w=\log(z/r_0)$ takes $\operatorname{Re} z>0$, $|z|>r_0$, to a half-strip of half-width $\pi/2$. On the real axis the harmonic measure of the inner semicircle is $(4/\pi)\arctan(r_0/\beta)$ by the half-strip sine series (Lemma HM). A bounded-domain argument needs an exhaustion or suitable finite lenses as $\beta$ grows. A law $a(\beta)=e^{-c_a\beta(1+o(1))}$ for *spacing* is compatible as an inequality of numbers with $m_{\rm lat}(\beta)=1$, or with $m_{\rm lat}(\beta)\asymp1/\beta$. A contradiction by this route would also need a mass **upper** bound, e.g. finite-energy spectral weight surviving in physical units on the relevant volumes. The number $11/(6\pi)=\pi/(2c_a)$ equates two exponents for $c_a=3\pi^2/11$. A width bound would require the additional spectral comparison. `gap-skeleton-max-b.md` 581 already records the plaquette-field proposal and an equal-coupling cube *numerical guide*. Its numbers are not used here.

For the actual assertion “every $\operatorname{Re} b_i>0$”, there is a direct hand test. In the cube formula take one coupling $b$ and the other five $z$. The series gives $A_1(z)=1+z^2/8+O(z^4)$, $A_2=A_1'$, $A_2(z)=z/4+O(z^3)$, and $A_d(z)=O(z^{d-1})$. The function $f(t)=A_1(it)$ has a simple zero $t_*\in(0,4)$. Indeed $f(0)=1$, and at 4 its alternating series has decreasing tail and satisfies

$$
 f(4)\ \le\ 1-2+\frac43-\frac49+\frac4{45}=-\frac1{45}.
$$

Termwise differentiation gives $f''+3f'/t+f=0$ on $t>0$, so $f=f'=0$ there would give the identically zero solution. Thus $b_*=it_*$ is a simple zero of $A_1$, with $A_2(b_*)=A_1'(b_*)\ne0$.

Divide the cube sum by $A_1(z)^5$ near zero. Uniformly for $b$ near $b_*$ the result is

$$
 A_1(b)+4A_2(b)(z/4)^5+O(z^7).
$$

The $d\ge3$ tail starts at order ten, by the factorial series above. The complex implicit function theorem now gives a simple zero

$$
 b(z)=it_*-4(z/4)^5+O(z^7).
$$

Choose $z=\delta e^{i\pi/5}$ for sufficiently small positive $\delta$. All five $z$’s have positive real part, and $\operatorname{Re} b(z)=\delta^5/256+O(\delta^7)>0$. To embed the example in a larger ordinary Wilson box or torus, set all other couplings to zero first, then give them all a sufficiently small positive real value. The finite partition function is entire in these parameters, so its simple zero persists with positive real part. A link-independent exponential normalization of the Wilson weight also has no zeros. This gives a zero with all six couplings in the open right half-plane. It gives neither a Fisher zero on the common-coupling diagonal nor the width or location of a zero-free region about that diagonal. The proposed explanation at C170–172 involving the mixed-action endpoint has no estimate relating its distance to a complex zero.
