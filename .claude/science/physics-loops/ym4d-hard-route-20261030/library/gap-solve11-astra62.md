If a seed with \(5041s=q<1\) is given at spatial side \(b_0\), set \(v=q^{2^k}/71\) for \(2^kb_0\le b<2^{k+1}b_0\). The large-cube assertions below concern \(v\) sufficiently small. **PROVED.** A periodic seed gives \(\|p_t-1\|_{L^2(\pi\otimes\pi)}^2\le v\). An all-magnetic neutral seed gives a measurable gauge-invariant label \(\ell\) on the periodic spatial slice, taking eight equiprobable values. It satisfies \(\ell(gx)=g+\ell(x)\) almost surely. For the latter seed,
\[
 \iint\big|p_t(x,y)-8{\bf1}_{\ell(x)=\ell(y)}\big|^2\,d\pi(x)d\pi(y)\le C v,
 \qquad
 {\mathbb P}_{\pi,P^t}\{\ell(X)\ne\ell(Y)\}\le C v.                    \tag{1}
\]
Thus the reference law in (1) first chooses a uniform label, then draws two independent slices with that label.

**PROVED (spectrum used in (1)).** In the periodic branch the squared norm is exactly the excited trace at \(2t\), bounded by propagation. In the magnetic branch put \(X_q(u)=\operatorname{Tr}_q P^u\) for an electric character, using \(q=0\) for the trivial character within this paragraph. Propagation gives \(X_0(t)\le1+v\), as \(t\ge 2^k b_0/2\). At time \(b\), each nonidentity temporal center insertion has trace \(I_g\in[0,8v]\). Rotate the cube so a nonzero component is spatial. Its magnetic sector is nonzero, its neutral weight belongs to the propagated excited sum, and each of its eight electric weights is at most that neutral weight by row 1161 (3). The cube has the same vacuum normalization in the rotated frame. Fourier inversion gives \(|X_q(b)-X_0(b)|\le14v\), since the identity insertion cancels, and \(X_q(t)\le X_0(t)\le1+v\) by positivity of the twisted integrals. Consequently its largest eigenvalue \(r_q\) satisfies
\[
 r_q^t\ge X_q(b)/X_q(t)\ge(1-14v)/(1+v),\qquad
 \sum_{i\ge2}r_{q,i}^{2t}\le C v.                                     \tag{2}
\]
In the neutral sector the largest eigenfunction is the constant. Its next eigenvalue \(\rho\) has \(\rho^t\le v\). These bounds imply simplicity of each leading level for small \(v\).

**PROVED (construction of the labels).** Take a real unit leading eigenfunction \(h_i\) in each of three basis characters. The absolute value is neutral. For the Dirichlet form \(\mathcal E_t(f)=\langle f,(1-P^t)f\rangle\), the stationary two-slice formula gives \(\mathcal E_t(|h_i|)\le\mathcal E_t(h_i)=1-r_i^t\). Hence
\[
 1-\big(\pi |h_i|\big)^2\le (1-r_i^t)/(1-\rho^t)\le C v .
\]
Round to \(\sigma_i=\operatorname{sgn}h_i\), taking an equivariant sign where \(h_i=0\). The sign of the trace of a winding loop in direction \(i\) supplies that choice off a Haar null set. The loop holonomy is Haar under the reference measure and \(\pi\) has a density there. Then \(\|\sigma_i-h_i\|_2^2=2(1-\pi |h_i|)\le C v\). Since \(0\le I-P^t\le I\), \(\mathcal E_t(\sigma_i)\le 2\mathcal E_t(h_i)+2\|\sigma_i-h_i\|^2\le C v\). The three signs define \(\ell\) by \(\sigma_i=(-1)^{\ell_i}\). Equivariance and invariance of \(\pi\) imply its exact uniform distribution. A sign changes with probability \(\mathcal E_t(\sigma_i)/2\), giving the second bound of (1) by the union bound.

**PROVED.** For each character \(\chi\), the product \(\sigma_\chi=(-1)^{\chi\cdot\ell}\) has unit norm and \(\mathcal E_t(\sigma_\chi)\le C v\), by the same union bound. Every nonleading eigenvalue at time \(t\) in that character is at most \(\sqrt{C v}\) by (2). The squared projection of \(\sigma_\chi\) off the leading line is therefore at most \(C v/(1-\sqrt{C v})\). The comparison projection has kernel \(\sum_\chi \sigma_\chi(x)\sigma_\chi(y)=8{\bf1}_{\ell(x)=\ell(y)}\). This proves (1).

## A Shannon information test on a single periodic cylinder

**PROVED (two-sided inequality, with no seed hypothesis).** Let \(F\) be the ordinary Wilson transfer on one spatial box. Write \(K^t(x,y)\) for its nonnegative \(t\)-step kernel in product Haar measure, \(\lambda=\|F\|\), \(Z_t=\operatorname{Tr}F^t\), \(X_t=Z_t/\lambda^t\). On the ordinary periodic cylinder of time length \(2t\), call \(I_{2t}\) the Shannon mutual information, in nats, between its opposite spatial slices. For all integer \(t\) for which the displayed traces exist,
\[
             \boxed{\ \log X_{2t}\ \le I_{2t}\ \le
                     2\log X_t-\log X_{2t}=J(t).\ }                     \tag{3}
\]
Here \(J(t)\) varies the time length at fixed coupling. The best product affinity of the two slices is the one computed in row 1147. The upper inequality uses the diagonal law at the shorter period. It controls the unbounded Shannon integrand. [Row 1147, §9](gap-solve3-astra62.md) · [Earlier two-slice and rectangle affinities](gap-solve7-astra62.md)

**PROVED.** The joint density is \(L(x,y)=K^t(x,y)^2/Z_{2t}\). Its equal marginal densities are \(l(x)=K^{2t}(x,x)/Z_{2t}\). Positivity of the kernel and the operator norm give
\[
  \int\sqrt{L(x,y)l(x)l(y)}\,dx\,dy
   =\langle\sqrt l,F^t\sqrt l\rangle/\sqrt{Z_{2t}}\le X_{2t}^{-1/2}.
\]
Taking Jensen's inequality for the logarithm under \(L\) gives \(I_{2t}\ge-2\log\int\sqrt{L\,l\otimes l}\). For the other direction put \(m(x)=K^t(x,x)/Z_t\). Positivity as a quadratic form gives \(|K^t(x,y)|^2\le K^t(x,x)K^t(y,y)\). One may take the two by two Gram minor using the continuous positive kernel and averages over shrinking neighborhoods of the endpoints. Hence \(L\le e^{J(t)}m\otimes m\). Integration of the logarithm gives
\[
 I_{2t}+2D(l\|m)=D(L\|m\otimes m)\le J(t).
\]
The densities on the finite Wilson box are continuous and strictly positive at positive coupling. The constant kernel covers zero coupling. This proves (3), including the extra nonnegative marginal term.

**PROVED (direct equivalence for (W)).** Set \(m=2\lfloor n/44\rfloor\). One ordinary, untwisted path measure suffices. With the permitted fixed dyadic enlargement, (W) is equivalent to
\[
        \exists L\quad \limsup_j I_m(L/a_j)\ <\ \log(3/2).               \tag{4}
\]
Indeed (3) gives \(S(m)=X_m-1\le e^{I_m}-1\). For \(T\ge m\), writing out the positive excited eigenvalues yields \(S(T)\le S(m)^{T/m}\). At \(T=n/2\), \(T/m\ge11\), so the bound is at most \(2^{-11}<1/1650\) when \(S(m)\le1/2\). Conversely, under (W), the ordinary trace approaches one at any prescribed fixed aspect as the physical side grows dyadically, with the cutoff upper limit first. ## A stable finite sum over the Wilson zeros

**PROVED (paired zeros and their moments).** Take all four side lengths even. This includes \(4\mid n\) on the physical grid. Remove the individual action constants and write \(\widetilde Z(z)=\int\exp(z\sum_p\epsilon_p\,\operatorname{tr}U_p/2)\), allowing fixed central plaquette signs \(\epsilon_p\). Multiplication of every forward link in direction \(\mu\) by \((-1)^{\sum_{\nu<\mu}x_\nu}\) is periodic, preserves Haar and changes the sign of every plaquette trace. Thus \(\widetilde Z\) is even, with paired zeros \(\{\zeta,-\zeta\}\). It equals one at zero and \(|\widetilde Z(z)|\le e^{6V|\operatorname{Re}z|}\). For each pair take just one representative, repeated with multiplicity. Then
\[
             \widetilde Z(z)=\prod_{\{\pm\zeta\}}(1-z^2/\zeta^2).
                                                                         \tag{8}
\]
This product converges locally uniformly.

**PROVED.** The circular mean of \(\log|\widetilde Z|\) at a radius between \(2R\) and \(3R\) bounds the number of zeros in the \(R\)-disk by \(C V R\). Each zero there contributes at least \(\log2\) by the disk mean formula. Summing on dyadic annuli gives \(\sum_{|\zeta|>R}|\zeta|^{-2}\le C V/R\). Here and below harmless factors of two from pairing are absorbed into \(C\). For a radius \(r\) avoiding all zeros, subtract all interior poles \(1/(z-\zeta)\), with multiplicity, from \(\widetilde Z'/\widetilde Z\). To bound the analytic remainder by \(CV\) on \(|z|\le r/2\), divide out the disk Blaschke factors, use \(6Vr-\log|g|\ge0\) for the zero-free factor on the disk, and differentiate its Poisson integral. The mean of this nonnegative harmonic function is at most \(6Vr\) since \(|g(0)|\ge1\). The differentiated kernel on the half disk costs \(C/r\), and each reflected denominator also costs \(2/r\), with at most \(CVr\) terms. The remainder is odd, since the interior zero set is symmetric. Schwarz's estimate thus bounds it by \(CV|z|/r\) on the half disk. Let \(r\) increase at fixed box. The paired pole series is absolutely convergent away from its poles because a pair contributes \(2z/(z^2-\zeta^2)\). The product in (8) and the integral have the same logarithmic derivative and value at zero. This proves (8). **PROVED.** For the thermal ratio \(R=Z_T^2/Z_{2T}\), write \(\nu\) for the order of its divisor, positive at a zero and negative at a pole. Identical factors cancel. The action constants cancel too. ## What remains after the collar estimates

**PROVED (the full center quotient loses the branch).** Under the alternative seeds at fixed coupling, even the joint law of two *complete* antipodal path segments modulo independent center flips converges in TV to the product of the two segment marginals on the symmetric \(b^4\) tori, provided each segment length \(h\le b/4\).
