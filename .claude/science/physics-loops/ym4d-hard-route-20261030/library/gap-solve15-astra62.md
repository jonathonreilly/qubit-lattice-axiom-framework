## Cutting spatial blocks out of a Wilson time collar

**GUIDE.** Row 1155, (3)–(9), already integrates histories of a positive density of mutually plaquette-disjoint links, with short memory on typical exteriors. Row 1169, (15)–(18), removes logarithmically many links from a *single slice*. The construction below allows plaquettes with all four edges missing. The observation in a time collar consists of the spatial boundaries of three-dimensional boxes, together with the temporal links on those boundaries. The comparison uses entire fundamental Wilson configurations. It does not use the unverified estimates of row 1171. [Column estimates](gap-solve7-astra62.md) · [Single-slice erasure](gap-solve13-astra62.md)

**PROVED (exchange of two conditional paths).** Take an ordinary periodic Wilson torus with spatial side \(n\), and a time interval \([-r,r]\) strictly inside its period. Tile the spatial torus by rectangular boxes with integer side lengths in \([b,2b]\), \(b\ge4\). For \(n\ge 2b\) this is possible by dividing each coordinate into \(\lfloor n/b\rfloor\) nearly equal intervals. Call their union of boundaries the skeleton. Observe every link in these spatial boundaries throughout the interval and every temporal link on a boundary vertex within the interval. Write \(E\) for this observation. Independently sample two ordinary Wilson paths given the same \(E\). In a box, gauge-fix each interior spatial vertex at each internal time by parallel transport from the lower 1-face along a straight 1-line. Boundary gauges are the identity. The change of variables is legitimate in both copies. Conditional Haar on the forest edges gives the vertex gauges by successive translations. All other links, including temporal links incident to an interior vertex, undergo left and right Haar translations given the forest. The Wilson density depends only on the transformed links. The discarded vertex gauges are independent Haar and can be restored. At the ends of the interval one can keep the end slices fixed and use identity gauges there.

**PROVED.** For each box choose bits \(\eta_s\in\{0,1\}\), with \(\eta_{-r}=\eta_r=0\). At internal time \(s\), exchange the two copies of all spatial links of this box off the common boundary according to \(\eta_s\). Assign its off-boundary temporal links from \(s\) to \(s+1\) to the same bit \(\eta_s\). The union of the cubical boundaries separates the boxes for every action face. These exchanges preserve product reference measure in the forest gauge and fix \(E\) and both paths outside the interval. A spatial face has the same two-copy action sum after every exchange. At a temporal face with top spatial edge \(e\), write \(v^\alpha\) for that top edge and \(a^\alpha\) for the bottom edge transported to its top endpoints along the two temporal links in copy \(\alpha=1,2\). These are unit quaternions with the dot product in \(\mathbb R^4\). When \(\eta_s=\eta_{s+1}\) the two contributions are \(\beta(a^1\cdot v^1+a^2\cdot v^2)\). Otherwise the two \(v\)'s are interchanged. Conditional on a finite exchange orbit, the ordering of the copies therefore has, in each box, the one-dimensional zero-field Ising weight with fixed ends and bonds
\[
 J_s=\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_e (a^1-a^2)\cdot(v^1-v^2).                      \tag{1}
\]
The representative fixes the signs of the \(J_s\). Their absolute values are orbit invariant. Distinct boxes have independent ordering bits on this conditional law. These claims also follow by averaging over the finite group with its Haar counting probability, so a stabilizer causes no change. A top edge lying on a boundary has \(v^1=v^2\) and contributes zero. Any endpoint change of gauge held fixed in this construction leaves the formula valid with the corresponding transported edges.

**PROVED.** Put \(u=\prod_{s=-r}^{-1}\tanh J_s\), \(v=\prod_{s=0}^{r-1}\tanh J_s\) in a box, with both end labels plus. Its middle label has mean \((u+v)/(1+uv)\). Indeed the bond signs are independent with means \(\tanh J_s\) before imposing that their product around the interval is plus. Taking the conditional mean of the product along the first arm gives the formula. With \(|u|,|v|\le\delta\le1/2\), exchanging the two middle configurations in that box changes their conditional middle law in TV by at most \(2\delta/(1-\delta^2)\). With several boxes the TV costs add by the product coupling. This comparison concerns the middle configurations and the fixed exterior. It does not propose flipping every bond of a prescribed time interface at once.


## A gauge match with the entire spatial boundary fixed

**PROVED.** In one spatial box of sides in \([b,2b]\), let \(U,V\) agree on all tangential boundary links. Let \(S_U=\sum_{p\subset B}(1-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}U_p)\), and define \(S_V\) likewise. There are absolute \(c,C>0\) such that if \(S_U+S_V\le c/b^2\), an internal gauge \(g\), identity at **every** boundary vertex, satisfies
\[
             \sum_{e\subset B}|U^g_e-V_e|^2\le Cb^2(S_U+S_V).          \tag{2}
\]
All distances here are quaternion chord distances. The factor \(b^2\) is the squared length scale of the box. The estimate compares two actual connections and keeps the prescribed tangential data. It uses neither a torus gauge chart nor a smallness condition on individual link representatives.

**PROVED.** Write sites as \((i,z)\), \(0\le i\le l\), with \(z\) in the transverse rectangle. In a temporary axial gauge use \(p_U(i,z)\), the straight transport from \((0,z)\) to \((i,z)\), as the gauge at that vertex. All normal links become one. Write \(\bar U_i(e)\) for a transformed transverse edge and \(B(e)\) for its common bottom edge. The difference of consecutive \(\bar U_i(e)\)'s has squared chord equal to twice the corresponding plaquette deficit. Summing the increments up a column and applying Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
 \sum_{i,e}|\bar U_i(e)-B(e)|^2\le 2(l+1)^2 S_U,\qquad
 \sum_e|\bar U_l(e)-B(e)|^2\le 2l S_U.                                \tag{3}
\]
The same inequalities apply to \(V\).

**PROVED.** Put \(H_z=p_V(l,z)p_U(l,z)^{-1}\). It equals one on the transverse boundary. The equality of the original top edges gives \(H_z\bar U_l(e)H_{z'}^{-1}=\bar V_l(e)\), \(e=zz'\). Thus
\[
 \sum_{zz'}|H_z-B(e)H_{z'}B(e)^{-1}|^2\le Cb(S_U+S_V).
\]
The scalar \(h_z=|H_z-1|\) has each increment bounded by the corresponding summand before squaring. Sum increments from the left boundary along transverse rows. Cauchy–Schwarz gives both \(\sum_z h_z^2\le Cb^3(S_U+S_V)\) and \(\max_z h_z^2\le Cb^2(S_U+S_V)\). For sufficiently small \(c\), all \(H_z\) are in a fixed ball about one where \(H\mapsto H^t=\exp(t\log H)\) is Lipschitz with an absolute constant for \(0\le t\le1\). This follows directly from \(\log(\cos\theta+v\sin\theta)=(\theta/\sin\theta)v\sin\theta\) on \(\theta<\pi/4\) and the exponential series on that ball. The maps commute with conjugation.

**PROVED.** Use \(g(i,z)=p_V(i,z)^{-1}H_z^{i/l}p_U(i,z)\). It is the identity on all six faces. The normal mismatch costs in total at most \(C l^{-1}\sum_z h_z^2\). For a transverse edge at height \(i\), compare each temporary edge to \(B(e)\) using (3). The remaining mismatch is at most \(C|H_z-B(e)H_{z'}B(e)^{-1}|\) by the power map. Squaring and summing over heights gives (2).

Enumerate a countable dense set for the unfixed links and use the first extension within \(\beta^{-1}\) of the infimum. Compactness and continuity identify this infimum with that over all extensions. If the small ball condition fails, one can still choose a representative using \(H_z^0=1\) at all heights below the top and the exact \(H_z\) at the top. Use the interpolation above whenever all top discrepancies belong to the fixed ball. Under an internal vertex gauge \(q\), \(p_U(i,z)\) changes to \(p_U(i,z)q(i,z)^{-1}\), and \(H_z\) is invariant. ## The cost of exchanging a wide box in one step

**PROVED.** Consider the exchange experiment in a single box of minimum side \(b\ge 4\). Use the representatives constructed above, or just the straight axial forest. There are absolute \(c>0,\ b_0<\infty\) such that for \(b\ge b_0\) and \(\beta\ge1\), uniformly in the skeleton,
\[
       \int\sqrt{dP\,d(\tau_* P)}\ \le\ \exp(-c b^3).                   \tag{7}
\]
The same bound holds if outside links of both copies are also fixed and at least one of the two exchange boundaries has both its adjacent spatial layers unfixed. **PROVED (a one-link variance).** For \(z\) on the unit three-sphere with law proportional to \(e^{H\cdot z}\) times sphere Haar, write \(h=|H|\) and \(\Pi_\perp\) for projection orthogonal to \(H\). Uniformly in \(d,H\),
\[
 {\rm Var}(d\cdot z)\ge c\left[
       \frac{|d|^2}{(1+h)^2}
       +\frac{|\Pi_\perp d|^2}{1+h}\right].                            \tag{8}
\]

Within either interval a transverse unit coordinate has conditional second moment \(\sin^2\theta/3\) and mean zero. Rotational symmetry makes the radial and transverse directions uncorrelated. ## A decoder with the same area exponent

**PROVED (an explicit statistic of the intervening field).** The area suppression in (25) need not be implemented by a conditional integral. Given \(\mathcal F\), consider all fundamental lifts of its adjoints consistent with its prescribed endpoint face signs. Let \(\mathcal P_1\) denote the plaquettes containing direction 1. \[
     \sum_{p\in\mathcal P_1}w_p(V)
              +\frac{K}{\beta}\sum_{p\notin\mathcal P_1}w_p(V),         \tag{26}
\]
where \(K\) is a sufficiently large absolute constant. Return \(J\), the value of the two-end winding-sign product in the maximizing class, using \(+1\) if both sign classes attain the same maximum. \[
   \Pr_\mu(J\ne Z)\le

under the conditions of (24), with the joint limit of (25) in the second assertion. ## Can a small change of the unshared adjoints randomize the bit?

**PROVED (margin between the two sign classes).** Let \(M_z\) be the maximum in (26) among lifts with product \(z\). Both classes occur, by a spatial central cut through an interval containing just one collar. In the ordinary law, for every \(0<\gamma<2\),

**PROVED (stability under changes of continuous links).** Consider any joint law of two ordinary paths \(U,U'\) with the same \(B_0,B_{N/2}\), including any stationary update preserving these observations. Let
