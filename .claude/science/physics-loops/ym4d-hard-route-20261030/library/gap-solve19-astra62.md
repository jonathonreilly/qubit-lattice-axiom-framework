## The neutral trace from one spatial boundary condition

**PROVED (finite-volume bounds).** On a periodic spatial cube of side \(n\) let \(P_+\) average the eight spatial centre flips, and let \(K_t(x,y)\) be the kernel of \(P_+U^t\) in product link Haar measure \(dx\). Write \(D_t=\operatorname{Tr}(P_+U^t)=1+s_+(t)\). Take integer \(t\ge3\). Close two such arms into a torus of period \(2t\). Its two observed slices have probability law \(\nu\) and common marginal \(\mu\) given by
\[
 d\nu(x,y)=K_t(x,y)^2\,dx\,dy/D_{2t},\qquad
 d\mu(x)=K_{2t}(x,x)\,dx/D_{2t}.                                     \tag{7}
\]
Each arm can be formed by independently summing its eight endpoint flips before sewing. All these Wilson integrals are positive. Two projections are needed to give (7) on raw link coordinates. A single projection already gives the same partition function, but one cannot erase the second projection in the raw two-slice numerator. A measurement of the probabilities requires no vacuum eigenvalue. The normalization by \(\lambda\) cancels.

**PROVED.** For a common boundary cell \(E\) with \(\mu(E)>0\), put \(R(E)=\nu(E,E)/\mu(E)^2\). For a point \(x\) define
\[
 R(x)=D_{2t}\left(\frac{K_t(x,x)}{K_{2t}(x,x)}\right)^2.
\]
Then \(R(x)\ge D_{2t}\) at *every* point. Indeed the spectral ratios lie in \([0,1]\), so \(P_+(U^t-U^{2t})\) is a positive form. Its continuous kernel has nonnegative diagonal, by testing normalized indicators of neighborhoods shrinking to a point and passing to the limit. Strict pointwise positivity of \(K_{2t}\) follows from the electric weights. On the other hand,
\[
 \int\sqrt{R(x)}\,d\mu(x)=D_t/\sqrt{D_{2t}}.                           \tag{8}
\]
The diagonal integrals are the traces, either by sewing or by splitting a positive integer power into kernels. Hence an atypically *small* value of \(R(x)\) gives a rigorous upper heat-trace bound even if no lower bound on the probability of that boundary condition is available.

**PROVED (finite resolution).** Put \(v=3n^3\). If both coordinates of a pair range over a cell where each link is within chordal distance \(\delta\) of that link at \(x\), then the logarithm of \(K_t(y,z)/K_t(x,x)\) has absolute value at most \(C\beta v\delta\). The same holds for \(K_{2t}(y,y)/K_{2t}(x,x)\). Changing one endpoint link affects at most four spatial plaquettes with half weight and one temporal plaquette with full weight. The change in the normalized real trace is bounded by the link's chordal distance, since multiplication by unit quaternions and inversion preserve that distance. Telescope over both endpoints, integrate the pointwise bounds and average the flips. One can take \(C=6\) for the stated metric. With \(\eta=C\beta v\delta\), (7) gives
\[
 e^{-4\eta}R(x)\le R(E)\le e^{4\eta}R(x),\qquad
 D_{2t}\le e^{4\eta}R(E).                                               \tag{9}
\]
Only the cell's Haar measure cancels here. No approximation to an unknown ground-state vector is needed. The local log bound is the endpoint method of row 1178, applied before specializing to any measured scalar words. [Endpoint oscillation in row 1178](gap-solve18-astra62.md)

**PROVED (a converse even for very rare cells).** For *any* finite measurable partition \(\mathcal P\) of the slice,
\[
 1\le R(E),\qquad
 \sum_{E\in\mathcal P}\mu(E)\sqrt{R(E)}
 =\sum_{E\in\mathcal P}\sqrt{\nu(E,E)}
 \le D_t/\sqrt{D_{2t}}.                                                 \tag{10}
\]
To see the first inequality, the bilinear form on real functions with kernel \(K_t(x,y)^2/D_{2t}\) is positive semidefinite. For simple functions this follows by writing the matrix as a Gram matrix of \(K_t^{1/2}M_f K_t^{1/2}\) in the real Hilbert–Schmidt space, where the square root denotes the operator square root of \(P_+U^t\). Bounded functions follow by approximation. Pair \(1_E\) with the unit function to get \(\nu(E,E)\ge\mu(E)^2\). For the upper bound, the positive kernel satisfies \(K_t(y,z)^2\le K_t(y,y)K_t(z,z)\), by testing the positive form on two shrinking neighborhoods. Integrate over \(E^2\), take square roots and sum. In particular
\[
 \mu\{x:R(E_x)>1+h\}
 \le \frac{D_t/\sqrt{D_{2t}}-1}{\sqrt{1+h}-1},\qquad h>0,
\]
where \(E_x\) is the cell containing \(x\). Equations (9)–(10) place the infimum of the finite-cell ratios between \(e^{-4\eta}D_{2t}\) and \(D_t^2/D_{2t}\).
