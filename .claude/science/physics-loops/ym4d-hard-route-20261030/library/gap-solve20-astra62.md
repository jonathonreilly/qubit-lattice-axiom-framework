**PROVED (local relative bound).** Write \(x(g)=\frac12\operatorname{Tr}g\) and \(w(g)=\exp(\beta x(g))\), with \(\beta\ge0\). Additive Wilson action constants cancel in vacuum units. For an integer \(m\ge0\) use
\[
 w_m(g)=e^{-\beta}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{\{\beta(1+x(g))\}^{k}}{k!}.
\]
For \(h=m+1\ge4e\beta\) and \(\delta=2^{-h}\),
\[
                  (1-\delta)w(g)\le w_m(g)\le w(g).                  \tag{1}
\]
In fact \(w_m/w\) is the partial sum of a Poisson law with mean \(b=\beta(1+x)\le2\beta\). For \(b>0\), its upper tail from \(h\ge b\) is at most \(e^{-b}(eb/h)^h\), by summing the exponential generating function with tilt \(\log(h/b)\). At \(b=0\) the tail vanishes. This gives (1), also at \(-g\). Each summand has nonnegative character coefficients. Indeed \(1+x\) is the sum of the trivial character and one half of the fundamental character. Taking a power tensors the representations with positive scalar coefficients. Orthogonal decomposition into irreducibles has nonnegative multiplicities, so each convolution is a positive operator. All terms have nonnegative *values* too. Each term of degree \(k\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(k\) in the real coordinates of any one link of the plaquette, holding the others fixed. Both multiplication by a fixed unit quaternion and inversion are real linear on these coordinates.

**PROVED (control after dividing by the actual vacuum).** Let \(Z_g(v)\) be the Wilson integral on \(n^3\times v\) with a temporal seam for the spatial centre element \(g\in(\mathbb Z/2)^3\), \(n,v\ge3\). Write \(Z_{m,g}\) for the replacement of every weight by \(w_m\). Set \(B=6n^3\), the number of plaquettes per time layer. All seam integrands are positive. Hence
\[
 (1-\delta)^{Bv} Z_g(v)\le Z_{m,g}(v)\le Z_g(v).
\]
For the ordinary integral the \(v\)-th root tends to the top transfer eigenvalue \(\lambda\) at fixed \(n,\beta\), and likewise to \(\lambda_m\). For a positive trace-class transfer this follows by factoring out the top eigenvalue and bounding the remaining normalized \(v\)-th power sum by its value at any fixed earlier trace-class power. GL8 applies to \(w_m\) by the same vertex projection and the positive convolution just proved. The kernels are strictly positive on spatial configurations, so their positive top eigenvectors are neutral: replacing a top vector by its absolute value raises its quadratic form strictly if it changes sign on sets of positive measure, and two orthogonal nonnegative top vectors cannot have strictly positive cross kernel integral equal to zero. Therefore
\[
 (1-\delta)^B\le\lambda_m/\lambda\le1,\qquad
 \left|\log\frac{D_{0,m}(v)}{D_0(v)}\right|

\]
Here \(D_{0,m}\) uses \(\lambda_m\); both neutral traces are the averages of the eight seam integrals divided by their own vacuum power. These multiplicative estimates also hold for a single positive seam integral in the same units. There is no division by an alternating sector sum.

## Exact finite sums and the ordinary Wilson weight

**PROVED (a positive integration rule).** For every integer \(d\ge1\) there is a discrete probability \(\eta_d\) on \(SU(2)\) with at most \(C(d+1)^3\) points which integrates every polynomial of total real degree at most \(d\) exactly as Haar. Use the first row \((z_1,z_2)\) of an \(SU(2)\) matrix and write
\[
 z_1=\sqrt{s}\,e^{i\theta_1},\qquad z_2=\sqrt{1-s}\,e^{i\theta_2}.
\]
Normalized Haar is uniform on the unit three-sphere: multiplication by a unit quaternion preserves its area and acts transitively. With \(s=\cos^2\alpha\), the area element is proportional to \(\sin\alpha\cos\alpha\,d\alpha\,d\theta_1\,d\theta_2\). Thus \(s\) is uniform on \([0,1]\) and the independent angles are uniform. Take both angles uniform on the \(q\)-th roots of unity, where \(q\) is the first multiple of six greater than \(d\). In a monomial in the \(z_i,\bar z_i\) every angular frequency has size at most \(d\). The finite geometric sum agrees with the circle integral. A surviving monomial is \(s^a(1-s)^b\) with \(a+b\le\lfloor d/2\rfloor\).

**PROVED.** For the remaining coordinate choose \(r=\lceil(d+2)/2\rceil\). There exists a unique monic polynomial \(p_r\) orthogonal in \(L^2([0,1],ds)\) to all lower-degree polynomials, by projection. Its sign-changing roots in \((0,1)\) must number \(r\), since otherwise the product of their distinct linear factors would have degree less than \(r\) and would pair with \(p_r\) with one strict sign. They are therefore distinct and exhaust its roots. At those nodes use weights \(\int_0^1 l_i(s)ds\), where \(l_i\) are the Lagrange cardinal polynomials. Dividing a polynomial of degree at most \(2r-1\) by \(p_r\) leaves an exactly interpolated remainder and an orthogonal multiple of \(p_r\). This proves exactness. Applying it to \(l_i^2\) shows that each weight is \(\int l_i^2>0\). Constants give total weight one. The \(q^2r\) atoms give the claimed rule. This is the classical Gaussian quadrature argument, here used with two circle rules on \(S^3\).

**PROVED (positivity in every transfer direction).** To retain the original exponential, use on *every* oriented positive link the symmetric law \(\nu_d\) of \(AB^{-1}\) for independent \(A,B\sim\eta_d\). The same finite rule is used in all directions. Its support has at most \(C^2(d+1)^6\) points and it is antipodally invariant. For fixed \(B\), right multiplication is real linear and preserves Haar, so \(\nu_d\) is still exact to degree \(d\). Inversion preserves \(\nu_d\). Its average in every unitary group representation \(R\) is \(M M^*\), where \(M=\mathbb E R(A)\). This positivity also holds for the action of independent site rotations, by using independent pairs at all vertices.

**PROVED.** On the finite spatial slice with product reference mass \(\nu_d^{\mathcal E}\), form the ordinary one-step temporal kernel by averaging the product of electric Wilson plaquettes over the temporal links. For fixed temporal site variables this product has the form \(k(x,g y)\), up to inversion of the site variables, where
\[
 k(x,y)=\prod_{e\in\mathcal E} w(x_e y_e^{-1})
\]
and \(g y\) denotes the vertex gauge action. The kernel \(k\) is positive definite on the continuous slice, by the positive character expansion or its limit in (1). Gauge transformations of both arguments leave it invariant. With \(g=ab^{-1}\), independence and invariance rewrite \(\mathbb E k(x,g y)\) as \(\mathbb E_{a,b}k(a^{-1}x,b^{-1}y)\). It is a Gram kernel: use the averaged vectors in any Gram representation of \(k\). For \(w_m\) the representation is finite by the tensor products above. For \(w\) the positive matrices follow by passing to the limit entrywise. Restriction to the finite spatial support preserves positivity. Multiplication on both sides by the square root of the spatial plaquette weight preserves it too. Both the finite exponential and polynomial models consequently have positive semidefinite transfers, strictly positive entries and simple neutral top eigenvalues. The centre flips preserve the support, weights and kernel. This proves positivity without treating the quadrature nodes as a subgroup. Temporal links are integrated independently exactly once in the trace, so the periodic integral equals the trace of the indicated power in each time direction, and a temporal seam inserts the corresponding centre flip.

**PROVED (every nonzero polynomial-model level agrees).** Set \(d=6m\), with \(m\ge1\). In the polynomial partition integral each link occurs in six factors, with degree at most \(m\) per occurrence, for \(n,v\ge3\). Seam signs preserve the degree. **PROVED (a local channel in both directions).** The smaller rule \(\eta_d\), with \(O((d+1)^3)\) states per link, suffices in the *polynomial* model in all four directions. This proves exact reflection-positive transfer for the polynomial model on the smaller alphabet, as well as equality of all its positive eigenvalues with the continuous-link polynomial model. Thus (4) also holds as an equivalence using this smaller positive polynomial transfer. Uniform relative-error control of action weights and traces must still be distinguished from approximation of individual signed observable averages.

**PROVED.** There is a local sampling realization of this replacement on any selected links \(S\), with arbitrary fixed exterior pins, real twists, and a product of Haar and discrete reference laws on the other links. Starting from the polynomial Gibbs law, resample each selected link from its exact one-link conditional at the new reference measure, keeping the others fixed. At each replacement the unnormalized conditional integral is unchanged for *every* exterior, since it is the integral of a polynomial of degree at most \(6m\). Hence its exterior marginal is unchanged, and multiplying that marginal by the new conditional gives exactly the mixed Gibbs law with the one reference switched. The reverse switch has the same property. The interaction graph joining links on a common plaquette has at most \(6\cdot3=18\) neighbors per link. Greedy coloring by 19 colors permits simultaneous independent updates of each color. Each conditional reads only incident plaquettes. The whole channel can thus be implemented in at most 19 rounds with a bounded lattice neighborhood, independent of the side and of the period. It fixes all other links, and applies also to separated observation collars by using independent random choices. The consecutive transitions in this argument change the reference law.

**PROVED (ordinary Wilson estimate, independent of the length of the cylinder).** The same replacements using the full exponential conditionals give a joint-law error bounded in log density by
\[
     12\,|S|[-\log(1-\delta)]                                         \tag{5}
\]
relative to the exact mixed exponential Gibbs law after all replacements. To see this, take one link, write \(J_H(\xi),J_Q(\xi)\) for the two conditional normalizers given the exterior \(\xi\), and \(J_m(\xi)>0\) for their common polynomial integral. The six incident weights give \(J_m\le J_H,J_Q\le (1-\delta)^{-6}J_m\). The exact new exterior marginal has Radon–Nikodym derivative \(J_Q/J_H\) divided by its old expectation; it is between \((1-\delta)^{12}\) and \((1-\delta)^{-12}\). The conditional resampling from the old exact law shares the new conditional, so the same two-sided comparison holds for the entire new law. A Markov kernel preserves inequalities between positive measures. Iterating proves (5), including along the reverse sequence. A layer costs order \(n^3\delta\) uniformly even if the total temporal length tends to infinity first. This proof uses positivity for the ordinary and individual twisted integrals; it would not permit normalizing an arbitrarily small signed combination. The finite representation therefore still has access to local changes of a Wilson boundary. Its finite support does not establish loss of boundary information over a physical time.


## Which physical energies survive the replacement

**PROVED (simultaneous control of the low levels).** For either common reference, continuous Haar or the positive finite rule \(\nu_d\), let \(F,F_m\) be the symmetrically sandwiched spatial transfers and set \(c_n=(1-\delta)^{6n^3}\). Their kernels on the same space satisfy \(c_n F(x,y)\le F_m(x,y)\le F(x,y)\). Each electric face has full weight, and the spatial faces at the two ends have half weight. This counts exactly \(6n^3\) effective factors before temporal integration. For arbitrary \(f\),
\[
 |(F-F_m)f|(x)\le(1-c_n)(F|f|)(x),\qquad
 \left\|\frac F\lambda-\frac{F_m}{\lambda_m}\right\|\le2(1-c_n).
                                                                    \tag{6}
\]
The first bound gives the operator norm after taking the \(L^2\) norm. For the second use \(\lambda_m\le\lambda\), \(\lambda_m\ge c_n\lambda\), and \(\|F_m\|=\lambda_m\). This is a kernel comparison and does not need a lower bound on the difference as a quadratic form. The min–max formula on each centre character bounds the change in each ordered normalized eigenvalue by (6). Pad finite lists by zeros. The two polynomial lists agree by their power traces. Hence corresponding normalized eigenvalues of the two *exponential* models differ by at most \(4(1-c_n)\), uniformly in the level and character. A fixed bounded interval of physical energies, with \(a=L/n\), corresponds to ratios at least \(e^{-aE_{\max}}\). The mean-value bound for \(-\log r\) on \(r\ge1/2\) therefore gives energy errors \(O((1-c_n)/a)=o(1)\) for matched levels whenever either member lies in the fixed interval. Threshold counts may be compared with this small energy padding. Comparing individual levels would not suffice for (3), since the number of levels grows.

**PROVED (a subspace for the original low-energy vectors).** Let \(A_m(x)=\prod_{p\ {\rm spatial}} w_m(x_p)^{1/2}\), and let \(V\) be the space of polynomials of link degree at most \(m\) separately in each spatial link. The range of the Haar \(F_m\) lies in \(A_m V\). This follows from the polynomial electric kernel at its outgoing endpoint, including integration over the site rotations. For \(u=A_m f\), \(f\in V\), the Haar norm integral of \(|u|^2\) has link degree at most \(4m+2m\). Evaluation at the small nodes \(\eta_d\), with their weights, is therefore isometric on \(A_mV\), including its inner products by polarization. If \(\Pi\) is the Haar orthogonal projection onto \(A_m V\) and \(U=F/\lambda\), then for every \(r>0\)
\[
           \|(1-\Pi){\bf1}_{[r,1]}(U)\|\le (1-c_n)/r .                \tag{7}
\]
Indeed \((1-\Pi)F_m=0\); apply the first estimate of (6) to the inverse of \(U\) restricted to the indicated spectral subspace. Thus every physical low-energy superposition is close in norm to this explicitly sampled boundary space. Its dimension still increases with the number of spatial links. In particular (7) does not place those vectors near the vacuum line.

## A lower bound on the alphabet under the Wilson boundary law

**PROVED (finite-measure inequality).** Let \(\sigma\) be normalized Haar on \(S^3=SU(2)\), \(Q\) any probability supported on \(N\) points, and
\[
 T_b f(y)=\frac{\int e^{b\, y\cdot x} f(x)d\sigma(x)}
                    {\int e^{b\,y\cdot x}d\sigma(x)},\qquad
 G_{Q,b}(y)=\frac{\int e^{b\, y\cdot x}dQ(x)}
                    {\int e^{b\,y\cdot x}d\sigma(x)},\quad b>0.
\]
For \(D_k=\sum_{\ell=0}^k(\ell+1)^2\), one has
\[
     N<D_k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
     \|G_{Q,b}-1\|_{L^2(\sigma)}
       \ge D_k^{-1/2}\exp[-(4k^2+6k)/b].                              \tag{8}
\]
The denominator is independent of \(y\). GL3 of Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text, gives the eigenvalue \(r_\ell=I_{\ell+1}(b)/I_1(b)\) of \(T_b\) on the spin-\(\ell/2\) matrix coefficients. Here is the needed lower bound. The power series in their proof gives
\(I_j(b)=(b/2)^j[\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(j+1/2)]^{-1}
\int_{-1}^1 e^{bu}(1-u^2)^{j-1/2}du\), by expansion and integrating the even powers (the beta integral, or repeated integration by parts). Integrating the derivative of \(e^{bu}(1-u^2)^{j+1/2}\) gives \(I_{j+1}/I_j\) as the mean of \(u\) under the preceding positive integrand. Thus \(I_{j+1}/I_j<1\). The recurrence \(I_j-I_{j+2}=2(j+1)I_{j+1}/b\), also obtained by subtracting the two positive power series, yields \(I_{j+1}/I_j\ge(1+2(j+1)/b)^{-1}\). Multiplying for \(j=1,\ldots,\ell\) and using \(\log(1+x)\le x\) gives \(r_\ell\ge e^{-(\ell^2+3\ell)/b}\). [Outline, GL3–GL4, 2195–2239](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex#L2195-L2239)

The representation of degree \(\ell\) on homogeneous polynomials in two variables has \(\ell+1\) distinct diagonal weights, and the two infinitesimal off-diagonal operators connect adjacent monomials, proving irreducibility. Averaging a matrix intertwiner against Haar and using Schur's lemma gives orthogonality of inequivalent coefficients and squared norm \(1/(\ell+1)\) of each unit matrix entry. Hence \(\dim V_k=D_k\). Products of two elements, including a conjugate, lie in \(V_{2k}\). Conjugation in the defining representation is equivalent to it by its invariant alternating form. In tensoring its \(\ell\)-th symmetric power by the defining representation, symmetrization maps onto the \((\ell+1)\)-st power. The lower summand is the \((\ell-1)\)-st power inserted by the alternating tensor, as is checked by symmetrization and dimensions. Iteration gives the asserted degree bound. For any orthonormal basis of \(V_k\), the sum of its pointwise squared moduli is constant by left-translation invariance and has integral \(D_k\).

**PROVED.** When \(N<D_k\), choose \(\|f\|_2=1\) in \(V_k\) vanishing at all the nodes, using the \(N\) linear evaluation equations. \]
Cauchy–Schwarz gives (8). The nodes and their weights may depend on the bare coupling and cutoff.

**PROVED (cost of continuous links).** On a rectangular four-torus let \(V\) be the number of vertices, \(E=4V\) the number of links, and \(\ell\ge3\) the shortest period. Fix a set \(A\) of Haar links and fix all remaining links. All real plaquette signs may also be fixed. Write
\[
 z_\beta=\int e^{-\beta(1-x(g))}\,d\sigma(g)
       \le C(1+\beta)^{-3/2}.
\]
The bound follows by the radial integral with density \(2\sin^2\theta/\pi\), using \(1-\cos\theta\ge2\theta^2/\pi^2\). For the faces incident to \(A\) one has
\[
 \int \prod_{p:\,p\cap A\ne\varnothing}
     e^{-\beta(1-\epsilon_p x(U_p))}\prod_{e\in A}d\sigma(U_e)
 \le z_\beta^{\,\frac34(1-1/\ell)|A|}.                               \tag{9}
\]
Choose a direction \(\mu\) containing at most \(|A|/4\) of these links, and hold the \(\mu\)-links fixed. For every other direction \(\nu\), arrange its parallel links in columns indexed by translation in the \(\mu\)-direction. In each column use a link outside \(A\) as a root if possible, and otherwise leave one Haar link unintegrated as a root. To each other member of \(A\) assign the \(\mu\nu\) plaquette connecting it to the previous link in cyclic order away from this root. All assigned faces are distinct. Discard all unassigned weights, which are at most one. Integrating backwards down each column costs exactly \(z_\beta\) per assigned link: the previous link and the two side links are fixed in its last face, and Haar removes the translations, inversions and sign. At most a fraction \(1/\ell\) of the transverse Haar links are lost to all-Haar columns. This proves (9). For a set \(A\) in a connected component of a defect expansion the same proof may use just the faces incident to that particular \(A\); it holds with all exterior links fixed. Axial gauge and plaquette elimination are established methods in the earlier Wilson comparisons; here the direction and the missing root are chosen for an arbitrary set of Haar labels. **PROVED (the change of reference has a sharp leading pressure scale).** Take \(s=\beta^{-\alpha}\), fixed \(0<\alpha<\infty\), on any sequence with \(\beta,\ell\to\infty\). Let \(Z_s\) be the ordinary partition function with the normalized action. Then
\[
       \frac{-\log Z_s}{E\log\beta}\longrightarrow
                      \min\{\alpha,9/8\}.                            \tag{10}
\]
Under its Gibbs law the fraction of Haar-labelled links tends in probability to zero if \(\alpha<9/8\), and to one if \(\alpha>9/8\). To prove (10), average (9) over the reference labels and the remaining signs. This gives the upper bound \([s+(1-s)z_\beta^{(3/4)(1-1/\ell)}]^E\). From below the all-\(+I\) configuration contributes \((s/2)^E\). For the all-Haar term fix a spanning tree to the identity by vertex changes of variables, one tree edge at a time. The \(E-V+1\) chords still have product Haar reference. Putting them all within angle \((1+\beta)^{-1/2}\) of \(I\) costs at most \(C E+\frac32(E-V+1)\log(1+\beta)\) in negative log, including the bounded action per face. Multiply by \((1-s)^E\). Since \(E=4V\), these two lower bounds give the other side of (10). For exactly \(k\) Haar labels, the contribution is at most \(\binom Ek s^{E-k}(1-s)^k z_\beta^{(3/4)(1-1/\ell)k}\). Uniformly in \(k\), the leading negative log in units \(E\log\beta\) is bounded below by \(\alpha(1-k/E)+(9/8)k/E-o(1)\); \(2^E\) bounds the entire binomial entropy. Restricting \(k/E\) to be at least a fixed distance from the minimizing endpoint costs \(c E\log\beta\) relative to the lower bound. No phase statement at \(\alpha=9/8\) follows from this pressure calculation alone.


## A neutral spectral gap on the centre-dominated side

**PROVED (auxiliary model).** Fix \(0<\alpha<9/8\). There are \(\ell_0,\beta_0,c,C>0\), depending only on \(\alpha\), such that for \(n,v\ge\ell_0\), \(\beta\ge\beta_0\) and \(\beta^{-\alpha}\le s\le1\), the vacuum-normalized neutral trace of the mixed model obeys
\[
        1\le D_{0,s}(v)\le
        \exp\{C n^3v\,e^{-c v\log\beta}\}.                            \tag{11}
\]
At fixed \(n,\beta,s\) in this range its nonvacuum neutral lattice energies are all at least \(c\log\beta\). In particular (4)'s physical numerical margin holds in this auxiliary model along any of the growing physical cubes when \(\beta\to\infty\). The proof keeps the degeneracy of the flat centre configurations through the temporal projection.

**PROVED (counting the signs).** Write \(U_e=\sigma_e V_e\), with independent uniform signs \(\sigma_e\). For the links of \(A\), \(V_e\) is Haar; elsewhere \(V_e=I\). Set \(\kappa=(1-s)/s\). With a fixed seam the set \(D\) of negative plaquette signs is a closed two-cochain over \(\mathbb F_2\) in the seam class, before multiplying by \(x(V_p)\). For each such \(D\) the number of link signs is \(2^{V-1+4}\). Indeed a flat link-sign assignment is determined by its \(V-1\) tree gauges and four winding signs: after removing the winding signs all loop products are one, because elementary square moves commute neighboring coordinate steps and inverse steps cancel. Multiplying a particular preimage by any such flat assignment gives all preimages. The analogous classification of closed plaquette signs can be seen by lifting to the cubical universal cover and filling paths by elementary squares. For precision, the cochain complex of a periodic line splits over \(\mathbb F_2\) into one constant degree-zero generator, one cohomology generator in degree one (odd total), and isomorphic degree-zero/one pairs connected by the coboundary. Indeed its difference map has kernel the constants and image the even-sum vectors. Tensor the four line complexes. A summand containing an isomorphic pair contracts by the inverse map on that factor; in characteristic two the cross terms cancel. The remaining degree-two generators are the six coordinate pairs, distinguished by their pairings with the coordinate two-tori. The three temporal seams vary exactly the three pairings involving time. Averaging these eight seams therefore gives
\[
 Z_{\rm neut}(v)=s^{4V}2^{-3V}
 \sum_{\substack{A,D:\ dD=0\\ [D]_{\rm spatial}=0}}
 \kappa^{|A|}e^{-2\beta|D\setminus\operatorname{St}(A)|}
 \int\prod_{p\in\operatorname{St}(A)}
          e^{-\beta(1-(-1)^{1_D(p)}x(V_p))}
          \prod_{e\in A}d\sigma(V_e).                                 \tag{12}
\]
Here \(\operatorname{St}(A)\) is the set of incident plaquettes. The factor \(2^{-3V}\) includes the factor \(1/8\) in the neutral projection. The maximal positive eigenvalue of the ordinary transfer lies in this sector by strict positivity and centre symmetry.

**PROVED (local expansion).** Temporarily omit the three global restrictions \([D]_{\rm spatial}=0\) in (12), retaining \(dD=0\). Call the resulting sum without its prefactor \(\Xi_v\). Form components of the objects of \(A\sqcup D\), joining objects whose base vertices have torus sup distance at most two. The cube equations for \(dD=0\) hold for each component separately. Every face factor in the integral sees variables of at most one component. Conversely pairwise separated components combine to an allowed configuration, with the product of their weights. This is a positive hard-core polymer gas. In a component let \(k\) be its number of Haar links, \(r\) its number of negative faces outside their star, and \(j\) its total number of objects. Then \(j\le7k+r\). By (9) the activity is at most
\[
       (\kappa z_\beta^{(3/4)(1-1/\ell)})^k e^{-2\beta r}.
\]
First take \(\ell_0\) so that \((9/8)(1-1/\ell_0)>\alpha\). Then choose \(\beta_0\) large. The activity is at most \(e^{-b j}\) with \(b=c_\alpha\log\beta\), \(c_\alpha>0\), uniformly in all the stated parameters. At \(s=1\) only \(k=0\) occurs.

**PROVED (finite-period error).** The graph has a uniform bound \(J=10\cdot5^4\) on its closed neighborhood size. The number of connected sets of \(j\) objects through a given object is at most \(J^{2(j-1)}\), by recording a depth-first walk on a deterministically chosen spanning tree. Put \(\mu=b/2\), increasing \(\beta_0\) if needed. The sum over polymers incompatible with \(\gamma\) of \(w(\eta)e^{(1+\mu)|\eta|}\) is at most
\[
       |\gamma| J\sum_{j\ge1}J^{2(j-1)} e^{-(b-1-\mu)j}\le|\gamma|.
\]
The connected logarithm consequently has absolute cluster weight of total size at least \(h\) meeting any one vertex at most \(C e^{-\mu h}\). In detail, expand the hard-core product in incompatibility edges; grouping graphs by connected components gives the exponential of the connected series. In the alternating connected sum, toggle the least edge whose endpoints are already joined by a path of smaller edges. The surviving graphs are trees. With root fixed, the rooted-tree sums for tilted weights \(w(\eta)e^{\mu|\eta|}\) have successive height bounds \(T_\gamma^{(0)}=1\), \(T_\gamma^{(k+1)}=\exp(\sum_{\eta\not\sim\gamma}w(\eta)e^{\mu|\eta|}T_\eta^{(k)})\le e^{|\gamma|}\). The factorials count unordered child subtrees. Summing over the root polymers meeting the vertex and removing the tilt proves the tail and absolute convergence. The exponential identity extends from a neighborhood of zero activity by this bound, first scaling all activities by a scalar of modulus less than one and then taking the limit. This uses the Kotecký–Preiss criterion in the form proved by Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text. **PROVED.** Distribute each logarithm term equally among its object occurrences as roots. Define \(p_n\) by the same rooted sum per time layer on the cylinder with spatial side \(n\) and infinite time. A connected cluster of total size \(j<v/16\), rooted at a specified occurrence, has a unique lift relative to that occurrence in the time direction. Its contact steps have time length at most two, every short cycle has displacement less than \(v\), and the lift and projection preserve all incident faces and cube equations. The inverse correspondence uses the same bounds on the total time span. Spatial wrapping is allowed on both sides. Short rooted terms thus agree. The tail bound gives
\[
              |\log\Xi_v-v p_n|\le C n^3v\, e^{-\mu v/16}.
\]
For the global restriction, if \([D]_{\rm spatial}\ne0\), at least one individually closed component has a nonzero pairing with a spatial coordinate two-torus. Its pairing is the same at every time by closure (sum the intervening cube equations), so it contains at least \(v\) negative faces. In the positive unrestricted gas the probability of a specified polymer is at most its activity, by removing that polymer. The sum of activities of such large polymers is at most \(C n^3 v e^{-\mu v}\). At fixed \(n,\beta,s\) this tends to zero as \(v\to\infty\). Taking \(v\)-th roots in (12) identifies the top eigenvalue with \(s^{4n^3}2^{-3n^3}e^{p_n}\); dropping the restriction now gives (11) at finite \(v\). If an excited neutral ratio is \(r_*>0\), then \(r_*^v\le D_{0,s}(v)-1\). Taking large-\(v\) roots at fixed spatial volume proves the energy bound in (11), with a possible decrease of \(c\).

**PROVED (why this limit alone cannot transfer the answer).** The reference distance \(\|\mu_s-\sigma\|_{\rm TV}=s\) tends to zero at \(s=\beta^{-\alpha}\), \(0<\alpha<9/8\). Nevertheless a spatial plaquette has central holonomy with probability tending to one there by (10), translation invariance and the union bound on four links, whereas under Haar Wilson it has probability zero at every finite cutoff. One can use the continuous reading \(f_\beta(U_p)=[1-\beta^2(1-x(U_p)^2)]_+\). If any of its four links is Haar-labelled, condition on all labels and all other links. Its conditional density is bounded by \(C(1+\beta)^{3/2}\) times Haar, from the single-link normalizer on a cap of radius \((1+\beta R)^{-1/2}\) about its maximizing direction, \(R\le6\). The two caps on which \(f_\beta>0\) have total Haar volume \(O(\beta^{-3})\). If all four labels are central, \(f_\beta=1\). Thus a gauge-invariant bounded reading separates the two regimes of (10) in mean. Row 1161, (19), also gives actual Haar neutral excitations at bounded lattice energy uniformly in \(\beta\ge1\), whereas the lower bound in (11) diverges. ## Trying to carry the gap through the reference interpolation

**GUIDE.** The unresolved question after (11) is whether the neutral estimate can be carried all the way to \(s=0\). A continuous interpolation at finite volume would be insufficient if an intermediate transfer acquired a very light *neutral* excitation. I test this inside the mixed Wilson law itself.

**PROVED (a strict cost for a periodic mixture of labels).** Take a four-torus with all periods divisible by four and sufficiently large. Tile it by closed cubes of side two. Prescribe a complete pattern of Haar or centre labels on one such cube and disseminate it by successive site reflections in its faces. This gives a consistent pattern of period four on the links, including shared boundary links. Call the pattern mixed when both labels occur. If \(k\) links on the full torus have the Haar label, then for some absolute \(\eta>0\) and every such mixed pattern the integral of the normalized Wilson weight, conditional on its labels, is at most
\[
                            z_\beta^{3k/4+\eta V}.                    \tag{13}
\]
It suffices to prove the bound with all the centre link values fixed. Let \(a_\mu\) be the number of Haar links in direction \(\mu\), divided by \(V\). The \(a_\mu\) are multiples of \(4^{-4}\). If they are unequal, taking the least as the axial direction in (9) gains at least \(V/(4\cdot4^4)\) beyond \(3k/4\) before dropping the full-column roots. At most \(4V/\ell\) roots are dropped.

**PROVED (the equal-frequency case).** A mixed pattern contains a Haar edge in some direction \(\mu\) and a centre edge in a perpendicular direction \(\nu\) incident to it at a common vertex. Otherwise every pair of perpendicular edges at a vertex has the same label, and using a third edge shows this for parallel incident edges as well. Connectivity would give a constant pattern. Starting at that centre \(\nu\)-edge, translate it in the signed \(\mu\)-direction of the incident Haar edge until the next centre \(\nu\)-edge is reached. This takes \(h\) steps, \(1\le h\le4\), by periodicity. The \(h-1\) intermediate \(\nu\)-edges are Haar. The strip of \(h\) faces has the chosen Haar \(\mu\)-edge on the first step of one side. Period-four translates give at least \(V/4^4\) candidates; greedy selection at sup distance greater than 12 leaves at least \(gV\) mutually separated strips, \(g=(4^4\,25^4)^{-1}\).

**PROVED.** Use direction \(\mu\) for the column argument, ordered in the sign just chosen. In each reserved strip retain the last face as well as the \(h-1\) faces already assigned to the intermediate Haar links. This face was previously unused because the ending \(\nu\)-edge is central. All other column eliminations may be performed first. They yield constants even where a side link is one of the reserved \(\mu\)-links. Each reserved interval ends at centre edges, so no remaining \(\nu\)-column segment needs an internal link of the interval. In the residual strip integrate the selected Haar \(\mu\)-edge in its first face, then the successive internal \(\nu\)-edges in the remaining faces. This yields \(z_\beta^h\) and gains one factor per strip. The disjoint strips share none of these variables. Equal directional frequencies gave \(3k/4\) before loss of at most \(4V/\ell\) roots. Taking \(\ell\) so large that \(4/\ell<\frac12\min\{g,1/(4\cdot4^4)\}\) proves (13), for example with \(\eta=\frac12\min\{g,1/(4\cdot4^4)\}\).

**PROVED (rare mixed tiles, uniformly in \(s\)).** There are constants \(c_0,C_0>0\) such that, once \(\beta,\ell\) are sufficiently large, for every set \(S\) of distinct tiles,
\[
 \Pr_{\rm ord}(\text{every tile in }S\text{ is mixed})\le q^{|S|},
 \qquad
 \Pr_{\rm neut}(\text{every tile in }S\text{ is mixed})\le8q^{|S|},
 \qquad q=C_0\beta^{-c_0}.                                           \tag{14}
\]
Here the second probability is the *positive average of temporal-seam integrals*, normalized by \(Z_{\rm neut}\). For a disseminated mixed pattern its ordinary numerator is at most \(s^{E-k}(1-s)^k z_\beta^{3k/4+\eta V}\). For \(0<s<1\) bound the denominator below by the geometric mean with exponents \(1-k/E,k/E\) of the two endpoint contributions used in (10). The label probabilities cancel exactly. The negative log cost left after this comparison is at least \(\frac32(\eta V-k/E)\log(1+\beta)-C V\). Thus the disseminated probability is at most \((C\beta^{-c})^V\), with \(c>0\) independent of \(s\). At \(s=0,1\) all mixed-tile probabilities vanish.

**PROVED.** For the chessboard step, site mirrors split the Wilson action into the two reflected halves with the face weights on the common planes shared equally. Conditional integration over their common spatial links gives reflection Cauchy–Schwarz, also when an indicator reads shared link labels. In one direction transport each half-closed or closed slab profile by reflection to its successive copies. Normalize a filling by the product of the disseminated partition functions of its profiles, each to the power \(1/N\) for \(N\) slabs. At a maximizing filling, reflecting either half into the other again maximizes the ratio, since Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its square by the product of those two ratios. Doubling a constant run in that filling until it covers a half-circle gives a constant maximizing filling, whose normalized ratio is one. For indicators first add a positive constant and take it to zero. Repeat in each of the four coordinate directions. This is the finite chessboard argument of Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text, applied to the extra local label. Unmarked tile profiles are one. For the marked tiles sum over the finitely many mixed patterns on the side-two cube (at most \(2^{216}\)); their disseminated probabilities enter to the power \(16/V\) each. This proves the first part of (14). [Outline, AM6–AM7, 2885–2942](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex#L2885-L2942)

**PROVED.** For the neutral projection cut the torus at times \(0,v/2\), both tile faces. With \(g\) on the final seam the numerator with marked tiles has the form \(\int K_+(x,y)K_-(y,gx)\,dx\,dy\), using the slice reference and half weights at the boundaries. Each half kernel retains its own tile indicators. The flip preserves boundary reference and every label. By Cauchy–Schwarz the numerator is bounded by the geometric mean of the ordinary numerators obtained by attaching a reflected copy of each half to itself. These have \(2|S_+|\) and \(2|S_-|\) marked tiles, respectively. The first inequality of (14) therefore bounds every seam numerator by \(Z_{\rm ord}q^{|S|}\). **PROVED (phase separation on a finite torus).** Let \(n\to\infty\) through multiples of four and let \(\beta\to\infty\). There are reference weights \(s_*(n,\beta)\) with

**PROVED (choice of the mixture).** Take \(v\) divisible by eight with \(e^{c_1 n\log\beta}\le v\le 2e^{c_1 n\log\beta}\), where \(0<c_1<c_0/100\) and \(c_0\) is from (14). **PROVED (two distant observations).** In a plane of tiles in one space direction and time, the periods are \(M=n/2,\ T=v/2\). ## What can be carried from the Haar endpoint

**PROVED (forest cancellation buys four powers).** On an \(n^3\times v\) torus with periods at least four, allow any fixed temporal centre seam. Let \(Z_s^g(v)\) denote its positive integral under \(\mu_s\) and \(Z_0^g(v)\) its Haar value. Put \(K=\lceil C(1+\beta)^{3/2}\rceil\), with \(C\) large enough for the single-link density bound above. There are absolute constants \(c,C'>0\) such that
\[
     CsK<c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
     |\log Z_s^g(v)-\log Z_0^g(v)|\le C' n^3 v\,K s^4.               \tag{17}
\]
Enlarging \(C\) only decreases the indicated sufficient range. Here the same estimate works when a pinned link is drawn from any prescribed link probability in place of the centre law. Only for the subsequent transfer application are centre symmetry and positivity needed.

**PROVED.** Independently mark links with probability \(s\) and denote their set by \(T\). First fix their prescribed values. For a forest of pinned links the ratio to the all-Haar integral is exactly one: independent Haar vertex rotations generate independent Haar edges on the forest, by integrating its leaves. Gauge invariance of the action, also with the seam, and Haar invariance on all other links give the equality. In a general \(T\) pin a maximal forest first. At each remaining edge the density relative to its Haar measure, even after conditioning on the previous pins, is bounded above by \(K\). Indeed, with every other link fixed, it is a translated one-link tilt with strength at most \(6\beta\), and the same density bound survives every positive mixture over the unfixed links. One can use the continuous conditional integrals pointwise in the prescribed pins. The fixed ratio is between zero and \(K^{b(T)}\), where \(b(T)\) is the number of independent cycles. These facts persist on averaging the prescribed values.

**PROVED.** For a lower bound retain forests. The events that each particular simple cycle is not wholly marked are decreasing events of independent bits; their joint probability is at least the product of their probabilities. This association follows by conditioning on the last bit, using induction and the nonnegative covariance of two monotone functions of one bit. Products of nonnegative decreasing functions remain decreasing. There are at most \(V8^l\) simple cycles of length \(l\ge4\), so the forest probability is at least \(\exp(-C V s^4)\) for sufficiently small \(s\), by summing \(-\log(1-s^l)\).

**PROVED.** For the upper bound, \(K^{b(T)}\) counts divergence-free currents on the edges of \(T\) valued in \(\mathbb Z/K\): choose the chord currents arbitrarily and solve uniquely on each rooted tree from its leaves. In addition to the forest contribution the raw integral is at least \(cV\beta^{3/2}s^4\) times that contribution, which proves (30). Along \(V\gg\beta^{9/2}\) the physical orbit laws thus converge in total variation for \(V\beta^{3/2}s^4\to0\), and have distance tending to one when \(V\beta^{3/2}s^4\to\infty,\ s\le1/2\). This is a separation threshold for entire microscopic configurations in a physical volume.

**PROVED (a nonlocal event test for the neutral heat trace).** Fix \(0<\alpha<9/8\) and set \(s=\beta^{-\alpha}\). For all sufficiently large \(n,v,\beta\) there is a number \(\gamma_s(n,\beta)=\lim_{w\to\infty}w^{-1}\log P_s(w)\) with
\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \log D_{0,0}(v)
     &=\log P_s(v)-v\gamma_s(n,\beta)
                         +O(n^3v e^{-c_\alpha v\log\beta}),\\
  J_0(v)&=\log\frac{P_s(v)^2}{P_s(2v)}
                         +O(n^3v e^{-c_\alpha v\log\beta}).
 \end{aligned}                                                        \tag{31}
\]
Both probabilities in the second line are measured in the centre-dominated model whose neutral spectral gap was established in (11). To prove this, write \(B_s(v)=(1-s)^{4V}\sum_T(s/(1-s))^{|T|}\) over forests. The connected components with edges are lattice trees with exclusion by a shared vertex. Each tree of size \(j\) has activity at most \((2s)^j\), and there are at most \(C^j\) such trees at any vertex. The rooted connected expansion used for (11) applies with decay proportional to \(\log(1/s)\); every cluster smaller than \(v/16\) lifts uniquely in time and acyclicity is preserved on its short supports. Hence \(\log B_s(v)=v b_s(n)+O(n^3v e^{-c_\alpha v\log\beta})\) for its cylinder pressure per time. Combining this with (11) in (28) and taking long-time roots of the original positive transfer proves existence of \(\gamma_s\) and the first identity. Taking the vacuum-free difference at \(v,2v\) proves the second. In particular an upper bound \(P_s(u)^2/P_s(2u)\le c_*<3/2\) at one fixed physical side for all fine cutoffs suffices by the purity inequalities and row 1161. Conversely the propagated neutral seed makes this ratio as close to one as desired in cutoff upper limit at a sufficiently large fixed side.

**PROVED (the precision issue for this test).** For \(\beta,\ell\to\infty\),

                \frac{-\log P_s(v)}{4V\log\beta}\ \longrightarrow\
                                        9/8-\alpha .                   \tag{32}
\]
This follows from (28), the pure-Haar estimate in (10), the mixed estimate (10) for the neutral average as proved following (15), and \(B_s(v)\ge(1-s)^{4V}\). ## How local the conditioning in the gapped model can be

**PROVED (upper bound for the required range).** Let \({\cal F}_R\) exclude central holonomy only on simple lattice cycles of length at most \(R\). In the atomic model this is almost surely the event that the centre-labelled graph has girth greater than \(R\), including a forest as having infinite girth. Write \(P_s^R(v)\) for its neutral Gibbs probability. For integers \(k\) with \(2k\le\ell,\ (2k)^4\le R\), if \(C s K^{1/k}\le1/2\), then
\[
  0\le\log\frac{P_s^R(v)}{P_s(v)}
                 \le C V(C s K^{1/k})^{R+1}.                          \tag{33}
\]
There is no inverse factor of the rare-event probability on the right. On the gauge quotient the total variation distance from the original Wilson law, after conditioning on \({\cal F}_R\), is exactly \(1-P_s(v)/P_s^R(v)\), by (29). Thus (33) controls all bounded gauge-invariant observations of the entire physical volume at once.

**PROVED.** For any edge support \(S\) of girth greater than \(R\), \(b(S)\le |S|/k\). Partition into the boxes used for (24) and translate the partition uniformly over the torus. Each edge crosses a box boundary with probability at most \(1/k\). Within a box a cycle would have length at most its number of vertices, at most \(R\); there is none. Removing the crossing edges gives a forest, hence their number bounds \(b(S)\) for every translate. Average over translates. On iid edges conditioned on \({\cal F}_R\), any given support is wholly occupied with probability at most \(s^{|S|}\) since occupation is increasing and the conditioning event is decreasing. As in (17), \(K^{b(T)}\) counts the divergence-free currents on \(T\) valued in \(\mathbb Z/K\). Any component of a nonempty current support has no leaf and hence at least \(R+1\) edges. For a connected candidate of \(j\) edges the count of currents is at most \(K^{j/k}\), and there are at most \(V C^j\) such candidates on the torus. Summing unordered collections of these possibilities and dropping disjointness gives
\[
 \mathbb E_{\rm iid}\big[K^{b(T)}\mid{\cal F}_R\big]
             \le \exp\{C V(C s K^{1/k})^{R+1}\}.
\]
The conditional iid probability of having no cycle longer than \(R\) is at least its unconditioned value by association of decreasing events. The latter is at least \(\prod_{C:\,|C|>R}(1-s^{|C|})\ge\exp[-CV(Cs)^{R+1}]\), again by association and the cycle count. Divide the Gibbs weight of \({\cal F}_R\), using the fixed-pin upper bound \(K^{b(T)}\), by the weight of forests, for which the fixed-pin integral is exactly the original Wilson integral. These two estimates give (33), enlarging \(C\) once.

**PROVED (a matching lower bound).** Suppose \(v\) is even, \(6\le R<\ell-4\), and \(s\le1/2\). Then for either the ordinary or neutral ensemble,
\[
  \frac{P_s^R(v)}{P_s(v)}
       \ge 1+cV\,[s(1-s)^7]^{\,2\lfloor R/2\rfloor+2}.               \tag{34}
\]
Use a space-time rectangle of height two in time and width \(\lfloor R/2\rfloor-1\) in space. Its perimeter is \(q=2\lfloor R/2\rfloor+2>R\), and it has at least \(cV\) distinct translates. The Haar Wilson integral with centre pins on that cycle is at least the unconstrained integral. Here is the sign argument for this last assertion. For any integer-spin \(SU(2)\) character \(\chi_j\), \(\mathbb E\chi_j(U_C)\ge0\). Reflect in the site-time plane through the middle of the rectangle. Its two halves are reflected open paths with the same endpoints; their representation-matrix product, after adjoint on the reflected half and trace, gives the character. Condition on the two mirror slices. The ordinary numerator is an integral of squared moduli of the half-integrals, summed over the two endpoint matrix indices. For the neutral numerator put the averaged seam on the opposite mirror slice. Each ordinary square becomes the inner product of a half-integral with its spatial-centre projection on that boundary variable. This is a nonnegative quadratic form, since the slice reference is invariant and the projection is an orthogonal average. Matrix insertions are away from the opposite slice. Translating the rectangle across the original seam has no effect for integer spin, which is trivial on centre signs. This proves the sign for each such character.

**PROVED.** The loop marginal has a continuous class density \(\rho_C\) by the single-link formula. To avoid any assumption on pointwise Fourier convergence, use the even approximate identity proportional to \((1+x)^m+(1-x)^m\) and let \(m\to\infty\). Its two equal caps concentrate at the two centre elements by the elementary strict maximum of \(1\pm x\). Expanding its even powers of \(x=\chi_{1/2}/2\) into tensor characters gives nonnegative coefficients of integer-spin characters; the coefficient of the trivial character after Haar normalization is one. Thus its expectation on the loop is at least one, and \((\rho_C(I)+\rho_C(-I))/2\ge1\) follows. This is the required centre-pin ratio, by the same tree elimination on a simple cycle used for (25). As in (30), start with any iid forest for which every edge at a vertex of this rectangle is unmarked, and add precisely its \(q\) edges. The result still satisfies \({\cal F}_R\), and determines the rectangle and the original forest from its unique cycle. There are at most \(8q\) edges that had to be empty; their conditional iid probability given forest is at least \((1-s)^{8q}\). Multiplying by the iid weight ratio \((s/(1-s))^q\) proves (34).

**PROVED (sharp length at physical size).** Take \(s=\beta^{-\alpha}\), \(0<\alpha<9/8\), let \(v\) be even, and assume
\[
       H=\frac{\log(n^3v)}{\alpha\log\beta}\longrightarrow\infty,
               \qquad H=o(\ell),\qquad\beta\to\infty.
\]
For every fixed \(0<\zeta<1\), conditioning the gapped model on no central cycles up to length \(\lceil(1+\zeta)H\rceil\) recovers the full Haar Wilson orbit law in total variation up to \(o(1)\). Conditioning only through \(\lfloor(1-\zeta)H\rfloor\) leaves total variation distance \(1-o(1)\). Indeed take \(k=\lfloor R^{1/4}/2\rfloor\) in (33); then \(\log(CsK^{1/k})=-(\alpha-o(1))\log\beta\). In (34), \(q=R+O(1)\) and \(\log[s(1-s)^7]=-(\alpha+o(1))\log\beta\). **PROVED given the earlier neutral seed propagation.** In (31)'s physical test, assume \(u=n/512\) is even on the allowed enlarged dyadic grid and the last display holds for \(v=u,2u\). Choose
\[
        R=\left\lceil(1+\zeta)\frac{\log(2n^3u)}{\alpha\log\beta}
                      \right\rceil .
\]
Then \(J_0(u)=\log[(P_s^R(u))^2/P_s^R(2u)]+o(1)\). Thus a strictly sub-\(3/2\) upper limit for this ratio at one fixed side suffices; after seed propagation the ratio has upper limit arbitrarily close to one on sufficiently large fixed sides. All loops in the replacement event lie in neighborhoods of lattice radius at most \(R\), which have vanishing physical diameter. The leading probability cost is still (32), by (33). Locality of the excluded loops and the unconditioned spectral gap (11) have not provided the needed relative estimate after excluding them throughout the whole torus.


## A finite-resolution test with smooth positive interactions

**PROVED (the smallest plaquette angle in the original field).** Put \(\Delta(U)=\operatorname{dist}(U,\{I,-I\})\) in the group angle metric. In ordinary Haar Wilson or the positive neutral seam average at \(\ell\ge\log(2+\beta)\), set \(\Lambda=V(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3\). For \(0<r\le(1+\beta)^{-1/2}\), along \(\beta,V\to\infty\),
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \Pr(\min_p\Delta(U_p)\le r)\to0 &\text{if }\Lambda\to0,\\
  \Pr(\min_p\Delta(U_p)\le r)\to1 &\text{if }\Lambda\to\infty .
 \end{array}                                                          \tag{35}
\]
For the first bound use the conditional density supremum and a union bound over \(6V\) plaquettes. For the second choose \(m\ge cV\) plaquettes at mutually separated base vertices, say sup distance at least four, and a pivot edge on each. Greedy selection suffices by bounded degree. Conditional on the seam and all nonpivot links, their holonomies are independent tilts \(e^{w_i y_i\cdot U}/Z(w_i)\): no plaquette in the action uses two pivots. The conditional variables \(w_i,y_i\) depend only on this exterior.

**PROVED.** The mean number of \(i\) with \(w_i<3\beta\) is \(O(V/\beta)\), since \(6-w_i/\beta\le\sum_{p\ni e_i}(1-\epsilon_p x(U_p))\) and the expected total deficit is \(O(V/\beta)\) by (26)'s proof. On \(w_i\ge3\beta\) the mean squared angle from \(U_{p_i}\) to \(y_i\) is conditionally at most \(C/\beta\) by the tilted radial integral. Also \(\sum_i\mathbb E\Delta(U_{p_i})^2\le C V/\beta\) by the same total energy bound. The triangle inequality gives expected total squared distance of the maximizing directions to the nearer centre, restricted to \(w_i\ge3\beta\), at most \(C V/\beta\). Thus, except on an exterior event of probability \(C/\beta+C/M^2\), at least \(m/2\) indices have \(w_i\ge3\beta\) and \(\Delta(y_i)\le M/\sqrt{\beta}\), for \(M\ge1\). At each of these the conditional probability of \(\Delta(U_{p_i})\le r\) is at least \(c(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3 e^{-C M^2}\), by integrating the tilt on the nearer radius-\(r\) cap. Their conditional independence bounds the probability of no hit by
\[
       C/\beta+C/M^2+\exp[-c\Lambda e^{-CM^2}].
\]
Taking \(M^2=(2C)^{-1}\log\Lambda\) for large \(\Lambda\) gives (35). This is an extreme-value scale in the original interacting ensemble; the argument requires no clustering assumption for distant Wilson observables.

**PROVED (regularization of the avoidance test).** Use the smooth two-cap comparison measure of (18), now with angular support \(\varepsilon\) around each sign as specified below, and \(s=\beta^{-\alpha}\), \(0<\alpha<9/8\). Use the **same** smooth reference for periods \(u\) and \(2u\). With \(V_*=2n^3u\), take
\[
  R=\left\lceil(1+\zeta)\frac{\log V_*}{\alpha\log\beta}\right\rceil,
  \qquad r=(1+\beta)^{-1/2}V_*^{-1/3-\eta},
  \qquad 0<\varepsilon\le
    \min\{r/(4R),(1+\beta)^{-1/2}/\log(2+\beta)\},                    \tag{36}
\]
for fixed \(\eta,\zeta>0\). Assume the physical-size hypotheses of (33)–(35), including \(R=o(\ell)\), \(\log V_*/\log\beta\to\infty\). Denote by \(p(v)\) the probability in this smooth neutral ensemble that \(\Delta(U_C)>r\) for every simple cycle with \(|C|\le R\). It is a condition on ordinary positive-volume sets of continuous link configurations. Then
\[
 \log\frac{p(u)^2}{p(2u)}=J_0(u)+o(1),\qquad
 \big\|{\mathbb P}_{s,\varepsilon}^{\rm orb}(\,\cdot\mid
       \Delta(U_C)>r\text{ for all }|C|\le R)
          -{\mathbb P}_0^{\rm orb}\big\|_{\rm TV}=o(1)                \tag{37}
\]
at both periods in the second assertion. Its two errors vanish on the actual physical sequence satisfying the stated conditions. This gives the same sufficient sub-\(3/2\) test for the neutral seed. At a cutoff each comparison ensemble has a smooth, strictly positive density and the uniform gap obtained after (18). Very narrow caps in (36) need not be close to one in \(L^p\) for \(p>1\).

**PROVED.** Use \({\cal F},{\cal F}_R\) initially for the **label graph** of the smooth mixture. Conditional on an acyclic label graph, independent site gauges still give the exact original orbit law, for arbitrary values drawn at its edges. The weight identity (28) still holds. The upper estimate (33) still applies to label events using only the fixed-pin upper bound \(K^{b(T)}\). A cycle with only cap labels has \(\Delta\le R\varepsilon\le r/4\) when its length is at most \(R\). Hence the readout event in (37) is contained in the label event \({\cal F}_R\). Conversely on \({\cal F}\) the probability of rejecting the readout is, by the exact orbit law, at most \(C V_*\,8^{R+1}(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3=o(1)\): for each simple loop one pivot link in the original field gives the tilted-density upper bound for its product. The error tends to zero since \(8^R=V_*^{o(1)}\). Consequently the symmetric difference of the readout event and \({\cal F}\) has probability \(o(\Pr({\cal F}))\), using (33). Conditioning on events with this relative difference changes the law by \(o(1)\) in total variation. Use (28) at \(u,2u\), the iid-forest estimate in (31), and the smooth version of (11) in (19)'s proof, valid also for the smaller \(\varepsilon\) since every all-cap remainder is smaller. This proves (37) for the neutral partition functions without accessing a vacuum eigenvector. On fixed physical aspects \(n^3u e^{-c u\log\log(2+\beta)}\to0\), as required by this last step.

**PROVED (resolution of the gauge-invariant reading).** If each observed link angle has error at most \(r/(4R)\), every reported cycle angle up to length \(R\) has error at most \(r/4\). The reconstructed avoidance event at the threshold \(r\) lies between the exact events at \(5r/4\) and \(3r/4\). The same two relative error bounds prove (37) for this reading, including deterministic finite angular nets. In the other direction, any conditioning law supported on \(\Delta(U_C)>r'\) for all these cycles has total variation distance tending to one from Haar Wilson whenever \(V(1+\beta)^{3/2}(r')^3\to\infty\), by (35) on elementary squares, using monotonicity for larger radii. Thus the power \(V^{-1/3}\) in the spatial resolution after thermal rescaling is necessary for recovery up to a subpower factor. This conclusion uses a hand estimate on the probability of accidental near-central plaquettes; no sampling or numerical detection serves as proof.


## Trying to choose wider caps in the original theory

**PROVED (probability of missing every elementary plaquette).** In the setting of (35), there are absolute \(c,C>0\) such that for all sufficiently large \(\beta\), and \(0<r\le c/\sqrt{1+\beta}\),
\[
 cV(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3
 \ \le\ -\log\Pr_0\{\Delta(U_p)>r\ \hbox{for every }p\}\
 \le C V(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3 .                                         \tag{38}
\]
To strengthen the lower bound on this log cost, retain the separated pivots of (35). The total signed Wilson deficit exceeds \(C_0 V/\beta\) with probability at most \(e^{-c_0 V}\): Markov's inequality for its exponential at \(\beta/2\) uses \(Z(\beta/2)/Z(\beta)\le e^{C V}\) from (26). Conditional on their exteriors, the independent maximizing-direction deviations on \(w_i\ge3\beta\) have uniformly bounded moments \(\mathbb E e^{c\beta\,\operatorname{dist}(U_{p_i},y_i)^2}\), by the tilted radial integral, taking \(c\) small. Thus their summed squared distances exceed \(C_1 V/\beta\) with probability at most \(e^{-c_1 V}\). On the complement of these two exceptional events, the pointwise deficit inequality in (35) leaves at most \(O(V/\beta)\) weak tilts, and the sum of \(\Delta(y_i)^2\) over the others is \(O(V/\beta)\). These last two conditions read only the exterior. With exterior probability at least \(1-C e^{-cV}\) at least \(m/2\) pivots are therefore favorable with a fixed \(M\), independent of \(V,\beta,r\). Their conditional hit probabilities are at least \(c(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3\). This gives an upper bound on the no-hit probability of \(e^{-c\Lambda}+C e^{-c'V}(1-e^{-c\Lambda})\). For \(\Lambda\le c''V\) with sufficiently small \(c''\) this is at most \(e^{-c'''\Lambda}\). For \(\Lambda\le1\) use \(1-e^{-c\Lambda}\ge c_2\Lambda\); for \(\Lambda\ge1\) absorb the exponentially smaller exceptional term, reducing \(c'''\) if necessary.

**PROVED.** For the other inequality in (38), let \(A_p\) denote a hit. Conditional on avoiding any collection of plaquettes with no edge in common with \(p\), its probability is at most \(b_0=C(1+\beta)^{3/2}r^3\), by integrating one edge of \(p\) conditional on all other links. Each plaquette shares an edge with at most a fixed number \(d_0\) of others. Choose \(x=2b_0\) with \((1-x)^{d_0}\ge1/2\), shrinking the stated radius constant. For any conditioning set of avoided plaquettes excluding \(p\), its conditional probability of \(A_p\) is at most \(x\). Induct on the cardinality: first condition only on nonneighbors, then divide its probability bound by the probability of also avoiding the neighbors. The latter is at least \((1-x)^{d_0}\) using the induction hypothesis successively, unless there are no neighbors in which case the direct bound already applies. Multiplying the avoidance probabilities for all \(6V\) faces gives at least \((1-x)^{6V}\). This proves the upper log cost in (38). The scale here concerns actual near-centre faces in Haar Wilson, while (32) concerns a different, extensive constraint in the comparison measure.

**PROVED (the thermal scale for adaptive gauge fixing).** In the setting of (24) with \(\ell\ge\log(2+\beta)\), fix \(t>0\) and \(r=t/\sqrt{1+\beta}\). For some \(c_t>0\), with probability at least \(1-C_t e^{-c_t V}\),
\[
                 1/4+c_t\le M_r/E\le1-c_t.                           \tag{39}
\]
For the lower bound, choose three consecutive edges of every separated square in (35). These disjoint paths extend to a spanning tree. Once the tree is gauged to \(I\), the fourth edge on each square is conjugate to its plaquette holonomy or inverse. With the exponentially high probability of favorable exteriors just proved, a positive fraction of the squares have independent conditional hit probabilities bounded below by \(c_t\). For \(t>1\) it suffices to use the smaller cap with \(t=1\). Exponential Markov applied to the independent hit bits shows that at least \(c_t V\) such fourth edges succeed except on probability \(C_t e^{-c_t V}\). Add them to the \(V-1\) tree edges.

**PROVED.** For the upper bound, for any exterior the tilted conditional probability of \(\Delta(U_{p_i})>4r\) is at least a positive constant depending only on \(t\). Indeed for \(1+w_i\le c(t)(1+\beta)\) the tilted-density upper bound times the volume of the two caps of radius \(4r\) is at most \(1/2\), choosing \(c(t)\) small. In the other case choose a ball of radius \(c/\sqrt{1+\beta}\) outside both caps within distance \(C_t/\sqrt{1+\beta}\) of the maximizing direction: if necessary move away from its nearer pole along a great circle. For sufficiently large \(\beta\) the two polar caps are separated. The tilted density on this ball is at least \(c_t(1+\beta)^{3/2}\), proving the claim. Conditional independence gives at least \(c_t V\) separated faces exceeding \(4r\) except on probability \(C_t e^{-c_t V}\). Each needs a failed edge in every gauge by the triangle inequality; the faces have disjoint edges. This proves the other side of (39).

**PROVED.** If instead \(r\sqrt{1+\beta}\to\infty\), then \(M_r/E\to1\) in probability. Use boxes with each side in \([k,2k]\) and fix the links along the canonical coordinate-ordered paths from each box root as its spanning tree. For an internal edge, the root path followed by that edge can be converted into the root path to its far endpoint by commuting the final step across at most \(8k\) transverse steps and cancelling any backtrack. The resulting tree-gauge link is a product of at most \(8k\) conjugates of elementary face holonomies or their inverses. Its squared distance to the centre has mean at most \(C k^2/\beta\) by the face energy estimate from (26) and Cauchy–Schwarz. The expected fraction of failed links is at most \(4/k+Ck^2/(\beta r^2)\), including inter-box edges. Choose \(k\to\infty\) with \(2k\le\ell\) and \(k^2=o(\beta r^2)\). Equations (24), (39), and this large-radius result locate the change from the tree fraction to near-complete gauge coverage. In the range where coverage tends to one, the worst-case action variation \(O(\beta r^2)\) on an all-cap face is no longer small. At any fixed thermal radius a positive density of edges still fails in **every** gauge. This prevents replacing the original Wilson field in the proof of (11) by a field-adapted choice of thin cap links using that small-remainder estimate.


## The cost of a phase change across a spatial slice

**PROVED (spatial-volume exponent for the tunnelling level).** The line exponent in (15) can be improved. For \(n\) divisible by four and \(\beta,n\to\infty\), there is a choice of \(s_*\) with \(-\log s_*/\log\beta\to9/8\) for which
\[
      m_{+,s_*}(n)\le C(1+\beta)n^3
                         \exp[-c n^3\log\beta].                        \tag{40}
\]
This holds both for centre atoms and for the smooth comparison of (18). Here is a four-dimensional argument using (14). Write \(M=n/2\) for the spatial tile side. In an interval of \(M\) consecutive tile times, reflect its sites into a temporal cycle with \(2M\) sites, by repeating the end layers at the turns. A connected component of bad sites for sup-distance-one adjacency in the doubled box projects into a single such component of the original slab; all copies of an original component have at most twice its size in total. Adjacent sites on the doubled torus cannot have opposite pure labels.

**PROVED (boundary estimate in the doubled slab).** On this torus a set \(W\) of at most half the \(D=2M^4\) sites satisfies \(|\partial W|\ge c |W|^{3/4}\), with \(\partial\) the edges of its boundary. Indeed let \(N_i\) be its four periods and \(p_i\) the fraction of lines parallel to coordinate \(i\) which meet \(W\). For a uniform site \(X\) of \(W\), the entropy chain rule gives \(\sum_i H(X_{\ne i})\ge3H(X)\): each coordinate occurs three times and has at most its full set of predecessors in these conditional entropies. Bounding these entropies by the logs of their support sizes yields \(\prod_i p_i\ge(|W|/D)^3\). At least a fraction \(p_i-|W|/D\) of the \(D/N_i\) lines meet \(W\) only partially, and each has two boundary edges. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality gives
\[
 |\partial W|\ge8\min_i(D/N_i)(|W|/D)^{3/4}
                       [1-(|W|/D)^{1/4}]\ge c |W|^{3/4}.
\]

**PROVED.** Let \(H\) be the pure Haar sites in the doubled slab, with all remaining sites temporarily given the opposite bit. Each edge across \(\partial H\) abuts a bad exterior site since opposite pure types cannot meet. Partition these boundary edges by the sup-connected bad component of that exterior site. For each component \(B\) its edges give a closed one-cochain mod two: on any elementary square all boundary crossings have bad endpoints at sup distance at most one, and the full bit boundary has even parity there. A closed one-cochain with nonzero period must have at least \(M^3\) edges, since all parallel lines have the same period by the square equations. If \(8|B|<M^3\), the cochain is exact: integrate it along a path from a fixed root, using the elementary squares and zero periods for path independence. Choose its primitive bit set \(W_B\) of smaller volume. The boundary estimate gives \(|W_B|\le C|B|^{4/3}\). The mod-two sum of these primitives is \(H\) or its complement whenever all components are this small, by equality of their coboundaries. The same argument applies to the pure cap or pure centre set. Returning to the original slab, if neither pure type covers at least \(7/8\), then some bad component has size at least \(M^3/16\), or \(\sum_B |B|^{4/3}\ge c M^4\). Indeed if the bad fraction is large their sizes already give this bound; otherwise at least one pure type occupies a fixed fraction with complement of fixed fraction too. Doubling the slab costs only an absolute factor in this argument.

**PROVED (slab probability).** There are at most \(C M^4 A^j\) connected sets of \(j\) sites in the original slab for an absolute \(A\). A component of size at least \(M^3/16\) contains a connected set of the threshold size up to rounding. Its probability is at most \(C M^4(Aq)^{\lfloor M^3/16\rfloor}\) by (14). To count many small components, take \(\theta=c_0\log(1/q)/M\) with \(c_0\) small. For the actual components of sizes \(j\le M^3/16\) expand \(e^{\theta\sum j^{4/3}}\) in products of \(e^{\theta j^{4/3}}-1\). Distinct components are disjoint. For any specified collection the probability they are all bad is at most \(8q^{\sum j}\) by (14); dropping all restrictions after using this estimate bounds the moment by
\[
       8\exp\!\left[C M^4\sum_{j\le M^3/16}
                    (Aq)^j(e^{\theta j^{4/3}}-1)\right]
