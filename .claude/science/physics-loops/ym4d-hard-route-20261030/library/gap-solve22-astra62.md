**PROVED (an exact conditional Wilson action).** Use unit quaternions for spatial links and write \(u\cdot v=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(u v^\dagger)\). On a periodic spatial cube of side \(n\ge m+4\), keep \(m\) links oriented in direction 1. Their bases are \((0,j,0)\), \(1\le j\le m\). Keep their whole time histories \(u_{j,t}\) and condition on the other links. There are periodic exterior values for which the negative log conditional weight, divided by \(\beta\), is, up to a constant,
\[
 {\cal A}(u)=\frac12\sum_{j,t}|\Delta_t(u_{j,t}-q_t)|^2+
             \frac12\sum_{j<m,t}|u_{j+1,t}-u_{j,t}|^2 .                 \tag{1}
\]
Here \(q\) is a fixed periodic walk in a three-plane in quaternion space. Its length, radius and period are independent of \(m,n,\beta\).

**PROVED.** Fix orthonormal real coordinates \(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4\) in quaternion space. Take \(\rho=1/8\) and period \(p=12\). For \(t=0,\ldots,6\) use, in order,
\[
 q_t=\rho(e_1,e_2,e_3,-e_1,-e_2,-e_3,e_1)_t .
\]
Reflect this list by \(q_{12-t}=q_t\) and repeat. Thus \(h_t=2q_t-q_{t-1}-q_{t+1}\) has norm at most \(4\rho\). Set all temporal links and all links in directions 2 and 3 to the identity. An interior transverse plaquette couples neighboring unknowns by \(u_{j,t}\cdot u_{j+1,t}\). Each temporal plaquette supplies \(u_{j,t}\cdot u_{j,t+1}\). A fixed parallel neighbor contributes its dot product with \(u_{j,t}\). At either end set the one fixed neighbor in direction 2 to a unit vector \(b\). If \(m=1\), choose the two end neighbors opposite. Let \(b_j\) denote their sum at each site, zero in the interior. The two remaining fixed neighbors, in directions \(+3,-3\), can be chosen to sum to \(h_t-b_j\). Its norm is at most \(3/2\). Every vector of norm at most two is the sum of two unit quaternions, by taking opposite perpendicular components of equal length. The chosen links are distinct for distinct \(j\). All other spatial links can be fixed arbitrarily. This realizes the desired linear field using the original isotropic Wilson action with every plaquette present. Periodic summation by parts gives
\(\sum_t\Delta u_t\cdot\Delta q_t=\sum_t u_t\cdot h_t\), which proves (1).

**PROVED.** The zero-action paths in (1) are exactly
\[
 u_{j,t}=q_t+d,\qquad d=\pm w e_4,\qquad w=\sqrt{1-\rho^2}.
                                                                    \tag{2}
\]
Indeed the first squares make \(d\) constant along each line. Testing \(|q_t+d|=1\) at the opposite visits to the three axes forces \(d\) perpendicular to all three. The remaining squares equate \(d\) on neighboring lines. The reflection of the fourth component interchanges the two paths and preserves Haar measure and (1).

## The time required to reverse the ribbon

**PROVED.** Choose \(e_4\) to be the identity quaternion. At an anchor time \(t=kp\) label each row by the sign of \(u_{j,t}\cdot e_4\). A zero can receive either sign. For odd \(m\), let \(S\) be the majority of these \(m\) signs. It is a physical reading in the prescribed exterior. Choose the direction-1 return link two steps away in direction 3 to be the identity for each \(j\). The trace of the contractible \(1\)-by-\(2\) rectangle through that return link is then \(2u_{j,t}\cdot e_4\). These return links were free in (1). The majority can thus be defined by Wilson loops before any conditioning.

**PROVED (a barrier proportional to the ribbon width).** On a segment of \(\ell\) periods let \(A_\ell\) be (1) with temporal squares on all its bonds and spatial squares at its two ends counted with half weight. There is \(c>0\), independent of \(m,n,\ell,\beta\), such that
\[
 \ell\ge m,\quad S(0)\ne S(p\ell)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A_\ell\ge c m .
                                                                    \tag{3}
\]
On one row and one period, the temporal action vanishes only on the two paths in (2). By compactness its minimum \(d\) is positive on the closed set where at least one endpoint has fourth component of absolute value at most \(w/2\) or the two endpoint fourth components have product at most zero. Fix \(d\) at this one-row, one-period stage.

**PROVED.** Draw the signs on the grid with \(m\) rows and \(\ell+1\) time layers. Count neighboring grid sites with different signs. Each vertical disagreement uses a row-period of temporal cost at least \(d\). A horizontal disagreement with a weak endpoint, of absolute fourth component at most \(w/2\), can be assigned to a row-period touching that endpoint. At most four such horizontal disagreements and one vertical disagreement can charge any row-period. Every other horizontal disagreement costs at least \(w^2/4\), even with the half weight at an end. The action is therefore at least \(\min(d/5,w^2/4)\) times the disagreement count. If all layers are mixed, each contains a horizontal disagreement. If a layer is constant, it differs from one of the two end majorities on at least half of the rows. Each such row has a vertical disagreement. This proves (3), with \(c=\tfrac12\min(d/5,w^2/4)\). An explicit path changes every row from the plus path in (2) to the minus path on one bond. It pays \(2w^2m\) and has no spatial cost. Thus the lower and upper action barriers have the same order in \(m\).

**PROVED (conditional transfer).** Let \(K(x,y)\) be the one-period kernel obtained by integrating \(e^{-\beta A_1}\) over its interior links with probability Haar measure. This is the original conditional transfer up to a positive scalar. The summation by parts on one period gives the endpoint coefficients \(h_0/2\) at both ends because \(q_{p-1}=q_1\). Split the spatial weights in halves there. The two half-periods are time reflections, so \(K=BB^*\) with half weights at the middle. The kernel is continuous, strictly positive and at most one. It is compact with simple top eigenvalue \(\lambda>0\) and a positive normalized eigenfunction \(\psi\). Indeed compactness gives a top vector, taking its absolute value preserves or increases the Rayleigh quotient, and strict positivity rules out a sign-changing real top vector. Reflection of every fourth component fixes \(\psi\) by simplicity, and makes \(S\) odd almost everywhere.

**PROVED.** There is \(C<\infty\), independent of \(m,n,\beta\ge1\), such that
\[
 1\ge\lambda\ge \exp[-C m\log(2+\beta)] .                              \tag{4}
\]
Test the Rayleigh quotient on the normalized indicator of all \(m\) starting quaternions lying within \((4\sqrt\beta)^{-1}\) of the plus anchor in (2). Integrate over the corresponding product of caps about the plus path at each layer, including the end. Each cap has Haar probability at least \(c_1\beta^{-3/2}\), by the spherical polar density proportional to \(\sin^2\theta\) and \(\sin\theta\ge2\theta/\pi\) on \([0,\pi/2]\). On the tubes \(\beta A_1\le C_1mp\) by the triangle inequality. The test normalization cancels one endpoint cap product, leaving \(mp\) cap factors. The period \(p\) is fixed.

**PROVED.** In the vacuum chain of this conditional ribbon, (3) and probability Haar give \(K^m(x,y)\le e^{-c\beta m}\) whenever \(S(x)\ne S(y)\). Its stationary pair density is \(\lambda^{-m}\psi(x)K^m(x,y)\psi(y)\). Since \(\int\psi\le1\), the normalized period transfer \(P\), conjugated to the measure \(\psi^2 dx\), satisfies
\[
 1-\langle S,P^m S\rangle
       \le 2\exp[-c\beta m+C m^2\log(2+\beta)].                       \tag{5}
\]
The vector has mean zero and norm one. For \(0\le z\le1\), \(1-z^k\le\lceil k/m\rceil(1-z^m)\) if \(k\ge1\). Spectral integration therefore bounds the loss at any \(k\) periods by \(\lceil k/m\rceil\) times the right side of (5).

**PROVED (physical separation).** Let \(\liminf\beta_n/\log n>\sigma>0\) with \(\beta_n\to\infty\). Take an odd *fixed* width \(m>2/(c\sigma)\). For \(kp\asymp n\) the right side of (5) times \(\lceil k/m\rceil\) tends to zero. Thus one bounded gauge-invariant reading has conditional time correlation tending to one at physical separation. Its spatial support is a fixed number of lattice links. This disproves contraction by any common factor less than one over all Wilson exteriors on fixed-width ribbons of arbitrary width. It concerns the conditional experiment.

## Returning to the ordinary Wilson law

**PROVED (finite periods and open sets of exteriors).** The obstruction also occurs for conditional laws on finite Wilson tori. With the fixed exterior repeated for \(N\ge2m+2\) periods, the normalizer in the convention of (1) is \(\operatorname{Tr}K^N\ge\lambda^N\). Restrict \(K^m\) to endpoints with opposite signs. Its Hilbert–Schmidt norm is at most \(e^{-c\beta m}\) by (3). Also \(\|K^{N-m}\|_{\rm HS}\le\lambda^{N-m-1}\|K\|_{\rm HS}\le\lambda^{N-m-1}\). Trace Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the probability of differing signs \(m\) periods apart by \(e^{-c\beta m}\lambda^{-m-1}\). The restriction is an integral kernel, and composing it with the complementary arc gives exactly the numerator of that probability. Taking a union along successive \(m\)-period arcs proves persistence for a separation of order \(n\) under the slope and width choice above, on rings long enough to contain those arcs. At each finite size the two-time majority correlation and its one-point means are continuous at the specified exterior. The conditional densities converge uniformly as the exterior varies. The zero sets of the finitely many loop traces have Haar measure zero on the interior. Dominated convergence also handles their signs. Thus an open set of exteriors of positive Wilson probability has the same lower bound on the connected correlation with an arbitrarily small loss.

**PROVED (a cost visible from the exterior).** Take the end neighbors \(b_j\) independent of time. At some step of the period, \(D=|h_{t+1}-h_t|>0\). Otherwise periodicity would give \(h_t=0\) and \(q_t\) constant. At this step the two fixed parallel neighbors in directions \(+3,-3\) have temporal increments with sum \(h_{t+1}-h_t\). Their two temporal plaquette deficits \(d_f=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}U_f\) sum to at least \(D^2/4\), by the squared chord formula and Cauchy–Schwarz. The marked plaquettes are distinct across rows and periods. A neighborhood of the constructed exterior therefore has total deficit at least \(D^2mk/8\) on \(2mk\) *entirely observed* faces in \(k\) periods. ## A bound for arbitrary states on the ends of a Wilson slab

**GUIDE.** I use the Wilson marked-face moment of row 1155, (9). Its proof rotates periodic rectangles to put a mark direction in time. The normalized one-link convolution decreases in positive-operator order on lowering its coupling, by the Bessel-ratio proof in row 1149, (16). The Haar normalizer costs a constant for halving the coupling. Trace Hölder over the layers, then ordinary Hölder over four classes of directions, gives \(\mathbb E\exp(\alpha\beta\sum_{f\in D}d_f)\le C^{|D|}\) on every periodic rectangle, with \(\alpha=1/8\). This is the *periodic* estimate used here. The positive transfer and sparse insertion argument GL8–GL10 are due to Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text, outline 2313–2381. [Row 1149](gap-solve4-astra62.md) · [Row 1155](gap-solve7-astra62.md) · [Outline index](sa-index/chunk-02.md)

**PROVED (open slab).** Let \(F=M C M\) be the physical Wilson transfer on a periodic spatial cube, with \(M\) the positive magnetic half weight and \(C\) the electric convolution including gauge projection. Use a single normalization throughout and put \(\lambda_0=\|F\|\). Write \(F_{[0,r]}(W)\) for its \(r\)-step kernel with nonnegative path weight \(W\) inserted. In particular \(F_{[0,r]}(1)=F^r\). For any deterministic set \(D\) of plaquettes in the slab, with spatial marks on interior slices and temporal marks on its bonds,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|F_{[0,r]}(\exp(\alpha\beta\textstyle\sum_{D}d_f))\|
      &\le \lambda_0^r C^{|D|},\\
 \lambda_0^{-r}\|F_{[0,r]}({\bf1}_{\{\sum_Dd_f\ge x\}})\|
      &\le \exp[-\alpha\beta x+|D|\log C] .                           \tag{6}
 \end{split}
\]
The estimates apply to arbitrary end vectors by pairing their absolute values with the nonnegative kernels. The constants have no dependence on the spatial volume, the slab length or \(\beta\ge1\).

**PROVED.** Given spatial marks \(H\) on a slice and temporal marks \(J\) on a step, insert the spatial half weight into \(M_H\) and lower the electric couplings on \(J\) by the factor \(1-\alpha\) in \(C_J\). Keep scalar normalizers from the original action. The kernel \(D_{H,J}=M_H C_J M_H\) is positive and trace class because each positive-coupling electric convolution has nonnegative, summable character multipliers and commutes with vertex gauge averaging. Repeat these same marks on every layer of a time circle of length \(b\). The periodic estimate gives
\(\operatorname{Tr}D_{H,J}^b/\operatorname{Tr}F^b\le C^{b(|H|+|J|)}\).
For a nonzero positive trace-class operator \(A\), its trace of the \(b\)-th power lies between \(\|A\|^b\) and \(\|A\|^{b-1}\operatorname{Tr}A\). Taking \(b\)-th roots gives
\(\|D_{H,J}\|\le\lambda_0 C^{|H|+|J|}\). In the actual inhomogeneous slab the step is \(M_{H_i}C_{J_i}M_{H_{i+1}}\). Factor at \(C_{J_i}^{1/2}\). Its norm is bounded by \(\|D_{H_i,J_i}\|^{1/2}\|D_{H_{i+1},J_i}\|^{1/2}\). Multiplying the estimates counts each interior spatial mark and each temporal mark once. Exponential domination gives the second line of (6). No ordering of noncommuting products is used.

**PROVED.** Applied to the exterior just constructed, (6) bounds the normalized transmission through its neighborhood by \(\exp[-\alpha\beta D^2mk/8+2mk\log C]\). In particular the slow conditional sign in (5) does not by this construction give a low-energy state of the unconditioned transfer.

## Contraction with a nearly flat spatial boundary

**GUIDE.** A prescribed rough boundary can keep the sign in (5). I now test a slab with its ordinary Wilson weight. The spatial walls below are the box boundaries of row 1173. Its estimate is in total variation under the periodic probability law. Here the end functions may be any unit vectors in the physical transfer space, including vectors large on a rare event.

**PROVED (conditional kernel on one box).** Fix an integer \(b\ge3\). Allow any spatial rectangular box \(B\) with sides in \([b,2b]\). Prescribe the tangential spatial links and the temporal links of its lateral boundary \(\partial B\times[0,s]\). For given interior spatial end configurations \(x,y\), integrate all remaining links to obtain a positive kernel \(K_E(x,y)\). Here \(E\) denotes the lateral data. End magnetic terms can have half weight. Pure boundary terms amount to scalars. Define the diameter of a kernel as
\[
 {\cal D}(K)=\sup_{x,x',y,y'}\left|\log
          \frac{K(x,y)K(x',y')}{K(x,y')K(x',y)}\right|.
\]
For every \(\tau>0\) there are an integer \(s\ge4\), a number \(\eta>0\) and a finite \(\beta_0\), in that order after \(b,\tau\), such that
\[
 d_f\le\eta\ \text{on every lateral plaquette}
       \quad\Longrightarrow\quad {\cal D}(K_E)\le 2\tau\beta
       \quad(\beta\ge\beta_0).                                        \tag{7}
\]
The bound is uniform in all four end configurations.

**PROVED (gauge and choice of parameters).** Two elementary finite-complex facts are needed. A flat connection on the spatial box is pure gauge. If the boundary links are the identity, the gauge can equal the identity on the whole boundary. A flat connection on the lateral surface is also pure gauge. To see these statements, transport from a root along a tree. The filled box is contractible, and its lateral complex in spacetime is a subdivided two-sphere times an interval. Hence each closed edge path contracts by inserting or removing face boundaries and backtracking pairs. This gives path independence. On a connected boundary of identity links the root transport is constant there. Compactness now yields the corresponding approximation statements. For example, if a sequence of spatial fields with boundary links tending to the identity and all deficits tending to zero could not be put within a prescribed distance of the identity by boundary-fixed gauges, take a convergent subsequence and apply a trivializing gauge for its limit. The same argument on the lateral complex uses unrestricted gauges there. These gauges extend to the slab by assigning any values at interior vertices. They only relabel the end arguments of a kernel. There are finitely many allowed spatial box shapes.

**PROVED (comparison of minimum actions).** First prescribe a small tolerance for the distances between the two sets of spatial links at a cut. Choose it so that the sum over at most \(C_b\) links of the products of two such small differences is at most \(\tau\), allowing also for the transported-link errors below. The spatial compactness statement gives a small enough threshold on the one-slice action and boundary distances. Choose \(s\) large, and then a distance \(\delta\) small, so that \(C'_b(1/s+\delta^2)\) is below that action threshold and \(\delta\) is below the boundary threshold. Choose \(\eta\) by lateral compactness for this \(s,\delta\). In a boundary gauge all the observed links are now within \(\delta\) of the identity.

**PROVED.** For arbitrary ends an interior path with all free intermediate links and all free temporal links equal to the identity costs at most \(C_b(1+s\delta^2)\) in deficit units. The two end layers and their incident steps cost at most \(C_b\). Every other plaquette is a product of at most four links within \(\delta\) of the identity, with deficit bounded by \(8\delta^2\). Consider minimizers for \(x,y\) and \(x',y'\), which exist by compactness. At one interface with interior top and bottom the sum of the two top spatial actions and both temporal actions on that step is at most \(C'_b(1/s+\delta^2)\), by averaging over the interior steps. Boundary-only faces may be included in this count. Apply the spatial gauge at that top slice separately to each minimizer, fixing the whole boundary. Its spatial links \(v_e^1,v_e^2\) are now close to the identity. The bottom links \(a_e^1,a_e^2\) transported to the top endpoints satisfy \(|a_e^i-v_e^i|^2=2d_e^i\) by the temporal plaquette formula. They too are close. Exchange the two paths above the interface, keeping the temporal links crossing that interface with their bottom paths. The change in the action sum is \(\sum_e(a_e^1-a_e^2)\cdot(v_e^1-v_e^2)\). Its absolute value is at most \(\tau\) by the choices just made. This proves the four-end inequality for minimum actions in one direction. Exchanging \(y,y'\) proves the other direction. The two-path exchange applies the geometric cut of row 1173, (1), to minimizers at arbitrary prescribed ends.

**PROVED.** For the fixed finite block let \(I_E(x,y)\) be its minimum action. Probability Haar gives \(\log K_E(x,y)\le-\beta I_E(x,y)\). For a lower bound, restrict every integrated link to a ball of radius \((1+\beta)^{-1}\) about a minimizer. Each face is uniformly Lipschitz in its four links, and the Haar ball bound used in (4) gives
\[
 \log K_E(x,y)\ge-\beta I_E(x,y)-C_{b,s}\log(2+\beta).
\]
All constants are uniform in the prescribed data. Thus the four-end diameter is at most \(\tau\beta+4C_{b,s}\log(2+\beta)\). This proves (7).

## What the walls retain of the full transfer spectrum

**PROVED (a uniform bound in the original law).** Take a sequence of periodic spatial cubes with \(n\to\infty\), \(\beta\to\infty\) and \(\beta\le B_0\log n\) for a fixed \(B_0<\infty\). Choose \(0<\gamma<1\) and \(r=\lceil n^\gamma\rceil\). Tile each spatial circle by intervals of integer lengths in \([b,2b]\), possible for \(n\ge2b\) by distributing \(n\) among \(\lfloor n/b\rfloor\) intervals. Observe just the lateral walls of these boxes through times \([0,r]\), including their boundary spatial links at the ends. Call the observation \(E\). For each fixed \(b\) there is an event \(G\) depending only on \(E\) such that, for every fixed \(A>0\) and all sufficiently large \(n\),
\[
 \begin{split}
  \lambda_0^{-r}\|F_{[0,r]}({\bf1}_{G^c})\|&\le n^{-A},\\
  \sup_{E\in G}{\cal D}(K^{\rm all}_E)&\le n^{-A}.                    \tag{8}
 \end{split}
\]
In the second line the variables are all the unobserved spatial links at the two ends. The Wilson estimate concerns the original measure. The boundary in (5) has the marked positive deficits found above.

**PROVED.** Choose \(\tau>0\) so small that \(\tau B_0<\gamma/2\), and take \(s,\eta,\beta_0\) from (7). Use \(k=\lfloor(r-2)/(s+1)\rfloor\) disjoint subintervals of \(s\) steps, starting at times \(1+i(s+1)\), \(0\le i<k\). All their spatial faces are internal to the slab. A subinterval of a box is good if each lateral deficit is at most \(\eta\). The event \(G\) requires at least \(k/2\) good subintervals for every box. If a box fails, its marked lateral faces have total deficit at least \(\eta k/2\), on at most \(C_{b,s}k\) fixed faces. Use (6) and sum the positive event kernels over the boxes. Their number is at most \(n^3\). The first line of (8) follows from \(n^3\exp[-\alpha\beta\eta k/2+C_{b,s}k\log C]\).

**PROVED.** At fixed \(E\), the interior integrals factor over boxes because every plaquette is contained in a box or in the observed boundary. Boundary-only factors and magnetic end weights do not affect the four-end ratio. Inside one box the bounded action on a finite set of faces and the normalized reference measure give every one-step kernel diameter at most \(C_b\beta\). For a positive kernel of diameter \(D\), propagation of positive densities contracts the oscillation of their log ratio by \(\tanh(D/4)\), as proved by interpolation of tilted input measures in row 1155, (3). The first step bounds the distance even for point inputs. The other steps do not increase it. Each good subinterval contracts by at most \(\tanh(\tau\beta/2)\le1-e^{-\tau\beta}\). The diameter on the entire interval is therefore at most \(C_b\beta\exp[-k e^{-\tau\beta}/2]\) in each box. The diameters of the product add, and \(k e^{-\tau\beta}\) grows at least as a positive constant times \(n^{\gamma/2}\). This gives the second line of (8).

**PROVED (low-energy vectors, without a bound on their values).** Take time infinite on this finite spatial cube. Use the stationary vacuum path measure, the slice law \(\pi=\psi_0^2 dx\), and the normalized transfer \(P=\psi_0^{-1}(F/\lambda_0)\psi_0\). Let \(J_i f=f(X_i)\) embed the slice space isometrically in the slab path space, \(i=0,r\), and let \(Q\) be conditional expectation onto \(E\). Put \(\varepsilon=n^{-A}\) as in (8). For either end,
\[
 \|(1-Q)J_i f\|^2
 \le 2(1+e^\varepsilon)\langle f,(1-P^r)f\rangle_\pi
             +2\varepsilon\|f\|_\pi^2 .                                 \tag{9}
\]
For energy at most \(a\Lambda\) in lattice units, \(a=L/n\), the first inner product is at most \(ra\Lambda\|f\|^2\). Thus (9) holds uniformly over the entire bounded-physical-energy subspace on a fixed physical cube.

**PROVED.** Conditional on a good \(E\), the density of the two ends has four-point ratio bounded by \(e^\varepsilon\). The two vacuum factors are one-sided and cancel. Integrating the ratio inequality over independent second arguments shows that this joint law dominates \(e^{-\varepsilon}\) times the product of its own marginals. Hence the conditional variance at either end is at most \(e^\varepsilon\) times the conditional mean of \(|f(X_0)-f(X_r)|^2\). For the complement set \(u^2=\mathbb E[{\bf1}_{G^c}|f(X_0)|^2]\), \(w^2=\mathbb E|f(X_0)-f(X_r)|^2\). The first line of (8) applied to the end functions \(|f|\psi_0\) gives \(\mathbb E[{\bf1}_{G^c}|f(X_0)f(X_r)|]\le\varepsilon\|f\|^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(u^2\le\varepsilon\|f\|^2+uw\le\varepsilon\|f\|^2+(u^2+w^2)/2\). This also holds with the ends reversed. Since \(w^2=2\langle f,(1-P^r)f\rangle_\pi\), integration proves (9). In particular a small stationary probability alone would not justify applying the estimate to all normalized eigenfunctions.

**PROVED (every singular value at a physical separation).** Observe the same walls on \([0,r]\) and \([t,t+r]\), with \(t>r\). Identify the two single-slab path spaces by translation. The joint expectation operator for the two observations, extended by zero off the observed subspace, is
\[
