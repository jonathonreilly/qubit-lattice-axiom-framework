**PROVED.** Integration of the temporal links inserts the vertex gauge projection in each time step, by independently changing variables at the vertices of the next slice. ## Neutral excitations with one spatial twist

**PROVED (convergence on the invariant normal directions).** With the twist of (1), let \(A\) have seam holonomies \((i,j,1)\), \(K\) be the product of the vertex gauge group with the three center flips, and \(H_A\) its stabilizer at \(A\). **PROVED (normal coordinates).** The entire flat set is \(O=KA\) by the seam classification. An infinitesimal gauge transformation is an adjoint gradient. At each shifted momentum its image is the longitudinal direction of the curl and there is no zero momentum. Thus \(T_AO=\ker D_A\), \(D_A|N\) is injective, and the orbit has dimension \(g=3n^3\). To determine the stabilizer, observe that a gauge preserving the open-cube links is constant. This constant must conjugate \(i,j\) to their assigned signs times \(i,j\). The only possibilities are \(\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\), with their determined flips on seams 1 and 2. The third flip must be plus. The spatial group metric makes all these actions isometries.

**PROVED.** For a sufficiently small fixed \(\delta_n>0\) a neighborhood of \(O\) is parametrized by \(k\exp_A v\), \(k\in K,\ v\in N,\ |v|<\delta_n\), with the identification \((k,v)\sim(kh,h^{-1}v)\) for \(h\in H_A\). Here \(\exp_A\) is the product Riemannian exponential, as used in (3). The derivative in orbit and normal coordinates at zero is the isomorphism \(T_AO\oplus N\to T_AM\), where \(M=SU(2)^{\mathcal E}\). The inverse function theorem gives a local chart. Equivariance gives the same radius along the orbit. If arbitrarily small normal vectors on two such charts represented the same point without the stated identification, compactness would give a limiting relative group element in \(H_A\). Composing with its inverse would contradict local injectivity. Compactness also ensures that the chart covers a neighborhood of \(O\). The invariant function on this tube depends just on \(v\) and is \(H_A\)-invariant. Induced Riemannian volume has the form \(j(v)\,dv\,d\mathrm{vol}_O(y)\), using a local isometric group frame above \(y\in O\). The change of variables just proved gives a smooth Jacobian with \(j(0)=1\), independent of \(y\) in the transported frame. Overlapping choices differ by \(H_A\). Since \(D_A|N\) is injective, Taylor expansion gives \(V_\eta(\exp_A v)\ge c_n |v|^2\) on this tube after shrinking it. The potential has a strictly positive minimum off the tube.

**PROVED (the operator limit).** Truncate (1) at both ends to \(|v|\le R/\sqrt\beta\) in the tube. The discarded operator has norm at most \(2e^{-c'_nR^2}\) for fixed \(R\) and all sufficiently large \(\beta\), by the last potential bound and \(\|C_\beta\|=1\). For invariant functions the truncated operator acts on \(v\) with a kernel integrated over the orbit of the second point. Rescale \(v=\xi/\sqrt\beta,\ v'=\zeta/\sqrt\beta\) and conjugate by the square root of the normal volume density. On \(|\xi|,|\zeta|\le R\), the orbit integral converges uniformly to the normal Gaussian kernel in the assertion. The normalization can be checked directly. Relative to product Riemannian volume the normalized electric density has prefactor \((\beta/(2\pi))^{d/2}(1+o(1))\). Write \(y\) near \(A\) in induced orbit coordinates \(u/\sqrt\beta\) with an orthonormal tangent frame and choose the section equal to the identity at \(A\). The electric exponent converges to \((|u|^2+|\xi-\zeta|^2)/2\), since the orbit displacement is tangent and the normal frames converge. The magnetic exponent on the ends tends to \((|D_A\xi|^2+|D_A\zeta|^2)/4\) by invariance. Normal volume supplies \(\beta^{-(d-g)/2}\) and orbit volume supplies \(\beta^{-g/2}\), and the remaining tangent integral is \((2\pi)^{g/2}\). The constant total orbit volume in the norm of invariant functions cancels in conjugating the operator. This leaves precisely \((2\pi)^{-(d-g)/2}\). For domination, the two points are at distance at least \((\operatorname{dist}(A,y)-2R/\sqrt\beta)_+\). Locally \(\operatorname{dist}(A,y)\) is bounded below by a positive constant times the size of the orbit coordinate. The electric inequality used in (3) gives an integrable bound \(C_R e^{-c|u|^2}\). Points outside the local orbit chart are exponentially suppressed, absorbing every fixed power of \(\beta\). This proves the uniform convergence, hence Hilbert–Schmidt convergence on the fixed normal ball. Different sections away from the chart give the same operator on invariant functions. The limiting operator has norm tail \(O(e^{-c'_nR^2})\) because \(D_A|N\) is injective. Min–max first on the ball, with zeros added on the orthogonal complements, then \(R\to\infty\), proves the claimed eigenvalue convergence.

**PROVED (the first neutral level).** Let \(m^m_+(n,\beta)=-\log(\lambda^m_{1,+}/\lambda_0^m)\). In lattice time units,
\[
       \lim_{\beta\to\infty}m^m_+(n,\beta)
        =2\mu_n,\qquad \mu_n=2\operatorname{arsinh}\sin(\pi/(2n)).
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the one-dimensional Gaussian in (2), divided by its top and conjugated by its ground function, gives \(Y\) conditional on \(X=x\) Gaussian of mean \(e^{-\omega}x\) and variance \(e^{-\omega}\). The stationary variance is \(1/(2\sinh\omega)\). Testing the centered Gaussian exponential \(\exp(tY-t^2\operatorname{Var}(Y)/2)\) gives the same exponential at \(x\) with parameter \(e^{-\omega}t\). Its Taylor coefficients give Hermite eigenvectors with ratios \(e^{-j\omega}\). Their span is dense because a function orthogonal to all polynomials has vanishing Gaussian-weighted Fourier transform, by expansion of the exponential and Cauchy–Schwarz. Tensoring yields the spectrum on the transverse real modes. Each color is a different nontrivial sign character of \(H_A/\{\pm1\}\) because the combined flip and constant gauge act on left edge variations just by \(\operatorname{Ad}h\). **PROVED.** Define the magnetic vacuum energy relative to the periodic vacuum by \(\mathcal E_m(n,\beta)=\log(\lambda_0^0/\lambda_0^m)\). There is a strictly positive finite constant \(c_m\) such that
\[
 \lim_{\beta\to\infty}\mathcal E_m(n,\beta)=\frac{c_m}{n}+O(n^{-3}),
 \qquad
 c_m=\frac4{\pi^2}
 \sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^3\\ (z_1,z_2)\notin(2\mathbb Z)^2}}
             \frac1{|z|^4}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Here the left side first takes its limit at each fixed \(n\). The error refers to the resulting sequence in \(n\). It gives no error uniform in the bare coupling for the original two-parameter quantity.

**PROVED.** Let \(f(q)=\omega(\ell(q))\) on the period-\(2\pi\) torus and use normalized Fourier coefficients. For integer \(v\ne0\),
\[
             \widehat f(v)=-\frac{1}{\pi^2 |v|^4}+O(|v|^{-6}).
\]
To verify the constant, subtract \(\chi(q)|q|\) where \(\chi\) is a smooth radial cutoff, supported strictly inside the coordinate chart and equal to one near zero. The subtracted function is smooth away from zero and obeys the bounds \(C_\alpha |q|^{3-|\alpha|}\) for every derivative of order \(|\alpha|\) near zero. Indeed in \(q=\rho u\) the Taylor series of \(\ell(\rho u)/\rho^2\) is smooth in \((\rho,u)\), equals one at zero, and differs from one by \(O(\rho^2)\). Taking the square root and composing with the odd analytic series for \(2\operatorname{arsinh}(\,\cdot\,/2)\) gives the bounds. On a smooth dyadic annulus of radius \(b\) the Fourier integral of this remainder is at most \(C_N b^6\min(1,(b|v|)^{-N})\), by integrating along a coordinate of \(|v_j|\ge |v|/\sqrt3\). Summing for \(N=7\), including the smooth outer part, costs \(O(|v|^{-6})\). The radial cutoff term contributes
\[
  \frac{1}{2\pi^2 |v|}\int_0^\infty \rho^2\chi(\rho)\sin(|v|\rho)\,d\rho.
\]
Three integrations by parts give \(-2/|v|^3\) inside this prefactor, with a rapidly decaying remainder since subsequent derivatives vanish near zero. This proves the coefficient and its summability.

**PROVED.** Summation on the shifted grid now gives
