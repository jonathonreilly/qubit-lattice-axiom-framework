## An exactly defined Wilson transfer for the constant spatial modes

**GUIDE.** I first take the spatial torus with one vertex and three periodic links. Each spatial plaquette is then a group commutator. This gives an exact test of the constant-mode mechanism in discrete Wilson time. The thermal test above uses spatial sides tending to infinity. The comparison to those sides is a separate step below. The transfer convention comes from Osterwalder–Seiler and Lüscher. For the corresponding two placements of the magnetic weight, see Sussman–Alpoge, GL3 and GL8. [Outline, lines 2197–2214](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex#L2197-L2214) · [Outline, lines 2315–2335](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex#L2315-L2335)

**PROVED.** Use unit quaternions and the round metric of radius one. Write the spatial links as $q_i=s_i+v_i$, where $v_i\in\mathbb R^3$, $1\le i\le3$. In electric normalization the transfer on simultaneous conjugation invariants is
\[
 T=M C_1C_2C_3 M,\quad M=e^{-\beta V/2},\qquad
 V=2\sum_{i<j}|v_i\times v_j|^2.                                      \tag{1}
\]
Indeed $1-\operatorname{Re}(q_iq_jq_i^{-1}q_j^{-1})=|q_iq_j-q_jq_i|^2/2$ and the latter difference of products is $2v_i\times v_j$. Each $C_j$ convolves one coordinate with the probability density proportional to $e^{-\beta(1-\operatorname{Re}z)}$. Integrating the temporal gauge link inserts the simultaneous conjugation average. The neutral space also imposes invariance under $q_i\mapsto-q_i$ independently. The $C_j$ are commuting positive contractions. Positivity as a quadratic form follows by expanding $e^{\beta\operatorname{Re}z}$ in powers of the fundamental character. The character of each tensor power has nonnegative irreducible multiplicities. Schur orthogonality gives a nonnegative convolution eigenvalue in each representation.

**PROVED (one coordinate translation).** Write $D_j(g)=\langle g,(1-C_j)g\rangle$ and $L_sg(x)=g(s^{-1}x)$ in coordinate $j$. For $\beta\ge1$ there is a numerical $C$ such that
\[
 \|L_sg-g\|\le C(1+\sqrt\beta\,d(1,s))D_j(g)^{1/2}.                    \tag{2}
\]
In fact $2D_j(g)$ is the average of $\|L_zg-g\|^2$ under the convolution probability. The probability density on $d(1,z)\le2/\sqrt\beta$ is at least $c\beta^{3/2}$. To see this, its normalizing integral is bounded above by a constant times $\int_0^\pi r^2 e^{-2\beta r^2/\pi^2}dr$, using the Haar radial factor $\sin^2r$ and $1-\cos r\ge2r^2/\pi^2$. The numerator there is at least $e^{-2}$. If $d(1,s)\le1/\sqrt\beta$, average the squared triangle inequality through $L_tg$ over the Haar ball $d(1,t)\le1/\sqrt\beta$. The ball has measure at least $c\beta^{-3/2}$ and both $t$ and $s^{-1}t$ lie in the larger ball. This proves (2) for one short displacement. Subdividing a group geodesic into at most $1+\sqrt\beta\,d(1,s)$ such translations gives (2) by unitarity.

**PROVED (confinement along the commuting directions).** There are numerical $c,C>0$ such that for every $f\in L^2(\mathrm{SU}(2)^3)$
\[
 \langle f,(1-T)f\rangle
 \ \ge\ c\int \sum_{i=1}^3 |v_i|\,|f|^2-C\beta^{-1}\|f\|^2.            \tag{3}
\]
For $i\ne j$, $C_1C_2C_3\le C_j$ by their joint spectral representation. Freeze the two coordinates other than $j$ in the larger transfer $M C_j M$. Normalize $f$ on this fibre and write $g=Mf$, $r=|v_i|$. Its energy is exactly
\[
 e=\int(1-M^2)|f|^2+D_j(g).
\]
Take $\beta r\ge400$, $w=(\beta r)^{-1/2}\le1/20$. In the $j$th copy of the three-sphere let $H$ be the great circle of quaternions commuting with $q_i$. The distance to $H$ is $\arcsin |(v_j)_\perp|$, where perpendicular is relative to the axis $v_i$. Outside the $w$-tube about $H$, (1) gives $\beta V\ge 2\beta r^2\sin^2w\ge c_0r$. Hence if $e\le\epsilon r$, the $L^2$ mass of $g$ outside the tube is at most $C_0\epsilon$, and $\|g\|^2\ge1-\epsilon r$. Left translation by a quaternion whose axis is perpendicular to $v_i$ and whose angle is $4w$ moves this tube to a disjoint one. Indeed the distance between the two circle cosets equals the translating quaternion's distance to $H$, by bi-invariance and the subgroup property. On the original tube the reverse triangle inequality now gives $\|L_sg-g\|\ge\sqrt{1-\epsilon-C_0\epsilon}-\sqrt{C_0\epsilon}$. But (2) gives $\|L_sg-g\|\le C(1+4/\sqrt r)\sqrt{\epsilon r}\le5C\sqrt\epsilon$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ sufficiently small to contradict these bounds. Thus $e\ge\epsilon r$ on these fibres. For $\beta r<400$ we have $e\ge0\ge\epsilon r-400\epsilon/\beta$. Integrate the homogeneous version of the fibre bound, apply it for each $i$, and average the three inequalities to obtain (3).

## The energy spacing of the one-vertex neutral transfer

**PROVED.** Put $h=\beta^{-1/3}$. List the eigenvalues $\lambda_{k,+}$ of (1) in decreasing order on the neutral conjugation-invariant space, with $k=0$ for the vacuum. On $L^2(\mathbb R^9)$ consider the Friedrichs operator
\[
 {\cal L}=-\tfrac12\sum_i\Delta_{x_i}
                  +2\sum_{i<j}|x_i\times x_j|^2,\qquad x_i\in\mathbb R^3.
                                                                        \tag{4}
\]
Write $\varepsilon_k$ for its eigenvalues, in increasing order with multiplicity, on the subspace invariant under simultaneous proper rotations. Then
\[
 \frac{1-\lambda_{k,+}}h\longrightarrow\varepsilon_k,\qquad
 \frac1h\log\frac{\lambda_{0,+}}{\lambda_{1,+}}
                  \longrightarrow\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_0>0.        \tag{5}
\]
The coefficient in (4) uses the round unit-quaternion metric in (1). No numerical eigenvalue of (4) enters (5).

**PROVED (compactness and simplicity for (4)).** At fixed $x_i$ take the two coordinates of $x_j$ perpendicular to $x_i$, $j\ne i$. In either coordinate $y$, completing the square $\|(\partial_y+2|x_i|y)u\|^2\ge0$ gives $-\partial_y^2/2+2|x_i|^2y^2\ge |x_i|$ as a form. Thus $\mathcal L\ge2|x_i|$ for each $i$. A bounded set in the form norm has uniformly small $L^2$ tails and bounded first derivatives. Local Rellich compactness then gives compactness in $L^2$. One proof of this local step is to cut off inside a larger cube, use Fourier series there, and bound the squared norm outside frequency $B$ by $B^{-2}$ times the gradient norm squared. The closed form has core the smooth compactly supported functions, by cutoff and convolution on compacts. Rotation averaging gives a core in the invariant space. This space has infinite dimension, for example by disjoint radial tests.

**PROVED.** The unrestricted lowest eigenvalue of (4) is simple. The absolute value of a real ground function has no larger energy and also minimizes. Every minimizer solves the elliptic eigen-equation and is smooth by local regularity of the Laplacian with smooth potential. A nonnegative solution vanishing at a point vanishes in a neighbourhood. In dimension $d=9$ its spherical mean $b(r)$ about that point has $b(0)=b'(0)=0$ and $(r^{d-1}b')'\le C r^{d-1}b$ on a fixed small ball, by the eigen-equation and boundedness of the potential there. Double integration gives $\sup_{[0,r]}b\le Cr^2\sup_{[0,r]}b/(2d)$, hence the asserted vanishing. Connectedness rules this out for a nonzero minimizer. Two linearly independent real minimizers would give a nonzero linear combination vanishing at a chosen point, whose absolute value would contradict the same argument. The unique normalized positive ground function is rotation invariant. Compactness now gives the strict inequality in (5).

**PROVED (compactness of the low Wilson states after rescaling).** Represent each link modulo sign by $\exp(hx_i)$ with $h|x_i|\le\pi/2$, ignoring its Haar-null equator. Let $J_h(x)\,dx$ be the product quotient Haar probability in these coordinates. Its density is a constant times $h^9\prod_i(\sin(h|x_i|)/(h|x_i|))^2$. Pull a neutral function to these coordinates, multiply by $J_h^{1/2}$, and extend by zero. This is an isometry $U_h$ into $L^2(\mathbb R^9)$. Suppose $\|f_h\|=1$ and $\langle f_h,(1-T)f_h\rangle\le Ah$ for fixed $A$. By (3) and $\sin u\ge2u/\pi$ on this hemisphere, the squared norm of $U_h f_h$ outside $\max_i|x_i|\le R$ is at most $C(A+1)/R$. Put $g_h=M f_h$. The identities preceding (3) give $\|g_h-f_h\|^2\le Ah$ and $D_j(g_h)\le Ah$ for each $j$.

**PROVED.** Smooth $g_h$ by left convolution in each coordinate with a nonnegative smooth probability bump supported in $d(1,z)\le h\delta$, $0<\delta<1$. A rescaled radial bump in exponential coordinates has gradient $L^1$ norm at most $C/(h\delta)$. Inequality (2) bounds the $L^2$ error of all three smoothings by $C\sqrt A(\delta+\sqrt h)$. The smoothed functions, mapped by $U_h$, have first derivatives bounded by $C_R(1+\delta^{-1})$ in every fixed rescaled cube. Indeed differentiation of the group convolution differentiates the bump with the indicated $L^1$ cost by invariance of Haar measure, and the rescaled chart contributes $O_R(h)$ per derivative. The logarithmic derivatives of the Haar density are bounded there. Local compactness follows by first holding $\delta$ fixed, then letting it decrease. The tail bound gives strong $L^2$ precompactness of $U_h f_h$ as $h\downarrow0$.

**PROVED (the limiting generator on test functions).** For every smooth compactly supported invariant $\phi$, set $\phi_h=U_h^{-1}\phi$ for sufficiently small $h$. Then
\[
 U_h h^{-1}(1-T)\phi_h\longrightarrow {\cal L}\phi\quad\hbox{in }L^2.
                                                                        \tag{6}
\]
On a fixed rescaled cube, $\beta V=h\,2\sum|x_i\times x_j|^2+O_R(h^3)$ with the same expansion through any fixed number of derivatives. For a convolution increment write $z=\exp(h^{3/2}Z)$. In these coordinates its probability has all moments bounded uniformly, is symmetric, and its covariance tends to the identity. Indeed the density after scaling tends to the three-dimensional Gaussian density with exponent $-|Z|^2/2$ and is bounded by $C e^{-2|Z|^2/\pi^2}$, using the radial Haar factor and a lower cap bound for the normalization. For bounded $x$, the logarithm divided by $h$ of $z^{-1}\exp(hx)$ is $x-\sqrt h Z+O_R(h^{3/2}|Z|+h^2|Z|^2)$ when $h^{3/2}|Z|$ is small. This follows by differentiating the smooth local product in the increment, whose linear coefficient differs from the identity by $O_R(h)$, and Taylor bounding its second derivative. The complement has exponentially small probability. The product increment therefore contributes $h\Delta\phi/2+o_R(h)$ by Taylor's formula and the third moment bound. The Haar factors in the conjugated convolution have ratio $1+O_R(h^2)$ on compact rescaled cubes. The two half magnetic factors together contribute minus $h$ times the potential in (4). Outside a fixed enlargement of the support a contributing increment has length at least $c h$ in some link. The Gaussian tail bounds the row and column integrals of this part of the kernel by $C e^{-c'/h}$, so its $L^2$ norm divided by $h$ tends to zero. These estimates prove (6).

**PROVED (min–max and the vacuum subtraction).** The form core and (6) give by trial spaces $\limsup_h(1-\lambda_{k,+})/h\le\varepsilon_k$. Conversely take the first $k+1$ orthonormal eigenfunctions on a subsequence realizing the lower limit. Their rescaled energies are bounded. The compactness above gives $k+1$ orthonormal strong limits after a subsequence, and their eigenvalues in the form $(1-\lambda)/h$ have limits. Testing their eigen-equations against $\phi_h$ and using (6) gives the corresponding distributional eigen-equations for (4). Invariant tests suffice by rotation averaging. Each limiting solution belongs to the Friedrichs operator domain. Indeed local elliptic regularity gives $H^2_{\rm loc}$, and testing with $\chi_R^2 f$, where $\chi_R=1$ on radius $R$, supported inside $2R$, $|\nabla\chi_R|\le C/R$, bounds the energy of $\chi_R f$ by the eigenvalue times $\|\chi_R f\|^2+C R^{-2}\|f\|^2$. Letting $R$ increase gives finite form energy and the weak form equation on its core. Thus min–max for (4) gives the reverse inequality. Finally $\log(1-hy)=-hy+O(h^2)$ for bounded $y$. This proves the gap formula in (5).

## Counting the neutral states and restoring the bare spatial volume factor

**PROVED.** The convergence in (5) extends to a vacuum-normalized heat trace. For fixed $t>0$ and any integers $m_h$ with $h m_h\to t$,
\[
 \lim_{h\downarrow0}\operatorname{Tr}_{+}\!\left[(T/\lambda_{0,+})^{m_h}\right]
          =\sum_{k\ge0}e^{-t(\varepsilon_k-\varepsilon_0)}<\infty.       \tag{7}
\]
Let $N_h(A)$ count eigenvalues with $1-\lambda\le hA$. There are numerical $b,C>0$ for which
\[
 N_h(A)\le C(1+A)^{27/2}\qquad(hA\le b,\ h\text{ sufficiently small}). \tag{8}
\]
Every unit vector in that spectral subspace satisfies the compactness estimates above, with this $A$. Choose $R=C_1(1+A)$ sufficiently large and $\delta=c_1(1+A)^{-1/2}$ sufficiently small. The map which multiplies by $M$, smooths at radius $h\delta$ in the three groups and then restricts to $\max_i |x_i|\le R$ approximates the identity on the subspace with norm error at most $1/2$. Indeed the three errors are bounded by $\sqrt{Ah}$, $C\sqrt A(\sqrt h+\delta)$ and $\sqrt{C(A+1)/R}$, using restriction as a contraction. Choose $b$ small after fixing the absolute constants. The output set has Haar volume at most $C(hR)^9$ over all eight sign charts. With squared $L^2$ norm at most $C(h\delta)^{-9}$ for the product smoothing kernel, the map has squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm at most $C(hR)^9(h\delta)^{-9}$. Multiplication by $M$ has norm at most one. Summing the squared image norms over an orthonormal basis gives (8).

**PROVED.** For $hA\le b$, an eigenvalue $\lambda=1-hA$ contributes at most $e^{-t A/2}$ to the unnormalized trace for small $h$. Grouping the energies in intervals of unit length and using (8) gives a uniformly vanishing large-$A$ tail below $b/h$. For the rest of the spectrum use $\operatorname{Tr}T\le C\beta^{9/2}=C h^{-27/2}$, from the diagonal of the continuous positive kernel in (1) or the trace of $C_1C_2C_3$. Its contribution to $\operatorname{Tr}T^{m_h}$ is at most $(1-b)^{m_h-1}\operatorname{Tr}T\to0$. Fixed eigenvalues converge by (5), and $\lambda_{0,+}^{-m_h}\to e^{t\varepsilon_0}$. This proves (7). It also implies a unique $t_*>0$ for which the excited sum on the right is $1/2$. That sum is continuous and strictly decreasing, tends to zero at infinity by summability at any fixed positive time, and tends to infinity at zero by the infinitely many invariant eigenstates. The first integer time giving an excited sum at most $1/2$ in (1) therefore satisfies $h m_*(\beta)\to t_*$, by testing on both sides of $t_*$.

**PROVED (the homogeneous restriction misses the physical thermal time).** On a periodic cube of $n^3$ sites impose the same directional spatial link $q_i(t)$ at every site on slice $t$ and take temporal gauge, with the common temporal holonomy integrated for the singlet constraint if time is periodic. Each of the magnetic commutators and each of the three electric plaquette terms occurs exactly $n^3$ times per slab. Defining the restricted model by product Haar on its three remaining links gives exactly (1) with coupling $B=\beta n^3$. Impose in this model the three sign symmetries of (1). At any $\beta\to\infty$, even with $n\to\infty$, its neutral thermal time is asymptotic to $t_* n\beta^{1/3}$. Its excited trace at every $1\le m\le n/K$ diverges for fixed $K>0$ because for each fixed number of excited eigenvalues (5) gives $m\log(\lambda_0/\lambda_k)\to0$, and the number retained is arbitrary. This last statement also holds on the full conjugation-invariant space by inclusion and positivity of the common vacuum. On an even $n$ cube the bare per-link sign flips of this homogeneous model do not implement the three seam flips of the full Wilson space. Neither the product Haar prescription nor this choice of symmetry identifies its spectrum with the physical Wilson spectrum. The positive eigenvalue difference in (5) alone gives the wrong thermal time for the proposed use of this restriction.

 d_\beta=\beta^{-1/4}(1+\log\beta),\qquad

 |\lambda_0-\Lambda_n|\le C_n d_\beta,\qquad
 F\le(\Lambda_n+C_n d_\beta)I-c_nR.                                    \tag{9}
\]
The last inequality is a quadratic-form bound, with $R$ acting by multiplication.

## A bound on the part of the physical trace seen by entire arrays of paths

**PROVED (trace of an observation).** Conjugate $U$ by $\psi$ into $L^2(\pi)$, where $\pi=\psi^2dX$. It is now a stationary self-adjoint Markov operator with constants representing the vacuum. One can first work without gauge projection. For a gauge- and centre-invariant observation $Y$ of a slice, let $P_Y$ be conditional expectation onto all square-integrable functions of $Y$. Its range lies in the physical neutral space. Define
\[
      {\cal T}_Y(u)=\operatorname{Tr}(P_Y U^u P_Y),\qquad u\ge1 .
\]
This trace includes the vacuum. For an increasing sequence of finite measurable partitions generating the range of $Y$, it is the limit of
\[
     \sum_{B:\ \pi_Y(B)>0}
             \frac{\mathbb P(Y_0\in B,\ Y_u\in B)}{\pi_Y(B)} .           \tag{19}
\]
The normalized indicators are an orthonormal basis for a partition subspace, the stationary endpoint law computes their diagonal matrix elements, and the partition projections increase strongly to $P_Y$. In an eigenbasis of the positive trace-class operator $U^u$, monotone convergence of the squared projected norms proves the trace limit. All observation spaces used below are standard Borel and admit countable generating partitions. Formula (19) needs no pointwise value of a continuum density on its diagonal.

**PROVED.** Suppose an additional array $Y$ has, given an exterior that includes $Z$ on its own slice and every other time slice, conditional law between $l\,dH$ and $v\,dH$, with $0<l\le1\le v$ and a fixed probability $H$. Here $Z,Y$ are both gauge and centre invariant. The bounds must hold also at the other marked time with the analogous exterior. Then
\[
       0\le {\cal T}_{Z,Y}(u)-{\cal T}_Z(u)
                        \le (v^2/l-1){\cal T}_Z(u)\qquad(u\ge1).        \tag{20}
\]
In the one-slice denominator of (19) on a product bin $A\times B$ the lower bound is $l\,\pi_Z(A)H(B)$. In the two-slice numerator condition at one marked time and then at the other. The upper bound is $v^2 H(B)^2\mathbb P(Z_0\in A,Z_u\in A)$. The other slice, including its measured variables, is in the conditioning exterior of the first one even when $u=1$. Summing over $B$ gives a factor $\sum_B H(B)=1$. Increasing both partitions proves the upper bound. Subspace inclusion and positivity give the lower one. In particular for constant $Z$, the *sum* of the excited thermal weights seen by $Y$ is bounded by $v^2/l-1$, irrespective of the dimension of $L^2(\pi_Y)$. This use of a trace strengthens a bound for one reading or one eigenfunction.

**PROVED.** More explicitly let $\phi_k$ be any orthonormal physical neutral transfer eigenbasis in the vacuum Markov representation, $k=0$ the constant. Then
\[
 {\cal T}_Y(u)-1=\sum_{k\ge1} e^{-uE_{k,+}}\,\|P_Y\phi_k\|_\pi^2.        \tag{21}
\]
The summands are nonnegative. This is the part of the actual thermal sum carried by the observation subspace. In particular no estimate on the number of unseen states is implicit in (20).

**PROVED (a physical-scale application).** Consider the entire array of the real traces of all straight spatial winding lines in the three directions, with the signs quotiented by the three seam flips (or take any gauge- and centre-invariant function of the matrix array). For large $n$ divisible by four, conditional on an exterior and both time neighbours, its law is bounded above and below by the corresponding pushforward of product Haar, with $v=(1+\eta)^M$, $l=(1-\eta)^M$, $M=3n^2$ and $\eta\le C\exp[-c n/(1+\beta)]$ when that last exponent is large. \[
                {\cal T}_Y(u)-1\le C n^2 e^{-c n/(1+\beta)}
                        \qquad\text{for every integer }u\ge1.           \tag{22}
\]
Row 1161 gave the density and overlap estimates. Here (19) sums over the entire observation Hilbert space without a multiplicity factor for its functions. ## Trying to cover the neutral spectrum by the path estimate

**GUIDE.** A possible way to use (22) would be to measure sufficiently many arrays with small trace contributions until they determine the slice. The relevant reconstruction is already in row 1171. Its bounded-length fundamental trace words, at most 18 links in each word and a fixed number per site, determine almost every spatial field up to the vertex gauges and the flat centre flips. Its local frame uses two based plaquettes with noncollinear imaginary parts. I use that reconstruction and its proof of generic nondegeneracy. [Local orbit reconstruction](gap-solve14-astra62.md)

**PROVED (joint determination with small individual traces).** Fix $A>0$ and $\beta\le B\log n$ with $B$ fixed and $4\mid n$. On the *original* Wilson cube there is a list of $O_{A,B}((\log n)^2)$ gauge- and centre-invariant arrays $Y^{(j)}$ such that
\[
 \sum_j[{\cal T}_{Y^{(j)}}(u)-1]\le n^{-A}\quad(u\ge1),\qquad
 {\cal T}_{(Y^{(1)},\ldots,Y^{(J)})}(u)
                 =\operatorname{Tr}_{+}U^u.                            \tag{23}
\]
Each reading uses a thin rectangular loop of length $O_{A,B}((\log n)^2)$ and bounded-length words. The statement concerns joint measurement, which permits products and all other measurable functions of the arrays. The following construction specifies their common colour frames.

**PROVED.** At each site $x$, orthonormalize the two local imaginary plaquette vectors of row 1171 and take their oriented cross product to complete a proper frame. Conjugation rotates all three columns together. In this frame a based quaternion is an invariant measured matrix, retaining its real part and the three components of its imaginary part. Choose any convention on the null set of degenerate frames. All contractible based words and these frames respect seam flips. Let $W_{x,j}$ be the finite list of short based words whose real traces suffice in row 1171, assigning the words on an edge to its starting site. Let $D$ be a fixed bound on the radius of these words and frames.

**PROVED.** Choose an integer $b=\lceil C_A(1+6\beta)\log n\rceil$, large compared with $D$ and below $n/4$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Colour the first-coordinate circle in at most $4b+4D+1$ colours so points of any one colour have circular distance greater than $2b+2D$. Greedy colouring works since at most $4b+4D$ vertices are within this distance from any vertex other than itself. Split each colour also by the parity of $x_2$. In one resulting class of starting sites let $\gamma_x$ go $b$ steps in direction 1, one step in direction 2, and back along the parallel line and the last transverse side. Write $G_x$ for its based holonomy. The two kinds of arrays for this class consist of the framed $G_x$ and, separately for each word type $j$, the framed $W_{x,j}G_x$.

**PROVED.** Mark private links on the long forward sides, staying more than $D+1$ axial steps from both ends, and using only the parity $x_1=x_2+x_3\bmod 2$ for link bases. No two selected links share a plaquette. The backward sides have the opposite transverse parity, the short words and frames lie outside the marked axial intervals, and distinct starts on the same line have disjoint intervals with buffers. There are at least $b/2-2D-4$ private links per word. Given the remaining links in the slice and all other times, every framed product in one array is therefore the product of independent one-link draws with fixed factors, followed by a fixed conjugation. The signs or factors from periodic seams preserve the Haar reference. The Fourier product bound of row 1145, (2), gives a one-word density between $1-\eta$ and $1+\eta$ with $\eta\le C\exp[-(b/2-2D-4)/(1+6\beta)]$. Conditional independence gives the corresponding $M$th powers for the array, $M\le n^3$. By (20) with $Z$ constant, its excess trace is $O(M\eta)$ whenever $M\eta$ is small. There are at most a fixed multiple of $b$ arrays. Taking $C_A$ large proves the first part of (23). In the second part, the two arrays for a class and a word type, read in the *same* frame at a site, give $W_{x,j}$ in that frame by right multiplication by the inverse of the framed $G_x$. They give every required real trace. The vacuum slice law is equivalent to Haar at finite $n,\beta$ by the positive vacuum density. The orbit reconstruction of row 1171 therefore identifies their joint $L^2$ space with the full neutral space. This proves the trace equality.

**PROVED (the paired readings).** Within one class put $Y=(G_x)_{\rm framed}$, $Y'=(W_{x,j}G_x)_{\rm framed}$ and $Z=(W_{x,j})_{\rm framed}$. The joint observation $(Y,Y')$ generates precisely $(Y,Z)$. The private-link exterior contains $Z$. Consequently (20) gives
\[
 {\cal T}_Z(u)\ \le\ {\cal T}_{Y,Y'}(u)\
                 \le (1+O(M\eta))\,{\cal T}_Z(u).                      \tag{24}
\]
Thus adding back one family of local plaquettes in these pairs can carry the complete thermal contribution of the plaquette family, even when (22) is arbitrarily small for each separate family. The comparison holds at the physical thermal time and in the original measure.

**PROVED (why iterating the Haar comparison stops).** For $W_{x,j}$ a fundamental plaquette, suppose the conditional law of one framed $Y'_x$ given $Y_x$ is between $l\,dH$ and $v\,dH$ in the vacuum slice. The marked-plaquette exponential moment of row 1155, (9), gives $\mathbb E_\pi(1-\operatorname{Re}W_{x,j})\le C/\beta$. Its one-mark proof on a periodic rectangle takes a plaquette direction as transfer time, halves the marked electric coupling, bounds the one-link normalizer ratio, and uses Schatten Hölder over layers. The bound is independent of the fourth period, so passage to the vacuum at fixed spatial cube uses positivity and simplicity of the transfer top. Since $1-\operatorname{Re}(Y'_xY_x^{-1})$ has Haar mean one when $Y'_x$ is integrated against Haar, the claimed lower comparison requires $l\le C/\beta$. Also $\mathbb P(1-\operatorname{Re}(Y'_xY_x^{-1})\le2C/\beta)\ge1/2$ by Markov's inequality. The corresponding Haar cap about $Y_x$ has measure $O(\beta^{-3/2})$ by the $\sin^2 r$ radial density. Thus $v\ge c\beta^{3/2}$. These restrictions apply even before conditioning on a distant time slice. They show the failure in the actual Wilson vacuum of the near-one conditional constants required to apply (20) across the pair. [Marked plaquette estimate](gap-solve7-astra62.md)

**PROVED (a counting obstruction for this sampling method).** Consider an iteration that records group-valued smooth path products and proves conditional uniformity for each new product by reserving at least two new links that occur in it, occur in no previous reading, and are private to that product at that step. With $3n^3$ spatial links it can record at most $3n^3/2$ such products. A quaternion product has at most three real dimensions. An almost-sure reconstruction of the neutral quotient by smooth invariant readings needs at least $6n^3$ real dimensions because the space of links has dimension $9n^3$ and every gauge orbit has dimension at most $3n^3$, with finitely many centre translates. Indeed, on an open set of maximal differential rank the constant rank theorem gives smooth product coordinates on the fibres. Haar and vacuum have positive densities there. By Fubini, a full-measure subset of almost every fibre in that chart still has full induced measure and cannot be contained in an orbit of smaller dimension. The same argument applies when the functions are smooth off the null set of local frame degeneracies. Since $3(3n^3/2)<6n^3$, even reserving two fresh links for every product cannot give simultaneous orbit determination in this scheme. The paired construction avoids that limit precisely by reading the same long links twice. This dimension argument concerns exact reconstruction by these smooth observations. A smaller subspace could still capture the surviving low-energy states.

## A direct test on unpadded local Wilson observables

**GUIDE.** For the continuum route one can take the spatial volume to infinity first. Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text, give an inverse-square estimate after subtracting the invariant plateau, under positivity for all sixteen families of lattice mirrors. Their theorem explicitly specifies an infinite-lattice law. I use their (PL1)–(PL3) as a named input in this section. [Outline index, chunk 03](sa-index/chunk-03.md) · [Outline, lines 3205–3254](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex#L3205-L3254)

**PROVED GIVEN (PL3).** Let $\nu$ be an infinite-volume Wilson Gibbs state at coupling $\beta\ge0$, invariant under lattice translations and coordinate reflections and satisfying the mirror hypotheses just specified. On time slice zero measure an arbitrary Borel array $Z$ of $p$ links, or any common observation of those links, supported strictly inside $Q_r=[-r,r]^4$, $r\ge1$ an integer. On $L^2(\nu_Z)$ let $C_t$ have form $\nu(\overline{f(Z_0)}g(Z_t))$, and $C_\infty$ have form $\nu(\overline{\mathbb E[f(Z_0)\mid\mathcal I]}\mathbb E[g(Z_0)\mid\mathcal I])$, where $\mathcal I$ is the full translation-invariant sigma-field. There is an absolute $C$ such that for integer $t>2r$
\[
 0\le\operatorname{Tr}(C_t-C_\infty)
 \ \le\ C\left[\left(\frac r{t-2r}\right)^2+
                \left(\frac r{t-2r}\right)^3\right]
                   [C(1+\beta)^{3/2}]^p .                              \tag{25}
\]
It is the trace on *all* square-integrable functions of the selected links. The bound allows mixtures. In an ergodic state $C_\infty$ is the constant projection.

**PROVED.** A single link conditional on all other links has a tilted Haar density $e^{h\cdot q}/I(h)$ with $|h|\le6\beta$. Its supremum is at most $C(1+\beta)^{3/2}$ because the cap of radius $(1+|h|)^{-1/2}$ at the maximizing quaternion has Haar volume at least $c(1+|h|)^{-3/2}$ and the exponent there is at least $|h|-1$. Conditioning instead on any subset of the other links preserves the bound by averaging. Revealing the $p$ links successively thus bounds their joint conditional density by $K=[C(1+\beta)^{3/2}]^p$ against product Haar, uniformly in any exterior of those links. Denote the pushforward of product Haar by $H_Z$. For a bin $B$ of the observation, the two copies in each of the reflected size expressions (PL1) have disjoint supports, because the original support is strictly inside $Q_r$. By reflection invariance and the conditional density bound, every such expression for $\mathbf1_B$ is at most $\nu_Z(B) K H_Z(B)$. Haar invariance accounts also for edge orientations under the coordinate reflections. The maximum over the reflected copies satisfies the same bound.

**PROVED.** The difference $C_t-C_\infty$ is positive. For a bounded time-zero reading $f$, write $c_s=\nu(\overline{f(Z_0)}f(Z_s))$. Reflection positivity in the time mirror at $-s/2$, applied to $f$ at times zero and one, gives the positive two-by-two Hankel matrix with entries $c_s,c_{s+1},c_{s+2}$. Here reflection fixes the spatial array apart from translating it to its reflected time. In particular $c_s\ge0$, $c_{s+1}^2\le c_s c_{s+2}$, and $c_s\le\|f\|_2^2$. Any strict increase would iterate to unbounded geometric growth (and $c_s=0$ implies $c_{s+1}=0$). Thus $c_t$ is at least its limit, which (PL3) identifies with the invariant plateau. Bounded readings are dense and the forms are bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz, giving positivity on the whole space.

**PROVED.** Apply (PL3) to the same bin indicator at the two times. Divide the bound by $\nu_Z(B)>0$ and sum over any finite partition. The reflected-size estimate leaves $\sum_B K H_Z(B)\le K$. The left side is the partition compression trace of the positive difference. Increasing the partitions as in (19) gives (25), including trace class, by exhaustion of the Hilbert space. This does not require regularity of the Borel measurements or bounds on their individual numbers of possible values.

**PROVED GIVEN the corresponding time-translation reconstruction.** If $J$ embeds these slice readings isometrically in the time-zero Hilbert space and $C_t=J^*e^{-tH_{\rm lat}}J$ with $H_{\rm lat}\ge0$, then $C_\infty=J^*\mathbf1_{\{0\}}(H_{\rm lat})J$ by the spectral theorem and the preceding limit. Hence for every $E>0$,
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(J^*\mathbf1_{(0,aE]}(H_{\rm lat})J\right)
                 \le e^{taE}\operatorname{Tr}(C_t-C_\infty).           \tag{26}
\]
At spacing $a\downarrow0$, $\beta=O(\log(1/a))$, take $t=\lfloor1/(aE)\rfloor$. For fixed $r,p$ the right side is $O_E(a^2r^2[C(1+\beta)^{3/2}]^p)\to0$. The same conclusion holds for increasing $p,r$ with $ar\to0$ and $a^2 r^2[C(1+\beta)^{3/2}]^p\to0$. Thus even the sum over all nonlinear measurements of an unpadded microscopic patch can lose its entire positive bounded-energy weight in the physical limit. For equal-time microscopic readings this is a normalized Hilbert-space statement. Physical smearing over macroscopically many sites and the needed continuum renormalizations still require estimates.

**OPEN.** On a fixed physical patch with an increasing number of links, (25) contains a factor exponential in that number. Its decay in the separation is polynomial. At fixed cutoff, positive spectral measures with density proportional to $E e^{-E}$ at the origin satisfy just such an inverse-square time bound and have positive weight in every interval above zero, as Sussman and Alpoge exhibit. Neither summing the microscopic estimates nor applying (PL3) to a periodically repeated finite torus proves the required uniform gap.

## Remaining physical estimate

**PROVED.** For one fixed physical side $L$ take $n_j=L/a_j$ and $u_j=\lfloor n_j/K\rfloor$ on the allowed sequence. The excited trace is decreasing in $u$, so this is the most favourable allowed time. Let $P_{Y_j}$ be the physical observation projection of (22), and let $U_{+,j}$ denote the neutral transfer in the vacuum Markov representation. There is an exact identity
\[
 s_+(u_j;n_j,\beta_j)
   ={\cal T}_{Y_j}(u_j)-1+
        \big\|(I-P_{Y_j})U_{+,j}^{\,u_j/2}\big\|_{\rm HS}^2.           \tag{27}
\]
Split the trace over the observation space and its orthogonal complement. The vacuum belongs to the first space. Positivity gives the Hilbert–Schmidt expression for the second trace, also when $u_j$ is odd. The first term tends to zero on the logarithmic coupling sequence.
