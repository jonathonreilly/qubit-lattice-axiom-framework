## 1. A single electric layer and its positive two-replica formula

\[
 \rho=C^{1/2}BC^{1/2}/Z,\qquad

\]

**PROVED.** Write \(\ell=(d_e)_e\) for an assignment of irreducible dimensions on the seam and \(Q_\ell\) for its physical isotypic projection (all multiplicities and intertwiners retained). \[
 \tau_\ell=\frac{B^{1/2}Q_\ell B^{1/2}}{\operatorname{Tr}(BQ_\ell)} .
\]
Terms of zero mass may be omitted. Normalized one-link convolution is scalar \(a_\beta(d)\) on dimension \(d\), so that \(p_\ell=c_\ell\operatorname{Tr}(BQ_\ell)/Z\). For two **independent** assignments with these probabilities there is an exact nonnegative expression

\]

## 12. Summing the crossing components before estimating an internal box

**PROVED (common coupling).** A second summation extends the interval on the literal path of (30) to

                \sup_{|u|\le u_{\rm int}}|J_D(u)|=o(1).                  \tag{30a}

**PROVED (a bookkeeping estimate for connected sums).** Suppose factors \(1+f_i\) on independent reference variables read connected sets \(S_i\) of sites. Extra sites may be included in these sets. Put \(w_i=\sup|f_i|\). For fixed \(a>0\), if
\[
     \sup_x\sum_{i:x\in S_i} w_i e^{(a+4)|S_i|}\le \delta_0
\]
with \(\delta_0\) sufficiently small, the log expectation is a sum over connected lists, with the absolute sum through any site bounded by \(C\delta_0\) even after multiplying each term by \(e^{a\sum|S_i|}\), counting occurrences. To see this, expand the product and group each selected set of distinct labels into overlap-connected components. Their activities are bounded by products of \(w_i\). Sum the connected sets through a designated first label using spanning trees rooted at it. With weights \(w_j e^{(a+2)|S_j|}\), the total over possible children intersecting \(S_i\), including the factor \(e^{|S_j|}\), is at most \(\delta_0|S_i|\). The recursion that sums unordered children by their exponential therefore bounds the trees above the root by \(e^{|S_i|}\). Every rooted connected set is counted, by at least one tree, with its nonroot label permutations cancelling their factorial. Summing over the possible first labels through \(x\) bounds the resulting polymer mass by \(\delta_0\). Two polymers are incompatible only when their unions overlap. The same bound per site now pays the incompatibility sum for a polymer by \(\delta_0\sum_{i\ {\rm in\ it}}|S_i|\). The hard-core tree inequality and the rooted log-series estimate of Kotecký–Preiss as written by Sussman and Alpoge (outline 1225–1269, 1273–1347) give the asserted log bound, using \(a_X=\sum_{i\in X}|S_i|\) and the remaining exponential reserve. Counting a list through a site by rooting at a polymer containing the site only overcounts. This proof also applies with complex factors, a fixed uniform majorant on a disk, and conditioned external parameters. In particular it gives a summable **termwise** majorant on that disk. Bridge paths conditioned on endpoints can equivalently be sampled from an independent random seed per component and block together with its two endpoint variables. Disjoint such variable sets factor under the reference.

**PROVED (first integration).** Prescribe both ends of the original short slab and every path of a large internal open box. As usual its extra half-layer endpoint multipliers are separable. Integrate all the *small* crossing components. Divide the \(h-1\) steps into fine blocks of lengths \(r\) to \(2r\), \(r=\lceil C(1+\beta)\rceil\), grouped in coarse time blocks of exactly \(k=\lceil A\log(b+2)\rceil\) fine blocks. After extracting their vacuum powers and endpoint functions, the factors are \(D_{\ell,c}\) for each small component and \(\exp(zY_p)\) for each ordinary face and fine time block. Here \(|Y_p|\le2r\). The large transports are just prescribed functions of time. The label graph has bounded degree because each face reads two small components, each such component has at most twelve edges, and only adjacent fine times can share endpoint variables. One can take a fine spacetime site per label and let its support include its closed dependency neighborhood, joining neighboring labels spatially or in consecutive times. This enlarges each support by a bounded constant. For any fixed desired exponential reserve \(A_0\), choose \(C\) so that the small-component \(D_{\ell,c}-1\) are sufficiently small by §11, then \(z_0=c_0/r\) so that the same holds for the magnetic factors on \(|z|\le z_0\). The bookkeeping estimate gives connected logarithm terms \(L_\gamma(z)\) with majorants \(a_\gamma\) satisfying
\[
 \sum_{\gamma:\,i\ {\rm occurs}}a_\gamma e^{A_0 s_\gamma}\le C_{A_0},
 \qquad s_\gamma=\text{number of fine labels with repetitions}.           \tag{31}
\]
The constants are uniform in all prescribed paths, endpoints and volumes.

**PROVED (the interior zero).** A term with a magnetic label vanishes at zero. If it reads no fixed small-component endpoint then its first derivative also vanishes. A linear coefficient would have just one magnetic insertion and otherwise electric factors. In its reference expectation apply to the relevant small component the *same* vertex-center gauge at every endpoint and along every bridge it reads. Its stationary endpoint measures, conditional path laws under the transformed endpoints, and electric insertions are invariant, while the single face trace changes sign because its other crossing link is in a different small component. Repeated or multiple magnetic occurrences already give at least two powers of \(z\). This reasoning applies within the polymer activity in a connected list, before any cancellation with a different list. The maximum principle on \(|z|\le z_0\) therefore gives \(|L_\gamma(u)|\le a_\gamma (u/z_0)^2\) for every such interior magnetic term. Terms without a magnetic label are independent of all large paths.

**PROVED (boundary terms).** Truncate (31) at \(s_\gamma\le R=\lceil C_2\log n\rceil\). The error in the small-component log is \(o(n^{-5})\) uniformly, by summing over at most \(C n^3h\) fine labels and choosing \(C_2\) large. Real weights give real log terms, and this log error is a multiplicative \(e^{\pm o(n^{-5})}\) error for the remaining positive path integral. Reserve guards of \(R+3\) **coarse time blocks** at each end, leaving a central interval of \(m=\lfloor(h-1)/(rk)\rfloor-2(R+3)\) blocks. Every truncated term reading a fixed small endpoint lies entirely in its corresponding guard since a connected fine list can move by at most a constant number of fine blocks per occurrence, and \(k\to\infty\). These boundary terms may be arbitrarily large. Exponentiate their exact real sums in the two guard weights. Terms without large-path dependence are constants or single-end factors, since no retained list reaches both fixed ends. Other terms confined to one guard can likewise be kept in that guard. All remaining terms have the interior quadratic bound of the preceding paragraph.

**PROVED (second integration).** Condition on the two large-box guard histories separately. In the middle use independent intermediate large-box endpoints and their normalized bridges, now on the coarse time blocks, extracting their vacuum powers and their endpoint functions. The coarse electric factors are within an arbitrarily small fixed tolerance of one by (4), the charge estimate (15) of row 1147 and the choice \(kr\ge A C(1+\beta)\log(b+2)\). Each remaining fine log term gives a factor \(e^{L_\gamma}-1\) in the product expansion, of modulus at most \(C a_\gamma (u/z_0)^2\). Its coarse support can be padded to a connected set of size at most \(C_3 s_\gamma\) by assigning each fine label its old cell and coarse time and including a bounded neighborhood of that site, covering both internal boxes it may read and the block endpoint seeds. Fine neighbors have bounded coarse distance. These supports may extend into a guard, where they designate fixed parameters. They are counted in the support for detecting dependence on either end. For each coarse site there are at most \(C(b+1)^2 k\) possible fine labels in its bounded neighborhood because only crossing components and face terms enter the fine expansion. Thus (31), with \(A_0\) chosen larger than \(C_3(a+4)\), gives the coarse weighted sum through a site
\[
   \sum_{\gamma:\,x\in S_\gamma}e^{(a+4)|S_\gamma|}
             \|e^{L_\gamma}-1\|_\infty
            \le C (b+1)^2 k\,(ur/c_0)^2 .                                \tag{32}
\]
The support need not describe statistically independent faces inside a large box. That entire box in the time block counts as one site. Terms solely in a guard have already been removed. The coarse electric labels add an arbitrarily small constant to this sum. Choose \(c\) in (30a) to apply the bookkeeping estimate again.

**PROVED.** Conditional on the two guard histories, the coarse log integral is a sum of a function of the left history, a function of the right history, and an error at most \(C n^3h\, e^{-c' m}\). Indeed any list depending on both sides has a connected padded support joining them, so the sum of its support sizes is at least \(c'm\). This is paid by the exponential reserve. The fixed large endpoints of the middle can only be read from their adjacent blocks. The error is \(o(1)\), since \(h\asymp n/\log n\) and \(rk\) is polylogarithmic in \(n\). Upon exponentiating, both guard path integrations separate since their only shared large-box steps were the middle interval that has just been integrated. Their own boundary weights, the extracted vacuum endpoint factors and the real small-component boundary logarithms are positive after exponentiation and each depends on its own side only. Uniform multiplicative errors survive these integrations. The full open kernel consequently lies between \(e^{\pm o(1)} f(x)g(y)\) for positive end functions. The one- and two-period integrals against the same nonnegative Wilson rest give \(J_D(u)=o(1)\). This proves (30a) on the original schedule.

**PROVED (cost of the uniform prescription in (32)).** The quadratic scale in (32) is attained as a conditional interaction, even after subtracting a field-independent term. Mark \(m_0\ge c b^2\) interior ordinary plaquettes of a shared face, of a single tangential orientation, with disjoint pairs of isolated free crossing links (use alternate tangential positions and stay away from the rim). Use these sources only in a coarse block of length \(H\asymp rk\), testing the absolute fine-label activities restricted to these marks. Use stationary small crossing walks, an admissible end average obtained by integrating the ends of these walks with their stationary two-time law. Prescribe the internal transports first all equal to one, then with one of the two internal links for each marked plaquette multiplied by \((-1)^i\) at time \(i\). These prescriptions are consistent since the chosen tangential links are distinct. For one marked pair the stationary equal-time Haar norm squared is \(1/4\) and the normalized time covariance at separation \(d\) is \(a_\beta(2)^{2d}/4\), by two independent one-link convolutions. For constant internal transports the variance of the trace sum is at least \(c H\beta\). With alternating internal transports it is at most \(H/4\), because the off-diagonal terms are an alternating sum with decreasing magnitudes. The pairs are independent. For one pair its analytic log moment on \(|z|\le c_0/r\) is bounded in modulus by \(C H/r\). Use the one-pair fine-block expansion. Sample independent stationary endpoints at every block boundary, including the two ends, then insert each \(D_{\ell,c}\) and the magnetic factors. The connected log bound sums over \(O(H/r)\) labels. At zero the partition integral is one. Cauchy’s coefficient bound leaves an error \(C(H/r)(|u|r/c_0)^3\) after the quadratic term for \(|u|r\) sufficiently small. Therefore for real \(0<|u|r\le c_3\) the two conditional log moments on this family differ by at least \(c_4 b^2 k (u\beta)^2\). This rules out uniform small absolute conditional interactions through an entire coarse site once that number is large, even on an independently marked subfamily. **PROVED (larger physical interval).** On the physical cylinder of (30) the logarithmic loss in (30a) can be removed:

**PROVED.** In the short open kernel insert the *physical* vertex average on every time step, as permitted by (GL8), commutation with every magnetic layer, and \(P^2=P\). **PROVED (locality in the temporal gauges).** For each individual fine log term the dependence on cumulative \(k_t\) cancels before any estimate. **PROVED (connected support after removing frames).** Split a given polymer activity into its small-component path variables and consider one small component \(c\) at a time. Within each run its reference is invariant under its own time-independent vertex gauges (with its prescribed outer endpoints transformed as applicable). ## 14. What would decide the remaining inequality

**PROVED (a relative test on the true long cylinder).** The sign restriction of §9 can also be used on *all* \(2N-2\) independent shelling plaquettes at once, retaining a sparse set of negative signs. Set \(q=C\beta^{3/2}e^{-\beta}\) with the constant from (23), and let \(P_g\) keep configurations with at most a fraction \(\sqrt q\) of negatives among these plaquettes. In either the \(T\)- or \(2T\)-period state the probability of the complementary event is at most \(\sqrt q\), by the one-mark bound and Markov's inequality. In the magnetic placement set \(\sigma_g=P_g \sigma P_g/\operatorname{Tr}(P_g\sigma)\). Exactly,
\[
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}\sigma_g^2}{\operatorname{Tr}\sigma^2}
 =\frac{{\mathbb P}_{2T}\{g\text{ at slices }0,T\}}
        {{\mathbb P}_{T}\{g\text{ at slice }0\}^2}=1+O(\sqrt q).           \tag{34}
\]
Sew the two \(F^T\) kernels to get the numerator, and use a union bound at the two endpoints. Thus the test of the *log* thermal ratio changes by \(o(1)\) even if its uncompressed purity tends to zero. The subspace retained in the sign factors has dimension at most \(\sum_{i\le(2N-2)\sqrt q}{2N-2\choose i}=\exp(o(N))\), by the binomial bound. The continuous degrees of freedom remain in each sign sector. The equal-time dephasing example in §9 applies inside this projection as \(P_g\) commutes with \(Y\). Sparse magnetic sign defects alone do not decide its purity.

**PROVED (scope of the obstructions).** For a product transfer the log thermal ratios of its factors add, by factorization of both traces. A scalar or abelian massless spectator can therefore invalidate a seed even if the other factor passes. The explicit scalar example in §10 has a cold modified cylinder and a divergent full restoration. For \(U(1)\) the three-color degree-one exclusion in (10) is absent. A Gaussian open-box oscillator still has deficit of order \(1/B\). Finite subgroups have a discrete identity neighborhood, so (11)'s three-dimensional Haar chart does not apply to them. The positive weak-coupling estimates above are estimates on specified modified cylinders of \(SU(2)\). On witness sizes the actual homogeneous short kernel has a physical excited eigenvalue ratio at least \(e^{-M_{\mathcal K}a_j h}\to1\) by row 865, lines 218–242 and row 1147 §9. Taking an identity rest, or a positive electric approximation to it, gives a stand-alone log ratio at least \(\log(1+e^{-M_{\mathcal K}a_jh})\) for that kernel because the normalized trace is at least \(1+e^{-M_{\mathcal K}a_jh}\) and the doubled trace is no larger. Thus requiring a full-strength short slab to satisfy the target bound uniformly in the exterior already fails at a positive constant.

**OPEN.** The missing number can be stated without differentiation. For one fixed admissible \(L\), the two independent electric assignments in (2) have to satisfy \(\limsup_j{\mathbb E}[1-\operatorname{Tr}(\tau_\ell\tau_k)]<1/1651\) for the actual \(B=M F^{T-1}M\). Equations (8), (18) and (24) constrain the assignments, and the center average has every one-period electric trace of \(B\), yet it loses the required overlaps. On the magnetic route, (33) controls the common-\(u\) integral only up to \(u_{\rm phys}\). The conditional oscillation in §§12–13 defeats the uniform two-stage estimate above that scale. Equation (15) leaves fewer full-strength joins, but (17) and the scalar comparison show why neither stand-alone box gaps nor counting the remaining faces bounds their signed cost. The periodic tree chart also fails with probability tending to one. I have no further estimate of the actual two-period overlaps, or of the signed remainder in (15), that distinguishes the two sides of the strict threshold. The arguments are analytic. No numerical run or computer-assisted proof was used.

RESULT — PROVED by hand and pending independent check. The signed contribution in the original (30) from \(u_*\) through \(c/[(1+\beta)(b+1)]\) tends to zero. Almost all of its ordinary faces can instead be restored to full strength with vanishing signed cost, leaving (15) on \(O(n^3/\beta^\theta)\) faces per affected layer for some fixed \(\theta>0\). There is also an equivalent one-step test on \(2n^3\) temporal plaquettes, with a sharp exterior-uniform starting scale \(n^3\beta v^2=o(1)\). OPEN — the strict upper bound for the actual remaining cost, (W), and the four-dimensional \(SU(2)\) Yang–Mills mass gap.
