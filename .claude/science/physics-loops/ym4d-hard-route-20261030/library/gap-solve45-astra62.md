**PROVED (what survives a sparse read).** Let $N=n^3$ and let $S$ be the retained edge set of the spatial cubic torus, sampled independently of the Wilson field. Conditional on $Y$ the law of $X$ is invariant under every spatial vertex gauge at time zero. On a tree the compact vertex gauge group is transitive on all edge assignments. On an isolated simple cycle its orbits are indexed by the conjugacy class of the ordered holonomy. Fix a root and set the tree links to the identity successively. The remaining root gauge conjugates the closing link. For SU(2) the conjugacy class is indexed by its angle in $[0,\pi]$. On disjoint components the gauges are independent factors. Averaging over their Haar measures therefore gives the unique invariant probability on each product of orbits, also after conditioning on $Y$ and the orbit labels. Thus a forest has exactly its product Haar law independently of $Y$. If the only cyclic components are isolated elementary squares, their exact angles contain all the dependence on $Y$ in the reading. The full observed links can be sampled back by applying independent vertex gauges to orbit representatives.

**PROVED (geometry of this read).** For $n\ge 8$, the probability of a component with five or more edges is at most $CNp^5$. Such a component contains a connected set of five edges. Starting at any vertex there are only a bounded number of these sets, since the degree is six. Each requires five independent retentions. With at most four edges per component, any cycle is an elementary square. The probability of two edge-disjoint retained squares at periodic sup-distance at most $4b$ between their base points is at most $CNb^3p^8$ for $1\le b<n/8$. Count the first face, the base and orientation of the second, then require all eight edges. The expected number of retained squares is $3Np^4$. These bounds include touching squares by the five-edge exception whenever the squares share edges.

**GUIDE.** To test genuinely informative reads, take $Np^4\to\infty$. At least $N/4$ squares of one orientation are edge-disjoint. The probability that none of those squares is read is at most $\exp(-Np^4/4)$. An exact observed angle then distinguishes its own input from an independent slice with probability tending to one. The equality test has zero accidental matches since the finite Wilson measure has a density relative to Haar and each face angle has a continuous law. The next question concerns dependence on the *opposite* slice.

**PROVED (Gaussian projections).** Let $U$ have a density relative to a finite-dimensional standard Gaussian $G$. For orthogonal projections $P_i$ with $\sum_iP_i\preceq K I$,
\[
 \sum_i D(\mathcal L(P_i U)\Vert\mathcal L(P_i G))
       \le K D(\mathcal L(U)\Vert\mathcal L(G)).                         \tag{2}
\]
This is the Gaussian entropy subadditivity argument of Carlen and Cordero-Erausquin in this setting. Here are the details used. For a positive smooth density $f$ relative to Gaussian measure $g$, the projected density is its average over the orthogonal complement. Its gradient is the corresponding average of $P_i\nabla f$. Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its relative Fisher information by $\int |P_i\nabla f|^2/f\,dg$. Sum over $i$. Apply the bound to $f_u(x)=\int f(e^{-u}x+\sqrt{1-e^{-2u}}z)\,dg(z)$. This evolution commutes with each marginal. It has generator $\Delta-x\cdot\nabla$. Gaussian integration by parts gives $-\partial_u\int f_u\log f_u\,dg=\int|\nabla f_u|^2/f_u\,dg$. Integration from zero to infinity gives (2) since the limiting constant density has entropy zero. For the bounded densities needed in (1), first mix in a positive constant density, smooth by this same evolution, and send both parameters to zero. The smoothed densities have bounded derivatives at positive times, all the densities have a common finite upper bound for fixed conditioning data, and $r\log r$ extends continuously to zero. Gaussian $L^1$ convergence and boundedness give convergence of the entropies. The large-time convergence follows from the Gaussian formula for bounded smooth $f$ and dominated convergence. This proves (2) for almost every law in (1).

## The full noisy slice at smaller noise

**GUIDE.** The preceding argument can also test the full noisy slice of row 1337. **PROVED (coupling in mean square).** Given the cuts one can couple the first-half array $Z$ in (1) with a reference standard Gaussian $G'$ independent in law of the cuts so that
\[
                 \mathbb E |Z-G'|^2\le C V\Delta_b .                       \tag{4}
\]
For clarity, the Gaussian quadratic transport inequality used here has a short triangular proof. In one dimension send a standard normal $g$ to a target by its increasing quantile map $D_0$. For a smooth density relative to the Gaussian bounded above and below by positive constants, change of variables and Gaussian integration by parts give
\[
 D(\mathcal L(D_0(g))\Vert\mathcal L(g))
  =\tfrac12\mathbb E|D_0(g)-g|^2+
    \mathbb E[D_0'(g)-1-\log D_0'(g)] .
\]
The boundary term vanishes since comparability of the normal tails gives $|D_0(x)|\le C(1+|x|)$, and integration on increasing intervals justifies the formula. For bounded densities that can vanish, mix with a positive constant density and smooth using the Gaussian evolution in the proof of (2). Entropy converges by boundedness. The quantiles converge almost everywhere under the standard normal, and Fatou gives the quadratic bound. For a bounded multivariate density relative to product normals, expose coordinates successively. At each stage use a fresh standard normal to sample the next target coordinate by its conditional quantile. Conditional densities are bounded for almost every prefix, with a prefix-dependent bound, by dividing the bound on the joint density by the positive prefix density. Summing the costs uses the entropy chain rule. This constructs a measurable triangular coupling, also for the conditional density given the cuts. In the one-dimensional formula the nonnegative remainder gives cost at most twice the entropy. Marginalize (1) to the first-half array to obtain (4). One can sample all original first-half variables conditional on that array and the cuts to retain the original Wilson coupling to its linearization. The second half is then sampled independently given the cuts. This is Talagrand's Gaussian transport inequality in the form needed here.

**PROVED (cost of a reference action array).** At a random translated first-half slice as above, set $A_q=\beta(1-\operatorname{tr}U_q/2)$. \[
 \frac1N\mathbb E\sum_{q\ {\rm spatial}}|A_q-B_q|^2
 \le C\{ M(\Delta_b+b^{-2})+b^{-1}+b/n+
                  M^2 e^{-c\beta/b^8}+e^{-cM}\}.                         \tag{5}
\]
To see this, on good interior boxes compose the radius comparison with $r\mapsto\min(r^2/2,M)$, of Lipschitz constant $\sqrt{2M}$. For the step from $Z$ to $G'$ the sum of squared curl errors is at most $C|Z-G'|^2$. For the preceding step the good-box squared radius error sums to at most $CV/b^2$ in mean. Bad boxes cost $M^2$ per marked face times their probability. At every face the Wilson exponential moment gives $\mathbb E(A_q-M)_+^2\le C e^{-cM}$ with absolute constants. **PROVED, given the finite-box identities of row 1337.** For the density $p_s$ of $(W_s,Y)$ set
\[

\]
All link measures in this formula are probability Haar. At $s=0$ the two-slice density is the square of the symmetric kernel of $F^{T/2}$ divided by $Z_T$.
