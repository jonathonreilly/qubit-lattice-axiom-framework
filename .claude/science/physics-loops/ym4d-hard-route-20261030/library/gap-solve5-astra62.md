## 2. The effective seam on the original interpolation

Equation (10) permits a larger starting coupling on almost the same number of temporal plaquettes. It does not estimate the intervening common-\(v\) cost for the unbroken longitudinal circles of (22).


## 5. Full-strength transverse links on the original seam

**PROVED.** Keep every original longitudinal link at \(\beta\). Pair neighboring longitudinal lines in the second direction in a fixed perfect matching of the transverse torus. There are \(n^2/2\) such pairs. Fix \(\gamma>5/6\), set \(h=\lfloor\gamma\beta\log n\rfloor\), and on the longitudinal circle mark positions with spacings \(h\) or \(h+1\), up to an immaterial \(O(1)\) adjustment of \(h\). More precisely, use \(m=\lfloor n/h\rfloor\) intervals of lengths \(\lfloor n/m\rfloor,\lceil n/m\rceil\) differing by at most one. Since \(h^2=o(n)\) their lengths lie in \([h,h+1]\) eventually, or equal \(h+1\) at an integer endpoint. At each mark put a transverse bond between each matched pair (a rung). All other transverse couplings are zero for now. Write \(K=m n^2/2\) for the number of rungs. Uniformly as their couplings increase independently from zero to full \(\beta\), the actual log thermal ratio with \(B\) is \(o(1)\). For \(5/6<\gamma<1\), the stand-alone ratio at full strength diverges. In particular,
\[
      K\sim \frac{N}{2\gamma\beta\log n}                                  \tag{11}
\]
original transverse temporal plaquettes can be restored to full coupling with this conclusion, without a single additional longitudinal cut.

**PROVED (Fourier scales).** If \(a_s(d)=I_d(s)/I_1(s)\), then
\[
 -s\log a_s(2)\longrightarrow 3/2,\qquad
 -s\log a_s(3)\longrightarrow 4 .                                         \tag{12}
\]
For the Wilson angle law with Haar radial density proportional to \(\sin^2 r\), set \(z=\sqrt s\,r\). The normalized radial integrals converge to those with weight \(z^2e^{-z^2/2}\,dz\), by \(1-\cos r\ge 2r^2/\pi^2\) and \(\sin r\le r\). This domination also applies with a factor \(z^2\) or \(z^4\). One integration by parts gives limiting second moment three. Since the normalized fundamental and three-dimensional characters are \(\cos r\) and \((4\cos^2r-1)/3\), the limits follow. Also \(a_s(d)\le a_s(k)e^{-(d-k)/(1+s)}\) for \(d\ge k\ge1\). Indeed the Riccati ratio comparison of row 1145 (1) gives \(I_{\ell+1}/I_\ell\le e^{-\operatorname{arsinh}((\ell+1/2)/s)}\le e^{-1/(1+s)}\) for \(\ell\ge1\). Multiplication gives the bound. Thus at \(h/(1+\beta)\to\infty\) the Fourier sum past dimension \(k=2\) or \(3\) is at most a constant times the corresponding leading multiplier to the power \(h\), including the factors \(d^2\).

**PROVED (integration against the actual rest).** In each \(k=1,2\) sewing integral start with \(k\) independent open cylinders of total mass \(\langle1,B1\rangle^k\), and the \(k\) projected seam integrals. Collapsing the longitudinal spin walks between marks gives on each rail a normalized central \(h_i\)-step density, \(h_i\in[h,h+1]\), with arguments transported by the two **rail holonomies of the boundary fields**. Integrating the intermediate vertex spins proves this by convolution. The rung weights remain normalized one-link densities. Retain the two boundary slices of every open cylinder in their original Wilson coordinates, including the time-like links in the open interior.

**PROVED.** On those boundary slices mark each longitudinal link based at \((i,y,z)\) with \(i+y+z\) even. Parallel boundaries of a plaquette have opposite transverse parities at the same \(i\), consecutive collinear links share no plaquette, and the two ends are separated by more than one interior time step. Hence the marked links occur in disjoint sets of plaquettes. Condition on all other links in all open cylinders. The marked links are now independent, each with density a translate of the normalized Wilson density at some \(s_e\le5\beta\). The count five is four spatial staples and **one** inward temporal staple, since the seam has been removed. The quaternion action is linear in a single link, which gives that conditional law, including Haar when its source vanishes. The spatial edge on a rung is transverse and remains conditioned. Each rail segment has at least \(\lfloor h/2\rfloor\) independent marked links at **each** of its two endpoints in the sewing construction, that is, in its two spatial boundary holonomies. These sets are disjoint between rail factors even in the twice-sewn integral.

**PROVED.** Integrate those marked links. Translation, inversion, convolution and the fixed insertions along a path preserve the bound by the product of the central Fourier multipliers. \(a_{s_e}(d)\le a_{5\beta}(d)\) by the one-link ordering. The resulting positive rail density has constant Fourier coefficient one. Each dimension-\(d\) contribution has pointwise modulus at most
\[
               d^2 a_\beta(d)^h a_{5\beta}(d)^{2\lfloor h/2\rfloor}.        \tag{13}
\]
One can also see (13) by multiplying the representation matrices of the independently translated central variables at fixed coarse vertex spins, then taking the trace. It is valid for all the remaining backgrounds. Write each rail density as its part even and its part odd under a central sign change at either end. The even part is \(1+O(b_3)\), the odd part \(O(b_2)\), with \(b_d=a_\beta(d)^h a_{5\beta}(d)^{2\lfloor h/2\rfloor}\). This follows by (12) and the Fourier tail estimate. All constants are absolute for large cutoffs.

**PROVED (the parity sum).** Flip the two vertex spins of a rung simultaneously by a central sign. This leaves the rung density and product Haar unchanged. For each gap between successive rungs, multiply its two rail densities. Its part even under this flip is \(1+O(b_3+b_2^2)\), and its odd part is \(O(b_2)\). Averaging over the signs at all rungs of one ladder leaves only the all-even product and the all-odd product. Indeed the parities of the two gaps incident to any rung must coincide. With the rails removed, the integral of the rung densities against product Haar is exactly one, since each rung uses its own two spins. Therefore the even product on all ladders differs multiplicatively from one by at most \(\exp[O(kK(b_3+b_2^2))]\), in either direction. The odd product on one ladder has modulus at most \((C b_2)^m\). Its contribution relative to that ladder's even product is at most \((2C b_2)^m\) when \(b_3+b_2^2\) is small. The latter errors sum to at most \(C k n^2(2C b_2)^m\) in the log. These estimates were uniform after conditioning, so integration gives the same bounds for the partition functions divided by \(\langle1,B1\rangle^k\). They are independent of the rung couplings in the full interval.

**PROVED.** By (12),
\(b_2^2=n^{-(18/5)\gamma+o(1)}\) and \(b_3=n^{-(24/5)\gamma+o(1)}\).
Since \(K\le n^3\) and \(m\asymp n/(\beta\log n)\), both partition ratios tend to one for \(\gamma>5/6\). This proves the stated signed restoration. If the base-point choice in (22) is used, its initial \(o(1)\) segment can first be removed to reach all transverse couplings zero. Equivalently start the integral at zero, which has the same limiting signed cost. From zero to the full endpoint the rung-first path is coordinatewise increasing on precisely the original transverse bonds. After this first leg the remaining one-step condition is
\[
  \limsup_j 2(2N-K)\int_0^{\beta_j}
             [w_{1,\mathrm{rest}}(v)-w_{2,\mathrm{rest}}(v)]\,dv
                        <\log(1651/1650)                                  \tag{14}
\]
at one fixed \(L\). Here the \(K\) rungs stay at \(\beta_j\) throughout.

**PROVED (sharp comparison with an arbitrary exterior for this placement).** If the rail holonomies are held fixed, use the same sign sum with \(b_d=a_\beta(d)^h\). This suffices for a uniform \(o(1)\) ratio when \(\gamma>1\). Conversely every pair of consecutive full rungs bounds a fundamental rectangle, all \(K\) rectangle characters having distinct supports. The seam has vacuum eigenvalue one and those orthonormal eigenstates have eigenvalues \(a_\beta(2)^{2h_i+2}\). Its trace is at least \(1+K a_\beta(2)^{2h+4}\), which diverges for \(\gamma<1\) by (12) and \(\beta=O(\log n)\). The identity-rest test of §4 applies. This separates the actual Wilson estimate from the sharp **leading threshold** for common-exterior bounds on these ladders. No assertion is made about the borderline values.

**PROVED (order of the count with unbroken rails).** More generally keep all longitudinal bonds at full coupling and restore an arbitrary set of \(K'\) full transverse seam bonds. Across the \(2n^2\) possible neighbor pairs of longitudinal lines, the sum of the cyclic spacings of rungs on nonempty pairs is at most \(2n^3\). Thus at least \(K'/2\) of the spacings are at most \(4N/K'\). If \(K'\gg N/(\beta\log n)\), this is \(o(n)\), so these are spacings between different rungs and give distinct simple rectangles. Their eigenvalues are at least \(\exp[-C(1+N/K')/\beta]\). The seam trace is at least one plus half \(K'\) times this last number, and diverges. This is an order obstruction for exterior-uniform restoration on the original longitudinal seam. It is not an upper bound on how many rungs the actual \(B\) can accept.


## 6. Where the conditional ladder comparison stops

**PROVED.** The boundary conditioning in §5 has a sharp leading threshold of its own. For every fixed \(0<\gamma<5/6\), there are two admissible choices \(A,D\) of the unmarked open-cylinder boundary environments for which the effective, marked-link-integrated seam \(H\) satisfies
\[
       \log\frac{H_{AA}H_{DD}}{H_{AD}H_{DA}}
            =(16+o(1))K\,a_\beta(2)^{2h}a_{5\beta}(2)^{2h}
                     \ \longrightarrow\ +\infty.                         \tag{15a}
\]
All rungs are full. This cross ratio permits arbitrary positive factors depending on each environment separately. Thus using a better one-end normalization cannot extend that **uniform conditional** method across \(5/6\). The two environments need not be typical under \(B\).

**PROVED (the boundary data).** Choose the number of longitudinal gaps even, \(m=2\lfloor n/(2h)\rfloor\). This keeps the same two possible gap lengths for large \(n\). Fix the unmarked fields of an open cylinder to the identity in \(A\). In \(D\) use the same fields except that at each boundary, on every matching transverse rung, the fixed link is the central sign \((-1)^i\) at mark \(i\). The two ends of each cylinder can be assigned the same data. Every remaining marked longitudinal link still has conditional density centered at the identity because its five staples are positive except possibly the one spatial staple across its matching neighbor. Thus its strength is \(5\beta\) or \(3\beta\). Only the links immediately next to a mark can have the latter strength. Each rail gap has \(O(1)\) exceptional links. Haar-open neighborhoods of both specifications have positive open-cylinder probability at every fixed cutoff, and their conditional integrals are continuous in the specifications. The four \(H_{rs}\) are obtained by attaching one seam to two ends of types \(r,s\) and performing the independent marked-link integrals there. A straight or crossed sewing of the two specified cylinders gives the products in (15a).

**PROVED.** Set \(b=a_\beta(2)^h a_{5\beta}(2)^h\), \(u=a_\beta(3)^h a_{5\beta}(3)^h\). Each rail density has the expansion \(1+2b_i\chi_2 + O(u)\) at its gauge-spin difference, with \(b_i=b(1+o(1))\) uniformly. Here \(\chi_2\) is the fundamental character of dimension two. Gap lengths, parity rounding, and replacing \(O(1)\) factors \(a_{5\beta}(d)\) by \(a_{3\beta}(d)\) change the leading \(d=2,3\) terms by \(1+o(1)\) from (12). The tail bound past \(d=3\) follows from the \(h\) bare convolutions alone after factoring the third multipliers. Equation (12) gives \(u=o(b^2)\). In the \(AA,DD\) cases all rung arguments have agreeing central signs. In the \(AD,DA\) cases, absorb the alternating relative rung signs into the upper spins, leaving the same all-positive rung measure and one reversed rail sign in every gap. Write \(\epsilon=+1\) for the first two cases and \(-1\) for the last two.

**PROVED.** Under the probability law of the rungs alone the pairs \((g_i,g_i')\) are independent, with \(g_i\) Haar and the relative spin distributed by the normalized \(p_\beta\). After the simultaneous sign average the even part of gap \(i\) is
\[
  1+4\epsilon\, b_i b'_i\,X_i+O(u),\qquad
  X_i=\chi_2(g_i g_{i+1}^{-1})\,\chi_2(g'_i g_{i+1}'{}^{-1}).
\]
Haar marginals and the fundamental multiplier at the two rungs give \(\mathbb E X_i=a_\beta(2)^2\). Explicitly condition on the unprimed spins and use \(\int\chi_2^2=1\). The even gap factors are positive for sufficiently large cutoffs. For bounded variables \(Y_i\) depending on two adjacent independent rungs and \(\|Y_i\|_\infty\le C b^2\), one has
\(\log\mathbb E\prod_i(1+Y_i)=\sum_i\mathbb E Y_i+O(K b^4)\).
For the upper bound, replace \(\log(1+Y_i)\) by \(Y_i+O(b^4)\), color these factors by at most three independent classes, and apply Hölder. Each centered moment at the fixed Hölder power has log \(O(b^4)\) by Taylor's inequality. Jensen gives the lower bound with the same error. The odd parity term per ladder is relatively at most \((C b)^m\) as in §5. Thus
\(\log H_{rs}=4\epsilon K b^2(1+o(1))+O(Ku+K b^4+n^2(Cb)^m)\).
Here \(Kb^2=n^{3-(18/5)\gamma+o(1)}\) diverges for the stipulated \(\gamma\). This proves (15a). The obstruction is a property of actual Wilson **conditional** integrals, and supplies no lower bound on the fully integrated thermal ratio.


## 7. What the genuine two-period overlap does at full coupling

**PROVED.** At the fully restored endpoint let \(A=C^{1/2}M\) on physical link space, \(L_e=AA^*\), \(\rho=L_e^T/\operatorname{Tr}L_e^T\). This is the electric density of row 1149 §1. Let \(Q_\eta\) project onto a local electric center-parity assignment on the \(3N\) spatial edges. In the actual twice-sewn measure define
\[
  \pi(\eta,\xi)=
      \frac{\|Q_\eta\rho Q_\xi\|_{\rm HS}^2}{\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2},
               \qquad \delta=\eta+\xi\pmod2 .
\]
These are probabilities regardless of how small the homogeneous purity is. For every fixed \(\epsilon>0\) and every fixed binary mask \(b\) (allowed to depend on \(j\)),
\[
 \pi\!\left(\left|\,|\delta+b|-3N/2\,\right|>\epsilon N\right)
                    \le \exp[-c\epsilon^2 N+o(N)] .                       \tag{16}
\]
Thus even **relative to the full two-period normalizer**, contributions between parities differing on about half the spatial links carry essentially all the purity. The one-period min-entropy estimate of row 1149 by itself would only give an absolute discarded weight. Here the denominator may tend to zero at an unspecified rate.

**PROVED (two prescribed twists).** Write \(U_s\) for multiplication of spatial links by fixed central signs \(s\) in position space, and \(m(s)\) for the number of spatial plaquettes on which their product is negative. The Fourier coefficient of \(\delta\) is exactly
\[
 \widehat\pi_\delta(s)=
 \frac{\operatorname{Tr}(L_e^T U_s L_e^T U_s)}{\operatorname{Tr}L_e^{2T}}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
       0\le\widehat\pi_\delta(s)\le e^{-c\beta m(s)}\quad(m(s)>0).          \tag{17}
\]
The identity is insertion of the two parity characters in the trace. The trace is nonnegative by the Hilbert–Schmidt square of \(L_e^{T/2}U_s L_e^{T/2}\). Its position integral has two twisted electric seams and nonnegative pointwise weights. The unconditioned double cylinder is the normalization throughout.

**PROVED (bad endpoints in a twisted trace).** At one end of the first twisted seam let \(D\) be the indicator that at least \(m(s)/4\) frustrated spatial plaquettes have \(1-w_p>1/32\). Write \(k=2T\). With an indicator at one magnetic slice, the twisted numerator is a trace of \(k\) factors \(U_i A D_i A^*\), with \(D_i=I\) except at the marked slice and \(U_i=I\) except at the two seams. This follows by cycling the half convolutions. Schatten Hölder with exponent \(k\), using unitarity of \(U_i\), bounds the normalized absolute trace by
\[
 \left[\frac{\operatorname{Tr}[(A D A^*)^k]}{\operatorname{Tr}L_e^k}\right]^{1/k}
   ={\mathbb P}_{k}\{D\text{ holds on every slice}\}^{1/k}.
\]
The equality sews the homogeneous Wilson cylinder with \(D\) at all slices. On that event choose the bad plaquettes among \(k\,m(s)\) candidates. At least \(k m(s)/4\) are bad. At least two thirds of these contain one common spatial direction. The homogeneous half-tilt estimate used for (6) applies to arbitrary such sets throughout this torus by taking that direction as time. The probability that a fixed set of \(l\) is bad is at most \((C e^{-\beta/64})^l\). There are at most \(3\cdot2^{k m(s)}\) choices. The \(k\)-th root is at most \(e^{-c_1\beta m(s)}\) for large \(\beta\). Apply this separately at both ends. Replicating the **event** with Hölder is what avoids division by a small one-period purity.

**PROVED (good endpoints).** When both ends are good, at least half of the frustrated plaquettes have \(1-w\le1/32\) at both ends. For any temporal vertex gauges, the triangle inequality around such a plaquette gives a sum of seam-link distances at least \(3/2\), because the sign product is \(-1\) and the two plaquette distances from the identity are at most \(1/4\) each. Cauchy–Schwarz and bounded incidence give total seam action at least \(c_2m(s)\) on the union of its frustrated boundaries. Use half the coupling there to pay \(e^{-c_2\beta m(s)/2}\), remove the restrictions and normalize the reduced electric convolutions. At most \(4m(s)\) scalar ratios \(z_{\beta/2}/z_\beta\le C\) occur. On that seam the new normalized operator is \(C^{1/2} H C^{1/2}\) where \(H\) is a contraction whose Fourier multipliers are the ratios for the reduced and unreduced convolutions. It commutes with the center masks. Now each \(U_i\), and the one product with \(H\), has norm at most one. Schatten Hölder against the \(k\) factors \(L_e\) bounds the remaining trace by \(\operatorname{Tr}L_e^k\). Combine the good part and the two bad parts, reducing \(c\), to prove (17). This argument extends the one-twist perimeter estimate of row 1149 §9. For a flat mask the elementary upper bound is one. In fact it is exactly one for this two-twist insertion because the global symmetry commutes with \(L_e\).

**PROVED (counting and concentration).** Both parities are cycles over \(\mathbb F_2\) by gauge invariance at a vertex. Fourier inversion of (17) gives
\[
      \max_d \pi(\delta=d)\le 2^{-(2N-2)}(1+e^{-c\beta})^{3N}.
\]
Parallel transport from a root on the lifted cube expresses each flat sign field as a vertex gradient times one of eight holonomy choices. This gives \(2^{2N-2}\) elements in the image of \(s\mapsto\operatorname{curl}s\). Sum the bound on the image by summing over all plaquette subsets. The uniform cycle law has mass \(2^{-(2N+1)}\) per cycle since the connected vertex-edge incidence has rank \(N-1\) (solve on a tree). Relative to this uniform law the density is thus at most \(8e^{3Ne^{-c\beta}}\).

**PROVED.** A uniform cycle is the sum of the boundaries of independently chosen fair plaquettes and three independently chosen fair straight coordinate windings. To see that these generators span, break a cycle into closed walks, interchange consecutive perpendicular steps by adding a square, cancel opposite steps, and translate the axial windings to fixed representatives by strips of squares. A uniform input to a surjective linear map is uniform on its image. Every edge is fair because it occurs on a square boundary. Hence the mean of the distance to any fixed binary vector is \(3N/2\). Flipping one fair plaquette changes this distance by at most four and flipping a straight winding changes it by at most \(n\). Revealing the independent bits in order yields a martingale with sum of squared increment ranges bounded by \(48N+3n^2\). Each conditional log moment is bounded by \(t^2\) times the squared range divided by eight, by the tilted-variance proof in row 1147 §6. The exponential Markov bound followed by the density comparison proves (16). In fact \(\delta\) has zero homology for the actual pair, consistently with the factor eight in this comparison.

**PROVED (a quantitative ambiguity in seam data).** Change the rest to \(B_\tau=\mathbb E_s U_s B U_s^*\) by independent link signs of mean \(e^{-\tau/N}\), for fixed \(\tau\ge0\). It is a positive rest with nonnegative position kernel. It has exactly the same one-period data for **every** normalized electric seam, since these commute with \(U_s\). At the full endpoint it multiplies the \((\eta,\xi)\) density block by \(e^{-\tau|\eta+\xi|/N}\). If \(J_\tau\) denotes its full-endpoint log ratio, then
\[
        e^{-(J_\tau-J_0)}
          ={\mathbb E}_\pi e^{-2\tau|\delta|/N}\longrightarrow e^{-3\tau},
          \qquad J_\tau-J_0\longrightarrow3\tau .                         \tag{18}
\]
This is relative even for diverging \(J_0\), by (16) and bounded convergence in probability. Blocks of discrepancy \(o(N)\) change by a factor \(1+o(1)\) uniformly on any specified sublinear radius. The perturbed two-period discrepancy law is an everywhere bounded tilt of \(\pi\) and retains (16). Thus even that full-cutoff concentration law and the entire set of one-period seam traces leave a finite additive ambiguity in the desired cost. For the comparison take \(\tau>\log(1651/1650)/3\). Since \(J_0\ge0\), the perturbed rest exceeds the threshold eventually. This is an obstruction to inferring the bound from those data. The perturbed rest is not claimed to be a homogeneous Wilson cylinder.


## 8. A terminal interval of the original spatial face bound

**PROVED.** Let \(S\) be a nonempty prescribed plaquette set in the \(n^3\times T\) Wilson torus and repeat it in the \(2T\) torus. Let \(\mathcal J_S(\beta-t)\) be their log ratio on decreasing just these couplings by \(t\) in each period. For every \(0<\eta<1/24\), uniformly for \(0\le u=t/\beta\le1/48\),
\[
 \left|\mathcal J_S(\beta-t)-\mathcal J_S(\beta)\right|
                  \le C_\eta |S|\,u\,(u+\beta^{-\eta}).                   \tag{19}
\]
In particular take \(S=D'_j\) from the original (15), or the \(2N\) transverse plaquettes of (22). For each \(0<\epsilon<1/24\) the whole terminal interval
\[
                0\le t\le \beta^{1+\epsilon}/|S|                           \tag{20}
\]
has vanishing signed cost. At its outer end, the separate one-period **action** log moment grows as \((3/4+o(1))\beta^\epsilon\). The two-period one grows twice as fast. This improves the absolute \(O(|S|t/\beta)\) bound of row 1149 (26) on the same interpolation. Here \(|D'_j|\) grows faster than every power of \(\beta\), for instance by the disjoint residual faces of row 1149 §6.

**PROVED (dissemination of one orientation).** Use a periodic rectangular torus with four-volume \(V\), spatial sides \(n\) and time \(T\) or \(2T\). Fix one plaquette orientation \(p\). There is one plaquette of that orientation per site. Write \(p_V(u)=V^{-1}\log Z_V(u)\) when every such coupling is \(\beta(1-u)\) and the others are \(\beta\), with unnormalized Wilson weights. For any subset \(S_p\) of this orientation and \(\beta-t\ge0\),
\[
 |S_p|\,u\,p'_V(0)\ \le\
 \log{\mathbb E}\exp\!\left[t\sum_{S_p}(1-w)\right]\
 \le |S_p|\,[p_V(u)-p_V(0)]                                               \tag{21}
\]
for \(t\ge0\). The lower bound is Jensen and translation invariance. For the upper bound take any axis as time. If it lies in the plaquette plane, the marked terms all belong to the crossing convolutions, so each layer can be written as a positive \(M C_i M\) with the same magnetic half-weight. Otherwise they all belong to the slice weights, so the factors are positive \(C^{1/2}M_i^2 C^{1/2}\). Gauge averaging commutes with these operators. Unnormalized convolutions may be used. Trace Hölder bounds the log partition function by the average, over that axis coordinate, of the log partition functions with the chosen layer repeated along the axis. This preserves a pattern already constant in other axes. Apply the operation successively along all four axes. The final patterns are the uniform original and uniform tilted ones with fractions \(1-|S_p|/V\) and \(|S_p|/V\). Subtract the original log partition function. This proves (21) on the actual finite torus without a period-divisibility assumption beyond the lane grid. This is a trace form of the chessboard method. Sussman and Alpoge give a reflection proof for slab functions in outline lines 2885–2912. [Finite chessboard inequality](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/YM4_sketch_500.tex#L2885-L2912)

**PROVED (common slope on both rectangles).** Uniformly in these tori and orientations, for \(|u|\le1/4\),
\[
                   p'_V(u)=3/4+O_\eta(|u|+\beta^{-\eta}).                 \tag{22a}
\]
The pressure comparison used here is the open-box Gaussian method of row 1143 §7, which credits Sourav Chatterjee for weak-coupling free-energy comparison. The following details check the anisotropic source, the shape and the derivative. Choose \(2\eta<\gamma<1/12\), \(b=\lceil\beta^\gamma\rceil\). In one open four-cube let \(D\) be the linear plaquette curl on the free scalar comb-gauge coordinates, \(K=D^*D\), \(K_p=D^*1_p D\), where \(1_p\) keeps the selected orientation. The Gaussian precision after removing the common \(\beta\) is \(K-uK_p\). If \(g_b(u)\) denotes the open Gaussian log partition function per site (three colors, including Haar tangent density), then for \(|u|\le1/2\)
\[
       |p_V(u)-g_b(u)|\le C[(\log\beta)/b+(b/n)\log\beta].                \tag{22b}
\]

**PROVED.** Tile as many disjoint open \(b\)-cubes as fit, leaving at most \(C Vb/n\) sites. Dropping crossing and leftover plaquettes gives the upper comparison since the action is nonnegative. For the lower comparison use separate tree gauges on the cubes. Haar on the remaining links, including joining links, stays product. Restrict all joining links, non-tree boundary tangents, and links in or bordering the leftover region to radius \(\beta^{-1}\), except for tree links already fixed. This costs \(C V(1/b+b/n)\log\beta\) in the log. On setting the restricted links to one, all plaquettes outside the internal boxes are flat. Every retained edge of a crossing plaquette is a boundary tangent, a tree edge or a joining link. Replacing the restrictions by identity in the action costs a precision factor \(1+\beta^{-1/4}\) on the internal boxes and an additive \(O(V(1/b+b/n))\) in the exponent by \(|z+w|^2\le(1+\beta^{-1/4})|z|^2+(1+\beta^{1/4})|w|^2\). This applies termwise with the orientation weights between \(1/2\) and \(3/2\).

**PROVED.** On an internal cube, also with those boundary variables fixed to identity, the shelling in row 1143 (14) gives \(cb^{-5}I\le K\le24I\) and squared maximal link distance at most \(Cb\) times the unweighted action. Thus the weighted linear precision is between \(cb^{-5}I/2\) and \(36I\). In tree logarithms of radius \(b^{-7/2}\), summing the Taylor remainders in the four link products costs relative \(O(b^{-7/2}b^{5/2})=O(1/b)\) in the quadratic action. The bounded positive orientation weights change only the constant. The Haar Jacobian costs \(O(b^{-7})\) per edge. The integral outside the chart is at most \(e^{-c\beta/b^8}\), negligible relative to a Gaussian lower bound \(e^{-Cb^4\log\beta}\). The Gaussian itself leaves the chart with probability at most \(Cb^4e^{-c\beta/b^{12}}\) by its coordinate variances. Restricting \(O(b^3)\) boundary variables to identity changes the determinant by \(O(b^3\log b)\), since the Schur complement has eigenvalues between the same spectral bounds by minimization. ## 12. The even electric channel and the actual overlap measure

**PROVED (full-endpoint law).** Resolve the two seams into **all** electric representation dimensions and keep all multiplicities in \(Q_{\ell}\). The actual probability on two full assignments is
\[
 {\cal P}(\ell,\ell')=
  \frac{\|Q_\ell\rho Q_{\ell'}\|_{\rm HS}^2}{\operatorname{Tr}\rho^2}.
\]
Let \(D_e,D'_e\) denote their dimensions at spatial link \(e\), and let \(\nu\) be the law of \(|Z|/\sqrt2\) for a three-component standard normal \(Z\). At any one fixed link the pair
\[
       (D_e/\sqrt\beta,D'_e/\sqrt\beta)\ \Rightarrow\ \nu\otimes\nu .
\]
In fact the **empirical law over the \(3N\) links** converges weakly in probability to \(\nu\otimes\nu\), in the sense of convergence of every bounded continuous test integral. \[
 {\cal P}\{\,\#\{e:|D_e-D'_e|\le w_j\}\ge\epsilon\,3N\,\}\to0.            \tag{27}
\]

**PROVED (one-face covariance of the reference).** In a periodic Gaussian four-cube of side \(b\), the orthogonal projection onto the linear curl image has diagonal \((1-b^{-4})/2\) on every oriented plaquette. Translations and rotations make the diagonal constant. The rank is \(4b^4-[(b^4-1)+4]=3b^4-3\), since a closed real edge cochain is a vertex gradient plus four constant periods. Subtract the straight periods and integrate the cochain from a root. Elementary square interchanges and cancellation give path independence. There are \(6b^4\) plaquettes. Embed the open-box curl image into the same plaquette space by zero on the wrapping plaquettes. The images of all edges strictly away from the boundary agree for the two constructions. Removing the boundary edges and the wrapping edges discards \(O(b^3)\) generators from each. The difference of the two projections therefore has rank \(O(b^3)\) and bounded norm (on the common subspace both are identity, and both vanish on its orthogonal complement outside their summed ranges). Its squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is \(O(b^3)\). In particular the mean squared difference of the diagonals tends to zero, also for any one of the six orientations. The readings at opposite times are different faces of the same permitted parity. Repeated choices of the same face within one field would invalidate this use of (23). Averaging over distinct \(S\) avoids them. We claim
\[
       \frac{\mathbb E_S\,\mathbb E\prod_{p\in S}H_p}{\mu_*^{rk}}
                         \longrightarrow1 .                                \tag{35}
\]
Here is the bound also allowing any fixed exponent \(0<\delta'<\delta_0\) in (34) such that \(\alpha<\delta'/512\). Call the configurations satisfying the band in (31) good, and set \(d_\beta=\beta^{-1/512}+C B_\beta^{-1/2}\). On that event the clipped mean \(a=P^{-1}\sum_C g_p\) satisfies \(|a-\mu_*^r|\le d_\beta\). On its complement Hölder with (34) bounds the expected product for any specified \(S\) by \(e^{Ck}\mathbb P(\mathrm{bad})^{\delta'/(1+\delta')}\). The exponential in (31) makes this \(o(1)\).

**PROVED.** On a good configuration the mean of the product of \(k\) clipped factors sampled without replacement is at least \(a^k(1-k^2 B_\beta/P)\). Indeed sample with replacement with probabilities \(g_p/(P a)\). Since \(a\ge1\), the collision probability is at most \(k^2B_\beta/P\), and the normalization for ordered distinct tuples is at most \(P^k\). This gives the lower bound in (35) because \(k d_\beta=o(1)\). For the upper bound expand in \(l\) factors \(h_p\) and \(k-l\) factors \(g_p\). Given the \(l\) distinct sites, the average over the other \(k-l\) distinct sites is at most \(e^{Ck^2/P}a^{k-l}\), on dropping distinctness in the numerator and using the falling factorial in the denominator. On the good event use \(a\le\mu_*^r+d_\beta\). For the remaining specified tail sites, \(h_p\le B_\beta^{-\delta'}H_p^{1+\delta'}\), so their expected product is at most \((e^{Cr}B_\beta^{-\delta'})^l\). Summing over the \(\binom{k}{l}\) possibilities gives \(e^{Ck^2/P}(\mu_*^r+d_\beta+e^{Cr}B_\beta^{-\delta'})^k\). Its ratio to \(\mu_*^{rk}\) tends to one since \(k B_\beta^{-\delta'}=o(1)\). This proves (35) without a bound on the value of a single untruncated configuration average.

**PROVED (the signed normalizers).** Write \(W_i(S)\) for the deletion action moment in an actual \(iT\)-torus, \(i=1,2\), with \(S\) repeated once per period. For fixed \(i\), (35) on one field gives \(\mathbb E_S W_i(S)/\mu_*^{ik}\to1\). On two independent fields of that period it gives \(\mathbb E_S[W_i(S)^2]/\mu_*^{2ik}\to1\), since the same subset is used in both. The two normalized variables therefore tend to one in mean square over \(S\). This proves the assertions on the individual deletion moments and \(2\log W_1(S)-\log W_2(S)\to0\) in probability.

**PROVED (simultaneous terminal interval).** Temporarily lower **all** \(D'_j\) couplings by \(t=\beta^{1+\epsilon}/|D'_j|\) at the hot end. In an \(iT\)-torus write \(Y_i=t\sum_{(D'_j)^{(i)}} a_p-(3/4)i\beta^\epsilon\). By the two-sided moment argument of §13, \(\mathbb E e^{qY_i}=1+o(1)\) for every fixed real \(q\). In an ensemble of one or two independent fields use \(Y=\sum Y_i\) and tilt by \(e^Y/\mathbb E e^Y\). Cauchy–Schwarz retains (31)'s exceptional-probability bound up to a constant and a square root. Given \(\delta'<\delta_0\) with \(512\alpha<\delta'\), Hölder with exponent \((1+\delta_0)/(1+\delta')\) on the product and its fixed conjugate on \(e^Y\) retains (34) with \(\delta'\) and adjusted constants. The good-event thresholds have not changed. Hence (35) and the second-moment argument apply to the terminally tilted laws too. There are only four first- and second-moment comparisons for each choice of upper endpoint. Convergence in mean square for the resulting four normalized action moments allows a deterministic \(S_j\) with all errors \(o(1)\), for example one minimizing their sum of squared errors. Inserting the extra full deletion on \(S_j\) and the terminal tilt on \(D'_j\) differs from deleting \(S_j\) and tilting only \(A_j\) by at most \(2i t k=o(1)\) in the exponent. Replacing the denominator \(|D'_j|\) in \(t\) by \(|A_j|\) costs at most \(2i t k\) again. The centering term cancels exactly between periods. Equation (28) follows for these \(S_j\) by its cold-end argument, with \(M_j=|A_j|\).

**PROVED (absolute accuracy required by this sampling argument).** A common bound \(d\) on the error in a candidate mean leaves order \(k d\) in the log of its \(k\)-site product, even for bounded positive readings with perfect concentration. A constant reading \(u(1+d)\) at each site has product divided by \(u^k\) equal to \((1+d)^k\). Assigning this perturbation only to the two-reading comparison mean changes its contribution by \(k\log(1+d)\). This is an obstruction for the moment information used in (31)–(35). At \(k\) comparable to the full number of residual sites it requires relative accuracy \(o(1/k)\) or a direct signed comparison between periods. Neither the short-period Gaussian error in (30) nor the folded concentration estimate (32) supplies that accuracy. No counterexample to the physical face inequality follows from the constant-reading comparison.

**PROVED.** Denote by \(M_0,U_0\) the sums of \(a_p\) over marked and unmarked plaquettes of the disseminated pattern. Extend each marked face in both signs of both perpendicular directions.
