## What a finite-volume comparison already gives

**PROVED, conditional on the named input.** Write \(P(k)\) for the log partition function of the symmetric \(k^4\) torus, and \(p(k)=k^{-4}P(k)\). Use unnormalized plaquette weights \(e^{-\beta(1-w)}\), with the same \(\beta\) on all shapes. The log partition function divided by the number of sites decreases as any side increases. In that direction this follows from the monotonicity of \((\sum_i\lambda_i^r)^{1/r}\), since division by the \(\ell^r\) norm leaves each eigenvalue at most one. The Wilson scalar used in passing from \(\operatorname{Tr}F^r\) to the partition function is exponential in \(r\) and cancels from \(J_r\). Thus for any \(u\le T=n/2\) and \(v\ge n\),
\[
       0\le J_T\le n^4\,[p(u)-p(v)].                                     \tag{1}
\]
Indeed \(2\log Z(n,n,n,T)\le n^4p(u)\) and \(P(n)\ge n^4p(v)\). One can take \(u=T,v=n\), or bracket these by two compatible account tori. A cutoff-uniform bound on their pressure difference **per physical four-volume**, \(a_j^{-4}[p(u)-p(v)]\le C_{u a_j,v a_j}\) at fixed physical endpoints, gives \(J_T\le L^4 C_{u a_j,v a_j}\). No infinite-volume subtraction is needed in (1).

## Tree coordinates in the actual homogeneous ensemble

**PROVED.** Let the spatial side be \(n\), the time period \(s\), and \(q(g)=|1-g|=\sqrt{2(1-\operatorname{tr}g/2)}\) in unit-quaternion coordinates. All plaquettes on this rectangular torus satisfy
\[
  {\mathbb E}e^{\beta q(U_p)^2/4}\le C .
\]
Turn either direction of \(p\) into time. In the trace, halving this one electric coupling reduces the normalized convolution in positive-operator order and costs the scalar \(z_{\beta/2}/z_\beta\le C\). The proof is row 1149 (16), including the monotonicity of the successive modified Bessel ratios. The rest of the homogeneous trace is positive. In particular \({\mathbb E}q(U_p)^2\le C/\beta\).

**PROVED.** A rectangular loop of width one and length \(h<n\) in a spatial direction obeys \({\mathbb E}q(U_{1\times h})^2\le C h/\beta\). Use the two charged endpoints of the width-one link in the rotated transfer, as in row 1151 §3. More explicitly, transporting a scalar state by that link is an isometry into the space with a fundamental and a dual colour index, using the normalized Hilbert–Schmidt norm on the link matrix. Integrating the bonds in the long direction inserts the charged projection along the arc and the scalar projection on the complement. If \(\lambda_i,\nu_k\) are the eigenvalues of the scalar and charged transfers, with the same normalization, and \(V_{ki}\) are the matrix elements of this isometry, then \({\mathbb E}w(U_{1\times h})\) is the \(h\)-th moment of \(\nu_k/\lambda_i\) with weights \(\lambda_i^n|V_{ki}|^2/\sum_i\lambda_i^n\). Zero \(\lambda_i\) carry no weight. Jensen and the one-plaquette bound give \({\mathbb E}w(U_{1\times h})\ge({\mathbb E}w(U_p))^h\ge1-Ch/\beta\) for large \(\beta\). This is the rectangular reflection-positivity argument associated with Bachas. [Open-cylinder version](gap-solve5-astra62.md) · [One-link ordering](gap-solve4-astra62.md)

**PROVED.** Partition space into open rectangular boxes with sides \(b\) or \(b+1\), keeping time periodic. In each spatial box fix the same corner comb tree on each slice. For a tree-fixed spatial link let \(Q_e\) be its chord distance from the identity. For a temporal link in these frames let \(Q_e\) be its chord distance from the root temporal link on the same step. These quantities are gauge invariant. In a box with \(v\) sites per slice,
\[
      {\mathbb E}\sum_e Q_e^2\le Cvs\, b/\beta .                           \tag{2}
\]
A spatial link transported to the comb is bounded in chord by the sum of at most two width-one rectangle chords. The first slide takes the link to the first tree plane along axis one, then, for a third-direction link in that plane, to the tree line along axis two. Tree edges on the sides of each strip equal one. The loop comparing a temporal link to the root consists of a tree path, a temporal link, the reverse tree path on the next slice and the inverse root link. Split the path into its at most three straight segments. The temporal bonds at their ends cancel when the strips are concatenated, up to conjugation. Each rectangle has length at most \(b+1\). The triangle inequality and \((x_1+x_2+x_3)^2\le3\sum x_i^2\) prove (2), including across the wrap. Pointwise, if every internal plaquette has chord at most \(d\), these same strips, tiled by their individual plaquettes, give \(Q_e\le C b d\). The two bounds serve different purposes below.

## A two-sided pressure estimate at growing period

**PROVED.** Let \(b=\lfloor\beta^{1/4}\rfloor\), \(2\le s\le b\) even, \(n\ge 2(b+1)^2\), and use \(e^{-\beta(1-w)}\) at every plaquette. Write \(Z_s=Z(n,n,n,s)\). Tile the three circles by intervals of \(b\) or \(b+1\) vertices, distributing the remainder among the intervals. In a box \(B\), fix the spatial trees on all slices and the root temporal links, including the last one, to one **in the Gaussian only**. Let \(G_{B,s}\) be the resulting integral with action \(\beta\|dX\|^2/2\) and measure \(h_0\,d^3X\) on each free link, \(h_0=1/(2\pi^2)\). Uniformly in these parameters, for sufficiently large \(\beta\),
\[
 \left|\log Z_s-\sum_B\log G_{B,s}\right|
 \le C N s\,\varepsilon_\beta,\qquad
 \varepsilon_\beta=\beta^{-1/4}[\log(2+\beta)]^{3/2}.                     \tag{3}
\]
The time wrap is present in both measures. The estimate improves the size of box on which the absolute comparison can be used by charging a tree-coordinate penalty to its **actual Wilson mean** (2).

**PROVED (upper bound).** For each \(B\) let \(v=|B|\), \(d=(K\log(2+\beta)/\beta)^{1/2}\), with a fixed \(K\) large enough that the probability \(p_B\) of an internal plaquette exceeding \(d\) in chord is at most \(\beta^{-8}\). This follows by the exponential bound preceding (2) and a union bound over at most \(6vs\le C\beta\) faces. Set
\[
 H_B=C_0\beta(d+b^2d^2)\sum_e Q_e^2 .
\]
All link penalties are nonnegative. The relative entropy of the full Wilson law against product Haar is at least the sum of the relative entropies of its box marginals against the corresponding Haar products. This follows by disintegrating over the unused joining links and then subtracting the relative entropy against the product of marginals, which is nonnegative by Jensen. Drop the nonnegative crossing actions in the variational expression for \(\log Z_s=-\beta{\mathbb E}A-D(\mu\Vert{\rm Haar})\). Split each marginal into good and bad configurations. If \(I_g,I_b\) are the box Haar integrals of \(e^{-\beta A_B-H_B}\) on the two sets, its contribution is at most
\[
 {\mathbb E}H_B+h(p_B)+(1-p_B)\log I_g+p_B\log I_b ,
\]
where \(h(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)\). This is the entropy chain rule on the two sets and nonnegativity of relative entropy to each normalized restricted integral. Here \(I_b\le1\).

**PROVED (holonomy with the penalty and cutoff).** After the spatial trees are fixed, write \(h_t\) for the root temporal link and \(k_{t,x}\) for each temporal link in the tree frames. The temporal penalty at \(x\) depends on \(q(k_{t,x}h_t^{-1})\). Set the root links to one successively by changing the remaining root frame on each slice. The product \(h\) around time remains integrated with Haar law and conjugates the spatial configuration at the wrap. With root link one on an ordinary step, integrate over \(k_{t,x}\), \(x\ne o\). Use half the spatial weights, penalties and spatial good indicators at each end, and the temporal weights, penalties and temporal good indicators on the step. The resulting real bounded kernel \(K_B(x,y)\) is symmetric under exchange of the slices and inversion of all temporal links. Each temporal plaquette becomes a conjugate of its inverse, and each temporal-link chord is preserved. Simultaneous root conjugation commutes with the kernel. Thus \(I_g=\int dh\,\operatorname{Tr}(K_B^s R_h)\), where \(R_h\) is unitary. For even \(s\ge2\), \(K_B^s\) is positive trace class since \(K_B\) is self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt. Hence \(I_g\le\operatorname{Tr}K_B^s\), the good, penalized integral with the final root link one. This argument permits a sharp plaquette cutoff.

**PROVED (comparison in the identity chart).** On this last integral (2)'s pointwise bound puts every free link at chord at most \(Cbd=o(1)\). Let \(X_e\) be its exponential coordinate. For each plaquette, multiplication of the four unit quaternions gives
\[
 \big|\,q(U_p)-|(dX)_p|\,\big|\le C\sum_{e\in p}|X_e|^2 .
\]
Indeed \(e^{\pm X}=1\pm X+O(|X|^2)\), and each product of two linear remainders is bounded by the sum of their squares. On the good set \(q(U_p)\le d\), the per-plaquette squared-norm error is consequently at most \(C(d+b^2d^2)\sum_{e\in p}|X_e|^2\). Also \(|X_e|\le2Q_e\) on this chart. Bounded incidence allows a fixed \(C_0\) for which \(\beta A_B+H_B\ge\beta\|dX\|^2/2\). The Haar density in these coordinates is \(h_0(\sin|X|/|X|)^2\le h_0\), so \(I_g\le G_{B,s}\). The linearized strip paths express each link as a signed sum of at most \(C b\) distinct plaquette curls, using faces on its own slice or time step. Squaring, using Cauchy–Schwarz on each path and summing the at most \(C b^3\) paths per slice gives \(c b^{-4}I\le d^*d\le24I\). The upper bound follows by incidence. In particular \(|\log G_{B,s}|\le Cvs\log(2+\beta)\). By (2) the expected penalty divided by \(vs\) is at most \(C b(d+b^2d^2)\). The other errors divided by \(vs\) are bounded by \(C p_B\log(2+\beta)+(\log2)/(vs)\). Inserting \(b=\lfloor\beta^{1/4}\rfloor\) proves the upper half of (3).

**PROVED (lower bound).** In the full torus fix the box trees and all root temporal links except the wrapping link in each box by independent vertex gauges. These are disjoint forests, so the other coordinates have product Haar. In each box restrict its last root link, its free links tangent to its spatial boundary (including the temporal ones there), and the joining links to chord at most \(\beta^{-1}\). There are at most \(Csb^2\) restrictions per box, costing \(Csb^2\log(2+\beta)\) in minus log measure since a Haar ball has mass at least \(c\beta^{-3}\). In a crossing plaquette all four links are restricted or tree-fixed. Inside each box compare to setting all its restricted links to one. Changing one such link costs at most a constant in \(\beta A\) per incident plaquette, by Lipschitz continuity of \(1-w\). A crossing plaquette costs at most a constant by the same argument. This leaves a principal coordinate restriction of the Gaussian above. Restoring the omitted Gaussian coordinates changes its log integral by at most \(Csb^2\log(2+\beta)\) because the Schur complement on these coordinates lies between \(cb^{-4}I\) and \(24I\), by minimization of the quadratic form with their values prescribed.

**PROVED.** In the restricted Gaussian the curl covariance on each colour is \(\beta^{-1}\) times an orthogonal projection (apply \(D(D^*D)^{-1}D^*\) to the restricted curl map \(D\)). Each bare component has variance at most \(C b/\beta\) by the signed path formula and the Euclidean norm of its coefficient vector. The elementary one-dimensional Gaussian tail and a union bound over the at most \(Cvs\le C\beta\) coordinates and plaquettes give probability at least \(1/2\) in each box to
\[
 \max_e |X_e|\le C\sqrt{b\log(2+\beta)/\beta},\qquad
 \max_p |(dX)_p|\le C\sqrt{\log(2+\beta)/\beta}.
\]
On this event the same product expansion bounds the difference of the two actions multiplied by \(\beta\), per plaquette, by \(C[b(\log(2+\beta))^{3/2}/\sqrt{\beta}+b^2(\log(2+\beta))^2/\beta]\). Each Haar Jacobian costs at most \(Cb\log(2+\beta)/\beta\) in minus log density relative to \(h_0\). The event and its probability are per box because the restricted Gaussian is a product over boxes. Adding all costs, including the boundary term per site \(C\log(2+\beta)/b\), proves the lower half. This uses Chatterjee’s box comparison as adapted to periodic time in row 1151 §14. The entropy inequality, the penalty and the probabilistic lower comparison are the changes here.

## The thermal sum in the range controlled by (3)

**PROVED.** At the physical time \(T=n/2\), with \(\ell_\beta=\log(2+\beta)\),
\[
 0\le J_T\le C N\beta^{-3/16}\ell_\beta^{9/8}.                             \tag{4}
\]
At every sequence of periods \(m\to\infty\) with \(m^4\varepsilon_\beta\to0\), the actual homogeneous Wilson transfer satisfies
\[
 \frac{m^3}{N}\,J_m\longrightarrow\frac{\pi^2}{8},\qquad
 \frac{m^3}{N}\log\operatorname{Tr}[(F/\|F\|)^m]
       \longrightarrow\frac{\pi^2}{15}.                                   \tag{5}
\]
Here \(n\ge2(b+1)^2\). On the physical grid this time window has vanishing physical duration. The constants count the two transverse polarizations in each of three colours. These are limits of the Wilson traces, including the temporal holonomy and the vertex projection.

**PROVED.** For even \(m,\,2m\le b\), subtract the two applications of (3). Per-step Gaussian scalar factors cancel. To see the remaining factors, integrate the rooted temporal vertex variables on each step to project the spatial increment orthogonally off gradients. The Gaussian normalizer of that integration is identical on each step. Subtracting a unique based gradient sets any spatial field to zero on the tree, so the map from tree-fixed fields to this transverse space is a linear bijection with another per-step Jacobian. The resulting periodic quadratic form on a transverse mode of curl-square \(\lambda\) has eigenvalues \(\lambda+2-2\cos(2\pi h/m)\). Their product is \(e^{m\omega}(1-e^{-m\omega})^2\), with \(2\cosh\omega=2+\lambda\), by factoring over the \(m\)-th roots of unity. Thus, writing \(f(x)=\log[(1+e^{-x})/(1-e^{-x})]\),
\[
 \left|J_m-\sum_B3\sum_{q\ne0}(r(q)-1)f(m\omega_q)\right|
             \le C N m\varepsilon_\beta .                                  \tag{6}
\]
For box sides \(b_i\) the indices are \(0\le q_i<b_i\), \(r(q)=\#\{i:q_i>0\}\), and \(\lambda_q=4\sum_i\sin^2(\pi q_i/(2b_i))\). In one direction the vertex cosine basis and its nonzero gradients give the edge basis. On their tensor products the gradient is the vector with entries \(2\sin(\pi q_i/(2b_i))\) in the \(r(q)\) available polarizations, and curl is exterior multiplication by that vector up to choices of basis signs. Its squared norm on the orthogonal complement is \(\lambda_q\). This gives the multiplicity in (6), including at the coordinate planes. The Gaussian determinant calculation is from row 1151 §14.

**PROVED.** The shell bound \(\omega_q\ge c|q|_\infty/b\) and at most \(Ck^2\) indices at radius \(k\ge1\) give \(\sum f(m\omega_q)\le C(b/m)^3\). Indeed \(f(x)=2\sum_{h\ {\rm odd}}e^{-hx}/h\) and \(\sum_{k\ge1}k^2e^{-yk}\le C y^{-3}\). The latter follows from the geometric-series derivative and the limits at zero and infinity. The eigenvalue expression \(J_s=2g_s-g_{2s}\), \(g_s=\log\operatorname{Tr}[(F/\|F\|)^s]\), is decreasing because convexity of \(g\) gives \(J_s'=2(g_s'-g_{2s}')\le0\) for \(s>1\). Also \(0\le g_s\le J_s\le2g_s\). Use (6) at the even integer \(m\) nearest \(\varepsilon_\beta^{-1/4}\). This gives \(J_T/N\le C\varepsilon_\beta^{3/4}\) and proves (4).

**PROVED.** For \(m\to\infty\), \(m/b\to0\), the sum in (6), divided by \(N/m^3\), tends to
\[
           \frac{6}{(2\pi)^3}\int_{\mathbb R^3}f(|u|)\,du=\frac{\pi^2}{8}.
\]
Use \(u_i=\pi m q_i/b_i\). On a compact annulus in the positive octant the dispersion tends uniformly to \(|u|\), and the three-dimensional Riemann sums converge there. Indices on its coordinate planes have vanishing relative weight. For \(|u|<\delta\) the omitted normalized sum is bounded by \(C\delta^3(1+|\log\delta|)+o(1)\), by shell counting and \(f(x)\le C(1+|\log x|)\) near zero. For \(|u|>R\) the shell bound gives an exponentially small tail in \(R\) times a quadratic polynomial. These bounds are uniform in the \(b\) or \(b+1\) choices. For the integral, radial integration of the odd series gives \(16\pi\sum_{h\ {\rm odd}}h^{-4}\). For example, Parseval on \(x^2=\pi^2/3+4\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^k\cos(kx)/k^2\) on \([-\pi,\pi]\) gives \(\sum k^{-4}=\pi^4/90\), where the coefficients follow by two integrations by parts. The normalized error in (6) is \(O(m^4\varepsilon_\beta)\). Monotonicity between adjacent even integers covers odd or real periods tending to infinity. Finally iterate \(g_m=J_m/2+g_{2m}/2\) a fixed \(h\) times. The last term is at most \(2^{-h}J_m\), and at each fixed dilation the first limit in (5) applies. Letting \(h\to\infty\) multiplies \(\pi^2/8\) by \(\sum_{k\ge0}2^{-k-1}2^{-3k}=8/15\), giving its second limit.

## What survives on cutting the spatial boxes apart

**PROVED.** Let \(\bar Z_s\) use the same torus with every plaquette crossing between the spatial boxes removed. Let \(E_\partial\) be the number of spatial joining bonds per slice. For **every** integer \(s\ge2\), every such nontrivial tiling with at least two intervals per direction and all sufficiently large \(\beta\),
\[
 \log\bar Z_s-\log Z_s
       \ \ge\ E_\partial(s-1)\big[\tfrac32\log\beta-C\big].               \tag{7}
\]
There is no small-chart or upper time restriction in (7). This gives an actual loss in the Wilson free energy when the spatial interfaces are dropped. If \(m\) periods are used to bound \(J_T\), the bound \(2\log\bar Z_m-\log Z_{2m}\), even with the **exact** second trace, exceeds \(J_m\) by at least twice (7). Since \(E_\partial\ge c n^2\), this procedure cannot give a bounded total at any \(2\le m\le T\).

**PROVED.** Write \(\mu_s,\nu_s\) for the full and cut laws on the original links. The density ratio gives
\[
 \log\bar Z_s-\log Z_s
      =\beta{\mathbb E}_{\mu_s} A_\partial+D(\mu_s\Vert\nu_s),
\]
where \(A_\partial\) sums the deleted actions. In the cut law every joining link on every time slice is independent Haar, independent of all internal links. Along each joining spatial bond choose its temporal plaquettes on steps \(0,\ldots,s-2\). Let \(B_0\) sum their actions. Conditional on the internal links, successively integrate the joining link at the free end of each resulting time path. Haar invariance contributes \(z_\beta=\int e^{-\beta(1-w)}\,dg\) per plaquette, even with arbitrary fixed temporal links at both ends. Distinct paths have disjoint joining links. Thus \({\mathbb E}_{\nu_s}e^{-\beta B_0}=z_\beta^{(s-1)E_\partial}\) exactly. By the variational inequality for entropy, \(D(\mu_s\Vert\nu_s)\ge-\beta{\mathbb E}_{\mu_s}B_0-(s-1)E_\partial\log z_\beta\). Because \(B_0\le A_\partial\), the two action terms even have the favourable sign. Finally \(z_\beta\le C\beta^{-3/2}\) by the Haar angle density \(2\sin^2r/\pi\) and \(1-\cos r\ge2r^2/\pi^2\) on \([0,\pi]\). This proves (7). Equivalently, one may integrate just the selected crossing plaquettes in the cut law and drop the others. The argument uses the physical joining bonds.

**PROVED (entropy upper bound in (3)).** Even before the Gaussian estimate, the bound obtained by discarding crossing actions in the actual entropy variational expression loses at least \(c s E_\partial\). For a link in any chosen plaquette the full conditional is proportional to \(e^{v\,\hat u\cdot U}\) on \(S^3\), with \(0\le v\le6\beta\). The other three links specify a unit vector for the plaquette trace. Its mean in that direction is at most the radial mean at \(6\beta\), because the derivative of the radial mean is a variance. The radial mean at \(6\beta\) is at most \(1-c/\beta\) because the angular interval \([\beta^{-1/2},2\beta^{-1/2}]\) has probability bounded below by a constant, from the same Haar calculation and the upper bound on \(z_{6\beta}\). Thus \(\beta{\mathbb E}a_p\ge c\) for each of the \(sE_\partial\) crossing temporal plaquettes. Discarding them costs their full mean in that unsummed upper bound. The result applies to that particular variational step even if the actual box marginals were known exactly. In the signed problem, terms of order \(s E_\partial\) can cancel between \(s\) and \(2s\), so applying (7) requires control of the difference to bound \(J_T\).

The sum of \(A_t-B_t\) is the increment of \((I+P)f\), and the two endpoint values of \((I-P)f\) have \(L^4\)-norm at most \(L\). For completeness, a scalar sum \(S_k\) of martingale differences of \(L^4\)-norm at most \(d\) satisfies \({\mathbb E}S_k^4\le C_0 k^2d^4\). In the expansion of \((S_k+D)^4\) the cubic-linear term vanishes and the remaining increments are bounded by \(6({\mathbb E}S_k^4)^{1/2}d^2+4({\mathbb E}S_k^4)^{1/4}d^3+d^4\). A fixed large \(C_0\) closes induction. Apply this to the four real coordinates in each time direction and use the triangle inequality for the two endpoints. Thus \(\|f(X_k)-f(X_0)\|_{L^4}\le C\sqrt{k}\,L\), proving (21).

**PROVED (finite-period obstruction to that argument).** A cyclic path of a symmetric positive kernel \(K\) and period \(s\) has conditional next-step density proportional to \(K(x_t,y)K^{s-t-1}(y,x_0)\) given its past through \(x_t\). Equality with its homogeneous vacuum transition \(K(x_t,y)\psi(y)/[\lambda\psi(x_t)]\), for all initial and current fields, requires \(K^{s-t-1}(\cdot,x_0)\) to be proportional to \(\psi\) for every \(x_0\), by strict positivity of \(K\). For \(s-t-1\ge1\) this requires rank one. The projected homogeneous Wilson transfer has infinitely many positive eigenvalues. Indeed its positive electric Fourier multipliers are nonzero in every representation, and its magnetic multiplier is boundedly invertible at finite volume. This applies already to the scalar periodic chain. On the short periods of (5), the physical trace state assigns vacuum probability \(e^{-g_m}\to0\). The reversed martingale calculation proves (21) for the vacuum, while applying it on the original periodic ensemble would require an additional estimate on the bridge factor. Neither (2) nor comparison with the vacuum alone bounds that error.

**PROVED (one-sided Wilson pressure bound).** The odd Taylor term *can* be removed in the lower comparison, unconditionally. With the definitions of (3) for boxes now of width \(b\), for \(2\le s\le b\), \(n\ge2(b+1)^2\), and \(2\le b\le\beta^{1/2}\), one has, for sufficiently large \(\beta\),
\[
 \log Z_s\ \ge\ \sum_B\log G_{B,s}
   -C Ns\left(\frac{\log(2+\beta)}b+\frac{b^2}{\beta}\right).               \tag{22}
\]
In particular \(b=\lfloor[\beta\log(2+\beta)]^{1/3}\rfloor\) gives a lower error per site \(O(\beta^{-1/3}[\log(2+\beta)]^{2/3})\). Pin the interfaces and wrapping root as for (3). The determinant comparison still costs \(Csb^2\log(2+\beta)\) per box by its polynomial eigenvalue bounds. In the resulting centered Gaussian take the event \(\max_e|X_e|\le c_*\) for a fixed small absolute \(c_*\). Its probability is at least \(1/2\) uniformly in the given range, since there are \(O(b^4)\) coordinates with variance at most \(Cb/\beta\). On this event the analytic function \(a_p=(1-\operatorname{tr}U_p/2)\) equals \(\tfrac12|(dX)_p|^2\) plus a homogeneous cubic \(P_{3,p}(X)\) and a remainder bounded by \(C\sum_{e\in p}|X_e|^4\) by Taylor expansion on the fixed four-link chart. The Haar log Jacobian is bounded below by \(-C\sum_e |X_e|^2\). Use Jensen for the exponential of the difference of actions under the Gaussian conditioned on this event. Its symmetry under the simultaneous sign change of all \(X_e\) makes each conditional cubic expectation zero. Its conditional quadratic and fourth moments are at most twice their unconditioned values, which are \(O(b/\beta)\) and \(O(b^2/\beta^2)\) per link. The cost of the event itself is at most \(\log 2\) per box. These bounds prove (22), including the interface restrictions of the earlier lower proof.

**PROVED (remaining loss in this comparison).** In the upper proof the conditioning and the penalty are paid in a Wilson marginal. The noncommuting product of four link exponentials need not have the same trace after all four \(X_e\) change sign with their order held fixed. For example take the first three exponentials to be the unit quaternions \(i,j,k\) and the last to be one, using the oriented entries of the product. The traces have opposite signs after simultaneous inversion. The local Taylor series already distinguishes these orders by its triple product, seen also for arbitrarily small rotations about these three axes. Thus the Gaussian symmetry used in (22) cannot be transferred to the Wilson marginal merely by an inversion of every chart coordinate. Even a postulated fourth moment \(C b^2/\beta^2\) would leave the absolute cubic estimate \(C b/\sqrt\beta\) per site by Cauchy–Schwarz on \(\beta q_p Q_e^2\). Balancing this estimate with the boundary term \(1/b\) leaves the exponent \(1/4\) in (3). Formula (22) strengthens the denominator estimate on its own. The interface free energies in (7) still enter the two periods separately.

**PROVED (sharper endpoint bound).** At \(b=\lfloor\beta^{1/4}\rfloor\), (22) improves the *two-sided* bound (3) to
\[
 \left|\log Z_s-\sum_B\log G_{B,s}\right|\le C Ns\,e_\beta,\qquad
 e_\beta=\beta^{-1/4}\log(2+\beta).
\]
Indeed the upper side of (3) already costs at most \(C[b\sqrt{\log(2+\beta)/\beta}+b^3\log(2+\beta)/\beta]\) per site besides the negligible bad-set and binary entropy terms. Applying (6) at an even \(m\) nearest \(e_\beta^{-1/4}\) now gives
\[
             0\le J_T\le C n^3\beta^{-3/16}[\log(2+\beta)]^{3/4}.           \tag{23}
\]
Both limits in (5) and the counting result (8) consequently hold throughout \(m\to\infty,\ m^4 e_\beta\to0\). The earlier, weaker logarithm remains sufficient for (11)–(20). Even if a comparable improvement held on the upper side at the larger box size of (22), (5) forbids a bounded short-period total anywhere in its stated window.

## Which energies are left at the homogeneous endpoint

**PROVED.** Write \(E_i=-\log(\lambda_i/\lambda_0)\), with \(E_0=0\). For the homogeneous endpoint one can use a smaller projector than for arbitrary boundary fields. Let \(m\) be the even comparison time used in (23), and \(R_0=C N e_\beta^{3/4}\) with the constant fixed large enough that \(g_m\le R_0\). Set
\[
 E_\circ=\frac{R_0+7\log n}{T-m},\qquad
 S_k^\circ=\sum_{0<E_i\le E_\circ}e^{-kTE_i}\quad(k=1,2).
\]
For both traces the omitted normalized sum is at most \(e^{g_m-(T-m)E_\circ}\le n^{-7}\). Therefore
\[
 J_T=2\log(1+S_1^\circ)-\log(1+S_2^\circ)+O(n^{-7}).                    \tag{24}
\]
The corresponding physical subspace has dimension at least
\(\exp(c R_0\log\beta/[T(1+\beta)])\) by (13), taking \(l\) to be a sufficiently small constant times \(R_0/[T(1+\beta)]\), rounded down. In particular the dimension diverges on the stipulated cutoff trajectory. The total is bounded exactly when \(S_1^\circ=O(1)\), since \(0\le S_2^\circ\le S_1^\circ\). The strict wall threshold still uses the ratio in (24).

**PROVED (using the exact first gap for every short-period state also fails).** Put \(\Delta=E_1\), the actual first excitation of the periodic Wilson cube. A frequently available spectral bound is
\[
 \sum_{i>0}e^{-TE_i}\le e^{-(T-m)\Delta}(e^{g_m}-1).
\]
For **every** \(m\to\infty\) with \(m^4e_\beta\to0\), the right side diverges even when both inputs \(\Delta,g_m\) are exact. Indeed (13) at \(l=1\) implies \(\Delta\le C(1+\beta)\) for all large \(\beta\), whereas (5) gives \(g_m\sim(\pi^2/15)n^3/m^3\). On the physical grid \(T(1+\beta)=o(n^3/m^3)\). This proves divergence of this particular upper estimate for the Wilson model, independently of whether its gap in *physical* units is positive. The high multiplicity measured at short periods must be suppressed according to the energies of the states that carry it. Applying the slowest decay rate to the whole measured multiplicity loses precisely this distinction.

**PROVED, conditional on a low-energy count.** For example let \(D_j(x)=\#\{i>0:TE_i\le\min(x,TE_\circ)\}\). If \(D_j(x)=0\) for \(x<X\) and \(D_j(x)\le K e^{\eta(x-X)}\) for \(X\le x\le TE_\circ\), with constants \(K<\infty\), \(0\le\eta<1\), then \(S_1^\circ\le K e^{-X}/(1-\eta)\). The same count bound holds above \(TE_\circ\) by the cap, and integration of \(e^{-x}D_j(x)\) on \([0,\infty)\) counts each state with exactly its thermal weight. Taking \(X=0\) would already give a bounded total. If \(2\log(1+K e^{-X}/(1-\eta))<\log(1651/1650)\) uniformly at one chosen \(L\), (24) supplies the strict wall test. The counting asymptotic (8) concerns \(E\) of order \(N/m^4\). In its window \(E_\circ/(N/m^4)=O(e_\beta^{3/4}m^4/T)=o(1)\) on the physical trajectory, so that asymptotic gives no such upper estimate at these energies.
