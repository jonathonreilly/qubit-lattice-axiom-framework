## Hand controls, before the counterterm inference

**T1: HOLDS.** For \(\mu>0\), let
\(T_n=(\mu^2+2)I-S-S^{-1}\) on the n-site cycle, n >= 1, and
\(2\cosh\theta=\mu^2+2\), theta > 0.
Write r = exp(-theta), 0 < r < 1. On the complexified
cycle the shift has the n-th roots of unity as eigenvalues. The real
positive determinant is

\[
 \det T_n=e^{n\theta}(1-r^n)^2,\qquad
 w(n):=\log\det T_n-n\theta=2\log(1-r^n).
\]

This follows by applying the polynomial identity
\(\prod_{\omega^n=1}(z-\omega)=z^n-1\) to both factors of
\(e^{-\theta}(e^\theta-\omega)(e^\theta-\omega^{-1})\).
No numerical spectrum was computed. Integration of \((1-s)^{-1}\) on
\([0,x]\) gives \(-\log(1-x)\le x/(1-x)\). Thus, for n >= n',

\[
 \left|\frac{\log\det T_n}{n}-
             \frac{\log\det T_{n'}}{n'}\right|
 \le \frac{4e^{-n'\theta}}{n'(1-e^{-n'\theta})}.
\]

Multiplication by n gives the *total extensive-subtraction* estimate.
It is not independence of the unscaled totals. For a four-dimensional
control, use n^3 independent copies on a cubic n-torus: multiply
\(\log\det T_n\) by n^3 and use volume n^4. The identical density bound
then gives a volume times an exponential tail. This verifies the scaling
and the need for a positive step gap; it does not identify the physical
step determinant. At fixed n, \(1-e^{-n\theta}\sim n\theta\), so the
wrapping remainder diverges as theta decreases to zero. Row 993's
counterexample and row 1058's identity are consistent. Calling that limit
“the model of D” is an analogy, not a computation of the SU(2) residual.

## WT: HOLDS as an implication, with the convention stated

Let A_m denote either a family satisfying literal (a)-(c), or the full
H_m with the corrected itemisation and actual-cell averaging just
specified. Fix K and the common run. Put \(\ell'=2L^{m'}\), m >= m',
\(u=K-1-j\), and \(D_j=\ell'L^u/(4M)-2\). Explicit per-cell constants
have equal density. At each root, the E tail on each torus is at most
\(E_0K_*e^{-\kappa D_j/2}\), and the R tail is at most
\(K_*e^{-\kappa D_j/2}\). There are \(L^{4u}\) roots per physical
unit volume. Define
\(\delta(\ell'):=2(E_0+1)K_*e^\kappa
\sum_{u\ge0}L^{4u}e^{-\kappa\ell'L^u/(8M)}\).
Thus

\[
 \left|\frac{A_m}{V_m}-\frac{A_{m'}}{V_{m'}}\right|
 \le 2(E_0+1)K_*e^\kappa
       \sum_{u=0}^{K-1}L^{4u}e^{-tL^u}
 \le\delta(\ell'),\quad t=\frac{\kappa\ell'}{8M}.
\]

This is the account's proof, with the source's smaller side retained.
The infinite sum converges for every t>0: its consecutive-term ratio is
\(L^4e^{-t(L-1)L^u}\), eventually at most 1/2. If
\(t\ge2\log L+1/2\) and L>=3, the ratio is already at most
\(e^{-1}<1/2\) at u=0. Summing the geometric majorant gives exactly

\[
 \delta(\ell')\le4e^\kappa(E_0+1)K_*e^{-\kappa\ell'/(8M)},\qquad
 |A_m-L^{4(m-m')}A_{m'}|\le V_m\delta(\ell').
\]

All terms are nonnegative majorants, so replacing the finite sum by the
infinite sum is uniform in K and the bare coupling. For fixed K, the
same estimate makes \(A_m/V_m\) Cauchy as m increases. Completeness of
the real numbers gives a limit a_inf, and taking the larger exponent
to infinity gives \(|A_m-a_\infty V_m|\le V_m\delta(2L^m)\).
K=0 has empty finite sum and causes no exception. For fixed size ratio,
\(V_m\delta(\ell')\to0\) as \(\ell'\to\infty\), since an exponential
dominates the fourth power. These are hand arguments suitable for
formalization; no machine estimate has been used.

The simplified exponential requires its displayed t condition. At the
floor it suffices to impose
\(\kappa\ge(8\log L+2)/L^{11}\), an allowed lower bound fixed before g.
Otherwise retain the convergent series. No decimal estimate is needed
or used here. The source's threshold for the
counting bound alone was not proved here to imply this extra inequality.

site-freeness of g_j gives that of z_j. CA-I 159093-159118,
