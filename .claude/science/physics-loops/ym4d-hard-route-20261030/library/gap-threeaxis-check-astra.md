## The three-vector Hamiltonian

**Lemma S(a–e) — HOLDS for the stated reduced operators (326–362).** Take the Friedrichs form on $L^2(\mathbb R^9)$ and $g,L>0$. The following checks use the oscillator method credited to Barry Simon. The small-volume interpretation is the one credited in row 1273 to M. Lüscher, P. van Baal and J. Koller.

**(a) The constant.** Split $K$ into the six ordered terms $\tfrac14(-\Delta_{y_j}+|y_i\times y_j|^2)$. Each kinetic energy occurs twice. Each unordered potential occurs twice. For fixed $y_i$ put $\rho=|y_i|$ and take two transverse coordinates $t$ of $y_j$. In either coordinate integration by parts gives

$$
\|\partial_t f\|^2+\rho^2\|t f\|^2
=\|(\partial_t+\rho t)f\|^2+\rho\|f\|^2 .
$$

The two coordinates give $\rho/2$ per ordered term, and the longitudinal kinetic term is nonnegative. At $y_i=0$ the lower bound is zero without choosing any axis. Integrating the other variables, summing, then closing the form yields $K\ge W=\sum_i|y_i|$.

**(b) Compactness.** A unit ball in the form norm has bounded $H^1$ norm since $K\ge-\Delta/2$. Its squared $L^2$ tail on $|y|>R$ is at most $C/R$ since $W\ge |y|$. Rellich compactness on balls and this uniform tail make the whole embedding compact. The resolvent maps bounded sets into a bounded set of the form domain, so it is compact. This also justifies the min–max argument at 355–356 without presuming discrete spectrum.

Hence the interacting kernel is strictly positive. A real ground vector changing sign would give a strictly larger heat-semigroup Rayleigh quotient on taking its absolute value, above the operator norm. Thus a ground vector has one sign and the kernel makes it positive almost everywhere. Two orthogonal real ground vectors are impossible. A colour rotation preserves the positive normalized ground vector by simplicity. The invariant subspace reduces $K$ and is infinite dimensional (use radial test functions on disjoint shells). Its first excitation therefore exists and $\kappa_3=E_1^{\rm inv}-E_0\ge E_1-E_0>0$.

**(d,e) Units and sectors.** The unitary dilation $\psi(c)\mapsto a^{9/2}\psi(ay)$ with $a=g^{2/3}$ rescales the kinetic coefficient to $g^2 a^{-2}=g^{2/3}$ and the quartic one to $g^{-2}a^4=g^{2/3}$.
