## Notation and object (fixed for the whole file)

N.1. $G=\mathrm{SU}(2)$, $dg$ the normalized Haar measure. It is invariant under $g\mapsto hg$, $g\mapsto gh$ and $g\mapsto g^{-1}$: identifying $G$ with the unit sphere $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$ via $\begin{pmatrix}a&-\bar b\\ b&\bar a\end{pmatrix}\mapsto(a,b)$, left and right multiplications and inversion act as orthogonal maps of $\mathbb R^4$, and $dg$ is the normalized surface measure.

N.2. For $g\in G$: $\operatorname{tr}g\in[-2,2]$ is real, $\operatorname{tr}g^{-1}=\operatorname{tr}g$, $g^{-1}=g^*$, and the entries of $g^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar a&\bar b\\-b&a\end{pmatrix}$ are, up to sign, entries of $g$ (namely $\bar a=g_{22}$, $\bar b=-g_{12}$, $-b=-g_{21}$, $a=g_{11}$).

N.3. $\Lambda=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^3$, $N\ge3$; vertices $\mathcal V=\Lambda$, $|\mathcal V|=N^3$; positively oriented spatial links $\mathcal E=\{(x,i):x\in\Lambda,i\in\{1,2,3\}\}$, $|\mathcal E|=3N^3$. $X=G^{\mathcal E}$ with the product Haar measure $du$, a compact group and a probability space; $\mathcal H=L^2(X,du)$, $\langle f,h\rangle=\int\bar f h$.

N.4. Spatial plaquettes: for $x\in\Lambda$, $1\le i<j\le3$, $u_p=u_{(x,i)}u_{(x+e_i,j)}u_{(x+e_j,i)}^{-1}u_{(x,j)}^{-1}$; there are $3N^3$ of them. $V(u)=\beta\sum_p(1-\frac12\operatorname{tr}u_p)$, so $0\le V\le 6\beta N^3$.

N.5. Gauge group $\mathcal G=G^{\mathcal V}$ acting on $X$ by $(h\cdot u)_{(x,i)}=h(x)u_{(x,i)}h(x+e_i)^{-1}$. This is a left action, $(hk)\cdot u=h\cdot(k\cdot u)$, and each map $u\mapsto h\cdot u$ preserves $du$ (each coordinate is multiplied on both sides by fixed elements, N.1). $V(h\cdot u)=V(u)$ because $(h\cdot u)_p=h(x)u_ph(x)^{-1}$.

N.6. The space-time lattice is $\Lambda\times\mathbb Z_T$, $T\ge2$. A configuration is $U=(U(t),g_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z_T}$ with $U(t)\in X$ the spatial links of slice $t$ and $g_t\in\mathcal G$ the time-like links, $g_t(x)=U_0(x,t)$ on the link from $(x,t)$ to $(x,t+1)$. The Wilson action is $S(U)=\beta\sum_P(1-\frac12\operatorname{tr}U_P)$ over all unoriented plaquettes $P$ (orientation is irrelevant by N.2). $Z_T=\int e^{-S}\,dU$ with normalized product Haar measure on all links, $\langle F\rangle_T=Z_T^{-1}\int Fe^{-S}dU$.

N.7. One-bond kernel (SA 2196): $c(g)=e^{-\beta(1-\frac12\operatorname{tr}g)}$, so $e^{-2\beta}\le c\le1$, $c(g^{-1})=c(g)$, $c(hgh^{-1})=c(g)$. Time-like kernel $K_0(u,v)=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal E}c(u_\ell v_\ell^{-1})$, real, symmetric, $e^{-6\beta N^3}\le K_0\le1$, and $K_0(h\cdot u,h\cdot v)=K_0(u,v)$ since $(h\cdot u)_\ell(h\cdot v)_\ell^{-1}=h(x)u_\ell v_\ell^{-1}h(x)^{-1}$.

N.8. Constants used throughout: $\kappa_-:=e^{-12\beta N^3}$, $\kappa_+:=1$.

## A. Four general lemmas (rebuilt, used below)

Throughout this section $(Y,\mu)$ is a compact metric space with a Borel probability measure, $\mathcal K=L^2(Y,\mu)$, and an ONB is any orthonormal basis $(e_n)_{n\in\mathcal N}$ (any index set; all sums below have nonnegative terms or are absolutely convergent, so the index set is immaterial).

**Lemma A0 (Hilbert-Schmidt calculus).** For a bounded operator $S$ on a Hilbert space put $\|S\|_{HS}^2=\sum_n\|Se_n\|^2\in[0,\infty]$.
(a) This does not depend on the ONB, and $\|S\|_{HS}=\|S^*\|_{HS}$.
(b) If $\|S_1\|_{HS},\|S_2\|_{HS}<\infty$ then $\sum_n\langle S_1e_n,S_2e_n\rangle$ converges absolutely to a number $\langle S_1,S_2\rangle_{HS}$ independent of the ONB. Define $\operatorname{Tr}(S_1^*S_2):=\langle S_1,S_2\rangle_{HS}=\sum_n\langle e_n,S_1^*S_2e_n\rangle$.
(c) $\|S\|\le\|S\|_{HS}$, $\|BS\|_{HS}\le\|B\|\|S\|_{HS}$, $\|SB\|_{HS}\le\|B\|\|S\|_{HS}$ for bounded $B$.
(d) If $\|S\|_{HS}<\infty$, $S$ is compact.
(e) If $k\in L^2(Y\times Y)$, the integral operator $(S_kf)(y)=\int k(y,z)f(z)\,d\mu(z)$ satisfies $\|S_k\|_{HS}=\|k\|_{L^2}$; $S_k^*=S_{k^*}$ with $k^*(y,z)=\overline{k(z,y)}$; $S_kS_l=S_{k\circ l}$ with $(k\circ l)(y,w)=\int k(y,z)l(z,w)d\mu(z)$; and $\operatorname{Tr}(S_kS_l)=\iint k(y,z)l(z,y)\,d\mu(y)d\mu(z)$.

Proof. A0.1. For two ONBs $(e_n)$, $(f_m)$: $\sum_n\|Se_n\|^2=\sum_n\sum_m|\langle f_m,Se_n\rangle|^2=\sum_m\sum_n|\langle S^*f_m,e_n\rangle|^2=\sum_m\|S^*f_m\|^2$ (Parseval twice, Tonelli). The right side does not involve $(e_n)$, which gives the independence; taking $f=e$ gives $\|S\|_{HS}=\|S^*\|_{HS}$.
A0.2. (b): $|\langle S_1e_n,S_2e_n\rangle|\le\frac12(\|S_1e_n\|^2+\|S_2e_n\|^2)$ gives absolute convergence. For a second ONB $(f_m)$, Parseval in the form $\langle y,x\rangle=\sum_m\langle y,f_m\rangle\langle f_m,x\rangle$ gives $\sum_n\langle S_1e_n,S_2e_n\rangle=\sum_n\sum_m\langle S_1e_n,f_m\rangle\langle f_m,S_2e_n\rangle=\sum_m\sum_n\langle S_2^*f_m,e_n\rangle\langle e_n,S_1^*f_m\rangle=\sum_m\langle S_2^*f_m,S_1^*f_m\rangle$. The exchange of sums is allowed because $\sum_{n,m}|\langle S_1e_n,f_m\rangle||\langle f_m,S_2e_n\rangle|\le\frac12(\|S_1\|_{HS}^2+\|S_2\|_{HS}^2)$. The last expression does not involve $(e_n)$.
A0.3. (c): for a unit vector $f$ complete it to an ONB $(f,e_2,\dots)$; then $\|Sf\|^2\le\|S\|_{HS}^2$. $\|BSe_n\|\le\|B\|\|Se_n\|$ gives the second; the third follows from the second and (a): $\|SB\|_{HS}=\|B^*S^*\|_{HS}$.
A0.4. (d): let $Q_F$ be the orthogonal projection on the span of a finite set $F\subset\mathcal N$. $SQ_F$ has finite rank and $\|S-SQ_F\|^2\le\|S(1-Q_F)\|_{HS}^2=\sum_{n\notin F}\|Se_n\|^2$, which is small for suitable finite $F$ because the full sum is finite. A norm limit of finite-rank operators is compact (a bounded sequence has, for each finite-rank approximant, a subsequence whose images converge; a diagonal subsequence is Cauchy).
A0.5. (e): by Fubini, $k(y,\cdot)\in L^2$ for a.e. $y$, and $(S_ke_n)(y)=\langle\overline{k(y,\cdot)},e_n\rangle$. Parseval gives $\sum_n|(S_ke_n)(y)|^2=\int|k(y,z)|^2d\mu(z)$; integrating in $y$ (Tonelli) gives $\|S_k\|_{HS}^2=\|k\|_2^2$. The adjoint formula is Fubini on $\iint\bar f(y)k(y,z)h(z)$, absolutely integrable because $|k|\,|f|\,|h|$ is integrable by Cauchy-Schwarz on $Y\times Y$ ($\iint|k||f(y)||h(z)|\le\|k\|_2\|f\|_2\|h\|_2$). The composition formula is Fubini with the bound $\iiint|k(y,z)||l(z,w)||f(w)|\le\|k\|_2\|l\|_2\|f\|_2$ (Cauchy-Schwarz in $w$, then in $z$, then in $y$, $\mu$ a probability). For the trace: by (b) with $S_1=S_k^*=S_{k^*}$, $S_2=S_l$, and the polarized form of the identity $\|S_m\|_{HS}^2=\|m\|_2^2$, $\operatorname{Tr}(S_kS_l)=\langle S_{k^*},S_l\rangle_{HS}=\langle k^*,l\rangle_{L^2(Y\times Y)}=\iint k(z,y)\,l(y,z)$. Renaming variables gives the claim. ∎

**Definition A1.** A bounded operator $R\ge0$ is *trace class* with trace $\operatorname{Tr}R$ if $R=S^*S$ with $\|S\|_{HS}<\infty$; then $\operatorname{Tr}R=\sum_n\langle e_n,Re_n\rangle=\|S\|_{HS}^2$, independent of the ONB by A0.1. (For compact $R\ge0$ this is the same as summability of the eigenvalues with multiplicity, by Lemma A2(iv).)

**Lemma A2 (spectral theorem for compact positive operators).** Let $R\ne0$ be a compact self-adjoint operator with $R\ge0$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H_1$.
(i) $\|R\|=\sup_{\|f\|=1}\langle f,Rf\rangle$, and there is a unit $\varphi_0$ with $R\varphi_0=\|R\|\varphi_0$.
(ii) There is a finite or infinite orthonormal sequence $(\varphi_j)$ and numbers $\lambda_0\ge\lambda_1\ge\dots>0$, with $\lambda_j\to0$ if the sequence is infinite, such that $Rf=\sum_j\lambda_j\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle\varphi_j$ for all $f$ and $R=0$ on $\{\varphi_j\}^\perp$.
(iii) Every $\lambda>0$ with $\ker(R-\lambda)\ne0$ is some $\lambda_j$, and $\ker(R-\lambda)=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_j:\lambda_j=\lambda\}$, of finite dimension. The spectrum $\sigma(R)$ is contained in $\{\lambda_j\}\cup\{0\}$. In particular $\lambda_{j}=\sup\{\langle f,Rf\rangle:\|f\|=1,f\perp\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{j-1}\}$.
(iv) $\sum_n\langle e_n,Re_n\rangle=\sum_j\lambda_j$ for every ONB (both possibly $+\infty$).

Proof. A2.1. Let $s=\sup_{\|f\|=1}\langle f,Rf\rangle\le\|R\|$. The form $(f,h)\mapsto\langle f,Rh\rangle$ is positive semidefinite, so Cauchy-Schwarz gives $|\langle f,Rh\rangle|^2\le\langle f,Rf\rangle\langle h,Rh\rangle\le s^2$ for unit $f,h$; hence $\|R\|\le s$, so $s=\|R\|>0$.
A2.2. Take unit $f_n$ with $\langle f_n,Rf_n\rangle\to s$. Then $\|Rf_n-sf_n\|^2=\|Rf_n\|^2-2s\langle f_n,Rf_n\rangle+s^2\le2s^2-2s\langle f_n,Rf_n\rangle\to0$. By compactness a subsequence has $Rf_n\to y$; then $sf_n\to y$, so $\|y\|=s$, $f_n\to\varphi_0:=y/s$ and $R\varphi_0=\lim Rf_n=y=s\varphi_0$. This is (i).
A2.3. $\mathcal H_2=\{\varphi_0\}^\perp$ is invariant ($\langle\varphi_0,Rf\rangle=\langle R\varphi_0,f\rangle=s\langle\varphi_0,f\rangle$). $R|_{\mathcal H_2}$ is compact, self-adjoint, $\ge0$. If it is zero stop; otherwise apply A2.2 to it. Inductively this gives orthonormal $\varphi_j$ with $\lambda_j=\|R|_{\mathcal H_{j+1}}\|$ nonincreasing, $\mathcal H_{j+1}=\{\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{j-1}\}^\perp$.
A2.4. If the sequence is infinite and $\lambda_j\ge\delta>0$ for all $j$, then $\|R\varphi_j-R\varphi_k\|^2=\lambda_j^2+\lambda_k^2\ge2\delta^2$ for $j\ne k$, so $(R\varphi_j)$ has no convergent subsequence, contradicting compactness. So $\lambda_j\to0$.
A2.5. On $\mathcal H_\infty=\{\varphi_j\}^\perp$: for $f\in\mathcal H_\infty$, $\langle f,Rf\rangle\le\lambda_j\|f\|^2$ for every $j$ (or $=0$ if the sequence stopped), so $\langle f,Rf\rangle=0$, and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality of A2.1 restricted to the invariant space $\mathcal H_\infty$ (invariant because $R$ is self-adjoint and leaves each $\varphi_j$'s span invariant), $Rf=0$. Writing $f=\sum_j\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle\varphi_j+f_\infty$ gives (ii).
A2.6. (iii): if $Rf=\lambda f$ with $\lambda>0$ then by (ii) $(\lambda_j-\lambda)\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle=0$ for all $j$ and $\lambda f_\infty=0$. So $f\in\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_j:\lambda_j=\lambda\}$, a finite set of $j$ because $\lambda_j\to0$. For $z\notin\{\lambda_j\}\cup\{0\}$, $d=\inf_j|\lambda_j-z|>0$ (the $\lambda_j$ accumulate only at 0), and $f\mapsto\sum_j(\lambda_j-z)^{-1}\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle\varphi_j-z^{-1}f_\infty$ is a bounded two-sided inverse of $R-z$. The sup formula follows from (ii) applied to $f\perp\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{j-1}$: $\langle f,Rf\rangle=\sum_{i\ge j}\lambda_i|\langle\varphi_i,f\rangle|^2\le\lambda_j\|f\|^2$, with equality at $f=\varphi_j$.
A2.7. (iv): $\langle e_n,Re_n\rangle=\sum_j\lambda_j|\langle\varphi_j,e_n\rangle|^2$; sum over $n$ (Tonelli) and use Parseval $\sum_n|\langle\varphi_j,e_n\rangle|^2=1$. ∎

**Lemma A3 (Jentzsch's theorem, rebuilt; credit R. Jentzsch 1912, not read).** Let $k$ be a real, symmetric, continuous function on $Y\times Y$ with $0<\kappa_1\le k\le\kappa_2$, and let the integral operator $K=S_k$ on $\mathcal K$ satisfy $K\ge0$. Put $\lambda_0=\|K\|$.
(a) $\kappa_1\le\lambda_0\le\kappa_2$, and $K$ is compact.
(b) (positivity improving) If $f\ge0$ a.e. and $f\ne0$ in $\mathcal K$, then $(Kf)(y)\ge\kappa_1\int f\,d\mu>0$ for every $y$.
(c) $\lambda_0$ is an eigenvalue with an eigenfunction $\Omega$ that has a continuous representative with $\kappa_1/\kappa_2\le\Omega(y)\le\kappa_2/\kappa_1$ for all $y$, $\|\Omega\|_2=1$.
(d) (gap with explicit constant) For every $\varphi\in\mathcal K$ with $\langle\Omega,\varphi\rangle=0$: $\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$, with $\delta:=\inf_{y,z}\frac{k(y,z)}{\lambda_0\Omega(y)\Omega(z)}\ge\frac{\kappa_1\lambda_0}{\kappa_2^2}\ge\frac{\kappa_1^2}{\kappa_2^2}$.
(e) $\ker(K-\lambda_0)=\operatorname{span}\{\Omega\}$: the eigenvalue $\lambda_0$ is simple.

Proof. A3.1. (a): $\lambda_0\le\|k\|_{L^2}\le\kappa_2$ by A0.5 and A0.3; $\lambda_0\ge\langle1,K1\rangle=\iint k\ge\kappa_1$ (A2.1, $\|1\|=1$). Compact by A0.4, A0.5.
A3.2. (b): $(Kf)(y)=\int k(y,z)f(z)\,d\mu(z)\ge\kappa_1\int f$, and $\int f>0$ since $f\ge0$ is not a.e. zero.
A3.3. Existence of a nonnegative maximizer. By A2.2 there is a unit $\varphi$ with $K\varphi=\lambda_0\varphi$. Since $k$ is real, $K\bar\varphi=\lambda_0\bar\varphi$, so $\operatorname{Re}\varphi$ or $\operatorname{Im}\varphi$ is a nonzero real eigenfunction; normalize it and call it $\varphi$. Then $\lambda_0=\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle=\iint k(y,z)\varphi(y)\varphi(z)\le\iint k(y,z)|\varphi(y)||\varphi(z)|=\langle|\varphi|,K|\varphi|\rangle\le\lambda_0$ (A2.1). So $|\varphi|$ is a unit maximizer of the form, and A2.2's computation gives $\|K|\varphi|-\lambda_0|\varphi|\|^2\le2\lambda_0^2-2\lambda_0\langle|\varphi|,K|\varphi|\rangle=0$. Put $\Omega=|\varphi|$.
A3.4. Continuity and bounds. Define $\Omega(y):=\lambda_0^{-1}\int k(y,z)\Omega(z)d\mu(z)$ for every $y$; this equals $\Omega$ a.e. and is continuous because $k$ is uniformly continuous on the compact $Y\times Y$ and $\Omega\in L^1$. Then $\Omega(y)\le\lambda_0^{-1}\kappa_2\|\Omega\|_1\le\kappa_2/\lambda_0\le\kappa_2/\kappa_1$ ($\|\Omega\|_1\le\|\Omega\|_2=1$). Next $1=\int\Omega^2\le\sup\Omega\cdot\|\Omega\|_1\le\lambda_0^{-1}\kappa_2\|\Omega\|_1^2$, so $\|\Omega\|_1\ge(\lambda_0/\kappa_2)^{1/2}$ and $\Omega(y)\ge\lambda_0^{-1}\kappa_1\|\Omega\|_1\ge\kappa_1(\lambda_0\kappa_2)^{-1/2}\ge\kappa_1/\kappa_2$ (using $\lambda_0\le\kappa_2$). This is (c).
A3.5. (d), Doeblin's argument. Let $\nu=\Omega^2\mu$, a probability measure. The map $W:L^2(\nu)\to\mathcal K$, $Wf=\Omega f$, is unitary (isometric by definition of $\nu$, onto because $1/\Omega$ is bounded). $Q:=\lambda_0^{-1}W^{-1}KW$ acts by $(Qf)(y)=\int r(y,z)f(z)\,d\nu(z)$ with $r(y,z)=k(y,z)/(\lambda_0\Omega(y)\Omega(z))$, which is real, symmetric, continuous, and satisfies $\int r(y,z)\,d\nu(z)=(K\Omega)(y)/(\lambda_0\Omega(y))=1$ for every $y$ (A3.4). Since $r\ge\delta$ and its $\nu$-average in $z$ is $1$, $\delta\le1$. Case $\delta=1$: then $r(y,\cdot)-1\ge0$ has $\nu$-integral $0$, so $r(y,\cdot)=1$ $\nu$-a.e. for every $y$, and $Qf=\langle1,f\rangle_\nu1$. Case $\delta<1$: put $r'=(r-\delta)/(1-\delta)\ge0$, symmetric with $\int r'(y,z)d\nu(z)=1$, and $Q'$ its integral operator on $L^2(\nu)$; then $Q=\delta\,|1\rangle\langle1|_\nu+(1-\delta)Q'$ and $|\langle f,Q'f\rangle_\nu|\le\iint r'(y,z)|f(y)||f(z)|\,d\nu d\nu\le\iint r'(y,z)\frac{|f(y)|^2+|f(z)|^2}2d\nu d\nu=\|f\|_\nu^2$ (symmetry of $r'$ for the second half).
A3.6. Let $\varphi\perp\Omega$ in $\mathcal K$ and $f=W^{-1}\varphi=\varphi/\Omega$. Then $\langle1,f\rangle_\nu=\int\Omega^2(\varphi/\Omega)\,d\mu=\langle\Omega,\varphi\rangle=0$ ($\Omega$ real). So $\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle=\lambda_0\langle f,Qf\rangle_\nu$, which is $0$ in the case $\delta=1$ and $\lambda_0(1-\delta)\langle f,Q'f\rangle_\nu\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|f\|_\nu^2=\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$ in the case $\delta<1$. The bound on $\delta$: $\delta\ge\kappa_1/(\lambda_0\sup\Omega^2)\ge\kappa_1/(\lambda_0(\kappa_2/\lambda_0)^2)=\kappa_1\lambda_0/\kappa_2^2\ge\kappa_1^2/\kappa_2^2$ by A3.4 ($\sup\Omega\le\kappa_2/\lambda_0$) and A3.1.
A3.7. (e): if $K\psi=\lambda_0\psi$, write $\psi=\langle\Omega,\psi\rangle\Omega+\varphi$ with $\varphi\perp\Omega$; then $K\varphi=\lambda_0\varphi$ and $\lambda_0\|\varphi\|^2=\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$ with $\delta>0$, so $\varphi=0$. ∎

**Lemma A4 (density of continuous functions).** $C(Y)$ is dense in $L^2(Y,\mu)$.

Proof. A4.1. Let $\mathcal S$ be the class of Borel sets $E$ such that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are $F$ closed and $O$ open with $F\subset E\subset O$, $\mu(O\setminus F)<\varepsilon$. A closed $E$ is in $\mathcal S$: take $F=E$, $O_n=\{y:d(y,E)<1/n\}$; $O_n\downarrow E$, so $\mu(O_n\setminus E)\to0$ by continuity from above. $\mathcal S$ is closed under complements (swap $F\leftrightarrow O^c$, $O\leftrightarrow F^c$). For $E=\bigcup_kE_k$ with $E_k\in\mathcal S$: choose $F_k\subset E_k\subset O_k$ with $\mu(O_k\setminus F_k)<\varepsilon2^{-k-1}$, $O=\bigcup O_k$, and $K$ with $\mu(\bigcup_kF_k\setminus\bigcup_{k\le K}F_k)<\varepsilon/2$, $F=\bigcup_{k\le K}F_k$ closed; then $\mu(O\setminus F)<\varepsilon$. So $\mathcal S$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing the closed sets: all Borel sets.
A4.2. For $E$ Borel and $F\subset E\subset O$ as above ($F\ne\emptyset\ne O^c$; the degenerate cases use the constants $0$, $1$), $h(y)=d(y,O^c)/(d(y,O^c)+d(y,F))$ is continuous, $0\le h\le1$, $h=1$ on $F$, $h=0$ off $O$, so $\|1_E-h\|_2^2\le\mu(O\setminus F)<\varepsilon$.
A4.3. Finite linear combinations of indicators are dense in $L^2$: for $f\in L^2$, the functions $f_n$ obtained by rounding $\operatorname{Re}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}f$ down (toward 0) to the grid $2^{-n}\mathbb Z$ and cutting at modulus $n$ are such combinations, $|f_n|\le|f|$ and $f_n\to f$ pointwise, so $\|f-f_n\|_2\to0$ by dominated convergence. With A4.2 this proves the lemma. ∎

Convention on traces. Every trace below is $\operatorname{Tr}Z=\sum_n\langle e_n,Ze_n\rangle$ for an operator $Z$ that is written as a product $S_1^*S_2$ of two Hilbert-Schmidt operators; by A0(b) the sum converges absolutely and does not depend on the ONB, and by A0(e) it equals the kernel integral when $S_1^*$, $S_2$ are integral operators with $L^2$ kernels.

## 1. Statement (1): the transfer operator and the trace identities

**Definitions.** On $\mathcal H=L^2(X)$:
- $M$: multiplication by $e^{-V/2}$; $M_F$: multiplication by a bounded measurable $F$.
- $C_{\mathcal E}=S_{K_0}$, the integral operator with the time-like kernel $K_0$ of N.7.
- $(Pf)(u)=\int_{\mathcal G}f(h\cdot u)\,dh$, $dh$ the normalized Haar measure of $\mathcal G=G^{\mathcal V}$.
- $\mathsf T=MC_{\mathcal E}M$, the integral operator with kernel $T_0(u,v)=e^{-V(u)/2}K_0(u,v)e^{-V(v)/2}$.
- $\mathsf T_P=P\mathsf TP$, and $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}=\operatorname{Ran}P$. A function $F$ on $X$ is *gauge invariant* if $F(h\cdot u)=F(u)$ for all $h\in\mathcal G$, $u\in X$.

**Statement (1).** Let $T\ge2$.
(1a) $P$ is an orthogonal projection; $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}=\{f:f\circ h=f$ a.e. for every $h\in\mathcal G\}$; $P\mathsf T=\mathsf TP$; and $\mathsf T_P=\mathsf TP$ is the integral operator with the explicit kernel
$$A_T(u,v)=\int_{\mathcal G}T_0(u,h\cdot v)\,dh=e^{-\frac{V(u)+V(v)}2}\int_{\mathcal G}\prod_{(x,i)\in\mathcal E}\exp\Big[-\beta\Big(1-\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\big(h(x)v_{(x,i)}h(x+e_i)^{-1}u_{(x,i)}^{-1}\big)\Big)\Big]dh ,$$
which is real, symmetric, continuous, satisfies $A_T(h\cdot u,k\cdot v)=A_T(u,v)$, and $e^{-12\beta N^3}\le A_T\le1$.
(1b) $Z_T=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathsf T_P^T)=\sum_n\langle e_n,\mathsf T^Te_n\rangle$ for any ONB $(e_n)$ of $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ ("the trace of $\mathsf T^T$ composed with the projection on the gauge-invariant functions").
(1c) For bounded measurable $F_1,\dots,F_k$ on $X$ and slices $0=t_1\le t_2\le\dots\le t_k\le T-1$, with $d_j=t_{j+1}-t_j$ ($j<k$) and $d_k=T-t_k\ge1$:
$$Z_T\Big\langle\prod_{j}F_j(U(t_j))\Big\rangle_T=\operatorname{Tr}\big(M_{F_1}\mathsf T_P^{d_1}M_{F_2}\mathsf T_P^{d_2}\cdots M_{F_k}\mathsf T_P^{d_k}\big).$$
If every $F_j$ is gauge invariant, this equals $\sum_n\langle e_n,M_{F_1}\mathsf T^{d_1}\cdots M_{F_k}\mathsf T^{d_k}e_n\rangle$ over an ONB of $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$. The restriction $t_1=0$ costs nothing: the shift $t\mapsto t+1$ permutes the links, preserves the Haar measure and $S$, so $\langle\cdot\rangle_T$ is invariant under time translation.

**Proof.**
1.1. (Plaquettes.) A plaquette of $\Lambda\times\mathbb Z_T$ spans two directions. If both are spatial it is a spatial plaquette of one slice $t$, and its $U_P$ is $U(t)_p$ of N.4. If one is time, it is the plaquette based at $(x,t)$ in the plane $(0,i)$, for a unique $(x,i)\in\mathcal E$ and $t\in\mathbb Z_T$; for $T\ge2$ these $3N^3T$ plaquettes are distinct (they contain different time-like links). Its holonomy, along $(x,t)\to(x,t+1)\to(x+e_i,t+1)\to(x+e_i,t)\to(x,t)$, is $g_t(x)\,U(t+1)_{(x,i)}\,g_t(x+e_i)^{-1}\,U(t)_{(x,i)}^{-1}=(g_t\cdot U(t+1))_{(x,i)}U(t)_{(x,i)}^{-1}$.
1.2. Hence, by N.7 ($c(g^{-1})=c(g)$),
$e^{-S(U)}=\prod_{t}e^{-V(U(t))}\prod_tK_0\big(U(t),g_t\cdot U(t+1)\big)$.
Since $\prod_te^{-V(U(t))}=\prod_te^{-V(U(t))/2}e^{-V(U(t+1))/2}$ (indices mod $T$) and $V(g_t\cdot U(t+1))=V(U(t+1))$ (N.5),
$$e^{-S(U)}=\prod_{t\in\mathbb Z_T}T_0\big(U(t),g_t\cdot U(t+1)\big).\tag{1.2}$$
1.3. Each $g_t$ occurs in exactly one factor of (1.2), and all factors are bounded and continuous. For $F$ depending only on $(U(t))_t$, Fubini gives $\int F e^{-S}\prod_tdg_t=F\prod_tA_T(U(t),U(t+1))$ with $A_T$ as in (1a).
1.4. Properties of $A_T$. Continuity: $T_0(u,h\cdot v)$ is continuous on the compact $X\times\mathcal G\times X$, hence uniformly continuous, so its $h$-integral is continuous in $(u,v)$. Bounds: $e^{-V/2}\ge e^{-3\beta N^3}$ (N.4) and $K_0\ge e^{-6\beta N^3}$ (N.7) give $T_0\ge e^{-12\beta N^3}$; $T_0\le1$. Invariance: $T_0(h\cdot a,h\cdot b)=T_0(a,b)$ (N.5, N.7), so $A_T(u,k\cdot v)=\int T_0(u,(hk)\cdot v)dh=A_T(u,v)$ (right invariance of $dh$), and $A_T(h\cdot u,v)=\int T_0(u,(h^{-1}k)\cdot v)dk=A_T(u,v)$ (left invariance). Symmetry: $A_T(v,u)=\int T_0(v,h\cdot u)dh=\int T_0(h^{-1}\cdot v,u)dh=\int T_0(u,h^{-1}\cdot v)dh=A_T(u,v)$ (invariance, symmetry of $T_0$, inversion invariance of $dh$).
1.5. $P$ is an orthogonal projection: $(P^2f)(u)=\iint f(k\cdot(h\cdot u))\,dk\,dh=\iint f((kh)\cdot u)\,dk\,dh=(Pf)(u)$; $\langle Pf,g\rangle=\int dh\int\overline{f(h\cdot u)}g(u)\,du=\int dh\int\overline{f(w)}g(h^{-1}\cdot w)\,dw=\langle f,Pg\rangle$ (substitution $w=h\cdot u$, which preserves $du$, N.5; Fubini is allowed because $|f(h\cdot u)||g(u)|$ is integrable on $\mathcal G\times X$ by Cauchy-Schwarz). $\|Pf\|\le\|f\|$ by Jensen. Range: $(Pf)(k\cdot u)=\int f((hk)\cdot u)dh=(Pf)(u)$ for every $k,u$ for which the integral is defined, so $\operatorname{Ran}P$ consists of invariant functions; conversely if $f\circ h=f$ a.e. for each $h$, then $\iint|f(h\cdot u)-f(u)|\,du\,dh=0$ (Tonelli) and $Pf=f$.
1.6. $\mathsf TP=S_{A_T}$: $(\mathsf TPf)(u)=\int T_0(u,v)\int f(h\cdot v)\,dh\,dv=\int dh\int T_0(u,h^{-1}\cdot w)f(w)\,dw=\int A_T(u,w)f(w)\,dw$ (substitution $w=h\cdot v$, then inversion invariance). Since $A_T$ is real symmetric, $S_{A_T}$ is self-adjoint, so $P\mathsf T=(\mathsf TP)^*=\mathsf TP$, and $\mathsf T_P=P\mathsf TP=\mathsf TP^2=\mathsf TP$. This proves (1a).
1.7. (1b) and (1c). Integrate the formula of 1.3 over $(U(t))_t$. For (1c) with $t_1=0$, the integrand is $\prod_tA_T(U(t),U(t+1))\prod_jF_j(U(t_j))$. By A0(e) and Fubini, the operator $M_{F_1}\mathsf T_P^{d_1}\cdots M_{F_k}\mathsf T_P^{d_k-1}$ is an integral operator whose kernel is the integral of the corresponding product over the intermediate slices (it contains at least one factor $\mathsf T_P$, because $\sum_jd_j=T\ge2$; products of $M_F$ with an integral operator with bounded kernel $a$ have kernels $F(u)a(u,v)$ or $a(u,v)F(v)$). A0(e) applied to this operator and $\mathsf T_P=S_{A_T}$ gives the trace formula; (1b) is the case $k=1$, $F_1=1$. For the second form of (1b) and (1c): $\mathsf T_P^d=P\mathsf T^dP$ for $d\ge1$ and $M_{F}P=PM_{F}$ for gauge-invariant $F$ (because $(P(Ff))(u)=\int F(h\cdot u)f(h\cdot u)dh=F(u)(Pf)(u)$), so the product equals $P\,(M_{F_1}\mathsf T^{d_1}\cdots M_{F_k}\mathsf T^{d_k})\,P$; evaluate the trace in an ONB of $\mathcal H$ made of an ONB of $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ and one of $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}^\perp$; the second part contributes $0$. ∎

Remark 1.8 (temporal gauge; not used in 1.1–1.7; corrected, row 671: the earlier text had the remaining holonomy in reverse order). Put h_0=1 and h_t=g_0...g_(t-1) for 1<=t<T, pointwise at each vertex, and set W(t)=h_t dot U(t). For fixed temporal links this preserves spatial product Haar measure. Simultaneous covariance changes each slab factor to T_0(W(t),(h_t g_t h_(t+1)^(-1)) dot W(t+1)), where the last next-slice transformation is h_0. The parenthesis is 1 for t<T-1 and H=g_0...g_(T-1) for t=T-1. Thus the weight is the product of the first T-1 ordinary transfer kernels and T_0(W(T-1),H dot W(0)). Holding the first T-1 temporal layers fixed, left Haar invariance gives dH=dg_(T-1); their remaining normalized integrals equal 1. The H integral inserts one P and gives Z_T=Tr(T^T P). Invariant slice insertions are unchanged. No periodic holonomy is set to 1.

Remark 1.9 (non-invariant observables). For $f\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ and bounded $F$, $P(Ff)=(PF)f$: $(P(Ff))(u)=\int F(h\cdot u)f(h\cdot u)\,dh$, and by Fubini, for a.e. $u$, $f(h\cdot u)=f(u)$ for a.e. $h$. So $PM_FP=M_{PF}P$. If the slices $t_j$ are distinct, every $M_{F_j}$ in (1c) stands between two factors $\mathsf T_P=P\mathsf T_PP$, and (1c) is unchanged when each $F_j$ is replaced by its gauge average $PF_j$, which is gauge invariant.

## 2. Statement (2): boundedness, self-adjointness, positivity

**Statement (2).** (2a) $\mathsf T$ and $\mathsf T_P$ are bounded, self-adjoint, $\|\mathsf T_P\|\le\|\mathsf T\|\le1$. (2b) The time-like kernel $K_0$ is a positive-definite kernel on $X$: $\sum_{a,b=1}^rz_a\overline{z_b}\,K_0(u_a,u_b)\ge0$ for all $r$, $u_1,\dots,u_r\in X$, $z\in\mathbb C^r$; and $C_{\mathcal E}\ge0$ as an operator. (2c) $\mathsf T\ge0$ and $\mathsf T_P\ge0$. (2d) (characters) $c(g)=e^{-\beta}\sum_{l\in\frac12\mathbb N_0}a_l\chi_l(g)$ with $a_l\ge(\beta/2)^{2l}/(2l)!>0$, the series converging absolutely and uniformly, and each $(g,h)\mapsto\chi_l(gh^{-1})$ is a positive-definite kernel on $G$.

**Proof.**
2.1. (2a): $T_0$ and $A_T$ are real, symmetric and bounded by $1$; by A0(e) and A0(c), $\|\mathsf T\|\le\|T_0\|_{L^2}\le1$, and both operators are self-adjoint. $\|\mathsf T_P\|=\|P\mathsf TP\|\le\|\mathsf T\|$.
2.2. (Gram expansion.) For $g,h\in G$: $\operatorname{tr}(gh^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(gh^*)=\sum_{a,b=1}^2g_{ab}\overline{h_{ab}}$. Let $I=\mathcal E\times\{1,2\}^2$, $|I|=12N^3$, and $\iota_{(\ell,a,b)}(u)=(u_\ell)_{ab}$. Then $\sum_\ell\operatorname{tr}(u_\ell v_\ell^{-1})=\sum_{i\in I}\iota_i(u)\overline{\iota_i(v)}$ and
$$K_0(u,v)=e^{-\beta|\mathcal E|}\exp\Big(\tfrac\beta2\sum_{i\in I}\iota_i(u)\overline{\iota_i(v)}\Big)=\sum_{n\ge0}w_n\sum_{\alpha\in I^n}p_\alpha(u)\overline{p_\alpha(v)},\quad w_n=\frac{e^{-\beta|\mathcal E|}(\beta/2)^n}{n!},\ p_\alpha=\prod_{r=1}^n\iota_{\alpha_r}.\tag{2.2}$$
Absolute and uniform convergence: $\sum_{\alpha\in I^n}|p_\alpha(u)||p_\alpha(v)|=(\sum_i|\iota_i(u)||\iota_i(v)|)^n\le(2|\mathcal E|)^n$, because $\sum_i|\iota_i(u)|^2=\sum_\ell\operatorname{tr}(u_\ell u_\ell^*)=2|\mathcal E|$ and Cauchy-Schwarz; and $\sum_nw_n(2|\mathcal E|)^n=e^{-\beta|\mathcal E|}e^{\beta|\mathcal E|}=1$. In particular $\sum_nw_n\sum_\alpha|p_\alpha(u)|^2=K_0(u,u)=1$.
2.3. (2b): by (2.2), $\sum_{a,b}z_a\overline{z_b}K_0(u_a,u_b)=\sum_nw_n\sum_\alpha\big|\sum_az_ap_\alpha(u_a)\big|^2\ge0$. For the operator: for $f\in\mathcal H$, the bound of 2.2 and $\|f\|_1<\infty$ allow exchanging sum and integrals (dominated convergence), so
$$\langle f,C_{\mathcal E}f\rangle=\iint\overline{f(u)}K_0(u,v)f(v)\,du\,dv=\sum_nw_n\sum_{\alpha}\big|\langle p_\alpha,f\rangle\big|^2\ge0.\tag{2.3}$$
2.4. (2c): $\langle f,\mathsf Tf\rangle=\langle Mf,C_{\mathcal E}Mf\rangle\ge0$ ($M$ real, bounded); $\langle f,\mathsf T_Pf\rangle=\langle Pf,\mathsf TPf\rangle\ge0$.
2.5. (2d), characters of SU(2). For $l\in\frac12\mathbb N_0$ let $D^l$ be the representation of $G$ on the symmetric tensors $\mathrm{Sym}^{2l}(\mathbb C^2)\subset(\mathbb C^2)^{\otimes2l}$, the restriction of $g^{\otimes2l}$ to this invariant subspace; it is unitary because $g^{\otimes2l}$ is. Its character $\chi_l=\operatorname{tr}D^l$ is a class function; every $g\in G$ is conjugate in $G$ to $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta})$ (unitary diagonalization, with the diagonalizing unitary multiplied by a scalar to have determinant 1), and on the symmetrized basis vectors $e_1^{2l-k}e_2^{k}$ that element acts by $e^{i(2l-2k)\theta}$, so $\chi_l=\sum_{k=0}^{2l}e^{i(2l-2k)\theta}=\frac{\sin((2l+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}$ (value $2l+1$ at $\sin\theta=0$, $\theta\in\{0,\pi\}$, up to the sign $(\pm1)^{2l}$). From $2\cos\theta\sin((2l+1)\theta)=\sin((2l+2)\theta)+\sin(2l\theta)$: $\chi_{1/2}\chi_l=\chi_{l+1/2}+\chi_{l-1/2}$ ($\chi_{-1/2}:=0$). By induction $\chi_{1/2}^n=\sum_lm_{n,l}\chi_l$ with integers $m_{n,l}\ge0$, $m_{n,n/2}=1$, $\sum_lm_{n,l}(2l+1)=2^n$ (evaluate at $g=1$). Since $\operatorname{tr}g=\chi_{1/2}(g)$,
$c(g)=e^{-\beta}\sum_n\frac{(\beta/2)^n}{n!}\chi_{1/2}(g)^n=e^{-\beta}\sum_la_l\chi_l(g)$, $a_l=\sum_n\frac{(\beta/2)^n}{n!}m_{n,l}\ge\frac{(\beta/2)^{2l}}{(2l)!}$. Absolute uniform convergence: $|\chi_l|\le2l+1$ and $\sum_la_l(2l+1)=\sum_n(\beta/2)^n2^n/n!=e^\beta$ (terms nonnegative, so rearrangement is allowed). Finally $\chi_l(gh^{-1})=\operatorname{tr}(D^l(g)D^l(h)^*)=\sum_{a,b}D^l_{ab}(g)\overline{D^l_{ab}(h)}$ is positive definite as in 2.3. This is the character argument for (2b) on one link; products over links of positive-definite kernels are positive definite (the product of Gram kernels $\langle\xi_a,\xi_b\rangle\langle\eta_a,\eta_b\rangle$ is the Gram kernel of $\xi_a\otimes\eta_a$). ∎

Remark 2.6 (link to SA GL3, read). With Schur orthogonality, convolution by $c$ acts on the matrix coefficients of $D^l$ as multiplication by $c_l=e^{-\beta}a_l/(2l+1)$; Sussman-Alpoge give $c_l=\frac{2e^{-\beta}}\beta I_{2l+1}(\beta)$ (GL3). Neither identification is used here; 2.3 needs no representation theory.

## 3. Statement (3): compact and trace class

**Statement (3).** $\mathsf T$ and $\mathsf T_P$ are Hilbert-Schmidt with $\|\cdot\|_{HS}\le1$, hence compact; they are trace class with $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf T=\int_Xe^{-V(u)}du\le1$ and $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf T_P\le\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf T\le1$; $C_{\mathcal E}$ is trace class with $\operatorname{Tr}C_{\mathcal E}=1$. Consequently (A2(iv)) the eigenvalues of $\mathsf T_P$, with multiplicity, have sum $\le1$.

**Proof.**
3.1. HS and compactness: A0(e) with $\|T_0\|_{L^2},\|A_T\|_{L^2}\le1$, and A0(d).
3.2. Let $J=\bigsqcup_{n\ge0}I^n$ and $R:\ell^2(J)\to\mathcal H$, $R\delta_\alpha=\sqrt{w_n}\,p_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in I^n$. Then $\sum_\alpha\|R\delta_\alpha\|^2=\sum_nw_n\int\sum_{\alpha\in I^n}|p_\alpha|^2du=\sum_nw_n(2|\mathcal E|)^n=1$ (2.2), so $R$ is HS (A0(a)-(d) hold verbatim between two Hilbert spaces), with $\|R\|_{HS}=1$. By (2.3) and polarization, $\langle f,RR^*g\rangle=\sum_\alpha w_n\langle f,p_\alpha\rangle\langle p_\alpha,g\rangle=\langle f,C_{\mathcal E}g\rangle$, so $C_{\mathcal E}=RR^*=(R^*)^*R^*$ and $\operatorname{Tr}C_{\mathcal E}=\|R^*\|_{HS}^2=\|R\|_{HS}^2=1$ (Definition A1, A0(a)).
3.3. $\mathsf T=MRR^*M=(R^*M)^*(R^*M)$, so $\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf T=\|R^*M\|_{HS}^2=\|MR\|_{HS}^2=\sum_\alpha w_n\int e^{-V}|p_\alpha|^2=\int e^{-V}\le1$ by the last line of 2.2 (Tonelli). $\mathsf T_P=(R^*MP)^*(R^*MP)$ and $\|R^*MP\|_{HS}=\|PMR\|_{HS}\le\|MR\|_{HS}$ (A0(a),(c)). ∎

## 4. Statement (4): strict positivity, simple top eigenvalue, positive ground state

**Statement (4).** Let $\lambda_0=\|\mathsf T_P\|$.
(4a) $\mathsf T_P$ and $\mathsf T$ improve positivity: if $f\ge0$ a.e., $f\ne0$, then $(\mathsf T_Pf)(u)\ge e^{-12\beta N^3}\int f>0$ and $(\mathsf Tf)(u)\ge e^{-12\beta N^3}\int f>0$ for every $u$.
(4b) $\lambda_0$ is a simple eigenvalue of $\mathsf T_P$ on $\mathcal H$, and $e^{-12\beta N^3}\le\lambda_0\le1$.
(4c) Its eigenvector $\Omega$, $\|\Omega\|=1$, has a continuous representative with $e^{-12\beta N^3}\le\Omega(u)\le e^{12\beta N^3}$ for all $u$, and $\Omega(h\cdot u)=\Omega(u)$ for all $h,u$; so $\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$.
(4d) $\|\mathsf T\|=\lambda_0$, $\mathsf T\Omega=\lambda_0\Omega$, and $\lambda_0$ is a simple eigenvalue of $\mathsf T$ as well: the ground state of the transfer operator on all of $L^2(X)$ is gauge invariant.

**Proof.**
4.1. $X$ is a compact metric space (a closed subset of $(\mathbb C^{2\times2})^{\mathcal E}$) and $du$ a Borel probability measure. $A_T$ is real, symmetric, continuous with $\kappa_-\le A_T\le\kappa_+$ (1a), and $\mathsf T_P\ge0$ (2c). Lemma A3 applies with $\kappa_1=\kappa_-=e^{-12\beta N^3}$, $\kappa_2=\kappa_+=1$: (b) gives (4a) for $\mathsf T_P$, (a) and (e) give (4b), (c) gives the continuity and the two bounds of (4c).
4.2. Invariance: the continuous representative is $\Omega(u)=\lambda_0^{-1}\int A_T(u,v)\Omega(v)\,dv$ (A3.4), and $A_T(h\cdot u,v)=A_T(u,v)$ (1a) gives $\Omega(h\cdot u)=\Omega(u)$. Then $P\Omega=\Omega$.
4.3. (4d) and (4a) for $\mathsf T$: $T_0$ is real, symmetric, continuous, $\kappa_-\le T_0\le1$ (1.4), $\mathsf T\ge0$; Lemma A3 gives a simple top eigenvalue $\lambda_0'=\|\mathsf T\|$ with positive continuous eigenvector $\Omega'$, and positivity improvement. For $h\in\mathcal G$, $(R_hf)(u)=f(h\cdot u)$ is unitary (N.5) and commutes with $\mathsf T$ ($T_0(h\cdot u,h\cdot v)=T_0(u,v)$ and substitution). So $R_h\Omega'$ is a positive unit eigenvector for $\lambda_0'$; by simplicity $R_h\Omega'=z\Omega'$ with $|z|=1$, and $z=1$ because both are positive. So $\Omega'\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ (1a) and $\mathsf T_P\Omega'=\mathsf TP\Omega'=\lambda_0'\Omega'$, whence $\lambda_0'\le\lambda_0$; with $\lambda_0=\|P\mathsf TP\|\le\|\mathsf T\|=\lambda_0'$, $\lambda_0=\lambda_0'$, and simplicity for $\mathsf T_P$ gives $\Omega'=\Omega$. ∎

## 5. Statement (5): the gap is strictly positive

**Definition.** $R_{\rm inv}=\mathsf T_P|_{\mathcal H_{\rm inv}}$ (it maps $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ into itself, since $\mathsf T_P=P\mathsf T_P$). $\lambda_1:=\sup\big(\sigma(R_{\rm inv})\setminus\{\lambda_0\}\big)$ and $m(\beta,N):=-\log(\lambda_1/\lambda_0)$.

**Statement (5).** (5a) $\lambda_1=\sup\{\langle\varphi,\mathsf T_P\varphi\rangle:\varphi\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv},\ \varphi\perp\Omega,\ \|\varphi\|=1\}$, and $\lambda_1$ is an eigenvalue of $R_{\rm inv}$ of finite multiplicity. (5b) $\lambda_1\le\lambda_0(1-e^{-12\beta N^3}\lambda_0)\le\lambda_0(1-e^{-24\beta N^3})$, so
$$m(\beta,N)\ \ge\ -\log\big(1-e^{-24\beta N^3}\big)\ >\ e^{-24\beta N^3}\ >0 .$$
(5c) $\lambda_1>0$, so $m(\beta,N)<\infty$.

**Proof.**
5.1. $R_{\rm inv}$ is compact (restriction of a compact operator to a closed invariant subspace), self-adjoint and $\ge0$ on the Hilbert space $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$, and nonzero ($R_{\rm inv}\Omega=\lambda_0\Omega$). Apply Lemma A2: its list $\lambda_0^{(A2)}\ge\lambda_1^{(A2)}\ge\dots$ starts with $\|R_{\rm inv}\|=\lambda_0$ (as $\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$), $\varphi_0=\Omega$ up to a phase, and by (4b) and A2(iii) $\lambda_0$ occurs once in the list. By A2(iii), $\sigma(R_{\rm inv})\setminus\{\lambda_0\}\subset\{\lambda_j^{(A2)}:j\ge1\}\cup\{0\}$ and contains every $\lambda^{(A2)}_j$, $j\ge1$. So $\lambda_1=\lambda_1^{(A2)}$ if the list has a second entry and $\lambda_1\le0$ otherwise, and the sup formula of A2(iii) with $j=1$ is (5a) once (5c) shows the second entry exists.
5.2. (5b): A3(d) on $\mathcal H$ with $\kappa_1=e^{-12\beta N^3}$, $\kappa_2=1$ gives $\langle\varphi,\mathsf T_P\varphi\rangle\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$ for $\varphi\perp\Omega$, with $\delta\ge\kappa_1\lambda_0\ge\kappa_1^2$ ($\lambda_0\ge\kappa_1$ by (4b)). With (5a), $\lambda_1\le\lambda_0(1-e^{-12\beta N^3}\lambda_0)\le\lambda_0(1-e^{-24\beta N^3})$. Finally $-\log(1-x)>x$ for $0<x<1$.
5.3. (5c). Suppose the list of 5.1 has no second entry; then $R_{\rm inv}=\lambda_0|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$ (A2(ii)), so $\langle\varphi,\mathsf T_P\varphi\rangle=0$ for every $\varphi\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ with $\varphi\perp\Omega$.
5.4. For such $\varphi$, $P\varphi=\varphi$ and by (2.3), $0=\langle\varphi,\mathsf T_P\varphi\rangle=\langle M\varphi,C_{\mathcal E}M\varphi\rangle=\sum_nw_n\sum_{\alpha\in I^n}|\langle p_\alpha,M\varphi\rangle|^2$. Since $\beta>0$, every $w_n>0$, so $\langle p_\alpha,M\varphi\rangle=0$ for every monomial $p_\alpha$, and $\langle q,M\varphi\rangle=0$ for every polynomial $q$ in the entries $\iota_i$.
5.5. Take the spatial plaquette $p_\star$ at $x=0$ in the plane $(1,2)$ and $W(u)=\operatorname{tr}u_{p_\star}=\operatorname{tr}\big(u_{(0,1)}u_{(e_1,2)}u_{(e_2,1)}^{-1}u_{(0,2)}^{-1}\big)$, with four distinct links. By N.2 the entries of $u^{-1}$ are $\pm$ entries of $u$, so $W$ is a polynomial (of degree 4) in the $\iota_i$; so is the constant $1=p_\emptyset$. Hence $\langle e^{-V/2},\varphi\rangle=\langle1,M\varphi\rangle=0$ and $\langle e^{-V/2}W,\varphi\rangle=\langle W,M\varphi\rangle=0$ ($W$ and $e^{-V/2}$ are real).
5.6. $e^{-V/2}$ and $e^{-V/2}W$ are bounded and gauge invariant (N.5; $W(h\cdot u)=\operatorname{tr}(h(0)u_{p_\star}h(0)^{-1})$), so they lie in $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ (1a). By 5.5 they are orthogonal to $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}\cap\Omega^\perp$, so both lie in $\operatorname{span}\{\Omega\}$: $e^{-V/2}=a\Omega$, $e^{-V/2}W=b\Omega$ a.e., with $a\ne0$. Hence $W=b/a$ a.e.
5.7. $W$ is continuous, $W=2$ at $u\equiv1$, and $W=-2$ at the configuration with $u_{(0,1)}=-1$ and all other links $1$. So the open sets $\{W>1\}$ and $\{W<-1\}$ are nonempty. A nonempty open $O\subset X$ has positive Haar measure: the translates $xO$, $x\in X$, cover the compact group $X$, so finitely many do, $X=\bigcup_{k\le r}x_kO$, and $1\le\sum_k|x_kO|=r|O|$ by left invariance. So $W$ is not a.e. constant. This contradicts 5.6; so the list has a second entry $\lambda_1^{(A2)}>0$, and $\lambda_1=\lambda_1^{(A2)}>0$. ∎

## 6. Statement (6): the limit $T\to\infty$ and exponential decay

Notation. $\rho_j=\lambda_j^{(A2)}/\lambda_0$ for the list of 5.1 ($\rho_0=1>\rho_1=e^{-m}\ge\rho_2\ge\dots>0$), $\varphi_j$ the eigenvectors ($\varphi_0=\Omega$), $Q_\Omega=1-|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|$. For a bounded $F$ on $X$ and a slice $t$, write $F_t=F(U(t))$.

**Statement (6).** Let $A,B$ be bounded, measurable, gauge invariant on $X$, $t\in\mathbb N_0$.
(6a) $\lim_{T\to\infty}\langle A_0B_t\rangle_T=\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty:=\lambda_0^{-t}\langle\Omega,M_A\mathsf T_P^tM_B\Omega\rangle=\lambda_0^{-t}\langle\Omega,M_A\mathsf T^tM_B\Omega\rangle$, and $\lim_T\langle A_0\rangle_T=\langle A\rangle_\infty:=\int A\,\Omega^2du$. Explicitly, for $T\ge t+2$,
$$\big|\langle A_0B_t\rangle_T-\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty\big|\le2\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty\,e^{12\beta N^3}e^{-m(T-t-1)}.$$
(6b) The truncated function is the matrix element
$$\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty-\langle A\rangle_\infty\langle B\rangle_\infty=\lambda_0^{-t}\big\langle Q_\Omega\overline A\Omega,\ \mathsf T_P^t\,Q_\Omega B\Omega\big\rangle=\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j^t\,\langle\overline A\Omega,\varphi_j\rangle\langle\varphi_j,B\Omega\rangle\quad(t\ge1),$$
and
$$\big|\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty-\langle A\rangle_\infty\langle B\rangle_\infty\big|\le e^{-mt}\,\sigma_A\sigma_B\le e^{-mt}\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty,\qquad\sigma_F^2=\langle|F|^2\rangle_\infty-|\langle F\rangle_\infty|^2 .$$

**Proof.**
6.1. (Trace in the eigenbasis.) By 1.7 (with $k=2$, or $k=1$ and $F_1=AB$ if $t=0$), $Z_T\langle A_0B_t\rangle_T=\operatorname{Tr}(Y\mathsf T_P^{s})$ with $Y=M_A\mathsf T_P^tM_B$ and $s=T-t\ge2$. Write $\mathsf T_P=\hat S^*\hat S$ with $\hat S=R^*MP$ HS (3.3); then $Y\mathsf T_P^s=(Y\mathsf T_P^{s-1}\hat S^*)\hat S$ is a product of two HS operators, so the trace may be computed in any ONB. Take the ONB of $\mathcal H$ formed by the $\varphi_j$ (they are eigenvectors of $\mathsf T_P$ on $\mathcal H$ with the eigenvalues $\lambda_0\rho_j$) and an ONB of their orthogonal complement in $\mathcal H$, on which $\mathsf T_P=0$ (A2(ii) for $R_{\rm inv}$, and $\mathsf T_P=0$ on $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}^\perp$). Hence
$Z_T\langle A_0B_t\rangle_T=\lambda_0^{s}\sum_j\rho_j^{s}\langle\varphi_j,Y\varphi_j\rangle$, and likewise $Z_T=\lambda_0^T\sum_j\rho_j^T$.
6.2. $|\langle\varphi_j,Y\varphi_j\rangle|\le\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty\lambda_0^t$. Put $G_j=\lambda_0^{-t}\langle\varphi_j,Y\varphi_j\rangle$, $a_T=\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j^sG_j$, $b_T=\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j^T$. Then $\langle A_0B_t\rangle_T=(G_0+a_T)/(1+b_T)$ and $|\langle A_0B_t\rangle_T-G_0|=|a_T-G_0b_T|/(1+b_T)\le|a_T|+|G_0|b_T\le2\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j^{s}$ (as $\rho_j^T\le\rho_j^s$).
6.3. $\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j^s\le\rho_1^{s-1}\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_j\le e^{-m(s-1)}\operatorname{Tr}\mathsf T_P/\lambda_0\le e^{-m(s-1)}e^{12\beta N^3}$ by A2(iv), (3) and (4b). This gives (6a) with $G_0=\lambda_0^{-t}\langle\Omega,M_A\mathsf T_P^tM_B\Omega\rangle$. The form with $\mathsf T$: $M_B\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ ($B$, $\Omega$ invariant), and $\mathsf T_P=\mathsf T$ on $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$. The one-point case is $B=1$, $t=0$.
6.4. (6b): $M_A^*=M_{\overline A}$, so $\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty=\lambda_0^{-t}\langle\overline A\Omega,\mathsf T_P^tB\Omega\rangle$ and $\langle A\rangle_\infty\langle B\rangle_\infty=\lambda_0^{-t}\langle\overline A\Omega,\lambda_0^t|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|B\Omega\rangle$. On $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$, $\mathsf T_P^t-\lambda_0^t|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|=\mathsf T_P^tQ_\Omega$ and its range is in $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}\cap\Omega^\perp$; $\overline A\Omega,B\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$; so the left vector may be replaced by $Q_\Omega\overline A\Omega$. The series is A2(ii). By (5a) and A2(ii), $\|\mathsf T_P^tQ_\Omega\psi\|\le\lambda_1^t\|\psi\|$ for $\psi\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$, so the modulus is at most $\rho_1^t\|Q_\Omega\overline A\Omega\|\|Q_\Omega B\Omega\|$, and $\|Q_\Omega B\Omega\|^2=\int|B|^2\Omega^2-|\int B\Omega^2|^2=\sigma_B^2\le\|B\|_\infty^2$ (same for $\overline A$, $\sigma_{\overline A}=\sigma_A$). ∎

## 7. Statement (7): the converse, and density

**Statement (7).** (7a) Let $\mathcal C$ be a set of bounded measurable gauge-invariant functions on $X$ such that $\{F\Omega:F\in\mathcal C\}$ is dense in $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$. Let $\mu>0$. If for all $A,B\in\mathcal C$ there is $C_{A,B}<\infty$ with $|\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty-\langle A\rangle_\infty\langle B\rangle_\infty|\le C_{A,B}e^{-\mu t}$ for all $t\in\mathbb N$, then $m(\beta,N)\ge\mu$.
(7b) The set $\mathcal C_c$ of continuous gauge-invariant functions on $X$ has this density property. So $m(\beta,N)$ equals the supremum of the rates $\mu$ for which the hypothesis of (7a) holds on $\mathcal C_c$ (by (6b) the rate $\mu=m$ holds with $C_{A,B}=\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty$).

**Proof.**
7.1. Complex conjugation $f\mapsto\bar f$ is an antiunitary map of $\mathcal H$ onto itself that commutes with $P$ (the action is on the arguments only), so it maps $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ onto itself and dense subsets to dense subsets. Since $\Omega$ is real, $\{\overline F\Omega:F\in\mathcal C\}$ is dense in $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$.
7.2. Let $\Pi_1$ be the orthogonal projection on $\ker(R_{\rm inv}-\lambda_1)=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_j:\rho_j=\rho_1\}$, nonzero and finite-dimensional ((5a), (5c)). If $\Pi_1B\Omega=0$ for all $B\in\mathcal C$, then the nonzero vector $\varphi_1\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ would be orthogonal to a dense subset ($\langle\varphi_1,B\Omega\rangle=\langle\Pi_1\varphi_1,B\Omega\rangle=\langle\varphi_1,\Pi_1B\Omega\rangle$). So fix $B\in\mathcal C$ with $\Pi_1B\Omega\ne0$; by 7.1 fix $A\in\mathcal C$ with $\gamma:=\langle\overline A\Omega,\Pi_1B\Omega\rangle\ne0$.
7.3. By (6b), $S(t):=\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty-\langle A\rangle_\infty\langle B\rangle_\infty=\rho_1^t\big(\gamma+\sum_{j\ge1,\rho_j<\rho_1}(\rho_j/\rho_1)^t\langle\overline A\Omega,\varphi_j\rangle\langle\varphi_j,B\Omega\rangle\big)$. The terms of the last sum tend to $0$ as $t\to\infty$ and are dominated by $|\langle\overline A\Omega,\varphi_j\rangle||\langle\varphi_j,B\Omega\rangle|$, which is summable (Cauchy-Schwarz and Bessel). By dominated convergence there is $t_0$ with $|S(t)|\ge\frac12|\gamma|\rho_1^t$ for $t\ge t_0$.
7.4. With the hypothesis, $(\rho_1e^\mu)^t\le2C_{A,B}/|\gamma|$ for all $t\ge t_0$, so $\rho_1e^\mu\le1$, i.e. $m=-\log\rho_1\ge\mu$.
7.5. (7b). Let $\psi\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$. $1/\Omega$ is continuous, bounded and gauge invariant, so $\psi/\Omega\in\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$: by 1.7, $P(Ff)=F\,Pf$ for bounded invariant $F$, hence $P(\psi/\Omega)=\Omega^{-1}P\psi=\psi/\Omega$. By Lemma A4 choose $f_n\in C(X)$ with $f_n\to\psi/\Omega$ in $\mathcal H$. Then $Pf_n\to P(\psi/\Omega)=\psi/\Omega$. Each $Pf_n$ is continuous ($(h,u)\mapsto f_n(h\cdot u)$ is uniformly continuous on the compact $\mathcal G\times X$) and gauge invariant everywhere (1.5), so $Pf_n\in\mathcal C_c$, and $\|(Pf_n)\Omega-\psi\|\le\|\Omega\|_\infty\|Pf_n-\psi/\Omega\|\to0$.
7.6. The last sentence of (7b): (6b) gives the hypothesis of (7a) for every $\mu\le m$, and (7a) gives $\mu\le m$ whenever it holds. ∎

Remark 7.7 (a smaller class can fail; not part of the row's request here, see row 668). If $\mathcal C$ consists of the constants, every truncated function vanishes and the hypothesis of (7a) holds for every $\mu$, while $m<\infty$ by (5c). The proof uses density only in 7.2, to find a pair with $\gamma\ne0$; the class must reach the eigenspace of $\lambda_1$ (and, for the identification of $m$ as the gap, nothing else is needed).

## 8. Statement (8): the infinite time direction as a limit of measures

**Slab observables.** For integers $a<b$, a *slab function* $F$ on $[a,b]$ is a bounded measurable function of $(U(a),\dots,U(b);g_a,\dots,g_{b-1})\in X^{b-a+1}\times\mathcal G^{b-a}$, i.e. of the spatial links of slices $a,\dots,b$ and the time-like links between them. Every bounded measurable function of finitely many links of $\Lambda\times\mathbb Z$ is a slab function for some $[a,b]$. For $T\ge b-a+2$ the slab sits in $\Lambda\times\mathbb Z_T$ with slices $a,\dots,b$ distinct mod $T$, and $\langle F\rangle_T$ is defined. Put
$$K_F(u,v)=\int F\prod_{t=a}^{b-1}T_0\big(U(t),g_t\cdot U(t+1)\big)\prod_{a<t<b}dU(t)\prod_{a\le t<b}dg_t\Big|_{U(a)=u,\ U(b)=v},$$
and $\mathcal K_F=S_{K_F}$.

**Statement (8).**
(8a) For every slab function $F$ on $[a,b]$ the limit $\langle F\rangle_\infty=\lim_{T\to\infty}\langle F\rangle_T$ exists, $\langle F\rangle_\infty=\lambda_0^{-(b-a)}\langle\Omega,\mathcal K_F\Omega\rangle$, and for $T\ge b-a+2$: $|\langle F\rangle_T-\langle F\rangle_\infty|\le2\|F\|_\infty e^{12\beta N^3}e^{-m(T-(b-a)-1)}$.
(8b) $\langle F\rangle_\infty=\int F\,\varrho_{[a,b]}$ with the explicit probability density, with respect to the product Haar measure of the slab,
$\varrho_{[a,b]}=\lambda_0^{-(b-a)}\,\Omega(U(a))\prod_{t=a}^{b-1}T_0\big(U(t),g_t\cdot U(t+1)\big)\,\Omega(U(b))>0$.
The family is consistent (a slab function on $[a,b]$ is also one on any $[a',b']\supset[a,b]$, with the same limit), invariant under time translation, and agrees with (6a) on single-slice functions.
(8c) (clustering for all local observables) If $F$ is a slab function on $[a,b]$ and $H$ one on $[a',b']$ with $d=a'-b\ge1$, then $|\langle FH\rangle_\infty-\langle F\rangle_\infty\langle H\rangle_\infty|\le\|F\|_\infty\|H\|_\infty e^{-md}$. Gauge invariance of $F,H$ is not required.
(8d) Which observables: all bounded measurable functions of finitely many links, gauge invariant or not. Not covered: unbounded functions; functions of infinitely many links; observables that wind around the time circle (Polyakov loops), which have no counterpart on $\Lambda\times\mathbb Z$.
(8e) Not proved here: that the consistent family (8b) comes from one probability measure on $G^{\{\text{links of }\Lambda\times\mathbb Z\}}$. This is Kolmogorov's extension theorem (A. N. Kolmogorov, 1933; **not read**, cited, not rebuilt). Nothing in (1)-(7) or (8a)-(8d) uses it.

**Proof.**
8.1. By time-translation invariance take $a=0$. Starting from (1.2), integrate $g_t$ for $t\notin[0,b-1]$ (1.3), then $U(t)$ for $0<t<b$ and $g_t$ for $0\le t<b$ (Fubini; all bounded). With $s=T-b\ge2$ this gives $Z_T\langle F\rangle_T=\iint K_F(u,v)\,a_s(v,u)\,du\,dv$, where $a_s$ is the kernel of $\mathsf T_P^s$ (A0(e)), so $Z_T\langle F\rangle_T=\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal K_F\mathsf T_P^s)$ ($K_F$ is bounded, hence in $L^2$).
8.2. As in 6.1, $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal K_F\mathsf T_P^s)=\lambda_0^s\sum_j\rho_j^s\langle\varphi_j,\mathcal K_F\varphi_j\rangle$.
8.3. $|K_F|\le\|F\|_\infty K_1$, and for $F=1$ the $g_t$-integrals produce $A_T$ (1.3), so $K_1=a_b$, the kernel of $\mathsf T_P^b$, which is $\ge0$. Hence $|\langle\varphi,\mathcal K_F\varphi\rangle|\le\|F\|_\infty\langle|\varphi|,\mathsf T_P^b|\varphi|\rangle\le\|F\|_\infty\lambda_0^b\|\varphi\|^2$. Now 6.2-6.3 with $t$ replaced by $b$ give (8a).
8.4. (8b): Fubini in $\lambda_0^{-b}\iint\Omega(u)K_F(u,v)\Omega(v)$ ($\Omega$ real). Positivity from (4c) and $T_0>0$. Normalization: $F=1$ gives $\lambda_0^{-b}\langle\Omega,\mathsf T_P^b\Omega\rangle=1$. Consistency and translation invariance hold for each $T$ and pass to the limit. For $F=A(U(0))B(U(b))$, $K_F(u,v)=A(u)a_b(u,v)B(v)$, so $\mathcal K_F=M_A\mathsf T_P^bM_B$, which is (6a).
8.5. (8c): with $a=0$, $FH$ is a slab function on $[0,b']$, and integrating the $g_t$, $b\le t<a'$, and the slices strictly between $b$ and $a'$ gives $K_{FH}(u,w)=\iint K_F(u,v)a_d(v,v')K_H(v',w)\,dv\,dv'$, i.e. $\mathcal K_{FH}=\mathcal K_F\mathsf T_P^d\mathcal K_H$. Put $x=P\mathcal K_F^*\Omega$, $y=P\mathcal K_H\Omega$, both in $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$. Since $\mathsf T_P^d=P\mathsf T_P^dP$ and $P\Omega=\Omega$: $\langle FH\rangle_\infty=\lambda_0^{-b'}\langle x,\mathsf T_P^dy\rangle$ and $\langle F\rangle_\infty\langle H\rangle_\infty=\lambda_0^{-b'}\langle x,\lambda_0^d|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|y\rangle$ ($b'=b+d+(b'-a')$). The difference is $\lambda_0^{-b'}\langle x,\mathsf T_P^dQ_\Omega y\rangle$, of modulus $\le\lambda_0^{-b'}\lambda_1^d\|x\|\|y\|$ (6.4). Pointwise $|\mathcal K_H\Omega|\le\|H\|_\infty\mathsf T_P^{b'-a'}\Omega=\|H\|_\infty\lambda_0^{b'-a'}\Omega$ ($\Omega>0$, 8.3), so $\|y\|\le\|H\|_\infty\lambda_0^{b'-a'}$; the kernel of $\mathcal K_F^*$ is $\overline{K_F(v,u)}$, bounded by $\|F\|_\infty a_b(u,v)$ ($a_b$ symmetric), so $\|x\|\le\|F\|_\infty\lambda_0^{b}$. Together: $\le\|F\|_\infty\|H\|_\infty\rho_1^d$. ∎

## 9. What this does not give

9.1. **The displayed lower bounds are not uniform in volume or coupling.** The kernel floor, the lower bounds on lambda_0 and m, and the reciprocal ground-state bounds degenerate as N or beta tends to infinity. The error prefactors in (6a) and (8a) grow. This describes the estimates, not the actual gap. At each fixed beta>0 and N>=3, compactness, operator positivity and positivity improvement give m>0; the additional argument 5.3–5.7 gives m<infinity. Strict positivity of a continuous kernel alone does not give a positive excited eigenvalue. (Corrected, row 671.)

9.2. **Uniform in everything (given $m$).** The identities (1b), (1c), (6b), (8b) and the inequalities $|{\rm trunc}|\le e^{-mt}\|A\|_\infty\|B\|_\infty$ (6b) and (8c) hold with constant $1$ for all $\beta>0$, $N\ge3$; and the converse (7) holds with no constant at all. So at each fixed $(\beta,N)$, exponential decay in the time direction for a dense class and the spectral gap of $\mathsf T_P$ on $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ are the same statement with the same rate. Uniformity in the lattice spacing is entirely a statement about $m(\beta,N)$.

9.3. **A sector structure the gap includes (proved here, short).** For $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ let $Z_k$ flip the sign of every link $(x,k)$ with $x_k=0$. A plaquette in a plane $(k,j)$ contains either none or two such links, and $-1$ is central, so $V\circ Z_k=V$, $K_0(Z_ku,Z_kv)=K_0(u,v)$ and $Z_k(h\cdot u)=h\cdot Z_ku$; $Z_k$ preserves $du$. Hence $f\mapsto f\circ Z_k$ commutes with $\mathsf T$ and $P$, and $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}=\bigoplus_{e\in\{\pm1\}^3}\mathcal H_e$ with $\mathsf T_P$ preserving each $\mathcal H_e$ (the electric-flux sectors). $\Omega\in\mathcal H_{(+,+,+)}$ ($\Omega\circ Z_k$ is a positive ground state, so equals $\Omega$). The lambda_1 of (5) is the maximum, over all eight e, of the norm of R_inv restricted to H_e intersect Omega-perp. In particular the vacuum is removed from the all-plus sector before taking this maximum (corrected, row 671). Whether (G-ell) should be stated on all of $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ or on $\mathcal H_{(+,+,+)}$ is a definitional choice for `gap-skeleton`; no identification of lowest states with those studied by 't Hooft or van Baal is proved here; those sources were not read.

9.4. **The exact uniform statement (G-ell) asks for (stated, not proved).** Fix a physical spatial side $\ell>0$ and the coupling trajectory $a\mapsto\beta(a)$ of the existence construction (T0). For $N\ge3$ put $a_N=\ell/N$ and $m_N=m(\beta(a_N),N)$, the gap of (5) on $\mathcal H_{\rm inv}$ (with the infinite time direction already taken, as in (6) and (8)). Then

> **(G-ell)** there are $m_\ell>0$ and $N_0$ such that $m_N\ge m_\ell\,a_N$ for all $N\ge N_0$; equivalently, lambda_1(beta(a_N),N)/lambda_0(beta(a_N),N) <= exp(-m_ell a_N), uniformly for N>=N_0. This file does not construct a logarithmic Hamiltonian on the entire invariant space; that formulation additionally needs injectivity and its operator domain (corrected, row 671).

By (6b) and (7) at each $N$, (G-ell) is equivalent to: for every $N\ge N_0$ and all $A,B$ in a class $\mathcal C_N$ with the density property of (7a), $|\langle A_0B_t\rangle_\infty-\langle A\rangle_\infty\langle B\rangle_\infty|\le C_{N,A,B}\,e^{-m_\ell\,ta_N}$ for all $t$, with constants that may depend on $N$, $A$, $B$ but a rate $m_\ell$ in physical units that does not. Sections 1–8 give this equivalence but do not prove (G-ell). If beta(a_N)>=beta_*>0 eventually, the ratio of the displayed lower bound exp(-24 beta(a_N)N^3) to m_ell ell/N is at most N exp(-24 beta_* N^3)/(m_ell ell), which tends to zero. This is a limitation of the lower bound, not an upper bound on the actual gap (corrected, row 671).

9.5. **Relation to (G-inf)** (row 662's names). (G-ell) is at fixed $\ell$; (G-inf) is about $\ell\to\infty$ (infinite volume), for which this file constructs no transfer operator and proves no gap. The finite-volume lower bound (5b) tends to zero as N tends to infinity at fixed beta>0 (corrected, row 671). This file says nothing about (G-inf).

9.6. **Overlap with row 668 (`gap-os-equiv`).** Statements (6) and (7) are the lattice, compact-operator case of that row's items (2) and (3); here they are proved from the explicit trace formulas, not from reflection positivity. Remark 7.7 is the trivial instance of that row's request for a counterexample with a smaller class.
