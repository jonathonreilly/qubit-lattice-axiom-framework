## Notation and object (fixed for the whole file)

N.1. $G=\mathrm{SU}(2)$, $dg$ the normalized Haar measure. It is invariant under $g\mapsto hg$, $g\mapsto gh$ and $g\mapsto g^{-1}$: identifying $G$ with the unit sphere $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$ via $\begin{pmatrix}a&-\bar b\\ b&\bar a\end{pmatrix}\mapsto(a,b)$, left and right multiplications and inversion act as orthogonal maps of $\mathbb R^4$, and $dg$ is the normalized surface measure.

N.2. For $g\in G$: $\operatorname{tr}g\in[-2,2]$ is real, $\operatorname{tr}g^{-1}=\operatorname{tr}g$, $g^{-1}=g^*$, and the entries of $g^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar a&\bar b\\-b&a\end{pmatrix}$ are, up to sign, entries of $g$ (namely $\bar a=g_{22}$, $\bar b=-g_{12}$, $-b=-g_{21}$, $a=g_{11}$).

N.3. $\Lambda=(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^3$, $N\ge3$; vertices $\mathcal V=\Lambda$, $|\mathcal V|=N^3$; positively oriented spatial links $\mathcal E=\{(x,i):x\in\Lambda,i\in\{1,2,3\}\}$, $|\mathcal E|=3N^3$. $X=G^{\mathcal E}$ with the product Haar measure $du$, a compact group and a probability space; $\mathcal H=L^2(X,du)$, $\langle f,h\rangle=\int\bar f h$.

N.4. Spatial plaquettes: for $x\in\Lambda$, $1\le i<j\le3$, $u_p=u_{(x,i)}u_{(x+e_i,j)}u_{(x+e_j,i)}^{-1}u_{(x,j)}^{-1}$; there are $3N^3$ of them. $V(u)=\beta\sum_p(1-\frac12\operatorname{tr}u_p)$, so $0\le V\le 6\beta N^3$.

N.5. Gauge group $\mathcal G=G^{\mathcal V}$ acting on $X$ by $(h\cdot u)_{(x,i)}=h(x)u_{(x,i)}h(x+e_i)^{-1}$. This is a left action, $(hk)\cdot u=h\cdot(k\cdot u)$, and each map $u\mapsto h\cdot u$ preserves $du$ (each coordinate is multiplied on both sides by fixed elements, N.1). $V(h\cdot u)=V(u)$ because $(h\cdot u)_p=h(x)u_ph(x)^{-1}$.

N.6. The space-time lattice is $\Lambda\times\mathbb Z_T$, $T\ge2$. A configuration is $U=(U(t),g_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z_T}$ with $U(t)\in X$ the spatial links of slice $t$ and $g_t\in\mathcal G$ the time-like links, $g_t(x)=U_0(x,t)$ on the link from $(x,t)$ to $(x,t+1)$. The Wilson action is $S(U)=\beta\sum_P(1-\frac12\operatorname{tr}U_P)$ over all unoriented plaquettes $P$ (orientation is irrelevant by N.2). $Z_T=\int e^{-S}\,dU$ with normalized product Haar measure on all links, $\langle F\rangle_T=Z_T^{-1}\int Fe^{-S}dU$.

N.7. One-bond kernel (SA 2196): $c(g)=e^{-\beta(1-\frac12\operatorname{tr}g)}$, so $e^{-2\beta}\le c\le1$, $c(g^{-1})=c(g)$, $c(hgh^{-1})=c(g)$. Time-like kernel $K_0(u,v)=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal E}c(u_\ell v_\ell^{-1})$, real, symmetric, $e^{-6\beta N^3}\le K_0\le1$, and $K_0(h\cdot u,h\cdot v)=K_0(u,v)$ since $(h\cdot u)_\ell(h\cdot v)_\ell^{-1}=h(x)u_\ell v_\ell^{-1}h(x)^{-1}$.

N.8. Constants used throughout: $\kappa_-:=e^{-12\beta N^3}$, $\kappa_+:=1$.

## A. Four general lemmas (rebuilt, used below)

Throughout this section $(Y,\mu)$ is a compact metric space with a Borel probability measure, $\mathcal K=L^2(Y,\mu)$, and an ONB is any orthonormal basis $(e_n)_{n\in\mathcal N}$ (any index set; all sums below have nonnegative terms or are absolutely convergent, so the index set is immaterial).

**Lemma A0 (Hilbert-Schmidt calculus).** For a bounded operator $S$ on a Hilbert space put $\|S\|_{HS}^2=\sum_n\|Se_n\|^2\in[0,\infty]$.
(a) This does not depend on the ONB, and $\|S\|_{HS}=\|S^*\|_{HS}$.
(b) If $\|S_1\|_{HS},\|S_2\|_{HS}<\infty$ then $\sum_n\langle S_1e_n,S_2e_n\rangle$ converges absolutely to a number $\langle S_1,S_2\rangle_{HS}$ independent of the ONB. Define $\operatorname{Tr}(S_1^*S_2):=\langle S_1,S_2\rangle_{HS}=\sum_n\langle e_n,S_1^*S_2e_n\rangle$.
(c) $\|S\|\le\|S\|_{HS}$, $\|BS\|_{HS}\le\|B\|\|S\|_{HS}$, $\|SB\|_{HS}\le\|B\|\|S\|_{HS}$ for bounded $B$.
(d) If $\|S\|_{HS}<\infty$, $S$ is compact.
(e) If $k\in L^2(Y\times Y)$, the integral operator $(S_kf)(y)=\int k(y,z)f(z)\,d\mu(z)$ satisfies $\|S_k\|_{HS}=\|k\|_{L^2}$; $S_k^*=S_{k^*}$ with $k^*(y,z)=\overline{k(z,y)}$; $S_kS_l=S_{k\circ l}$ with $(k\circ l)(y,w)=\int k(y,z)l(z,w)d\mu(z)$; and $\operatorname{Tr}(S_kS_l)=\iint k(y,z)l(z,y)\,d\mu(y)d\mu(z)$.

Proof. A0.1. For two ONBs $(e_n)$, $(f_m)$: $\sum_n\|Se_n\|^2=\sum_n\sum_m|\langle f_m,Se_n\rangle|^2=\sum_m\sum_n|\langle S^*f_m,e_n\rangle|^2=\sum_m\|S^*f_m\|^2$ (Parseval twice, Tonelli). The right side does not involve $(e_n)$, which gives the independence; taking $f=e$ gives $\|S\|_{HS}=\|S^*\|_{HS}$.
A0.2. (b): $|\langle S_1e_n,S_2e_n\rangle|\le\frac12(\|S_1e_n\|^2+\|S_2e_n\|^2)$ gives absolute convergence. For a second ONB $(f_m)$, Parseval in the form $\langle y,x\rangle=\sum_m\langle y,f_m\rangle\langle f_m,x\rangle$ gives $\sum_n\langle S_1e_n,S_2e_n\rangle=\sum_n\sum_m\langle S_1e_n,f_m\rangle\langle f_m,S_2e_n\rangle=\sum_m\sum_n\langle S_2^*f_m,e_n\rangle\langle e_n,S_1^*f_m\rangle=\sum_m\langle S_2^*f_m,S_1^*f_m\rangle$. The exchange of sums is allowed because $\sum_{n,m}|\langle S_1e_n,f_m\rangle||\langle f_m,S_2e_n\rangle|\le\frac12(\|S_1\|_{HS}^2+\|S_2\|_{HS}^2)$. The last expression does not involve $(e_n)$.
A0.3. (c): for a unit vector $f$ complete it to an ONB $(f,e_2,\dots)$; then $\|Sf\|^2\le\|S\|_{HS}^2$. $\|BSe_n\|\le\|B\|\|Se_n\|$ gives the second; the third follows from the second and (a): $\|SB\|_{HS}=\|B^*S^*\|_{HS}$.
A0.4. (d): let $Q_F$ be the orthogonal projection on the span of a finite set $F\subset\mathcal N$. $SQ_F$ has finite rank and $\|S-SQ_F\|^2\le\|S(1-Q_F)\|_{HS}^2=\sum_{n\notin F}\|Se_n\|^2$, which is small for suitable finite $F$ because the full sum is finite. A norm limit of finite-rank operators is compact (a bounded sequence has, for each finite-rank approximant, a subsequence whose images converge; a diagonal subsequence is Cauchy).
A0.5. (e): by Fubini, $k(y,\cdot)\in L^2$ for a.e. $y$, and $(S_ke_n)(y)=\langle\overline{k(y,\cdot)},e_n\rangle$. Parseval gives $\sum_n|(S_ke_n)(y)|^2=\int|k(y,z)|^2d\mu(z)$; integrating in $y$ (Tonelli) gives $\|S_k\|_{HS}^2=\|k\|_2^2$. The adjoint formula is Fubini on $\iint\bar f(y)k(y,z)h(z)$, absolutely integrable because $|k|\,|f|\,|h|$ is integrable by Cauchy-Schwarz on $Y\times Y$ ($\iint|k||f(y)||h(z)|\le\|k\|_2\|f\|_2\|h\|_2$). The composition formula is Fubini with the bound $\iiint|k(y,z)||l(z,w)||f(w)|\le\|k\|_2\|l\|_2\|f\|_2$ (Cauchy-Schwarz in $w$, then in $z$, then in $y$, $\mu$ a probability). For the trace: by (b) with $S_1=S_k^*=S_{k^*}$, $S_2=S_l$, and the polarized form of the identity $\|S_m\|_{HS}^2=\|m\|_2^2$, $\operatorname{Tr}(S_kS_l)=\langle S_{k^*},S_l\rangle_{HS}=\langle k^*,l\rangle_{L^2(Y\times Y)}=\iint k(z,y)\,l(y,z)$. Renaming variables gives the claim. ∎

**Definition A1.** A bounded operator $R\ge0$ is *trace class* with trace $\operatorname{Tr}R$ if $R=S^*S$ with $\|S\|_{HS}<\infty$; then $\operatorname{Tr}R=\sum_n\langle e_n,Re_n\rangle=\|S\|_{HS}^2$, independent of the ONB by A0.1. (For compact $R\ge0$ this is the same as summability of the eigenvalues with multiplicity, by Lemma A2(iv).)

**Lemma A2 (spectral theorem for compact positive operators).** Let $R\ne0$ be a compact self-adjoint operator with $R\ge0$ on a Hilbert space $\mathcal H_1$.
(i) $\|R\|=\sup_{\|f\|=1}\langle f,Rf\rangle$, and there is a unit $\varphi_0$ with $R\varphi_0=\|R\|\varphi_0$.
(ii) There is a finite or infinite orthonormal sequence $(\varphi_j)$ and numbers $\lambda_0\ge\lambda_1\ge\dots>0$, with $\lambda_j\to0$ if the sequence is infinite, such that $Rf=\sum_j\lambda_j\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle\varphi_j$ for all $f$ and $R=0$ on $\{\varphi_j\}^\perp$.
(iii) Every $\lambda>0$ with $\ker(R-\lambda)\ne0$ is some $\lambda_j$, and $\ker(R-\lambda)=\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_j:\lambda_j=\lambda\}$, of finite dimension. The spectrum $\sigma(R)$ is contained in $\{\lambda_j\}\cup\{0\}$. In particular $\lambda_{j}=\sup\{\langle f,Rf\rangle:\|f\|=1,f\perp\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{j-1}\}$.
(iv) $\sum_n\langle e_n,Re_n\rangle=\sum_j\lambda_j$ for every ONB (both possibly $+\infty$).

Proof. A2.1. Let $s=\sup_{\|f\|=1}\langle f,Rf\rangle\le\|R\|$. The form $(f,h)\mapsto\langle f,Rh\rangle$ is positive semidefinite, so Cauchy-Schwarz gives $|\langle f,Rh\rangle|^2\le\langle f,Rf\rangle\langle h,Rh\rangle\le s^2$ for unit $f,h$; hence $\|R\|\le s$, so $s=\|R\|>0$.
A2.2. Take unit $f_n$ with $\langle f_n,Rf_n\rangle\to s$. Then $\|Rf_n-sf_n\|^2=\|Rf_n\|^2-2s\langle f_n,Rf_n\rangle+s^2\le2s^2-2s\langle f_n,Rf_n\rangle\to0$. By compactness a subsequence has $Rf_n\to y$; then $sf_n\to y$, so $\|y\|=s$, $f_n\to\varphi_0:=y/s$ and $R\varphi_0=\lim Rf_n=y=s\varphi_0$. This is (i).
A2.3. $\mathcal H_2=\{\varphi_0\}^\perp$ is invariant ($\langle\varphi_0,Rf\rangle=\langle R\varphi_0,f\rangle=s\langle\varphi_0,f\rangle$). $R|_{\mathcal H_2}$ is compact, self-adjoint, $\ge0$. If it is zero stop; otherwise apply A2.2 to it. Inductively this gives orthonormal $\varphi_j$ with $\lambda_j=\|R|_{\mathcal H_{j+1}}\|$ nonincreasing, $\mathcal H_{j+1}=\{\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{j-1}\}^\perp$.
A2.4. If the sequence is infinite and $\lambda_j\ge\delta>0$ for all $j$, then $\|R\varphi_j-R\varphi_k\|^2=\lambda_j^2+\lambda_k^2\ge2\delta^2$ for $j\ne k$, so $(R\varphi_j)$ has no convergent subsequence, contradicting compactness. So $\lambda_j\to0$.
A2.5. On $\mathcal H_\infty=\{\varphi_j\}^\perp$: for $f\in\mathcal H_\infty$, $\langle f,Rf\rangle\le\lambda_j\|f\|^2$ for every $j$ (or $=0$ if the sequence stopped), so $\langle f,Rf\rangle=0$, and by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality of A2.1 restricted to the invariant space $\mathcal H_\infty$ (invariant because $R$ is self-adjoint and leaves each $\varphi_j$'s span invariant), $Rf=0$. Writing $f=\sum_j\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle\varphi_j+f_\infty$ gives (ii).
A2.6. (iii): if $Rf=\lambda f$ with $\lambda>0$ then by (ii) $(\lambda_j-\lambda)\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle=0$ for all $j$ and $\lambda f_\infty=0$. So $f\in\operatorname{span}\{\varphi_j:\lambda_j=\lambda\}$, a finite set of $j$ because $\lambda_j\to0$. For $z\notin\{\lambda_j\}\cup\{0\}$, $d=\inf_j|\lambda_j-z|>0$ (the $\lambda_j$ accumulate only at 0), and $f\mapsto\sum_j(\lambda_j-z)^{-1}\langle\varphi_j,f\rangle\varphi_j-z^{-1}f_\infty$ is a bounded two-sided inverse of $R-z$. The sup formula follows from (ii) applied to $f\perp\varphi_0,\dots,\varphi_{j-1}$: $\langle f,Rf\rangle=\sum_{i\ge j}\lambda_i|\langle\varphi_i,f\rangle|^2\le\lambda_j\|f\|^2$, with equality at $f=\varphi_j$.
A2.7. (iv): $\langle e_n,Re_n\rangle=\sum_j\lambda_j|\langle\varphi_j,e_n\rangle|^2$; sum over $n$ (Tonelli) and use Parseval $\sum_n|\langle\varphi_j,e_n\rangle|^2=1$. ∎

**Lemma A3 (Jentzsch's theorem, rebuilt; credit R. Jentzsch 1912, not read).** Let $k$ be a real, symmetric, continuous function on $Y\times Y$ with $0<\kappa_1\le k\le\kappa_2$, and let the integral operator $K=S_k$ on $\mathcal K$ satisfy $K\ge0$. Put $\lambda_0=\|K\|$.
(a) $\kappa_1\le\lambda_0\le\kappa_2$, and $K$ is compact.
(b) (positivity improving) If $f\ge0$ a.e. and $f\ne0$ in $\mathcal K$, then $(Kf)(y)\ge\kappa_1\int f\,d\mu>0$ for every $y$.
(c) $\lambda_0$ is an eigenvalue with an eigenfunction $\Omega$ that has a continuous representative with $\kappa_1/\kappa_2\le\Omega(y)\le\kappa_2/\kappa_1$ for all $y$, $\|\Omega\|_2=1$.
(d) (gap with explicit constant) For every $\varphi\in\mathcal K$ with $\langle\Omega,\varphi\rangle=0$: $\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$, with $\delta:=\inf_{y,z}\frac{k(y,z)}{\lambda_0\Omega(y)\Omega(z)}\ge\frac{\kappa_1\lambda_0}{\kappa_2^2}\ge\frac{\kappa_1^2}{\kappa_2^2}$.
(e) $\ker(K-\lambda_0)=\operatorname{span}\{\Omega\}$: the eigenvalue $\lambda_0$ is simple.

Proof. A3.1. (a): $\lambda_0\le\|k\|_{L^2}\le\kappa_2$ by A0.5 and A0.3; $\lambda_0\ge\langle1,K1\rangle=\iint k\ge\kappa_1$ (A2.1, $\|1\|=1$). Compact by A0.4, A0.5.
A3.2. (b): $(Kf)(y)=\int k(y,z)f(z)\,d\mu(z)\ge\kappa_1\int f$, and $\int f>0$ since $f\ge0$ is not a.e. zero.
A3.3. Existence of a nonnegative maximizer. By A2.2 there is a unit $\varphi$ with $K\varphi=\lambda_0\varphi$. Since $k$ is real, $K\bar\varphi=\lambda_0\bar\varphi$, so $\operatorname{Re}\varphi$ or $\operatorname{Im}\varphi$ is a nonzero real eigenfunction; normalize it and call it $\varphi$. Then $\lambda_0=\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle=\iint k(y,z)\varphi(y)\varphi(z)\le\iint k(y,z)|\varphi(y)||\varphi(z)|=\langle|\varphi|,K|\varphi|\rangle\le\lambda_0$ (A2.1). So $|\varphi|$ is a unit maximizer of the form, and A2.2's computation gives $\|K|\varphi|-\lambda_0|\varphi|\|^2\le2\lambda_0^2-2\lambda_0\langle|\varphi|,K|\varphi|\rangle=0$. Put $\Omega=|\varphi|$.
A3.4. Continuity and bounds. Define $\Omega(y):=\lambda_0^{-1}\int k(y,z)\Omega(z)d\mu(z)$ for every $y$; this equals $\Omega$ a.e. and is continuous because $k$ is uniformly continuous on the compact $Y\times Y$ and $\Omega\in L^1$. Then $\Omega(y)\le\lambda_0^{-1}\kappa_2\|\Omega\|_1\le\kappa_2/\lambda_0\le\kappa_2/\kappa_1$ ($\|\Omega\|_1\le\|\Omega\|_2=1$). Next $1=\int\Omega^2\le\sup\Omega\cdot\|\Omega\|_1\le\lambda_0^{-1}\kappa_2\|\Omega\|_1^2$, so $\|\Omega\|_1\ge(\lambda_0/\kappa_2)^{1/2}$ and $\Omega(y)\ge\lambda_0^{-1}\kappa_1\|\Omega\|_1\ge\kappa_1(\lambda_0\kappa_2)^{-1/2}\ge\kappa_1/\kappa_2$ (using $\lambda_0\le\kappa_2$). This is (c).
A3.5. (d), Doeblin's argument. Let $\nu=\Omega^2\mu$, a probability measure. The map $W:L^2(\nu)\to\mathcal K$, $Wf=\Omega f$, is unitary (isometric by definition of $\nu$, onto because $1/\Omega$ is bounded). $Q:=\lambda_0^{-1}W^{-1}KW$ acts by $(Qf)(y)=\int r(y,z)f(z)\,d\nu(z)$ with $r(y,z)=k(y,z)/(\lambda_0\Omega(y)\Omega(z))$, which is real, symmetric, continuous, and satisfies $\int r(y,z)\,d\nu(z)=(K\Omega)(y)/(\lambda_0\Omega(y))=1$ for every $y$ (A3.4). Since $r\ge\delta$ and its $\nu$-average in $z$ is $1$, $\delta\le1$. Case $\delta=1$: then $r(y,\cdot)-1\ge0$ has $\nu$-integral $0$, so $r(y,\cdot)=1$ $\nu$-a.e. for every $y$, and $Qf=\langle1,f\rangle_\nu1$. Case $\delta<1$: put $r'=(r-\delta)/(1-\delta)\ge0$, symmetric with $\int r'(y,z)d\nu(z)=1$, and $Q'$ its integral operator on $L^2(\nu)$; then $Q=\delta\,|1\rangle\langle1|_\nu+(1-\delta)Q'$ and $|\langle f,Q'f\rangle_\nu|\le\iint r'(y,z)|f(y)||f(z)|\,d\nu d\nu\le\iint r'(y,z)\frac{|f(y)|^2+|f(z)|^2}2d\nu d\nu=\|f\|_\nu^2$ (symmetry of $r'$ for the second half).
A3.6. Let $\varphi\perp\Omega$ in $\mathcal K$ and $f=W^{-1}\varphi=\varphi/\Omega$. Then $\langle1,f\rangle_\nu=\int\Omega^2(\varphi/\Omega)\,d\mu=\langle\Omega,\varphi\rangle=0$ ($\Omega$ real). So $\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle=\lambda_0\langle f,Qf\rangle_\nu$, which is $0$ in the case $\delta=1$ and $\lambda_0(1-\delta)\langle f,Q'f\rangle_\nu\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|f\|_\nu^2=\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$ in the case $\delta<1$. The bound on $\delta$: $\delta\ge\kappa_1/(\lambda_0\sup\Omega^2)\ge\kappa_1/(\lambda_0(\kappa_2/\lambda_0)^2)=\kappa_1\lambda_0/\kappa_2^2\ge\kappa_1^2/\kappa_2^2$ by A3.4 ($\sup\Omega\le\kappa_2/\lambda_0$) and A3.1.
A3.7. (e): if $K\psi=\lambda_0\psi$, write $\psi=\langle\Omega,\psi\rangle\Omega+\varphi$ with $\varphi\perp\Omega$; then $K\varphi=\lambda_0\varphi$ and $\lambda_0\|\varphi\|^2=\langle\varphi,K\varphi\rangle\le\lambda_0(1-\delta)\|\varphi\|^2$ with $\delta>0$, so $\varphi=0$. ∎

**Lemma A4 (density of continuous functions).** $C(Y)$ is dense in $L^2(Y,\mu)$.

Proof. A4.1. Let $\mathcal S$ be the class of Borel sets $E$ such that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there are $F$ closed and $O$ open with $F\subset E\subset O$, $\mu(O\setminus F)<\varepsilon$. A closed $E$ is in $\mathcal S$: take $F=E$, $O_n=\{y:d(y,E)<1/n\}$; $O_n\downarrow E$, so $\mu(O_n\setminus E)\to0$ by continuity from above. $\mathcal S$ is closed under complements (swap $F\leftrightarrow O^c$, $O\leftrightarrow F^c$). For $E=\bigcup_kE_k$ with $E_k\in\mathcal S$: choose $F_k\subset E_k\subset O_k$ with $\mu(O_k\setminus F_k)<\varepsilon2^{-k-1}$, $O=\bigcup O_k$, and $K$ with $\mu(\bigcup_kF_k\setminus\bigcup_{k\le K}F_k)<\varepsilon/2$, $F=\bigcup_{k\le K}F_k$ closed; then $\mu(O\setminus F)<\varepsilon$. So $\mathcal S$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing the closed sets: all Borel sets.
A4.2. For $E$ Borel and $F\subset E\subset O$ as above ($F\ne\emptyset\ne O^c$; the degenerate cases use the constants $0$, $1$), $h(y)=d(y,O^c)/(d(y,O^c)+d(y,F))$ is continuous, $0\le h\le1$, $h=1$ on $F$, $h=0$ off $O$, so $\|1_E-h\|_2^2\le\mu(O\setminus F)<\varepsilon$.
A4.3. Finite linear combinations of indicators are dense in $L^2$: for $f\in L^2$, the functions $f_n$ obtained by rounding $\operatorname{Re}f$ and $\operatorname{Im}f$ down (toward 0) to the grid $2^{-n}\mathbb Z$ and cutting at modulus $n$ are such combinations, $|f_n|\le|f|$ and $f_n\to f$ pointwise, so $\|f-f_n\|_2\to0$ by dominated convergence. With A4.2 this proves the lemma. ∎
