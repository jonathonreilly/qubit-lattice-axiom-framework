## Step 2. What the floor l_*(g) is, and where the thin fibre stops the account's run

Read: Y 84157-84330 (sec. "A common companion and the torus floor"), outside the brief's ranges because F1 cites (RF35).

**Statement 3.4 of Y (Y 84159-84176), an input of the account.** For each frame and weight prefix there is one family E^(j)(X, P, z) (and a companion of R^(j)) on the infinite level-j lattice that agrees with the torus term whenever the projection of X is injective with its margin and lies one full level-j cell layer inside; "a sufficient margin places X in a rectangle with sides less than half the corresponding torus side". Admissibility (RF34), Y 84170: l L^{K'-i} >= 2 L^{11} M R_i for i <= j, i.e. the torus side in level-i lattice units is at least 2 L^{11} M R_i (R_i = R(x_i) the cube size). The coefficient b_j is a function of the frame and the weights through level j-1 only. Y 84187-84191: the induction is simultaneous over tori of unbounded side; b_j is not defined from domains that fit one torus. Y 84196-84199: walk, propagator, localized background and gauge choices are made "once by a rule natural under covering maps, cube translations and the later dilations".

**The floor (RF35), Y 84322-84330.** "Wrapping domains have d_j(X) >= c l L^{K-j}/M. Their exponential decay is used directly and does not define an additional renormalized coefficient." For a self-calibrated run (RF34) at the terminal level implies it at all earlier levels, and since x_K <= g^{-2} + B_sharp it suffices that l >= l_*(g) := 2 L^{11} M R(g^{-2} + B_sharp).

**Consequence for the thin fibre (derived here, not in Y).** On T^tw_{s,l} with base side l >= l_*(g), condition (RF34) holds in the base at every level, and in the fibre exactly at the levels

  i <= i_s := max{ i : s L^{K-i} >= 2 L^{11} M R(x_i) }

(the left side decreases in i by the factor L per level; R(x_i) is non-increasing in i if R is non-decreasing in its argument, since x_i decreases; so the set of good levels is an initial segment, provided R(y) is non-decreasing; Y's R(y) is a cube-size function of the coupling, and monotonicity is to be checked at its definition: **assumption M**, not verified here). At i = i_s the fibre has

  n_s := s L^{K - i_s} sites, with 2 L^{11} M R(x_{i_s}) <= n_s < 2 L^{12} M R(x_{i_s}).

So the account's run on T^tw cannot be continued past level i_s with the account's own locality: past it, localization domains wrap the fibre, and the wrapping terms' decay exp(-c s L^{K-j}/M) is no longer below the activity bounds. **This is the exact place where the method stops on a thin torus, and it is the place where T1 must be stated.** The natural T1 is therefore not "after K block steps" (the full run to the unit lattice, which needs s >= l_*(g)) but "after i_s block steps", on the lattice Lambda(n_s, M_s) with M_s = l L^{K-i_s}, at the running coupling beta_* := x_{i_s}(beta). T1 as written in `gap-skeleton-final.md` line 82 ("after K block steps", "one fixed n_0") must be changed in these two points.

**The coupling at the stopping level.** x_{i_s} is the running inverse coupling at the length s/n_s ~ s/(2 L^{11} M R) in units in which the coupling at unit length is g. Since x_k decreases by b_k <= B_sharp per step and by at least c per step (Y 1748 (a): c(beta - g^{-2}) - C <= K(beta, g)), x_{i_s} - x_K is between c(K - i_s) - C and B_sharp (K - i_s), and K - i_s = log_L(n_s/s): so beta_* = x_{i_s} >= g^{-2} + c log_L(1/s) - C', which grows as s -> 0. And n_s ~ 2 L^{11} M R(beta_*). **Matching condition for T2.** T2 (W-twist-lat) needs beta_* >= beta_0(n_s) with n_s = n_s(beta_*) ~ 2 L^{11} M R(beta_*). This is a condition on the growth of R(y) against beta_0(n): it holds for all small s if beta_0(C R(y)) = o(y) as y -> infinity. With beta_0(n) of order n^4 log n / delta_0(n) (gap-skeleton-max P2-next) and delta_0(n) >= n^{-q}, it needs R(y) = o(y^{1/(4+q)}) up to logs. **Open:** Y's choice of R(y) (cube-size function, Y 1682 "cube sizes R_k change through integer values") must be read to decide this; it is the first of two quantitative conditions that make T1 usable (the second is the norm of W, Step 4).

**Locality transfer (the port principle, derived here).** Write the twisted model in the frame where V is trivial (as in gap-skeleton-max Fact 3): configurations U on the covering lattice Z^2 x (base torus), n fibre sites, with U(x + n e_1) = Gamma_1 U(x) Gamma_1^{-1}, U(x + n e_2) = Gamma_2 U(x) Gamma_2^{-1}, Gamma_1 Gamma_2 = -Gamma_2 Gamma_1, and the ordinary Wilson weight a_p = 1 - (1/2) Re tr U_p on one fundamental domain (well defined because a_p is conjugation invariant). In this frame the twist-eating configuration is U = 1, and the twisted measure is the ordinary Wilson measure of a field on the cover with conjugation-periodic boundary condition. (Equivalence with the P_0 form of 3.1: the gauge transformation equal to 1 on the fundamental domain [0, n-1]^2 and the seam-crossing links carrying Gamma_i; standard, written in gap-skeleton-max Fact 3; I did not re-derive it beyond that.) Two lifts of a domain X whose fibre projection lies in a rectangle of sides < s/2 differ by a deck translation composed with the constant gauge transformation Ad(Gamma_1^a Gamma_2^b). Every local term of Y is a gauge-invariant function, or covariant under gauge transformations by Y 84238-84246 (rep(O^u) = rep(O)^{u-natural}), so the companion term evaluated on either lift gives the same value. Hence **at every level i <= i_s, every term of Y on a fibre-nonwrapping domain is defined on T^tw by the same companion family as on the periodic torus, with the same bounds.** This needs Y's Statement 3.4 (an input of Y, not proved in Y) with one named change: compatibility under covering maps composed with a constant gauge transformation (Y requires naturality under covering maps; constant gauge covariance is a property of each rule, to be checked rule by rule; the comb-tree gauge rule of Y 84233-84246 has it, since a constant u gives u-natural = u(o) constant).

**What the locality transfer does not give.** (i) Terms on fibre-wrapping domains at levels i <= i_s (Y: "exponential decay used directly"): on T^tw they see the twist, so their bounds need a proof on the twisted torus (the bound has the same form; the object is new). (ii) Global objects: the background field on a small-field region that wraps the fibre (A1), and the coercivity on it (A2); the partition function and the vacuum sector; anything proved by a chessboard estimate (F10). These are taken package by package in Step 3.
