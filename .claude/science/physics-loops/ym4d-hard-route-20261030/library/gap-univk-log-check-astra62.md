## Independence and next row

STR controls the interpolating path. RAR controls the fraction discarded at each physical endpoint. **Corrected STR and quantitative RAR are sufficient open inputs for these two steps. Neither is a necessary reformulation of LOG.** Scalar compatibility and the account's comparison hypotheses are additional inputs. The absence of base smallness is genuine: one can add a constant to every cutoff defect without changing its increments; check 1392 L44–48 separates small seed margins in the converse direction. L121 and L180 do not establish non-equivalence merely by noting the absence of a rate or of a comparison symbol; the class direction and even the definition of the aligned good set use the pair.

- LOG can hold exactly with RAR perfect and STR false. Take one good label with $F(s)=1+4s(1-s)/R^2,\ R>2$, no bad labels, and equal masses $F(0)=F(1)=1$. The polynomial is bounded on $|s|<R$ by $1+4(1+1/R)$, but its zeros $(1\pm\sqrt{1+R^2})/2$ lie inside. At $1/2$ the second derivative of $\log F$ is negative. This also fails the long-fibre version around $1/2$. Thus the prescribed interpolation can fail both tests even at zero endpoint error.
- LOG can hold exactly with STR perfect and quantitative RAR false. Give both runs total mass 1 and the same two positive weights, constant along the interpolation. For arbitrarily large $V$ take $Z_G=e^{-V^2}$ and the discarded weight $1-e^{-V^2}$. The retained sum is positive, log-convex and zero-free; its log fraction costs $V^2$. Even an age-$a$ discarded label can respect an absolute $e^{CV}q_a^a$ allowance on sides with $CV\ge a|\log q_a|+1$.
