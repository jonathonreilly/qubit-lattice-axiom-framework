## A positive tile model for the unaligned multiplier

The obstruction in check 1397 used a private old label. Here every label is common, every live component has age zero, and the bad event is the union of reflected single-tile events. Take one bit at the centre of each of $V$ unit tiles of an even periodic four-dimensional board. Write $S$ for its set of occupied tiles. Fix $b>0$, as small as desired, and
$$
  d_k=d_0\vartheta_\chi^k,\qquad 0<d_0\le\min\{1,4C_\chi^\sharp\}.
$$
Choose $b$ so that $\log(1+2b)$ lies below the prescribed positive upper densities. This uses $C_\chi^\sharp>0$. Use the same positive label weights on every board and at both ends,
$$
 w(\varnothing)=1,\qquad w(S)=d_k b^{|S|}\quad(S\ne\varnothing).
 \tag{4}
$$
Declare the empty label good. For every sufficiently large $k$, each nonempty label has first index $k>n_c$, flag value $\varpi=1/40$, and components born at $k$. A component is a connected component of $S$. The common good mass and the full mass at zero residual source are exactly
$$
 Z_G=1,\qquad \widetilde Z=1-d_k+d_k(1+b)^V .
 \tag{5}
$$

**Verification against the printed bounds.** The extensive lower bound holds even for $Z_G$. The unmarked total is at most $(1+b)^V$. On the allowed common-unaligned disc,
$$
  \sum_S |\mu|^{\mathbf1_{\{S\ne\varnothing\}}}w(S)
  =1+|\mu|d_k\big((1+b)^V-1\big)\le(1+b)^V .
$$
The exact unaligned variation is $d_k((1+b)^V-1)$. Doubling each connected component's majorant multiplies a term by at most $2^{|S|}$, so the marked modulus is still at most $(1+2b)^V$. All old sums for $a\ge1$ and the low-index sum vanish. At $a=0$ the printed age disc has radius one. A fixed extra charge per occupied terminal cell can likewise be paid by reducing $b$ before choosing the volumes. The aligned terms and both ramps are constant.

**Chessboard check.** Let $\nu$ be (4) divided by (5). This is a probability on the tile bits with $Z_G/\widetilde Z=\nu(S=\varnothing)$. The tile event $E_t$ is occupation of $t$. Its reflected copies cover exactly the bad set. The unnormalized law is the sum of $(1-d_k)\delta_\varnothing$ and $d_k(1+b)^V$ times the product Bernoulli law of parameter $p=b/(1+b)$. Thus it obeys the mixed chessboard inequality for this single board. Indeed, condition on the two terms of the mixture. In either term the expectation of $\prod_t f_t(\sigma_t)$ factors. Hölder over the two-point mixing law bounds the mixture of those products by the product of the $V$-th roots of the corresponding constant-profile expectations. This holds for every choice of nonnegative single-tile functions, including indicators. With face data fixed, the disseminated bad-event root is
$$
 \nu(S=\Lambda)^{1/V}
 =\frac{b}{1+b}\left(1+\frac{1-d_k}{d_k(1+b)^V}\right)^{-1/V}.
 \tag{6}
$$
This positive board law has the partition identity and tile cover required by PART and TILE, together with the mixed chessboard inequality. Equation (6) tests the remaining input PROD.

**Rate on the two sides.** From (5),
$$
 \frac1V\left|\log\frac{Z_G}{\widetilde Z}\right|
 \ge \log(1+b)-\frac{k|\log\vartheta_\chi|+|\log d_0|}{V}.
 \tag{7}
$$
For the inequalities on the geometric torus family, choose the smaller $V$ to be the first lattice volume $(2L^{m_0+\mathsf w})^4$ at least $2(k|\log\vartheta_\chi|+|\log d_0|)/\log(1+b)$. Adjacent such volumes have fixed ratio $L^4$. Both $V$ and $L^4V$ are of order $k$. On each side (7) is at least $\tfrac12\log(1+b)>0$ and (6) tends to $b/(1+b)$. This excludes **every** $r_K=o(1)$ on unrestricted sides as a consequence of these inequalities. For $\kappa_0>8$, III 53901–53907 gives $\Pi_n\le Cn^{-(\kappa_0-4)/2}(\log n)^{c_3}$, so on both boards $V\sqrt{\epsilon}\to0$ and $V\vartheta_\chi^k\to0$ (using the corresponding volume on each). Even the numerical smallness test $V\sqrt{\epsilon}\le 1/2$ does not fix the inference. A floor on the **actual** sum of disseminated roots would fail here.

There is also a direct check. At a specified site the odds for adding the first occupied tile to a completely empty outside are $d_k b$. With any occupied tile already present the odds are $b$. Thus the total unaligned weight on $v$ sites satisfies $U_{v+1}=(1+b)U_v+d_kb$, $U_0=0$. Solving gives (5) independently of the mixture argument. Pricing a first failure against an all-good background is consistent with the extensive loss (7). The marked polynomial is $1+\mu U_V$ and its zero $-1/U_V$ approaches zero on these volumes.
