## One boundary test on the signed scalar

Here is the precise open sublemma used below. Fix the native constants, the common scaffold, and a fresh geometric joint QC25 cell at a newly determined cut. Consider admitted pairs whose read on this double block lies in one Cartan subgroup in the retained frames, with a common physical angular increment \(xH\) on that crossing. The native cutoff Jacobian has its boundary address. Require that *both signs* at \(r=a,1\) pass in both runs with the same assignments. Write \(D_h(a;x)\) for the actual signed scalar of 1559 on this ray.

**Open sublemma.** Uniformly in these admitted configurations, the cut, the volume, \(x\) and \(0\le a<1\),
\[
 \left|\mathcal O_h(a,x)\right|\le K\ell_n,\qquad
 \mathcal O_h(a,x)=\tfrac12\{D_h(a;x)-D_h(a;-x)\}.
 \tag{C}
\]
This tests (S) itself. If the quotient in (C) is unbounded, at least one of the two actual \(|D_h|/\ell_n\) is unbounded. A proof of (C) alone would leave every even contribution and the rays with only one passing orientation untested. ## 1. Solve the stationary transmission equation at the cut

Use unit positive Wilson plaquette weights and a periodic four-torus. Put the last coarse spacing equal to one, \(L\ge3\) odd, and let the two bare spacings be \(\eta=L^{-(j+1)}\) and \(\eta'=\eta/L\). Work with the level-\(j+1\) region made of entire slabs normal to direction 2, with both a slab and its complement present. Take the earlier layers full. Choose a period and slab widths made of complete blocks, large enough for embedded double blocks. Tangential determining rows have spacings \(h_m=1\) in the last region and \(h_m=1/L\) outside, where level \(j\) is retained. In transverse order their centred intervals \(J_m\) tile the circle, with complete finer intervals on the outer side of each cut face. This is the two-level geometry of the determining problem (O 9607). Whether a common RG history selects it is a separate test.

In a Cartan representative put \(H=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), \(U_1(i)=\exp(iu_iH)\) with \(i\) the bare transverse index, and \(U_\nu=1\) for \(\nu=2,3,4\). Prescribe horizontal angles \(h_m f_m\) on the determining rows above \(J_m\), and identity on the other determining rows, including the outward normal rows. Work on the small logarithm branch. Row 1561 and check 1562 show that one horizontal blocking sums the \(L\) transverse angles. In the other directions the mean angle vanishes by translation or reflection in the horizontal displacement. This applies to the individual complete reads on both sides of the cut, also to an outward normal row. Thus the exact constraints are
\[
 F_i=u_i/\eta,\qquad
 \frac1{N_m}\sum_{i\in J_m}F_i=f_m,\qquad N_m=h_m/\eta.
 \tag{1}
\]
Here and below \(i\in J_m\) refers to the site centre. The cell endpoints are bare faces for both meshes. Full periodic action keeps both plaquettes adjoining a bare interface face.

Let \(r_e\) be a real variable at each face between two determining intervals. Interpolate the face values affinely to \(p\) on every \(J_m\). Prescribe
\[
 F_{i+1}-F_i=\eta T_\eta(p(x_{i+1/2})),\qquad
 T_\eta(t)=\eta^{-2}\arcsin(\eta^2t).
 \tag{2}
\]
The sine of the plaquette angle is then \(\eta^2p\), with one shared value at the interface. Its discrete divergence is constant on each \(J_m\), including the site beside the face. This verifies the full stationary equation as follows. In the Cartan direction the Wilson force on horizontal links is the backward minus forward sine. All other components vanish by horizontal translation or zero curvature. Give each determining horizontal row over \(J_m\) the same Cartan multiplier, constant along direction 1. In its pairing with the linearized block mean the endpoint contour derivatives cancel by horizontal telescoping. The straight terms give \(N_m^{-3}\) times the multiplier on every bare horizontal link of that transverse block, by iteration of the \(L^{-3}\) count. At each determining height the sum telescopes *along* a complete horizontal periodic line. Choose the multiplier equal to \(N_m^3\) times the forward-minus-backward sine, with the common Wilson normalization. Other rows have zero multiplier. Constant diagonal conjugation fixes field and multipliers and negates both perpendicular colour directions, so the first variation of the augmented action vanishes there as well. This proves stationarity for arbitrary tangent variations of the original four-dimensional problem.

**The face equation.** Let \(\phi_e\) be the piecewise-linear hat equal to one at face \(e\), zero at the other faces. Its integral is \(H_e=(h_-+h_+)/2\), with \(h_\pm\) the adjoining widths. Subtract the two means in (1). A bare increment at the \(k\)-th edge from the left endpoint of the left interval has weight \(k/N_-\). On the right the weights decrease linearly to zero. The interface increment has weight one. Therefore the equation for the two sides of the cut is
\[
 y_e=\frac{f_{m+1}-f_m}{H_e}
     =\frac{\eta}{H_e}\sum_{\text{bare edges }i+1/2}
          \phi_e(x_{i+1/2})\, T_\eta(p(x_{i+1/2})) .
 \tag{3}
\]
The hats sum to one. Multiplication by \(H_e\) and summation over faces gives zero period increment in (2). Integrating (2) and fixing one mean then gives (1) everywhere. This derives the boundary flux from the lower rows themselves. The unknown end flux in row 1561 (7) was an input when the calculation stopped at the end of a uniform slab.

For the linear part of (3) write \(A_\eta=\operatorname{diag}(H_e)^{-1}M_\eta\). With consistent face indexing,
\[
 M_\eta=M_0+\frac{\eta^2}{6}\mathcal K,\qquad
 M_0|_{J_m}=\frac{h_m}{6}\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \mathcal K|_{J_m}=\frac1{h_m}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The blocks are assembled at the two faces of each interval. Indeed the off-diagonal entry is \(\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N_m-1}(k/N_m)(1-k/N_m)=h_m/6-\eta^2/(6h_m)\). The hat sum splits equally between the ends in the trapezoidal sum. This gives the diagonal. In particular \(M_\eta\) is positive definite and \(\mathcal K\) is positive on the complement of constants. The normalized diagonal is at least \(2/3\), the nonnegative off-diagonals sum to at most \(1/3\), and the rows sum to one. A maximum component gives \(\|A_\eta^{-1}\|_\infty\le3\), uniformly in the period, the widths and the position of the cut.

Take \(R=6\|y\|_\infty\le1/4\). For \(|t|\le R\), \(|T_\eta'(t)-1|\le2R^2\eta^4\). The weights in (3) after normalization sum to one. Its nonlinear remainder \(E_\eta\) thus satisfies \(E_\eta(0)=0\) and has Lipschitz constant at most \(2R^2\eta^4\) on the ball. The map \(r\mapsto A_\eta^{-1}(y-E_\eta(r))\) preserves that ball and contracts by at most \(6R^2<1/2\). This constructs both exact stationary solutions. The implicit function theorem gives analytic dependence on the data near zero. From (2), plaquette angles are bounded by \(2R\eta^2\) and physical currents by \(2R/\min h_m\), up to the fixed normalization of \(H\). The mean fixes the potential within \(2R\max h_m\) of \(f_m\). Small \(f,R\) give the curvature and current tests of O 9616–9623, and small test holonomies, including mixed plaquettes, since each of their finitely many forming links is within \(C_L(\|f\|_\infty+R)\) of one. The sketched orbit-uniqueness theorem then identifies the regular orbit on the prescribed geometry. A regular stationary solution alone does not furnish the history’s failure sets or its two passing points.

**All real amplitudes on the two spacings.** For \(s=A,B\), let \(r_s^0=A_0^{-1}y_s\), where \(A_0=\operatorname{diag}(H_e)^{-1}M_0\). From (3)–(4),
\[
 \|r_s-r_s^0\|_\infty\le
 3\left(\frac{2\eta_s^2}{3(\min h)^2}R_s+2R_s^3\eta_s^4\right)
 \le C_L R_s\eta_s^2,\qquad R_s=6\|y_s\|_\infty .
 \tag{5}
\]
This holds on the actual interface face. On each \(J_m\), take the quadratic \(P_{s,m}\) of mean \(f_{s,m}\) whose derivative interpolates \(r_s^0\) at the two ends. The integral of that derivative between consecutive sites of the interval equals its midpoint value times \(\eta_s\). Equations (2) and (5) bound the error of each increment of \(F_i\) by \(C_L R_s\eta_s^3\). The oscillation of \(F_i-P_{s,m}(x_i)\) within the interval is at most \(C_L R_s\eta_s^2\). The midpoint quadrature error of its polynomial mean is at most \(\eta_s^2\|P_{s,m}''\|_\infty/24\), with \(|P_{s,m}''|\le 6\|y_s\|_\infty/\min h\). Equation (1) fixes the remaining constant. Every forming horizontal angle, including those just inside the cut, is therefore the integral of this polynomial over a forming interval of length \(1/L\), within \(C_L R_s\eta_s^2\). On the fine-determined side that angle is exactly \(h_m f_{s,m}\).

For equal determining data, all common forming links differ by at most \(C_L R\eta^2\), and all outgoing targets agree. Bounded-length products, the smooth endpoint means and the regular pivot determinant on a smaller chart give the same bound with a fixed \(C_L\) for each log pivot at either radius when the post-strip left multipliers agree there. This also holds for noncommuting common fluctuations in the comparison frame. For native axial frames the required comparison includes the actual frame identifications. In the diagonal example below each normalization can be diagonal, and conjugation leaves its Cartan fluctuation fixed.

There is a useful relaxation for commuting rays and common Cartan left multipliers. Put \(\delta y=y_B-y_A\), \(\bar R=\max_s R_s\). On the bounded set of intervals read by a row, subtract \(c=\delta f_{m_0}\) from \(f_B\), with any one interval \(m_0\) of the row. Adding a constant \(c\) to the entire profile adds \(\eta_s c\) to each horizontal bare angle and preserves (2). On the row it adds the same open transport angle to all paths with the same displacement, including the target. Their relative traces are also preserved after a commuting fluctuation. The remaining variations of \(\delta f\) there are bounded by \(C_L\|\delta y\|_\infty\) by telescoping adjacent means. Since \(\|r_B^0-r_A^0\|_\infty\le3\|\delta y\|_\infty\), the polynomial reconstruction just proved gives a per-row bound \(C_L(\|\delta y\|_\infty+\bar R\eta^2)\) for the complete two-radius inverse-pivot difference. This permits an arbitrary common-mode displacement of the boundary potential within the stated branch.

In the matched geometric filing with common free factors, and with any cutoff Jacobian at its boundary address, this controls the *signed scalar* whenever these are the actual reads at the two passing points. For equal profile data the whole cell costs \(C_{L,M}(1+\check R_n)^4R\eta^2\), including for a row straddling the interface. The same bound covers a separate geometric background-pivot filing. Indeed \(n\le j+1\), \(\check R_n\le L(\log x_n)^{r_{\rm pr}}\), \(c_2\ge 2p_0\ge10r_{\rm pr}\), and \(\ell_n\ge E_0 C^\natural_{\rm cl}(\log x_n)^{c_2}L^{-\theta_2 n/4}\), \(0<\theta_2<1/4\). ## 2. Evaluate the odd part of the transverse Jacobian exactly

This test uses any Cartan row in (C), without the slab ansatz. At a fixed radius \(r\in\{a,1\}\), leave every actual cutoff multiplier in its place and turn off only the signed increment \(\pm rxH\). This defines a reference row for the algebra. The radial cutoff has the same value for both signs. On the common small logarithm chart the reference straight angle is also the midpoint of the two solved straight angles. No passing test at this midpoint is needed. Write \(q_0,w,u_i\) for the reference straight, target and noncentral open *angles*. Set \(s_i=u_i-q_0\) and \(z_0=w-q_0=\sum_i\alpha_i s_i\). These reference angles can depend on the run and on \(r\).

Write \(C\) for the selected column of \(L\) starts, of total weight \(b=L^{-3}\). An increment of angle \(z\) on its crossing adds \(z\) to its \(L\) open angles. Since \(w=bq+\sum_i\alpha_i u_i\) on the Cartan chart, the new straight angle is exactly \(q_0-z\). Define \(k(t)=t\cot t\), \(k(0)=1\), and
\[
 H_{s,r}(z)=\operatorname{sinc}(z_0+z)
 \left[k(z_0+z)-\sum_i\alpha_i k\big(s_i+(1+\mathbf1_{i\in C})z\big)\right].
 \tag{9}
\]
The factor multiplying each of the two transverse directions in the spherical differential of \(Y\) 75233–75246 is \(H_{s,r}(z)\), from the transverse Hessian eigenvalue \(k\) and the inverse target-log differential \(\operatorname{sinc}\). On the common Cartan axis the eigenvalue is \(b\). The forward determinant is \(bH_{s,r}(z)^2\). We use the positive branch guaranteed by the real row bound there and at \(Y\) 75248–75268. In particular (9) has already solved the pivot at the perturbed target.

Let \(J_s(r,x)=\log[H_{s,r}(-rx)/H_{s,r}(rx)]\). The half difference of log *inverse* pivots is exactly \(J_s\). On the specified geometric filing the resulting odd scalar is
\[
 \mathcal O_h(a,x)=\Delta J(a,x)-a^2\Delta J(1,x).
 \tag{10}
\]
All even weights cancel before taking the modulus, including any radius-dependent even weight. This argument retains the actual zero-shift convention separately at the two radii. A change of branch of the cutoff between \(a\) and \(1\) need not be small.

I tested whether reflection of the two endpoints of a stationary column forces (10) to vanish. Suppose \(z_0=0\) and the weighted multiset of *all* the \(s_i\) is symmetric under sign reversal at the reference point. For example the outward normal row in the planar construction has this symmetry. Let \(\nu=\sum_{i\in C}\alpha_i\delta_{s_i}\), the submeasure of the chosen column. Evenness of \(k\) gives the exact identity
\[
 \frac{H_{s,r}(z)-H_{s,r}(-z)}{-\operatorname{sinc}z}
 =\int\!\big[k(t+2z)-k(t-2z)-k(t+z)+k(t-z)\big]\,d\nu(t).
 \tag{11}
\]
Indeed the all-column sum with shifts \(+z,-z\) cancels by reflection. The selected column replaces the shifts by \(+2z,-2z\). Thus a sign-symmetric *selected column* gives \(J_s=0\) at that radius. This proves (C) with zero when both stationary reads have that symmetry at both radii, even if their determinants and the two even totals differ. Reflection of the whole row is weaker. Its derivative is
\[
 \frac{d}{dz}\{-\log(bH_{s,r}(z)^2)\}_{z=0}
   =\frac{2\int k'(t)\,d\nu(t)}{1-\sum_i\alpha_i k(s_i)} .
 \tag{12}
\]
The denominator is positive on the chart. This gives a direct check on the mixed coefficients from the Gram expansion. On the outward row of §1, \(q_0=w=0\), so the selected \(s_i\) are the \(\gamma_l\). Since \(k'(t)=-2t/3+O(t^3)\), differentiating (12) in \(\tau\) at zero gives \(-4\sum_l\dot\gamma_l/(3L)\) in each run. Applying the two-radius subtraction recovers (8), (8a) and (8d). On the horizontal ray of (8b), all noncentral anchors move by \(z\). With \(\kappa=(1-b)/b\), the shifted arguments in (9) become \(z_0+\kappa z\) and \(s_i+z/b\). Using \(\sum_i\alpha_i s_i=z_0\) in the first-order expansion of \(\partial_z[-2\log H]\) gives \(-2(1+b)z_0/(3b)\) to first order in the background, as in (8b). This check uses the spherical Hessian on the solved row directly.

In (8) the reflected mate of the selected column is a *different* column. Reversing the field on both would change the tested ray. On fixed smaller charts the bounded derivatives of (9) give
\[
 |J_B(r,x)-J_A(r,x)|
 \le C_L r|x|\left(|\Delta z_0|+\max_i|\Delta s_i|\right)
 \tag{13}
\]
for commonly indexed regular rows on that chart. To see the factor \(r|x|\), integrate \(\partial_z\log H_{s,r}\) on \([-rx,rx]\), then use its bounded data derivative between the two angular arrays in a smaller convex neighbourhood of zero. The variables \(s_i,z_0\) are target-relative, so a common endpoint transport cancels. The reflection identity settles the symmetric-column case. ## 3. Average the boundary discrepancy before pricing the row

At a cut the error need not be small on every bare link for (10) to be small. The Cartan map permits a direct test of this possibility. Start with the unshifted stationary centre, or with known commuting shifts on its nonpivots. Use compatible real angle lifts on the complete footprints of the row. The argument allows both a fixed small-field lift and the addition of vertex gradients. Let \(A\) denote the *angle per link* in the next two identities. On a block of \(D\) bare spacings define
\[
 Q_D A(y,\mu)=D^{-4}\sum_{x\in B_D(y)}
       \sum_{t=0}^{D-1} A(x+t e_\mu,\mu).
\]
At \(D=L\), QC1–QC3 give the true Cartan forming angle \(Q_L A+\nabla\sigma\), where the vertex term is the difference of the two averaged end-contour angles, with sign fixed by orientation. Next, \(Q_L\) sends a vertex gradient to the coarse gradient of the block mean. The identity \(Q_L Q_D=Q_{LD}\) follows because the starting blocks tile the large block and the \(L\) successive \(D\)-segments concatenate to the long segment for each starting offset. These identities prove by induction that on every closed linear combination of complete paths the iterated average equals \(Q_D A\) exactly. No replacement of an individual non-Abelian transport by a plain average is involved here. At the forming scale each reference \(s_i=u_i-q_0\) for an unshifted centre is just such a closed combination, by the linearity of the Cartan endpoint means and by the return along the central corridor. So is \(z_0\). This is where angular relative data supply a cheaper test than pointwise transport of a complete bare row.

Block run B once to the coarser bare lattice. At forming spacing \(\lambda=1/L\) in outgoing units put \(D=\lambda/\eta\). Let \(\mathcal U\) contain all bare path footprints in these closed combinations, padded by the extra fine blocks needed to compute the B input. On the common \(\eta\)-lattice let \(E_e\) be the B-minus-A angular link discrepancy *per unit length*, after that first block and any compatible common-frame alignment. Vertex gradients may be subtracted before estimating it, because every combination used is closed. For a fixed oriented bare link and a fixed forming segment, at most \(D\) of the \(D^4\) starting paths can use that link. Its transverse coordinates fix three coordinates of the start, and there are at most \(D\) longitudinal starts. The number of forming segments per row and all outer contour weights are bounded at fixed \(L\). Consequently the actual angular error satisfies
\[
 \max_i|\Delta s_i|+|\Delta z_0|
 \le C_L\frac{\eta^4}{\lambda^3}
          \sum_{e\in\mathcal U\ {\rm on\ the}\ \eta{\rm\ lattice}} |E_e|
       + C_L m_r .
 \tag{14}
\]
Here \(m_r\) bounds discrepancies of the actual angular nonpivot shifts on that row at radius \(r\), in the chosen Cartan frames. The target of the unshifted stationary centre is its own actual average. To check the last term, changing the nonpivot angles by \(\delta m\) changes every open anchor by a bounded sum of \(\delta m\).  |E_e|\le C(\eta^\alpha d^{-1-\alpha}+\eta/d).
 \tag{15}
\]
Then its contribution to (14) is at most \(C_L\{\eta^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}+\eta(1+|\log\eta|)\}\). Indeed split at \(u=\eta^{\alpha/(1+\alpha)}\). The inner layer costs \(C_Lu\). A shell with distances between \(2^p u\) and \(2^{p+1}u\) costs \(C_L[\eta^\alpha(2^p u)^{-\alpha}+\eta]\). Sum the geometric series to physical distance \(O_L(1)\).
