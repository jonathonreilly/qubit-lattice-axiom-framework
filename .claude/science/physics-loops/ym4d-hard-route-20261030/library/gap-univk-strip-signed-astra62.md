## What the level sum and Theorem T read

Take the absolute value **after** forming an actual signed contribution. If \(d_{b,k',c}\) already sums all the strip occurrences assigned to one class, reading level and cell in the same exponent, then
\[
 \left|\sum_{k'=b}^k d_{b,k',c}\right|
 \le \sum_{k'=b}^k|d_{b,k',c}|
 \le C(k-b+1)\ell_b
\]
given the proposed bound in every such group. The exceptional T-born boundary member of class \(b-1\) can be anchored at a zone-\(b\) cell, starting at its birth at level \(b\) (III 13380–13393). Including that *separate* charge gives \(C(k-b+1)(\ell_b+\ell_{b-1})\) when both estimates hold, omitting absent classes. If one has only a bound for **one strip** at \(c\), the middle inequality needs the sum over all the operator incidences there. Bounding the sum of the absolute **endpoint differences** of the distinct occurrences suffices. A direct uniform estimate of their signed aggregate would also suffice for that real exponent. \(Y\) 62128 counts active drop-out marks. III 44764–44779 bounds a component's age. Neither bounds this incidence sum.

The published analytic estimates have a more specific order of operations. III 13337–13379 sums the absolute values of individual zone-resolved boundary members with their domain weights.
