\]

The \(L\) terms on the central axial line cancel the straight return identically when the pivot varies. In every other term a pivot variation changes only the straight return. \[
 D(f)=\frac1L\sum_{q=-k_L}^{k_L}\bigl[1-\alpha_q\cot\alpha_q\bigr],
 \qquad [1-x\cot x]_{x=0}=0 .
\]
The factor \(1/L\) counts all \(L^3\) starting points at a given \(q\). The \(L\) central terms give the separate \(w\). \]

In one blocking, the two internal contour means of a horizontal row cancel by translation along direction 1. \[

 I_m=\{md-(d-1)/2,\ldots,md+(d-1)/2\}.                        \tag{1}
\]
The energy in this ansatz is proportional to \(\sum_i[1-\cos(u_{i+1}-u_i)]\).

**Why the reduced stationarity test suffices for all SU(2) variations.** Put \(s_{i+1/2}=\sin(u_{i+1}-u_i)\). The Wilson derivative on direction-1 bonds is proportional to \(s_{i-1/2}-s_{i+1/2}\). At a diagonal field the off-colour directions have zero first variation, both for the action and for a coarse-row derivative paired with a Cartan multiplier. For arbitrary Cartan variations the one-step differential in the outgoing horizontal angle is the averaged straight sum plus the difference of the two averaged internal contour angles at the end blocks. A given fine horizontal bond occurs in \(L\) straight terms in that pairing, one for each longitudinal step, with the multiplier of its unique transverse block and weight \(L^{-4}\). Thus whenever \(s_{i+1/2}-s_{i-1/2}\) is constant on each \(I_m\), in unweighted bond-sum coordinates take the outgoing multiplier to be \(d^3(s_{i+1/2}-s_{i-1/2})H\) on block \(m\) for the unit-weight action \(A(U)\). The argument allows variations that break translation symmetry and change colour.

**Exact equation for the constrained fields.** In outgoing spatial units, write \(F_i=d u_i\). Interpolate numbers \(r_{m+1/2}\) linearly between successive block faces at half integers, calling the interpolant \(p(x)\). Set \(T_d(t)=d^2\arcsin(t/d^2)\) and impose
\[
 F_{i+1}-F_i=d^{-1}T_d\big(p((i+1/2)/d)\big).                 \tag{2}
\]
This gives \(s_{i+1/2}=d^{-2}p((i+1/2)/d)\), hence precisely the piecewise-constant current just proved sufficient. The constraint is the mean \(d^{-1}\sum_{I_m}F_i=b_m\). Subtract neighbouring means, telescope each \(F_{i+d}-F_i\) into \(d\) increments, and count the occurrences at each edge. At \(x=m+1/2+t/d\) the count is \(d-|t|\). Thus the equation for the face values is
\[
 b_{m+1}-b_m=\frac1d\sum_{|t|<d}(1-|t|/d)\,
                 T_d\!\left(p(m+1/2+t/d)\right).                 \tag{3}
\]
Conversely, summing (3) over a period makes the left side zero. The hat weights at each fine edge add to one across the adjacent faces, so (2) has zero total increment. It integrates to a periodic \(F\). Fix its additive constant by the mean in one block. Equation (3) then gives (1) in every block. At \(d=1\), as a normalization check, (3) reads \(b_{m+1}-b_m=\arcsin(r_{m+1/2})\), the original Wilson sine-flux relation.

With \(T_d(t)\) replaced by \(t\), (3) has matrix \(A_d\), with diagonal \((2d^2+1)/(3d^2)\) and each neighbour \((d^2-1)/(6d^2)\). The diagonal is \(d^{-1}\sum_{|t|<d}(1-|t|/d)^2\). Let \(E_d(r)\) be the difference of the right side of (3) from \(A_dr\). For \(\|r\|_\infty\le R\le1/4\), the derivative of \(T_d(t)-t\) is bounded by \(2R^2d^{-4}\), by the derivative of arcsine. There is also a local test of the proposed boundary forcing. Retain the one-colour profile on an interval of complete transverse blocks in both meshes. Suppose their full plaquette stencils and mean constraints give (2)–(3) on its interior, and \(\|r^A\|_\infty,\|r^B\|_\infty\le R\le1/4\) there and on its two end faces. Write \(H_d(r)\) for the right side of (3), and \(\delta r=r^B-r^A\) in common face coordinates. Set
\[
 e_0=\|\Delta b^B-\Delta b^A\|_{\infty,{\rm int}}+
                  \tfrac23 R d^{-2}+4R^3d^{-4},
 \qquad q=\tfrac12(1-R^2)^{-1/2}<1 .
\]
At an interior face, the mean derivative of \(H_{Ld}\) on the segment from \(r^A\) to \(r^B\) has diagonal at least \(2/3\) because \(T'_{Ld}\ge1\). The sum of the two off-diagonals is at most \((1-R^2)^{-1/2}/3\) by the positive interpolation weights. The residual of \(r^A\) in the finer equation has modulus at most \(e_0\), since \(H_t(r)=A_t r+E_t(r)\) and \(\|A_{Ld}-A_d\|_\infty\le 2/(3d^2)\). Consequently
\[
 |\delta r_e|\le \tfrac32 e_0+
                 q\max\{|\delta r_{e-1}|,|\delta r_{e+1}|\}
\]
at each interior face \(e\). If \(s(e)\) is its distance in face steps to the interval ends, then
\[
 |\delta r_e|\le \frac{3e_0}{2(1-q)}
             +q^{s(e)}\max_{\partial}|\delta r| .                \tag{7}
\]
Indeed the right side dominates the boundary values and is a supersolution of the preceding inequality. A positive interior maximum of the excess would be at most \(q\) times itself. This comparison retains the *difference* of the actual end fluxes. Choosing them independently in a cut slab would leave their values at the same native inner point unverified. A large upper bound from (7) would still need a lower bound for the signed cell product and two passing evaluations.
