### 1.2 Proof of Theorem 1

#### Proof of Part 1
On $H_0$, $T(\beta_t, \beta_s) = M_{\beta_s} C_{\beta_t} M_{\beta_s}$ is a real-analytic family of positive trace-class self-adjoint operators for $(\beta_t, \beta_s) \in (0, \infty)^2$ with strictly positive continuous kernel. Hence its top eigenvalue $\lambda_0(\beta_t, \beta_s)$ is simple and real-analytic, with unique positive unit eigenvector $\Omega(\beta_t, \beta_s)$. At $(\beta, \beta)$:
$$\partial_{\beta_t} T = M_\beta C_\beta' M_\beta, \qquad \partial_{\beta_s} T = -\tfrac{1}{2}(V T + T V).$$
First-order perturbation of the simple top eigenvalue gives $\partial_{\beta_t}\log\lambda_0 = \lambda_0^{-1}\langle \Omega, M_\beta C_\beta' M_\beta \Omega \rangle$ and $\partial_{\beta_s}\log\lambda_0 = -\langle \Omega, V \Omega \rangle$. Compressing $r^{-1} \partial_{\beta_s}\widehat{T} = \lambda^{-1} \partial_{\beta_s} T - (\partial_{\beta_s}\log\lambda_0) r^{-1} \widehat{T}$ to $\widetilde{F}_r$ (where $T\widetilde{F}_r = \widetilde{F}_r T = \lambda \widetilde{F}_r$) yields
$$\widetilde{b}_r^{(M)} = \frac{1}{\lambda}\widetilde{F}_r\left(-\frac{V T + T V}{2}\right)\widetilde{F}_r + \langle V \rangle_\Omega \widetilde{F}_r = \widetilde{F}_r\bigl(\langle V \rangle_\Omega - V\bigr)\widetilde{F}_r.$$
By the chain rule along $(\beta_t, \beta_s) = (\beta, \beta)$ and $(\xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)$ at $\xi = 1$:
$$\partial_\beta \widehat{T}(\beta,\beta) = (\partial_{\beta_t} + \partial_{\beta_s})\widehat{T}\big|_{\beta,\beta}, \qquad \left.\partial_\xi \widehat{T}(\xi\beta, \xi^{-1}\beta)\right|_{\xi=1} = \beta(\partial_{\beta_t} - \partial_{\beta_s})\widehat{T}\big|_{\beta,\beta}.$$
Compressing to $\widetilde{F}_r$ and conjugating by the frozen isometry $U$ gives (1.1), and adding/subtracting gives (1.2).

For absolute convergence at any real $t > 0$: since $0 \le V \le 2|\mathcal{P}|$, $\|\widetilde{b}_r^{(M)}\| \le 2|\mathcal{P}|$; by row 1618 Theorem 1(1)–(2) (and its verbatim $U(1)$/5D counterparts), $|b_\psi| \le C_{n,\beta}(1 + m)$, so $\|\widetilde{b}_r^{(E)}\| \le C_{n,\beta}(1 + m) + 2|\mathcal{P}|$. By row 1618 Corollary 1C, $G(t) < \infty$ and $W_t < \infty$ for every real $t > 0$, whence $N_E$, $N_M$, $A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}$ converge absolutely and satisfy (1.3).

For (1.4)–(1.5): since $\operatorname{Tr}(P^t) = 1 + G(t) < \infty$ for every $t > 0$, $P^{t/2}$ is Hilbert–Schmidt and $\langle V \rangle_\Omega - V$ is a bounded multiplication operator on $L^2(\pi)$, so $K_t := P^{t/2}(\langle V \rangle_\Omega - V) P^{t/2}$ is trace class. Because $J Q = \bigoplus_{0<r<1} J_r$ commutes with $P$ and $J_r \le F_r$:
$$\operatorname{Tr}(J Q K_t) = \sum_{0<r<1} \operatorname{Tr}\bigl(J_r F_r P^{t/2}(\langle V \rangle_\Omega - V) P^{t/2} F_r\bigr) = \sum_{0<r<1} r^t \operatorname{Tr}(J_r b_r^{(M)}) = N_M(t,\beta).$$
Let $J_{L,Q} := T_{L,Q}^* \Gamma_{L,Q}^\dagger T_{L,Q}$. Since $(\operatorname{ran} E_Q)^{L+1}$ is finite-dimensional, $\Gamma_{L,Q} = T_{L,Q} T_{L,Q}^*$ has closed range $\operatorname{ran} T_{L,Q} = (\ker T_{L,Q}^*)^\perp$, so $J_{L,Q}$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\operatorname{ran} T_{L,Q}^* = \operatorname{span}\{P^{1+a}\operatorname{ran} E_Q : 0 \le a \le L\} \subset \operatorname{ran}(JQ)$. Conversely, for every $0 < r < 1$ and $g \in \operatorname{ran} E_Q$, applying the polynomial $[1 - (x - r)^2]^q$ of row 1628 (17) to $P g \in \operatorname{ran} J_{0,Q}$ yields a vector in $\operatorname{ran} J_{2q, Q}$ converging in norm to $r F_r g$. Since $F_r \operatorname{ran} E_Q = F_r Q \operatorname{ran} E = F_r \operatorname{ran} E$ for $r < 1$ and $P$ is compact and injective on $H_0$, $J_{L,Q} \uparrow J Q$ strongly as $L \to \infty$. Because $K_t$ is trace class and $\|J_{L,Q}\| \le 1$, approximating $K_t$ in trace norm by finite-rank operators gives $J_{L,Q} K_t \to J Q K_t$ in trace norm. Hence
$$N_M(t,\beta) = \lim_{L\to\infty}\operatorname{Tr}(J_{L,Q} K_t) = \lim_{L\to\infty}\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(\Gamma_{L,Q}^\dagger T_{L,Q} K_t T_{L,Q}^*\right),$$
whose $(a,b)$-block is $E_Q P^{1+a+t/2}(\langle V \rangle_\Omega - V) P^{1+b+t/2} E_Q$, proving (1.4). At $t = 2v$, time-reversibility of the stationary Markov chain $(X_\tau)$ (self-adjointness of $P$ on real $L^2(\pi)$) gives $\mathbb{E}_\pi[\phi_1(X_{-m_1}) f(X_0) \phi_2(X_{m_2})] = \langle P^{m_1}\phi_1, f P^{m_2}\phi_2\rangle = \langle \phi_1, P^{m_1} f P^{m_2}\phi_2\rangle$, yielding (1.5). $\square$

#### Proof of Part 2
Let $\psi \in \operatorname{ran}\widetilde{F}_r$ be a unit eigenvector at $\lambda = r\lambda_0$. Because $C_\beta$ is diagonal in the commuting link-spin projections $\Pi_{\mathbf{k}}$ with eigenvalues $c_{\mathbf{k}}(\beta) = \prod_e c_{k_e}(\beta) = e^{-\eta(\mathbf{k};\beta)} \in (0, 1]$, we have $q_\psi(\mathbf{k}) := \|\Pi_{\mathbf{k}} M_\beta \psi\|^2 = \|M_\beta\psi\|^2 \hat{q}_\psi(\mathbf{k})$ and $w_\psi(\mathbf{k}) = \lambda^{-1} c_{\mathbf{k}}(\beta) q_\psi(\mathbf{k})$. Whenever $\hat{q}_\psi(\mathbf{k}) = 0$, $w_\psi(\mathbf{k}) = 0$ as well; whenever $\hat{q}_\psi(\mathbf{k}) > 0$,
$$\log\frac{w_\psi(\mathbf{k})}{\hat{q}_\psi(\mathbf{k})} = \log\frac{\|M_\beta\psi\|^2}{\lambda} - \eta(\mathbf{k};\beta).$$
Since $0 < c_k(\beta) \le 1$, $x\log(1/x) \le (2/e) x^{1/2}$ on $(0,1]$, and $c_k(\beta)$ decays super-exponentially in $k$ (so $\sum_{\mathbf{k}} c_{\mathbf{k}}(\beta)^{1/2} < \infty$), the sum $\sum_{\mathbf{k}} w_\psi(\mathbf{k}) \eta(\mathbf{k};\beta) \le \lambda^{-1}\sum_{\mathbf{k}} c_{\mathbf{k}}(\beta)(-\log c_{\mathbf{k}}(\beta)) < \infty$ converges absolutely, whence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(w_\psi \| \hat{q}_\psi) < \infty$ and
$$-\log\lambda = -\log\langle e^{-\beta V}\rangle_\psi + \mathbb{E}_{w_\psi}[\eta(\mathbf{k};\beta)] + D_{\mathrm{KL}}(w_\psi \| \hat{q}_\psi).$$
Substituting $-\log\langle e^{-\beta V}\rangle_\psi = \beta\langle V \rangle_\psi - \Lambda_\psi(\beta)$ (where $\Lambda_\psi(\beta) \in [0, 2\beta|\mathcal{P}|]$ by Jensen) and subtracting the same identity at $\psi = \Omega$ (noting $\beta(\langle V \rangle_\psi - \langle V \rangle_\Omega) = -\beta B_M(\psi)$):
$$m = -\beta B_M(\psi) - \Delta_\psi\Lambda(\beta) + \Delta_\psi\mathbb{E}_w[\eta(\mathbf{k};\beta)] + \Delta_\psi D_{\mathrm{KL}}(w\|\hat{q}).$$
By definition of $\delta_\beta(\mathbf{k})$, $\beta B_E(\psi) = \Delta_\psi\mathbb{E}_w[\eta(\mathbf{k};\beta)] + \Delta_\psi\mathbb{E}_w[\delta_\beta(\mathbf{k})]$. Substituting into $a_\psi^{\mathrm{aniso}} = \beta(B_E(\psi) - B_M(\psi))$ gives (1.6), and plugging (1.6) into (1.2) gives (1.7).

For (1.8), fix any integer $\nu \ge 0$ and $\beta > 0$. Recall the standard integral representation (row 1618 Lemma 1; valid at $\nu = 0$ via $\Gamma(1/2) = \sqrt{\pi}$):
$$I_\nu(\beta) = \frac{(\beta/2)^\nu}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\nu + 1/2)} \int_{-1}^1 e^{\beta x} (1 - x^2)^{\nu - 1/2}\,dx.$$
Let $d\mu_\nu(x) \propto e^{\beta x}(1 - x^2)^{\nu - 1/2}\,dx$ be the normalized probability measure on $(-1, 1)$. Differentiating $\log I_\nu(\beta)$ using the integral gives $I_\nu'(\beta)/I_\nu(\beta) = \nu/\beta + \mathbb{E}_{\mu_\nu}[x]$, while term-by-term differentiation of the power series $I_\nu(\beta) = \sum_{m=0}^\infty \frac{(\beta/2)^{\nu+2m}}{m!(\nu+m)!}$ gives $I_\nu'(\beta) = \frac{\nu}{\beta} I_\nu(\beta) + I_{\nu+1}(\beta)$. Hence
$$r_\nu(\beta) := \frac{I_{\nu+1}(\beta)}{I_\nu(\beta)} = \mathbb{E}_{\mu_\nu}[x].$$
Because $\mu_\nu$ is a non-degenerate exponential family in $\beta$ with sufficient statistic $x$, differentiating again gives $r_\nu'(\beta) = \operatorname{Var}_{\mu_\nu}(x) > 0$, whence $\beta \frac{d}{d\beta}\log r_\nu(\beta) = \beta \operatorname{Var}_{\mu_\nu}(x)/r_\nu(\beta) > 0$. On the other hand, conditioning on $y = |x| \in (0, 1)$ gives $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_\nu}[x \mid |x| = y] = y\tanh(\beta y) =: f(y)$ with marginal law $d\nu_\nu(y) \propto \cosh(\beta y)(1 - y^2)^{\nu - 1/2}\,dy$. Then $d\nu_{\nu+1}(y) = \frac{g(y)}{\mathbb{E}_{\nu_\nu}[g]}\,d\nu_\nu(y)$ with $g(y) := 1 - y^2$. On $(0, 1)$, $f$ is strictly increasing and $g$ is strictly decreasing, so Chebyshev's covariance identity gives
$$r_{\nu+1}(\beta) - r_\nu(\beta) = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}_{\nu_\nu}(f, g)}{\mathbb{E}_{\nu_\nu}[g]} = \frac{1}{2\mathbb{E}_{\nu_\nu}[g]}\iint_{(0,1)^2} (f(y) - f(z))(g(y) - g(z))\,d\nu_\nu(y)\,d\nu_\nu(z) < 0.$$
Finally, applying $I'_\nu = I_{\nu+1} + (\nu/\beta) I_\nu$ at $\nu$ and $\nu + 1$ gives
$$\beta\frac{d}{d\beta}\log\frac{I_{\nu+1}(\beta)}{I_\nu(\beta)} = \beta\left(\frac{I_{\nu+2}}{I_{\nu+1}} + \frac{\nu+1}{\beta} - \frac{I_{\nu+1}}{I_\nu} - \frac{\nu}{\beta}\right) = 1 + \beta\bigl(r_{\nu+1}(\beta) - r_\nu(\beta)\bigr) < 1,$$
proving (1.8). For $SU(2)$, $g_\beta(j) - g_\beta(j-1) = \frac{d}{d\beta}\log r_j(\beta) \in (0, 1/\beta)$ ($j \ge 1$), so summing $j = 1, \dots, k$ gives $0 < g_\beta(k) - g_\beta(0) < k/\beta$ and $\delta_\beta(k) < k - (\eta_k(\beta) - \eta_0(\beta)) < k$ (for $U(1)$, sum $j = 0, \dots, |k|-1$). $\square$

#### Proof of Part 3
By (1.3), if $N(t,\beta) \ge (\kappa_0 + \varepsilon) W_t$ and $\beta \ge C/\varepsilon$, then $A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}(t,\beta) \le C W_t$ implies $\frac{1}{\beta} A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}(t,\beta) \le \varepsilon W_t$, hence $2 N_M(t,\beta) = N(t,\beta) - \frac{1}{\beta} A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}(t,\beta) \ge \kappa_0 W_t$. By row 1631 (4), at every cold point $|B_M(\psi) - B_M^{[q]}(\psi)| \le \frac{8(6+2\delta)}{q} m$ uniformly on every unit eigenvector $\psi \in \operatorname{ran}\widetilde{F}_r$; summing over orthonormal bases of $J_r$ gives $|N_M - N_M^{[q]}| \le \frac{8(6+2\delta)}{q} W_{\mathrm{obs}} \le \eta W_t$ for $K \ge K_\eta$ (since $q \ge 6e^2\beta \to \infty$ on the window). If in addition $A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}(t,\beta) \ge -C W_t$, then $2 N_E(t,\beta) = N(t,\beta) + \frac{1}{\beta} A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}(t,\beta) \ge (\kappa_0 + \varepsilon - C/\beta) W_t \ge \kappa_0 W_t$. $\square$

#### Proof of Part 4
*(i) Free transverse photon and compact Gaussian $U(1)$ with $P_0$.*
In transverse Fourier modes $(k, \alpha)$ ($k \in \Lambda_n^*$, $\alpha \in \{1, 2\}$), each mode is an independent real harmonic oscillator with transfer kernel $\exp\bigl(-\frac{\beta_s}{4}\omega_0(k)^2 (q^2 + q'^2) - \frac{\beta_t}{2}(q - q')^2\bigr)$, where $\omega_0(k) := 2\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^3 \sin^2(k_j/2)} = 2\sinh(\omega(k)/2)$. At $\beta_t = \beta_s = \beta$, completing the square in the Gaussian eigen-equation gives vacuum $\Omega_{k,\alpha}(q) \propto \exp(-\frac{1}{2}\beta\sinh\omega(k)\,q^2)$ and ladder representation $q_{k,\alpha} = (2\beta\sinh\omega(k))^{-1/2}(a_{k,\alpha} + a_{k,\alpha}^\dagger)$, so
$$V_{k,\alpha} = \frac{1}{2}\omega_0(k)^2 q_{k,\alpha}^2 = \frac{\tanh(\omega(k)/2)}{2\beta}\Bigl(2 N_{k,\alpha} + 1 + a_{k,\alpha}^2 + (a_{k,\alpha}^\dagger)^2\Bigr),$$
using $\omega_0(k)^2 / (4\sinh\omega(k)) = \sinh^2(\omega(k)/2) / (2\sinh(\omega(k)/2)\cosh(\omega(k)/2)) = \frac{1}{2}\tanh(\omega(k)/2)$.
In compact Gaussian $U(1)$ with $P_0$ (row 1618 §2.1), the three flat holonomy modes $\Phi_1, \Phi_2, \Phi_3$ have zero spatial curvature ($V_{\mathrm{flat}} \equiv 0$), so $V_{\mathrm{cG}} = \sum_{k,\alpha} V_{k,\alpha} \otimes I_{\mathrm{flux}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{cG}}(\beta_t, \beta_s) = T_{\mathrm{phot}}(\beta_t, \beta_s) \otimes R_{\beta_t}^{\mathrm{flux}}$. On any energy eigenspace $F_r$ of $R_\beta^{\mathrm{cG}}$ (even at accidental photon–photon or photon–rotor degeneracies), the operators $a_{k,\alpha}^2$ and $(a_{k,\alpha}^\dagger)^2$ preserve $Q$ and shift total energy by $\mp 2\omega(k) \ne 0$, so their compression to $F_r$ vanishes identically. Hence $\widetilde{b}_r^{(M)} = \widetilde{F}_r(\langle V_{\mathrm{cG}}\rangle_\Omega - V_{\mathrm{cG}})\widetilde{F}_r$ is **exactly diagonal** in the $|\mathbf{n}, Q\rangle$ basis with eigenvalues
$$b^{(M)}(|\mathbf{n}, Q\rangle) = -\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{k,\alpha} n_{k,\alpha}\tanh\frac{\omega(k)}{2} \le 0.$$
Since $b(|\mathbf{n}, Q\rangle) = e_Q/\beta$ (row 1618 Theorem 2, row 1628 (22)), (1.2) yields
$$a^{\mathrm{aniso}}(|\mathbf{n}, Q\rangle) = \beta\bigl(b - 2 b^{(M)}\bigr) = e_Q + 2\sum_{k,\alpha} n_{k,\alpha}\tanh\frac{\omega(k)}{2}.$$
Because $0 < 2\tanh(x/2) < x$ for all $x > 0$ and $m = e_Q + \sum_{k,\alpha} n_{k,\alpha}\omega(k)$, we have $0 < a^{\mathrm{aniso}} \le m$ on every excited state, with strict inequalities $b^{(M)} < 0$ and $a^{\mathrm{aniso}} < m$ whenever $\mathbf{n} \ne 0$. In the pure photon ($Q$ absent), every excited state has $\mathbf{n} \ne 0$. In compact Gaussian $U(1)$ with contractible flowed loops $E$, flat-shift invariance gives $J_r \le F_r \Pi_{Q=0}$ (row 1626 (12)–(13), check 1627 §1.8), so every visible excited state has $Q = 0$ and $\mathbf{n} \ne 0$, giving $N_M^{\mathrm{cG}} < 0$ and $0 < A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso,cG}} < W_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{cG}} \le W_{t, Q=0}^{\mathrm{cG}}$. At $\beta \ge 2$ and $G^{\mathrm{cG}}(t;\beta) \le 4/21$, row 1618 Theorem 2(2) proves $E_{\mathrm{phot}} / E_{\mathrm{flux}} \le 280\beta e^{-2.5\beta}$; since $W_{t, Q=0}^{\mathrm{cG}} = (1 + G_{\mathrm{phot}}) E_{\mathrm{phot}}/t$, $W_t^{\mathrm{cG}} = (1 + G^{\mathrm{cG}})(E_{\mathrm{flux}} + E_{\mathrm{phot}})/t$, and $1 + G_{\mathrm{phot}} \le 1 + G^{\mathrm{cG}}$, we obtain
$$\frac{W_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{cG}}}{W_t^{\mathrm{cG}}} \le \frac{W_{t, Q=0}^{\mathrm{cG}}}{W_t^{\mathrm{cG}}} \le \frac{E_{\mathrm{phot}}}{E_{\mathrm{flux}} + E_{\mathrm{phot}}} < \frac{E_{\mathrm{phot}}}{E_{\mathrm{flux}}} \le 280\beta e^{-2.5\beta},$$
proving (1.9).

*(ii) Strong-coupling 4D $SU(2)$, 5D $SU(2)$, and 4D $U(1)$ (fixed $n \ge 4$, $\beta \downarrow 0$).*
Write $d_{\mathrm{sp}} := |\mathcal{P}|$, $d_{\mathrm{lk}} := |E(B)|$, and $V = d_{\mathrm{sp}} - S$ with $S := \sum_{p \in \mathcal{P}} W_p$. Rescale $T_\beta$ by the positive scalar $e^{\beta d_{\mathrm{sp}}} c_0(\beta)^{-d_{\mathrm{lk}}}$ (which leaves all eigenvectors, eigenspaces, and $B_M(\psi) = \langle S \rangle_\psi - \langle S \rangle_\Omega$ unchanged):
$$\widetilde{T}_\beta := e^{\frac{\beta}{2} S} \widehat{C}_\beta e^{\frac{\beta}{2} S}, \qquad \widehat{C}_\beta := c_0(\beta)^{-d_{\mathrm{lk}}} C_\beta.$$
On $H_0$, every non-constant spin configuration $\mathbf{k}$ has $\sum_e |k_e| \ge 4$:
- In both $SU(2)$ (Clebsch–Gordan triangle inequalities at each vertex) and $U(1)$ ($\operatorname{div}(k) = 0$), no vertex can have degree $1$ in $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{k})$, and if some edge $e = (x,y)$ has $|k_e| \ge 2$, then the other edges incident to $x$ and to $y$ are disjoint (no parallel edges since $n \ge 4$) and each set contributes $\ge |k_e|$, forcing $\sum_{e'} |k_{e'}| \ge 3|k_e| \ge 6$.
- If all nonzero edges have $|k_e| = 1$, then $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{k})$ is a nonempty Eulerian simple subgraph of $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^d$ ($n \ge 4$), so it has at least $4$ edges.
- If $\sum_e |k_e| = 4$, $\operatorname{supp}(\mathbf{k})$ is a simple $4$-cycle; for $n \ge 5$ the only $4$-cycles are elementary plaquettes, and at $n = 4$ any straight non-contractible $4$-cycle has odd winding $1$ in its direction and is annihilated by the centre flip $C_{e_i}$ in $P_0$.
- If $\sum_e |k_e| = 5$, then $|k_e| \le 1$ everywhere, forcing a simple $5$-cycle; every $5$-cycle on $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^d$ ($n \ge 4$) has odd coordinate-sum change $5 \equiv \sum_i m_i n \pmod 2$, hence at least one odd winding $m_i$ (impossible for even $n$, and killed by $C_{e_i}$ in $P_0$ for odd $n$).

Therefore, on $H_0$ we have the orthogonal decomposition $H_0 = \mathbb{C}1 \oplus F_4 \oplus H_{\ge 6}$ (projections $\Pi_0$, $F_4$, $Q_{\ge 6}$), where:
- For $SU(2)$, $F_4 = \operatorname{span}\{\chi_p : p \in \mathcal{P}\}$ ($\chi_p := \operatorname{tr} U_p = 2 W_p$ orthonormal, $\dim F_4 = d_{\mathrm{sp}}$), $S = \frac{1}{2}\sum_p \chi_p \in \operatorname{ran} F_4$, $\widehat{C}_\beta|_{F_4} = a(\beta) F_4$ with $a(\beta) := (I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta))^4 = (\beta/4)^4(1 + O(\beta^2))$, and on $H_{\ge 6}$ the series bound $I_{j+1}/I_j \le \beta/(2(j+1))$ gives $\widehat{c}_k(\beta) \le (\beta/2)^k / (k+1)! \le (\beta/4)^k$, whence $0 \le R_\beta := \widehat{C}_\beta Q_{\ge 6} \le (\beta/4)^6 Q_{\ge 6}$.
- For $U(1)$, $F_4 = \operatorname{span}\{c_p, s_p : p \in \mathcal{P}\}$ ($c_p := \sqrt{2}\cos\theta_p$, $s_p := \sqrt{2}\sin\theta_p$ orthonormal, $\dim F_4 = 2 d_{\mathrm{sp}}$), $S = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_p c_p \in \operatorname{ran} F_4$, $\widehat{C}_\beta|_{F_4} = a(\beta) F_4$ with $a(\beta) := (I_1(\beta)/I_0(\beta))^4 = (\beta/2)^4(1 + O(\beta^2))$, and $0 \le R_\beta := \widehat{C}_\beta Q_{\ge 6} \le (\beta/2)^6 Q_{\ge 6}$.

Two exact plaquette-combinatorics identities hold on $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^d$ ($n \ge 4$) for both groups:
- **(I1) $F_4 S F_4 = 0$:** For any three elementary plaquettes $p, q, r$, sending $U_e \mapsto -U_e$ (for $SU(2)$) or $\theta_e \mapsto \theta_e + \pi$ (for $U(1)$) on a single edge $e$ flips the sign of every plaquette containing $e$; thus the triple integral vanishes unless every edge appears $0$ or $2$ times in $(p, q, r)$, i.e. $\partial p \triangle \partial q = \partial r$. Since $n \ge 4$, any two distinct elementary plaquettes share at most $1$ edge, so $|\partial p \triangle \partial q| \in \{0, 6, 8\} \not\ni 4$. Hence $F_4 S F_4 = 0$.
- **(I2) Pairings only for four-plaquette integrals, and pairwise Haar independence:** For any four elementary plaquettes $p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4$, if some $p_i$ appears with odd multiplicity ($1$ or $3$), the remaining distinct plaquettes (at most $3$ of them, or $1$ if one appears thrice) share at most $1$ edge each with $p_i$, so at least one edge of $p_i$ has odd multiplicity $1$, killing the Haar integral by $U_e \mapsto -U_e$. Thus only pairings survive. Moreover, for any two distinct plaquettes $p \ne q$, since $|\partial p \cap \partial q| \le 1$, each possesses an edge not in the other; integrating those two edges first makes $(U_p, U_q)$ independent Haar random variables.

Now write $\widetilde{T}_\beta = |u_\beta\rangle\langle u_\beta| + W_\beta$, where $u_\beta := e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S} 1$ and $W_\beta := a\, e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S} F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S} + e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S} R_\beta e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}$. Since $\langle 1, S \rangle = 0$, Jensen gives $\|u_\beta\|^2 \ge 1$ and $u_\beta = 1 + \frac{\beta}{2}S + O_n(\beta^2)$ with $\|u_\beta\| = 1 + O_n(\beta^2)$, while $\|W_\beta\| = O_n(\beta^4)$. By Weyl's inequality $\tilde{\lambda}_0 = \|u_\beta\|^2 + O_n(\beta^4) \ge 1 - O_n(\beta^4)$; projecting $\tilde{\lambda}_0 \Omega_\beta = \langle u_\beta, \Omega_\beta\rangle u_\beta + W_\beta \Omega_\beta$ orthogonal to $\hat{u}_\beta := u_\beta / \|u_\beta\|$ gives $\|(I - \Pi_{\hat{u}_\beta})\Omega_\beta\| \le \tilde{\lambda}_0^{-1}\|W_\beta\| = O_n(\beta^4)$, hence (as $\Omega_\beta, \hat{u}_\beta > 0$) $\|\Omega_\beta - \hat{u}_\beta\| = O_n(\beta^4)$ and
$$\Omega_\beta = 1 + \frac{\beta}{2}S + O_n(\beta^2). \tag{1.12}$$
Consequently $e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Omega_\beta = 1 + \beta S + O_n(\beta^2)$. Because $F_4 1 = 0$, $F_4 S = S$, and $Q_{\ge 6} 1 = Q_{\ge 6} S = 0$, we have $\|F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Omega_\beta\| = O_n(\beta)$ and $\|Q_{\ge 6} e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Omega_\beta\| = O_n(\beta^2)$. Pairing $\tilde{\lambda}_0\Omega_\beta = \langle u_\beta, \Omega_\beta\rangle u_\beta + W_\beta \Omega_\beta$ with any unit vector $\phi \in \Omega_\beta^\perp$ yields
$$|\langle u_\beta, \phi\rangle| \le \frac{a \|e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\|\,\|F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Omega_\beta\| + \|e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\|\,\|R_\beta\|\,\|Q_{\ge 6} e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Omega_\beta\|}{\langle u_\beta, \Omega_\beta\rangle} = O_n(\beta^5) + O_n(\beta^8) = O_n(\beta^5).$$
Thus $v_\beta := (I - \Pi_{\Omega_\beta}) u_\beta$ satisfies $\|v_\beta\| = O_n(\beta^5)$, and the restriction $\widetilde{T}_\beta^\perp$ of $\widetilde{T}_\beta$ to $\Omega_\beta^\perp$ takes the form
$$\widetilde{T}_\beta^\perp = a\, K_\beta^* K_\beta + E_\beta, \qquad K_\beta := F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}(I - \Pi_{\Omega_\beta}) : \Omega_\beta^\perp \to \operatorname{ran} F_4,$$
with $\|E_\beta\| = \| |v_\beta\rangle\langle v_\beta| + (I - \Pi_{\Omega_\beta}) e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S} R_\beta e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}(I - \Pi_{\Omega_\beta}) \| = O_n(\beta^6) = a \cdot O_n(\beta^2)$. By (I1) and $\|F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Omega_\beta\| = O_n(\beta)$,
$$K_\beta K_\beta^* = F_4 e^{\beta S} F_4 - F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\Pi_{\Omega_\beta} e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S} F_4 = F_4 + O_n(\beta^2).$$
Hence $K_\beta^* K_\beta$ has $\dim F_4$ eigenvalues in $1 + O_n(\beta^2)$ and all remaining eigenvalues $0$. By Weyl's inequality, for small $\beta > 0$, $\widetilde{T}_\beta^\perp$ has an isolated cluster $P_4(\beta)$ of $\dim F_4$ eigenvalues (with multiplicity) in $a(1 + O_n(\beta^2))$, while every remaining eigenvalue is $O_n(\beta^6)$.

Now let $\psi \in \operatorname{ran} P_4(\beta)$ be **any** unit eigenvector of $\widetilde{T}_\beta$, with eigenvalue $\tilde{\lambda} = a(1 + O_n(\beta^2))$. Dividing $\tilde{\lambda}\psi = a K_\beta^* K_\beta \psi + E_\beta \psi$ by $a$ gives
$$(1 + O_n(\beta^2))\psi = (I - \Pi_{\Omega_\beta}) e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\bigl(F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\psi\bigr) + O_n(\beta^2). \tag{1.13}$$
Applying $(I - F_4)$ gives $\|(I - F_4)\psi\| = O_n(\beta)$. Putting $\psi_0 := F_4\psi$, we have $\|\psi_0\| = 1 + O_n(\beta^2)$, and by (I1):
$$F_4 e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\psi = \psi_0 + \frac{\beta}{2} F_4 S\bigl(\psi_0 + (I - F_4)\psi\bigr) + O_n(\beta^2) = \psi_0 + O_n(\beta^2).$$
Using (1.12) and $\langle 1, \psi_0 \rangle = 0$, $\Pi_{\Omega_\beta} e^{\frac{\beta}{2}S}\psi_0 = \langle 1 + \frac{\beta}{2}S, (I + \frac{\beta}{2}S)\psi_0 \rangle \cdot 1 + O_n(\beta^2) = \beta\langle S, \psi_0\rangle\cdot 1 + O_n(\beta^2)$. Substituting back into (1.13) gives, uniformly over all unit eigenvectors $\psi \in \operatorname{ran} P_4(\beta)$:
$$\psi = \psi_0 + \frac{\beta}{2} S\psi_0 - \beta\langle S, \psi_0\rangle\cdot 1 + O_n(\beta^2). \tag{1.14}$$
From (1.12), $\langle \Omega_\beta, S\Omega_\beta\rangle = \beta\|S\|^2 + O_n(\beta^2)$; from (1.14) and $\langle \psi_0, S\psi_0\rangle = 0$ (by (I1)),
$$B_M(\psi) = \langle \psi, S\psi\rangle - \langle \Omega_\beta, S\Omega_\beta\rangle = \beta\Bigl(\|S\psi_0\|^2 - 2|\langle S, \psi_0\rangle|^2 - \|S\|^2\Bigr) + O_n(\beta^2). \tag{1.15}$$

Evaluate (1.15) using (I2) (putting $\hat{\psi}_0 := \psi_0/\|\psi_0\| = \psi_0 + O_n(\beta^2)$):
- **$SU(2)$ (4D and 5D):** Write $\hat{\psi}_0 = \sum_p \alpha_p \chi_p$ ($\sum_p |\alpha_p|^2 = 1$). Then $\|S\|^2 = d_{\mathrm{sp}}/4$, $2|\langle S, \hat{\psi}_0\rangle|^2 = \frac{1}{2}|\sum_p \alpha_p|^2$, and $\int \chi_p^4 = \int(1 + \chi_1)^2 = 2$. By (I2):
  $$4\|S\hat{\psi}_0\|^2 = \sum_{p} |\alpha_p|^2 \bigl(2 + (d_{\mathrm{sp}} - 1)\cdot 1\bigr) + 2\sum_{p \ne p'} \bar{\alpha}_{p'} \alpha_p = (d_{\mathrm{sp}} + 1) + 2\Bigl|\sum_p \alpha_p\Bigr|^2 - 2 = d_{\mathrm{sp}} - 1 + 2\Bigl|\sum_p \alpha_p\Bigr|^2.$$
  Dividing by $4$ gives $\|S\hat{\psi}_0\|^2 = \|S\|^2 + 2|\langle S, \hat{\psi}_0\rangle|^2 - \frac{1}{4}$. Both extensive terms cancel identically in (1.15), proving (1.10): $B_M^{SU(2)}(\psi) = -\frac{\beta}{4} + O_n(\beta^2)$.
- **$U(1)$ (4D):** Write $\hat{\psi}_0 = \sum_p (\alpha_p c_p + \gamma_p s_p)$ ($\sum_p (|\alpha_p|^2 + |\gamma_p|^2) = 1$). Here $\|S\|^2 = d_{\mathrm{sp}}/2$, $2|\langle S, \hat{\psi}_0\rangle|^2 = |\sum_p \alpha_p|^2$, $\int c_p^4 = 3/2$, $\int c_p^2 s_p^2 = 1/2$, $\int c_p^3 s_p = 0$. By (I2):
  $$2\|S\hat{\psi}_0\|^2 = \sum_p \Bigl[\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2} + d_{\mathrm{sp}} - 1\bigr)|\alpha_p|^2 + \bigl(\tfrac{1}{2} + d_{\mathrm{sp}} - 1\bigr)|\gamma_p|^2\Bigr] + 2\sum_{p \ne p'} \bar{\alpha}_{p'} \alpha_p = d_{\mathrm{sp}} - \frac{3}{2}\sum_p |\alpha_p|^2 - \frac{1}{2}\sum_p |\gamma_p|^2 + 2\Bigl|\sum_p \alpha_p\Bigr|^2.$$
  Dividing by $2$ and substituting into (1.15) cancels $\|S\|^2$ and $2|\langle S, \hat{\psi}_0\rangle|^2$ identically, giving
  $$B_M^{U(1)}(\psi) = -\beta\left(\frac{3}{4}\sum_p |\alpha_p|^2 + \frac{1}{4}\sum_p |\gamma_p|^2\right) + O_n(\beta^2),$$

## 2. Theorem 2: All-Delay Loop-Algebra No-Go and Exact Non-Equivalence of `(E1)`

Before attempting to prove `(E1)` from flowed-loop correlation inequalities, one must ask what data `(E1)` actually requires. ### 2.2 Proof of Theorem 2

#### Proof of Part 1
Since $0 < r_M = (\delta/M)^{1/t} < 1$, every off-diagonal entry of $P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}$ is $(1 - r_M)/(M+1) > 0$ and every diagonal entry is $r_M + (1 - r_M)/(M+1) > 0$, so $P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}$ is a strictly positive self-adjoint Markov operator on $L^2(\pi_{\mathrm{hid}})$ with eigenvalues $1$ (simple, constant vector $1_{\mathrm{hid}}$) and $r_M$ (multiplicity $M$, orthogonal complement $1_{\mathrm{hid}}^\perp$). Hence $G_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}(t) = M r_M^t = \delta$ and $W_{t,\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)} = M m_M r_M^t = (\delta/t)\log(M/\delta)$.

*(a)* Because $P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}$ is coupling-independent, $(P^{\mathrm{prod}})' = P_1' \otimes P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}$ (in frozen Doob coordinates, since $\pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$ is fixed), and $\Pi_{\mathrm{hid}} P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)} = P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)} \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$. Hence for every $f_0, \dots, f_k \in \operatorname{ran} E_1$ and any combination of powers $(P^{\mathrm{prod}})^{a_j}$ and derivatives $(P^{\mathrm{prod}})'$ inserted between $E(f_j \otimes 1_{\mathrm{hid}})$, every factor acting on the second tensor slot collapses to $\langle 1_{\mathrm{hid}}, 1_{\mathrm{hid}}\rangle = 1$, leaving the exact $(P_1, E_1)$ expectation. For each $r \in (0, 1)$, the spectral projection of $P^{\mathrm{prod}}$ at $r$ is the orthogonal sum
$$F_r^{\mathrm{prod}} = \bigl(F_r^{(1)} \otimes \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}\bigr) \;\oplus\; \bigl(F_{r/r_M}^{(1)} \otimes (I - \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}})\bigr), \tag{2.6}$$
with the convention $F_s^{(1)} = 0$ if $s \notin \operatorname{spec}(P_1)$. Since $\operatorname{ran} E = \operatorname{ran} E_1 \otimes \mathbb{C}1_{\mathrm{hid}}$ is annihilated by $I - \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$, we have $F_r^{\mathrm{prod}}\operatorname{ran} E = (F_r^{(1)}\operatorname{ran} E_1) \otimes \mathbb{C}1_{\mathrm{hid}}$, whence $J_r^{\mathrm{prod}} = J_r^{(1)} \otimes \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$ and
$$D_r^{\mathrm{prod}} = \bigl(D_r^{(1)} \otimes \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}\bigr) \;\oplus\; \bigl(F_{r/r_M}^{(1)} \otimes (I - \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}})\bigr). \tag{2.7}$$
Because $(P^{\mathrm{prod}})' = P_1' \otimes P_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}$ commutes with $\Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$, the slope operator $b_r^{\mathrm{prod}}$ is block-diagonal with respect to (2.6): on $F_r^{(1)} \otimes \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$ it equals $b_r^{(1)} \otimes \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}}$, and on $F_s^{(1)} \otimes (I - \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}})$ ($s = r/r_M$) dividing $P_1' \otimes r_M(I - \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}})$ by $r = s r_M$ gives $b_s^{(1)} \otimes (I - \Pi_{\mathrm{hid}})$ (with $b_1^{(1)} = 0$ since $\langle 1, P_1' 1 \rangle = 0$). Taking traces against $J_r^{\mathrm{prod}}$ gives (2.1).

*(b)* Multiplicativity of partition functions gives $1 + G^{\mathrm{prod}}(t) = (1 + G_1(t))(1 + \delta) = 1 + c_{\mathrm{col}}$, so $G^{\mathrm{prod}}(t) = c_{\mathrm{col}}$. By the block-diagonality of $b_r^{\mathrm{prod}}$ across (2.7):
$$\operatorname{Tr}_{D_r^{\mathrm{prod}}}\bigl((-b_r^{h,\mathrm{prod}})_+\bigr) = \operatorname{Tr}_{D_r^{(1)}}\bigl((-b_r^{h,(1)})_+\bigr) + M \operatorname{Tr}_{F_{r/r_M}^{(1)}}\bigl((-b_{r/r_M}^{(1)})_+\bigr).$$
Multiplying by $r^t$ and summing over $0 < r < 1$ (substituting $s = r/r_M$ in the second sum and using $r^t M = s^t (M r_M^t) = \delta s^t$, with $s = 1$ contributing $0$) yields (2.2) identically.

*(c)* Differentiating $1 + G^{\mathrm{prod}}(t) = (1 + G_1(t))(1 + G_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}(t))$ with respect to $-t$ gives
$$W_t^{\mathrm{prod}} = (1 + G_{\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)}(t)) W_t^{(1)} + (1 + G_1(t)) W_{t,\mathrm{hid}}^{(M)} = (1 + \delta) W_t^{(1)} + (1 + G_1(t))\frac{\delta\log(M/\delta)}{t},$$
which proves (2.3) and $N^{\mathrm{prod}}/W_t^{\mathrm{prod}} \to 0$, $1 - h^{\mathrm{prod}} \to 0$, and $H_0^{\mathrm{prod}}/W_t^{\mathrm{prod}} \to 0$ as $M \to \infty$. $\square$

### 4.2 Five Band Attacks Tried and Where Each Stopped

1. **Anisotropic–magnetic split (`PROVED` in Theorem 1; band sign OPEN):** Eliminates temporal plaquettes, electric placements $w$, and Bessel functions, reducing `(E1)` (under bounded $A_{\mathrm{obs}}^{\mathrm{aniso}}/W_t$) to the single-slice magnetic Hankel trace $N_M(2v, \beta) \ge \frac{\kappa_0}{2} W_{2v}$. Stopped at proving $\langle V \rangle_\Omega - \langle V \rangle_\psi > 0$ on cold band states, which is the non-perturbative lattice trace-anomaly sign at order-one effective coupling (and flips sign from strong coupling).
2. **Gauge-invariant skew-adjoint commutator $[O_e, T]$ (`ARGUED` / abandoned):** Tested $O_e = \sum_a \bigl((X_e^a V) X_e^a + X_e^a(X_e^a V)\bigr)$ with left-invariant link derivatives ($[X_e^a, C_\beta] = 0$). Using $C_\beta M_\beta \psi = \lambda M_\beta^{-1}\psi$, the identity $\langle \psi, [O_e, T]\psi \rangle = 0$ collapses algebraically to $0 = 0$; moreover row 1618 Corollary 1B already rules out any all-state operator conjugation lower bound on $H_0^\perp$.
3. **Single-slice Schwinger–Dyson / integration-by-parts force identity (`ARGUED` / stopped):** Relates $\langle V \rangle_\psi$ to $\langle |\nabla V|^2 \rangle_\psi$ plus kinetic boundary insertions, but vacuum subtraction leaves an $O(1/n)$ difference inside $O(N/\beta)$ extensive fluctuations and holds identically in 4D $U(1)$ and 5D $SU(2)$.
4. **Pure flowed-loop correlation inequalities (`PROVED` obstructed by Theorem 2(1)):** Any argument reading only unmarked and action-marked flowed-loop correlations (at arbitrarily many delays), coldness $G(t) \le c_{\mathrm{col}}$, and `(E2)` ($H_0 / W_t \le \varepsilon$) cannot establish `(E1)` at any fixed $\kappa_0 > 0$: a global Wilson completeness bound on $W_D / W_t$ is strictly required.
5. **Strong-coupling continuation and fixed-box large-$\beta$ asymptotics (`PROVED` obstructed / stopped):** Strong-coupling positivity $b_r \succeq (2\beta)^{-1}$ (row 1632 (11)) operates on $\beta \le 1/(48 n^3) \to 0$ and has the *opposite* magnetic sign $b_r^{(M)} < 0$ (Theorem 1(4)); fixed-box $\beta \to \infty$ violates `(g5)` (Remark STAB) and faces the uncontrolled Weyl-chamber boundary of $\Omega_\beta$ on $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{flat}}$.
