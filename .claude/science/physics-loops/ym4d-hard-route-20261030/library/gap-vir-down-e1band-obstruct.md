$$

The same holds for the isotypic projector of **every** complex irreducible representation of the full periodic spatial group $\Gamma=(\mathbb Z/n)^3\rtimes O_h$, where $O_h$ consists of signed coordinate permutations. *Proof.* Put $L=n/32$. On the flowed configuration choose three $xy$-squares of side $L$. Their lower vertices, in units of $L$, are $(0,0,0),(2,3,5),(0,0,12)$. For each square take $h(w)={\bf1}_{\{w\ge0\}}-1/2$ of its normalized trace. Zero is a bin boundary. Let $f$ be the product of the three readings. Under product Haar measure on the *output* link variables, integration in any single link of a square centers its factor, by Haar invariance, the null Haar measure of the trace-zero set, and the symmetry $g\mapsto-g$. Also $h^2=1/4$.

The unordered triple has trivial stabilizer in $\Gamma$. A symmetry preserving it must preserve the normal axis up to sign. The layers $0,5,12$ on the circle of length 32 have distinct successive gaps $5,7,20$. No nonidentity rotation preserves those gaps, and reversal reverses their cyclic order. Thus the symmetry fixes each layer and its square. The difference of the first two square centres in the $xy$-plane is $(2,3)L$. Only the identity signed permutation in that plane fixes this vector modulo $n$. Fixing one centre then fixes the translation.

Consider two distinct images of the triple. Some square of the first is absent from the second by the trivial stabilizer. It can meet at most one square of the second because their layers perpendicular to their common normal have cyclic separation at least $5L$, while the chosen square has extent at most $L$ on that axis. If it meets one, their edge sets differ and have equal size. The chosen square therefore has a link in none of the other five squares. Integrating on that link kills the Haar inner product of the two products. The squared norm of each product is $4^{-3}>0$. Every product also has Haar mean zero. Thus the $|\Gamma|$ images are linearly independent, with no nonzero constant in their span.

Spatial Wilson flow at the specified finite time is an equivariant smooth diffeomorphism of the compact link manifold (row 1624). Its pullback preserves null sets in both directions. The strictly positive invariant $\Omega$ gives another equivalent measure $\Omega^2 dx$. Linear independence and the exclusion of constants therefore hold for the pulled-back readings in the vacuum space. Haar orthogonality was used only to establish these two facts. The map sending the group basis vector $\delta_g$ to the $g$-image of $f$ intertwines left translation and is injective. Work in the complexified Hilbert space. A momentum projector applied to $\delta_e$ has nonzero identity coefficient $1/n^3$. For a spatial irrep $\sigma$ the character projector has identity coefficient $(\dim\sigma)^2/|\Gamma|$. Both projected readings are consequently nonzero and nonconstant.

Each projected reading takes finitely many complex values since it is a finite linear combination of cell functions. A continuous representative on the connected link manifold would take its values in that same finite set everywhere by full support, and hence would be constant. The vacuum $\Omega$ is continuous and strictly positive, and every eigenvector of $A$ at $v>0$ has a continuous representative by the continuous kernel eigenvalue equation. Finitely many spectral components of the projected vector divided by $\Omega$ would give a continuous representative. Injectivity and compact self-adjointness of the neutral transfer give completeness of the positive eigenspaces (row 1628 (17)). The vacuum accounts for at most one component. Spatial projections commute with $A$ and with multiplication by $\Omega$, proving (1).

## How much hidden thermal weight would suffice?

**PROVED (the size of the observation space).** Write $M=\dim\operatorname{span}\{{\bf1}_C\}-1$ in the vacuum measure. The prescribed design has

$$
             M+1\ \ge\ 16^{32^3/2}.
$$

At each of the 32 grid heights choose the $xy$-faces whose two base coordinates in grid units have even sum. They have disjoint edge sets, also across the periodic seams. Their holonomies on the output reference field are independent Haar. Every one of the 16 bins has positive Haar probability. Each bin assignment to these $32^3/2$ squares therefore has positive probability under the equivalent flowed vacuum law and contains at least one positive-measure joint cell. Coldness gives

$$
       \sum_{0<t m_v\le B}\dim F_v\le c e^B
$$

because each state counted contributes at least $e^{-B}$ to $G$. In particular, for $B<\log(16^{32^3/2}/c)$ even the *entire* set of states in this range has dimension at most $M$. A dimension count from the total number of readings cannot force one of those states into the kernel. Actual linear dependences can. Across different eigenvalues row 1628 (17) separates the readings by time delays. The use of a cold trace to limit symmetry multiplicity is already in row 771, §5.

**PROVED (hidden thermal energy on a cold interval).** Keep $n,\kappa$ fixed. Let $[x,y]$, $x<y$, be wholly in the cold calibrated band, with $t'= \kappa t$ and $\min t>3$. Use a finite cell partition fixed during this interval, as in row 1624. Set $j_v=\operatorname{rank}J_v,\ W_o=\sum m_vv^t j_v,\ W_D=W-W_o$. There is a continuous function $\widehat G_o$ equal to $\sum j_v v^t$ outside a countable set, with $0\le\widehat G_o\le G$, for which

$$
  \widehat G_o(y)-\widehat G_o(x)
     =\int_x^y t\, [N-\kappa W_o]\,d\beta .                              \tag{2}
$$

Thus a positive surplus in E1 requires the endpoint difference to pay at least
$\kappa\int_x^y t W_D\,d\beta+\epsilon\int_x^y t W\,d\beta$.
In particular, if

$$
                   \kappa\int_x^y t W_D\,d\beta\ \ge\ G(y),              \tag{3}
$$

then $N\le\kappa W$ at some interior cold point. This implication also holds if a known lower bound replaces $W_D$ in (3). It compares hidden occupation on a cold interval with the unmarked endpoint heat trace. The integral can miss isolated rank losses. E1 also tests those points.

*Proof of (2).* At fixed box the Wilson kernel is real analytic in $\beta>0$ in Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Indeed its unprojected kernel is the exponential of $-\beta$ times a bounded slab action, and the gauge and centre averages preserve the locally converging power series. The vacuum is simple. Its spectral projection, obtained by a resolvent contour and the Neumann series near any positive $\beta$, is analytic. Applying it to the positive vacuum at that point and normalizing gives the local real analytic $\Omega_\beta$. The same argument applies to $A=T/\lambda_0$ and every isolated finite cluster at positive multiplier. Thus $\Omega_\beta{\bf1}_C$ is analytic as a Hilbert-space vector for fixed $C$. [Fixed-box analytic input in rows 710 and 1613](gap-direction7-fable-check-astra62.md)

An isolated cluster has an analytic orthonormal frame, by projecting a basis at the centre and using Gram–Schmidt nearby. In that frame the restricted matrix is analytic Hermitian for real $\beta$. Near a collision, first split unequal eigenvalues by contours. On a block scalar at the collision subtract its mean eigenvalue. If the resulting analytic matrix is identically zero, the block is a single persistent eigenvalue. Otherwise divide by the first nonzero Taylor power. The leading matrix is nonzero traceless Hermitian. Its distinct eigenvalues split the block into smaller analytic blocks by contours. Induction on the dimension gives local analytic eigenvalues $v_i$ and projections $P_i$, grouping locally identical branches. Distinct branches meet only at isolated points in this chart.

The rank of the columns $P_i(\Omega_\beta{\bf1}_C)$ is a fixed integer $j_i$ off isolated points, by the analytic minors of their Gram matrix. Include these generic ranks even at a local crossing or a rank loss when defining the continuation. Differentiating $A P_i=v_iP_i$ and compressing gives $P_i A'P_i=v_i'P_i$. Away from the exceptional points, $P_i=F_{v_i}$ and

$$
       (j_i v_i^t)'=t v_i^t j_i\, (v_i'/v_i-\kappa m_i).
$$

Different local continuations of a finite sum agree where the eigenvalues and ranks are regular, hence agree everywhere by continuity.

For the infinite sum, use a $C^1$ factor $\chi_\eta(v)$ equal to zero up to $\eta$, one above $2\eta$, with $|\chi_\eta'|\le 2/\eta$. One choice rescales $3u^2-2u^3$ on the transition interval. Sum the continued $j_i\chi_\eta(v_i)v_i^t$ locally over a finite cluster containing every excited $v_i\ge\eta$, choosing its lower contour boundary in a spectral gap below $\eta$ at the chart centre. This gives a global continuously differentiable sum on the interval. Along $\eta=2^{-j}\downarrow0$, the exceptional sets form a countable union. Write $t_0=\min t>3,\ H=\sup_\beta\sum_v v^2\dim F_v<\infty$ and $D=\sup_\beta\|A'\|<\infty$. The two bounds follow from fixed-box norm continuity on the compact interval. The omitted value is at most $H(2\eta)^{t_0-2}$ at regular points, hence the continued sums converge uniformly. Their limit is $\widehat G_o$, bounded by $G$ also at exceptional points by continuity of the full trace (the same tail bound with full multiplicities).

For derivatives at regular points use $|v_i'|\le D$. The omitted terms involving $v_i'$ are bounded by $(\max t+4)DH(2\eta)^{t_0-3}$, including the derivative of the cutoff since $v|\chi_\eta'|\le4$. The terms differentiating $t$ cost at most $(\max |t'|)H\sup_{0<v\le2\eta}v^{t_0-2}|\log v|$. Both tend to zero. This permits integration followed by the limit and proves (2) with the actual $N,W_o$ almost everywhere. If $N>\kappa W$ throughout the regular interior then (2) is strictly greater than $\kappa\int tW_D$. Its left side is at most $G(y)$, contradicting (3).
