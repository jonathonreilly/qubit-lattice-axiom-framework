### 1.1 Theorem 1.1 (Exact $\kappa_0 h W_t$ cancellation, reduction to $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}}$, and universal ceiling $\rho \le 0$) — **PROVED**

For every positive trace-class transfer operator $P$ with trace-class derivative $A'_\beta$ (in particular every finite Wilson box $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$, every $\beta > 0$, every real $t \ge 4$), every finite neutral observation $E$, and every $\kappa_0 \ge 0$:
1. **(Exact sandwich for $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adv}}(\kappa_0)$)**
   $$\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} \;\le\; \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adv}}(\kappa_0) \;\le\; \kappa_0 h W_t + \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}}. \tag{1.1}$$
2. **(Exact cancellation of $\kappa_0 h W_t$ in row 1628 (19))**
   $$\mathcal{B}(\kappa_0) = \bigl(\mathcal{O}_t - \kappa_0 W_t\bigr) + \bigl(\kappa_0 h W_t - \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adv}}(\kappa_0)\bigr) \;\ge\; \bigl(\mathcal{O}_t - \kappa_0 W_t\bigr) - \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}}. \tag{1.2}$$
   In particular, given `(E1)` in check 1630 §3.3's form $\mathcal{O}_t \ge (\kappa_0 + \varepsilon) W_t$, the single thermally weighted inequality
   $$\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} \;<\; \varepsilon W_t \tag{1.3}$$
   closes $\mathcal{B}(\kappa_0) > 0$ **without any upper bound on the invisible fraction $h \in [0, 1]$ and without pointwise $\rho \ge 0$**.
3. **(Observation-independent majorant)** For every $0 < r < 1$, $\operatorname{Tr}_{D_r}((b_r^h)_-) \le \operatorname{Tr}_{F_r}((b_r)_-)$, and hence
   $$\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} \;\le\; \mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{rise}}(t; \beta). \tag{1.4}$$
4. **(Universal ceiling $\rho \le 0$ on every finite Wilson box)** On every finite 4D Wilson $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ box $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ ($n \ge 3$) and every $\beta > 0$, if $D_r \ne 0$ for infinitely many $r \in \sigma(P) \cap (0, 1)$ (or for the full blocks $b_r$ on $F_r$), then
   $$\sup\bigl\{\rho \in \mathbb{R} : b_r^h \ge \rho m I \ \forall r \in (0, 1)\bigr\} \;\le\; 0. \tag{1.5}$$

*Proof.*
(1) For every real number $x$ and every $a \ge 0$, $(-x)_+ \le (a - x)_+ \le a + (-x)_+$ (check both cases: if $x \ge 0$, $0 \le (a-x)_+ \le a$; if $x < 0$, $-x \le a - x = a + (-x)$). Applying functional calculus to the self-adjoint matrix $b_r^h$ on the finite-dimensional space $\operatorname{ran} D_r$ with $a = \kappa_0 m \ge 0$ gives $(b_r^h)_- \le (\kappa_0 m I - b_r^h)_+ \le \kappa_0 m I_{D_r} + (b_r^h)_-$. Taking traces, multiplying by $r^t > 0$, and summing over $0 < r < 1$ yields (1.1), since $\sum_{0 < r < 1} m r^t \dim D_r = h W_t$. Absolute convergence of every sum for $t \ge 2$ follows from $\sum_{0 < r < 1} r^t \operatorname{Tr}_{F_r}(|b_r|) \le \sum_{0 < r < 1} r^{t-1}\|F_r U^{-1} A'_\beta U F_r\|_1 \le \|A'_\beta\|_1 < \infty$ (pinching inequality, row 1628 L220) and $m r^t \le e^{-1} r^{t-1}$ with $P$ trace-class.

(2) Since $\sum_{0<r<1} m r^t \dim J_r = (1 - h) W_t$, expanding (0.1) gives
$$\mathcal{B}(\kappa_0) = \mathcal{O}_t - \kappa_0 (1 - h) W_t - \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adv}}(\kappa_0) = (\mathcal{O}_t - \kappa_0 W_t) + (\kappa_0 h W_t - \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adv}}(\kappa_0)).$$
Inserting the upper bound of (1.1) gives $\mathcal{B}(\kappa_0) \ge (\mathcal{O}_t - \kappa_0 W_t) - \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}}$. If $\mathcal{O}_t \ge (\kappa_0 + \varepsilon) W_t$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} < \varepsilon W_t$, then $\mathcal{B}(\kappa_0) > 0$.

(3) On each finite-dimensional eigenspace $F_r$, the variational formula of row 1628 (7) gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{D_r}((b_r^h)_-) = \sup_{0 \le Y \le D_r} \operatorname{Tr}(-Y b_r) \le \sup_{0 \le Y \le F_r} \operatorname{Tr}(-Y b_r) = \operatorname{Tr}_{F_r}((b_r)_-)$ because $D_r \le F_r$. Weighting by $r^t$ and summing gives (1.4).

(4) For any unit vector $\psi \in \operatorname{ran} \widetilde{D}_r \subset \operatorname{ran} \widetilde{F}_r$, $\lambda_{\min}(b_r^h)/m \le b_\psi / m$. By row 1617 (2) (checked in check 1621 §1.3; also row 1618 Theorem 1(2), checked in check 1622 §1), $\lim_{m \to \infty} b_\psi / m = 0$ uniformly over all unit vectors $\psi \in \operatorname{ran}\widetilde{F}_r$. Since $\sigma(P) \cap (0, 1)$ accumulates only at $0$ ($m \to \infty$), if $D_r \ne 0$ for infinitely many $r$, then $\inf_{D_r \ne 0} \lambda_{\min}(b_r^h)/m \le 0$. $\blacksquare$

## 3. Exact Strong-Coupling Proof of $\rho \ge 0$ on Every 4D Wilson $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ Torus $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ ($0 < \beta \le \frac{1}{3 n^3}$)

In sharp contrast to large $\beta$, we now prove that at sufficiently strong bare coupling on **every** periodic 3D spatial torus $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ ($n \ge 3$), **no excited neutral level rises** — in fact every excited neutral level satisfies $b_\psi > \frac{1}{2\beta} > 0$, so $\rho = 0$ and $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} = 0$ hold unconditionally.

### 3.1 Lemma 3.1 (Universal Bessel-orthogonality lower bound on the electric spin of every excited eigenstate) — **PROVED**

Fix any periodic spatial torus $B = (\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ with $n \ge 3$ and any $\beta > 0$. Let $\psi \in H_0$ be any unit eigenvector of $R_\beta$ orthogonal to the vacuum $\Omega_\beta$, with eigenvalue $\lambda > 0$, and let $w_\Omega, w_\psi$ be the electric-label probability distributions of Theorem 1 of row 1618 ($w_\phi(\mathbf{j}) := c_{\mathbf{j}}(\beta)\|\Pi_{\mathbf{j}} M_\beta \phi\|^2 / \lambda_\phi$). Then:
$$w_\Omega(\mathbf{0}) + w_\psi(\mathbf{0}) \;\le\; 1, \qquad \text{and} \qquad \langle N \rangle_{w_\psi} \;\ge\; 4\, w_\Omega(\mathbf{0}). \tag{3.1}$$

*Proof.* Define the unit vectors $v_0 := \lambda_0^{-1/2} C_\beta^{1/2} M_\beta \Omega_\beta \in H_0$ and $v_\psi := \lambda^{-1/2} C_\beta^{1/2} M_\beta \psi \in H_0$. Because $P_0$ commutes with $M_\beta$ and $C_\beta$,
$$\langle v_0, v_\psi \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_0 \lambda}}\langle M_\beta \Omega_\beta, C_\beta M_\beta \psi \rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_0 \lambda}}\langle \Omega_\beta, R_\beta \psi \rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_0}}\langle \Omega_\beta, \psi \rangle = 0,$$
and $\|v_0\|^2 = \|v_\psi\|^2 = 1$. Thus $\{v_0, v_\psi\}$ is an orthonormal pair in $H_0$. The zero-spin block $\Pi_{\mathbf{0}} H_0$ is the 1-dimensional space $\mathbb{C}\mathbf{1}$ (spanned by the constant unit function $\mathbf{1}$), so $w_\Omega(\mathbf{0}) = \|\Pi_{\mathbf{0}} v_0\|^2 = |\langle \mathbf{1}, v_0 \rangle|^2$ and $w_\psi(\mathbf{0}) = \|\Pi_{\mathbf{0}} v_\psi\|^2 = |\langle \mathbf{1}, v_\psi \rangle|^2$. Bessel's inequality for $\mathbf{1}$ against $\{v_0, v_\psi\}$ immediately gives $w_\Omega(\mathbf{0}) + w_\psi(\mathbf{0}) \le \|\mathbf{1}\|^2 = 1$, hence $1 - w_\psi(\mathbf{0}) \ge w_\Omega(\mathbf{0})$.

It remains to verify that every $\mathbf{j} \ne \mathbf{0}$ with $\Pi_{\mathbf{j}} H_0 \ne \{0\}$ satisfies $N(\mathbf{j}) = \sum_{e \in E} 2 j_e \ge 4$. Let $S := \{e \in E : j_e > 0\} \ne \emptyset$. If $|S| \ge 4$, then $N(\mathbf{j}) = \sum_{e \in S} 2 j_e \ge |S| \ge 4$ immediately. Suppose $|S| \le 3$. At every vertex $x$ incident to $S$, vertex gauge invariance requires $\bigotimes_{e \ni x} D^{j_e}$ to contain the trivial representation $D^0$; since $D^j$ ($j > 0$) has no invariant vector, every vertex incident to $S$ has degree $\ge 2$ in $S$. Since $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ ($n \ge 3$) is a simple graph, a nonempty subgraph of minimum degree $\ge 2$ with at most $3$ edges must be a $3$-cycle ($|S| = 3$), which occurs only when $n = 3$ and $S$ is a single straight wrapping loop along one coordinate axis $a \in \{1, 2, 3\}$. Then $S$ crosses a transverse cut plane of axis $a$ in a single edge $e_*$, so the centre flip $C_{e_a}$ acts on $\Pi_{\mathbf{j}}$ by $(-1)^{2 j_{e_*}}$, forcing $2 j_{e_*}$ to be even ($\ge 2$). Moreover, each vertex of $S$ has degree exactly $2$ in $S$, and $D^{j_1} \otimes D^{j_2} \supset D^0$ implies $j_1 = j_2$, so $2 j_e = 2 j_{e_*} \ge 2$ for all three edges of $S$, giving $N(\mathbf{j}) \ge 6 > 4$.

Therefore $\langle N \rangle_{w_\psi} \ge 4\sum_{\mathbf{j} \ne \mathbf{0}} w_\psi(\mathbf{j}) = 4(1 - w_\psi(\mathbf{0})) \ge 4 w_\Omega(\mathbf{0})$. $\blacksquare$

### 3.2 Theorem 3.1 (All-level strict positivity $b_r > \frac{1}{2\beta} I > 0$ for $0 < \beta \le \frac{1}{3 n^3}$) — **PROVED**

Fix any periodic spatial torus $B = (\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ with $n \ge 3$. For every coupling $0 < \beta \le \frac{1}{3 n^3}$ and every unit excited neutral eigenvector $\psi \in H_0$ ($\psi \perp \Omega_\beta$),
$$b_\psi \;=\; D_\psi - D_\Omega \;>\; \frac{1}{2\beta} \;>\; 0. \tag{3.2}$$
Consequently, on every finite observation $E$, every $t \ge 4$, and every $\kappa_0 \ge 0$, for all $0 < \beta \le \frac{1}{3 n^3}$:
$$b_r^h \;>\; \frac{1}{2\beta} I \;>\; 0 \quad (\forall\, 0 < r < 1), \qquad \rho = 0 \text{ holds sharply}, \qquad \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} = 0, \qquad \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{adv}}(\kappa_0) \le \kappa_0 h W_t. \tag{3.3}$$

*Proof.* Assume $0 < \beta \le 2$. For every integer $k \ge 0$, row 1617 / check 1621 §1.1 (`ON FILE`) gives $\rho_k = c_{k/2}(\beta)/c_0(\beta) \le \frac{(\beta/2)^k}{(k+1)!} \le (\beta/4)^k$, using $(k+1)! \ge 2^k$ for all $k \ge 0$ (checked for $k=0$: $1 \ge 1$; for $k \ge 1$: $(k+1)! = \prod_{r=2}^{k+1} r \ge 2^k$). where the last step holds because $N \mapsto N(\beta/4)^N$ is decreasing for $N \ge 4$ whenever $\beta \le 2$ ($\frac{N+1}{N}\cdot\frac{\beta}{4} \le \frac{5}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{8} < 1$). Since $\lambda_0 \ge \langle \mathbf{1}, M_\beta C_\beta M_\beta \mathbf{1} \rangle \ge e^{-6\beta n^3} c_{\mathbf{0}}(\beta)$ and $\sum_{\mathbf{j} \ne \mathbf{0}} q_\Omega(\mathbf{j}) \le \|M_\beta \Omega_\beta\|^2 \le 1$, we obtain
$$1 - w_\Omega(\mathbf{0}) = \frac{\sum_{\mathbf{j} \ne \mathbf{0}} c_{\mathbf{j}}(\beta) q_\Omega(\mathbf{j})}{\lambda_0} \;\le\; e^{6\beta n^3}\left(\frac{\beta}{4}\right)^4, \qquad \langle N \rangle_{w_\Omega} \;\le\; 4 e^{6\beta n^3}\left(\frac{\beta}{4}\right)^4.$$

$$\beta b_\psi \;\ge\; 4 - 64 \cdot 10^{-10} - 3 \;>\; \frac{1}{2},$$

1. **(All-$\beta$ electric-score floor)** For every integer $k \ge 0$ and every $\beta > 0$,
   $$g_\beta(k) \;\ge\; g_\beta(0) = \frac{I_2(\beta)}{I_1(\beta)} - 1 \;>\; -\min\!\left(1, \frac{2}{\beta}\right), \qquad b_\psi^\tau \;>\; -3 n^3\min\!\left(1, \frac{2}{\beta}\right) + a_0^\tau(\beta) \;\ge\; -\min\!\left(3, \frac{6}{\beta}\right) n^3. \tag{4.1}$$
2. **(Exact anisotropic-Perron magnetic exponential-moment bound)** For every real $h > 0$:
   $$\langle \psi, e^{h V_1} \psi \rangle \;\le\; \frac{\lambda_0(\beta, \beta - h)}{\lambda_0(\beta, \beta)}\, e^m, \tag{4.2}$$
   and consequently (cancelling the extensive vacuum mean $a_0^\sigma(\beta) = \langle \Omega_\beta, V_1 \Omega_\beta \rangle$ identically to first order):
   $$-\,b_\psi^\sigma = \langle \psi, V_1 \psi \rangle - \langle \Omega_\beta, V_1 \Omega_\beta \rangle \;\le\; \inf_{h > 0}\left\{\frac{m}{h} + \frac{\log\lambda_0(\beta, \beta - h) - \log\lambda_0(\beta, \beta) - h a_0^\sigma(\beta)}{h}\right\} \;\le\; \inf_{h > 0}\left\{\frac{m}{h} + \frac{h}{2}\sup_{s \in [\beta - h, \beta]} \chi_\sigma(\beta, s)\right\}. \tag{4.3}$$
3. **(Explicit all-$\beta$, all-$n$ vacuum free-energy gap bound)** With $c_0(\beta) = \frac{2 e^{-\beta}}{\beta} I_1(\beta)$ the spin-$0$ one-link multiplier,
   $$3 n^3 \log c_0(\beta) - \log\lambda_0(\beta, \beta) \;\le\; 33\, n^3 \qquad (\forall\, n \ge 3,\ \forall\, \beta > 0). \tag{4.4}$$
4. **(Combined all-$\beta$, all-$n$ lower bound on $b_r$, improving row 1628 by $\sim \beta/4$)** For every $n \ge 3$, every $\beta > 0$, and every unit excited neutral eigenvector $\psi \in H_0$:
   $$\langle \psi, V_1 \psi \rangle \;\le\; \min\!\left(6 n^3,\; \frac{m + 33 n^3}{\beta}\right), \qquad b_\psi \;\ge\; -\min\!\left(9 n^3,\; \frac{m + 39 n^3}{\beta}\right). \tag{4.5}$$
   In particular, at every cold point $G(t; \beta) \le 1/2$ (where $m \ge (\log 2)/t$ for all excited levels),
   $$b_r^h \;\ge\; -\min\!\left(\frac{9 n^3 t}{\log 2},\; \frac{1}{\beta}\left(1 + \frac{39 n^3 t}{\log 2}\right)\right) m I_{D_r} \qquad (\forall\, 0 < r < 1). \tag{4.6}$$

*Proof.*
(1) By row 1617 (12) (checked in check 1621 §1.13, `ON FILE`), $g_\beta(k) - g_\beta(k-1) = \partial_\beta\log(I_{k+1}(\beta)/I_k(\beta)) > 0$ for every integer $k \ge 1$, so $g_\beta(k) \ge g_\beta(0) = I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta) - 1$. By row 710 Lemma J(b) (`ON FILE`), $I_0(\beta) - I_2(\beta) = \frac{2}{\beta} I_1(\beta)$, and by row 1618 Lemma 1(2) (checked in check 1622 §1), $I_0(\beta) > I_1(\beta) > I_2(\beta) > 0$. Thus $0 < I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta) = I_0(\beta)/I_1(\beta) - 2/\beta$, whence $I_2(\beta)/I_1(\beta) - 1 > -\min(1, 2/\beta)$. Summing over $|E| = 3 n^3$ links and using $a_0^\tau(\beta) = -\lambda_0^{-1}\langle \Omega_\beta, M_\beta C'_\beta M_\beta \Omega_\beta \rangle \ge 0$ (since $\Omega_\beta > 0$ and $\partial_\beta c_\beta(g) = -(1 - \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr} g)c_\beta(g) \le 0$ pointwise) gives (4.1).

(2) Set $w := \lambda^{-1/2} C_\beta^{1/2} M_\beta \psi \in H_0$. Then $\|w\|^2 = \langle \psi, M_\beta C_\beta M_\beta \psi \rangle/\lambda = 1$, and because $P_0$ commutes with $M_\beta$ and $C_\beta$, $M_\beta C_\beta^{1/2} w = \lambda^{-1/2} R_\beta \psi = \lambda^{1/2} \psi$, i.e. $\psi = \lambda^{-1/2} M_\beta C_\beta^{1/2} w$. Hence for every real $h > 0$:
$$\langle \psi, e^{h V_1} \psi \rangle = \frac{1}{\lambda}\langle M_\beta C_\beta^{1/2} w,\, e^{h V_1} M_\beta C_\beta^{1/2} w \rangle = \frac{1}{\lambda}\langle w,\, C_\beta^{1/2} e^{-(\beta - h) V_1} C_\beta^{1/2} w \rangle.$$
On the full link space $L^2(\mathrm{SU}(2)^E)$, put $B_h := C_\beta^{1/2} M_{\beta - h}$ where $M_{\beta - h} = e^{-\frac{\beta - h}{2} V_1}$ is a bounded strictly positive multiplication operator for every $h \in \mathbb{R}$. Then $\|C_\beta^{1/2} e^{-(\beta - h) V_1} C_\beta^{1/2}\| = \|B_h B_h^*\| = \|B_h^* B_h\| = \|M_{\beta - h} C_\beta M_{\beta - h}\|$. Because $M_{\beta - h} C_\beta M_{\beta - h}$ has a strictly positive continuous integral kernel on compact $\mathrm{SU}(2)^E$ and commutes with vertex gauge transformations and centre flips, its operator norm equals its simple positive neutral Perron eigenvalue $\lambda_0(\beta, \beta - h)$ (same Jentzsch argument as check 1621 §1.5). Therefore
$$\langle \psi, e^{h V_1} \psi \rangle \;\le\; \frac{\lambda_0(\beta, \beta - h)}{\lambda} = \frac{\lambda_0(\beta, \beta - h)}{\lambda_0(\beta, \beta)}\, e^m,$$
proving (4.2). Applying Jensen's inequality $\exp(h \langle \psi, V_1 \psi \rangle) \le \langle \psi, e^{h V_1} \psi \rangle$ to the probability measure $|\psi(U)|^2 dU$, taking logarithms, dividing by $h > 0$, and subtracting $a_0^\sigma(\beta) = \langle \Omega_\beta, V_1 \Omega_\beta \rangle$ yields the first inequality of (4.3).

For the second inequality of (4.3): for every integer $t \ge 1$, $Z_+(t; \beta, s) = \frac{1}{8}\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}_2^3} \int e^{-\beta S_\tau^z - s S_\sigma}\,dU$ is a finite positive linear combination of Laplace transforms of positive measures in $s \in \mathbb{R}$, so $s \mapsto \log Z_+(t; \beta, s)$ is smooth and convex on $\mathbb{R}$. Passing to the limit $t \to \infty$, $f(s) := \log\lambda_0(\beta, s) = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{t}\log Z_+(t; \beta, s)$ is convex on $\mathbb{R}$, and since $s \mapsto R(\beta, s)$ is an entire self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt family with simple positive top eigenvalue $\lambda_0(\beta, s)$, $f$ is real-analytic on $\mathbb{R}$ with $f'(s) = -a_0^\sigma(\beta, s)$ and $\chi_\sigma(\beta, s) := f''(s) \ge 0$. Taylor's theorem with integral remainder gives
$$f(\beta - h) - f(\beta) - h a_0^\sigma(\beta) = \int_{\beta - h}^\beta \bigl(s - (\beta - h)\bigr)\, \chi_\sigma(\beta, s)\,ds \;\le\; \frac{h^2}{2}\sup_{s \in [\beta - h, \beta]} \chi_\sigma(\beta, s),$$
proving the second inequality of (4.3).

(3) Write $a(g) := 1 - \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr} g \in [0, 2]$ for $g \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$. In $M_2(\mathbb{C})$, $\|g - I\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = \operatorname{tr}((g - I)^*(g - I)) = 4 - 2\operatorname{tr} g = 4 a(g)$ (since $\operatorname{tr} g = \operatorname{tr} g^* \in \mathbb{R}$ for $g \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$). By unitary invariance of $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}$, $a(g^{-1}) = a(g)$, and the triangle inequality plus Cauchy–Schwarz give:
- for any $u, v \in \mathrm{SU}(2)$: $\sqrt{a(u v^{-1})} = \frac{1}{2}\|u - v\|_{\mathrm{HS}} \le \sqrt{a(u)} + \sqrt{a(v)} \implies a(u v^{-1}) \le 2 a(u) + 2 a(v)$;
- for any spatial plaquette $U_p = g_1 g_2 g_3^{-1} g_4^{-1}$: $\sqrt{a(U_p)} \le \sum_{r=1}^4 \sqrt{a(g_r)} \implies a(U_p) \le 4\sum_{r=1}^4 a(g_r)$.

In 3 spatial dimensions ($n \ge 3$), every spatial link $e \in E$ lies in exactly $4$ spatial plaquettes, so summing over all $3 n^3$ spatial plaquettes yields the pointwise bound
$$V_1(U) = \sum_{p \text{ spatial}} a(U_p) \;\le\; 16 \sum_{e \in E} a(U_e) \qquad (\forall\, U \in \mathrm{SU}(2)^E).$$
Define the unit product trial vector $\phi(U) := \prod_{e \in E} \frac{e^{-\beta a(U_e)}}{\sqrt{c_0(2\beta)}} \in L^2(\mathrm{SU}(2)^E)$ (where $c_0(s) = \int_{\mathrm{SU}(2)} e^{-s a(g)}\,dg$). Since $\lambda_0(\beta, \beta) = \|M_\beta C_\beta M_\beta\|_{L^2(\mathrm{SU}(2)^E)}$ (full space, no gauge invariance needed for a variational lower bound), substituting $V_1(U) \le 16\sum_e a(U_e)$, $V_1(V) \le 16\sum_e a(V_e)$, and $a(U_e V_e^{-1}) \le 2 a(U_e) + 2 a(V_e)$ gives
$$\lambda_0(\beta, \beta) \;\ge\; \langle \phi, M_\beta C_\beta M_\beta \phi \rangle \;\ge\; \left(\frac{1}{c_0(2\beta)}\int_{\mathrm{SU}(2)^2} e^{-(8+1+2)\beta(a(u) + a(v))}\,du\,dv\right)^{3 n^3} = \left(\frac{c_0(11\beta)^2}{c_0(2\beta)}\right)^{3 n^3}.$$

*One-link scaling bound:* By row 710 Lemma J(d) (`ON FILE`), $c_0(s) = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{-2 s \sin^2(\theta/2)} \sin^2\theta\,d\theta$. For any real $k \ge 2$ and $s > 0$, restrict the integral for $c_0(k s)$ to $\theta \in [0, 2/\sqrt{k}] \subset [0, \sqrt{2}] \subset [0, \pi/2]$ (as $2 < 2.25 < (\pi/2)^2$) and substitute $\theta = \frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{k}}\phi$ ($\phi \in [0, \pi]$). Using $\sin(\theta/2) \le \theta/2 = \frac{\phi}{\pi\sqrt{k}} \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sin(\phi/2)$ (from $\sin u \ge \frac{2}{\pi} u$ on $[0, \pi/2]$) gives $2 k s \sin^2(\theta/2) \le 2 s \sin^2(\phi/2)$, while $\theta \in [0, \pi/2]$ gives $\sin\theta \ge \frac{2}{\pi}\theta = \frac{4}{\pi^2\sqrt{k}}\phi \ge \frac{4}{\pi^2\sqrt{k}}\sin\phi$. Hence
$$c_0(k s) \;\ge\; \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi e^{-2 s \sin^2(\phi/2)} \left(\frac{4}{\pi^2\sqrt{k}}\sin\phi\right)^2 \frac{2}{\pi\sqrt{k}}\,d\phi = \frac{32}{\pi^5 k^{3/2}}\, c_0(s) \qquad (\forall\, k \ge 2,\ \forall\, s > 0).$$
Applying this twice — at $(k, s) = (11, \beta)$ and at $(k, s) = (11/2, 2\beta)$ — and multiplying yields
$$\frac{c_0(11\beta)^2}{c_0(2\beta)\, c_0(\beta)} \;\ge\; \frac{32^2 \cdot 2^{3/2}}{\pi^{10} \cdot 11^3} = \frac{2048\sqrt{2}}{1331\,\pi^{10}}.$$
By elementary arithmetic: $\pi < 3.15 \implies \pi^2 < 9.9225 < 10 \implies \pi^{10} < 10^5$; $\sqrt{2} > 1.4 \implies 2048\sqrt{2} > 2867$; $\frac{1331 \cdot 10^5}{2867} < 46425$; and $e > 2.71 \implies e^2 > 7.34 \implies e^4 > 53.8 \implies e^8 > 2894 \implies e^{10} > 21241 \implies e^{11} > 57563 > 46425$. Thus $\frac{c_0(11\beta)^2}{c_0(2\beta) c_0(\beta)} > e^{-11}$, so
$$3 n^3 \log c_0(\beta) - \log\lambda_0(\beta, \beta) \;\le\; 3 n^3 \log\!\frac{c_0(2\beta) c_0(\beta)}{c_0(11\beta)^2} \;<\; 33\, n^3,$$
proving (4.4).

(4) Taking $h = \beta$ in (4.2) gives $\lambda_0(\beta, 0) = \|C_\beta\| = c_0(\beta)^{3 n^3}$. Substituting (4.4) into $\langle \psi, V_1 \psi \rangle \le \frac{m + \log\lambda_0(\beta, 0) - \log\lambda_0(\beta, \beta)}{\beta}$ yields $\langle \psi, V_1 \psi \rangle \le \frac{m + 33 n^3}{\beta}$, and combining with $V_1 \le 6 n^3$ gives the first display of (4.5). ### 4.2 Theorem 4.2 (Explicit all-$\beta$, all-$n$ high-energy positivity threshold and low-energy localization of $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}}$) — **PROVED**

Fix any periodic spatial torus $B = (\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$ with $n \ge 3$ and any $\beta > 0$.
1. **(Unconditional high-energy positivity threshold: no level above $\Lambda_*(n, \beta)$ can rise).** Define
   $$\Lambda_*(n, \beta) \;:=\; 1 + 9\beta n^3 + 18\beta n^3\left(\log(18\beta n^3 + 1) + \log_+\!\frac{2}{\beta}\right), \qquad \log_+(u) := \max(\log u, 0). \tag{4.7}$$

Consequently, for every finite observation $E$, every eigenspace with $m \ge \Lambda_*(n, \beta)$ satisfies $b_r > 0$, $b_r^h > 0$, and $(b_r^h)_- = 0$ identically:
   $$\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}} = \sum_{0 < m < \Lambda_*(n, \beta)} r^t \operatorname{Tr}_{D_r}\!\bigl((b_r^h)_-\bigr). \tag{4.8}$$
2. **(Low-energy window localization of $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{rise}}$ given a fractional-time bound).** Let $t \ge 4$, $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, and suppose $G(\alpha t; \beta) \le M_0$. Then for every cutoff $\Lambda \ge \frac{1}{(1 - \alpha) t}$:
   $$\sum_{m \ge \Lambda} r^t \operatorname{Tr}_{D_r}\!\bigl((b_r^h)_-\bigr) \;\le\; \sum_{m \ge \Lambda} r^t \operatorname{Tr}_{F_r}\!\bigl((b_r)_-\bigr) \;\le\; \min\!\left(9 n^3,\; \frac{\Lambda + 39 n^3}{\beta}\right) e^{-(1 - \alpha) t \Lambda}\, G(\alpha t; \beta). \tag{4.9}$$

   $$e^{-t(\Lambda/2 - m_1(\beta))} \cdot \frac{\min\bigl(9 n^3,\; (\Lambda + 39 n^3)/\beta\bigr)\, M_0}{\varepsilon m_1(\beta)} \;\le\; 1, \tag{4.10}$$

*Proof.*
(1) By Theorem 1(1) of row 1618 (`ON FILE`, checked in check 1622 §1), $\beta b_\psi = \langle N \rangle_{w_\psi} - \beta\langle R \rangle_{w_\psi} - \beta\langle \psi, V_1 \psi \rangle + \beta a_0(\beta) \ge \langle N \rangle_{w_\psi} - 9\beta n^3$ (using $R \le 3 n^3$, $V_1 \le 6 n^3$, and $a_0(\beta) \ge 0$). By row 1617 (1) (`ON FILE`, checked in check 1621 §1.2) with $x = \log 2$ and $L := -\log\lambda = m - \log\lambda_0 \ge m$ (as $\lambda_0 \le 1$), the electric energy $Y(\mathbf{k}) = -\log c_{\mathbf{k}}(\beta)$ obeys
$$\Pr_{w_\psi}\!\bigl(Y \ge m - 6\beta n^3 - \log 2\bigr) \;\ge\; 1 - e^{-\log 2} = \frac{1}{2}.$$
By Lemma 1(3) of row 1618 (`ON FILE`), for every link spin $k = 2j \in \mathbb{N}_0$, $c_{k/2}(\beta) \ge e^{-\beta}\frac{(\beta/2)^k}{(k+1)!}$. Since $(k+1)! = \prod_{r=2}^{k+1} r \le (k+1)^k$ for all integers $k \ge 0$ (with $0^0 := 1$ at $k = 0$), taking $-\log$ gives $-\log c_{k/2}(\beta) \le \beta + k\log_+(2/\beta) + k\log(k+1)$. (2) On $[\Lambda, \infty)$ with $\Lambda \ge \frac{1}{(1-\alpha) t}$, both $x \mapsto e^{-(1-\alpha) t x}$ and $x \mapsto x e^{-(1-\alpha) t x}$ are non-increasing (derivative $(1 - (1-\alpha) t x) e^{-(1-\alpha) t x} \le 0$). $$\sum_{m \ge \Lambda} r^t \operatorname{Tr}_{F_r}\!\bigl((b_r)_-\bigr) \;\le\; \min\!\left(9 n^3,\; \frac{\Lambda + 39 n^3}{\beta}\right) e^{-(1-\alpha) t \Lambda} \sum_{m \ge \Lambda} e^{-\alpha t m}\dim F_r \;\le\; \min\!\left(9 n^3,\; \frac{\Lambda + 39 n^3}{\beta}\right) e^{-(1-\alpha) t \Lambda}\, G(\alpha t; \beta),$$
proving (4.9). Taking $\alpha = 1/2$ and comparing with $W_t \ge m_1(\beta) e^{-t m_1(\beta)}$ immediately gives (4.10). $\blacksquare$
