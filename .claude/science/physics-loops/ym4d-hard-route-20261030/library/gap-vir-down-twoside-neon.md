### 1.2 Theorem 1.1 (Exact necessary and sufficient rate-profile inequalities on $[\beta_K, \beta_{K+r}]$) — **PROVED**

Fix an admissible consumer pair $(A, s_0)$ (with divisibility $D_A \mid \ell$), release witnesses $(b, g_0)$, a fixed UV physical side $L_h \in D_A\mathbb{N}_{>0}$, and an integer $r \ge 1$ with $s r > W(r)$. Set the seed physical side $\ell := L_h b^r$. At each $K \ge K_I$, the lattice of side $n_K = \ell b^K = L_h b^{K+r}$ at coupling $\beta_{K+r}$ is the calibrated physical box of side $L_h$ at cutoff $K' := K + r$. Define the exact minimal hot-end cooling aspect at threshold $s_0$:
$$\alpha_*^{(K+r)} := \frac{1}{n_K}\inf\bigl\{t \ge 1 : G(t; \beta_{K+r}) \le s_0\bigr\}.$$

1. **(Exact profile condition for `(HOT)` on $[\beta_K, \beta_{K+r}]$).** Let $\kappa: [\beta_K, \beta_{K+r}] \to [0, \infty)$ be continuous, and put $t(\beta) = (n_K/A)\exp(\int_{\beta_K}^\beta \kappa)$. Then `(HOT)` $G(t(\beta_{K+r}); \beta_{K+r}) \le s_0$ holds **if and only if**
   $$\int_{\beta_K}^{\beta_{K+r}} \kappa(\beta)\,d\beta \;\ge\; \log_+\!\bigl(A\,\alpha_*^{(K+r)}\bigr), \tag{1.3}$$
   where $\log_+(x) := \max(\log x, 0)$. In particular, if $G(1; \beta_{K+r}) > s_0$, then $\alpha_*^{(K+r)}$ is the unique root of $G(\alpha_*^{(K+r)} n_K; \beta_{K+r}) = s_0$, and satisfies the explicit two-sided spectral bracket:
   - *(Necessary lower bound)*
     $$\alpha_*^{(K+r)} \;\ge\; \frac{\log(1/s_0)}{n_K m_1(\beta_{K+r})}, \qquad \int_{\beta_K}^{\beta_{K+r}} \kappa(\beta)\,d\beta \;\ge\; \log_+\!\frac{A\log(1/s_0)}{n_K m_1(\beta_{K+r})}. \tag{1.4}$$
   - *(Sufficient upper bound, Corollary B)* If $n_K m_1(\beta_{K+r}) \ge \gamma_h > 0$ and $G(\alpha_0 n_K; \beta_{K+r}) \le N_0$ with $\alpha_0 > 0$ and $N_0 \ge s_0$, then
     $$\alpha_*^{(K+r)} \;\le\; \alpha_h := \alpha_0 + \frac{1}{\gamma_h}\log\frac{N_0}{s_0}, \tag{1.5}$$
     so every continuous $\kappa \ge 0$ with $\int_{\beta_K}^{\beta_{K+r}} \kappa(\beta)\,d\beta \ge \log_+(A \alpha_h)$ satisfies `(HOT)`.
2. **(Exact average-rate bracket from the calibration).** Writing $\bar{\kappa}_K := (\beta_{K+r} - \beta_K)^{-1}\int_{\beta_K}^{\beta_{K+r}} \kappa(\beta)\,d\beta$, for every $K \ge K_I$:
   $$\bar{\kappa}_K \;\ge\; \frac{\log_+(A \alpha_h)}{s r - W(r)} \quad \text{suffices for (1.3)}, \qquad \bar{\kappa}_K \;\ge\; \frac{\log_+\!\bigl(A\log(1/s_0) / (n_K m_1(\beta_{K+r}))\bigr)}{s r + W(r)} \quad \text{is necessary for (1.3)}, \tag{1.6}$$
   where $s = \frac{11\log b}{3\pi^2}$ and $W(r) = 2 C_I + 2 c + \frac{2 Q}{s}(1 + \log r)$.

*Proof.* On a fixed finite Wilson box, $t \mapsto G(t; \beta_{K+r}) = \sum_{i \ge 1} e^{-t m_i(\beta_{K+r})}$ is continuous, strictly decreasing on $[1, \infty)$ (injectivity of $P$, check 1613 `(R)` item 1), and tends to $0$ as $t \to \infty$. Thus $G(t; \beta_{K+r}) \le s_0$ holds if and only if $t \ge \alpha_*^{(K+r)} n_K$ (with $t \ge u_K = n_K/A$ automatically satisfied since $\kappa \ge 0$). Substituting $t(\beta_{K+r}) = (n_K/A)\exp(\int_{\beta_K}^{\beta_{K+r}} \kappa)$ gives (1.3). Keeping only $i = 1$ in $G(t; \beta_{K+r}) \le s_0$ gives $e^{-t m_1} \le s_0$, i.e. $t \ge \log(1/s_0)/m_1$, proving (1.4). Under the Corollary B hypotheses, for $t \ge \alpha_0 n_K$, $G(t; \beta_{K+r}) \le e^{-(t - \alpha_0 n_K) m_1} G(\alpha_0 n_K; \beta_{K+r}) \le N_0 e^{-(t - \alpha_0 n_K)\gamma_h/n_K} \le s_0$ at $t = \alpha_h n_K$, proving (1.5). Dividing (1.3) by $\beta_{K+r} - \beta_K$ and inserting the two-sided calibration bracket (1.2) proves (1.6). $\blacksquare$

**Two structural lessons (ARGUED / GUIDE):**
1. **Why the $1\%$ margin at $8/3$ was an artifact of fixing $\ell/L_h = 512$ and $A = 512$.** Because `(PUR-io)` only asks for *one* fixed physical side $\ell$ (independent of $K$), increasing $\ell = L_h b^r$ increases the available bare width linearly in $r$ ($\beta_{K+r} - \beta_K \sim s r$) while keeping the hot box $(L_h b^{K+r}, \beta_{K+r})$ and its required integral $\log(A\alpha_h)$ completely **independent of $r$**. Thus a rate far below $8/3$ — in fact, **any fixed $\kappa_0 > 0$** — suffices, trading a lower required rate for a longer bare window of width $\sim \log(A\alpha_h)/\kappa_0$ that extends past the order-one band into the infrared scaling regime ($L(\beta) \gg \Lambda_{\mathrm{YM}}^{-1}$). Along the characteristic $t(\beta) = u_K e^{\kappa_0(\beta-\beta_K)}$ with $\kappa_0 < \kappa_{\mathrm{scale}}$, the physical time $T_{\mathrm{phys}}(\beta) \propto L(\beta)^{1 - \kappa_0/\kappa_{\mathrm{scale}}}$ grows as a positive power of $L(\beta)$ in the IR scaling regime, so the box becomes exponentially colder (not hotter) as $\beta$ decreases toward $\beta_K$.

### 2.1 Notation and the general Jordan-split test class

**ON FILE (rows 1624, 1626, checks 1625, 1627).** Fix a finite Wilson box $(\mathbb{Z}/n)^3$, $\beta > 0$, trial rate $\kappa \ge 0$, real $t \ge 4$, and buffer $k \ge 0$ with $t - h \ge 2$, where $h := 2k + 2$, $j := \lfloor (t-h)/2 \rfloor \ge 1$, and $v := k + j + 1 = \lfloor t/2 \rfloor$. Write $X := SU(2)^{3n^3}$ with stationary probability $\pi(dx) = \Omega_\beta(x)^2 dx$, neutral Markov operator $P$ with real orthonormal neutral eigenbasis $\{f_i\}_{i \ge 0}$ ($f_0 = 1$, $1 > r_1 \ge r_2 \ge \cdots > 0$), $Q := I - |1\rangle\langle 1|$, and a fixed finite neutral observation partition $\mathcal{C}$ with orthogonal projection $E$ ($E 1 = 1$, $d := \operatorname{rank}(E Q) < \infty$). Denote the exact thermal secant contrast (row 1624 (10), (16)) by
$$\mathcal{E} := \int D_{*,k}\,d\mu_k = -2\operatorname{Tr}\bigl(P^{t-2} S\bigr) = 4\left[\frac{Z(t)}{t}\partial_\beta X(t) - \kappa\sum_{i>0} r_i^t(e^{m_i} - 1)\right], \tag{2.1}$$
where $S = S^0 + 2\kappa(P - P^2)$, $S^0 = -Q U^{-1}(A_\beta^2)' U Q$, $\mu_k = b_k q_h$ has density $S_k = P^k S P^k$ relative to $\pi \otimes \pi$ (with zero marginals), and $D_{*,k}(x, y) := \sum_{i>0} r_i^{t-h}(f_i(x) - f_i(y))^2$. Row 1624's one-sided test (15) is $\mathcal{M}_k^{(15)} := \int L_{j+1}\,d\mu_k^+ - \int D_j\,d\mu_k^- \ge 0$.

For any pair of continuous, symmetric, neutral (gauge- and centre-invariant in each argument) kernels $W_+, W_-: X \times X \to \mathbb{R}$, consider the general pointwise-weight Jordan test
$$\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu_k) := \int W_+\,d\mu_k^+ - \int W_-\,d\mu_k^- \;\ge\; 0. \tag{2.2}$$
Let $\mathcal{Z}_0$ denote the real vector space of symmetric neutral $L^2(\pi \otimes \pi)$ densities $\mu(x, y)$ with zero marginals ($\int \mu(x, y)\,\pi(dy) = 0$ a.e.), identified with signed measures $\mu(x, y)\,\pi(dx)\pi(dy)$.

### 2.2 Theorem 2.1 (Exact potential characterization of all sound Jordan-split tests) — **PROVED**

Fix a finite Wilson box at $\beta > 0$, $\kappa \ge 0$, real $t \ge 4$, and buffer $k \ge 0$ with $t - h \ge 2$. Let $W_+, W_-$ be continuous, symmetric, neutral kernels on $X \times X$. Assume:
- *(Nontriviality)* There exists $\mu_0 \in \mathcal{Z}_0$ with $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu_0) > 0$;
- *(Soundness)* For every smooth one-parameter family $b \mapsto P(b)$ of symmetric, positive-definite, strictly positive-kernel neutral Markov operators on $L^2(\pi)$ with fixed stationary law $\pi$ and anchor $P(\beta) = P$, writing $A' := P'(\beta)$ ($A' 1 = 0$), $S^0 := -Q(P A' + A' P)Q$, $S := S^0 + 2\kappa(P - P^2)$, and $\mu_k := (P^k S P^k)\,d(\pi \otimes \pi)$,
  $$\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu_k) \ge 0 \implies \frac{Z(t)}{t}\partial_b X(t)\Big|_{b=\beta} \ge \kappa Z(t)\bar{E}_t. \tag{2.3}$$

Then:
1. $W_+(x, y) \le W_-(x, y)$ for all $(x, y) \in X \times X$;
2. There exist a constant $\lambda > 0$ and a continuous neutral potential $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$ such that
   $$\lambda W_+(x, y) \;\le\; D_{*,k}(x, y) - f(x) - f(y) \;\le\; \lambda W_-(x, y) \qquad \forall (x, y) \in X \times X. \tag{2.4}$$
Conversely, if (2.4) holds for some $\lambda > 0$ and bounded measurable $f: X \to \mathbb{R}$, then $\lambda \mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu_k) \le \mathcal{E}$ for every zero-marginal symmetric $\mu_k$, so (2.2) implies `(VIR-down)` at rate $\kappa$.

*Proof.* The converse is one line: since $\mu_k$ has zero marginals and $f$ is bounded, $\int (f(x) + f(y))\,d\mu_k = 0$, so (2.4) yields $\lambda \mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu_k) \le \int (D_{*,k} - f \oplus f)\,d\mu_k = \int D_{*,k}\,d\mu_k = \mathcal{E}$, and $e^m - 1 \ge m$ gives `(VIR-down)`.

For the direct implication, proceed in four steps.

**Step 1 (Scaling to cone inclusion on $\mathcal{Z}_0$).** Let $\mathcal{V}_L := \operatorname{span}\{f_1, \dots, f_L\} \subset Q L^2(\pi)$. Each $f_i$ is smooth and bounded (row 1624 (14)) with $r_i > 0$. For any symmetric finite-rank operator $T$ on $\mathcal{V}_L$ (identified with its smooth kernel in $\mathcal{Z}_0$), set $S_T := P^{-k} T P^{-k}$ on $\mathcal{V}_L$ and define the smooth bounded self-adjoint Lyapunov operator
$$A'_T := -\sum_{i,l=1}^L \frac{\langle f_i, S_T f_l \rangle}{r_i + r_l}\, |f_i\rangle\langle f_l|.$$
Then $A'_T 1 = 0$, $A'_T$ vanishes on $\mathcal{V}_L^\perp$, and $-(P A'_T + A'_T P) = S_T$. Because the Wilson kernel obeys $p_1(x, y) \ge c_0 > 0$ on compact $X \times X$ (row 1624 L273) and $P|_{\mathcal{V}_L} \ge r_L I_{\mathcal{V}_L} > 0$, for every scalar $c > 0$ the affine family $P_c(b) := P + (b - \beta) c A'_T$ is symmetric, strictly positive-kernel, strictly positive-definite (unchanged on $\mathcal{V}_L^\perp$ and $\ge (r_L/2) I_{\mathcal{V}_L}$ on $\mathcal{V}_L$), trace-class, and has simple top eigenvalue $1$ with fixed stationary law $\pi$ on a sufficiently small interval $|b - \beta| < \delta_c$. Its trace derivative at $b = \beta$ is $\frac{Z(t)}{t}\partial_b X(t) = c\sum_{i=1}^L r_i^{t-1}\langle f_i, A'_T f_i\rangle = \frac{c}{4}\int D_{*,k}\,dT$, and its buffered signed measure is $\mu_k = c T + \nu_\kappa$ where $\nu_\kappa := 2\kappa P^{2k}(P - P^2)\,d(\pi \otimes \pi)$.

Now suppose $T \in \operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{V}_L \otimes \mathcal{V}_L)$ satisfies $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; T) > 0$. Since $W_\pm$ are bounded, $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \cdot)$ is continuous in $L^1(\pi \otimes \pi)$ and positively homogeneous of degree $1$. For all large $c > 0$, $\frac{1}{c}\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; cT + \nu_\kappa) = \mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; T + c^{-1}\nu_\kappa) > 0$. By Soundness (2.3), $\frac{c}{4}\int D_{*,k}\,dT \ge \kappa Z(t)\bar{E}_t$; dividing by $c \to +\infty$ gives $\int D_{*,k}\,dT \ge 0$. Since $\bigcup_{L \ge 1}\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal{V}_L \otimes \mathcal{V}_L)$ is dense in $\mathcal{Z}_0$ in $L^2(\pi \otimes \pi)$ (hence in $L^1(\pi \otimes \pi)$), continuity gives:
$$\mu \in \mathcal{Z}_0,\quad \mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu) > 0 \implies \int D_{*,k}\,d\mu \ge 0. \tag{2.5}$$

**Step 2 (Proof that $W_+ \le W_-$ everywhere).** Suppose for contradiction that $W_+(x_0, y_0) - W_-(x_0, y_0) \ge 4\delta > 0$ at some $(x_0, y_0) \in X \times X$. By continuity and neutrality, there are neutral open neighborhoods $U$ of $x_0$ and $V$ of $y_0$ on which $W_+ - W_- \ge 2\delta$ and $|(W_+ + W_-)(u, v) - (W_+ + W_-)(x_0, y_0)| \le \delta/4$. Pick distinct gauge-centre orbits $[x_1] \in U$ and $[y_1] \in V$ such that $p_{t-h}(x_0, y_0) + p_{t-h}(x_1, y_1) - p_{t-h}(x_0, y_1) - p_{t-h}(x_1, y_0) \ne 0$: such points exist because $p_{t-h}(\cdot, y_1) - p_{t-h}(\cdot, y_0)$ is real-analytic and nonconstant on $X$ for $[y_1] \ne [y_0]$ (by injectivity of $P$ and analyticity, row 1624 (14)), so it cannot be constant on the open set $U$. Choose small neutral neighborhoods $U_0, U_1 \subset U$ of $x_0, x_1$ and $V_0, V_1 \subset V$ of $y_0, y_1$ (with $U_0 \cap U_1 = \emptyset$ and $V_0 \cap V_1 = \emptyset$), and set $\phi_a := \mathbf{1}_{U_a}/\pi(U_a)$, $\psi_a := \mathbf{1}_{V_a}/\pi(V_a)$, and
$$\mu := \frac{1}{2}\Bigl[(\phi_0 - \phi_1) \otimes (\psi_0 - \psi_1) + (\psi_0 - \psi_1) \otimes (\phi_0 - \phi_1)\Bigr] \in \mathcal{Z}_0.$$
Note $\int d|\mu| \ge 2$ (if $U \cap V = \emptyset$, $\int d|\mu| = 4$; if $x_0 = y_0$ and we take $V_a = U_a$, $\int d|\mu| = 4$ as well; in general, choosing small enough neighborhoods so $U_0, U_1, V_0, V_1$ are either pairwise disjoint or $U_a = V_a$, $\int d|\mu| = 4$). Writing $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \pm \mu) = \pm \int \frac{W_+ + W_-}{2}\,d\mu + \int \frac{W_+ - W_-}{2}\,d|\mu|$, the second term is at least $\delta \int d|\mu| \ge 2\delta$, while the first term has magnitude at most $\frac{\delta}{4}\int d|\mu| \le \delta$. Hence **both** $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu) > 0$ and $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; -\mu) > 0$. By (2.5), $\int D_{*,k}\,d\mu = 0$. Yet letting the neighborhoods shrink gives $\int D_{*,k}\,d\mu \to -2[p_{t-h}(x_0, y_0) + p_{t-h}(x_1, y_1) - p_{t-h}(x_0, y_1) - p_{t-h}(x_1, y_0)] \ne 0$, contradiction. This proves (1).

**Step 3 (Existence of $\lambda > 0$).** By Step 2, $W_- - W_+ \ge 0$, so $\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu) = \int \frac{W_+ + W_-}{2}\,d\mu - \int \frac{W_- - W_+}{2}\,d|\mu|$ is concave and positively homogeneous on $\mathcal{Z}_0$. Hence
$$K := \left\{\left(\int D_{*,k}\,d\mu + u,\; -\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu) + v\right) : \mu \in \mathcal{Z}_0,\ u \ge 0,\ v \ge 0\right\} \subset \mathbb{R}^2$$
is convex and contains $(0, 0)$. By (2.5), $K$ is disjoint from $(-\infty, 0) \times (-\infty, 0)$. By the separating hyperplane theorem in $\mathbb{R}^2$, there exists $(a, b) \ne (0, 0)$ with $a x + b y \ge 0$ on $K$. Letting $u, v \to +\infty$ gives $a, b \ge 0$. Nontriviality ($\mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu_0) > 0$) rules out $a = 0$ (which would force $b \le 0$). Thus $a > 0$, and putting $\lambda := b/a \ge 0$ yields $\lambda \mathcal{T}(W_+, W_-; \mu) \le \int D_{*,k}\,d\mu$ for all $\mu \in \mathcal{Z}_0$. Taking $\mu = f_1 \otimes f_1$ gives $\int D_{*,k}\,d\mu = -2 r_1^{t-h} < 0$, ruling out $\lambda = 0$. Hence $\lambda > 0$.

**Step 4 (Constructing the continuous neutral potential $f$).** Put $g_+ := D_{*,k} - \lambda W_+$ and $g_- := \lambda W_- - D_{*,k}$. Both are continuous, symmetric, and neutral, with $g_+ + g_- = \lambda(W_- - W_+) \ge 0$, and Step 3 says $\int g_+\,d\mu^+ + \int g_-\,d\mu^- \ge 0$ for all $\mu \in \mathcal{Z}_0$. Whenever $\mu = \nu_+ - \nu_-$ with nonnegative densities $\nu_\pm$, we have $\nu_\pm = \mu^\pm + \rho$ ($\rho \ge 0$), so $\int g_+\,d\nu_+ + \int g_-\,d\nu_- \ge \int g_+\,d\mu^+ + \int g_-\,d\mu^- \ge 0$. For any $m \ge 1$ and any $x_1, y_1, \dots, x_m, y_m \in X$ ($x_{m+1} := x_1$), replacing point masses by normalized indicators of shrinking neutral neighborhoods in $\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^m (\delta_{(x_r, y_r)} + \delta_{(y_r, x_r)} - \delta_{(y_r, x_{r+1})} - \delta_{(x_{r+1}, y_r)})$ and passing to the limit by continuity yields the bipartite cycle inequality
$$\sum_{r=1}^m \bigl(g_+(x_r, y_r) + g_-(y_r, x_{r+1})\bigr) \ge 0. \tag{2.6}$$
Fix a base point $x_0 \in X$. On the bipartite graph with left copy $X_L = X$, right copy $X_R = X$, edge weights $g_+(u, v)$ ($X_L \to X_R$) and $g_-(v, u)$ ($X_R \to X_L$), define the shortest-path distances from $x_0 \in X_L$:
- $d_R(y) := \inf\{\sum_{r=1}^m g_+(u_r, v_r) + \sum_{r=1}^{m-1} g_-(v_r, u_{r+1}) : m \ge 1,\, u_1 = x_0,\, v_m = y\}$,
- $d_L(x) := \inf\{0\} \cup \{\sum_{r=1}^m g_+(u_r, v_r) + \sum_{r=1}^m g_-(v_r, u_{r+1}) : m \ge 1,\, u_1 = x_0,\, u_{m+1} = x\}$.

By (2.6), $d_R(y) \ge -g_-(y, x_0)$ and $d_L(x) \ge -g_+(x, x_0) - g_-(x_0, x_0)$, while $d_R(y) \le g_+(x_0, y)$ and $d_L(x) \le g_+(x_0, x_0) + g_-(x_0, x)$, so $d_L, d_R$ are bounded. Shortest-path subadditivity gives $g_+(x, y) \ge d_R(y) - d_L(x)$ and $g_-(y, x) \ge d_L(x) - d_R(y)$ for all $x, y \in X$. Moreover, $|d_R(y) - d_R(y')| \le \sup_x |g_+(x, y) - g_+(x, y')|$ and $|d_L(x) - d_L(x')| \le \sup_y |g_-(y, x) - g_-(y, x')|$, so $d_L, d_R$ are continuous and neutral on compact $X$. Putting $f(x) := \frac{1}{2}(d_R(x) - d_L(x))$ and averaging the two inequalities with their $(x \leftrightarrow y)$ mirrors (using symmetry of $g_\pm$) gives $g_+(x, y) \ge f(x) + f(y)$ and $g_-(x, y) \ge -f(x) - f(y)$, which is exactly (2.4). $\blacksquare$

### 2.3 Corollary 2.2 (Four sharp obstructions to pure observation-only two-sided tests) — **PROVED**

Under the setting of Theorem 2.1:

1. **(No improvement on $W_-$ without making $W_+$ negative on the diagonal).** If $W_+(x, x) \ge 0$ for all $x \in X$ (in particular, if $W_+ \ge 0$), then (2.4) at $x = y$ forces $f(x) \le 0$ for all $x$, and therefore
   $$\lambda W_-(x, y) \;\ge\; D_{*,k}(x, y) \qquad \forall (x, y) \in X \times X. \tag{2.7}$$
   If in addition $W_+ = L_{j+1}$ and $W_-(x, x) = 0$ for all $x$, then $f \equiv 0$, $\lambda \le \lambda_{\max} := \inf_{L_{j+1}(x, y) > 0} \frac{D_{*,k}(x, y)}{L_{j+1}(x, y)}$, and for every $\mu_k$,
   $$\mathcal{T}(L_{j+1}, W_-; \mu_k) \;\le\; \int L_{j+1}\,d\mu_k^+ - \frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}\int D_{*,k}\,d\mu_k^-. \tag{2.8}$$
   In particular, for any test normalized by $\lambda = 1$ (direct domination $\mathcal{T} \le \mathcal{E}$) with $0 \le W_+ \le L_{j+1}$, no choice of $W_-$ can beat row 1624's test (15) by more than the integer-rounding slack $\int (D_j - D_{*,k})\,d\mu_k^-$ (which is **identically zero** at every even integer $t$).

2. **(Pure observation-only negative weights $W_- = \beta L_j$ are impossible on every late Wilson box and on a 4-state positive Markov chain).**
   - *(Late Wilson boxes)* Whenever $6 n^3 > |\mathcal{C}|$ (true at all sufficiently late cutoffs $K$ since $|\mathcal{C}|$ is independent of $K$), there exist distinct gauge-and-centre orbits $[x] \ne [y]$ with $L_j(x, y) = 0$ and $D_{*,k}(x, y) > 0$. By (2.7), no sound test with $W_+(x, x) \ge 0$ can have $W_-(x, y)$ vanish whenever $L_j(x, y) = 0$ (in particular, no $W_- = \beta L_j$ for any $\beta \ge 0$).
   - *(4-state positive Markov chain counterexample)* On $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ with uniform $\pi$, 2-cell partition $\mathcal{C} = \{\{0,1\}, \{2,3\}\}$, $f_1 = (+1,+1,-1,-1)$ (observed), $f_2 = (+1,-1,+1,-1)$ and $f_3 = (+1,-1,-1,+1)$ (hidden), let $P = |1\rangle\langle 1| + r|f_1\rangle\langle f_1| + s(|f_2\rangle\langle f_2| + |f_3\rangle\langle f_3|)$ with $r = e^{-m}$, $s = e^{-0.6 m}$ ($m \in (0, 1]$), $r' = 3 m r$, $s' = 0$. All entries of $P$ are strictly positive (within-cell off-diagonal is $\frac{1}{4}(1 + e^{-m} - 2 e^{-0.6 m}) > 0$ for all $m > 0$ because $g(m) := 1 + e^{-m} - 2 e^{-0.6 m}$ has $g(0) = 0$, $g'(m) = -e^{-m} + 1.2 e^{-0.6 m} > 0$ for all $m \ge 0$). Across cells ($f_1(x) f_1(y) = -1$), $f_2(x)f_2(y) + f_3(x)f_3(y) \equiv 0$, so $S_k(x, y) = 2 r^{2k+1}[3 m r - \kappa(1 - r)] > 0$ on every across-cell pair whenever $\kappa \le 3 r$ (since $1 - r = 1 - e^{-m} < m$; for $m \le 1$, $3 r \ge 3 e^{-1} > 1$). Since $L_j$ is supported exclusively on across-cell pairs, $\int (b_k)_- L_j\,dq_h = 0$ while $\int (b_k)_+ L_{j+1}\,dq_h > 0$: every test $\int (b_k)_+ L_{j+1}\,dq_h \ge C \int (b_k)_- L_j\,dq_h$ ($C > 0$) passes strictly. Yet at $t = 5/m$ (e.g. $m = 1/4, t = 20$; $G = e^{-5} + 2 e^{-3} < 0.11 < 4/21$, cold!), the true thermal rate is $R = \frac{3 e^{-5}}{e^{-5} + 1.2 e^{-3}} = \frac{3 e^{-2}}{e^{-2} + 1.2} < \frac{1}{3}$, violating `(VIR-down)` for every $\kappa \in [1/3, 3r]$ (and taking $s' < 0$ violates `(VIR-down)` even at $\kappa = 0$).

3. **(No functional of finitely many observed projections $\{E P^a S P^b E : 0 \le a, b \le M\}$ alone can imply `(VIR-down)`).** On **every** finite Wilson box (every $n$, every finite partition $\mathcal{C}$ of nonconstant rank $d < \infty$) and every integer $M \ge 0$, choose $L := (M + 1) d + 1$. The linear map $g \mapsto (E P^a g)_{a=0}^M$ from $\mathcal{V}_L$ (dimension $L$) into $(\operatorname{range}(E Q))^{M+1}$ (dimension $(M+1)d < L$) has a nonzero unit vector $g \in \mathcal{V}_L$ in its kernel. Perturbing $P$ via the Step 1 Lyapunov operator $A'_T$ for $T = c |g\rangle\langle g|$ ($c > 0$) adds $c|g\rangle\langle g|$ to $S$ while leaving $E P^a S P^b E$ **identically unchanged** for all $0 \le a, b \le M$, yet sends $\frac{Z(t)}{t}\partial_b X(t)$ down by $\frac{c}{2}\langle g, P^{t-2} g\rangle \ge \frac{c}{2} r_L^{t-2} \to +\infty$ as $c \to +\infty$, violating `(VIR-down)` even at $\kappa = 0$.

4. **(Charging $\mu_k^-$ only on $L_s$ IS possible by shifting $f(x)$ positive, but gives a dual one-sided test capped by row 1626's ceiling).** With $s, R_t$ as in Theorem 2.3 below, let $R_s := P^{2s} - P^s E P^s \ge 0$ and $u_s(x) := \langle e_x, R_s e_x \rangle$. Choosing $f(x) = 2 u_s(x)$ gives $D_s(x, y) - 2 u_s(x) - 2 u_s(y) = L_s(x, y) - \|R_s^{1/2}(e_x + e_y)\|^2 \le L_s(x, y)$, yielding the sound dual test
   $$\mathcal{M}_k^{(+)} := \int \bigl(L_s - \|R_s^{1/2}(e_x + e_y)\|^2\bigr)\,d\mu_k^+ - \int (L_s + R_t)\,d\mu_k^- = V_s - \operatorname{Tr}(M_+) - \int R_t\,d\mu_k^- \ge 0, \tag{2.9}$$
   where $M_\pm := \int |R_s^{1/2}(e_x + e_y)\rangle\langle R_s^{1/2}(e_x + e_y)|\,d\mu_k^\pm \ge 0$ satisfy $M_+ - M_- = 2 B_s$ ($B_s := R_s^{1/2} S_k R_s^{1/2}$, since $(\phi(x) + \phi(y))^2$ integrates to $+2\langle \phi, S_k \phi \rangle$). Hence $\operatorname{Tr}(M_+) \ge 2\operatorname{Tr}((B_s)_+)$, so $\mathcal{M}_k^{(+)} \le V_s - 2\operatorname{Tr}((B_s)_+)$: it removes the hidden weight from $\mu_k^-$ only by placing the identical spectral penalty onto $\mu_k^+$.

*Proof of 2.3(2) (Wilson fiber dimension).* Fix a spanning tree of the $n^3$ spatial vertices and gauge-fix its $n^3 - 1$ links to $I$. The remaining $2n^3 + 1$ links parameterize $SU(2)^{2n^3 + 1}$ (dimension $6 n^3 + 3$). On the open dense subset where the spatial link holonomies generate $SU(2)$, the residual global conjugation group $SO(3)$ (dimension $3$) acts freely and the centre group $\mathbb{Z}_2^3$ is finite, yielding an embedded smooth submanifold $U_0$ of dimension $6 n^3$ whose points lie in pairwise distinct gauge-and-centre orbits. The smooth map $\Phi: U_0 \to \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{C}|}$, $\Phi(x) := (P^j(x, C))_{C \in \mathcal{C}}$, has maximal differential rank $r_* \le |\mathcal{C}| < 6 n^3$. Pick distinct $x, y \in U_0$ in that level set; then $L_j(x, y) = 0$. Since $[x] \ne [y]$ are distinct orbits in the compact Hausdorff quotient $X / (\mathcal{G} \times \mathbb{Z}_2^3)$, continuous neutral functions separate them, so completeness of $\{f_i\}$ in neutral $L^2(\pi)$ and continuity of each $f_i$ force $f_i(x) \ne f_i(y)$ for some $i \ge 1$, giving $D_{*,k}(x, y) \ge r_i^{t-h}(f_i(x) - f_i(y))^2 > 0$. All other parts are proved in the displays above. $\blacksquare$

### 2.4 Theorem 2.3 (The centered two-sided test $\mathcal{M}_k^{\mathrm{sym}}$ and cell-residual test $\mathcal{M}_k^{\mathrm{decsym}}$) — **PROVED**

To genuinely **beat** both row 1624's one-sided test (15) and row 1626's spectral ceiling $V - 2\operatorname{Tr}(B_+)$, choose the **midpoint** potential $f(x) = u_s(x) = C_s(x, x)$ (subtracting the one-end within-cell variance!), and optionally peel off the coarse cell-averaged signed measure $\mu_k^{\mathrm{cell}}$ before taking Jordan parts.

**Definition (within-cell transition covariance and cell-observed distance).** For any integer $s \ge 1$, define
$$C_s(x, y) := p_{2s}(x, y) - \sum_{C \in \mathcal{C},\, \pi(C)>0} \frac{P^s(x, C) P^s(y, C)}{\pi(C)} = \langle e_x, (P^{2s} - P^s E P^s) e_y \rangle = \sum_{C \in \mathcal{C}} \pi(C)\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi(\cdot \mid C)}\bigl(p_s(x, \cdot),\, p_s(y, \cdot)\bigr). \tag{2.10}$$
For any two cells $C, C' \in \mathcal{C}$ with $\pi(C), \pi(C') > 0$, set $M_{2s}(C, C') := \frac{q_{2s}(C \times C')}{\pi(C)\pi(C')}$ and
$$\widetilde{L}_s(C, C') := M_{2s}(C, C) + M_{2s}(C', C') - 2 M_{2s}(C, C') = \left\|P^s\left(\frac{\mathbf{1}_C}{\pi(C)} - \frac{\mathbf{1}_{C'}}{\pi(C')}\right)\right\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2 \ge 0. \tag{2.11}$$
Given real $t \ge h + 2$ ($h = 2k + 2$, $j = \lfloor (t-h)/2 \rfloor$), set:
- $s := j$ and $R_t := 0$ if $t$ is an even integer (so $t - h = 2 j$ and $D_{*,k} = D_j$);
- $s := j + 1$ and $R_t := D_j - D_{j+1} \ge 0$ otherwise (so $0 \le D_{*,k} - D_s \le R_t$).

Put $V_s := \int L_s\,d\mu_k = -2\operatorname{Tr}(E P^{k+s} S P^{k+s} E)$. Define the **Centered Two-Sided Test**:
