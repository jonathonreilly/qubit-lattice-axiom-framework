## 1. What an incident cell must pay

**ON FILE (rows 1118, 1121, 1123).** Let the ring marginal on a periodic torus, after the cell interiors have been integrated out, have density proportional to $\prod_c Z_c(\omega_{S_c})$ against product reference measure on disjoint ring blocks. Set $A_c=Z_c(\omega)Z_c(\eta)$ for two independent rings, $1-p_c=\min_{T\subset S_c} A_c(\omega^T,\eta^T)/A_c(\omega,\eta)$, where $T$ exchanges the indicated blocks. All factors are positive. For $N_z=\{c:z\in S_c\}$, row 1118 (4.1) allows $|N_z|\le81$ in four dimensions. Either (4.4) or (4.5) implies $\mathbb E p_c\le\varepsilon<1/1250$ for **each** cell. The first bound follows from the singleton bad event, the second from the singleton path. Proposition J already refutes the original ownership at the window. Lemma JI identifies the **expected signed sum** of log costs with twice the sum of forward and reverse information. Check 1123, lines 130–143, gives a counterexample to inferring a marking probability from this mean.

**PROVED (a bounded test for any ownership).** Write $P=\rho\otimes\rho$ for the ring-pair law and $Q_z=(S_z)_*P$ for its image under exchange of just block $z$. Use TV in the convention $\sup_E|P(E)-Q(E)|$. For every $z$,
$$
 \|P-Q_z\|_{\rm TV}\ \le\ 1-\mathbb E_P\prod_{c\in N_z}(1-p_c)
 \ \le\ \sum_{c\in N_z}\mathbb E_P p_c.                                      \tag{1}
$$
The exchange is a product-reference-preserving involution. Its likelihood ratio is $dQ_z/dP=\prod_{c\in N_z}A_c(S_z(\omega,\eta))/A_c(\omega,\eta)$. Each ratio is at least $1-p_c$. The product of these lower bounds is at most one, hence at most $\min(1,dQ_z/dP)$. Integrate and use $\|P-Q_z\|_{\rm TV}=1-\mathbb E_P\min(1,dQ_z/dP)$. The second inequality is the union bound. Thus (1) includes all integrated-core factors and any allocation of ring-only energy preserving the exact product density.

**PROVED (one split plaquette).** Suppose all four distinct links of a simple elementary plaquette $p$ remain on the ring, and $z$ owns a nonempty proper subset. Let $m_p=\mathbb E_{\rm Wilson}\,\mathrm{tr}(U_p)/2$ in the ordinary periodic law. Under $P$ replica one's plaquette has its original Wilson distribution. Under $Q_z$ its holonomy is Haar. Indeed the two replicas have independent local gauge symmetries. At a vertex where the replica label changes along the four-cycle, insert an independent Haar vertex gauge in just one replica. It occurs exactly once in the hybrid holonomy, between fixed factors, possibly inverted. Integration gives the Haar law. This is the mixed-plaquette observation of rows 1121/1123 and 1131. The bounded conclusion here is
$$
 \sum_{c\in N_z}\mathbb E p_c\ \ge\ \|P-Q_z\|_{\rm TV}\ \ge\ |m_p|/2.             \tag{2}
$$
Use the first replica's normalized trace as test, whose oscillation is two and whose Haar mean is zero. The same argument with the indicator of *any* plaquette-holonomy event gives its full probability difference. No independence among plaquettes and no concentration of the log cost is needed.

**PROVED (the incidence budget already fails at $\beta\ge23/10$).** Use the ordinary periodic four-dimensional Wilson torus with every side at least four and an even time length. Write $V$ for its number of sites, $\bar m=(6V)^{-1}\sum_p m_p$, and $z(t)=\int_{SU(2)}e^{t\,\mathrm{tr}(g)/2}\,dg$. Then
$$
 \frac{\bar m}{2}\ \ge\ \frac{\log z(\beta)}{4\beta}\ >\ \frac{13}{200}\ >\ \frac{81}{1250}.        \tag{3}
$$
Here is the pressure argument of rows 863/879 with its normalization. Retain only the three temporal plaquette orientations. The normalized one-spatial-link convolution with kernel $e^{\beta\mathrm{tr}(xy^{-1})/2}/z(\beta)$ is symmetric and fixes constants. The product of the convolutions on a slice commutes with the gauge projection, by centrality. Integrating the time links implements that projection on each slice. If the time length is $t$, the electric-only partition function is therefore $z(\beta)^{3V}$ times the trace of the $t$-th power of this normalized gauge-projected product. This trace is at least one by the constant eigenvector and even $t$. In the electric-only law, flipping one spatial bond and all its time translates preserves every temporal plaquette and reverses the trace of any spatial plaquette incident to that bond. Thus the spatial action has zero mean in the trace convention. Jensen gives $Z_{\rm Wilson}\ge z(\beta)^{3V}$ for the weight $e^{\beta\sum_p\mathrm{tr}(U_p)/2}$. Convexity of $\log Z_{\rm Wilson}$, whose value at zero is zero, gives $6V\beta\bar m\ge\log Z_{\rm Wilson}$. The gauge projection accounts for the temporal dependence on a periodic torus. [Pressure bound and its check, row 879](gap-crux-skeleton-check-astra.md)

For the constants, $\log z(t)/t$ is nondecreasing by the same convexity. Under Haar measure the scalar quaternion coordinate $s$ has density $(2/\pi)\sqrt{1-s^2}$ on $[-1,1]$. Its even moments satisfy $M_0=1$, $M_{2j}/M_{2j-2}=(2j-1)/(2j+2)$ by integration by parts. At $t=23/10$ put $x=t^2/4=529/400$. The first four terms of $z(t)$ are $1+x/2+x^2/12+x^3/144$. The last three exceed $661/1000,145/1000,16/1000$, respectively, and the omitted terms are positive. Hence $z>911/500$ and $q=(z-1)/(z+1)>291/1000$. Integrating $2/(1-q^2)$ from zero gives $\log z\ge2(q+q^3/3)>2(291+8)/1000$, since $(29/100)^3>24/1000$. Division by $4t$ gives (3).

On a cubic torus every orientation has $m_p=\bar m$. On a rectangle choose an orientation attaining at least $\bar m$ (translation invariance within each orientation). If a ring plaquette of that orientation is split, (2)–(3) contradict even the singleton requirement $\mathbb E p_c<1/1250$ when $|N_z|\le81$. In particular this covers **every partition of the usual separating ring into raw-link blocks with incidence (4.1) such that no block contains an entire coordinate two-torus of the ring**. For each orientation, take the plane of that orientation in a cell-interface hyperplane. All its plaquettes lie on the ring. If none is split, neighboring plaquettes sharing an edge belong to the same block. The whole connected plane then belongs to one block. Local blocks of diameter smaller than the diameter of the plane satisfy the stated hypothesis. This argument selects the orientation before the block, so it does not assume rotation symmetry of the ownership or of a rectangular torus. It allows the block partition and the cell side to change with the cutoff.

**PROVED (a lower bound for the buffered core–outside coefficient, from the fixed-graph limit).** In the ordinary torus, or in one seam-free arc of the positive neutral twist mixture, take a four-cube of side $R$ in lattice units, centred up to rounding at time zero. Its central core is at depth $R/3$. Take a separating ring with the usual Wilson cell specification. For independent true ring values $\Omega,\Omega'$ write $m_\Omega$ for the core law conditioned on the ring. Put
$$
 D_{K,R}^{(1)}=\sup_{0\le F\le1}\mathbb E\left|m_\Omega(F)-m_{\Omega'}(F)\right| ,
$$
where the supremum is over gauge- and centre-invariant functions **supported in the core**. Let $D_{K,R}^{\rm TV}=\mathbb E\|m_\Omega-m_{\Omega'}\|_{\rm TV}$. For every fixed admitted choice of witnesses and physical side there is a positive constant $c_\phi$, specified below independently of those choices, such that
$$
 \liminf_{R\to\infty}\ \liminf_{K\to\infty}D_{K,R}^{\rm TV}
 \ \ge\ \liminf_{R\to\infty}\ \liminf_{K\to\infty}D_{K,R}^{(1)}
 \ \ge\ c_\phi>0.                                                        \tag{5}
$$
Use a single embedded cube, so that $R$ need not tile the torus. If a tiling is required, the same lower bound applies wherever that fixed cube size is admissible along an infinite subsequence. The assertion also refutes cutoff-uniform exponential clustering in **lattice units** with the support prefactor of row 1118 (4.6) on these finite tori.

*Proof.* Fix a smooth nonnegative nonzero $\phi$ compactly supported in $(-1/20,1/20)^3$. Use the spatial $12$ plaquettes at a time $s$, with coefficients $a_R(x)=\phi(x/R)$. Transport each plaquette to one spatial root along paths inside the supporting cube enlarged by one link, and sum the principal quaternion logarithms, scaled by $\sqrt{\beta_K}$, with these coefficients. Call the vector $Z_{K,R}(s)$. Its norm is gauge and centre invariant. The paths at $s=0$ lie inside the core for large $R$. At $s=R$ they lie outside the cell. To compare the norms, join the two roots by a time path. The common-root convention conjugates the second sum and preserves its norm. At fixed $R$ all the words now lie in one fixed finite connected graph inside a contractible box. In its tree gauge every loop is a fixed product of chords. The derivative of the log of that product at the identity is the signed sum of the chord logs. Tightness of the scaled chords bounds the remainder after scaling by $O_R(1/\sqrt{\beta_K})$ on each fixed bounded set of scaled chords. The on-file limit therefore applies to both norms jointly. The common graph may be translated wholly into one arc before refinement.

In one free colour let $V_R(s)$ be the covariance of the two linear readings at times $0,s$. For $h_i=|e^{ik_i}-1|^2$, $h=\sum_{i=1}^3 h_i$, $E(k)=2\operatorname{arsinh}(\sqrt h/2)$, and $\widehat a_R(k)=\sum_x a_R(x)e^{-ik\cdot x}$, it is
$$
 V_R(s)=\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^3}|\widehat a_R(k)|^2
          \frac{h_1+h_2}{\sqrt{h(h+4)}}e^{-|s|E(k)}\,\frac{d^3 k}{(2\pi)^3}.
$$
Indeed the $12,12$ curl entry is $(h_1+h_2)/(h+2-2\cos k_0)$. The time transform of the reciprocal denominator is the decaying solution of $(h+2)g_s-g_{s+1}-g_{s-1}=\mathbf1_{s=0}$, giving the displayed denominator and exponential. Values at $k=0$ do not affect the integral. In particular $V_R(0)>0$ for all sufficiently large $R$. With $\widehat\phi$ the ordinary Fourier transform, define
$$
 W(p)=|\widehat\phi(p)|^2\frac{p_1^2+p_2^2}{2|p|},\qquad
 r_\phi=\frac{\int_{\mathbb R^3} W(p)e^{-|p|}\,d^3p}
              {\int_{\mathbb R^3} W(p)\,d^3p}.
$$
Then $V_R(R)/V_R(0)\to r_\phi\in(0,1)$. To check the limit set $p=Rk$. The normalized sums $R^{-3}\widehat a_R(p/R)$ converge to $\widehat\phi(p)$ by Riemann sums. They are bounded by $C_j(1+|p|)^{-j}$ on the rescaled zone for each integer $j\ge1$. Taking $j$ lattice differences in the largest coordinate of $p$ gives the bound because each difference is at most $C_j R^{-j}$ and $|e^{ip_i/R}-1|\ge2|p_i|/(\pi R)$. The trivial sum bound handles $|p|\le1$. Also $R(h_1+h_2)/\sqrt{h(h+4)}\le|p|/2$ and tends to $(p_1^2+p_2^2)/(2|p|)$ for $p\ne0$, while $RE(p/R)\to|p|$. Dominated convergence after division of each covariance by $R^2$ gives the ratio. Positivity follows from $\widehat\phi(0)>0$.

Use $F=\exp[-|Z_{K,R}(0)|^2/(2V_R(0))]$ and the same $G$ at time $R$. After common-root transport and at fixed $R$, the vectors have three independent bivariate normal pairs in the limit with variance $V_R(0)$ and correlation $r_R=V_R(R)/V_R(0)$. A centred normal vector with covariance $\Sigma$ gives $\mathbb E e^{-|X|^2/2}=(\det(I+\Sigma))^{-1/2}$ by completing the Gaussian integral. Thus the bounded tests have limiting covariance $(4-r_R^2)^{-3/2}-1/8$. Condition on all links outside the cell, also conditioning on the twist label when present. As the cell lies away from every seam, its interior density uses only the ordinary Wilson factors incident to the cell and depends on the exterior only through the ring. This same conditional law survives averaging the label. It follows that $\operatorname{Cov}(F,G)=\mathbb E[(m_\Omega(F)-\mathbb EF)G]$. Since $0\le G\le1$, independence of $\Omega'$ and Jensen bound its absolute value by $D_{K,R}^{(1)}$. Since $0\le F\le1$, this coefficient is at most $D_{K,R}^{\rm TV}$. This proves (5) with $c_\phi=(4-r_\phi^2)^{-3/2}-1/8$. The time supports have separation $R$ and at most $C R^3$ spatial links each. Any fixed $C',m>0$ in a lattice bound $C'\min(|A|,|B|)e^{-m\,\mathrm{dist}(A,B)}$ would force the covariance to tend to zero in this order of limits. The same contradiction applies on any infinite cutoff subsequence on which those constants are common.

**ARGUED (interpretation of (5)).** For every sufficiently large *fixed* cell, its coefficient is bounded below at all sufficiently late cutoffs, with a cutoff threshold allowed to depend on the cell. **PROVED (failure of that converse for positive projected transfer kernels at the actual aspect).** Here is the count explicitly with neutral readings on the same spatial slice. Select $M=M(n)\to\infty$ edge-disjoint contractible plaquettes, for instance on a grid of spacing four. Under product Haar the signs $\xi_i$ of their normalized traces are independent fair signs. Haar holonomies on disjoint four-link sets are independent, and a central flip changes the sign of the trace. These signs are gauge and electric-centre invariant. Let $v=n/1024$, $r=M^{-1/4}$ and $t=r^{1/v}$ for $M>1$. Give one time step the kernel $\prod_{i=1}^M(1+t\xi_i(x)\xi_i(y))$. It is pointwise positive, and positive semidefinite by expansion in the orthonormal products of signs. Every nonzero mode is neutral. The $v$-step kernel has $r$ in place of $t$, since the product indexed by $I$ has eigenvalue $t^{|I|}$.

The squared endpoint law has Haar marginals. Its conditional operator first averages a test over the fibres of the signs and then sends a product indexed by $I$ to that product times $[2r/(1+r^2)]^{|I|}$, by integrating the normalized square of each factor. Thus for every $|F|\le1$, the variance of $q_x(F)$ is at most $[2r/(1+r^2)]^2$. Independent $x,x'$ give $\mathbb E|q_x(F)-q_{x'}(F)|\le\sqrt2\,2r/(1+r^2)\to0$, uniformly even over nonlocal bounded neutral $F$. Yet the purity is $[(1+r^4)/(1+r^2)^2]^M\to0$, since the bracket is $1-2r^2/(1+r^2)^2$ and $Mr^2\to\infty$. By (6) the full two-sample TV tends to one.

If smooth kernels are wanted, replace each sign by the mean-zero, Haar-$L^2$-normalized $\tanh(j\,\mathrm{tr}(U_p)/2)$. The products are still orthonormal, so the eigenvalues and purity are identical. At each fixed $n$ these functions converge almost everywhere to the sign, their supremum norms tend to one, and eventually $t\|\xi_{i,j}\|_\infty^2<1$. Dominated convergence gives variation convergence of the squared endpoint laws at this $n$. On two joint laws at TV distance $e$, every covariance of tests bounded by one differs by at most $6e$ by subtracting the two expectations and the two products of marginal expectations. TS3 therefore preserves the vanishing N1 bound on choosing $j=j(n)$ with $e\le1/n$. This example changes the action. It rules out a one-period inference based solely on positivity, neutrality and one-observable mixing. It gives no counterexample to Wilson (CONV-two) with a specified path tail.
