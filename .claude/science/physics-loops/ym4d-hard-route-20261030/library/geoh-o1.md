### The endpoint and the surviving records

Write \(\mathcal A_k(X)\) for the levels of earlier operation records on the surviving ancestry of \(X\).
Swallowed records remain in the historical record, but not as standing marks.
With all other class requirements fixed, the quarantine predicates are
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{open}}:\quad
   \mathcal A_k(X)\cap(h,k)=\varnothing,\qquad
 Q_{\mathrm{closed}}:\quad
   \mathcal A_k(X)\cap[h,k)=\varnothing.
 \tag{1}
\]
For CA, the excluded records are the surviving drop-outs and second-class outputs specified in I 45996–46001.
N requires the ancestral reading of its operations, as used by its tracing proof.
On these common surviving records, the closed predicate implies the open predicate.
The reverse fails at a record of level \(h\).
This is the known endpoint distinction, not the new obstruction.

A standing mark inside a selected component therefore obeys \(s\le h\) in CA and \(s<h\) in N.
Joining carries all surviving records into the new containing component.
It does not reset their ages.
N also rejects a joining within \((h,k]\), even without a new test failure.
CA's displayed class does not impose that separate single-trace requirement.
Thus equality of the full classes must not be inferred from (1).

The compulsory rule concerns admission to an attempt.
A failed first test can immediately stop an admitted attempt and leave a state-zero mark.
It cannot be represented as withholding the component from the class.
Positive normalized kernels alone would permit an identity move everywhere.
Such kernels do not supply compulsory admission.

Healing and island formation erase the older entries specified in the table.
The ported tracing argument already handles the resulting loss of old records.
It also distinguishes surviving ancestry from a healed location later covered again.
No new waiting or tracing argument is needed for these cases.

## 3. The first differing move: the prepared-core selector

**Result: the adopted thin selector is not the selector used by geoh-live-ab.**
The difference occurs before the first preparatory integration.
It occurs even on N's compulsory closed-endpoint class.

This comparison uses an adopted rule, not an arbitrary alternative invented here.
[ti-2.md](ti-2.md), lines 39–52, fixes rule (N) for each eligible component.
Its lines 69–85 list the readers; line 226 replaces their earlier face rule.
[r-inv.md](r-inv.md), lines 39–41 and 131, also uses this adopted selector.
[geoh.md](geoh.md), Input P, retains a selector as an input.
It does not identify that selector with CA's erosion formula.
[pda.md](pda.md), “The geometric data actually used”, constructs the CA preparation separately.
It does not identify that preparation with ti-2's rule.

Let \(C=G_h=X\cap Z_h\) be a nonempty quiet core.
Write \(C=\prod_{\alpha=1}^4[c^-_\alpha,c^+_\alpha]\) in an admissible local lift.
Use the same core, grids and input label in both moves.
The two selectors are as follows.

**Local adopted selector.** Put \(D_h^{\rm thin}=C\).
At each level \(i>h\), choose the smallest box on the face grid
\(F_i=u_i(\mathbb Z+\tfrac12)\) containing
\(D_{i-1}^{\rm thin}+[-u_i,u_i]^4\).
The two outward face increments lie in \([u_i,2u_i)\).
This is ti-2 (N), with its \(m_i\) renamed \(u_i\).

**Selector used by the live-strip proof.** At \(h<i\le k_0\), use
\[
 D_i^{\rm CA}=H_i^{[3a_i]},\qquad
 H_i=\operatorname{hull}_{a_i}(G_{i-1}).
 \tag{2}
\]
This is Sussman–Alpoge's prepared-box formula, N 63773–63825.
It follows there from five boxing-cell layers removed from the eight-layer T buffer.
I 53776–53785 specifies the same lower preparation, with its grid at I 63703–63716.
We port the formula and its grid justification.
Since \(N_k>N_0\), the first prepared index \(i=h+1\) belongs to this lower range.

Fix a coordinate face \(c^+_\alpha\) of \(C\), and put \(u=u_{h+1}\).
The first selector gives
\[
 (d^{\rm thin}_{h+1})^+_\alpha<c^+_\alpha+2u.
 \tag{3}
\]
The hull in (2) contains \(C\). The other selector therefore gives
\[
 (d^{\rm CA}_{h+1})^+_\alpha
 \ge c^+_\alpha+3R_{h+1}u.
 \tag{4}
\]
Take the point with coordinate \(c^+_\alpha+2u\) and the other coordinates at the centre of \(C\).
It lies outside \(D_{h+1}^{\rm thin}\) and strictly inside \(D_{h+1}^{\rm CA}\).
Indeed \(R_{h+1}\ge1\) makes \(3R_{h+1}u>2u\).
Both boxes lie in \(X\), so this is an interior discrepancy.
It does not depend on a boundary ownership convention.

If the first stage completes, the replacement rule gives
\[
 \Omega_{h+1}^{\rm thin}\cap X=X\setminus D_{h+1}^{\rm thin},
 \qquad
 \Omega_{h+1}^{\rm CA}\cap X=X\setminus D_{h+1}^{\rm CA}.
 \tag{5}
\]
The displayed point belongs to the first domain and not the second.
The prepared determining labels differ.
An interruption after that stage retains this difference.
I 53806–53814 supplies the stage replacement; SA 17415–17440 supplies its retention on a drop-out.

Thus the two deterministic P moves are unequal on every such nonempty quiet core.
Changing the background representative or proving measurability cannot change (3)–(5).
The latter are formulas for sets.
Adopting the compulsory closed class does not repair them.

## 4. Consequence for the live-strip bound

The first failed input is [geoh-live-ab.md](geoh-live-ab.md), equation (6):
\[
 N_3H_{h+1}\subset D_{h+1}.
 \tag{6}
\]
Its \(N_3\) means three boxing-cell layers.
Equations (3)–(4) show that the thin selector violates this inclusion.
The proof uses (6) for (8), the treated P-collar bound, and its first-stage bond estimate.
P-gap supplies only one native cube layer. It cannot replace (6).

The failure can also reach the stated P-collar bound with a closed endpoint.
Here is an explicit geometric record.
It uses the single-creation quiet-box prefix in [geo-3.md](geo-3.md), section 3.
That prefix implements the source free evolution; it is not a claim about positive Wilson weight.

Choose odd \(L\ge27\), a constant \(R=L^p\) with \(p\ge2\), and \(N_t=R\).
Increase \(p\), if needed, for the named scale floors.
Use a torus large enough for all displayed boxes.
Let \(E_i\) be the centred boxing cell of side \(a_i=Ru_i\).
At the first allowed operation, \(m=R+1\), take the island output
\[
 Y=E_m^{[10a_m]}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Its side is \(21a_m\), below the cutoff \(100a_m\).
Give the following steps no failures, joining, or other components.
Since \(21<L\), the next boxing-cell hull of each displayed box is its centred cell.
The free-evolution rule therefore gives
\[
 G_i=E_i^{[10a_i]},\qquad O_i=E_i^{[8a_i]}
 \quad(m<i\le k).
 \tag{8}
\]
The island mark prevents intervening attempts through \(m+R\).
At \(k=m+R+1\), the lower endpoint is \(h=m+1\).
The mark at \(m<h\) is allowed.
All traces in the new window are single boxes, small and quiet.
N's compulsory rule selects this component.
The old island is still live at its first P stage.

Now \(C=G_h\) has half-side \(21Ru_h/2\).
Its faces lie on \(F_{h+1}\), because \(21R/L\) is odd.
The thin rule therefore adds exactly \(u_{h+1}\) to each face:
\[
 D_{h+1}^{\rm thin}
 =
 \left[-\left(\frac{21R}{2L}+1\right)u_{h+1},
        \left(\frac{21R}{2L}+1\right)u_{h+1}\right]^4.
 \tag{9}
\]
The birth strip of (7) has half-side
\[
 \left(\frac{21R}{2}+\frac{R-1}{2}\right)u_m
 =\left(11R-\frac12\right)u_m.
 \tag{10}
\]
This uses Sussman–Alpoge's fixed strip definition, I 63524–63535.
The strip lies inside (9), since \(21L>22\) makes the difference of their half-sides positive.
The first collar is \(R_0=(G_{h+1}\setminus D_{h+1}^{\rm thin})\cap X\),
by I 53806–53814.
Its nearest face is the face of (9).
Consequently, in units \(u_{h+1}=L^2u_m\),
\[
 \operatorname{dist}_{h+1}(Y^\sharp,R_0)
 =\frac{21R}{2L}+1-\frac{11R-\tfrac12}{L^2}
 <\frac{21R}{2L}+1
 \le\frac{8R}{9}
 <R.
 \tag{11}
\]
The penultimate inequality uses \(L\ge27\) and \(R\ge2\).
The bound in geoh-live-ab, section 5, assertion 7, requires at least \(5R/2\).
Thus the stated geometric constant does not transfer to the thin selector.
Every number in (7)–(11) follows by the displayed hand arithmetic.
No enumeration or numerical run is used.

This is a counterexample to the geometric implication with the substituted selector.
It is not a construction of a positive-mass analytic history.
The deterministic mismatch (3)–(5) already suffices to reject the proposed identification.
The stronger claim that every actual analytic branch violates a collar bound is not made.
Nor does (11) show that a P integration bond meets the strip; its distance remains positive.

## 5. Disposition

The dictionary does not close as an equality of the specified operations.
The obstruction is a fixed selector, before any estimate of analytic weights.
It is additional to the endpoint distinction already recorded in prior/geoh.
It is not a new attack on waiting, tracing, lower-grid existence, or FM-MATCH.

The conclusion of geoh-live-ab remains conditional on its printed preparation.
This note does not refute that conditional theorem.
It blocks its application to the operation carrying ti-2's selector.
Closing the lower endpoint does not remove the obstruction: section 4 already uses that endpoint.

Using the CA/N nest would change the actual P move, its collar variables, and its retained labels.
Such a change requires matching its readers and interrupted branches.
Keeping the thin nest instead requires different geometric bounds; (11) excludes the stated P constant.
Neither change is made here.
