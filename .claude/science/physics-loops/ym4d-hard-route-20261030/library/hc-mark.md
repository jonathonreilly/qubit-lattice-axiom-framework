## 4. A precise supplier for carrying the rim branch

Call the missing estimate **HCM-R: the weighted rim-renewal bound**.
The following formulation isolates the problem even with one birth and no mergers.
All other analytic and coordinate inputs are held fixed at their stated scopes.

Start with a paid creation at level \(h\).
Stop its quiet evolution at the first unpaid rim failure.
Fix the exterior data and all stored inner data.
Keep the actual stopped nest, frames, delta functions, normalizers and earlier factors.
The state \(s\) includes these data and the current level.
It also records the original birth and every debit from its reserve.
The exterior evaluation stays in the original creation domain throughout.

Continue only until the next funded event, healing, or the chosen terminal level.
A funded event means a new ordinary creation or a mark with its own valid certificate.
An inner island is a stopping boundary with its already supplied mass bound.
A merger is also a stopping boundary; its other branch is not charged to this birth.
Between these boundaries, allow any number of failed full-stage rim tests.
Do not erase their changes to the stored determining set.

Use nonnegative Borel kernels for these continuations in the common coordinates required by H-E.
Use that supply with zero asserted gain for the unpaid rim stops.
Do not assume the missing mark factor from IN1 (16).
They must dominate the complete integrands simultaneously over compatible evaluations.
This kernel and domain supply is an input here.
It is not inferred from a scalar normalization.
For each transition, retain its actual stored factors and normalized frame weight.
Extract already funded gains once only.
Include the reserve, node-decay and entropy weights of IN1 in the transition kernels.
In particular every recorded node retains its contribution to \(V^\circ\).
The envelope costs are included once, with the total coefficient bounded by the chosen \(c_1\) in (1).

Write \(T\) for the resulting positive kernel from one unpaid rim stop to the next.
Write \(a(s)\ge0\) for the weighted contribution that reaches a stopping boundary before another rim stop.
At a terminal state set \(a(s)=1\) and \(T=0\).
Time increases strictly under \(T\).
These definitions use the literal operation, not a newly chosen reference probability law.

More explicitly, let \(\mathcal A_-(s)\) be the literal continuation records ending at the next unpaid rim stop.
Let \(\mathcal A_{\rm out}(s)\) contain those ending at a stopping boundary first.
For either kind, write \(S_\rho(s,\zeta)\) for the outgoing full state.
Write \(\pi_\rho\) for its actual normalized frame factor.
Let \(H_\rho(s,\zeta)\,d\mu_\rho(\zeta)\) be the supplied common majorant for the new kernel factors.
Old factors remain in the state and are not multiplied a second time.
This definition requires the whole read set, not only the failed plaquette.

Let \(\Delta V_\rho\) be the IN1 volume of the nodes in this segment.
Let \(\Delta N_\rho\) be their total cell count.
Include the ending rim node, but exclude the starting node.
At a funded exit, stop before counting that event's new segment.
Terminal segments include their last live node.
These conventions count each continuing node once.
Define
\[
 \begin{split}
 w_\rho&=\exp\{(c+c_{\rm ent})\Delta V_\rho
                         +\kappa_1\Delta N_\rho\},\\
 (Tf)(s)&=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal A_-(s)}\pi_\rho
       \int w_\rho H_\rho(s,\zeta)
                    f(S_\rho(s,\zeta))\,d\mu_\rho(\zeta),\\
 a(s)&=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal A_{\rm out}(s)}\pi_\rho
       \int w_\rho H_\rho(s,\zeta)\,d\mu_\rho(\zeta).
 \end{split} \tag{3a}
\]
An exit uses boundary reward one after its separately supplied funded estimate.
The constants \(C_{\rm env},C_{\rm int},c_{\mathsf B}\) bound the costs in \(H_\rho\).
They are not additional weights in \(w_\rho\).
Their sum with the explicit volume price is at most the \(c_1\) of (1).
Neither \(H_\rho\) nor its normalized conditional law may be replaced by a different law.
If this common majorant has not been supplied, H-E is still an input.

Let \(E_h^{\rm pay}(s_h)\) be a designated witness share at the birth.
It excludes the share reserved for integration and every share assigned to another event.
H-C for ordinary births supplies \(E_h^{\rm pay}\ge c_GP_h^2\).
After reserving the first waiting cost and the leaf mass, its available balance is

\[
 B_h(s_h)=E_h^{\rm pay}(s_h)-q_h-2P_h\ge0. \tag{3}
\]

The last inequality follows at the final coupling floor.
This balance is counted only once, even if its field is read many times.

**HCM-R, supplier statement.** Uniformly in the cutoff, terminal level, admitted exterior data and stored inner data,
there is a nonnegative Borel function \(F\) on these actual continuation states such that

\[
 F\ge1,\qquad a+TF\le F,\qquad
 F(s_{\rm first\ rim})\le e^{B_h(s_h)}. \tag{4}
\]

The inequalities must hold for one common majorant before the exterior supremum is integrated.
All constants obey IN1's order of choice.
At a new funded event the account may restart from that event's separately owned share.
The statement already asks for the one-birth, no-merger subcase.
That subcase is not supplied by another registry row.

**Why (4) is sufficient for this segment.** At the last permitted state the remaining contribution is \(1\le F\).
Suppose the continuation after every later rim stop is at most \(F\).
Its contribution at the current state is at most \(a+TF\le F\).
Backward induction proves this for every finite horizon.
Nonnegative partial sums then give the same bound over all finite stopping paths.
The bound includes the terminal path that has not yet exited.
Thus it does not discard arbitrarily long surviving paths.

Multiplication by the birth's extracted factor gives

\[
 e^{-E_h^{\rm pay}}\,e^{q_h}\,
 F(s_{\rm first\ rim})\le e^{-2P_h}. \tag{5}
\]

This leaves the leaf mass used in IN1 (32).
The entropy price remains in the weighted kernels, so no uncounted rim code is free.
Ordinary funded segments can then be treated by the existing ledger.
General gluing still uses the separately supplied H-G, H-E and ownership inputs.
Equation (5) is a proved implication, not a proof of (4).
Defining \(F\) as the total future weighted mass only restates the missing upper bound.

### 6.1 Why contraction and the flow do not prove (7)

Here is a geometric schedule allowed by the contraction and waiting rules.
It is not asserted to be a field history.
In particular, no repeated rim indicators are assigned by a field in this construction.

Choose arbitrarily large \(\Theta\) such that \([\Theta/2,\Theta]\) lies in one rounded-radius plateau.
Such choices exist.
The plateau in \(\log\theta\) is \((L^{(j-1)/r},L^{j/r}]\).
Its width tends to infinity, so its midpoint eventually has distance more than \(\log2\) from each end.
Take that midpoint as \(\log\Theta\).

Let \(R\) be the constant rounded radius there.
Fix a drift \(\lambda\in[\underline\lambda,\overline B]\), and set

\[
 D=\left\lfloor\frac{\Theta}{2\lambda}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad\theta_{h+s}=x_{h+s}=\Theta-\lambda s
 \quad(0\le s\le D). \tag{9}
\]

This satisfies the frame and tube bounds.
It can be preceded by the same descending affine frame.
The horizon remains within the one plateau.
Choose the torus large enough for the required margins.
There is no upper bound on that torus in the stated hypotheses.

Start from one isolated creation cell and use ordinary free propagation.
Its hull stays nonempty and becomes a bounded-size box.
On this plateau, the source's side estimate is \(s_{j+1}\le s_j/L+22\) in current cell units.
Starting at one cell gives \(s_j\le22L/(L-1)+1\), by summing a geometric series.
Thus the boxes stay small.
This is the geometric estimate within Y 72324–72439; no new geometry theorem is needed.

Schedule attempts first at \(h+R\), then every \(R+1\) levels.
After an attempt at \(m\), the next bottom is \(m+1\).
Hence the earlier attempt lies strictly below the next quarantine window.
Between attempts there is one predecessor, no new ordinary creation, and no merger.
Each stretch has the permitted waiting length.
Mark each attempted node symbolically as an unpaid stop.
Nothing in geometric contraction forbids this schedule.

Every nonempty node contributes at least one cell.
Thus the \(D\) levels alone have charged volume

\[
 V^\circ\ge D\,[1+\log(\Theta/2)](MR)^4. \tag{10}
\]

For any fixed \(c_1>0\) and any fixed polynomial birth deposit \(C(\log\Theta)^{2p_0}\),

\[
 \frac{c_1D[1+\log(\Theta/2)](MR)^4}
      {C(\log\Theta)^{2p_0}}\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{11}
\]

Indeed \(D\ge\Theta/(4\lambda)\) for large \(\Theta\), and \(R\ge(\log(\Theta/2))^r\).
The ratio is bounded below by a constant times

\[
 \frac{\Theta}{(\log\Theta)^{2p_0-4r-1}}.
\]

This diverges: put \(u=\log\Theta\) and use \(e^u\ge u^n/n!\) with an integer \(n>2p_0-4r-1\).
The same schedule has order \(D/R\) marks.
Its sum of the allowances \(q_m\) is of order \(DR^5\), and also exceeds any such deposit.

This calculation does not refute HCM-R on actual histories.
It proves that the frame, shrinking and quarantine rules alone cannot establish (7).
One needs an additional statement tying repeated unpaid rim failures to energy or to a small conditional mass.
The missing ingredient is the realization and weight of those failures, not the count of quiet boxes.

### 7.1 The exact operation that can be carried

Let \(s\) contain every variable read by the prospective stage test.

Then

\[
 \nu_m^-=(1-\chi_m)\nu_m,\qquad
 \nu_m^+=\chi_m\nu_m,\qquad
 \nu_m^-+\nu_m^+=\nu_m. \tag{13}
\]

On the negative branch, store the determining list at the actual departure stage.
Keep all previously formed terms, their masks, and their reference factors.
Do not perform the unpassed stage or invent its later rows.

For any supplied later continuation kernel \(K_{m,n}\), and any nonnegative Borel \(f\),

\[
 \int f(s')\,(\nu_m^-K_{m,n})(ds')
 =
 \int (1-\chi_m(s))
       \left[\int f(s')K_{m,n}(s,ds')\right]\nu_m(ds). \tag{14}
\]

This follows directly from the definition of a kernel integral and nonnegative integration.
For integrable complex functions, decompose the real and imaginary parts into positive and negative parts.
It keeps the failure at its original date.

### 7.2 Conservation does not pay the history weight

Even grant a stronger premise than supplied here: every continuation kernel is a probability kernel.
That premise still does not prove (4).
It controls an unweighted mass, while IN1 weights every recorded node.

This can be checked without any field calculation.
Consider one state at each time.
Let the continuation probability be \(r\), and the exit probability be \(1-r\).
Every continuation incurs a fixed positive price \(d\).
After \(n\) possible renewals, including the path still alive at the cutoff, the weighted total is

\[
 W_n=(1-r)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(re^d)^j+(re^d)^n. \tag{15}
\]

For \(d=0\), the geometric identity gives \(W_n=1\).
For \(d>0\), choose \(r=(1+e^{-d})/2\).
Then \(0<r<1\) but \(re^d=(1+e^d)/2>1\).
The last term in (15) tends to infinity.
No finite initial balance bounds these weighted totals.

This is a counterexample to the inference from probability conservation to a weighted lifetime bound.
It is not a Yang–Mills history or a counterexample to the target.
Its role is to identify the missing quantitative estimate in the propagation argument.
With constant coefficients, summing only exiting paths requires \(re^d<1\).
If \(re^d\ge1\), their geometric series diverges too.
With variable states, (4) is the corresponding reserve inequality.

In the actual problem the price includes
\[
 c(1+\log x_t)(MR_t)^4|C_t|,
\]
as well as the chosen decay and entropy costs.
Contracting a run to one label does not remove this price from the unchanged norm (7) of IN1.
It must retain the factor for every erased node.
Dropping those factors would prove a bound in a different norm.
Similarly, summing signed functions before taking moduli would not bound the sum of their majorants.

The source example also prevents an automatic strict gain at the final split itself.
For its fixed exterior top datum, the four determining bonds force the failed rim plaquette.
Varying interior stage variables does not change that boundary value.
One would have to estimate the mass of reaching such a state, or spend an incoming reserve.
A scalar normalization of the later reference law supplies neither estimate.

**Failure of route three.** Exact storage and propagation give (13)–(14), but no exponential moment of the number of waits.
The step from conservation to that moment is false in (15).
For the actual kernels, the missing estimate is exactly HCM-R.
