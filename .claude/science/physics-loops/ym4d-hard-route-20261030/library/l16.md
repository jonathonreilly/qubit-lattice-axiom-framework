## Attack 1: common tree gauge, then row geodesics

This attack first removes the pure-gauge defect in LOC's logarithmic contraction. It uses a common coordinate tree on a supplied cube, with the same root for both endpoints.

**Local estimate.** On a unit-mesh cube of side \(n\), suppose every plaquette has defect at most \(\epsilon\). In coordinate-tree gauge, each remaining link is a product of at most \(3n\) conjugated plaquettes. To see this, move that link past the three other coordinate segments of its root path. Each move crosses one plaquette. Tree links are identity. Telescoping unitary products gives
\[
 \|U^g(b)-I\|\le3n\epsilon,
 \qquad \|\Log U^g(b)\|\le\tfrac{3\pi}{2}n\epsilon
 \tag{1}
\]
when the first bound is less than two. The logarithm estimate follows from
\(2\sin(t/2)\ge2t/\pi\), \(0\le t\le\pi\).

Suppose the two gauged endpoints have Hermitian link logarithms \(A^0,A^1\) of norm at most \(a\le1/8\). Put
\[
 A_s=(1-s)A^0+sA^1,\qquad W_s(b)=e^{iA_s(b)}.
\]
The four-exponential remainder on one plaquette is at most
\(e^{4a}-1-4a\le16a^2\). Indeed it is at most \(8a^2e^{4a}\), and \(e^{1/2}<2\). The linear signed sum of its four logarithms is affine in \(s\). Applying the remainder estimate at both endpoints and at \(s\) gives
\[
 \|\partial W_s-I\|\le\epsilon+32a^2,
 \qquad
 \|i^{-1}\dot W_s(b)W_s(b)^{-1}\|
       \le\|A^1(b)-A^0(b)\|\le2a.                 \tag{2}
\]
For the velocity, differentiate the exponential series to obtain
\(i^{-1}\dot e^{iA_s}e^{-iA_s}=\int_0^1\operatorname{Ad}(e^{itA_s})\dot A_s\,dt\).
Unitarity gives the bound. Thus the nonlinear curvature error is quadratic after a small gauge is supplied. This is better than an estimate merely proportional to link distance.

For a fixed cell-scale cube, a final coupling ceiling can make \(n^2\epsilon\) small, as LOC (L3) shows. This proves a local interpolation estimate. It does not prove its admission on mixed rows, a common protected gauge, or a global cover with compatible deformations. CP12's mesh-uniform gauge starts with a supplied initial gauge and enlarged carrier; it does not supply these missing identifications.

**An exact global tree-gauge failure.** Tree fixing alone does not put the non-tree torus holonomies in a small ball. On a periodic lattice, set all links to identity except those crossing the coordinate seams in directions 1 and 2. Put
\[
 U=\frac{I+i\sigma_1}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 V=\frac{I+i\sigma_2}{\sqrt2}.
\]
At endpoint zero use seam values \((U,U)\). At endpoint one use \((V,V^{-1})\). Other directions have identity links. Each endpoint is flat: its only potentially nontrivial plaquette is the commutator of its two seam values, and those commute. A spanning tree inside the cut-open fundamental cube has identity links at both endpoints. The endpoints also agree on any protected box away from the seams.

Identify \(SU(2)\) with the unit quaternions. For non-antipodal unit vectors \(q,r\), the shortest geodesic midpoint is \((q+r)/|q+r|\). This follows by parametrizing their great circle. The two interpolated seam midpoints are therefore
\[
 a=\sqrt{\tfrac23}I+\frac{i(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)}{\sqrt6},\qquad
 b=\sqrt{\tfrac23}I+\frac{i(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)}{\sqrt6}.
\]
Using \([\sigma_1,\sigma_2]=2i\sigma_3\),
\[
 ab-ba=\tfrac{2i}{3}\sigma_3,
 \qquad \|aba^{-1}b^{-1}-I\|=\tfrac23.           \tag{3}
\]
Thus simultaneous shortest row geodesics fail every plaquette ceiling below \(2/3\), despite flat endpoints and a fixed common maximal tree. This example tests that construction; it is not a counterexample to L16. Its endpoints can instead be joined through identity seam holonomies, keeping the two seam values commuting on each half of the path. That repair stays flat and has uniformly bounded speed.

**Exact failure of this attack.** The local estimate (2) works. A global common small chart does not follow from separate cube gauges or from a maximal tree. The torus example exposes the missing holonomy treatment even before mixed seam tests enter. Interpolating cube gauges independently would additionally require the transition deformation already missing in LOC.

## Attack 2: interpolate averaged data and take critical backgrounds

On a supplied admitted raw chart this construction works. If
\(D^1=e^{iB}D^0\), \(B|_P=0\), and the whole real segment belongs to the chart's admitted class, then
\[
 D_s=e^{isB}D^0,
 \quad U_s=\Phi_{D^0}(sB),
 \quad v(D_s)=\|B\|_\infty.                     \tag{4}
\]
The average identity gives \(M_{\mathbf B}(U_s)=D_s\). Reality and CV come from the chart; consistent chart changes come from ST1 Lemma 5. This proves the local path statement under its stated membership premise.

The ball in SA 9693 is a ball of **coordinates about one reference datum**. It is not the whole set of regular data. The flat seam data above already carry arbitrary non-small holonomies. Taking the critical background after interpolating those data cannot repair a failed datum test, because its average is exactly the chosen datum. At depth zero the background map is the identity: every fine link is prescribed and there is no constrained variation. This is also the base case in `a1-balaban.md`, §8. Thus minimization cannot turn that example's midpoint into a different, admissible datum.

Replacing one segment by a chain of charts does not remove the quantitative problem. If the increments are \(B_1,\ldots,B_N\), each with an admitted real segment, the resulting path can have speed at most
\(\sum_j\|B_j\|_\infty\): give a nonconstant segment time proportional to its norm. There is no supplied uniform bound on this sum, nor a proof that such a chain connects arbitrary relative fibres. Compactness of the ambient product group does not bound intrinsic path length in an open constrained subset.

**Exact failure of this attack.** Critical backgrounds lift an already admitted datum path. Their existence and local uniqueness do not make the global datum space a ball. The missing step is global connectivity with controlled length, not differentiation of the minimizer.

## Attack 3: a bundle obstruction, constructed on the lattice

The old instanton warning in LOC does not supply a lattice invariant. Here is a direct construction on a complete uniform lattice. Its application to the actual stage class is kept separate.

**Lemma 1 (a bundle preserved by small-plaquette paths).** Let a four-torus have a periodic cubical lattice, with at least three sites in each direction. If
\[
             \max_p\|\partial U(p)-I\|<1/16,            \tag{5}
\]
the links define a continuous quaternionic line bundle \(\mathscr E(U)\) over the torus. Real lattice gauge transformations preserve its isomorphism class. Every continuous path satisfying (5) preserves that class.

*Proof.* Triangulate each unit cube by coordinate order. A simplex has vertices forming a monotone chain, with at most five vertices. Between comparable vertices use the increasing-coordinate edge path. Store its parallel transport \(T_{vw}\) as a map from the fibre at \(w\) to that at \(v\), and set \(T_{wv}=T_{vw}^{*}\). The path uses only coordinates in which the vertices differ, so it agrees on shared faces and across the periodic triangulation.

For three ordered vertices, the concatenation of the two short paths differs from the third path by at most four adjacent exchanges. There are at most four coordinate steps in all; the two disjoint subsets have sizes whose product is at most four. Each exchange inserts one transported plaquette. Reversing or cyclically permuting the triangle only conjugates or inverts its product. Hence, for any three vertices of one simplex,
\[
        \|T_{vw}T_{wa}-T_{va}\|\le4\epsilon,
 \qquad \epsilon=\max_p\|\partial U(p)-I\|.             \tag{6}
\]

Let \(\lambda_v(x)\) be the global piecewise affine vertex functions of this triangulation. They are nonnegative, sum to one, and have simultaneous positive support in one simplex. On the fixed space \(\bigoplus_v\mathbb C^2\), form the Hermitian matrix
\[
 Z_U(x)_{vw}=\sqrt{\lambda_v(x)\lambda_w(x)}\,T_{vw}.    \tag{7}
\]
An entry is zero when either factor vanishes; transports for pairs outside one simplex are immaterial. Thus (7) is continuous, including at faces.

Fix a vertex \(a\) of the active simplex. The column with blocks
\(W_v=\sqrt{\lambda_v}T_{va}\) satisfies \(W^*W=I_2\). Therefore \(Z_a=WW^*\) is a rank-two projection. Equation (6) implies
\[
                         \|Z_U-Z_a\|\le4\epsilon.       \tag{8}
\]
Indeed the absolute quadratic form of the difference is bounded by
\(4\epsilon(\sum_v\sqrt{\lambda_v}\,|z_v|)^2\le4\epsilon\sum_v|z_v|^2\).

The spectrum of \(Z_U\) lies within \(4\epsilon<1/4\) of \(\{0,1\}\). One can check this by the Neumann inverse of \(z-Z_a\) away from those neighbourhoods. The spectral projection
\[
 \Pi_U(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|z-1|=1/2}(z-Z_U(x))^{-1}\,dz          \tag{9}
\]
is continuous and has rank two. Rank follows by the straight interpolation from \(Z_a\) to \(Z_U\), along which the same spectral gap holds. Each \(SU(2)\) block commutes with the usual quaternionic anti-linear structure on \(\mathbb C^2\). Hence so do (7) and its real spectral projection. Their ranges form the asserted quaternionic line bundle.

A gauge change conjugates (7) by the single block diagonal unitary with blocks \(g(v)\). It therefore gives a bundle isomorphism. For a path \(U_s\), (9) is continuous in \((x,s)\). Compactness permits a finite subdivision of \([0,1]\) on which successive projections differ uniformly by less than one. The restriction of \(\Pi_{s'}\) to \(\operatorname{ran}\Pi_s\) is injective: a vector in its kernel would satisfy
\(\|v\|\le\|\Pi_{s'}-\Pi_s\|\|v\|<\|v\|\).
Equal finite ranks give a continuous fibrewise isomorphism. Composing these maps proves path invariance. \(\square\)

**Lemma 2 (sampling preserves the smooth bundle).** Let \(\mathscr E\) be a smooth quaternionic line bundle with an \(SU(2)\) connection on the torus. Sample its parallel transports on a lattice of mesh \(\eta\), using arbitrary unitary quaternionic frames at the vertices. Write \(f=\max_{\mu,\nu,x}\|F_{\mu\nu}(x)\|\). If \(\eta^2 f\) is sufficiently small, the sampled field \(W\) satisfies (5), and
\[
                         \mathscr E(W)\simeq\mathscr E.            \tag{10}
\]
The smallness constant is independent of the number of lattice sites.

*Proof.* On a cube, choose radial gauge about a point \(x\). Parallel transport along every ray from \(x\) is identity in this gauge. The connection obeys
\[
 A_\mu(y)=\int_0^1 t(y-x)^\nu F_{\nu\mu}(x+t(y-x))\,dt,           \tag{11}
\]
where repeated \(\nu\) is summed and curvature is expressed in this radial frame. For completeness, radial gauge gives \((y-x)^\nu A_\nu(y)=0\). Differentiate that equation in \(y_\mu\), insert the definition of \(F\), and obtain
\(d[tA_\mu(x+t(y-x))]/dt=t(y-x)^\nu F_{\nu\mu}\).
Integration gives (11). The radial trivialization itself is supplied by the linear transport equation along each ray.

On a mesh cube, (11) gives \(\|A_\mu\|\le2\eta f\). Along a coordinate path of at most four edges, integration of the unitary transport equation bounds its departure from identity by \(8\eta^2f\). The same estimate applies to a plaquette, so
\[
                      \max_p\|\partial W(p)-I\|\le8\eta^2f.       \tag{12}
\]

Embed \(\mathscr E_x\) in \(\bigoplus_v\mathbb C^2\) by the blocks
\(\sqrt{\lambda_v(x)}\,\mathsf T_{v\leftarrow x}\), where \(\mathsf T\) is transport along the straight segment within the simplex. This is an isometry, since \(\sum_v\lambda_v=1\). It is continuous across faces: the surviving straight segments agree and the other blocks vanish. Denote its range projection by \(Z_{\mathscr E}(x)\).

Use radial gauge about this \(x\). The straight transports are identity in the radial frames. Each coordinate transport in (7) differs from their two-segment transport by at most \(8\eta^2f\). The quadratic-form estimate used in (8) therefore gives
\(\|Z_W-Z_{\mathscr E}\|\le8\eta^2f\).
Also \(\|\Pi_W-Z_W\|\le4\epsilon\le32\eta^2f\), by the spectral localization above. Thus
\[
                 \|\Pi_W-Z_{\mathscr E}\|\le40\eta^2f.            \tag{13}
\]
Choose \(\eta^2f<1/256\). Then (12) is below \(1/16\), and (13) is below one. Projection from one range to the other is an isomorphism by the injectivity argument in Lemma 1. This proves (10). \(\square\)

**Lemma 3 (a nontrivial bundle with arbitrarily small remote curvature).** In a nonwrapping ball of radius \(R\) in a four-torus, there is a smooth \(SU(2)\) bundle and connection, trivial with zero connection outside that ball, for which
\[
 \max\|F_{\mu\nu}\|\le C/R^2,
 \qquad -\frac1{8\pi^2}\int\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)=1.         \tag{14}
\]
In particular the bundle is not isomorphic to the trivial bundle.

*Proof.* Centre the ball at zero. On \(\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}\), put
\[
 q(x)=\frac{x_4 I+i(x_1\sigma_1+x_2\sigma_2+x_3\sigma_3)}{|x|},
 \qquad \omega=q^{-1}dq.
\]
Use an inner chart \(|x|<R\) and an outer chart \(|x|>3R/4\), with transition \(q\). Choose a fixed smooth function \(\chi\) equal to zero below \(1/4\), equal to one above \(1/2\), and taking values in \([0,1]\). On the inner chart set \(A=\chi(|x|/R)\omega\); on the outer chart set \(A=0\). On the overlap the inner connection is \(q^{-1}dq\), exactly the transform of the outer connection. It is smooth at the centre because it vanishes near it.

Write \(u(r)=\chi(r/R)\). The identity \(d\omega=-\omega\wedge\omega\) follows by differentiating \(q^{-1}q=I\). Therefore
\[
 F=u'\,dr\wedge\omega+(u^2-u)\omega\wedge\omega.                  \tag{15}
\]
Its support is in \(R/4\le r\le R/2\). There \(\omega=O(R^{-1})\) and \(u'=O(R^{-1})\), proving the bound with one fixed finite \(C\).

Orient the ball so that \(q\) preserves the orientation of the unit three-sphere. To compute the angular integral, write
\(\omega=i\sum_a\sigma_a\vartheta_a\).
The forms \(\vartheta_a\) are an orthonormal left-invariant coframe of the unit three-sphere: quaternionic left multiplication is an orthogonal map, and at the identity these are its three Euclidean tangent coordinates. The Pauli identity gives
\(\operatorname{tr}(\omega^3)=12\vartheta_1\wedge\vartheta_2\wedge\vartheta_3\).
The sphere volume is \(2\pi^2\): its polar-coordinate integral is
\(\int_0^\pi\sin^2t\,dt\int_0^\pi\sin u\,du\int_0^{2\pi}dv=2\pi^2\).
Consequently \(\int_{S^3}\operatorname{tr}(\omega^3)=24\pi^2\). Equation (15) now gives
\[
 \int\operatorname{tr}(F\wedge F)
 =24\pi^2\,2\int_0^1(u^2-u)\,du=-8\pi^2.                         \tag{16}
\]

Here no integrality theorem is needed. The displayed integral distinguishes the bundles directly. For two connections on one smooth bundle, their difference \(a\) is a global endomorphism-valued one-form. Along their affine interpolation, direct differentiation and the Bianchi identity give
\[
 \frac d{dt}\operatorname{tr}(F_t\wedge F_t)
                   =2d\operatorname{tr}(a\wedge F_t).            \tag{17}
\]
The Bianchi identity follows by expanding \(dF+[A,F]\); its terms cancel. Cyclicity of trace then proves (17). Stokes' theorem on the closed torus makes the integral independent of the connection. On the trivial bundle it is zero, by comparison with the zero connection. A bundle isomorphism preserves it.

This also excludes a continuous bundle isomorphism. Such an isomorphism between smooth bundles can be smoothed: on smaller members of a finite trivializing cover, convolve its continuous matrix coefficients with smooth kernels of sufficiently small support. Uniform continuity gives uniform approximation. Multiply the local approximations by a subordinate smooth partition of unity and sum them as sections of the homomorphism bundle. On the compact base, a sufficiently small uniform error preserves invertibility. This produces a smooth isomorphism, which would contradict (16)–(17). \(\square\)

Lemmas 1–3 already give a hand counterexample to connectivity of the full small-plaquette class on a uniform four-torus. The following two steps check the actual background and stage issues; that uniform-lattice example alone would not settle L16.

## Passing the remote field to the actual critical background

**Lemma 4 (small critical correction preserves the bundle).** Fix one finite-depth separated determining system with \(\Omega_0\) the whole torus. Retain the actual-row, local-gauge, inverse, nonlinear-force and uniqueness inputs in S. Suppose a family of real reference fields \(W_R\) has, uniformly in the increasing torus volume:

* normalized curvature and current tending to zero;
* witnessing small gauges, with first differences tending to zero, on every fixed local operator carrier;
* the actual means defined, with \(D_R=M_{\mathbf B}(W_R)\) admitted.

If \(W_R\) is sampled from Lemma 3, then for all sufficiently large \(R\),
\[
             \mathscr E(\Phi(D_R))\simeq\mathscr E(W_R).           \tag{18}
\]
This uses the same conditional analytic inputs as L16. It adds no physical premise and assumes no path between distinct determining data.

*Proof.* We spell out the small correction, rather than infer (18) from uniqueness alone. The actual-row critical equation is the equation in `a1-balaban.md`, §4, (4.1)–(4.5), with its actual-row transport in §§7.6–8. Its reference need only be regular; it need not already be critical. The sampled reference has exactly the desired datum, so its reduced mismatch is \(B=0\).

Let \(J_R\) be its physical Wilson current. Let \(\mathfrak G_{1,R}\) be the constrained inverse, and let \(V_R\) be the nonlinear remainder after straightening. The supplied estimates, in the weighted value/first-difference norm \(X\) and force norm \(F\), are
\[
 \|\mathfrak G_{1,R}\|_{F\to X}\le B_0,
 \quad \|V'_R(a)\|_F\le C_4\|a\|_X^2,
 \quad \|V'_R(a)-V'_R(b)\|_F\le4C_4r\|a-b\|_X                 \tag{19}
\]
on a sufficiently small radius-\(r\) ball. Constants do not grow with volume. Precisely, the inverse estimate is A1 P99.H, (3.5), with the local perturbation in §3.9 and the actual rows in §7.8; the force estimates are A1 (4.4) and the contraction estimate following (4.5). The nonzero-current correction in \(\mathfrak G_{1,R}\) is retained. This does not apply the critical-centre derivative theorem at a noncritical field. All the required local-gauge and carrier hypotheses remain those in S and are verified below for this family.

Put \(j_R=B_0\|J_R\|_F\). On \(\|a\|_X\le2j_R\), solve
\[
             a=-\mathfrak G_{1,R}J_R
                    -\mathfrak G_{1,R}V'_R(a).                    \tag{20}
\]
For \(8B_0C_4j_R\le1/2\), (19) maps this ball into itself and has Lipschitz constant at most \(1/2\). Successive differences form a geometric series in the complete finite-dimensional normed space. Its limit is real and solves (20), with \(\|a\|_X\le2j_R\).

The straightening map is \(T(a)=a-H\mathcal D(a)\), with \(\mathcal D(a)=O(\|a\|_X^2)\); hence \(\|T(a)\|_X\le Cj_R\). The exact constraint identities and endpoint gauge factors turn this solution into a critical field with **raw** datum \(D_R\). In particular one must not identify zero straightened datum with zero raw datum before applying those factors. Equations (4.1) and (4.5) supply this conversion. Recovery of second derivatives in A1 §5 gives the regular critical class. For large \(R\), uniqueness therefore identifies this field with \(\Phi(D_R)\), up to a real gauge, including the change to CV.

For the bundle comparison no current estimate along an interpolation is needed. Set
\(W_{R,t}=e^{it\eta T(a)}W_R\).
Since the local scale is at least \(\eta\), its links differ from those of \(W_R\) by at most \(Cj_R\). Telescoping the four unitary links of a plaquette yields
\[
 \|\partial W_{R,t}-I\|
       \le\|\partial W_R-I\|+4Cj_R<1/16                           \tag{21}
\]
for large \(R\), uniformly in \(t\). Lemma 1 identifies the bundles at the two ends. Its gauge invariance then identifies the corrected field with the CV background. This auxiliary path need not satisfy L16's tests or protection: it is used only to compute the endpoint bundle. \(\square\)

**Verification for the sampled family.** The mesh and depth are fixed in Lemma 4; only the remote scale and volume increase. Lemma 3 and (12) give
\(\|\partial W_R-I\|\le8C\eta^2/R^2\).
The Wilson current formula in CP12 (8), with six incident plaquettes and \(\|\pi(P)\|\le\|P-I\|\), gives
\[
                   \|J_R\|_\infty\le48C\eta^{-1}/R^2.             \tag{22}
\]
No mesh-uniform limit is used in this estimate. All layer scales are fixed and at most one, so its weighted force norm tends to zero too. If the reference inverse uses the full matrix divergence, bound each of its finitely many transported plaquette terms by (12). Its normalized bound is also \(C\eta^{-1}R^{-2}\). Thus the stronger matrix-divergence clause is small as well; it is not inferred solely from the anti-Hermitian current.

On any required enlarged cube of physical side at most \(S_0\), radial gauge (11) gives continuum potential bounded by \(C S_0/R^2\). Transport along a fine link and the small logarithm then give a lattice potential bounded by \(C'S_0/R^2\). Its ordinary first difference is at most twice this bound divided by \(\eta\). Thus every required value/first-difference gauge bound tends to zero. The finitely many carrier sizes are fixed by the chosen depth and early geometry, independently of the torus volume. Cubes outside the curvature support can still be treated by the same local radial construction; no global trivialization is asserted.

The actual average is gauge covariant and analytic on these local small gauges. Its curvature recursion, AV/CURV, puts every datum test at \(O(R^{-2})\) for this fixed depth. Only finitely many scale and exceptional-index thresholds occur. Each is positive. Increasing \(R\) therefore puts all tests below their required (58) thresholds, with room; (56) is geometric and unchanged. S and ST1 Lemma 10 give the prescribed background admission and records. This uses those inputs with their original qualifications; it does not prove F0, A1 or roster realization anew.

## A closed-torus stage and the protected rows

It remains to prevent a putative path from losing charge through a missing first region or an enlarged path radius. Both can be handled within one allowed stage geometry.

Let \(b_{\rm path}<\infty\) be the fixed normalized curvature ceiling for the backgrounds in \(\mathcal A_{\rm path}\). Such a ceiling is part of the admitted real class: CP12 D3/Theorem 3 and S's regular uniqueness class. In particular every such background satisfies
\[
 \|\partial U(p)-I\|
              \le b_{\rm path}\eta^2/l(p)^2.                       \tag{23}
\]
Choose an integer \(h\ge2\) such that
\[
                   b_{\rm path}L^{-2(h-1)}<1/16.                   \tag{24}
\]
This does not shrink the path class or impose (58) along it.

Use the single-cell history from SA 17575–17583 and GEOH's R/P specifications. There is no creation before level \(h\), one cell is created at level \(h\), and its evolution is quiet, without mergers or operations, through an eligible operation level \(t\) with \(t-N_t=h\). One can choose a sufficiently long finite frame: fix its last entry first, set \(t=h+N_t\), and choose the preceding entries with the allowed finite increments. All size and waiting conditions are those of that single-cell class construction. The torus can be made arbitrarily large; its room conditions are lower bounds.

This does not suppress a required earlier operation. The running-minimum scale gives \(N_s\ge N_t\) for \(s<t\). Thus \(s-N_s<h\); any earlier class window after the creation contains that creation and fails quietness. Before level \(h\) there is no live component. At \(t\), the creation is the permitted lower endpoint of the quiet window. The single-box and size clauses are the single-cell construction just cited.

There are no first-step failures. Thus initially \(\Omega_1=\Omega_0=\mathbb T\), and
\[
                   \Omega_i=\mathbb T\quad(0\le i<h).             \tag{25}
\]
Preparation changes only the later members, as in GEOH Input P. Hence (25) remains true at the selected stage. The determining system still has \(\Omega_0=\mathbb T\), and every point has level at least \(h-1\). This is an allowed geometric record of the stage construction, not a replacement by a uniform determining system. L16 quantifies over admitted data at that geometry; no probability or positive-measure assertion about a historical output is required.

For any path at this fixed geometry, \(l(p)\ge L^{h-1}\eta\). Equations (23)–(24) therefore give (5) for **every** fine plaquette throughout the path. This uses the fixed admitted radius, even if the path is allowed much larger tests than its endpoints. It avoids inferring a path threshold from a coupling-dependent endpoint threshold.

## Result

Under the same named background and admission inputs S used to formulate L16, its universal protected-fibre path conclusion is false.
