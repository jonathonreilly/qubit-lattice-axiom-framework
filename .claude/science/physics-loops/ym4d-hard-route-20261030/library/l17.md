## 2. Attack A: continue the real estimate across a fixed fraction of its radius

The proposed route was to obtain a complex supremum from the real
estimate, then apply Cauchy on a smaller polydisc. A fixed constant loss
could be absorbed in an early enlargement of \(B_3\).

### A1. The analytic implication this would need is false

Fix \(r,H>0\) and \(0<\theta<1\). For each positive integer \(m\) put
\[
 f_m(z)=He^{-m}\sin(mz/r),\qquad \delta_m=He^{-m}.       \tag{4}
\]
These functions are entire, real on the real line, and vanish at zero.
For \(|z|<r\), the exponential formula for sine gives
\(|\sin(mz/r)|\le e^{m|\operatorname{Im}z|/r}\le e^m\).
Thus \(|f_m|\le H\) on the disc, while \(|f_m(x)|\le\delta_m\)
on its real diameter. But
\[
 \frac{|f_m(i\theta r)|}{\delta_m}=\sinh(m\theta)
   =\tfrac12(e^{m\theta}-e^{-m\theta})\longrightarrow\infty. \tag{5}
\]
No constant depending only on the fixed radius fraction works.
This one-dimensional example also applies to polydiscs by ignoring
their other coordinates. It refutes the proposed analytic inference,
not L17 or any property of its actual minimizer.

Cauchy's formula needs a supremum on a **complex** circle. The real
bound is not that supremum. The uniform analytic ball in `b109` P20
supplies \(H\), not \(C\epsilon_0s^2\). Even granting that a complex
ball of twist radius \(R\) stays in the admitted class with curvature
budget \(\epsilon_0\), its quadratic estimate gives
\(C(\epsilon_0+R^2)s^2\). Admission of that entire ball is an
additional requirement. To make this bound proportional to
\(\epsilon_0s^2\), one must take \(R^2\le C'\epsilon_0\).
Covering the requested twist then requires
\(\epsilon_1\le\theta R\), a new relation between the radii.

### A2. Why coefficient estimates do not repair the route

On a complex ball of radius \(r\), the crude bound gives homogeneous
Taylor coefficients at most \(Hr^{-k}\) along every unit complex
direction. Summing from degree two reproduces the quadratic remainder
already present in (3). The real bound does not instead give
\(C\epsilon_0r^{-k}\) uniformly in \(k\): (4)–(5) would contradict
the resulting convergent series on a fixed smaller disc.

One could use additional structure of the actual map, for example
factorization through curvature with a bounded holomorphic coefficient.
That is extra information, pursued in Attack C. Analyticity and the
real estimate alone do not furnish it. **Attack A fails at the complex
supremum, before any constant can be absorbed into \(B_3\).**

## 3. Attack B: a gauge-covariant interpolation to real data

This attack tries to transport the real theorem along complex gauge
orbits, rather than split a potential into real and imaginary parts.
There are two distinct issues: which orbits the path reaches, and the
norm of its motion transverse to an orbit.

### B1. The exact gauge evolution

For a differentiable oriented loop \(P=U_1\cdots U_k\), direct
product differentiation gives
\[
 \dot P P^{-1}
  =\sum_{i=1}^k\operatorname{Ad}(U_1\cdots U_{i-1})
                      (\dot U_iU_i^{-1}).                  \tag{6}
\]
If \(g(\cdot,0)=I\), \(U_b(t)=g(b_-,t)U_b(0)g(b_+,t)^{-1}\), and
\(\lambda=\dot g g^{-1}\), then
\(\dot U_bU_b^{-1}=\lambda(b_-)-\operatorname{Ad}(U_b)\lambda(b_+)\).
In (6) consecutive terms cancel. Thus
\[
 \dot P=[\lambda(x),P],\qquad
 P(t)-I=g(x,t)(P(0)-I)g(x,t)^{-1}.                         \tag{7}
\]
Curvature therefore keeps its small factor under a gauge path with
bounded condition numbers. The physical current has the same conjugation
law. The analytic tower uses the induced node action, with the source's
axial normalization; frozen `mu` identifies that action on common charts.

This is a useful equality. It cannot reach every field in (1) from a
real field.

### B2. A flat complex input outside every real gauge orbit

Let \(N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\), so \(N^2=0\), and
let the physical torus side be \(\ell=m\eta\). Take \(t>0\) small and
\[
 U_r=I,\quad a_1=-itN,\quad a_2=a_3=a_4=0,
 \qquad W_1=I+\eta tN,\quad W_2=W_3=W_4=I.             \tag{8}
\]
The potential is traceless and complex, as allowed. Its first differences
vanish. Every plaquette equals \(I\). The real gauge clause holds with
zero potential. Thus (1) holds for every positive \(\epsilon_0\), with
any fixed small \(\epsilon_1>t\); the independent current can be zero.

The first coordinate cycle has holonomy
\[
                        H=I+\ell tN.                    \tag{9}
\]
Indeed \((I+\eta tN)^m=I+m\eta tN\), by induction using \(N^2=0\).
A periodic gauge only conjugates \(H\). This nonidentity matrix has
both eigenvalues one and is not diagonalizable. It cannot be conjugate
to a unitary matrix: a unitary matrix is diagonalizable, and with both
eigenvalues one it equals the identity. The latter fact follows, for
example, by choosing a unit eigenvector and its invariant orthogonal
complement in dimension two.

Consequently no periodic complex gauge takes (8) to a real connection.
This also rules out an approximation by real connections with error
\(O(\epsilon_0)\) and a uniform bound on gauge condition numbers.
To see this, keep this finite torus and \(t\) fixed and send
\(\epsilon_0\) to zero. For determinant-one two-by-two gauges, bounded
condition number bounds both singular values and the inverse. Extract a
subsequence at each of the finitely many sites and bonds. The limit
would be an invertible gauge taking \(W\) to a unitary connection,
contradicting (9).

This is **not** a counterexample to L17. It is an obstruction to the
proposed real-orbit reduction. A flat branch can retain this nonunitary
holonomy and still have zero curvature and current. A repaired
interpolation must retain complex flat data, including their holonomies.

### B3. Retaining flat data still does not give a global uniform path bound

The next attempt was to keep those flat variables and solve only in
directions transverse to them. A uniform inverse of curl in the
unweighted potential norm would suffice for such a path. It already
fails in the additive abelian subproblem.

Take a periodic lattice of side \(R\), with \(R/(2\eta)\) an integer.
Let \(v(x_1)\) be the periodic piecewise-linear tent with values zero
at \(0,R\) and \(t\) at \(R/2\). Put
\(b_2(x)=v(x_1)H\), \(b_1=b_3=b_4=0\), where \(|H|=1\) is a
fixed abelian color. Then
\[
                         \|db\|_\infty=2t/R.              \tag{10}
\]
For any closed periodic abelian one-form \(c\), the mean of \(c_2\)
around an \(x_2\)-cycle is independent of \(x_1\). Sum
\(D_1c_2=D_2c_1\) around that cycle; the right side telescopes to zero.
Call the resulting common mean \(h\). Cycle averages of \(b_2-c_2\)
at \(x_1=0,R/2\) are \(-h\) and \(tH-h\), respectively.
Their difference has norm \(t\). The triangle inequality therefore gives
\[
          \|b-c\|_\infty\ge t/2=(R/4)\|db\|_\infty.      \tag{11}
\]
Allowing exact gauge gradients in \(c\) does not change this conclusion.
Thus even a flat reference with freely chosen periods cannot make this
global potential estimate uniform in volume. A uniformly bounded path
speed by \(C\|db\|_\infty\) would imply the same false bound by integration.
This test concerns that interpolation estimate, not the elliptic tower
response, which may suppress the loss in (11).

The corrected local estimate has a factor of the box diameter. Exponential
response could absorb it if the local deformations and their complete
reads were compatible. Attack C pursues that correction.

**Attack B fails at its proposed reduction:** pure gauge paths miss the
flat complex orbits (8), and a global curvature-controlled transverse
path in the value norm incurs the diameter loss (11). Neither failure
rules out a local, gauge-covariant interpolation with weighted response.

## 4. Attack C: estimate the curvature readout on a value chart

The first-difference obstruction (115)–(117) of `b109` need not be paid
to enter an identity-based **raw-datum** chart. Only values of the
gauged field are needed for its averages. The output's special second
derivatives come from the critical equation, not from differentiating
the gauged input. This gives the following limited positive result.

### C1. A curvature-linear estimate when all reads fit in one box

Fix \(R\ge1\). Suppose the entire datum, action and gauge reads of a
tower problem are supplied in one embedded coordinate cube \(Q\) of
physical side at most \(Rs\), and (1) holds throughout \(Q\).
Include every imposed fine boundary field among the transformed raw
data, so that zero raw datum is the flat identity problem, with no
unaccounted exterior field. Keep the actual rows and axial convention.
There are positive **individual** radius ceilings depending on \(R\)
and the earlier analytic constants, but not on the mesh, such that
\[
                 |\mathcal F_x(W)|\le C R\epsilon_0s^2   \tag{12}
\]
at its top-layer readouts. The same estimate holds for \(U_r\).
Here \(C\) uses the uniform raw-datum branch bound of `a1-balaban`
(9.1)–(9.2), in the actual-map form used by `b109` P20, and the
actual averaging bound of `mu` (4.3). Their supplier premises remain
explicit inputs. This is a finite-box implication, not the uniform
statement for arbitrary \(Z\).

**Proof.** Use the coordinate-tree gauge \(g(W)\), rooted at a corner
of \(Q\). The product argument in `b109` P21 applies on this supplied
cube. It gives
\[
 \sup_Q\|g\|\|g^{-1}\|\le e^{cRs\epsilon_1},\qquad
 W^g=e^{i\eta A},\qquad
 \|A\|_\infty\le cRs\epsilon_0 e^{cRs\epsilon_1}.        \tag{13}
\]
For clarity, the link strip has at most \(cRs/\eta\) plaquettes.
Each transported defect is at most
\(e^{cRs\epsilon_1}\epsilon_0\eta^2\). Their product differs from
one by at most the exponential of their summed defects minus one.
The convergent logarithm series then gives (13). No first difference
of \(A\) is estimated.

Every determining row has physical length at most \(s\). The actual
plain average bound, including the bare rows, gives
\[
 B(c)=i^{-1}\log M^{q(c)}(W^g)(c),\qquad
 \|B\|_\infty\le C_A s\|A\|_\infty
           \le cC_A R\epsilon_0s^2e^{cRs\epsilon_1}.       \tag{14}
\]
This uses the plain group average. No straightened row is substituted.
Choose the radius ceilings so that the right side stays below half the
fixed identity-based raw-datum radius \(r_B\). This is possible by
\(s\le1\), with ceilings on \(R\epsilon_1\) and \(R\epsilon_0\)
separately. There is no comparison between the two radii.

In top units the branch has, at \(B=0\), zero curvature and current.
Its uniform analytic estimate, from the critical equation in the
supplier, is
\[
 \eta_n^{-2}|\partial\Phi_1(B)-I|,
                \ |J_n(\Phi_1(B))|\le C_B\|B\|_\infty. \tag{15}
\]
This is precisely the part of the analytic theorem used in `b109`
(108). It is not Cauchy differentiation in the fine lattice variable.
Combining (14) and (15) proves (12) in the gauged chart, once the
exponential in (14) has been bounded by a fixed constant.

Here is the branch identification needed to undo that gauge. For fixed
\(R\), take a common, smaller complex ball in the original field
coordinate \(a\), using its value and first-difference norm. On this
ball the plaquette product expansion bounds the curvature by
\(C(\epsilon_0+\|a\|+\|a\|^2)\eta^2\). Formula (13), now with
this bound, and (14) place **every** point of the ball in the identity
datum chart after its own tree gauge. Choose its radius so that
\(CR(\epsilon_0+\|a\|+\|a\|^2)e^{cR\|a\|}<r_B/2\).
This again requires only individual ceilings for fixed \(R\).

Tree transport is a finite product of bonds and their inverses. It is
holomorphic there. Both the original continued tower and the gauged
identity-chart construction are holomorphic on this common ball. For
real \(a\), the tree gauge is unitary. Real orbit uniqueness and the
fixed axial convention identify the two representatives by the induced
node gauge. The real uniqueness ceilings are met by decreasing the
same ball once. The identity theorem, one real coordinate at a time,
gives that identity on the whole complex ball.

Under the prescribed node lift, each site uses its assigned determining
root value, or its unchanged fine-shell value. Thus its condition
number has the bound in (13); it is not a product of a fixed loss at
every depth. This is the axial covariance used in `mu` sections 5–6.
Curvature and current conjugate at their base sites. Undoing the gauge
therefore costs at most another factor \(e^{cRs\epsilon_1}\).
Fixing the individual ceiling so \(e^{2cR\epsilon_1}\le2\) and using
(14)–(15) proves (12), with \(C=2cC_AC_B\). Taking \(a=0\)
proves the real-factor claim. \(\square\)

This proof removes the **input first-derivative** obstruction on a
fixed box. It does not supply a regular gauge in the sense of `cp12`
(8). In particular `cp12`'s massive gauge lemma cannot be applied to
(13): its forcing includes \(\operatorname{div}A_1\), and its hypothesis
already bounds \(\nabla A_1\). The two statements have different inputs.

The local axial-datum mechanism is Balaban's, also stated at SA
7724–7739 and explained at 7911. C1 combines that mechanism with the
raw-datum output theorem. It does not claim a new construction of the
finite mean, pivot or local gauge.

### C2. Complex averaged plaquettes retain the linear curvature factor

There is a useful version of the same argument whose constant does
not depend on a macroscopic enclosing box. Let \(t=L^q\eta\le1\).
Take a plaquette of the actual \(q\)-fold average, with all four
complete averaging carriers contained in an embedded cube of side
\(c_Lt\). Suppose (1) holds on that cube. Then, at individual small
radius ceilings,
\[
                 |\partial M^q(W)-I|\le C_{\rm av}
                                      \epsilon_0t^2.     \tag{16}
\]
The constant depends only on the fixed averaging data and \(L\), not
on \(q\), \(\eta\), volume, or \(\epsilon_1/\epsilon_0\).

**Proof.** Apply (13) on this cube, with physical side \(c_Lt\).
The gauged fine potential is at most
\(C_Lt\epsilon_0e^{C_Lt\epsilon_1}\). The value-only average bound
therefore gives logarithms \(E_i\) of the four gauged averaged links,
with
\[
                   |E_i|\le C_L\epsilon_0t^2
                                            e^{C_Lt\epsilon_1}.
\]
The fixed-size common-chart argument of C1, with \(R=c_L\),
identifies the original averages with the node-gauge transforms of
these small-branch averages. Their plaquette is the product of those
four links and their oriented inverses. Hence its defect is at most
\(e^{\sum_i|E_i|}-1\le2\sum_i|E_i|\), after an individual ceiling
on \(\epsilon_0\). Undoing the node gauge costs at most
\(e^{C_Lt\epsilon_1}\). Since \(t\le1\), an individual ceiling on
\(\epsilon_1\) bounds both exponentials by fixed constants. This
proves (16).

The carriers do have bounded relative size for an ordinary averaged
plaquette: each backwards averaging step adds at most a fixed multiple
of \(L^{i-q}t\), and
\(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}L^{i-q}<(L-1)^{-1}\). The four initial carriers
and their one-stencil margins add only a fixed multiple of \(t\).
This is the complete-read calculation of `b109` P15 and `piv` (20).
The cube must be supplied and embedded; data are not extended across
a hole or across a torus cut.

More generally the proof applies to a closed test with a bounded
number of rows, total physical row length at most \(C_Lt\), and
complete carriers in the same size cube. Sum their logarithm bounds
instead of four terms. This last assertion is conditional on that
test geometry; it does not identify every artificial boundary row of
a localized tower with such a test. \(\square\)

Thus complex averaging itself need not create an
\(\epsilon_1^2\) error in these test bounds. The missing step is the
uniform **critical-field** response to this curved datum. In particular
(16) alone does not bound its current. The current contains a difference
of plaquette terms divided by the fine spacing in physical units.
Bounding the individual terms without their critical-equation
cancellation loses that inverse spacing.

### C3. Attempted removal of the box size

Applying C1 directly to a filled cube containing all of \(Z\) sets
\(R\) at least of order \(\operatorname{diam}(Z)/s\). This is
unbounded even with fixed physical \(Z\) as \(p-n\) increases.
Moreover an arbitrary \(Z\) need not supply the filling cube at all.
Neither the factor \(R\) in (12) nor its \(R\)-dependent input
ceilings can be absorbed into an early \(B_3\).

The next attempt uses the first moment of the response kernel instead
of that diameter. Its sufficient estimate can be stated exactly.
Let \(V(u)\), \(0\le u\le1\), be a path of the **actual layered
data** on a common admitted analytic branch. Suppose the normalized
readout (2) vanishes at \(V(0)\) and is the requested readout at
\(V(1)\). Use left logarithmic velocities
\(h_c(u)=i^{-1}\dot V_c(u)V_c(u)^{-1}\). If there are nonnegative
numbers \(k_{x,c}(u),w_c(u)\) such that
\[
 \|D_L\mathcal F_x(V(u))[h]\|
       \le\sum_c k_{x,c}(u)|h_c|,\qquad
 |h_c(u)|\le\epsilon_0s^2w_c(u),\qquad
 \int_0^1\sum_c k_{x,c}(u)w_c(u)\,du\le C_*,             \tag{17}
\]
then the chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus give
\[
                    |\mathcal F_x(V(1))|
                                  \le C_*\epsilon_0s^2. \tag{18}
\]
No derivative of the input axial potential occurs. The kernels in
(17) must read the curvature or **physical current**, with the actual
node factors included. A potential-only or energy-norm inverse is not
that estimate. Nor may a counting sum over fine sites replace the
physical row weights.

For a local axial construction one expects \(w_c\) to grow at most
linearly with its distance, multiplied by its complex transport cost.
If a weighted row estimate at rate \(m\) were available on the
**same path**, it would absorb such a factor at a smaller rate. For
example, for \(0\le a<m\),
\[
 \sup_{r\ge0}(1+r)e^{-(m-a)r}
                      \le 1+(m-a)^{-1}.                  \tag{19}
\]
The bound follows from \(re^{-(m-a)r}\le(m-a)^{-1}\).
Thus polynomial comb length is not itself fatal after weighting.
This explains why (11) does not refute a local response proof.

I tried two ways to construct the required comparison.

First, localize \(W\) in its own tree gauge, replace it by the
identity near \(x\), and retain the old field outside a collar.
C1 controls the newly created local branch, but it does not identify
that branch with the restriction of the original tower. The outside
determining data have changed. Frozen `mu` identifies the restriction
when its **own** averages are prescribed; the replacement has different
averages. Even granting a crude exponential comparison, its boundary
amplitude is controlled by the analytic radius or \(\epsilon_1\).
That leaves a term of the form \(C\epsilon_1s e^{-mR}\), which is
not bounded by \(C'\epsilon_0s^2\) for a fixed early \(R\).

Second, use the already constructed critical field in the artificial
collar, so that its preliminary regularity, rather than the input
twist, pays for the collar. In the real reset, `a1-balaban` (6.1)–(6.6),
one first aligns the local field gauge with the comb gauge of its
prescribed datum. Near rows then cost the datum curvature. Artificial
far rows cost the preliminary field radius. The desired complex
version would have the same separation: C2 pays for the near rows;
exponential response makes the coefficient of the far radius less
than one. Repeating the estimate on the same critical field would
remove (3)'s quadratic term.

The construction is not completed here. A tree gauge of the input
gives (16), while a tree gauge of the critical field gives its own
small values. They are not the same node gauge. One must align them
on the actual determining roots, include the outward and artificial
boundary rows, and exhibit a common complex raw-datum chart throughout
the local replacement. Its boundary forcing must have a bound in the
physical-current response norm, proportional to the preliminary field
radius. Separate local value bounds do not prove these compatibility
and response assertions. PIV solves an averaging constraint on an
admitted chart; it does not supply this boundary comparison.

Allowing \(R\) to grow with \(\log(1/\epsilon_0)\) does not finish
either construction. A formal exponential tail could then become small
enough, but C1's common-chart proof used \(R\epsilon_1\) small.
That fails for a fixed admissible \(\epsilon_1>0\) and arbitrarily
small \(\epsilon_0\). A different proof could retain the exponential
condition numbers in a weighted norm, as (19) suggests. The present
argument does not establish its common branch or boundary admission.

This is also the precise distinction in SA 12226–12292: its collar
estimate is an implication from the actual compatible equations,
forcing and analytic domains. It does not construct them for the
replacement above. SA 9734–9742 gives response on an admitted
polydisc, not a curvature-coordinate path through arbitrary local
replacements. The relevant local reset at SA 9860–9867 is sketched.

**Attack C therefore succeeds at fixed-box curvature transfer and
at (16), but fails to remove the box restriction.** The missing
estimate is the compatible complex local comparison just specified,
with the physical-current readout and constants uniform in both
tower rules. Equation (17) is a sufficient mechanism, not a proved
input and not a claim to weaken L17 by renaming it.

## 5. Constant order and what the consumer can presently use

The constants in (4)–(11) are examples testing particular methods;
they are not choices in the construction. C1 chooses a fixed \(R\)
before its two individual ceilings. Its constant is \(CR\), so it
does not give an allowed choice of \(B_3\) when \(R\) grows with
the domain or depth. C2 chooses its carrier multiple, averaging
constants and two individual ceilings at the early fixed-\(L\)
stage. Neither result assumes \(\epsilon_1^2\lesssim\epsilon_0\).

The available full-tower bound remains `b109` (110). At a hereditary
label with \((\epsilon_0,\epsilon_1)=(\mu\alpha_0',\mu\alpha_1')\),
\(0<\mu\le1\), it yields
\[
 K(\mu\alpha_0'+\mu^2\alpha_1'^2)
                       \le K\mu(\alpha_0'+\alpha_1'^2). \tag{20}
\]
Thus \(K(\alpha_0'+\alpha_1'^2)\le\alpha_0\) remains a sufficient
budget for that use, together with its other conditions. This is
the outline's quadratic budget. It does not follow from CA-I's

### 6.1 Attack D: holomorphic division by the plaquette defects

One could try to factor the readout as
\[
 \mathcal F_x(W)=\sum_P K_{xP}(W)[\partial W(P)-I],
 \qquad \sum_P\|K_{xP}(W)\|\le C\eta_n^{-2}.       \tag{22}
\]
The sum is over the actual supplied fine plaquettes. Each coefficient
is a linear map on matrices; the sum uses counting coefficients.
Since \(\eta_n^{-2}\eta^2=s^2\), (22) would give the
required scale and radius, with an early enlargement of \(B_3\).
The same bound is required for the physical-current readout.
No such factorization is assumed here.

The first construction tried was a holomorphic retraction onto flat
connections, followed by Taylor's integral formula in transverse
coordinates. This construction is unavailable even on a fixed finite
torus near the identity.

**D1. There is no twice differentiable local retraction onto all flat
periodic connections near the identity.** This includes holomorphic
retractions. The claim concerns bond configurations before quotienting
by gauge, with at least two periodic coordinate directions.

**Proof.** Let \(\mathscr Z\) denote the set of flat connections.
Suppose a map \(R\) on a neighborhood of identity satisfies
\(R(W)\in\mathscr Z\) and \(R(W)=W\) for
\(W\in\mathscr Z\). Use the small bond-logarithm coordinates.
For every constant Lie-algebra element \(X\), the curve with
links \(e^{tX}\) in direction 1 and identity links otherwise
is flat. Thus \(DR_0\) fixes its tangent. It also fixes the
analogous direction-2 tangent with any constant element \(Y\).
Linearity makes it fix their sum.

Choose \([X,Y]\ne0\). For example take
\(X=i\sigma_1\), \(Y=i\sigma_2\), where the Pauli
matrices obey \([i\sigma_1,i\sigma_2]=-2i\sigma_3\).
Apply \(R\) to the connection with links \(e^{tX}\)
and \(e^{tY}\) in these two directions. Its flat image has
logarithms
\[
 Z_1(x,t)=tX+t^2B_1(x)+o(t^2),\qquad
 Z_2(x,t)=tY+t^2B_2(x)+o(t^2).
\]
The coefficient of \(t^2\) in its oriented 12-plaquette is
\[
 B_1(x)+B_2(x+e_1)-B_1(x+e_2)-B_2(x)+[X,Y].        \tag{23}
\]
Here \(e_i\) denotes one fine step; physical mesh factors are
irrelevant to this finite Taylor identity. Multiplication of the
four exponential series gives (23): the pure constant product is
\(I+t^2[X,Y]+o(t^2)\), and each \(B\) occurs linearly.
Flatness makes (23) zero at every site. Sum over one periodic
12-plane. Each difference of \(B\)'s telescopes. The remaining
sum is the positive number of sites times \([X,Y]\), a
contradiction. The same argument applies on a four-dimensional
torus, with the other coordinates fixed. \(\square\)

This obstruction applies at the identity, also to a real twice
differentiable retraction, and does not rely on a large condition
number. The flat locus has obstructed tangent directions: every separate constant
direction integrates to a flat curve, but arbitrary sums need not.

Vanishing on that locus would still not, by itself, prove a bounded
division theorem. For an elementary warning, put
\(P(z)=z^2\), \(f(z)=z\). They have the same zero set,
but \(f=KP\) has no bounded holomorphic coefficient near zero.
This example is not the lattice curvature map. It only specifies
the logical inference which needs an additional proof.

**Outcome of D.** The flat-retraction construction is refuted by
(23). It cannot furnish (22). A division argument using the full
plaquette ideal, including its relations, remains possible. Such an
argument would need the coefficient sum in (22), uniformly in depth
and volume. Neither D1 nor the scalar warning disproves that statement
or L17.

### 6.2 Attack E: close an equation for the curvature itself

This attack retains the flat modes and avoids a retraction. It tries
to solve for the output curvature and physical current, using the
Bianchi identity and constrained stationarity. There is no linear
obstruction at the identity. In fact the existing quadratic estimate
already proves the exact linear statement that this route needs.

**E1. The identity response factors boundedly through the input curl.**
Fix one of the finite actual tower problems. Let
\[
 \mathscr L_x a=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}
                   \mathcal F_x(e^{i\eta t a}),
\]
with either readout (2), based at the identity. Let \(d_\eta a\)
be the ordinary physical lattice curl on all supplied plaquettes.
Then, with the constant of `b109` P20 and its premises,
\[
                 |\mathscr L_x a|
                     \le K s^2\|d_\eta a\|_\infty.       \tag{24}
\]
It holds for complex \(a\), uniformly in both rules and all
the parameters for which P20 holds.

**Proof.** Put \(A_* =\max(\|a\|_\infty,
\|\nabla a\|_\infty)\). At any fixed finite problem this
number is finite. The four-link expansion gives, for positive
\(t\) tending to zero,
\[
 \|\partial e^{i\eta ta}-I\|_\infty
 \le\eta^2\{t\|d_\eta a\|_\infty+C t^2 A_*^2\}.
\]
Take \(U_r=I\). It satisfies the real cube-gauge condition
with zero potential. Choose slightly larger positive radii
\[
 \epsilon_0(t)=t\|d_\eta a\|_\infty+
                         (C A_*^2+1)t^2,
 \qquad \epsilon_1(t)=tA_*+t^2.
\]
All the individual ceilings hold for small enough \(t\).
The independent input current may be zero. P20 gives
\[
 |\mathcal F_x(e^{i\eta ta})|
       \le K s^2\{t\|d_\eta a\|_\infty+O(t^2)\}.
\]
The readout vanishes at identity. Divide by \(t\) and take
the derivative, which exists on the supplied holomorphic branch.
This proves (24). The choice of how small \(t\) must be may
depend on the finite input. The surviving constant \(K\) does not.

In particular \(\mathscr L_x\) vanishes on \(\ker d_\eta\).
Consequently
\[
 \mathscr B_x(d_\eta a):=s^{-2}\mathscr L_x a
\]
is a well-defined complex linear map on \(\operatorname{im}d_\eta\),
with norm at most \(K\) in the inherited supremum norm.
No choice of a uniformly bounded inverse of curl was used.
This is a consequence of P20, not an additional assumption about
the inverse LQ. \(\square\)

Thus a nonlinear proof cannot stop at the assertion that a curl
inverse loses the volume. The linear output map has already canceled
that loss. The question is whether its nonlinear counterpart keeps
the cancellation.

**E2. The multiplier survives the curvature equation.** In top
units, write \(h=\eta_n\). At the identity, let \(v\) be
the linearized critical potential, \(f=d_1v\) its curvature,
and \(\mathsf Q\) the actual linearized constraint in a
fixed straightened chart. All adjoints below use physical pairings.
The linear constrained equations are
\[
 d_1^*f=\mathsf Q^*\lambda,\qquad
 d_2f=0,\qquad \mathsf Qv=b.                            \tag{25}
\]
These follow by differentiating the action and constraint, and by
\(d_2d_1=0\). Hence
\[
 (-\Delta_h)f=d_1\mathsf Q^*\lambda,
 \qquad J_{\rm lin}(v)=\mathsf Q^*\lambda.              \tag{26}
\]
The cochain identity
\(d_1d_1^*+d_2^*d_2=-\Delta_h\) is componentwise:
expanding it cancels the mixed differences, while the diagonal
terms are the four negative second differences. This verifies (26).
It does not identify \(\lambda\) with the independent input current.

Even in this flat problem, setting the right side of stationarity
to zero is wrong. Take the uniform periodic row geometry and a
small real coarse datum \(b\) with nonzero coarse curl. A
real constrained solution exists in the supplied linear problem.
If its physical current were zero, then
\[
 0=\langle v,d_1^*d_1v\rangle=\|d_1v\|_2^2.
\]
It would be closed. The torus decomposition in `b109` P10 gives
\(v=d_0\phi+H\), with \(H\) constant. Its actual flat
straightened average is
\(d_cQ_s\phi+H\), by (43)–(46) there, and has zero
coarse curl. This contradicts the chosen datum. This example is
a constrained real background, not a counterexample to L17.

For the nonlinear field the full stationarity equation is
\(F'(A)=K_A^{\mathsf T}\omega\). Differentiating it gives
\[
 \left(F''(A)-\langle\omega,
                  (\mathcal Q^{\rm st})''(A)\rangle\right)\dot A
             -K_A^{\mathsf T}\dot\omega=0,
 \qquad K_A\dot A=D\mathsf b(B)\dot B.                  \tag{27}
\]
The notation for the multiplier pairing includes its row weights.
This is SA BGR1, BGR4–BGR5, not a replacement equation.
In particular the constraint Hessian and the actual reduced-datum
derivative remain present at outward rows.

I tried to perturb the bounded curvature map of E1 using (27).
The available inverse gives a perturbation estimate in potential
and multiplier coordinates. Applying it with a potential of size
\(s\epsilon_1\) again gives a quadratic error of size
\(s^2\epsilon_1^2\). To replace that error by
\(C\epsilon_1\eta_n^{-2}\|\partial W-I\|\), one needs an estimate
in the curvature quotient itself. No such estimate follows from
invertibility in the potential norm.

There is also an exact gauge term away from the identity. For an
input gauge \(e^{t\chi}\), covariance on common charts gives
\[
 \left.\frac d{dt}\right|_0\mathcal F_x(W^{e^{t\chi}})
        =[\bar\chi_{\mathcal T}(x),\mathcal F_x(W)],     \tag{28}
\]
where \(\bar\chi_{\mathcal T}\) is the prescribed lift of
the node values, including the fixed shell. This is differentiation
of conjugation; the identity part of the plaquette commutes.
Thus the ordinary differential no longer annihilates gauge directions.
One must retain the moving frame, or control its commutator with the
unknown output. The source also retains the slice derivative when
the reference moves, SA 11748–11751.

**Outcome of E.** Equations (24)–(26) give a bounded linear
curvature transfer and the correct curvature equation. The proposed
nonlinear inversion stops at a specific missing estimate: control
the coupled curvature, multiplier and frame response in supremum
norm, with the actual row weights and boundary rows. LQ's energy
inverse is not that estimate. Dropping the multiplier would already
fail in the real example above. Dropping the constraint Hessian or
(28) would not differentiate the actual problem.

### 6.3 Attack F: recover a regular gauge from curvature and current

The first round used values alone to enter a local datum chart.
This attack uses the physical current to recover the missing spatial
derivative before attempting the reset. An additive Hodge construction
does this on a supplied cube. For commuting fields it is an exact
complex gauge transformation.

**F1. A local Hodge gauge without an input derivative bound.**
Let \(Q_R\) have radius \(R\) in maximum norm, and let
\(R\ge h\). Suppose a complex one-form \(A\) is supplied
on \(Q_{4R}\), with every required stencil, and
\[
 \|A\|_\infty\le a_0,\qquad
 \|dA\|_\infty\le f_0,\qquad
 \|d^*dA\|_\infty\le j_0.                              \tag{29}
\]
On a torus the whole cube and its stencils must have an embedded
coordinate lift. No extension through a missing read is allowed.
The differences have mesh \(h\), and \(d^*\) is the
physical adjoint. If \(R\ge512h\), there is a scalar
site field \(\lambda\), with the same coefficient space as
\(A\), such that \(B=A-d\lambda\) on \(Q_{3R}\),
\(d^*B=0\) there, and, on \(Q_R\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|\lambda\|_\infty&\le CRa_0,\\
 \|B\|_\infty&\le C(a_0+Rf_0),\\
 \|\nabla B\|_\infty+R^\alpha[\nabla B]_\alpha
      &\le C_\alpha(R^{-1}a_0+f_0+Rj_0),
                       \qquad 0<\alpha<1.                \tag{30}
 \end{aligned}
\]
The first two estimates hold on the whole auxiliary lattice for
the extensions constructed below. There is no bound on
\(\nabla A\) among the hypotheses. Constants are independent
of mesh and ambient volume. If \(h\le R<512h\), the
last two estimates on \(Q_R\) hold with \(B=A\),
\(\lambda=0\); no Coulomb condition is asserted in this case.

**Proof.** We use `cp12` Lemma 5's heat-kernel and interior
estimates componentwise. Those statements hold for complex coefficients
by applying the real scalar argument to their real and imaginary
parts. They are rebuilt there from Sussman–Alpoge's BGH estimates.
The additional compact-support massless estimate needed here follows
as follows.

Let \(G=(-\Delta_h)^{-1}\) on compactly supported fields
on \(h\mathbb Z^4\), defined by the integral of the heat
kernel. If \(f\) is supported in a cube of radius at most
\(5R\), then
\[
                 \|DGf\|_\infty\le CR\|f\|_\infty.     \tag{31}
\]
All convolutions have measure \(h^4\) times counting.
For \(t\le h^2\), the heat-gradient sum is at most
\(C h^{-1}\). For \(t\ge h^2\), it is at most
\(Ct^{-1/2}\), by `cp12` (11).
The Fourier formula also gives
\[
                   \sup_x|Dp_t^h(x)|\le Ct^{-5/2}
                                      \quad(t\ge h^2).
\]
Indeed a unit difference contributes at most \(C|\theta|\).
The physical prefactor is \(h^{-5}\), while
\(\omega(\theta)\ge c|\theta|^2\). Integrating
\(|\theta|e^{-ct|\theta|^2/h^2}\) and substituting
\(\theta=(h/\sqrt t)y\) gives \(Ct^{-5/2}\).
The finite Gaussian integral is bounded by separating coordinates;
its radial tail is an exponential times a polynomial.

The three time intervals \([0,h^2]\), \([h^2,R^2]\)
and \([R^2,\infty)\) therefore bound (31) respectively by
\[
 Ch\|f\|_\infty,\qquad
 C\|f\|_\infty\int_{h^2}^{R^2}t^{-1/2}dt,
 \qquad CR^4\|f\|_\infty\int_{R^2}^{\infty}t^{-5/2}dt.
\]
Each is at most \(CR\|f\|_\infty\). The last estimate
uses the physical support volume, at most \(CR^4\).
The same argument without the derivative proves convergence of
\(Gf\). Integrating the heat equation gives
\((-\Delta_h)Gf=f\); the terminal heat term tends to zero
by its \(Ct^{-2}\) pointwise bound. Differences commute
with this convolution. These arguments justify all uses of \(G\)
below, at each fixed mesh, with the uniform bounds just proved.

Choose a sampled smooth cutoff \(\chi\), equal to one on
\(Q_{3R}\) and zero before the edge of \(Q_{4R}\), with
\(|D\chi|\le C/R\). Extend \(a=\chi A\) by zero.
The discrete product rule gives
\[
              \|da\|_\infty\le f_0+Ca_0/R.             \tag{32}
\]
This needs values of \(A\), not its differences separately.
For example
\(D_\mu(\chi A_\nu)=\chi D_\mu A_\nu+
(D_\mu\chi)A_\nu(\cdot+he_\mu)\). Subtracting
the two terms of the curl leaves \(\chi dA\) and the
two cutoff terms. Their full stencils lie in the supplied cube.

Define on the auxiliary infinite lattice
\[
       \lambda=Gd^*a,\qquad B=d^*Gda=a-d\lambda.        \tag{33}
\]
The second equality follows from
\(dd^*+d^*d=-\Delta_h\) and the inverse identity on the
compactly supported \(a\). It can also be obtained by
integrating the heat equation for \(a\). Equation (31),
applied after moving one difference to the kernel, bounds
\(\lambda\) by \(CRa_0\). Applied to (32), it bounds
\(B\) by \(C(a_0+Rf_0)\).

Now \((d^*)^2=0\), so \(d^*B=0\). In \(Q_{3R}\),
\(a=A\) and \(dB=dA\). Thus, on its interior stencils,
\[
                         -\Delta_hB=d^*dA.              \tag{34}
\]
The componentwise interior estimate `cp12` (10) gives (30).
For specificity, center cubes of radius \(R/4\) at points
of \(Q_R\). Their double enlargements lie in \(Q_{3R}\),
and their radius is at least \(64h\). They control close
pairs in the Hölder seminorm. Pairs separated by more than
\(R/16\) use twice the gradient supremum. Changing these
fixed cube ratios changes only \(C_\alpha\).

If \(R<512h\), use \(B=A\). Every first difference
is at most \(2a_0/h\le1024a_0/R\). Distinct evaluation
sites have distance at least \(h>R/512\); twice that
gradient bound gives the asserted Hölder bound. This proves the
remaining case without a continuum argument. \(\square\)

**F2. For commuting complex fields this uses the physical current.**
Suppose every \(A_b\) lies in one fixed commuting matrix
subalgebra, and \(U_b=e^{ihA_b}\). On the small logarithm
branch its plaquettes are exactly
\[
                   P=e^{ih^2F},\qquad F=dA.
\]
In SU(2)'s complexification the analytic current uses
\(\pi(P)=(P-P^{-1})/(2i)\), the continuation of the
real formula in `cp12` D5. Its power series gives
\(\pi(e^{ih^2F})=h^2F+O(h^6\|F\|^3)\).
Pairing the opposite plaquettes at a bond gives the linear
current \(d^*F\). Transport causes no additional term in
this commuting case. With the consistent current orientation,
\[
                \|d^*F\|_\infty
                   \le\|J(U)\|_\infty+Ch^3f_0^3.       \tag{35}
\]
There are six incident plaquettes; the remainder estimate uses
that fixed count, not a difference bound on \(F\).

The field \(\lambda\) in (33) lies in the same subalgebra.
Hence the site gauge \(g=e^{i\lambda}\) gives exactly
\(U^g=e^{ih(A-d\lambda)}=e^{ihB}\) on the interior.
Moreover
\[
                 \|g\|\,\|g^{-1}\|\le e^{2CRa_0}.     \tag{36}
\]
Thus, if a tree gauge has \(a_0\le C_0Rq\) and
\(f_0,\|J(U)\|_\infty\le q\), then for fixed \(R\)
and small \(q\), (30) gives
\[
                   |B|+|\nabla B|\le C_R q.             \tag{37}
\]
Here (35) supplies \(j_0\). A logarithm bound converts a
small plaquette-defect bound to the stated \(f_0\) bound
with a fixed factor. All the constants in (37) are independent
of the fine mesh. No relation between input radii is used.
This repairs the derivative obstruction for commuting fields with
controlled **physical** current. The independent current in (1)
does not supply (35) for the input field.

**Attempted noncommutative extension.** For a small noncommuting
potential the logarithmic curvature satisfies
\[
 F=dA+E(A),\qquad |E(A)|\le C|A|^2,
 \qquad |d^*E(A)|\le C|A|\max_{\rm stencil}|\nabla A|. \tag{38}
\]
These are the four-link expansion and its translated difference,
as in `cp12` (17). The current controls \(d^*F\), with
the transport error in `cp12` (16). It does not yet control
\(d^*dA\): (38) contains exactly the unknown derivative.
Putting this in (34) would bound \(\nabla B\) in terms
of \(\nabla A\). The two fields differ by \(d\lambda\),
whose second differences have not been bounded. There is no
absorption of the unknown derivative at that step.

More basically, (33) is not an exact nonabelian gauge change.
The convergent group series gives
\[
 A^{e^{i\lambda}}=A-d\lambda+\mathcal N(A,\lambda),
\quad
 |\mathcal N|\le C\{ |\lambda|(|A|+|d\lambda|)
                         +h(|A|+|d\lambda|)^2\}.        \tag{39}
\]
The estimate follows by expanding
\(h^{-1}i^{-1}\log(e^{i\lambda(x)}e^{ihA_b}
e^{-i\lambda(x+h e_\mu)})\) about
\((\lambda(x),\lambda(x),0)\). Its linear terms are
the transported \(A\) and a matrix times \(d\lambda\);
their differences from \(A-d\lambda\) cost \(|\lambda|\).
The remainder is quadratic in the two bond increments.
Differentiating (39)'s actual series introduces
\(\lambda\nabla A\) and \(\lambda\nabla d\lambda\).
The value bound alone does not control those terms.

I next tried solving the exact Coulomb equation by correcting
(33). Formally the correction is \(Gd^*\mathcal N\),
with cutoff and boundary terms included. To contract in the
value-and-gradient norm for \(\lambda\), this asks for
\(DGd^*\) on bounded forcing. The absolute heat bounds
used above now have an intermediate integral
\[
                       \int_{h^2}^{R^2}t^{-1}dt
                                      =2\log(R/h).       \tag{40}
\]
They give a mesh-dependent bound, not the constant in (31).
Thus this proposed estimate would require a ceiling involving
\(q\log(R/h)\), which is not allowed. Equation (40)
records the failure of this estimate; it does not prove that
the structured nonlinear forcing cannot enjoy better cancellation.
An \(L^p\) gauge construction with \(p>4\), followed by
elliptic regularity, is a possible alternative. Its uniform discrete
singular-integral and nonlinear gauge estimates have not been proved
here and are not inputs of frozen `cp12` Lemma 6.

Even a completed local complex Coulomb gauge must be aligned with
the actual determining roots to use the reset. The unnormalized
gauge (33) supplies no such alignment. `a1-balaban` section 6
requires both its near datum bound (6.1) and its artificial
boundary bound before (6.3). Frozen `mu` supplies nesting on
admitted charts, and `piv` solves its specified averaging equations.
Neither turns this unconstrained local gauge into that datum-and-frame
comparison. SA 12226–12292 retains the corresponding boundary
forcing and common-chart requirements.

**Outcome of F.** Equations (29)–(37) prove a mesh-uniform
local repair for commuting complex fields, using physical current.
For the actual noncommuting towers the first unclosed step is

The attempted correction produces (38)–(40). The actual root and

No general noncommutative gauge theorem is inferred from F1.

## 7. Second-round conclusion and constant order

The three new attacks are D, E and F. They do not repeat
the first round's real-to-complex supremum argument, real-orbit
interpolation, or value-only box transfer.

D proves a concrete obstruction to a proposed coordinate method.
E proves that the actual identity response already factors boundedly
through curl. F repairs the lost derivative on a supplied cube
for commuting complex fields with controlled physical current.
None proves the nonlinear two-rule tower estimate.

In E, the constant is the early constant of P20. The shrinking
Taylor parameter is used only to take a derivative of one fixed
finite problem. It makes no choice of a construction constant from
the mesh. In F, the cube ratio is fixed, and the constants depend
on that ratio and the chosen Hölder exponent. An application at a
fixed reset size would choose that size before its radius ceilings.
Letting it grow with the input-radius ratio would not give L17.
The mesh-dependent ceiling suggested by (40) is explicitly rejected.

The missing nonlinear estimate cannot be supplied by treating a
constrained critical field as a zero-current field. Nor is a
local Coulomb gauge alone the normalized tower comparison needed
in the reset. This is why the current proof stops at the same
precisely quantified L17, rather than declaring a conditional reset
to have closed it.
