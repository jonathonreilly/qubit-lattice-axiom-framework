## 2. The corrected supplier and hereditary admission

Fix the early constants and the two actual maximal tower rules.
Retain (104)–(105) of `b109`, their individual ceilings, complete reads,
outward rows and torus floors. In particular \(M\ge2LR_1M_1\).
For \(p\ge1\), an admissible \(Z\), \(1\le n\le p\), put
\(\eta=\xi_p\), \(s=L^n\eta\), and suppose
\[
 W=e^{i\eta A}U,\qquad U\in G,\qquad
 |\partial U-I|,|\partial W-I|<e_0\eta^2,\qquad
 |A|,|\nabla_U A|<e_1.                                  \tag{1}
\]
The real cube gauges retain their bound at \(e_0\), not at an
enlarged radius. The independent input current retains its own ceiling.
Let \(T_n^{\mathfrak r}(W)\) be the actual axial analytic tower branch.
There is an early constant \(C>K\), with \(K\) from (110), such that
\[
\boxed{\begin{aligned}
 |\partial T_n^{\mathfrak r}(W)-I|
   &< C(e_0+e_1^2)\xi_p^2,\\
 |J_n(T_n^{\mathfrak r}(W))|
   &< C(e_0+e_1^2)(L^n\xi_p)^2
       &&\text{on }Z^{\wr p}.
\end{aligned}}                                                   \tag{2}
\]
For the real factor the same bounds hold with \(e_1=0\).
The constant is uniform in rule, depth, region, mesh and volume.
It precedes the later constants and cannot depend on \(e_1/e_0\).

**Proof from (110).** Choose, for example, \(C=2\max\{K,1\}\).
Existence and the two non-strict estimates are exactly P20.
Since \(e_0>0\), replacing \(K\) by \(C>K\) makes them strict.
Taking \(A=0\) gives the real-factor assertion. No analytic
continuation of an inequality is being asserted. \(\square\)

Consequently the corrected hypothesis `4.8:eq-small-pairs-1` is
\[
 e_1\le a_1,\qquad C(e_0+e_1^2)\le a_0.                    \tag{3}
\]
Take \(C\ge1\); then (3) also gives \(e_0\le a_0\).
All other hypotheses of `4.8:prop-small-pairs` remain attached.

Here is the full additional calculation needed in that proposition.
At a label \((p,Z)\in\mathfrak T_j(X)\), put
\(\mu=L^{-(j-p)}\in(0,1]\). Its change of units gives
\[
 (e_0^{(p)},e_1^{(p)})=(\mu e_0,\mu e_1).
\]
This is I 34107–34145, including the single power needed for the
real cube gauge. The new estimate has coefficient
\[
 C(\mu e_0+\mu^2e_1^2)
 \le\mu C(e_0+e_1^2)\le\mu a_0.                           \tag{4}
\]
For \(U\), its coefficient is \(C\mu e_0\le\mu a_0\).
Thus both configurations obey the unchanged tower clause at every label.
The curvature and current factors remain respectively \(\xi_p^2\)
and \((L^n\xi_p)^2\). The interior check is unchanged:
I 34177–34189 gives collar width at most \(R_1M_1\) and
\(2M/L\ge4R_1M_1\). Hence the required stencils lie in the top layer.
This proves membership in the same \(U_j^{\Sigma^1}(X;a_0,a_1,\gamma_0)\).
No domain predicate or target radius has been weakened.

An optional sufficient budget is
\[
 e_1^2\le c e_0,\qquad C(1+c)e_0\le a_0.                 \tag{5}
\]
It is preserved at every label, since
\((\mu e_1)^2\le c\mu e_0\). Condition (5) is not a
consequence of independent individual ceilings. The correction must
state (3), or state (5), instead of claiming the old budget suffices.

## 3. The small-pair consumers keep their stated radius dependence

The actual new complex seeds are small exponentials, including true
pairs and the polydisc about zero. Their potential radius is a fixed
multiple of \(\min(a_0,a_1)\), not an arbitrary point of the full
\((a_0,a_1)\) class. This distinction supplies the needed budget.
The following is the explicit version of the argument of Sussman and
Alpoge, with Claude and Codex, at SA 6586–6630. Its tower input ER7
(SA 6571–6584) is supplied here by `b109` (110).

**Small-exponential lemma.** Retain the determining sets, geometry and
individual smallness ceilings of §2. There is one constant
\(c_*>0\), independent of the radii, level, volume and coupling,
such that, for all target radii \(0<a_0,a_1\le1\),
\[
 \max\{|A|,|\nabla A|,|\mathbf J|\}\le r,
 \qquad r=c_*\min(a_0,a_1),                              \tag{6}
\]
implies that \((e^{i\eta A},\mathbf J)\) belongs to the
unchanged target class, including its one-power hereditary version.
Here the current target is \(a_0\), as in these consumers.
For a true pair, replace the independent-current bound in (6) by
\(|\partial^*\partial A|\le r\); the conclusion then holds
with \(\mathbf J=J(e^{i\eta A})\). Norms here are in the
top units of the label, on the complete reads required by the source.

**Proof.** Take the real witness \(U=I\), whose cube gauge is zero.
Here is a direct plaquette estimate, also valid for complex \(A\).
For the four oriented links of a plaquette, write their potentials
as \(A_1,\ldots,A_4\). In a submultiplicative matrix norm, the
linear term in \(\prod_{\nu=1}^4 e^{i\eta A_\nu}\) is
\(i\eta\sum_\nu A_\nu=i\eta^2(dA)(p)\), of norm at most
a fixed multiple of \(\eta^2r\). The remaining exponential-series
terms are bounded by
\[
 e^{4b\eta r}-1-4b\eta r
 \le 8b^2\eta^2r^2e^{4b\eta r},                         \tag{7}
\]
where \(b\) is a fixed norm-equivalence constant. The inequality
follows by twice integrating the exponential, or term by term from
its series. Since \(\eta,r\le1\), a constant \(D\ge1\)
therefore gives
\[
 |\partial e^{i\eta A}-I|\le Dr\eta^2.                  \tag{8}
\]
No inverse power of the lattice spacing remains.

For the true-pair assertion, specialize
`4.4:lem-current-remainder`, I 10350–10376, to \(U_0=I\),
zero reference curvature and top length one. Its identity is at
I 10313–10321 and its product-expansion proof at I 10378–10502.
It gives
\[
 J(e^{i\eta A})=\partial^*\partial A+F(I,A),\qquad
 |F(I,A)|\le c_F\max(|A|,|\nabla A|)^2.                 \tag{9}
\]
This input does not use (1.17). In its elementary proof, the
quadratic commutators from the two plaquettes on opposite sides of
a bond cancel at constant \(A\); their difference contains
\(\eta\nabla A\). The remaining terms, after the current's
\(\eta^{-3}\) normalization, are bounded by fixed multiples of
\(|A||\nabla A|+|A|^3+\eta|\nabla A|^2\).
These are \(O(r^2)\). Thus (9) gives
\(|J(e^{i\eta A})|\le(1+c_F)r\); enlarge \(D\) to cover it.

After fixing \(C,D\), choose
\[
 c_*\le\min\{\tfrac14,(16CD)^{-1}\},\qquad
 e_0=\frac{a_0}{4C},\qquad
 e_1=\min\{\tfrac12a_1,\sqrt{a_0/(4C)}\}.              \tag{10}
\]
Then \(Dr\le e_0/4\) and \(r<e_1\). For the latter,
\(r\le a_1/4\) and
\(r^2\le c_*^2a_0^2\le a_0/(16C)<e_1^2\) whenever
the square-root member is the minimum. Thus (1), the own-radius
gauge condition, and the current bound at \(e_0\) all hold, with
strict room even for the closed ball in (6). Moreover
\[
 C(e_0+e_1^2)\le a_0/2.                                \tag{11}
\]
The auxiliary radii lie below the target radii and hence obey their
individual ceilings. Equations (3)–(4) now prove every required
tower clause for both rules. At the top label they give ordinary
\(U^c_j\) membership as well. This proves the lemma. \(\square\)

For `4.7:not-true-pairs`, I 29522–29544, the norm \(N_j\)
controls precisely the rescaled value, first difference, Laplacian
and \(\partial^*\partial\) needed above. Its radius \(r_j\)
is \(\min(a_{0,j},a_{1,j})\); see I 29060–29102.
Choose the constant named \(c_T\) once with the additional
ceiling \(c_T\le c_*\), alongside
\(c_T\le c_T^\circ,1/8,1/C_{IV}\) at I 29529–29535.
Use this same choice at all its occurrences. This is a permissible
choice of an unspecified constant depending on the earlier fixed
parameters; it is not a radius-dependent redefinition. Equations
(10)–(11) replace the free-prime choice in I 29557–29589.

This also repairs the proof of `5.2:lem-polydisc-containment`,
II 4333–4398. Its sentence at II 4381–4384 claiming that sufficiently
small (i)–(iii) alone imply (iv) must be replaced by (6)–(11),
applied to that polydisc. The stated radii remain
\(\mathfrak r_j=c_T\min(a_{0,j},a_{1,j})\) and
\(\mathfrak r_\circ=c_T\min(a_0,a_1)\). Their ratio is unchanged:
\[
 \mathfrak t_j^{-2}
 =\left(\frac{\min(a_0,a_1)}{\min(C_0,C_1)}\right)^2
   x_j(\log x_j)^{-2q}.                                \tag{12}
\]
The common \(c_T\) cancels. In the coupling-extraction consumer,
I 32452–32491, the Cauchy radius remains
\(\xi_i^3c_Tr_i/(8d)\). Its dependence on coupling and mesh is
unchanged; making the fixed \(c_T\) smaller only changes an earlier
finite prefactor in the extraction estimate. The final coupling
ceiling is still chosen afterwards.

This accounts for the indirect uses of \(c_T\) as well. The
fundamental-case Cauchy argument uses
\(\min\{c_Tr_{i'},\alpha_{3,i'}^\sharp/4\}\), at
I 31348–31379. Its comparison constant \(\widetilde K\) is
explicitly allowed to depend on \(c_T\), I 29828–29865.
The simple case uses \(\alpha_2=c_Tr_{i'}\), the far-case
increment lemma and its already admitted base, I 31604–31628.
Its fluctuation-to-radius ratio is paid by
\[
 C_\vartheta=2C_sA_1\max\{(c_\alpha C_0)^{-1},
             (c_T\min(C_0,C_1))^{-1}\},\qquad
 \vartheta(\gamma)=C_\vartheta
                    (\log\gamma^{-2})^{-(q-p_0)}.
\]
These are I 30028–30056. Since \(q>p_0\), any fixed increase
in \(C_\vartheta\) is met by the same kind of final \(\gamma\)
ceiling. The beta-coefficient extraction at I 32226–32247 and
32326–32352 uses the admitted true-pair ball twice and pays
\((c_Tr_j)^{-2}\), exactly as before. Thus no later use requires
the numerical value of the previously unspecified \(c_T\), and
no changed exponent or lower bound on the coupling is introduced.

## 4. Constants: which square ceiling is allowed?

The order in I 1601–1638 and 2106–2140 is
\[
 C_0\ \longrightarrow\ C_1\ \longrightarrow\
 \alpha_1'\ \longrightarrow\ \alpha_0'\ \longrightarrow\
 \alpha_1\ \longrightarrow\ \alpha_0\ \longrightarrow\
 \alpha_2\ \longrightarrow\ \cdots\ \longrightarrow\
 \varepsilon^{\mathrm I}_0\ \longrightarrow\
 \varepsilon^{\mathrm I}_1\ \longrightarrow\ \gamma.
                                                                  \tag{13}
\]
Here the primes are the **larger operator radii**, and \(\gamma\)
is last. In particular, after choosing \(\alpha_1>0\), the
absolute \(\alpha_0\) remains free to decrease. Imposing
\(\alpha_1^2\le c\alpha_0\) on these absolute radii would
put a positive lower bound on that later choice. It is not an
additional downward ceiling in the printed order. Likewise the
larger \(\alpha_1'\) precedes the larger \(\alpha_0'\).
Neither pair may be confused with the auxiliary admission radii.

For the auxiliary pair the order is instead
\[
 \text{early tower constant }C\ ;\quad M\ ;\quad
 (a_0,a_1)\ ;\quad e_0\ ;\quad e_1.                    \tag{14}
\]
After both targets are fixed, take \(e_0\le a_0/(2C)\)
under its other ceilings, and then
\(e_1\le\min\{a_1,\sqrt{e_0}\}\) under its other ceilings.
This is (5) with \(c=1\) and has no backward dependency.
For the actual seeds, (10) proves that these choices admit the
whole required ball of radius \(c_T\min(a_0,a_1)\).
This last containment is essential: merely shrinking the auxiliary
radii without checking the supplied fields would not suffice.
The separate table pair \(\varepsilon^{\mathrm I}_0\), then
\(\varepsilon^{\mathrm I}_1\), also permits a square ceiling on
its second member if such a use is made, but (14) does not identify
that pair with either set of primed radii.

`in1.md`, “Constant order”, places tower constants early, \(M\)
later, absolute radii and their ratios after \(M\), and the final
coupling cutoff last. Its example
\(a_1=t,\ a_0=t/(2D_*)\), followed by small \(t\), proves
that a jointly chosen absolute-radius window with
\(CMa_0<a_1\) and \(a_1^2\le Ca_0\) is nonempty.
That example is a joint choice; it does not establish that the
second inequality survives arbitrary subsequent decreases of
\(a_0\) in (13). No such joint reordering is needed here.

There is a second, compatible way to obtain a square budget when
a consumer uses fixed fractions of the **running** radii. Put
\(x_n=g_n^{-2}\). From I 1605–1613 and 1667–1669,
\[
 a_{i,n}=C_i x_n^{-1/2}(\log x_n)^q,
 \qquad
 \frac{a_{1,n}^2}{a_{0,n}}
   =\frac{C_1^2}{C_0}x_n^{-1/2}(\log x_n)^q.             \tag{15}
\]
Thus for fixed positive fractions \(e_{0,n}=\theta_0a_{0,n}\),
\(e_{1,n}=\theta_1a_{1,n}\), any ceiling
\(e_{1,n}^2\le c e_{0,n}\) holds uniformly at every level
after decreasing the final \(\gamma\). To verify the limit by
hand, set \(t=\log x\) and choose an integer \(m>q\).
The exponential series gives
\(e^{t/2}\ge (t/2)^m/m!\), hence
\(x^{-1/2}(\log x)^q\le2^m m!t^{q-m}\to0\).
This also bounds the supremum over all \(x\ge\gamma^{-2}\).
It is exactly an allowed Stage-8 ceiling, I 1642–1649.
All constants in it precede \(\gamma\), so there is no cycle.

By contrast, the linear running comparison
\(B_3^2O(1)Ma_{0,n}<a_{1,n}/2\) cancels the coupling and
must be met by the earlier floor on \(C_1/C_0\).
The source makes that distinction at I 1674–1684. A quadratic
comparison retains a factor of \(g_n\), as (15) shows. The proof
in §3 already works without this optional running-radius ceiling.

## 5. Uniqueness, own gauges, and clause (0)(g)

**MU.** The use of `4.13:lem-minimizer-unique`, I 45162–45201,
is uniqueness in the admitted variational chart and nesting on arbitrary
subdomains. It is not a request to deduce every defining tower bound
from (i)–(iii). `mu.md` §§4–7 supplies this identity on its stated
domains, admitting the actual complex data by its bound (7.4):
\[
 \|D_{\rm raw}\|_\infty
 \le 2LC_A[\alpha_2+2K_\mu C_VM
                (\alpha_0'''+\alpha_1''')]+2\alpha_3<\delta.
                                                                  \tag{16}
\]
Here \(K_\mu\) is that note's chart constant, not the tower
constant \(K\) of (110). This separate smallness test reads the
sum of the radii, not a bound proportional to curvature alone.
It survives §4's choices. The near-case consumer already assumes
the incoming tower clause; it uses MU to identify the outgoing
minimizer with a restriction and then applies its perturbation
estimates. Nothing in (16) asks for the discarded complex
\(2B_3e_0\) estimate. Uses of nesting at other stage radii retain
their separate admission requirements, exactly as the scope paragraph
in the freeze log of `mu.md` specifies.

**OC.** `4.13:lem-own-constant`, I 45203–45234, really does
require the gauge bound at the pair's *own* printed curvature radius,
not at a newly enlarged radius. That requirement is met without a
complex curvature-only tower estimate:

* At the unit pair and at \((e^{i\eta A},J(e^{i\eta A}))\),
  choose \(U=I\). The potential in every real cube gauge is zero,
  so its bound holds at the exact \(e_0\) of (10), or at any
  other positive printed radius.
* For the real background \(U_k(V)\), the actual proof in
  `oc.md` §4.2 uses real CMP 98 averaging and CMP 102b's
  minimal-orbit gauge theorem. It gives the cube potential and
  derivative below \(SB_{12}\varepsilon_0\) at level \(k\),
  and below \(SB_{12}\mu\varepsilon_0\) at level \(j\le k\),
  \(\mu=L^{-(k-j)}\). These are its exact own radii. The same
  early \(B_{12}\ge2B_3\) works. The real tower estimate
  \(2B_3\varepsilon_0\), obtained with no complex perturbation,
  is unchanged; alternatively (2) at \(e_1=0\) has the same
  linear dependence. Choose \(\varepsilon_0\) below the target
  admission ceiling as before.
* Restrictions and small perturbations of an already exhibited
  representative keep its real factor and transport its existing
  gauge. They do not replace that factor's radius by
  \(e_0+e_1^2\).

These are the three cases in `oc.md` §§4–5 and in
I 35400–35435, 36806–36891. Thus OC is not a failing use:
its curvature-only conclusion remains literal. Neither OC nor §2
proves the extra stage-background or re-minimization assertions
excluded at I 38108–38144.
