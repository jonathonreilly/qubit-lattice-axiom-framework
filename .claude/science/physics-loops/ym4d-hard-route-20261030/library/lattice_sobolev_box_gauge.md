## 1. Sobolev on Z^4

**Lemma 1.** For finitely supported u : Z^4 -> R^N, ||u||_{l^4} <= (3/2) ||grad u||_{l^2}, with |grad u|^2 = sum_i |u(x + e_i) - u(x)|^2.

*Proof.* It is enough to treat the scalar |u|, since | |u(x + e_i)| - |u(x)| | <= |u(x + e_i) - u(x)|.

Step 1. For finitely supported w >= 0, ||w||_{4/3} <= prod_i ||d_i w||_1^{1/4}. Indeed w(x) <= sum_t |d_i w(x + t e_i)| =: g_i(x with coordinate i removed), so w(x)^{4/3} <= prod_i g_i^{1/3}. The sum over x of a product of four functions, each independent of one coordinate, and each raised to the power 1/3, is at most prod_i (sum g_i)^{1/3}: sum over the coordinates one at a time and use Holder with exponents (3, 3, 3) on the three factors that depend on that coordinate. This gives ||w||_{4/3}^{4/3} <= prod_i ||d_i w||_1^{1/3}.

Step 2. Put w = |u|^3. Then |d_i w| <= (3/2) (u(x)^2 + u(x + e_i)^2) |d_i u|, since |a^3 - b^3| = |a - b| (a^2 + ab + b^2) <= (3/2) |a - b| (a^2 + b^2). So by Cauchy-Schwarz ||d_i w||_1 <= 3 ||u||_4^2 ||d_i u||_2. And ||w||_{4/3} = ||u||_4^3. Therefore ||u||_4^3 <= 3 ||u||_4^2 prod_i ||d_i u||_2^{1/4}, so ||u||_4 <= 3 prod_i ||d_i u||_2^{1/4} <= 3 (sum_i ||d_i u||_2^2 / 4)^{1/2} = (3/2) ||grad u||_2, by the inequality of the means.  []

**Corollary 2 (torus).** For u on the torus of side N in Z^4, with mean zero, ||u||_{l^4} <= C_1 ||grad u||_{l^2}, with C_1 absolute.

*Proof.* Lift u to a periodic function on Z^4. Let psi be a cutoff equal to 1 on one period cell, supported in the cube of three period cells per axis, with |grad psi| <= 2 / N. Lemma 1 for psi u gives ||u||_{l^4(cell)} <= (3/2) ||grad(psi u)||_2 <= C ( ||grad u||_{l^2(torus)} + N^{-1} ||u||_{l^2(torus)} ). The smallest non-zero eigenvalue of the lattice Laplacian on the torus is 4 sin^2(pi / N) >= 4 N^{-2} for N >= 2, so ||u||_2 <= (N / 2) ||grad u||_2 at mean zero.  []

## 2. A gauge on a box

Let D be a box in Z^4 whose sides are N_i, with all N_i comparable to N, and a a real or Lie-algebra-valued (linear, componentwise) field on the links of D. da is the lattice curl on the plaquettes of D.

**Lemma 3.** There is a scalar lambda on the sites of D such that a' = a + d lambda satisfies

    ||a'||_{l^4(D)} <= C_2 ||da||_{l^2(D)},    ||a'||_{l^2(D)} <= C_2 N ||da||_{l^2(D)},

with C_2 depending only on the ratios of the sides. The map da -> a' is linear.

*Proof.* Extend a to the torus T of side 2 N_i by the group generated by the reflections R_i in the faces of D, as a pull-back: for a link b, a(R_i b) := a(b) with the orientation of b carried along, so a link along axis i changes sign relative to the positive orientation of that axis, and other links keep their value. Links lying in a reflection plane are fixed by R_i, and they are not along axis i, so the definition is consistent. Pull-back commutes with d, so d a_T is the reflected extension of da, and each plaquette of T is the image of a plaquette of D. The number of images of one plaquette is at most 2^4, so ||d a_T||^2_{l^2(T)} <= 16 ||da||^2_{l^2(D)}.

The mean of the component of a_T along axis i is zero, by the sign change under R_i. So a_T has no harmonic part on T. Solve Delta lambda = - d^* a_T on T, which is possible since the right side has mean zero, and put a'_T = a_T + d lambda. Then d^* a'_T = 0 and d a'_T = d a_T. On the torus, in Fourier space, the lattice identity ||grad b||^2 = ||db||^2 + ||d^* b||^2 holds for every link field b, since lattice derivatives commute. So ||grad a'_T||_2 = ||d a_T||_2. Each component of a'_T has mean zero, since gauge terms have mean zero and a_T has mean zero. Corollary 2 and the eigenvalue bound, applied by components, give the two bounds on T. The solution lambda is unique up to a constant and invariant under the reflections, so its restriction to D gauges a itself on D. The bounds restrict to D. Linearity is clear.  []

**Corollary 4 (Poincare on a box, for scalars).** For a scalar zeta on a box D as above, ||zeta - mean||_{l^2(D)} <= C_2 N ||d zeta||_{l^2(D)}.

*Proof.* The even reflection of zeta to T has the same mean, and the eigenvalue bound applies.  []

## 3. Union of boxes

**Lemma 5 (l^p Poincare on a box).** For a scalar zeta on a box D of sides at most N, of mean zero, and 1 <= p < infinity, ||zeta||_{l^p(D)} <= C N ||d zeta||_{l^p(D)}, with C absolute.

*Proof.* zeta(x) = |D|^{-1} sum_w 1[x + w in D] (zeta(x) - zeta(x + w)). For fixed w, zeta(x) - zeta(x + w) is a sum of |w|_1 values of d zeta on an axial path, which stays in D since D is a box, and which is a fixed translate of one path as x varies. So Minkowski's inequality in x gives ||1[x, x + w in D] (zeta(x) - zeta(x + w))||_{l^p} <= |w|_1 ||d zeta||_{l^p(D)}. Summing over the at most 2^4 |D| values of w, and using |w|_1 <= 4 N, gives the claim.  []

**Lemma 6.** Let Omega = D_1 u ... u D_m be a finite union of boxes of comparable sides N, ordered so that each intersection O_j := D_j n (D_1 u ... u D_{j-1}) is a box of comparable sides. Then the conclusions of Lemma 3 hold on Omega, with a constant depending on m and the ratios only, and the map da -> a' is linear.

*Proof.* Induct on j. Let a'_1 be the gauge on D_1 u ... u D_{j-1} and a'_2 the gauge of Lemma 3 on D_j, both for the same a. On O_j they differ by a gradient, a'_1 - a'_2 = d zeta, and we take zeta of mean zero on O_j. Then ||d zeta||_{l^p(O_j)} <= ||a'_1||_{l^p(O_j)} + ||a'_2||_{l^p(O_j)} for p = 2, 4, and by Lemma 5, ||zeta||_{l^p(O_j)} <= C N ||d zeta||_{l^p(O_j)}. Extend zeta to D_j by reflection across the faces of O_j, and multiply by a cutoff equal to 1 on O_j, vanishing on the faces of D_j that meet the previous boxes outside O_j, with gradient at most C / N; call the result zeta_E. Put a' := a'_2 + d zeta_E on D_j and a' := a'_1 on the previous boxes. They agree on O_j. Reflection preserves the l^p norm of a function and of its gradient, up to a bounded multiplicity. The cutoff adds at most C N^{-1} ||zeta||_{l^p}, which is at most C ||d zeta||_{l^p(O_j)}. So ||a'||_{l^p(D_j)} <= C ( ||a'_1||_{l^p} + ||a'_2||_{l^p} ) for p = 2, 4, and with Lemma 3 this bounds both by ||da||_{l^2(Omega)}, with the factor N for p = 2.  []

## 4. Any finite pattern of cells, by Cech collating

**Lemma 7.** Let Omega be a finite union of cells, with the crossing links between face-adjacent cells, and N the side of a cell. For a link field a on Omega there is a gauge a'' = a + d lambda with ||a''||_{l^4(Omega)} <= C ||da||_{l^2(Omega)} and ||a''||_{l^2(Omega)} <= C N ||da||_{l^2(Omega)}, with C depending on the pattern only, not on N, and linear in da.

*Proof.* Thicken each cell i into its present face neighbours by a strip of width N / 4, without corners, to get D_i. Strips of different axes do not overlap, each strip overlaps the cell in a box, so Lemma 6 gives on D_i a gauge a_i = a + d lambda_i, with the bounds of Lemma 3 in terms of ||da||_{l^2(D_i)}. On a non-empty overlap O_ij, which is a box of side comparable to N, a_i - a_j = d zeta_ij, zeta_ij of mean zero, and by Lemma 5, ||zeta_ij||_{l^p(O_ij)} <= C N (||a_i||_{l^p} + ||a_j||_{l^p}), p = 2, 4. On a triple overlap, c_ijk := zeta_ij + zeta_jk + zeta_ki is a constant, of size at most C N^{-1} ||zeta||_{l^4}, by Holder at volume N^4, so |c| <= C ||a_.||_{l^4}. Since a is a single field, zeta_ij = lambda_i - lambda_j + const on O_ij, so the constants c_ijk form a Cech coboundary, c = delta b, on the finite nerve; choose b linear in c, with |b| <= C |c|, C depending on the nerve only. Replacing zeta_ij by zeta_ij - b_ij makes c = 0 on triple overlaps, though the sum of zeta around a cycle of the nerve need not vanish. Let h_k be a partition of unity subordinate to the D_k, with |d h_k| <= C / N. On D_i put mu_i := sum_k h_k zeta_ik, each term extended by zero off D_k, and a'' := a_i - d mu_i. On O_ij, mu_i - mu_j = sum_k h_k (zeta_ik - zeta_jk) = zeta_ij, using c = 0 (zeta_ik + zeta_kj + zeta_ji = 0), so a_i - d mu_i = a_j - d mu_j on O_ij, since a_i - a_j = d zeta_ij, and a'' is single valued. Bound: ||d mu_i||_{l^p(D_i)} <= sum_k ( ||d zeta_ik||_{l^p} + (C / N) ||zeta_ik||_{l^p} ) <= C sum_k ||a_k||_{l^p}, with the shift by the constants b contributing C N^{-1} |b| N^{4 / p}, which is C |b| at p = 4, and C N |b| at p = 2. H^1(Omega) = 0 makes a'' gauge equivalent to a. If H^1(Omega) != 0, the same construction still gives a single-valued a'' with d a'' = da and the same bounds, where a'' - a is a closed form, possibly with periods. *The l^{4/3} -> l^2 form:* the same proof, with the box gauge a' = K * f, |K(w)| <= C (1 + |w|)^{-3}, and Lemma 2 of `library/lattice_gehring_hls.md`, gives ||a''||_{l^2(Omega)} <= C ||da||_{l^{4/3}(Omega)}.  []

## Hostile check

(a) In Lemma 3 the identity ||grad b||^2 = ||db||^2 + ||d^* b||^2 is on the torus, where it is exact in Fourier space; it is not used on D, which is why a re-entrant corner of a region does no harm: only boxes occur. (b) Plaquettes in a reflection plane have fewer images than interior ones, which only helps the bound 16. (c) In Lemma 6 the order of the boxes and the condition on O_j are a restriction, used only inside one thickened cell, where it holds. Lemma 7 needs no order on the pattern. (d) The constants in Lemma 3 do not depend on the lattice scale N, and that is the point: they are uniform in the depth of the fine lattice. (e) In Lemma 6 the cutoff must vanish where D_j meets earlier boxes outside O_j. The hypothesis that O_j is the whole intersection makes that set disjoint from O_j, but its distance from O_j can be small when the boxes touch at a corner. On the lattice, cells that are only diagonal neighbours share no site, so such contacts do not occur between cells, but the condition has to be read at the level of the boxes used.
