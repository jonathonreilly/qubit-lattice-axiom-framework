## 1. Sobolev on Z^4

**Lemma 1.** For finitely supported u : Z^4 -> R^N, ||u||_{l^4} <= (3/2) ||grad u||_{l^2}, with |grad u|^2 = sum_i |u(x + e_i) - u(x)|^2.

*Proof.* It is enough to treat the scalar |u|, since | |u(x + e_i)| - |u(x)| | <= |u(x + e_i) - u(x)|.

Step 1. For finitely supported w >= 0, ||w||_{4/3} <= prod_i ||d_i w||_1^{1/4}. Indeed w(x) <= sum_t |d_i w(x + t e_i)| =: g_i(x with coordinate i removed), so w(x)^{4/3} <= prod_i g_i^{1/3}. The sum over x of a product of four functions, each independent of one coordinate, and each raised to the power 1/3, is at most prod_i (sum g_i)^{1/3}: sum over the coordinates one at a time and use Holder with exponents (3, 3, 3) on the three factors that depend on that coordinate. This gives ||w||_{4/3}^{4/3} <= prod_i ||d_i w||_1^{1/3}.

Step 2. Put w = |u|^3. Then |d_i w| <= (3/2) (u(x)^2 + u(x + e_i)^2) |d_i u|, since |a^3 - b^3| = |a - b| (a^2 + ab + b^2) <= (3/2) |a - b| (a^2 + b^2). So by Cauchy-Schwarz ||d_i w||_1 <= 3 ||u||_4^2 ||d_i u||_2. And ||w||_{4/3} = ||u||_4^3. Therefore ||u||_4^3 <= 3 ||u||_4^2 prod_i ||d_i u||_2^{1/4}, so ||u||_4 <= 3 prod_i ||d_i u||_2^{1/4} <= 3 (sum_i ||d_i u||_2^2 / 4)^{1/2} = (3/2) ||grad u||_2, by the inequality of the means.  []

**Corollary 2 (torus).** For u on the torus of side N in Z^4, with mean zero, ||u||_{l^4} <= C_1 ||grad u||_{l^2}, with C_1 absolute.

*Proof.* Lift u to a periodic function on Z^4. Let psi be a cutoff equal to 1 on one period cell, supported in the cube of three period cells per axis, with |grad psi| <= 2 / N. Lemma 1 for psi u gives ||u||_{l^4(cell)} <= (3/2) ||grad(psi u)||_2 <= C ( ||grad u||_{l^2(torus)} + N^{-1} ||u||_{l^2(torus)} ). The smallest non-zero eigenvalue of the lattice Laplacian on the torus is 4 sin^2(pi / N) >= 4 N^{-2} for N >= 2, so ||u||_2 <= (N / 2) ||grad u||_2 at mean zero.  []

## 2. A gauge on a box

Let D be a box in Z^4 whose sides are N_i, with all N_i comparable to N, and a a real or Lie-algebra-valued (linear, componentwise) field on the links of D. da is the lattice curl on the plaquettes of D.

**Lemma 3.** There is a scalar lambda on the sites of D such that a' = a + d lambda satisfies

    ||a'||_{l^4(D)} <= C_2 ||da||_{l^2(D)},    ||a'||_{l^2(D)} <= C_2 N ||da||_{l^2(D)},

with C_2 depending only on the ratios of the sides. The map da -> a' is linear.

*Proof.* Extend a to the torus T of side 2 N_i by the group generated by the reflections R_i in the faces of D, as a pull-back: for a link b, a(R_i b) := a(b) with the orientation of b carried along, so a link along axis i changes sign relative to the positive orientation of that axis, and other links keep their value. Links lying in a reflection plane are fixed by R_i, and they are not along axis i, so the definition is consistent. Pull-back commutes with d, so d a_T is the reflected extension of da, and each plaquette of T is the image of a plaquette of D. The number of images of one plaquette is at most 2^4, so ||d a_T||^2_{l^2(T)} <= 16 ||da||^2_{l^2(D)}.

The mean of the component of a_T along axis i is zero, by the sign change under R_i. So a_T has no harmonic part on T. Solve Delta lambda = - d^* a_T on T, which is possible since the right side has mean zero, and put a'_T = a_T + d lambda. Then d^* a'_T = 0 and d a'_T = d a_T. On the torus, in Fourier space, the lattice identity ||grad b||^2 = ||db||^2 + ||d^* b||^2 holds for every link field b, since lattice derivatives commute. So ||grad a'_T||_2 = ||d a_T||_2. Each component of a'_T has mean zero, since gauge terms have mean zero and a_T has mean zero. Corollary 2 and the eigenvalue bound, applied by components, give the two bounds on T. The solution lambda is unique up to a constant and invariant under the reflections, so its restriction to D gauges a itself on D. The bounds restrict to D. Linearity is clear.  []

**Corollary 4 (Poincare on a box, for scalars).** For a scalar zeta on a box D as above, ||zeta - mean||_{l^2(D)} <= C_2 N ||d zeta||_{l^2(D)}.

*Proof.* The even reflection of zeta to T has the same mean, and the eigenvalue bound applies.  []

## 3. Union of boxes

**Lemma 5 (l^p Poincare on a box).** For a scalar zeta on a box D of sides at most N, of mean zero, and 1 <= p < infinity, ||zeta||_{l^p(D)} <= C N ||d zeta||_{l^p(D)}, with C absolute.

*Proof.* zeta(x) = |D|^{-1} sum_w 1[x + w in D] (zeta(x) - zeta(x + w)). For fixed w, zeta(x) - zeta(x + w) is a sum of |w|_1 values of d zeta on an axial path, which stays in D since D is a box, and which is a fixed translate of one path as x varies. So Minkowski's inequality in x gives ||1[x, x + w in D] (zeta(x) - zeta(x + w))||_{l^p} <= |w|_1 ||d zeta||_{l^p(D)}. Summing over the at most 2^4 |D| values of w, and using |w|_1 <= 4 N, gives the claim.  []

**Lemma 6.** Let Omega = D_1 u ... u D_m be a finite union of boxes of comparable sides N, ordered so that each intersection O_j := D_j n (D_1 u ... u D_{j-1}) is a box of comparable sides. Then the conclusions of Lemma 3 hold on Omega, with a constant depending on m and the ratios only, and the map da -> a' is linear.

*Proof.* Induct on j. Let a'_1 be the gauge on D_1 u ... u D_{j-1} and a'_2 the gauge of Lemma 3 on D_j, both for the same a. On O_j they differ by a gradient, a'_1 - a'_2 = d zeta, and we take zeta of mean zero on O_j. Then ||d zeta||_{l^p(O_j)} <= ||a'_1||_{l^p(O_j)} + ||a'_2||_{l^p(O_j)} for p = 2, 4, and by Lemma 5, ||zeta||_{l^p(O_j)} <= C N ||d zeta||_{l^p(O_j)}. Extend zeta to D_j by reflection across the faces of O_j, and multiply by a cutoff equal to 1 on O_j, vanishing on the faces of D_j that meet the previous boxes outside O_j, with gradient at most C / N; call the result zeta_E. Put a' := a'_2 + d zeta_E on D_j and a' := a'_1 on the previous boxes. They agree on O_j. Reflection preserves the l^p norm of a function and of its gradient, up to a bounded multiplicity. The cutoff adds at most C N^{-1} ||zeta||_{l^p}, which is at most C ||d zeta||_{l^p(O_j)}. So ||a'||_{l^p(D_j)} <= C ( ||a'_1||_{l^p} + ||a'_2||_{l^p} ) for p = 2, 4, and with Lemma 3 this bounds both by ||da||_{l^2(Omega)}, with the factor N for p = 2.  []

## 4. Any finite pattern of cells, by Cech collating

**Definition.** Around a face of the cell complex of codimension d there are 2^d cells, the vertices of a cube Q_d. For the set S of cells in Omega, K(S) has vertices S, an edge for each pair of face-adjacent cells of S, and a square for each 2-face of Q_d with all four in S. The local pattern at an edge (d = 3) is good if every component of K(S) is simply connected. At a vertex (d = 4) it is good if the same holds, if its facets are good, and if it is not an antipodal pair removed with nothing else.

**Lemma 7.** Let Omega be a finite union of cells, with the crossing links between face-adjacent cells, N the side of a cell, and all local patterns good. For a link field a on Omega there is a single-valued a'' with d a'' = da, ||a''||_{l^4(Omega)} <= C ||da||_{l^2(Omega)} and ||a''||_{l^2(Omega)} <= C N ||da||_{l^2(Omega)}, with C depending on the pattern only, not on N, and linear in da. Also a -> a'' is bounded on l^2, ||a''||_{l^2} <= C ||a||_{l^2}, uniformly in N: every step of the proof bounds its output by the l^2 norm of its input, the box gauge of Lemma 3 being a projection on the reflected torus. If Omega is simply connected, a'' is gauge equivalent to a. Otherwise a'' - a is a closed form, possibly with periods.

**This is false without the condition on local patterns, even for a simply connected Omega.** Let two cells be removed that touch along an edge, with offset (1, 1, 1, 0). The six other cells around the edge form a ring, and a loop around it has length 6 at each of the N points of the edge. With a closed form omega around the ring, |omega| of order 1 / r, a := h(t) omega has energy of order h^2 and ||a||^4_{l^4} of order h^4 N in every gauge, since each loop forces a link of size h / 6.

*Proof.* **Two earlier proofs here were false.** The first used thickened cells without corners, and a partition of unity subordinate to them with |d h_k| <= C / N: where four cells meet at a face of codimension 2 there is no such partition. The second used Whitney charts down to the lattice scale, and fluxes accumulate along the tangential directions. Charts indexed by the vertices of the cell lattice at scale N do it.

*Step 1: a chart.* For a vertex v and a component sigma of K(S_v), let U be the union, over cells z in sigma, of the orthant box of side 5 N / 8 in z with corner v, with the crossing links between face-adjacent cells of sigma. By exhaustion, exact (`checks/hard-core--independent_shelling.py`), every component of a good pattern has a shelling: an order of its vertices in which each z has a non-empty set I of directions with an earlier neighbour, and z + J is earlier for every non-empty J in I. Build the gauge along it. Let P be the earlier part, with gauge a'_P = a + d lambda_P. The orthants z + J, J in I, with z, form a lattice box B, since they are all present; let a_B = a + d lambda_B be the gauge of Lemma 3 on B. On O := B minus the orthant z, which is simply connected, a'_P - a_B = d zeta. Extend zeta to z by the folding map, with q_i the distance from the apex along direction i and t := min_{i in I} q_i: Phi(q) has coordinates q_i - 2 t for i in I, and the others unchanged. It is continuous, it is the identity at t = 0, it is piecewise affine, injective on each of the |I| pieces with determinant of modulus one, and it maps z into O, since a coordinate i achieving the minimum goes to -t, and all sign patterns that occur are in O. On the lattice round Phi to the nearest site; neighbouring sites go to sites at bounded distance, joined by paths in O, so ||d(zeta o Phi)||_{l^p(z)} <= C ||d zeta||_{l^p(O)}, and the same in the sup norm. Put a' := a_B + d(zeta o Phi) on z, and keep a'_P on P. It is a gauge of a on P u z. So ||a'||_{l^p(U)} <= C ||da||_{l^2(U)} for p = 4, ||a'||_{l^2(U)} <= C N ||da||_{l^2(U)}, in at most 16 steps. This is where good patterns are used, and a pattern with a ring has no shelling.

*Step 2: partition.* On a cell z with corners v, let w_v(q) := prod_i psi(|q_i - v_i| / N), with psi = 1 on [0, 3/8] and 0 beyond 5/8, and h_{v,sigma} := w_v / sum over corners of z of w, with sigma the component containing z. Across a crossing link from z to z', a corner of z off their common face has w_v = 0 there, and a corner on the common face is a corner of z' with the same sigma. So |d h| <= C / N. At a corner where four cells of Omega meet they are all in one chart.

*Step 3: gluing, at scale N only.* Charts that meet in a cell overlap there in a box, of side comparable to N, and two charts can have several such boxes, joined by crossing links or not: index zeta by the connected components of the overlap, each a finite chain of boxes. On it a_i - a_j = d zeta_ij, zeta_ij of mean zero, and by Lemma 5 along the chain, ||zeta_ij||_{l^p} <= C N (||a_i||_{l^p} + ||a_j||_{l^p}), p = 2, 4. Constants: on a triple overlap, c_ijk := zeta_ij + zeta_jk + zeta_ki is a constant, with |c_ijk| <= C N^{-1} ||zeta||_{l^4} <= C ||da||_{l^2(local)}, by Holder at volume N^4. Since a is a single field, the c_ijk are a coboundary on the finite nerve of the charts; choose b linear in c, with |b| <= C |c|, C depending on the nerve only, and replace zeta_ij by zeta_ij - b_ij, so that c = 0 on triple overlaps, though the sum of zeta around a cycle of the nerve need not vanish. On chart i put mu_i := sum_k h_k zeta_ik, and a'' := a_i - d mu_i. On an overlap, mu_i - mu_j = zeta_ij, so a'' is single valued. Bound: ||d mu_i||_{l^p} <= sum_k ( ||d zeta_ik||_{l^p} + (C / N) ||zeta_ik||_{l^p} ) <= C sum_k ||a_k||_{l^p}, with the shift by b contributing C N^{-1} |b| N^{4 / p}, which is C |b| at p = 4, and C N |b| at p = 2. If Omega is simply connected, a'' is gauge equivalent to a. Otherwise a'' - a is a closed form, possibly with periods. *The l^{4/3} -> l^2 form:* the same proof, with the box gauge a' = K * f, |K(w)| <= C (1 + |w|)^{-3}, and Lemma 2 of `lattice_gehring_hls.md`, gives ||a''||_{l^2(Omega)} <= C ||da||_{l^{4/3}(Omega)}. ## 5. A pointwise bound under a Morrey decay

**Lemma 8.** Let Omega be a box of side comparable to N, or a chart of Step 1 of Lemma 7, and let f = da obey, for every link l of Omega and 1 <= r <= 4 N,

    sum over plaquettes p in Omega with |p - l| <= r of |f_p|^2 <= A^2 (r / N)^{2 sigma},     0 < sigma <= 1/2.

Then the gauge a' of Lemma 6 obeys sup |a'| <= C A N^{-sigma}, with C depending on m, on the ratios and on sigma only.

*Proof.* On one box, in the proof of Lemma 3, d^* a'_T = 0 and d a'_T = d a_T, so a'_T = G_T * d^* (d a_T), with G_T the Green's function on the torus at mean zero; so a' = K * f_T, with |K(w)| <= C (1 + |w|)^{-3}, by Lemma 2b of `lattice_gehring_hls.md`. f_T is the reflected extension of f. A reflected image of a plaquette p of D is at least as far from a point of D as p is, since D is convex and each reflection is across a face of D. So, with at most 16 images per plaquette, and splitting into dyadic shells about l,

    |a'(l)| <= C sum_{j >= 0, 2^j <= 4 N} 2^{-3 j} ( 2^{4 j} )^{1/2} ( sum over p in D, |p - l| <= 2^{j + 1}, of |f_p|^2 )^{1/2} <= C A sum_j 2^{-j} ( 2^j / N )^sigma <= C A N^{-sigma},

where the last step uses sigma <= 1/2. For a chart, Step 1 of Lemma 7 holds in the sup norm: sup |d(zeta o Phi)| <= C sup |d zeta| <= C ( sup |a'_P| + sup |a_B| ) at each of at most 16 steps.  []

## Hostile check

(a) In Lemma 3 the identity ||grad b||^2 = ||db||^2 + ||d^* b||^2 is on the torus, where it is exact in Fourier space; it is not used on D, which is why a re-entrant corner of a region does no harm: only boxes occur. (b) Plaquettes in a reflection plane have fewer images than interior ones, which only helps the bound 16. (c) In Lemma 6 the order of the boxes and the condition on O_j are a restriction, used only inside one thickened cell, where it holds. Lemma 7 needs no order on the pattern. (d) The constants in Lemma 3 do not depend on the lattice scale N, and that is the point: they are uniform in the depth of the fine lattice. (e) In Lemma 6 the cutoff must vanish where D_j meets earlier boxes outside O_j. The hypothesis that O_j is the whole intersection makes that set disjoint from O_j, but its distance from O_j can be small when the boxes touch at a corner. On the lattice, cells that are only diagonal neighbours share no site, so such contacts do not occur between cells, but the condition has to be read at the level of the boxes used. (f) In Lemma 8, the bound does not improve past N^{-1/2} as stated, because of sigma <= 1/2; a consumer that needs more must not use this form. The balls in the hypothesis are cut by Omega, and paths in a box are straight, so within a box the distance is the one in the hypothesis.
