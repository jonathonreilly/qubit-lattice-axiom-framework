## 3. The raw saddle supplied by S5 at switches one

Fix one admitted determining set and its fine reference.
Use physical bilinear transposes throughout.
Write $`A`$ for S5's linearizing field and $`h`$ for its raw logarithmic field.
Set

```math
L=\ell Q,\qquad K=\Delta_a=G_0^{-1},\qquad
B_0=G_0-G_0Q^Tq_0QG_0,\qquad
\Xi(A)=\Theta_JA-\mathbb V'^J(A).
\tag{2}
```

Here $`B_0`$ denotes an operator, not a datum.
Porting I 13585–13589, 13723–13783 and layer-thm-part (49), S5 at switches one gives

```math
A+j=H_0b+B_0\Xi(A).
\tag{3}
```

Thus its unpreconditioned saddle is

```math
K(A+j)-\Theta_JA+\mathbb V'^J(A)-L^T\lambda=0,
\qquad L(A+j)=b.
\tag{4}
```

This is a constrained equation, not inversion of the singular operator $`B_0`$.

```math
K(A+j)-\Xi(A)
 =Q^Tq_0\bigl(\ell^{-1}b-QG_0\Xi(A)\bigr).
```

```math
\chi(h)=C(\ell h),\qquad q(h)=Lh+\chi(h),\qquad
A=\phi_J(h):=h+H^J\chi(h).
\tag{5}
```

```math
\tag{6}
```

```math
\mathscr S_J(A;j)
```

## 4. Route 1: pull back the whole action before subtracting

This route tests whether the raw equation becomes local after both preconditioners are removed.
Keep the free-current corrections, rather than impose the physical slice identity.
The following identity exhibits the remaining terms explicitly.

Put $`H=H^J`$, $`g(h)=\Lambda H\chi(h)`$, and $`\Lambda=G'RD_U^T`$.
Let $`\Delta`$ be the Wilson action Hessian at the reference.
Thus $`K=\Delta+D_URD_U^T+Q^TaQ`$, as in I 13585–13589.
Using the account's current insertions $`\mathfrak j_J,\mathfrak m_J`$, define

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr G_J(h)={}&
 \langle g(h),\mathfrak j_J(h)\rangle
 +\langle\Lambda h,\mathfrak m_Jg(h)\rangle
 +\tfrac12\langle g(h),\mathfrak m_Jg(h)\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
```

The exact pullback of (7) is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\mathscr S_J(\phi_J(h);j)={}&
 \tfrac12\langle h,\Delta h\rangle+V_0(h)
 +\tfrac12\langle D_U^Th,RD_U^Th\rangle\\
 &+\tfrac12\langle\ell^{-1}q(h),a\ell^{-1}q(h)\rangle
 -\langle H\chi(h),J\rangle+\mathscr G_J(h)
 +\langle h+H\chi(h),Kj\rangle .
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
```

The first two terms are the action expansion with its constant and linear terms removed.
In particular, (10) is not asserted to be the Wilson action at an independent current.

Here is the algebra, using Sussman and Alpoge's definitions at I 13723–13745 and 14156–14183.
The quadratic $`\Theta_J`$ term combines with $`D_3^J`$ to give $`-\langle H\chi,J\rangle`$.
Expand the quadratic term at $`A=h+H\chi`$ and use

```math
KH=\Theta'_JH+Q^Tq_J\ell^{-1},\qquad
\Theta'_J=\mathfrak k_J\Lambda+\Lambda^T\mathfrak j_J
                         +\Lambda^T\mathfrak m_J\Lambda.
```

The terms containing $`q_J`$ cancel the two terms containing $`\mathsf k=(q_J-a)\ell^{-1}`$.
Their remainder, together with the row mass in $`K`$, is exactly the fourth term of (10).
The remaining quadratic cross terms are

```math
\langle h,\Theta'_JH\chi\rangle
 +\tfrac12\langle H\chi,\Theta'_JH\chi\rangle
 -\langle\Lambda(h+H\chi),\mathfrak j_J(H\chi)\rangle .
```

Use $`\mathfrak k_J=\mathfrak j_J^T`$ and the symmetry of $`\mathfrak m_J`$.
The terms $`\langle\Lambda h,\mathfrak j_J(H\chi)\rangle`$ and $`\langle g,\mathfrak j_J(H\chi)\rangle`$ cancel.
The three remaining terms are (9).
This proves (10), including the contribution of the last two terms of the seven-term gradient.
The calculation extends the source's true-pair reduction, I 15544–15629, without setting $`\Lambda H=0`$ prematurely.

The row mass can be absorbed by the explicit multiplier change
$`\widetilde\lambda=\lambda-\ell^{-1}a\ell^{-1}q(h)`$.
This removes that scalar term from (8).
It does not remove $`H\chi`$ or (9).
Their complete reads can reach the changed collar even when the field output lies in the interior.

```math
={}&(LH_{0,s}-I)b+LG_sv_s\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{15}
```

At switches one, all three operator coefficients on the right vanish.

```math

 =L\dot H_0b+L\dot G\bigl(\Theta u-\mathbb V'(A)\bigr)
                    -L\dot H\chi(h).
\tag{16}
```

This formula also holds when the original datum is substituted before differentiation.
The derivatives of $`b,v_s,\chi_s(h_s)`$ multiply coefficients that are zero at one.

It does not justify dropping the response derivative from the whole field equation.
The derivatives of $`b-Lj`$ on the left remain part of the actual row forcing.
If the caller also moves the fine reference, differentiate $`L`$ and every reference-dependent coefficient as well.
Formula (16) alone does not contain those additional chain-rule terms.

In (16), $`\dot H_0,\dot G,\dot H`$ are derivatives of the prescribed decorated sums.

Formula (16) is the explicit row test for the auxiliary-system route.

```math
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix}
\binom{x}{z}=\binom{f}{g}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(A-BD^{-1}C)x=f-BD^{-1}g.
```

## 6. Route 3: divide the difference by the original equation

This route asks whether the term $`C\widehat F_0`$ in B-RAW absorbs the unresolved interior terms automatically.
There is an exact finite-dimensional criterion.
It shows what this approach can and cannot supply.

Work in the actual admitted local coordinates, after fixing residual node gauge.
Suppose the two raw maps have been identified there.
Write $`F_0=\widehat F_0`$, $`\Delta F=\widehat F_1-\widehat F_0`$ and
$`F_0(X_0(P),P)=0`$.
Assume $`A_*=D_XF_0(X_*,P_*)`$ is invertible, where $`X_*=X_0(P_*)`$.
Let $`P_{\rm out}`$ and $`P_\Gamma`$ project the original output pair onto the complement of the collar and the collar.

Then a holomorphic first-order factorization

```math
\Delta F(X,P)=\iota_\Gamma B_\Gamma(X,P)+C(X,P)F_0(X,P)+E(X,P),
\qquad j^1_{(X_*,P_*)}E=0
\tag{19}
```

exists if and only if

```math
\begin{gathered}
P_{\rm out}\Delta F(X_*,P_*)=0,\\
P_{\rm out}\left[
 \partial_P\Delta F-D_X\Delta F\,A_*^{-1}\partial_PF_0
\right]_{(X_*,P_*)}=0
\quad\text{on every allowed parameter direction.}
\end{gathered}
\tag{20}
```

The parameter directions include the independent current and each original switch.
Use the actual parameter manifold if the caller imposes relations.
Do not enlarge or shrink its tangent space for this criterion.

**Proof.** Along $`X_0(P)`$, the term $`CF_0`$ vanishes identically.
Differentiating $`F_0(X_0(P),P)=0`$ gives
$`D_PX_0=-A_*^{-1}\partial_PF_0`$ at the anchor.
Taking the outside-collar value and derivative of (19) gives (20).

Conversely, take

```math
\begin{aligned}
\iota_\Gamma B_\Gamma&=P_\Gamma\Delta F,\\
C_*&=P_{\rm out}D_X\Delta F(X_*,P_*)A_*^{-1},\\
E&=P_{\rm out}\Delta F-C_*F_0.
\end{aligned}
\tag{21}
```

The first condition in (20) makes $`E(X_*,P_*)=0`$.
The definition of $`C_*`$ makes $`D_XE(X_*,P_*)=0`$.
The second condition makes $`D_PE(X_*,P_*)=0`$.
Thus (21) proves (19), with $`C=C_*`$ constant in this chart.
This proves the criterion.

Its collar derivative is the original residual derivative:

```math
D_P\bigl[\iota_\Gamma B_\Gamma(X_0(P),P)\bigr]_{P_*}
=P_\Gamma\left[\partial_P\Delta F+D_X\Delta F\,D_PX_0\right]_{P_*}.
\tag{22}
```

Therefore the required normalized bound still has to be proved for this expression.
Finite-dimensional division supplies no bound uniform in mesh, history or depth.
It also supplies no original-domain chart.

**Failure of route 3.** Division modulo $`F_0`$ removes field-direction derivatives but cannot change the residual on its solution branch.
Its remaining condition (20) is exactly the complete transported kill test, including the response derivative.
Neither choosing $`C`$ differently nor using the implicit-function theorem makes that condition true.
The criterion is useful for testing a future factorization. It is not a proof of B-RAW.
