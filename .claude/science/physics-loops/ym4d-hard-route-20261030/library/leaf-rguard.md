## Route 2: integrate each actual guard boundary

The native coarea chart is available and should be used.

Work first on one regular finite coordinate chart.
Absorb its Haar factors into the density $\rho$.
Suppose $f_\ell$ is smooth there and $\nabla f_\ell\ne0$ on the part being integrated.
For a deterministic bound $w_{\ell q}\le w$, coarea gives

\[
 \mu\{z\in A:|f_\ell(z)|\le w\}
 =\int_{-w}^{w} H_\ell(s)\,ds,\qquad
 H_\ell(s)=
 \int_{A\cap\{f_\ell=s\}}
 \frac{\rho}{|\nabla f_\ell|}\,d\mathcal H^{d-1}.           \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) applies to the regular part of this chart only.
Locally choose a coordinate $x_1$ with $\partial_{x_1}f_\ell\ne0$ and replace it by $s=f_\ell$.
The volume Jacobian is $1/|\partial_{x_1}f_\ell|$.
The graph-area factor for the level surface converts this to $1/|\nabla f_\ell|$ in (5).
Other charts must be partitioned without double counting.
Any critical-set mass, unsupported chart piece or existence exit still needs its own bound.
All earlier indicators and the selected rim-passing condition remain inside $H_\ell$.

A sufficient estimate on these regular pieces would be

\[
 H_\ell(s)\le B_\ell\mu(A),\qquad
 \frac2n\sum_{q,\ell}B_\ell w_{\ell q}
 +\frac{\text{total exceptional loss}}{n\mu(A)}<1,          \tag{6}
\]

with a uniform strict upper bound in the second inequality.
Here each $w_{\ell q}$ denotes a valid deterministic bound on its piece.
Integrating (5), summing (4), and adding the exceptional loss would prove (RG).
This gives the exact conditional surface estimate needed by this attempt.

 D(\xi)=\int 1_A(u,\xi)J(u,\xi)\,du.

\[
 \frac{\int1_A(u,\xi)1_{\{|f_\ell(u,\xi)|\le w\}}

\]

Bounds on the bare Wilson density do not remove the indicators in this denominator.

## Route 3: transport the auxiliary draws inside the accepted record

This attempt changes the auxiliary variables together with the bare field.
It seeks to preserve outcomes by a conditional change of coordinates.
It does not discard or redraw the stored history independently.

Disintegrate the full experiment over the bare field:

\[
 d\mu(U,\omega)=d\nu_0(U)\,Q_U(d\omega),\qquad
 E_U=\{\omega:(U,\omega)\in E\},\qquad
 k_E(U)=Q_U(E_U).
\]

Here $Q_U$ is the actual law of the prescribed auxiliary choices.
Define $A_U$ and $k_A(U)$ in the same way.
For the fixed tree transport write $F_aU$ for the displaced bare field.
A calibration has the form

\[
 T_a(U,\omega)=(F_aU,\Phi_{a,U}\omega).
\]

Consider calibrations nonsingular with respect to the target auxiliary law on each fibre.
If $k_E(F_aU)=0$, such a calibration cannot send positive conditional mass into that accepted fibre.
For either sign this prevents membership in $G_q$.
Consequently this attempt has the lower bound

\[
 \mu(A\setminus G_q)\ge
 \int k_A(U)
 1_{\{k_E(F_{a_q}U)k_E(F_{-a_q}U)=0\}}\,d\nu_0(U).        \tag{8}
\]

To verify (8), fix $U$ with a zero target factor.
Nonsingularity makes the preimage of that target's null accepted set null under $Q_U$.
Almost every point of $A_U$ is therefore lost for this pair.
Integrating this fibrewise statement gives (8).
An empty accepted target fibre gives the same obstruction without a nonsingularity assumption.

Any test determined by the displaced bare field alone is fixed before auxiliary calibration.
In particular, a gauge draw cannot change a failed bare-plaquette test.
Other tests may depend on the auxiliaries; their allowed choices remain part of $E_U$.
SA 17499–17508 proves that the probabilities of all outcomes sum to one.
It gives no positive lower bound, or support stability, for this particular $k_E$.
The affine check (2) addresses only some of its deterministic guards.
It does not establish positive accepted fibres for the required fraction of the actual $k_A\nu_0$ mass.

There is a further cost even if that support problem is solved.
Grant, favourably, an acceptance coordinate $0<r<k_E(U)$ with uniform reference $dr$.
When both masses are positive, the map

\[
 r'=r\,\frac{k_E(F_aU)}{k_E(U)}                            \tag{9}
\]

preserves acceptance and has auxiliary Jacobian $k_E(F_aU)/k_E(U)$.
Since the bare tree map preserves Haar measure, the complete density ratio on this piece is

\[
 e^{-S(F_aU)+S(U)}\frac{k_E(F_aU)}{k_E(U)}.                 \tag{10}
\]

For fixed $U$, the scalar change of variables gives $dr'=[k_E(F_aU)/k_E(U)]\,dr$.
Integrate first in $r$, then use bare Haar invariance to obtain (10).
This needs no derivative of the possibly nonsmooth function $k_E$ in $U$.
Thus conditional calibration can move guard probabilities into the later density comparison.
It does not remove them.
Equation (9) is an analysis of a favourable representation, not a construction of the actual auxiliary chart.
