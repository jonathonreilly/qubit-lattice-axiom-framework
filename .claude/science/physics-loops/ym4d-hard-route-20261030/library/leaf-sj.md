## 6. Route 3: eliminate intermediate outputs before fixing the read

Try the opposite order: integrate the retained graphs first, then form the next conditional read.
This would resolve (1) by composition if the intermediate value were not independently prescribed.
Use the exact ancestral identities from the repository hits in the opening section.
Keep every outside-only factor, the original slice, and the external base measure.

For the two candidate rows, write their joint measure as

\[
 \lambda(dz,dw)
 =\int \rho(dx,ds)\,
       \delta_{A(x)}(dz)\,
       \delta_{B(z,s)}(dw).
 \tag{7}
\]

Here `rho` denotes the retained lower measure, including its weights and slice.
It is not assumed to be a product or normalized.
The version of `z` in both arrows is the same.
Additional prescribed arguments are held fixed throughout.

The exact substitution, ported from Y 75803–75817, says that every nonnegative joint test obeys

\[
 \int \psi(z,w)\,d\lambda
 =\int\psi\bigl(A(x),B(A(x),s)\bigr)\,\rho(dx,ds).
 \tag{8}
\]

If the caller reads only `w`, (8) gives the composed output measure.
It is then legitimate to use the existing composition chart with its lower joint bound.
If the caller reads `(z,w)`, (8) still retains its dependence on `A(x)`.
At a prescribed `z_0`, the lower generating constraint remains.
Substitution has not removed that constraint or made a different link free.

In measure terms, discarding `z` replaces `lambda` by its pushforward under `(z,w) -> w`.
That is a marginal, not the joint measure demanded by this caller.
Disintegrating `lambda` at `z` instead retains its `z`-marginal as the base measure.
Normalizing the conditional kernel does not bound that base density.
This is exactly the representation distinction in PR #10114, `SOFTHARD.md` 534–547 and 584–598.
It remains true when the base is singular and deterministic relations are retained.

There are two genuine reductions.
If the intermediate value is only a weight argument, keep it in that weight, as in (8).
If all upper inputs are already fixed on the retained graph, retain the resulting target relation.
These are the source's test-only and redundant cases, Y 76249–76296.
Neither reduction applies merely because `z` was generated earlier.

One can instead consume the lower prescription first and then solve the upper row.
But this requires the lower joint chart at the specified `z_0`, with a valid upper pivot still free.
This is SJ-PB's unresolved resolution, not a consequence of changing integration order.
A direct bound for a reachable residual kernel would belong to SJ-TWO-ROW.
No such reachable kernel has been identified by the kill test.

**First failed step:** justify replacing the actual joint prescribed read by its composed marginal.
Equation (8) permits this only after removing the independent `z` prescription from the caller.
The task forbids that change, and the exact conditional identity does not authorize it.
