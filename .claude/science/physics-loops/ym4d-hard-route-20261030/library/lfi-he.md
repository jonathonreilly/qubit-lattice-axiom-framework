## 2. What must be bounded

Fix one finite outer history, its frame, genuine exterior `e`, and inner stored data `zeta`.

This is the source convention, Y 75998–76014 and 75766–75816.

Let `R` be the complete outgoing read of the next operation.
It includes outputs used to reconstruct its exterior.
It also includes generated spectator slots that a later constraint prescribes.
A slot used only in a test stays in that test's inverse graph.
It is not a new independent target. See Y 76263–76305.

Write `u` for the remaining free coordinates of a branch.

It cannot change with the external evaluation.
Every solved coordinate is read at its inverse value in every surviving weight.

\[
 D_{\omega,e,\zeta,p}(W,u).
 \tag{1}
\]

Here `p` ranges over compatible evaluations, and `W` denotes the prescribed outgoing read.

every gauge and Gaussian normalizer, and the reference numerator divided by its actual denominator.

\[
 \sup_p |D_{\omega,e,\zeta,p}(W,u)|

 \tag{2}
\]

The notation suppresses fixed genuine exterior data only when the same bound holds uniformly in them.

## 5. Route 2: bound the joint constraint by its full quadratic form

This route avoids a choice of one pivot per row.
Keep every old quadratic, including its mixed terms.
Linearize the simultaneous outgoing and spectator equations in their actual free variables.
Then try to control the constraint directly by its row Gram matrix.

Here is the exact finite-dimensional calculation needed by this route.
It is a diagnostic calculation, not a Yang-Mills counterexample.
Let `a` have the normalized Gaussian law with positive precision `K`.
Write `Sigma = K^{-1}`.
Let `C a = z` be the independent spectator rows, and `B a = w` the outgoing rows.
Assume first that the combined row list has full row rank.
Its covariance is

\[
 \Gamma=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 C\Sigma C^{\mathsf T}&C\Sigma B^{\mathsf T}\\
 B\Sigma C^{\mathsf T}&B\Sigma B^{\mathsf T}
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{6}
\]

Put

\[
 S=B\bigl[\Sigma-\Sigma C^{\mathsf T}
       (C\Sigma C^{\mathsf T})^{-1}C\Sigma\bigr]B^{\mathsf T}.
 \tag{7}
\]

Completing the square in the joint Gaussian density gives

\[
 \det\Gamma=\det(C\Sigma C^{\mathsf T})\det S,
 \qquad
 \sup_{z,w}p(z,w)
  =(2\pi)^{-(r+m)/2}(\det\Gamma)^{-1/2},
 \tag{8}
\]

where `r` and `m` are the numbers of spectator and outgoing scalar rows.
For completeness, write `a=Sigma^{1/2}x` with `x` standard Gaussian.
An orthogonal change in `x` separates the row span of `(C;B)Sigma^{1/2}` from its kernel.
The remaining square map has Gram matrix `Gamma`.
Its determinant in absolute value is `sqrt(det Gamma)`.
Changing those coordinates gives the joint density and its maximum in (8).
Block elimination of `Gamma` gives its determinant identity.
Thus (8) uses the joint law directly. It does not divide an unknown spectator marginal.

A gap `K >= gamma I` bounds `Sigma` from above.
It supplies no lower bound on (7).
The Schur gap for stored **field coordinates** is not a lower bound for this **row covariance**.

An explicit family separates these two questions.
Take `K=I` on two real coordinates, and let

\[
 C=(1,0),\qquad B=(1,\varepsilon),\qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1.
 \tag{9}
\]

Both individual output variances lie between one and two.
The original precision and all its coordinate compressions have gap one.
Yet `S=epsilon^2`, and the joint density is

\[
 p_\varepsilon(z,w)=\frac1{2\pi\varepsilon}
    \exp\left(-\frac{z^2}{2}-\frac{(w-z)^2}{2\varepsilon^2}\right).
 \tag{10}
\]

At `z=w=0` this tends to infinity.
The inverse map is `a_1=z`, `a_2=(w-z)/epsilon`; its Jacobian proves (10) directly.
At `epsilon=0`, the outgoing row becomes the exact graph `w=z`.
Keeping that graph handles rank zero, but does not bound the positive-rank densities approaching it.

For the actual rows, a possible repair would construct a simultaneous right inverse.
It must satisfy

\[
 BT=I,\qquad CT=0,
 \tag{11}
\]

with a quantitative norm or determinant bound paid by the outer volume.
The ordinary disjoint-pivot construction supplies (11) when its selected links avoid every spectator read.
For overlapping reads, try to correct an outgoing right inverse `T_0`.
If `C R_C=I`, the correction `T_0-R_C C T_0` kills the spectator rows.
Its outgoing matrix is

\[
 B(T_0-R_C C T_0)=I-BR_C C T_0.
 \tag{12}
\]

One must invert the last matrix without a depth-dependent loss beyond the allowed site price.
The individual row estimates do not bound that inverse.
In (9), a right inverse satisfying (11) has second component `1/epsilon`.
No fine-field coercivity estimate removes this loss.

This calculation locates the missing estimate for this approach to the actual construction.
The quadratic/coarea route needs transversality after the spectator equations have been imposed.
The known kept-form comparison controls `K`, with its original masks.
It does not control the projection in (7).
The averaging-energy inequality in `sa-2.13-2.md` §6 concerns field values and action.
It contains no lower bound for the derivative of this simultaneous row map.

Keeping a Wilson payment does not supply the missing step either.
A positive scalar gain multiplies the constrained density.
It does not change its rank or its coarea denominator.
To use such a gain here, one would need a quantitative relation between that denominator and the paying action.
No such relation has been proved on these joint stopped fibres.

**First failed step.** Establish the actual uniform row-Gram or right-inverse bound after all prescribed spectator rows.
Even the linear comparison route lacks that bound.
A nonlinear extension would additionally need a chart with a uniform relative derivative error.
This route therefore does not prove SJ.

The family (9) refutes an inference from the available matrix bounds alone.
It does not refute SJ on an actual history.

## 6. Route 3: shorten the joint read using locality

The third route tries to avoid the simultaneous transversality problem.
Replace remote reconstructed arguments by a local reference before forming the joint kernel.
Then use the already available independent row charts on the shorter stock.
Background and carrier locality suggest an error of order `exp(-delta D)` across a buffer of width `D`.
The question is whether that error controls the required positive kernel domination.

First retain the exact averaging graph.
For an old output `u=A(x)` and the next output `w=B(u,s)`, the source proves

\[
 \delta_{A(x)}(du)\,\delta_{B(u,s)}(dw)
 \quad\longmapsto\quad
 \delta_{B(A(x),s)}(dw).
 \tag{13}
\]

This is Y 75803–75817.
Every surviving weight is evaluated at `u=A(x)` in this substitution.
The map `B` reads the assembled current field, including stored and generated slots.
It is an iterated bare average only where all its inputs come from the first average.
Equation (13) is exact, but it does not turn `A(x)` into an independent Haar coordinate.

Try to replace `A` by a nearby local reconstruction `A_D`.
Even an arbitrarily accurate pointwise approximation does not give domination of these graph measures.
At fixed `x,s`, put `w_0=B(A(x),s)` and `w_D=B(A_D(x),s)`.
If `w_0 != w_D`, the singleton `{w_0}` has mass one under the first graph and zero under the second.
Consequently

\[
 \delta_{w_0}\not\le C\delta_{w_D}
 \quad\text{for every finite }C.
 \tag{14}
\]

This calculation applies to an exact deterministic read slot.
Small distance between its values cannot replace equality of its graph.
It does not assert that two such values occur in one actual admissible fibre.
It proves that a response error alone cannot justify the proposed graph replacement.

Keep those graphs exact instead, and try to shorten only the test reads.
A sharp test also lacks the proposed uniform continuity.
In a commuting `SU(2)` coordinate let

\[
 U(a)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{ia},e^{-ia}),\qquad
 \chi_r(a)=\mathbf1_{\{|a|<r\}},\qquad 0<r<1.
\]

For `0<h<r/2`, choose `a=r+h/2`.
Then `chi_r(a)=0`, but `chi_r(a-h)=1`.
The centre changes by `U(h)`, whose distance from identity is `2 sin(h/2) <= h`.
Both relative coordinates lie in the larger box `|a|<2r`.
Thus the supremum difference of the two indicators is one for every positive `h`.
The source's main-shell predicate uses precisely the eigenangle threshold in this example;
see Y 134877–134899.
The calculation concerns the predicate, not a completed counterexample history.

There is a possible integral repair.
The triangle inequality gives

\[
 |\chi_r(a-h)-\chi_r(a)|
    \le\mathbf1_{\{\,||a|-r|\le h\,\}}.
 \tag{15}
\]

In a free Haar slot this band has volume at most `C r^2 h`.
Indeed the radial Haar density is a fixed multiple of `sin^2(a)`, bounded by `a^2` on this small interval.
The interval has length `2h`, and its radius is at most `3r/2`.
The source already uses such free-slot bounds, Y 134850–134876 and 134900–134978.

Here, however, the slot may be prescribed by the outgoing joint graph.
It may also be part of the fixed inner data.
Its original Haar volume is then not its conditional volume in the required integral.
If it is fixed in the band, the band indicator equals one throughout that conditional integral.
If it is solved by a row, its inverse density must be included before any band estimate.
Using (15) uniformly therefore returns to SJ for the constrained stock.

Dropping a failure indicator is harmless only after constructing the actual inverse kernel and its envelope.
Dropping a constraint, or the passed condition used to admit that kernel, is a different operation.
Enlarging a chart might be sufficient, but its joint density would still require proof.

**First failed step.** Turn an exponentially small read error into domination of the exact constrained kernel.
It fails for graph replacement by (14).
For sharp tests the available repair requires the still-unproved constrained density estimate.
Keeping both graphs and tests exact restores the full stock that this route sought to avoid.
This route therefore does not prove SJ.
