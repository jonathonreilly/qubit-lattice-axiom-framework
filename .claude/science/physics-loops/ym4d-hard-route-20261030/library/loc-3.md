## 2. Lemma S: a collared region cannot cut a seam

Proved by Claude, not yet checked by the other model.

**Setting.** A separated sequence \(\Omega_0\supset\Omega_1\supset\dots\supset\Omega_k\) of closed unions of cubes in the torus. Put \(\Omega_{k+1}=\varnothing\). The layer of a point \(u\in\Omega_0\) is \(j(u)=\max\{j:u\in\Omega_j\}\), and \(\mathcal L_j=\{j(u)=j\}=\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}\). A seam is \(\partial\Omega_q\) with \(1\le q\le k\). Separation gives \(\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_0^c,\Omega_1)>0\), so every seam lies in \(\operatorname{int}\Omega_0\).

A \(\mu\)-collared region (SA L9776–9786, with the meet of `a3-close.md` Lemma J(b)) is \(W=\Omega_0\cap\bigcap_bA_b\), with finitely many parts. Each part \(A_b\) is a union of closed grid cubes of side \(\mu\xi_{j_b}\). Its collar contains at least its outermost layer, the cubes of \(A_b\) that meet \(\overline{A_b^c}\), and the collar lies in \(\mathcal L_{j_b}\). Layers of cubes are read in the sup-norm sense (cubes that meet), as for the outline's enlargements \(\sim\).

**(F)** If \(y\in A_b\) and \(\operatorname{dist}_\infty(y,A_b^c)<\mu\xi_{j_b}\), then \(y\in\mathcal L_{j_b}\).

*Proof.* Let \(Q\) be a cube of \(A_b\) that contains \(y\). Suppose \(Q\) misses \(\overline{A_b^c}\). A grid cube \(Q'\) that meets \(Q\) and is not a cube of \(A_b\) lies in \(\overline{A_b^c}\), so it would make \(Q\) meet \(\overline{A_b^c}\). Hence every cube meeting \(Q\) is in \(A_b\). Their union is the concentric cube of side \(3\mu\xi_{j_b}\), so \(\operatorname{dist}_\infty(y,A_b^c)\ge\mu\xi_{j_b}\), a contradiction. So \(Q\) is in the outermost layer, and \(y\in Q\subset\mathcal L_{j_b}\). \(\square\)

**Lemma S.** Let \(W\) be \(\mu\)-collared, \(1\le q\le k\), and \(S\subset\partial\Omega_q\) connected. Then \(S\subset\operatorname{int}W\) or \(S\cap\operatorname{int}W=\varnothing\).

*Proof.* Suppose neither holds. \(S\cap\operatorname{int}W\) is relatively open in \(S\), not empty and not all of \(S\). Since \(S\) is connected, it is not relatively closed. So there are \(x\in S\setminus\operatorname{int}W\) and \(s_m\in S\cap\operatorname{int}W\) with \(s_m\to x\). Then \(x\in\partial W\). For a finite intersection, \(\partial W\subset\partial\Omega_0\cup\bigcup_b\partial A_b\). As \(x\in\operatorname{int}\Omega_0\), \(x\in\partial A_b\) for some \(b\). Each \(s_m\) is interior to \(W\) and lies on \(\partial\Omega_q\). So there are \(u_m\in W\cap\Omega_q\) and \(u'_m\in W\setminus\Omega_q\) with \(|u_m-s_m|,|u'_m-s_m|<1/m\). Both sequences tend to \(x\). Both lie in \(W\subset A_b\), and \(\operatorname{dist}_\infty(\cdot,A_b^c)\le|\cdot-x|_\infty\to0\) because \(x\in\overline{A_b^c}\). For large \(m\), (F) puts \(u_m\) and \(u'_m\) in the single layer \(\mathcal L_{j_b}\). But \(j(u_m)\ge q\), since \(u_m\in\Omega_q\), and \(j(u'_m)\le q-1\), since \(u'_m\in\Omega_0\setminus\Omega_q\). Contradiction. \(\square\)

**Corollary S1.** A collared region that has one point of a seam component in its interior has the whole component in its interior.

**Corollary S2 (no small localisation at a strip bond).** Let \(b\) be a strip bond of step \(t\) with \(b\in\Omega_t\setminus\Omega_t^{\sim-1}\). Its level-\(t\) cell meets a level-\(t\) cell outside \(\Omega_t\), so a point \(x_b\in\partial\Omega_t\) lies within \(2\ell_t\) of \(b\), where \(\ell_t\) is the cell side. Let \(S_b\) be the component of \(\partial\Omega_t\) that contains \(x_b\). Then every \(\mu\)-collared \(W\) with \(\operatorname{dist}_\infty(b,W^c)>2\ell_t\) has \(S_b\subset\operatorname{int}W\). So its localised background reads the label and the data along all of \(S_b\).

*Proof.* \(x_b\in\operatorname{int}W\); apply Corollary S1. \(\square\)

\(S_b\) bounds a component of \(\Omega_t^c\supset Q_t'^{\sim2}\cup P_t'^{\sim4}\cup\widehat Y^{\sim8}\) (SA L17317). Its size has no bound over histories: if \(\Omega_t^c\) has a component that is a cube of \(R\) cells, \(S_b\) is its boundary. This explains the outline's choice. SA L17518 localises the strip background to the whole component \(\Lambda_t^c\cap\Lambda_{k'}\), and YM3_I (II-13-5) to \(Z_t\cap\Lambda_k\cap\Omega_0^\diamond\). By Corollary S2, no essentially smaller collared region exists around \(b\).

## 3. The nest route stops at (N2′)

Proved by Claude, not yet checked by the other model. Units: \(d_t\) counts level-\(t\) cells.

**Proposition O.** Let \(W_0\subset W_1\subset\dots\subset W_N\) be \(\mu\)-collared regions of the step-\(t\) sequence, with \(W_N=\Omega_0\) (no parts) and \(W_{n+1}\subset W_n^{+\mu_W}\), as (N1′) requires. Let \(S\) be a component of a seam \(\partial\Omega_q\), \(1\le q\le t\), with \(S\cap\operatorname{int}W_0=\varnothing\). Then exactly one index \(n_S\) has \(S\cap\operatorname{int}W_{n_S}=\varnothing\) and \(S\subset\operatorname{int}W_{n_S+1}\). Moreover:

- (a) \((n_S+1)\mu_W\ge\max_{x\in S}d_t(x,W_0)\);
- (b) \(d_{\mathbf B}(b,W_{n_S}^c)\le d_{\mathbf B}(b,S)\) for every bond \(b\).

*Proof.* \(S\subset\operatorname{int}\Omega_0=\operatorname{int}W_N\), and \(\operatorname{int}W_n\) increases with \(n\). By Lemma S each \(W_n\) contains \(S\) in its interior or misses it there. Let \(n_S+1\) be the first \(n\) of the first kind; \(n_S\ge0\) because \(S\cap\operatorname{int}W_0=\varnothing\). For (a), iterate the growth bound: \(S\subset W_{n_S+1}\subset W_0^{+(n_S+1)\mu_W}\). For (b), every point of \(S\) lies in \(W_{n_S}^c\) or on \(\partial W_{n_S}\), hence in \(\overline{W_{n_S}^c}\). \(\square\)

**What (N2′) would assert.** By `s210-fix.md` Lemma N′, (N2′) gives \(\|H^{(m+1)}-H^{(m)}\|\le2\varepsilon_Ne^{-\delta_Nm}\). Summing from \(m=n_S\) and using (a),
\[
 \|H-H^{(n_S)}\|\le h_*e^{-\delta_Nn_S}\le h_*e^{\delta_N}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{\delta_N}{\mu_W}\max_{x\in S}d_t(x,W_0)\Bigr),\qquad h_*=\frac{2\varepsilon_N}{1-e^{-\delta_N}}.
 \tag{3.1}
\]
The left side compares the true average at \(b\) with the average of \(U^\circ_{W_{n_S}}\). By (b), the complement of \(W_{n_S}\) comes within \(d_{\mathbf B}(b,S)\) of \(b\). The only cited estimate for this difference is Theorem 6(c) (`a3-derivative.md` Lemma 10.1). It decays only in \(d_{\mathbf B}(b,W_{n_S}^c)\le d_{\mathbf B}(b,S)\).

**A family of histories.** At step \(t\), let one failed plaquette cube lie next to \(b\), and let a straight row of \(R\) failed plaquette cubes lie at a fixed cell distance \(D\). Take \(D\) larger than all the margins of SA L17317, so the two give distinct components of \(Z_t\). Take no older large field, and let the collar and rim tests pass. Each failure condition is open in the field, so this family has positive mass. Then \(W_0\) has at most \(C_0\) cells, with \(C_0\) independent of \(R\). The row gives a component of \(\Omega_t^c\supset P_t'^{\sim4}\); let \(S\) be its boundary. Then \(S\cap\operatorname{int}W_0=\varnothing\), \(d_t(b,S)\le D+C_0\), and \(\max_{x\in S}d_t(x,W_0)\ge R-D-C_0\). So (3.1) asks for
\[
 \|H-H^{(n_S)}\|\le h_*e^{\delta_N}e^{-\delta_N(R-D-C_0)/\mu_W}\longrightarrow0\quad(R\to\infty),
\]
while the cited estimate stays at a size fixed by \(D\) and \(C_0\).

**Consequence.** (N2′) cannot be derived from the cited inputs with \(\varepsilon_N,\delta_N,\mu_W\) independent of the history, for any nest that obeys (N1′). A proof would have to show more than Theorem 6(c). Indeed, \(H^{(n_S)}\) reads only the label and data in \(W_{n_S}\). Take two data with the same label that agree on \(W_{n_S}\). Then (3.1) gives \(|H(\mathbf V)-H(\mathbf V')|\le2h_*e^{\delta_N}e^{-\delta_N(R-D-C_0)/\mu_W}\). So, as \(R\to\infty\) with the geometry near \(b\) fixed, the true background at \(b\) would become independent of the data just across \(S\), at a fixed distance \(D+C_0\) cells. I prove no lower bound on the response, so this is not a counterexample to (N2′). But no input in the repo or in the outline gives such independence. **This is the first failing input of the nest route.**

**The output of Lemma N′ also has the wrong form.** Suppose (N1′)–(N3′) held. Each piece \(t_n\) is assigned to \(W_{n+1}^+\supset W_0\supset S_b\) (Corollary S2). Item 3 of `s210-3.md` §7 asks \(|V_{\mathfrak X}(Y)|\le Ce^{-\kappa'd(Y)}\), with \(d\) the tree length in cubes (SA's \(d^\natural\), L6191–6198). Lemma N′ bounds \(t_0\) by \(4\,{\rm Lip}(\theta)\varepsilon_N\), a fixed number. Over histories with one large component, a cube of \(R\) failed cells, \(d(W_1^+)\ge d(W_0)\) grows at least like \(R^3\). So Lemma N′'s bound does not imply item 3. The same holds for the dependence on a healed cluster \(\mathfrak c\): `s210-fix.md` bounds it through \(d(W_0,\mathfrak c)\), not \(d(b,\mathfrak c)\). So every strip bond of a large retained component next to \(\mathfrak c\) gets an \(O(\varepsilon_N)\) piece that reads \(\mathfrak c\).

**(N3′) against SA's constants.** This is moot, but here is the condition for completeness. Suppose (N2′) held with \(\varepsilon_N\le C'_\mu\varepsilon_ke^{-\delta_WcM\check R_t}\), the size Theorem 6(c) gives at \(n=0\). With \({\rm Lip}(\theta)\le4/\delta_k\) (`s210-fix.md` §6) and \(\delta_k=(A_1/A_0)\varepsilon_k\) (SA L17257), (N3′) reads \(8C'_\mu(A_0/A_1)e^{-\delta_WcM\check R_t}<1-e^{-\delta_N}\). It holds once \(\check R_t\) is large, since \(\check R_t\ge(\log\min_{i\le t}x_i)^r\to\infty\) (SA L17317).

## 4. The walk route: (LOC) as an implication

Method credited to Sussman–Alpoge (SA L17905–17918, L5762–5800) and to YM3_I (II-71-34, Proposition II-155-54, Proposition II-73-32, (II-73-25)). Written for YM4 by Claude; proved as an implication, not yet checked by the other model.

**The form of (LOC) that the consumers read.** A term is a function of the current pair \(P\), of stored variables \(\xi\), and of the history. Its read set is where it reads \(P\), \(\xi\) and the trace of the history. (LOC) asks: every formed term, every carried term after every later move, and every correction \(V_{\mathfrak X}(Y)\) is a finite sum of pieces. Each piece has a domain \(Y\), fixed by its index, and reads only in \(Y\). This is the outline's form: "a term is regarded as a partial map on the fields on \(W\)" (SA L17453). It is what the healed gas reads. Its summands share the output label and the exterior data (SA L17451, L17966), so they are evaluated at one current pair. (Act) reads the regular slots, which "are the same for every label" (SA L17548); these are the pieces with \(Y\subset\Lambda\). Locality in the stored data alone, at different physical pairs, is not needed. Proposition D of `s210-3.md` is consistent with this: the strip Jacobians of \(h\) and \(h^-\) differ, and the difference sits in pieces whose domain meets \(\mathfrak c\).

**Inputs**, for every move of the construction: a step \(\mathbf T\), a preparatory step or a stage of \(\mathbf R\), and the localisation of a strip background. Each has its separated sequence \(\mathbf S\) (SA Theorem 6(a)) and the fixed parameter grid \(\mathcal P\) of `o1-decoupled.md` §2.1, which does not depend on the label.

- (D1) *Decoupled response.* The move's response \(\mathfrak h(s;P,B')\) solves (R9) with the Definition D operators, exists for \(s\in\{0,1\}^{\mathcal P}\) (and is holomorphic on \(|s_\Delta|<e^{\bar\kappa}\) for the bounds), and at \(s\equiv1\) equals the physical response (SA L5762–5800; ledger "1c principal 8 / 1c.10"). In the repo, `dec-field.md` Proposition 5.1 gives it from [PW] (`pw-bridge.md`) and [NL] (`nl-r9.md`). Their own inputs are open as recorded there.
- (D1c) *Component property.* If \(s\) vanishes on every cube outside \(\mathcal K\), then on each wall-component of \(\mathcal K\) the response is that of the problem restricted to the component, and it is zero on a component where \(B'=0\). SA L5795 requires this. YM3_I proves its three-dimensional form: Proposition I-91-59 (L49055; component argument L49131–49165) and Proposition II-70-150 (L49223). Its proof keeps every partial walk sum, with four neighbourhoods of its end bonds, strictly inside one wall-component. Then the local nonlinear maps agree with the component maps near each carrier. In the repo, `o1-decoupled.md` Lemma WC gives the geometric half: a surviving walk has its hull in one wall-component. Lemma C below ports the analytic half to YM4 and Definition D, as an implication from (D1).
- (D2) *Read hulls.* A walk term reads \(P\), the sequence and the datum only in its hull \([\![\omega]\!]^+\) ([RT], `um1-fix.md` Corollary R′; checked as `loc.md` §2 records).
- (D3) *New terms are polymer sums.* Each new term of a move (Gaussian logarithm, normalisers, kept forms, \(z\)-factors) is a finite sum of pieces, each reading only its polymer. This is the exact-locality half of SA (1d) and of YM3_I §"Local Gaussian integration" (L37769 on), not their bounds.
- (SUP) Superposition and the margin (SA L17449–17451), as cited in `s210-3.md` §3.

**Lemma M (Möbius localisation of one move).** Let \(F(P,\mathbf A)\) read the pair \(P\) and a field \(\mathbf A\) only on \(X^{+r}\). For a carried term \(f\) with domain \(X\), take \(F(P,\mathbf A)=f(P\boxplus\mathbf A)\). Let \(\mathcal A\) be the set of grid cubes that meet \(X^{+r}\) (the anchor). Every grid cube that contains a point of \(X^{+r}\) is in \(\mathcal A\). For \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal P\setminus\mathcal A\) put \(\varphi(\mathcal K)=F\bigl(P,\mathfrak h(\mathbf 1_{\mathcal K\cup\mathcal A};P,B')\bigr)\), and for \(\mathcal Y\subset\mathcal P\setminus\mathcal A\)
\[
 \varphi_{\mathcal Y}=\sum_{\mathcal K\subset\mathcal Y}(-1)^{\#(\mathcal Y\setminus\mathcal K)}\varphi(\mathcal K).
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Then, under (D1), (D1c), (D2):

- (i) \(\sum_{\mathcal Y}\varphi_{\mathcal Y}=\varphi(\mathcal P\setminus\mathcal A)=F(P,\mathfrak h(\mathbf1;P,B'))\) exactly; for a carried term this is \(f(P^{\rm old})\).
- (ii) \(\varphi_{\mathcal Y}\) reads \(P\), \(B'\) and the sequence only in \(\bigcup(\mathcal Y\cup\mathcal A)\). Its domain \(\bigcup(\mathcal Y\cup\mathcal A)\) is fixed by \(\mathcal Y\) and \(X\) alone.
- (iii) \(\varphi_{\mathcal Y}=0\) for \(\mathcal Y\ne\varnothing\) unless every cube of \(\mathcal Y\) lies in a wall-component of \(\mathcal Y\cup\mathcal A\) that meets \(\mathcal A\), and \(B'\ne0\) on that union.
- (iv) If \(\varphi\) extends holomorphically to \(|s_\Delta|<e^{\bar\kappa}\) for \(\Delta\in\mathcal Y\), with supremum \(b_\varphi\), then \(|\varphi_{\mathcal Y}|\le b_\varphi(e^{\bar\kappa}-1)^{-\#\mathcal Y}\).

*Proof.* (i) is Möbius inversion on the finite Boolean lattice of subsets of \(\mathcal P\setminus\mathcal A\): \(\varphi(\mathcal K)=\sum_{\mathcal Y\subset\mathcal K}\varphi_{\mathcal Y}\); take \(\mathcal K=\mathcal P\setminus\mathcal A\). At that point \(s\equiv1\), so (D1) gives the physical response and \(P\boxplus\mathfrak h=P^{\rm old}\).

(ii) At \(s=\mathbf 1_{\mathcal K\cup\mathcal A}\) a walk survives only if every cube that meets its hull lies in \(\mathcal K\cup\mathcal A\); the hull is covered by those cubes. So by (D2) every surviving walk reads inside \(\bigcup(\mathcal K\cup\mathcal A)\). By (D1c) the response there is that of the restricted problems on the wall-components of \(\mathcal K\cup\mathcal A\), which read only inside those components. \(F\) reads only \(X^{+r}\subset\bigcup\mathcal A\). (iii) Let \(\Delta\in\mathcal Y\) lie in a wall-component \(\mathcal C\) of \(\mathcal Y\cup\mathcal A\) that misses \(\mathcal A\). For \(\mathcal K\subset\mathcal Y\setminus\{\Delta\}\), the wall-component of \(\Delta\) in \(\mathcal K\cup\{\Delta\}\cup\mathcal A\) lies in \(\mathcal C\), so it misses \(\mathcal A\). Adding \(\Delta\) merges no component that meets \(\mathcal A\), since \(\Delta\)'s own component misses \(\mathcal A\). By (D1c) the response on the components that meet \(\mathcal A\) does not change. \(F\) reads only those, because no point of \(X^{+r}\) lies in a cube outside \(\mathcal A\). So \(\varphi(\mathcal K\cup\{\Delta\})=\varphi(\mathcal K)\), and the terms of (4.1) cancel in pairs. If \(B'=0\) on \(\bigcup(\mathcal Y\cup\mathcal A)\), then by (D1c) every response in (4.1) vanishes on the components that meet \(\mathcal A\). So all \(\varphi(\mathcal K)\) are equal, and (4.1) vanishes for \(\mathcal Y\ne\varnothing\).

(iv) \(\varphi_{\mathcal Y}=\bigl(\prod_{\Delta\in\mathcal Y}\delta_\Delta\bigr)\varphi\), with \(\delta_\Delta g=g|_{s_\Delta=1}-g|_{s_\Delta=0}\). For \(g\) holomorphic on \(|z|<\rho\), \(\rho>1\), Cauchy's formula gives \(g(1)-g(0)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z|=\rho'}\frac{g(z)}{z(z-1)}dz\), so \(|g(1)-g(0)|\le\sup|g|/(\rho'-1)\) for \(1<\rho'<\rho\). Apply this in each variable of \(\mathcal Y\) in turn, and let \(\rho'\to e^{\bar\kappa}\). \(\square\)
