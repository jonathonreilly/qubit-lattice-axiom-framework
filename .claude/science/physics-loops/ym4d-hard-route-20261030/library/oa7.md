## 4. A first-response estimate that does follow

Fix one admitted determining sequence, reference pair and all fine parameters.
Retain [PW], [NL] and the adopted [RED] specification, checked in `../gates/pw-bridge-gate.md`.
The specification is also recorded in `red.md`; no off-physical Euler identity is used.
Let \(\dot B'\) be supported in the step-data carriers \(E\subset\Omega_k\), with \(\|\dot B'\|_\infty\le u\).
Write \(\mathcal B=Ce^{16\bar\kappa}\) for the operator bound, distinct from `d4-chart.md`'s gauge bound \(B\).
The two differentiated fixed-point equations are
\[
 (I+\mathfrak G W_s'(a))\dot a=H_1\dot B',\qquad
 (I+H\mathcal C'(h))\dot h=\dot a.
 \tag{1}
\]
Their perturbation norms are at most \(1/8\) and \(1/4\) in every admitted weight.
The Neumann series therefore give
\[
 \|\dot h\|_{X,w}\le\frac{32\mathcal B}{21}\|\dot B'\|_{Y,w},
 \qquad w(y)=e^{\lambda d(y,E)},\quad\lambda=\tau_L/16.
 \tag{2}
\]
This holds throughout the contraction domain, not only at zero datum.
It is Proposition 5.1 of `dec-field.md`, with the distance weight of `ld-decoupled.md`.

In layer \(p\), write \(\xi_p=L^{p-k}\) and \(\theta_L=\lambda\min(M,\rho)\ge16\log L\).
The checked layer-crossing argument gives
\[
 \xi_p|\dot h|+\xi_p^2|\nabla_{\mathbf U}\dot h|
 \le C u e^{-\theta_L(k-p-1)_+}.
 \tag{3}
\]
In level-\(i\) units this also bounds the direct potential and gradient responses by \(C\sigma_i u\).
Use \(L^{2n}e^{-\theta_L(n-1)_+}\le L^2\), \(n=k-p\), and \(\sigma_i^2\le\sigma_i\).
The exponential-coordinate tangent has the same value bound.
Indeed its extra factor is \(\int_0^1\operatorname{Ad}(e^{it\eta h})\,dt\), bounded on this small chart.
For the window multiplier derivative in (WF3), the tangent is exactly \(\zeta h\).
The checked (LD) estimate applies directly in that case.

Let \(c\) be a raw determining row of level \(q\le i\), including its full contour carrier.
Let \(b_c\) be the logarithm of its actual nonlinear average relative to the fixed real reference.
The admitted average estimate gives
\[
 |\dot b_c|\le C u\frac{\xi_c}{\xi_p}e^{-\theta_L(k-p-1)_+},
 \qquad \xi_c=L^{q-k}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Adjacent-layer carriers cost a fixed factor depending on \(L\).
For \(n=k-p\ge0\), the elementary bound
\[
 L^n e^{-\theta_L(n-1)_+}\le L
\]
follows separately at \(n=0\) and \(n\ge1\), since \(\theta_L\ge\log L\).
As \(\xi_c\le\sigma_i\) and \(r_{B,i}\ge c\alpha_i\), this proves
\[
 \boxed{\ |\dot b_c|/r_{B,i}\le C\sigma_i\alpha_i^{-1}u.\ }
 \tag{5}
\]
Thus the normalized datum clause holds for this fixed-sequence substitution.
It uses the nonlinear average, including both endpoint factors.
It does not identify \(\dot B'\) with the tangent of an arbitrary earlier composition.

For a fixed tower, Theorem L(b) also gives its differential version:
\[
 |D\Psi_e(w)[v]|
 \le C_b\left(\sup_{d(x,y)\le2}\|v\|_{1,y}
       +\sup_y e^{-\theta_b d(x,y)}\|v\|_{0,y}\right).
 \tag{6}
\]
Apply its repaired finite-difference bound to \(w+\varepsilon v,w\), divide by \(|\varepsilon|\), and take the limit.
Use the enlarged radii in the check so both points remain admitted.
If \(\|v\|_{1,y}\le h_0e^{-\gamma d(y,E)}\), \(\gamma\le\theta_b\), the triangle inequality gives
\[
 |D\Psi_e(w)[v]|\le2C_be^{2\gamma}h_0e^{-\gamma d(x,E)}.
 \tag{7}
\]
This retains the actual tower metric and current normalization \(l^2\xi'\).
It applies to each erosion rule under Theorem L's containment and chart hypotheses.
It supplies no changed-sequence identification or removed-map distance by itself.

## 6. What would bound \(A_e\) and \(G_e\)

There is a precise conditional estimate, including the gauge derivative.
Use the notation of `d4-chart.md` (3)–(5).
Suppose an assigned path \((\widehat{\mathbf U}_r,u_r)\), \(0\le r\le1\), connects the actual endpoints.
Require it on the entire larger source, uniformly in \(\zeta\).
Freeze \(u_0\) when defining \(\widetilde W_r=u_0\cdot\widehat{\mathbf U}_r\).
Put \(v_r=u_ru_0^{-1}\), \(\gamma_r=\dot v_rv_r^{-1}\), and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T&=\int_0^1\sup_x|\gamma_r(x)|\,dr,\\
 a_e&=\int_0^1\sup_b\frac{\lambda_e(b)}\eta
                                  |\dot{\widetilde W}_r(b)|\,dr,\\
 g_e&=\int_0^1\sup_{b=(x,y)}\frac{\lambda_e(b)}\eta
                    |\gamma_r(x)W_0(b)-W_0(b)\gamma_r(y)|\,dr.
 \end{aligned}                                                    \tag{9}
\]
Each supremum also includes \(P\) and \(\zeta\).
Then
\[
 \boxed{A_e\le a_e,\qquad B\le e^T,\qquad
        G_e\le e^Tg_e,\qquad E_e\le e^{2T}(a_e+g_e).}
 \tag{10}
\]

The first bound integrates \(\dot{\widetilde W}_r\).
The equations \(\dot v_r=\gamma_rv_r\) and \(\dot v_r^{-1}=-v_r^{-1}\gamma_r\) give the second.
For \(D_r=v_r(x)W_0-W_0v_r(y)\), direct differentiation gives
\[
 \dot D_r=\gamma_r(x)D_r+
       [\gamma_r(x)W_0-W_0\gamma_r(y)]v_r(y),\qquad D_0=0.
\]
Variation of constants bounds both propagator factors together by \(e^T\).
This proves the third bound; `d4-chart.md` (5) gives the fourth.

These estimates do not supply \(a_e,g_e,T\) for the actual stage.
A pointwise bound on \(\gamma_r\) alone loses \(\lambda_e/\eta\) in \(g_e\).
A bound on a gauge condition number does not control its covariant difference.
Setting \(v_r=1\) would require identifying the assigned representatives in one common gauge first.
The checked common-reference case does not establish that identification on this remade shell.
