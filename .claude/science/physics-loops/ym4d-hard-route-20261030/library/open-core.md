## 1. What can be reduced

The seven positive endpoint lemmas are not seven names for stage admission. Their domains, maps, currents and estimates differ. No implication between distinct positive endpoints has been established here. Seven separate proof obligations therefore remain in the dependency map. This is not a theorem of their logical independence or of cardinal minimality.

There is also a conflict in a literal reading of “imply all of them.” LOC asks for a universal path. L16 claims a counterexample to precisely that path. Conditional on the counterexample's admission inputs, no consistent theorem package can imply both. The right reduction keeps the obstruction as a test of the proposed method, not as another positive lemma to prove alongside LOC-flat.

Two qualifications matter:

* A smallest set has no invariant cardinality unless admissible core statements are specified. Any finite list can be conjoined into one statement. A stronger universal theorem can also hide all seven conclusions in its hypotheses or definition. Neither operation is a scientific reduction.
* Failure to derive one statement from another does not prove logical independence for the fixed Yang–Mills construction. Below, **U** means separate obligations with no proved implication from either endpoint alone. The tables identify the exact missing match. They do not claim models of the account in which one endpoint is true and the other false.

The seven retained statements below are a sufficient *uncompressed basis* for the seven positive lane endpoints, with identity implications. A smaller non-tautological basis remains unproved. Full DOM contains additional coverage and chart obligations; its Result is not a single eighth equivalent admission lemma.

### 7.2 The common algebra, with its limits

There is real shared structure behind RB and WD, and potentially ARR. It is **not** their domain theorem. On one finite gauge slice, write the actual constraint map as `q`, its derivative as `Q`, and stationarity as

\[
q(u)=d,\qquad \nabla S(u)=Q^{\mathsf T}\lambda.
\]

For a differentiable admitted solution family, differentiate both equations. With

\[
K=\nabla^2S-\sum_a\lambda_a\nabla^2q_a,
\qquad C=Z(Z^{\mathsf T}KZ)^{-1}Z^{\mathsf T},
\]

where `Z` spans `ker Q`, and with any right inverse `E` of the onto map `Q`, one gets

\[
Q\dot u=\dot d,\quad K\dot u=Q^{\mathsf T}\dot\lambda,
\quad \dot u=(I-CK)E\dot d.
\tag{R}
\]

Indeed `dot u=E dot d+Zz`; multiplication by `Z^T` solves uniquely for `z`. If `E` changes by `ZT`, then `CKZT=ZT`, so R is unchanged. This proves the identity in `rb.md` Lemma 11. WD's displayed KKT equation is the same algebra with the sign of the multiplier reversed. The actual substitutions differ: RB has `q=M_{B_E}`, `d=M_{B_E}bar U`; WD has its window determining map and independent filed current. Their lifts, inverses, norms, domains and constants are not thereby identified. ARR additionally needs the unprojected `Q^*lambda` itself.

A path-free linear comparison has an equally exact identity. For invertible matrices `A,B` on the **same identified space**,

\[
A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}.
\tag{I}
\]

Multiplication on the left by `A` and on the right by `B` verifies I. If the defect is supported on a collar and both inverses have summable decaying kernels, the kernel formula

\[
\sum_{x,y}A^{-1}(b,x)(B-A)(x,y)B^{-1}(y,b')
\]

locates the comparison at that collar without a path of solutions. But different determining systems require explicit lifts/restrictions to a common space, and nonlinear residuals require their own bounds. I is therefore a useful common proof mechanism, not a proof of CR, CW or CM.

For example, even a perfect tangent inverse cannot bound the datum lift: `S(x,z)=(x^2+z^2)/2`, `q(x,z)=x/N` has unit Hessian and tangent inverse `diag(0,1)`, but minimizer `(Nd,0)` and derivative `(N,0)`. This hand example shows the missing normalization in R; it is not a countermodel to the actual lattice statements.

### 7.3 Why sectorwise work is not an automatic repair

If T's locally equal data have different sectors, a rule depending only on their common `E` data must return the same endpoint. To allow an endpoint in each sector one must supply the sector as extra information, or retain nonlocal data. Either changes F. One must then prove uniform constants over all sectors represented in the allowed volumes, and compatibility of local analytic branches and the actual filing rules. Proving one fixed sector has a path does not establish this. Sectorwise pointwise field estimates, operator estimates and reality identities can still be legitimate routes to CR, CM, CO or CV, because those statements themselves ask for no path between sectors.
