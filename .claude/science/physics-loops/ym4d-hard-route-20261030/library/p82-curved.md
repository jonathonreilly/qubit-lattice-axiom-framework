## 2. Counterexample attempts first

The target failure is a regular \(U\) at which the weighted inverse needs a constant growing with the number of layers \(k\). A depth factor can only enter through something that accumulates across layers or through a hypothesis that is weaker than it looks. Four candidates were tried. None gives a counterexample; two of them fix the form the hypotheses must have.

**2.1 Curvature against the averaging mass (Nielsen–Olesen direction).** In one layer of scale \(s\), take an abelian constant field: links in direction 2 equal \(\exp(i\eta Bx_1\sigma_3/2)\), all others \(\mathbf 1\), with \(|B|=\alpha s^{-2}\). Its plaquettes are within \(C\alpha\eta^2s^{-2}\) of \(\mathbf 1\), and on a cube of side \(Ms\) its centred potential has \(s|A|\le M\alpha/2\), so it lies in Balaban's form (3.35) of the class with \(O(M)\alpha\). For the charged colour components, \(d^{\mathsf T}d+DD^{\mathsf T}=\Delta_U+\mathcal W\) (Weitzenböck, `a3-derivative.md` (40)) has, at leading order in \(\eta^2|B|\), the continuum spectrum of \(-D^2\) in a constant field shifted by the spin term \(\pm2|B|\); its bottom is \(|B|-2|B|=-|B|<0\). So without the averaging mass the curved field block is not even positive. The averaging mass is \(c_Ls^{-2}\) in the same layer (F0-E). The negative part is therefore \(\alpha\) times the mass, in every layer, with the same power of \(s\). This shows that the regularity ceiling must be a fixed number depending on \(L\). It cannot produce a depth factor, because mass and curvature bound scale identically in each layer and a cell sees at most two adjacent layers. (Heuristic test; the leading-order spectral statement is standard Landau-level algebra and is not used below.)

**2.2 Accumulation along the straightened rows.** A row of height \(j\) is a composition of \(j\) one-step averages. If each step were compared with the flat step in a gauge chosen afresh at that level, the relative errors \(O(\alpha)\) would multiply to \(e^{Cj\alpha}\), a genuine depth factor. In one gauge on the whole read set, the level-\(m\) averaged reference is within \(C_L\alpha L^{m-j}\) of flat, and the site-factor transports between consecutive levels telescope to \(z_m^{\pm1}\) with \(|z_m-\mathbf 1|\le C_L\alpha L^{m-j}\). The product is \(\exp(C_L\alpha/(L-1))\) (Lemma 4.2). A reference realizing per-level errors of order \(\alpha\) at every level would need level-\(m\) plaquettes of order \(\alpha\) for all \(m<j\), hence fine plaquettes of order \(\alpha L^{2(j-m)}\eta^2s_j^{-2}\), which violates the layer-adapted plaquette bound. Within the class there is no such reference.

**2.3 Global holonomy.** A flat connection with nontrivial holonomy around a cycle of the torus, or around a hole of \(\Omega_0\), has all plaquettes equal to \(\mathbf 1\) and is not gauge-equivalent to \(\mathbf 1\). Every cube contained in \(\Omega_0\) carries a gauge with \(A=0\), so the reference is in the class with \(\alpha=0\). A proof comparing with one global flat inverse would fail here. The construction below uses only cell gauges, and cells touching \(\Omega_0^c\) are bare, where the local inverse exists at any unitary \(U\) without a gauge (Lemma 5.0). The inverse then exists with the flat constants. Global holonomy does not enter.

**2.4 A curvature-only class would lose a depth factor in this method.** If only plaquettes were bounded, the axial gauge on a cube of side \(Ms\) gives \(s|A|\le CM\alpha\) but only \(|\nabla A|\le CM\alpha/(\eta s)\): a transverse difference of an axial potential is a ladder of \(|x|/\eta\) plaquettes. That is a factor \(s/\eta=L^j\). The derivative member enters twice below, in the comparison of the scalar second-order block on \(H^2_m\) (Lemma 4.1(iii)) and in the Hölder upgrade (Lemma 6.1). So the route needs the derivative member of the class. The outline's class and Balaban's (3.35) both include it. At a critical reference it comes from the current bound (CMP 99a Proposition 6, (1b) Theorem 2). This constrains the input. It is not a counterexample to P82°, whose conclusion might survive with Coulomb-type gauges; that variant is not needed and not attempted.

**No numerics.** No numeric job was run. A run cannot test uniformity in depth here: one extra layer multiplies the number of sites by \(L^4\), so two layers in four dimensions are already out of reach, and a run on one or two layers would only re-test the flat constants. No step below rests on a computed value.

## 3. The curved local system, its Schur complement, covariance

The device is the local saddle system of `flat-port.md` (8.1), enlarged by the row multiplier of its §9.2, formed at the curved \(U\). Its coefficients are local, and \(R\) is not one of them. This is what makes a localized perturbation from the flat case possible: the open step of `a3-balaban.md` §14, a localized comparison of the scalar projection kernels on changing sequences, is never needed.

**Definition.** Unknowns \(X=(A,h,\lambda,\mu,\nu)\): a 1-form on the bonds of \(\Omega_0\), two site functions, a function on tops, a function on rows. Put \(B_v=d^{\mathsf T}d+DD^{\mathsf T}+K^{\mathsf T}K\) and
\[
 \mathbb M(U)=\begin{pmatrix}
 B_v&-D&0&0&-K^{\mathsf T}\\
 -D^{\mathsf T}&1&A_s&0&0\\
 0&A_s&0&-Q'^{\mathsf T}&0\\
 0&0&-Q'&0&0\\
 -K&0&0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \tag{3.1}
\]
all operators at \(U\), Dirichlet outside \(\Omega_0\). It is symmetric in the pairings of Section 1. Norms, as in `flat-port.md` (8.3) but with covariant differences at \(U\):
\[
 \|X\|_{\mathcal X}^2=\|\nabla_UA\|_2^2+\|m^{-1}A\|_2^2+\|h\|_2^2
 +\|m^{-2}\lambda\|_2^2+\|m^{-1}D\lambda\|_2^2+\textstyle\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\|D_\alpha D_\beta\lambda\|_2^2
 +\|m^2\mu\|_{\rm top}^2+\|\nu\|_{\rm count}^2 ,
\]
with \(m\) the larger endpoint scale on bonds; \(\mathcal X^*\) is the dual space of sources \(F=(f,g_1,g_2,g_3,g_5)\).

**Lemma 3.1 (Schur complement).** Let \(U\) be real. (i) \(A_s>0\) and \(Q'\) is onto. Hence the scalar block \(\mathbb S\) (rows and columns 2–4) is invertible, and its \(h\leftarrow g_1\) block is \(P=1-R\). (ii) \(\mathbb M(U)\) is invertible if and only if
\[
 \Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}d^{\mathsf T}d+DRD^{\mathsf T}+K^{\mathsf T}K&-K^{\mathsf T}\\-K&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
is, and then the \((A,\nu)\) outputs of \(\mathbb M(U)^{-1}\) on sources \((f,0,0,0,g_5)\) are \(\Sigma^{-1}(f,g_5)\). (iii) \(S^\circ\) is invertible if and only if \(\Sigma\) is, and \(S^{\circ-1}(f,b)=\Sigma^{-1}(f+K^{\mathsf T}b,\,-b)\), the outputs being \((a,\lambda)\).

*Proof.* (i) \(\langle\lambda,A_s\lambda\rangle=\|D\lambda\|_2^2+\|m^{-1}Q'\lambda\|_{\rm top}^2\). If it vanishes, \(\lambda\) is parallel along every bond, Dirichlet bonds included. For parallel \(\lambda\), every path transport from \(x\) to a top \(y\) maps \(\lambda(x)\) to \(\lambda(y)\). These transports form one coset \(V_1\operatorname{Stab}(\lambda(x))\) of an abelian stabilizer. The contour mean, the solution of \(\sum_\gamma\log(V_\gamma V^{-1})=0\) near the \(V_\gamma\) (SA 7065), is then \(V_1\bar h\) with \(\bar h=\exp(\text{mean of }\log h_\gamma)\) in that stabilizer, by uniqueness of the mean. So every averaged transport carries \(\lambda(x)\) to \(\lambda(y)\), the iterated transported mean gives \(Q'\lambda(y)=\lambda(y)\), and \(Q'\lambda=0\) forces \(\lambda=0\) at every top. Each site lies in a pillar, which is connected and contains its top, so \(\lambda=0\). This is the gate's argument for `a3-derivative.md` Lemma 6.3, written out. \(Q'\) is onto because pillars are disjoint and the top's own coefficient is a positive scalar times \(\mathbf 1\) (\(W(y,y)=\mathbf 1\) at every level). Solving the rows of \(\mathbb S\) in the order 3, 2, 4 gives \(h=S(g_2+Q'^{\mathsf T}\mu)\), \(\lambda=S(g_1-h)\), \(\mu=N(Q'Sg_1-Q'S^2g_2+g_3)\), with \(N\) existing because \(\langle b,Q'S^2Q'^{\mathsf T}b\rangle=\|SQ'^{\mathsf T}b\|^2>0\) for \(b\ne0\). At \(g_2=g_3=0\) this is \(h=SQ'^{\mathsf T}NQ'Sg_1=Pg_1\). (ii) This is the Schur complement of the invertible block \(\mathbb S\). With sources only in the \(A,\nu\) slots, row 2 reads \(h+A_s\lambda=D^{\mathsf T}A\), rows 3–4 are homogeneous, so \(h=PD^{\mathsf T}A\). Row 1 becomes \((B_v-DPD^{\mathsf T})A-K^{\mathsf T}\nu=f\), and \(B_v-DPD^{\mathsf T}=d^{\mathsf T}d+DRD^{\mathsf T}+K^{\mathsf T}K\). This is `flat-port.md` (8.2); it uses no flatness. (iii) If \(S^\circ(a,\lambda)=(f,b)\), adding \(K^{\mathsf T}\) times the second equation to the first gives \(\Sigma(a,\lambda)=(f+K^{\mathsf T}b,-b)\), and conversely. \(\square\)

**Lemma 3.2 (gauge covariance).** For a gauge transformation \(g\) let \(\mathcal G\) act by \(\operatorname{Ad}g\) at base points: at \(b_-\) on bonds, at sites, at tops, and at \(c_-\) on rows. Then \(\mathbb M(U^g)=\mathcal G\,\mathbb M(U)\,\mathcal G^{-1}\), and \(\mathcal G\) is an isometry from \(\mathcal X(U)\) to \(\mathcal X(U^g)\) and from each consumer norm at \(U\) to the same norm at \(U^g\).

*Proof.* With start-point transport, \((e^{i\eta A}U)^g=e^{i\eta\operatorname{Ad}(g)A}U^g\), and \(d,D,D^{\mathsf T}\) intertwine \(\operatorname{Ad}g\). The contour variables transform as \(W(y,x)\mapsto g(y)W(y,x)g(x)^{-1}\) (SA 7065–7080; the Federbush mean is left and right equivariant), so the transported pillar mean intertwines \(\operatorname{Ad}g\) at sites and tops. For the rows use the exact composition of `a3-balaban.md` §8.5: \(\mathcal M^m(e^{i\eta A}U)=v_me^{ia_m}W_mv_m^{-1}\), \(a_{m+1}=S_{W_m}(a_m)\), \(v_{m+1}=v_m\mathsf v_m(a_m)\). The block means of the ratios \(W(y,x)W_0(y,x)^{-1}\) transform by conjugation with \(g(y)\). By induction on \(m\), the pair \((\operatorname{Ad}(g)a_m,\,gv_mg^{-1})\) solves the recursion at \((U^g,\operatorname{Ad}(g)A)\); it is the solution by uniqueness of each finite step. Hence \(\mathcal Q^{\rm st}(U^g;\operatorname{Ad}(g)A)(c)=\operatorname{Ad}(g(c_-))\mathcal Q^{\rm st}(U;A)(c)\), and differentiating at \(A=0\) gives \(K_{U^g}\operatorname{Ad}g=\operatorname{Ad}(g|_{c_-})K_U\). The masses \(m^{-2}\) and the \(1\) are scalar. All norms are built from \(|\cdot|\), which is \(\operatorname{Ad}\)-invariant, and from covariant differences. \(\square\)

**Locality.** A row of \(\mathbb M(U)\) located over a top \(y\) reads only components within one fine stencil, the pillar of \(y\), and the double blocks of the rows meeting that pillar, with their outer blocks at outward rows. All of this lies within \(a_Lm_y\) of the pillar of \(y\), so at \(d_{\mathbf B}\)-distance at most \(C_L\). Call it the read set of the row. The same holds for every block of \(\mathbb M(U)\), and for each estimate in Lemma 4.2 below with the enlarged read neighbourhood of `a3-balaban.md` §8.5 (width \(a_Ls_j\) about the double block).

## 4. Near-flat coefficients in a cell gauge

**Lemma 4.2 (rows and pillar means; `a3-balaban.md` (8.7), (8.11), (8.12), re-derived).** Let \(Z\) be a cube carrying a real gauge in which \(U=e^{i\eta A_0}\), \(\sup_Z m|A_0|\le\delta\le\delta_0(L)\), \(m\) the local scale. For every row \(c\) of height \(j\) whose read neighbourhood lies in \(Z\), and every top \(y\) whose pillar lies in \(Z\),
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \textstyle\sum_b|K_U(c,b)|\le C_Ls_j,\qquad |K_U(c,b)|\le C_L\eta^4s_j^{-3},\\
 \textstyle\sum_b|K_U(c,b)-K_1(c,b)|\le C_L\delta s_j,\qquad \sum_{c\ \text{of height }j}|K_U(c,b)-K_1(c,b)|\le C_L\delta\,\eta^4s_j^{-3},\\
 \textstyle\sum_x|Q'_U(y,x)-Q'_1(y,x)|\le C_L\delta,\qquad \max_x|Q'_U(y,x)-Q'_1(y,x)|\le C_L\delta\,\eta^4m_y^{-4}.
 \end{gathered}
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Here \(K_1\) is the flat straightened row, which is the straight-segment average \(n^{-4}\sum_{v\in P_-}\sum_{t<n}\eta A_\mu(v+t\eta e_\mu)\), \(n=s_j/\eta\): composing \(j\) unit straight-path steps gives the uniform average of length-\(n\) segments over the pillar, because \(t'L+t\) runs once through \(0,\dots,L^2-1\). This is `flat-port.md`'s \(K=sQ\) of (2.1). \(Q'_1\) is the plain pillar mean. Entries are operators on the colour space, measured in norm.

*Proof.* Straighten \(U\) itself at reference \(\mathbf 1\): \(\mathcal M^m(U)=t_me^{ib_m}t_m^{-1}\), \(b_0=\eta A_0\), \(t_0=\mathbf 1\), \(t_{m+1}=t_mz_m\). The value recursion of `a3-balaban.md` §8.5 at zero reference curvature gives \(|b_m|\le C_L\delta L^{m-j}\) and \(|z_m-\mathbf 1|\le C_L\delta L^{m-j}\) on the read sets, hence \(|t_m-\mathbf 1|\le C_L\delta\). The one-step straightened Jacobian at \(W=e^{ib}\) is holomorphic in \(\log W\) on a fixed ball and equals the unit straight-path matrix \(Q_0\) at \(W=\mathbf 1\) (§8.4 there: the endpoint contour derivatives cancel against the block factors). So \(S'_{e^{ib}}=Q_0+E\) with \(\|E\|_{\rm row}\le C_L|b|L\), \(\|E\|_{\rm col}\le C_L|b|L^{-3}\). By Lemma 3.2, \(S'_{W_m}=\operatorname{Ad}(t_m)|_{m+1}\,S'_{e^{ib_m}}\,\operatorname{Ad}(t_m)^{-1}|_m\). In the product \(S'_{W_{j-1}}\cdots S'_{W_0}\) the adjacent transports combine at each intermediate level to \(\operatorname{Ad}(t_{m+1})^{-1}\operatorname{Ad}(t_m)=\operatorname{Ad}(z_m^{-1})\), a site-diagonal factor within \(C_L\delta L^{m-j}\) of \(1\). Thus \(K_U/\eta=\operatorname{Ad}(t_j)\prod_m(Q_0+E_m)(1+O(C_L\delta L^{m-j}))\). Row and column norms of products of matrices are submultiplicative, and \(Q_0\) has row sum \(L\) and column sum \(L^{-3}\). Replacing one factor at a time and bounding the others by \(\exp(C_L\delta\sum_mL^{m-j})\le\exp(C_L\delta/(L-1))\) gives the second line of (4.1), the factor \(\operatorname{Ad}(t_j)-1=O(C_L\delta)\) included. The first line follows from the second and the flat row and column sums. Multiplying by \(\eta\) gives the stated powers: \(\eta L^j=s_j\) and \(\eta L^{-3j}=\eta^4s_j^{-3}\). The pillar mean is the same telescoping without the Jacobians: each level's contour mean for \(e^{ib_m}\) is within \(C_L|b_m|\) of \(\mathbf 1\), and consecutive levels meet through \(z_m^{\pm1}\). So each averaged transport is within \(C_L\delta\) of \(\mathbf 1\). Every fine site enters one pillar mean of its height with weight \(\eta^4m_y^{-4}\). At an outward row the outer block lies one level finer, by separation, and its gauge bound costs a factor \(L\). This is the computation of §8.7 there, checked step by step; the only change is that the curved and flat Jacobians are compared in one common gauge. \(\square\)

**Lemma 4.1 (coefficient comparison).** Let \(Z\) be a cube with a real gauge in which \(U=e^{i\eta A_0}\), \(m|A_0|+m^2|\nabla A_0|\le\delta\le\delta_0(L)\) on \(Z\). Let \(Z^\circ\subset Z\) be the set of points whose read sets lie in \(Z\). For \(X\) supported in \(Z\), the norms satisfy \((1+C_L\delta)^{-1}\|X\|_{\mathcal X(\mathbf 1)}\le\|X\|_{\mathcal X(U)}\le(1+C_L\delta)\|X\|_{\mathcal X(\mathbf 1)}\). For \(X\) supported in \(Z\) and \(Y\) supported in \(Z^\circ\),
\[
 |\langle Y,(\mathbb M(U)-\mathbb M(\mathbf 1))X\rangle|\le C_L\delta\,\|Y\|_{\mathcal X}\|X\|_{\mathcal X}.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

*Proof.* Write \(D_U=D_1+\mathcal A\) on sites, with \((\mathcal A\lambda)(b)=\eta^{-1}(\operatorname{Ad}e^{i\eta A_0(b)}-1)\lambda(b_+)\). Then \(\mathcal A\) is a bond-local multiplication by a coefficient bounded by \(2|A_0|\le2\delta/m\). The same holds for \(d_U-d_1\), for the component differences \(\nabla_U-\nabla_1\), and for the adjoints. (i) Norms. First differences change by \(O(\delta/m)\) times the field, which \(\|m^{-1}\cdot\|_2\) controls. A second difference changes by \(D_1\mathcal A+\mathcal AD_U\). The product rule writes \(D_1(\mathcal A\lambda)\) through \(\nabla A_0\cdot\lambda\) and \(A_0\cdot\nabla\lambda\), bounded by \(\delta(m^{-2}|\lambda|+m^{-1}|\nabla\lambda|)\); the exponential remainder carries extra factors \(\eta|A_0|\). (ii) Vector block \(B_v\), in form. \(\langle B,(d_U^{\mathsf T}d_U-d_1^{\mathsf T}d_1)A\rangle=\langle(d_U-d_1)B,d_UA\rangle+\langle d_1B,(d_U-d_1)A\rangle\le C\delta\|B\|_E\|A\|_E\). The same holds for \(DD^{\mathsf T}\). For \(K^{\mathsf T}K\) use \(\langle K_UB,(K_U-K_1)A\rangle+\langle(K_U-K_1)B,K_1A\rangle\) and the Schur bound from (4.1), \(\|(K_U-K_1)A\|_{\rm count}^2\le(C_L\delta s)(C_L\delta\eta^4s^{-3})\eta^{-4}\|A\|_2^2\cdot C=C_L^2\delta^2\|s^{-1}A\|_2^2\), summed over the bounded number of heights meeting a bond. (iii) Scalar second-order blocks \(A_s:H^2_m\to L^2\) and \(L^2\to(H^2_m)^*\). \(D_U^{\mathsf T}D_U-D_1^{\mathsf T}D_1=D_U^{\mathsf T}\mathcal A+\mathcal A^{\mathsf T}D_1\). Here \(|\mathcal A^{\mathsf T}D_1\lambda|\le C\delta m^{-1}|D_1\lambda|\), and \(D_U^{\mathsf T}(\mathcal A\lambda)\) is bounded by \(C\delta(m^{-2}|\lambda|+m^{-1}|\nabla\lambda|)\), by the product rule with \(|\nabla A_0|\le\delta/m^2\). This is the place where the derivative member of (Reg) is used. So \(\|(A_s(U)-A_s(\mathbf 1))\lambda\|_2\le C_L\delta\|\lambda\|_{H^2_m}\), the mass part included through (iv); the transposed block follows by duality. (iv) Pillar blocks. Since \((Q'^{\mathsf T}\nu)(x)\) is the transport of the top value to \(x\) in the physical pairings, \(\|Q'^{\mathsf T}\nu\|_2^2=\sum_ym_y^4|\nu_y|^2\). Schur on each pillar with (4.1) gives \(\|m^{-2}(Q'_U-Q'_1)\lambda\|_{\rm top}\le C_L\delta\|m^{-2}\lambda\|_2\), and the transposed block and the mass term \(Q'^{\mathsf T}m^{-2}Q'\) follow. (v) Gradient and row blocks. \(|\langle B,\mathcal Ah\rangle|\le2\delta\|m^{-1}B\|_2\|h\|_2\), and \(\|(K_U-K_1)A\|_{\rm count}\le C_L\delta\|m^{-1}A\|_2\) together with its transpose. The identity block does not change. Every estimate is local, and all read sets of rows located in \(Z^\circ\) lie in \(Z\). \(\square\)
