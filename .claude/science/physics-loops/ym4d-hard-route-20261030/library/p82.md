## 1. Definitions

**Geometry and boundary spaces.** Fix odd \(L\), dimension four, depth \(k\), \(\eta=L^{-k}\), and \(\xi_j=L^j\eta\). A separated sequence has
\[
 \Omega_0\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_k,\qquad
 \operatorname{dist}_\infty(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})>\rho\xi_j,
 \quad \rho=R_1M_1.
\]
Each \(\Omega_j\) is a union of aligned cubes of side \(M_1\xi_j\); \(M_1,R_1\) are powers of \(L\), \(R_1\ge L\). Set \(\Omega_{k+1}=\varnothing\) and \(\mathcal L_j=\Omega_j\setminus\Omega_{j+1}\), with boundary sites assigned by the fixed block convention. A top \(y\) in layer \(j\) has scale \(m_y=\xi_j\) and pillar \(P_y=B^j(y)\), of physical volume \(m_y^4\). Pillars partition the fine sites. Write \(m=\xi_j\) on that layer; a bond or plaquette has the largest corner scale.

Use the outline's default determining set: interior and outward rows, with inward rows omitted at every level. An outward row is oriented from its layer-\(j\) top; write \(y_c\) for the assigned top and \(s_c=\xi_j\) for its length. There are at most \(C_L\) rows per top. Fine variables include the bonds omitted from the *constraint* set. The fine site, bond and plaquette spaces, outer Dirichlet convention, and adjoints are the matched spaces of [flat-port.md](flat-port.md), §§2, 8–9: variations are zero outside their allowed coordinates, and the curl uses the actual inner plaquettes. All derivative norms include their zero-extension stencils. Interior stencils are the ordinary lattice stencils. This boundary matching is part of import I1; an alternative compression is not identified with it.

The distance is the outline's ambient distance
\[
 d(y,z)=\inf_{\Gamma\ni y,z}
 \left(\sum_{j\ge1}\xi_j^{-1}\operatorname{length}(\Gamma\cap\mathcal L_j)
       +\eta^{-1}\operatorname{length}(\Gamma\setminus\Omega_1)\right).
 \tag{1}
\]
The infimum is over connected paths in the torus, including paths outside \(\Omega_0\). All local boxes used below must embed in the torus with their read margins; this is the torus-room hypothesis.

**Colour, pairings and derivatives.** Identify the real colour space with traceless Hermitian \(2\times2\) matrices, with positive pairing \(\frac12\operatorname{Tr}(XY)\), and use its complexification for complex linear sources. Colour vectors have the induced Euclidean norm, group matrices the matrix operator norm, and matrix entries between colour spaces the induced operator norm. Fine pairings have weight \(\eta^4\), top pairings weight \(m_y^4\), and row pairings are counting pairings. Transpose means transpose in these pairings, extended complex linearly.

For \(U(b)\in SU(2)\),
\[
 (D_U\lambda)(b)=\eta^{-1}
   \big(\operatorname{Ad}U(b)\lambda(b_+)-\lambda(b_-)\big),
 \qquad \Delta_U=D_U^{\mathsf T}D_U .
\]
The same start-point differences on components of a 1-form are denoted \(\nabla_U\). There are two curls:
\[
 (d_Ua)_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_\mu a_\nu-\nabla_\nu a_\mu,\qquad
 \mathfrak d_Ua(p)=\eta^{-1}\sum_{r=1}^4
          \operatorname{Ad}(U(b_1)\cdots U(b_{r-1}))a(b_r),
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(a(\bar b)=-\operatorname{Ad}(U(b)^{-1})a(b)\). The latter is SA **PX115**, not PX114. Write \(q\in\{d_U,\mathfrak d_U\}\).

**The actual averages.** For a nearby family, \(\operatorname{av}(V_i)\) is the unique nearby solution of \(\sum_i\log(V_iV^{-1})=0\). The contour family from \(y\) to \(x\in B(y)\) consists of the at most \(4!\) shortest paths completing each coordinate displacement before the next. Denote its mean transport by \(W(y,x)\). With \(x'=x+c_+-c_-\),
\[
 Z_x=W(c_-,x)W([x,x'])W(x',c_+)W[c]^{-1},\qquad
 \mathcal M(W)(c)=\exp\!\left(L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(c_-)}\log Z_x\right)W[c].
\]
All these formulas use their nearby analytic branches. Define
\[
 u_W(a)(y)=\exp\!\left(L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(y)}
        \log[(e^{ia}W)(y,x)W(y,x)^{-1}]\right),
\]
\[
 S_W(a)(c)=\frac1i\log\!\left[
 u_W(a)(c_-)^{-1}\mathcal M(e^{ia}W)(c)
 u_W(a)(c_+)\mathcal M(W)(c)^{-1}\right].
\]
For a fine variation \(v\), put \(W_n=\mathcal M^nU\), \(a_0(t)=t\eta v\), and \(a_{n+1}(t)=S_{W_n}(a_n(t))\). The **raw straightened row** is
\[
 (K_Uv)(c)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}a_j(t)(c)\right|_{t=0}
       \quad(c\text{ of height }j).
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(K=\eta I\) at height zero. This is neither the derivative of the plain iterate nor merely its leading straight covariant average. At outward rows the same recursion retains the outer endpoint's own-level block factors (I5).

If \(\mathsf T(y,x)\) is the product of successive averaged contour transports down the pillar, then
\[
 (Q'\lambda)(y)=\eta^4m_y^{-4}\sum_{x\in P_y}
                    \operatorname{Ad}\mathsf T(y,x)\lambda(x).
 \tag{4}
\]
The branch existence needed in (3)–(4) follows from Lemma 1. Define
\[
 A_s=\Delta_U+Q'^{\mathsf T}m^{-2}Q',\quad S=A_s^{-1},\quad
 N=(Q'S^2Q'^{\mathsf T})^{-1},\quad
 P=SQ'^{\mathsf T}NQ'S,\quad R=I-P .
 \tag{5}
\]
Their existence and the identification of \(R\) are proved in Lemma 2.

**Consumer norms.** Assign fine components to the pillar of their base point, including the neighbouring stencils in its supremum. Adjacent scales differ by at most \(L\), so this and largest-corner weights differ by fixed \(L\)-factors. Set
\[
 \|a\|_{X,w}=\sup_y w(y)\max\{m_y|a|_y,m_y^2|\nabla_Ua|_y\},\quad
 \|f\|_{F,w}=\sup_y w(y)m_y^3|f|_y,\quad
 \|b\|_{Y,w}=\sup_c w(y_c)|b(c)|,
\]
\[
 \mathcal W_\theta=\{w>0:w(y)/w(z)\le e^{\theta d(y,z)}\}.
 \tag{6}
\]
For \(0<\beta<1\), \([\nabla_Ua]_{\beta,y}\) compares like components with base points over \(P_y\), at lattice separations \(\eta\le |x-x'|_\infty\le m_y\). Transport the second component along a fixed coordinate-order shortest lattice path between the base points. Its length is at most \(4|x-x'|_\infty\); include that path and the endpoint stencils in the read neighbourhood. This specifies the “straight path” convention.

For a kernel \(A_{ij}\) written as \((Av)_i=\sum_j\mu_jA_{ij}v_j\), its BGR11 **row** bound here means
\[
 \sup_i\sum_j\mu_j\,\frac{\sigma_j}{\sigma_i}
        \|A_{ij}\|e^{\theta d(y_i,y_j)}\le C .
 \tag{7}
\]
Fine input measure is \(\mu_j=\eta^4\), row input measure is \(1\); amplitudes are
\(\sigma_f=m^{-3},\sigma_a=m^{-1},\sigma_{\nabla a}=m^{-2},
\sigma_b=\sigma_\ell=1\).
The row multiplier \(\ell\) is in counting normalization.

**Local system and localization norms.** Put \(B_q=q^{\mathsf T}q+DD^{\mathsf T}+K^{\mathsf T}K\) and
\[
 \mathbb M_q=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 B_q&-D&0&0&-K^{\mathsf T}\\
 -D^{\mathsf T}&I&A_s&0&0\\
 0&A_s&0&-Q'^{\mathsf T}&0\\
 0&0&-Q'&0&0\\
 -K&0&0&0&0
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad X=(a,h,\psi,\mu,\nu).
 \tag{8}
\]
The scalar subsystem \(\mathbb S\) consists of rows and columns 2–4. Write
\[
 \|a\|_E^2=\|\nabla_Ua\|_2^2+\|m^{-1}a\|_2^2,\quad
 \|\psi\|_{H_m^2}^2=\|m^{-2}\psi\|_2^2+
       \|m^{-1}D\psi\|_2^2+\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}\|D_\alpha D_\gamma\psi\|_2^2,
\]
\[
 \|X\|_{\mathcal X}^2=\|a\|_E^2+\|h\|_2^2+
       \|\psi\|_{H_m^2}^2+\|m^2\mu\|_{\rm top}^2+\|\nu\|_{\rm count}^2 .
 \tag{9}
\]
Sources have the dual norm \(\mathcal X^*\). A *local output norm* restricts the summands in (9) **after** taking derivatives. A supported dual source annihilates every test field whose coordinates vanish on its support. No sharp characteristic function is asserted to act boundedly on \(\mathcal X^*\).

**Regularity hypotheses.** Reg\(_\alpha\) means that every embedded cube \(Z\) of side \(c_{\rm reg}M_P\xi_j\), contained in \(\Omega_0\cap(\mathcal L_j\cup\mathcal L_{j+1})\), carries a real gauge with
\[
 U^g=e^{i\eta A_0},\qquad
 \xi_j|A_0|+\xi_j^2|\nabla A_0|\le\alpha
 \tag{10}
\]
throughout \(Z\), on all stencils lying in \(Z\). Here \(\nabla\) is ordinary difference. The cell geometry below provides margins before using (10).

An \(\varepsilon_1\)-regular default datum \(\mathbf V_0\) has all the outline's test-loop products within \(\varepsilon_1\) of \(I\): these are its level plaquettes with \(\mathcal M(V_{j-1})\) substituted on edges in the finer layer. No inward-row or pinch constraints are added. Theorem P82 uses the A1 representative for this datum.

## 3. Theorems

**Theorem P.** Assume I1–I5 and I8, the default geometry and boundary conventions of §1, the constants chosen in §2, \(M_P\mid M_1\), \(\rho\ge\rho_P\), torus room, and real \(U\) satisfying Reg\(_\alpha\), \(0\le\alpha\le\alpha_*\). For either curl, let
\[
 S_q^\circ=\begin{pmatrix}q^{\mathsf T}q+DRD^{\mathsf T}&-K^{\mathsf T}\\K&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(S_q^\circ\) is invertible, \(K\) is onto, and
\(\Delta_U:\ker Q'\longrightarrow\operatorname{ran}R\) is an isomorphism. The operators
\[
 G=(q^{\mathsf T}q+DRD^{\mathsf T}+K^{\mathsf T}K)^{-1},\quad
 C_0=KGK^{\mathsf T},\quad H=GK^{\mathsf T}C_0^{-1},\quad T=G-HKG
 \tag{14}
\]
exist. For every \(0\le\theta\le\theta_0\), every \(w\in\mathcal W_\theta\), and all sources,
\[
 (a,\ell)=S_q^{\circ-1}(f,b)
       =(Tf+Hb,\,-H^{\mathsf T}f+(C_0^{-1}-I)b),
\]
\[
 \|a\|_{X,w}+\|\ell\|_{Y,w}
   +\sup_yw(y)m_y^{2+\beta}[\nabla_Ua]_{\beta,y}
 \le C^\circ(\|f\|_{F,w}+\|b\|_{Y,w}),
 \tag{15}
\]
\[
 \|D(I-R)D^{\mathsf T}a\|_{F,w}\le C^\circ\|a\|_{X,w}.
 \tag{16}
\]
Every block of this inverse, including differentiated field outputs, has (7). The constant is uniform in depth, mesh, volume and admissible shapes, and serves both curls.

**Theorem P82.** Assume I1–I8 and all their stated admissibility conditions. Let \(\mathbf V_0\) be a real \(\varepsilon_1\)-regular default datum, \(0<\varepsilon_1\le\varepsilon_P\), on geometry satisfying the P82 floors and (13). Let \(U_0=U^\circ(\mathbf V_0)\) be its real axial A1 representative. Then Theorem P holds at \(U_0\), with \(K=K_0\) the actual straightened derivative. In particular it supplies the real-reference linear premise of [a3-derivative.md](a3-derivative.md), Lemma 8.1, including its all-weight reading, Hölder member, \(D(I-R)D^{\mathsf T}\) member and counting multiplier.

## 4. Lemmas and proofs

**Lemma 1 (cells, averages and coefficient comparison).** Every read set belongs to a bare cell or to a cell contained, with read margins, in a Reg cube. Bare cells have \(m=\eta\), \(Q'=I\), \(K=\eta I\). In an interior cell carrying
\(m|A_0|+m^2|\nabla A_0|\le\delta\), the actual averages exist for sufficiently small \(\delta=\delta(L)\). For rows of height \(j\), \(s=\xi_j\), whose complete read sets lie in that cell,
\[
 \sum_b|K_U(c,b)|\le C_Ls,\qquad
 \sum_{c:\,{\rm ht}(c)=j}|K_U(c,b)|\le C_L\eta^4s^{-3}.
 \tag{17}
\]
The column sums range over those rows. The same bounds for \(K_U-K_1\) acquire a factor \(\delta\), and, for pillars with their complete reads in the cell,
\[
 |Q'_U(y,x)-Q'_1(y,x)|\le C_L\delta\,\eta^4m_y^{-4},\qquad
 \sum_x|Q'_U(y,x)|=1.
 \tag{18}
\]
For fields supported in the cell the curved and flat \(\mathcal X\) norms are comparable by \(1+C_L\delta\). If the test field's read set remains in that cell,
\[
 |\langle Y,(\mathbb M_q(U)-\mathbb M_q(I))X\rangle|
       \le C_L\delta\|Y\|_{\mathcal X}\|X\|_{\mathcal X}.
 \tag{19}
\]
All operators and norms are covariant under real gauge changes.

*Proof.* Start with I2's tiles. Enclose each support, both read widths and a margin in a cube of side \(c_LM_Ps_i\). Take \(c_{\rm reg}\ge Lc_L\). A concentric cube of side \(c_{\rm reg}M_Ps_i\) cannot meet three layers, or both neighbours of the tile's layer: this would cross a complete collar of width at least \(\rho s_i/L\), larger than its diameter by (11). If it meets the finer layer \(j_i-1\), use instead the concentric cube of side \(c_{\rm reg}M_Ps_i/L\). That cube still contains the entire read neighbourhood and lies in layers \(j_i-1,j_i\). Otherwise the original cube lies in layers \(j_i,j_i+1\). The first collar likewise puts any such cube meeting \(\Omega_1\) inside \(\Omega_0\); remaining boundary cells are bare. Thus an interior cell lies, with its reads, in a full Reg cube at scale \(\xi_{j'}\), \(j'=j_i\) or \(j_i-1\), and (10) gives \(\delta\le L^2\alpha\). A cell disjoint from \(\Omega_1\) has only height-zero sites and rows, so its two averages are identities in the stated normalizations. I5 bounds every remaining read set by \(a_Ls_i\) and its metric diameter by \(C_L\).

Here is the depth-uniform averaging estimate. Straighten \(U\) itself relative to \(I\):
\(\mathcal M^mU=t_me^{ib_m}t_m^{-1}\), with endpoint gauges understood,
\(b_0=\eta A_0\), \(t_0=I\), \(t_{m+1}=t_mz_m\).
I5 and the finite Taylor remainder give
\[
 u_0:=\|b_0\|_\infty\le C_L\delta L^{-j},\qquad
 u_{m+1}\le L(1+C_Lu_m)u_m,\qquad |z_m-I|\le C_Lu_m .
\]
The factor \(C_L\) allows an outward row's one-level finer outer block. Inductively,
\[
 u_m\le2C_L\delta L^{m-j},\qquad
 \prod_{m<j}(1+C_Lu_m)\le
       \exp\!\left(C_L\delta\sum_{m<j}L^{m-j}\right)\le2
\]
after decreasing the fixed ceiling on \(\delta\). Thus all one-step arguments stay in their analytic balls and \(|t_m-I|\le C_L\delta\).

The one-step Jacobian at \(e^{ib_m}\) is \(Q_0+E_m\), with relative row and column errors at most \(C_Lu_m\), by the finite analytic estimate in I5. Gauge covariance gives intermediate factors
\(\operatorname{Ad}(t_{m+1}^{-1}t_m)=\operatorname{Ad}(z_m^{-1})\).
Their errors are also \(O(C_Lu_m)\). Telescoping the product of Jacobians therefore gives row error at most
\(C_L\delta L^j\) and column error at most \(C_L\delta L^{-3j}\): the relative errors sum as \(\sum_{m<j}L^{m-j}\), not as \(j\).
Multiplication by the initial \(\eta\) gives (17) and its difference version. The bounded number of heights meeting a fine bond is part of I5.

The same product argument makes each transport in (4) \(I+O(C_L\delta)\). Its coefficient is exactly \(\eta^4m_y^{-4}\), giving (18). For arbitrary real transports the last equality in (18) follows from their being colour isometries.

For completeness, the estimates on every block of (8) are as follows. Write \(D_U-D_1=\mathcal A\). Its coefficients have size \(C\delta/m\), and their ordinary first differences have size \(C\delta/m^2\), by (10) and the exponential series. The same zeroth-order comparison holds for both curls. Consequently first- and second-difference norms are comparable, and
\[
 \|(\Delta_U-\Delta_1)\psi\|_2
 \le C_L\delta\big(\|m^{-2}\psi\|_2+\|m^{-1}D\psi\|_2\big).
\]
For the vector quadratic blocks, expand differences as
\(\langle(q_U-q_1)a,q_Uv\rangle+\langle q_1a,(q_U-q_1)v\rangle\);
the \(DD^{\mathsf T}\) block is treated the same way.
Schur's inequality using (17) gives
\[
 \|(K_U-K_1)a\|_{\rm count}\le C_L\delta\|m^{-1}a\|_2,\qquad
 \|K_Ua\|_{\rm count}\le C_L\|m^{-1}a\|_2 .
\]
Indeed its squared scale factor is
\((\delta s)(\delta\eta^4s^{-3})\eta^{-4}=\delta^2s^{-2}\).
For (4), the physical transpose is
\((Q'^{\mathsf T}\mu)(x)=\operatorname{Ad}\mathsf T(y,x)^{-1}\mu(y)\);
hence (18) controls the two pillar blocks and \(Q'^{\mathsf T}m^{-2}Q'\).
Finally \(|\langle a,\mathcal Ah\rangle|\le
C\delta\|m^{-1}a\|_2\|h\|_2\). These estimates exhaust (8), proving (19).

Under \(U^g(b)=g(b_-)U(b)g(b_+)^{-1}\), every insertion transforms by \(\operatorname{Ad}g\) at its base. The defining mean equation is left and right equivariant; the ratios defining \(u_W\) transform by conjugation. Induction in (3) therefore gives the same covariance for \(K\), including outward factors; (4) is immediate. Unitarity makes these transformations isometries in (6) and (9), including their covariant Hölder comparisons. \(\square\)

**Lemma 2 (projection and elimination).** The operators in (5) exist; \(R\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\Delta_U\ker Q'\), independent of the choice of positive top mass in \(A_s\). The scalar subsystem is invertible, and its \(h\leftarrow g_1\) block is \(P\). Moreover \(\mathbb M_q\) is invertible exactly when \(S_q^\circ\) is, with
\[
 \Sigma_q=\begin{pmatrix}
 q^{\mathsf T}q+DRD^{\mathsf T}+K^{\mathsf T}K&-K^{\mathsf T}\\
 -K&0
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S_q^{\circ-1}(f,b)=\Sigma_q^{-1}(f+K^{\mathsf T}b,-b).
 \tag{20}
\]

*Proof.* The physical normalization in (4) gives \(Q'Q'^{\mathsf T}=I\), so \(Q'\) is onto. If
\(\langle\psi,A_s\psi\rangle=\|D\psi\|_2^2+\|m^{-1}Q'\psi\|_{\rm top}^2=0\),
then \(\psi\) is parallel on every fine bond. Parallel transport along every path in a pillar carries \(\psi(x)\) to its top value. The contour mean does too: for a nonzero real colour vector its stabilizer in \(SU(2)\) is abelian, and the nearby logarithmic mean of a coset of that stabilizer remains in the coset. Explicitly, writing the path transports as \(Vs_i\), their mean is \(V\exp(\operatorname{mean}\log s_i)\). The zero-vector case is immediate. Each closed ratio \(Z_x\) also fixes the base vector; its nearby logarithm is in that stabilizer's Lie algebra. Thus \(\mathcal M(U)\) preserves parallel top values, and induction applies to every level of (4). Hence \(Q'\psi(y)=\psi(y)\), and \(Q'\psi=0\) forces \(\psi=0\). Positivity on real vectors also gives invertibility on the complexification.

Put \(T_s=Q'S\). Then \(T_sT_s^{\mathsf T}>0\) and
\(P=T_s^{\mathsf T}(T_sT_s^{\mathsf T})^{-1}T_s\) is the orthogonal projection onto \((\ker T_s)^\perp\).
If \(Q'\psi=0\), then \(A_s\psi=\Delta_U\psi\), so \(Q'S\Delta_U\psi=0\).
Conversely \(Q'Sf=0\) implies \(\psi=Sf\in\ker Q'\) and \(\Delta_U\psi=f\).
This identifies \(\operatorname{ran}R\) and proves mass independence.

For scalar sources \((g_1,g_2,g_3)\), solve the three rows explicitly:
\[
 \mu=N(Q'Sg_1-Q'S^2g_2+g_3),\quad
 h=S(g_2+Q'^{\mathsf T}\mu),\quad \psi=S(g_1-h).
 \tag{21}
\]
At \(g_2=g_3=0\), \(h=Pg_1\). Eliminating these rows in (8) subtracts \(DPD^{\mathsf T}\) from \(B_q\), giving \(\Sigma_q\). Adding \(K^{\mathsf T}\) times \(Ka=b\) to the first equation of \(S_q^\circ\), and reversing the second row's sign, gives (20). \(\square\)

**Lemma 3 (bare local inverses).** On any bare cell, with its matched boundary spaces, \(\mathbb M_q(U)^{-1}\) and \(\mathbb S(U)^{-1}\) have an absolute scaled-norm bound for every unitary \(U\), for either curl.

*Proof.* Here \(m=\eta\), \(Q'=I\), \(K=\eta I\), \(K^{\mathsf T}=\eta^{-3}I\). Unitary translations give \(\|D\|,\|\nabla_U\|\le4/\eta\). Each curl has four transported edge insertions per plaquette, and each bond belongs to at most six plaquettes, whence
\(0\le q^{\mathsf T}q\le24\eta^{-2}I\).
Thus \(A_s=\Delta_U+\eta^{-2}I\) has norm at most \(17\eta^{-2}\), and the norms \(E,H_m^2\), and the top-\(\mu\) norm are equivalent to
\(\eta^{-1}\|\cdot\|_2,\eta^{-2}\|\cdot\|_2,\eta^2\|\cdot\|_2\), respectively.

For arbitrary sources \(F=(f,g_1,g_2,g_3,g_5)\), all five rows are solved without any inverse estimate:
\[
 a=-g_5/\eta,\quad \psi=-g_3,\quad
 h=g_1+D^{\mathsf T}a+A_sg_3,\quad
 \mu=A_sh-g_2,\quad
 \nu=\eta^3(B_qa-Dh-f).
 \tag{22}
\]
Counting norm equals \(\eta^{-2}\) times fine \(L^2\) norm on bare rows. The source norms are equivalent to
\(\eta\|f\|_2,\|g_1\|_2,\eta^2\|g_2\|_2,\eta^{-2}\|g_3\|_2,
\|g_5\|_{\rm count}\).
Substitution into (22) bounds each output by their sum with an absolute constant \(B_0\). Removing \(a,\nu\) proves the scalar assertion. This argument uses no small gauge at the outer boundary. \(\square\)

**Lemma 4 (parametrix and decay for supported sources).** The systems \(\mathbb M_q\) and \(\mathbb S\) are invertible with uniform \(\mathcal X^*\to\mathcal X\) bounds. If \(F\) is already supported within a fixed read neighbourhood of a top \(z\), its solution satisfies
\[
 \|X\|_{\mathcal X,\operatorname{loc}(y)}
       \le C e^{-\delta_wd(y,z)}\|F\|_{\mathcal X^*}.
 \tag{23}
\]

*Proof.* On an interior cell, conjugate I1's matching flat periodic local model by the cell gauge of Lemma 1, extended arbitrarily to the rest of its model box. Call this pure-gauge model \(\mathbb M_i\). The two curls agree in it. On a bare cell take the actual unitary \(U\), actual curl and matched Dirichlet spaces, using Lemma 3. Norm comparison and I1 give local inverse bounds by a fixed multiple of \(\max\{B_L,B_0\}\).

Set \(\mathbb B=\sum_i\chi_i\mathbb M_i^{-1}\chi_i\), with the cutoff-supported inputs and outputs transplanted between matching coordinates. Direct multiplication gives
\[
 \mathbb M_q\mathbb B=I+\mathbb R,\qquad
 \mathbb R=\sum_i\big([\mathbb M_i,\chi_i]
       +(\mathbb M_q-\mathbb M_i)\chi_i\big)\mathbb M_i^{-1}\chi_i .
 \tag{24}
\]
Only a **model** commutator occurs. For interior models its bound is I2, unchanged by gauge conjugation. For bare models the covariant product rule and unitary transports give the same bound: a first cutoff difference contributes \(C/(M_P\eta)\), and a second contributes \(C/(M_P\eta)^2\); in the bare scaled norms these cost \(C/M_P\). The row and pillar blocks there are diagonal.

For the coefficient term, pair against a test field and insert a smooth cutoff equal to one on the entire read closure of \(\operatorname{supp}\chi_i\). Lemma 1 applies to this supported test field; I2 controls multiplication by that cutoff in the primal norm. Duality and the bounded-overlap assembly estimate therefore give
\[
 \|\mathbb R\|_{\mathcal X^*\to\mathcal X^*}
       \le A_0(M_P^{-1}+\alpha)\le\tfrac14,\qquad
 \|\mathbb B\|_{\mathcal X^*\to\mathcal X}\le B_* .
\]
This does not multiply an arbitrary dual source by a sharp indicator. Since the systems are finite square systems,
\[
 \mathbb M_q^{-1}=\mathbb B\sum_{n\ge0}(-\mathbb R)^n,\qquad
 \|\mathbb M_q^{-1}\|\le\tfrac43 B_* .
 \tag{25}
\]
The same construction, with I1's scalar models, proves the assertion for \(\mathbb S\).

Both \(\mathbb B\) and \(\mathbb R\) have metric propagation at most \(h_*=D_LM_P\): their local inverses stay in their finite model boxes and every transplantation has the specified read margin. Thus the \(n\)-th term of (25), for an already supported \(F\), has zero local output near \(y\) if \(d(y,z)>(n+1)h_*+C_L\). Its global norm is at most \(B_*4^{-n}\|F\|\). The geometric-series tail gives (23), with the weaker rate \(\delta_w=\log2/h_*\) absorbing fixed support margins. Local norms in (23) restrict evaluated derivatives; no assertion about sharply restricted negative-order norms is used. \(\square\)

**Lemma 5 (real local estimate).** Let \(Q\) have side \(r\ge16\eta\), and let \(Q^+\) be the concentric cube of side \(2r\), with the equation and its stencils available there. Suppose
\[
 \Delta_Uu=g+Eu,\quad U=e^{i\eta A_0},\quad
 r|A_0|+r^2|\nabla A_0|\le a,\quad
 |Eu(x)|\le Ca r^{-2}\max_{|x'-x|_\infty\le2\eta}|u(x')|.
\]
For a fixed sufficiently small \(a\le a_0\),
\[
 |u|_Q+r|\nabla_Uu|_Q+r^{1+\beta}[\nabla_Uu]_{\beta,Q}
 \le C_\beta\big(r^{-2}\|u\|_{2,Q^+}+r^2\|g\|_{\infty,Q^+}\big).
 \tag{26}
\]

*Proof.* For bounded \(r/\eta\), the pointwise \(L^2\) bound and elementary difference bounds prove (26), with a fixed constant. Otherwise let \(d_+(x)\) be distance to \(\partial Q^+\), and
\(\Phi=\sup(d_+/r)^2|u|\).
At \(d_+(x)\ge16\eta\), apply I8 on a cube of side comparable to \(d_+(x)\), with its read neighbourhood inside \(Q^+\). On that neighbourhood \(|u|\le C(r/d_+(x))^2\Phi\). Multiplying the value estimate by \((d_+(x)/r)^2\) gives
\[
 \Phi\le C(r^{-2}\|u\|_2+r^2\|g\|_\infty)+Ca\Phi.
\]
The strip \(d_+<16\eta\) satisfies the first term directly by
\(|u(x)|\le\eta^{-2}\|u\|_2\).
Absorb \(Ca\Phi\). This bounds \(u\) on the cube of side \(3r/2\).
I8 itself uses the real covariant Kato inequality
\(\Delta|u|\le|\Delta_Uu|\); no complex-background comparison is being made.

Paired shifts give, componentwise,
\[
 \Delta_Uu-\Delta u=\mathcal B\nabla u+\mathcal C u,\qquad
 |\mathcal B|\le Ca/r,\quad |\mathcal C|\le Ca/r^2 .
\]
The bound on \(\mathcal C\) uses \(\nabla A_0\); it does not follow from a value bound alone.
On the cube of side \(3r/2\) put
\(\Psi=\sup d_1(x)|\nabla u(x)|\), where \(d_1\) is distance to its boundary.
Apply I3's free central gradient estimate on cubes of side comparable to \(d_1(x)\).
The value bound just proved handles \((\mathcal C+E)u\), while the drift contributes \(Ca\Psi\).
The few-mesh boundary strip is bounded directly by the value bound. Thus
\(\Psi\le C(|u|_{3r/2}+r^2\|g\|_\infty)+Ca\Psi\).
After absorption, \(|\nabla u|\le8\Psi/r\) on the cube of side \(5r/4\): its distance from the boundary of the \(3r/2\) cube is **\(r/8\)**.

It follows that \(|\Delta u|\le C(r^{-4}\|u\|_2+\|g\|_\infty)\) on that intermediate cube. I3 with zero divergence source gives the ordinary gradient Hölder estimate on \(Q\).
The covariant correction has coefficient \(O(a/r)\); its change over distance \(t\) is bounded by
\(C(|\nabla A_0||u|+|A_0||\nabla u|)t\).
The comparison path in §1 has transport differing from \(I\) by at most \(Cat/r\).
Since \(t/r\le(t/r)^\beta\), both corrections satisfy (26).
Choose all intermediate cubes with a few extra fine stencils; the bounded-mesh case already covers any rounding loss. \(\square\)

**Lemma 6 (supported pointwise estimates).** Let \((a,\ell)=S_q^{\circ-1}(f,b)\), in either of the following cases:
(i) \(b=0\), \(f\) is supported over \(P_z\), \(|f|\le m_z^{-3}\);
(ii) \(f=0\), \(b\) is supported at one row \(c\), \(y_c=z\), \(|b(c)|\le1\).
Then
\[
 m_y|a|_y+m_y^2|\nabla_Ua|_y+
       m_y^{2+\beta}[\nabla_Ua]_{\beta,y}
       \le Ce^{-\delta_wd(y,z)},\qquad
 |\ell(c')|\le Ce^{-\delta_wd(y_{c'},z)} .
 \tag{27}
\]
If \(g\) is supported over \(P_z\) and \(|g|\le m_z^{-2}\), then
\[
 m_y^3|DPg|_y\le Ce^{-\delta_wd(y,z)} .
 \tag{28}
\]

*Proof.* Lemmas 2 and 4 already give invertibility. The enlarged source for (27) is
\(F=(f+K^{\mathsf T}b,0,0,0,-b)\).
In case (i), \(\|f\|_{E^*}\le\|mf\|_2\le C\), because a pillar has volume \(m_z^4\).
In case (ii), (17) gives \(|K^{\mathsf T}b|\le C_Ls_c^{-3}\) on a bounded union of comparable-scale pillars, and the same estimate bounds its \(E^*\) norm; \(\|b\|_{\rm count}\le1\).
Their supports lie within bounded metric distance of \(z\). Thus (23) bounds every required local energy norm by \(Ce^{-\delta_wd(y,z)}\).

For \(m_y\le128L\eta\), pointwise \(L^2\) bounds give
\(m_y|a|+m_y^2|\nabla_Ua|\le C_L\|X\|_{\mathcal X,\rm loc}\);
a difference at separation \(t\ge\eta\) gives the Hölder bound with an additional factor at most \((128L)^\beta\).
This also covers the arbitrary outer boundary.

For larger \(m_y\), take a lattice cube of side \(r\) with
\(m_y/(8L)\le r\le m_y/(4L)\), so \(r\ge16\eta\).
Its enlarged cube lies in an interior cell, with all read scales comparable to \(m_y\).
The regular gauge there satisfies Lemma 5's ceiling by the choice of \(\alpha_*\).
First, row 3 of (8) gives
\[
 \Delta_Uh=Q'^{\mathsf T}\mu-Q'^{\mathsf T}m^{-2}Q'h .
\]
Its right side is bounded by
\(Cm_y^{-4}(\|m^2\mu\|_{\rm top,loc}+\|h\|_{2,\rm loc})\):
a top value is bounded by \(m_y^{-4}\|m^2\mu\|_{\rm top}\), and
\(|Q'h|\le Cm_y^{-2}\|h\|_{2,P_y}\).
Lemma 5 with \(E=0\) therefore gives
\[
 |Dh|\le Cm_y^{-3}e^{-\delta_wd(y,z)} .
 \tag{29}
\]

Row 1 gives
\[
 (q^{\mathsf T}q+DD^{\mathsf T})a
       =f+K^{\mathsf T}b+Dh-K^{\mathsf T}Ka+K^{\mathsf T}\nu .
 \tag{30}
\]
On these interior stencils,
\(d_U^{\mathsf T}d_U+DD^{\mathsf T}=\Delta_U+\mathcal V_U\),
where the off-diagonal terms are
\([\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu^{\mathsf T}]a_\nu\).
Each coefficient is \(\eta^{-2}(\operatorname{Ad}T_1-\operatorname{Ad}T_2)\) for the two paths round a plaquette. Since
\(|U(\partial p)-I|\le C\eta^2(|\nabla A_0|+|A_0|^2)\),
\(\mathcal V_U\) is a range-\(2\eta\) multiplication/shift error of size \(Ca/r^2\).

For the plaquette curl, SA PX117 gives \(E_c=\mathfrak d_U-d_U\), with coefficients bounded by
\(C\eta^{-1}|I-\operatorname{Ad}U(\partial p)|\le Ca\eta/r^2\).
Consequently
\[
 \mathfrak d_U^{\mathsf T}\mathfrak d_U-d_U^{\mathsf T}d_U
       =d_U^{\mathsf T}E_c+E_c^{\mathsf T}d_U+E_c^{\mathsf T}E_c
\]
has fixed finite range and coefficients at most \(Ca/r^2\). This uses the entry bound \(C/\eta\) on \(d_U\), **not a derivative of curvature**. Both curls therefore reduce (30) to Lemma 5.

Every forcing term in (30) is bounded by \(Cm_y^{-3}e^{-\delta_wd(y,z)}\).
The source terms vanish unless their support is nearby. Equation (29) handles \(Dh\).
For the other terms, (17) and Cauchy–Schwarz give, on their read carriers,
\[
 |K^{\mathsf T}Ka|\le C s^{-4}\|a\|_{2,\rm carrier}
       \le C s^{-3}\|m^{-1}a\|_{2,\rm carrier},\qquad
 |K^{\mathsf T}\nu|\le Cs^{-3}\|\nu\|_{\rm count,loc}.
\]
The carriers have bounded metric radius and comparable scales. Apply (26), using
\(\|a\|_{2,\rm loc}\le Cm_y\|X\|_{\mathcal X,\rm loc}\), to obtain (27) locally.
Separations from \(r\) up to \(m_y\) follow from twice the gradient bound, with fixed \(L,\beta\) loss.
The multiplier bound follows directly from \(\ell=\nu\) and the local counting norm.

Finally \(Pg\) is the \(h\) output of \(\mathbb S^{-1}(g,0,0)\) by (21), and \(\|g\|_2\le C\).
Its second equation still has zero source, so the argument for (29) proves (28).
At bounded \(m_y/\eta\), one difference and the pointwise \(L^2\) bound give the same conclusion. \(\square\)

**Lemma 7 (all weights and row kernels; proof of Theorem P).** Estimates (15)–(16) and (7) follow from (27)–(28).

 \le C\sum_z e^{-(\delta_w-\theta)d(y,z)}
                (\|f\|_{F,w}+C_L\|b\|_{Y,w}).
\]
Use the output row's own centre for the multiplier bound.
Since \(\delta_w-\theta\ge\delta_w/2\), I4 and (11) bound the sum. This proves (15).

For \(g=D^{\mathsf T}a\), the backward differences are transported versions of the forward stencils in \(X_w\). Adjacent scales and weights differ by bounded factors. At the outer Dirichlet boundary, \(m=\eta\) and the value bound controls the zero-extension term. Hence
\[
 |g|_z\le C_Lm_z^{-2}w(z)^{-1}\|a\|_{X,w}.
\]
Split this bounded site function into pillar pieces, apply (28), and sum by I4, obtaining (16).

For (7), fix an output component \(i\) and use

\(\sigma_j/w(y_j)\) to maximize the nonnegative real quantity
\(e_r^*A_{ij}v_j\). The all-weight estimate gives

 \sum_j\mu_j(\sigma_j/\sigma_i)

Summing over the fixed finite number of colour/direction coordinates bounds \(\|A_{ij}\|\).

Writing the same operator with physical row cell measures requires dividing its row-input kernel entries by those measures; (7) is unchanged.

**Lemma 8 (the reference class and the repaired gap condition).** Under I6–I7, the A1 admissibility floors, \(B_A\varepsilon_1\le1\), (13), and torus room, there are \(C_{\rm reg},a_{\rm reg}>0\), fixed with \(L\) and the auxiliary flat parameters, such that

 C_{\rm reg}M'\varepsilon_1\le a_{\rm reg}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 U_0\text{ satisfies Reg}_{C_{\rm reg}M'\varepsilon_1}.
 \tag{31}

*Proof.* I6 gives the curvature and current bounds. Balaban's local-gauge class uses the full matrix expression \(\eta^{-2}D_U^*\partial U\), not just its anti-Hermitian current part. For a unitary plaquette \(V\),
\[
 \operatorname{Re}V-I=-\tfrac12(V-I)^*(V-I).
\]
On a bond whose six incident plaquettes are inner, the constant \(I\) contributions cancel in the full divergence stencil. Its Hermitian part is therefore bounded by
\(CB_A^2\varepsilon_1^2\eta m_b^{-4}
 \le C_LB_A^2\varepsilon_1^2m_b^{-3}\).
Its anti-Hermitian part is the current, with the fixed normalization absorbed in \(C_L\).
Thus the full class has radius
\(\alpha_0=C_L(B_A\varepsilon_1+B_A^2\varepsilon_1^2)\le C_LB_A\varepsilon_1\).
Below every divergence stencil stays inside \(\Omega_0\).

For \(j\ge1\), let \(Z\) be a Reg cube of side \(n\xi_j\).
Enclose it in **one aligned cube** \(\square\) on the grid of spacing \(W_{\rm aux}\xi_j\).
Choose the lower corner by rounding down and the common side by rounding the largest coordinate extent up. Then its side is \(M''\xi_j\), with \(M''\) a multiple of \(W_{\rm aux}\) and
\(M''\le n+2W_{\rm aux}=M'\).
This corrects the draft's claim that the union of all intersecting grid cubes must itself be a cube; such a union can be rectangular.

Around \(\square\), construct I7's auxiliary nested family, using the independently fixed block length \(m_{\rm aux}\) and gap number \(r_{\rm aux}\).

\(\operatorname{dist}_\infty(\Omega_{j-1}^c,\Omega_j)>
 \rho\xi_j/L\), (13) places the cover inside \(\Omega_{j-1}\), with more than \(2\xi_j\) of stencil room.
