## 2. The exact object at issue

**Discharged — identification from the definitions.** In I:980, the one-old-factor term before its higher-order error is
\[
 \frac{N_{\rm good}^{Z}(W)}{N_{\rm good}(W)}
 E_W^Z[e^{e(Y)}-1].                                      \tag{1}
\]
Its linearization in the old activity is therefore the normalized good expectation, multiplied by the displayed normalizer ratio. The replacement of this ratio by one, the replacement of the exponential by its linear term, and the change of cuts are separate estimates. In particular, replacing (1) by a free Gaussian expectation is not an identity of the actual recursion.

**Discharged — identification from I:988–1000.** The zone is \(Z=N_{r_e}(Y)\), of diameter \(D_e=D+2r_e\), and \(N:=\operatorname{Vol}(Z)=D_e^4\); it is smaller than a whole constraint tube. The coefficient \(\beta_Y(e)\) is the quadratic response through the local saddle displacement in the marginal density, evaluated at the flat reference. Its arguments are curvature tensors. The field \(F^*\) is the curvature of the exact constrained minimizer \(V^*(W)\), not an independently prescribed smooth field. For the linearized curvature, the stated interior representation is
\[
 F^*_{\rm lin}(x)=L^{-2}\sum_{x'}K(x,x')F'_{x'},\qquad
 |K(x,x')-K(z,x')|\lesssim (|x-z|/L)^a
 \times\text{the stated decay weight}.                    \tag{2}
\]
I:9.4 additionally asserts a nonlinear correction quadratic in the coarse curvature. That correction is not part of the linear identity (2). Use of the lift estimates, with their asserted constants, will be labelled an implication. D1 must be tested against these two restrictions: the coefficient is generated by a density, and the curvature is generated by a lift.

**Gap — the disputed inference, before its possible repair.** I:1020 averages \(\beta_Y\) over positions in one block and assigns an \(L^{-a}\) gain to the remainder merely because its mean is zero. The modulus in (2) is small on distances \(D_e\ll L\), but is only of order one across an entire block of side \(L\). Thus the small-zone Taylor estimate and the claimed block-average estimate are different assertions. The next sections test whether the actual coefficient has additional structure sufficient for the latter.

## 3. What the example establishes, including the lift restriction

**Discharged — a discrete version of the cancellation test.** Let \(L=2m+1\ge3\), \(B=\{-m,\ldots,m\}^4\), \(u=x_1/L\), \(b_0>0\), and take one fixed colour and one curvature component. Put
\[
 b(x)=b_0(1+u),\qquad f(x)=L^{-2}F_0(1+u).
\]
Then \(\sum_B(b-b_0)=0\), both profiles have a scale-\(L\) Lipschitz modulus, and
\[
 \sum_{x\in B}(b(x)-b_0)|f(x)|^2
 =b_0|F_0|^2\frac{L^2-1}{6L^2}
 \ge\frac4{27}b_0|F_0|^2.                              \tag{3}
\]
Odd powers of \(u\) sum to zero, and the remaining average is \(2L^{-1}\sum_{j=-m}^m j^2/L^2\). The identity \(\sum_{j=-m}^m j^2=m(m+1)(2m+1)/3\) follows by summing \((j+1)^3-j^3=3j^2+3j+1\), together with the analogous square difference for \(\sum j\). Substitution gives (3). This disproves the stated mean-zero inference even with Lipschitz regularity, stronger than the asserted Hölder regularity. It does not identify \(b\) with an actual \(\beta_Y(e)\).

**Discharged — scale-\(L\) variation really occurs in the linearized block map.** This can be checked without choosing an arbitrary \(\phi\). Use the flat Wilson Hessian, one colour, and a fine torus of side \(2JL\), with \(J\) as large and divisible as the torus and tile conventions require. Prescribe the coarse potential \(B_2(y)=B(-1)^{y_1/L}\), with the other components zero. Work modulo gradients. The pure-gauge \(h(y)-h(y+L e_\mu)\) in I:1.2 then drops out, leaving its displayed straight-path average \(Q_{\rm str}\).

Average a competitor over all transverse coordinates. For direction 2, averaging its block constraints over all transverse blocks gives exactly \(L\) times the direction-1 block average of the averaged potential. For direction 1 it gives the straight-path average of the averaged component; the other transverse directions have zero prescribed component. Hence this averaging preserves the prescribed coarse field modulo gradients. It cannot increase \(\sum|dA|^2\): apply \(|n^{-1}\sum v_i|^2\le n^{-1}\sum|v_i|^2\) to each translated curvature component and sum. The averaged direction-1 component is a gradient, apart from a constant holonomy which can be set to zero; components 3 and 4 minimize at zero. Thus the physical minimum is attained with only \(A_2(j)=a_j\), and minimizes
\[
 \sum_j(a_{j+1}-a_j)^2,
 \qquad L^{-1}\sum_{j=-m}^m a_j=B/L,\quad
 L^{-1}\sum_{j=-m}^m a_{j+L}=-B/L.
\]
Impose these two mean conditions also at every translate by \(2L\). The unique minimizer is the \(2L\)-periodic sequence
\[
 a_j=\lambda\left(\frac{L^2+1}{8}-\frac{j^2}{2}\right),
 \quad a_{j+L}=-a_j,\quad
 \lambda=\frac{12B}{L(L^2+2)}\qquad(-m\le j\le m).       \tag{4}
\]
Indeed its block means have the required values by the square-sum identity above; \(-\Delta a_j\) is the constant \(\lambda\) in the first block and \(-\lambda\) in the second, including the two boundary links. For any feasible perturbation \(v\), summation by parts gives \(\sum da\,dv=\sum(-\Delta a)v=0\). The energy difference is consequently \(\sum|dv|^2\ge0\); its only null vectors are constants, excluded by the means. Its curvature is
\[
 f_{12}(j)=-\lambda(j+\tfrac12),\quad -m\le j\le m.      \tag{5}
\]
The boundary value follows from \(a_{m+1}=-a_{-m}\), so (5) holds there too. At \(j=m\) its absolute value is \(6|B|/(L^2+2)\); near the block centre it is of order \(|B|/L^3\). This is an actual linearized constrained lift with variation of order \(|B|L^{-2}\) across a block, not a field varying only on much larger scales. It also has a nonconstant square. The pure-gauge term in the actual linearized map can be restored by a fine gauge transformation and does not change (5).

**Implication — relation of (4) to the nonlinear model.** If the analytic saddle construction asserted in I holds at the flat field, (4) is its first derivative for these coarse data at the Wilson starting scale. The quaternion mean linearizes to the stated \(Q\), and the Wilson quadratic energy is the one just minimized. No uniform nonlinear remainder is inferred here. In particular this calculation does not insert a nonzero old factor at the starting scale, where the actual old remainder is zero.

**Unchecked — the coefficient half of D1's counterexample.** Choosing \(b-b_0\) proportional to the centered square of (5) makes its pairing with \(|f|^2\) strictly positive, by \(\sum (q-\bar q)q=\sum(q-\bar q)^2\). It does not show that this choice is generated by \(\beta_Y(e)\) for a permitted old family. Multiplying a translated family by an arbitrary block-scale profile would introduce positional data beyond that family and can violate I:3.3(d). Neither (3) nor (5) is therefore a counterexample to the coefficient recursion. They identify a real defect in the argument for its gain.

**Implication — precisely why Text 11.1 avoids this particular inference.** Its hypotheses include covariance under level-\(j\) translations of the family *before* evaluation on a lift. Step 5 consequently gives the same second moment at each fine base site. Subtracting \(\beta(E)f_j\) at that scale leaves zero second moment at each such site, not just zero average over a new block. Step 4 then uses the modulus on each polymer diameter \(D\ll L\), which supplies \((D/L)^a\); the identity \(F_j(V_j)=F_{j+1}(W)\) passes the subtracted term through the lift. Conditional on its stated analytic, locality, lift and symmetry hypotheses, this answers D1's objection to that step. Term (2) instead averages in a law constrained by the *new* block map. Translation by one fine site changes that constraint grid relative to \(Y\); coarse covariance alone cannot repeat Step 5. Covariance of the final coarse quadratic functional does not force its kernel to equal the classical quadratic kernel. Thus “covariant” must specify the fine scale, and is not by itself an escape for term (2).

## 4. The hardest term: exact normalization, interactions and cuts

**Discharged — the conditional law is not the old product law.** At the flat quadratic chart, set \(M=2^{-1/2}P_{Z,d}\), \(U=(G_{1,Z},G_{2,Z})\), \(\Sigma_0=MM^T>0\), and \(z=v_Z/g\), on independent near coordinates. The coordinate equation is \(z=M\xi_d+U\eta\). Orthogonally integrating the kernel of \(M\) leaves the spectator density
\[
 \exp\{-|\eta|^2/2-(z-U\eta)^T\Sigma_0^{-1}(z-U\eta)/2\}.
                                                               \tag{6}
\]
Its precision is \(I+U^T\Sigma_0^{-1}U\). Multiplication verifies that its covariance and mean are
\[
 I-U^T(\Sigma_0+UU^T)^{-1}U,
 \qquad U^T(\Sigma_0+UU^T)^{-1}z.
\]
These are the matrices in `mixed-coefficient-2.md`, (I)–(J). The original Gaussian of \(\eta\) occurs once in (6). The added quadratic form can couple different spectator blocks; whenever \(U\ne0\), its precision already differs from the identity because \(\operatorname{tr}(U^T\Sigma_0^{-1}U)>0\). The change of variables therefore does not permit identifying this law with the original product noise and applying its product-measure cluster lemma without another argument.

**Discharged — the actual second coefficient to be controlled.** Use the fixed inverse coordinates of `conditional-normalization.md`, Section 6, for one real field parameter \(w\). Write \(\Xi=\Xi_0+w\Xi_1+w^2\Xi_2+\cdots\), \(\log J=\ell_0+w\ell_1+w^2\ell_2+\cdots\), and \(\Psi=\Psi_0+w\Psi_1+w^2\Psi_2+\cdots\). On the fixed replacement domain the two log-density coefficients are exactly
\[
 A_1=\ell_1+\Psi_1-\Xi_0\cdot\Xi_1,\qquad
 A_2=\ell_2+\Psi_2-\Xi_0\cdot\Xi_2-\tfrac12\Xi_1\cdot\Xi_1.\tag{7}
\]
For a test \(e(v)\) independent of \(w\), let brackets normalize that density at zero, let \(N(w)\) be the *original* good normalizer, and put \(\theta(w)=\widetilde N(w)/N(w)\). Series division gives
\[
 [w^2]\frac{\widetilde P_e(w)}{N(w)}
 =\theta_0\{\operatorname{Cov}(e,A_2)
                   +\tfrac12\kappa_3(e,A_1,A_1)\}
  +\theta_1\operatorname{Cov}(e,A_1)+\theta_2\langle e\rangle.
                                                               \tag{8}
\]
To verify it, the unnormalized multiplier is \(1+wA_1+w^2(A_2+A_1^2/2)\); invert its expectation and multiply. Here \(\kappa_3(e,A,A)=\langle eA^2\rangle-\langle e\rangle\langle A^2\rangle-2\langle eA\rangle\langle A\rangle+2\langle e\rangle\langle A\rangle^2\). All terms in (7), and the last two terms in (8), belong to the actual question. The difference between replacement and original numerators must additionally be restored. If the old factor itself has field coefficients, their products with the lower density coefficients must also be included.

**Gap — first attempted source of a noncontracting term.** The term \(\theta_2\langle e\rangle\), and the connected terms containing \(\Psi_i,\ell_i\) in (8), are linear in the old activity. Algebra alone supplies neither an \(L\) gain nor a \(g\) gain for them. Dropping them produces exactly the invalid Gaussian-only repair. Conversely, seeing no gain in their formula is not a lower bound: the normalizer subtractions can cancel them. I have not found a permitted field and old factor for which their complete sum has a block-scale component bounded below independently of both gains.

**Discharged — what ordinary total variation does and does not survive.** For probabilities \(\mu,\nu\) on the *same* measurable space, use \(\|\mu-\nu\|_1\) for the total variation norm of their signed difference. Integrating a bounded test costs at most \(\|e\|_\infty\|\mu-\nu\|_1\); independence is unnecessary. If a common cut \(C\) has \(\mu(C),\nu(C)\ge q>0\), then
\[
 \left\|\frac{1_C\mu}{\mu(C)}-
             \frac{1_C\nu}{\nu(C)}\right\|_1
 \le\frac{2}{q}\|\mu-\nu\|_1.                            \tag{9}
\]
Add and subtract \(1_C\nu/\mu(C)\), and use \(|\mu(C)-\nu(C)|\le\|1_C(\mu-\nu)\|_1\) to prove (9). For different domains an additional signed domain-change measure is required. Globally imposing good cuts gives no volume-independent lower bound \(q\), and conditioning on \(v\) can make a near cut improbable even when its unconditional probability is high. Thus (9) does not justify the proposed comparison after the actual conditioning and cut division. Non-product dependence does not refute total variation as a method; it invalidates the asserted product-law proof of its needed bound.

**Discharged — real mass control is not derivative control.** The real function \(\epsilon(1-\cos Hw)\) is bounded by \(2\epsilon\), whereas its \(w^2\) coefficient is \(\epsilon H^2/2\). Consequently a small real signed cut mass alone does not estimate (8). This is a logical test of that inference, not a model of the actual cut. A continuation estimate or a direct differentiated density estimate must supply the missing information.

## 5. What can be proved for the Gaussian coefficient

**Discharged — the reference must be specified.** Take a finite real physical coordinate space with positive quadratic Hessian \(H\), covariance \(C^0=H^{-1}\), and surjective linearized block map \(Q\). Gauge and constant null directions must first be removed consistently. Put
\[
 S=QC^0Q^T,\quad l=C^0Q^TS^{-1},\quad
 C=C^0-lSl^T.                                           \tag{10}
\]
For a centered Gaussian \(X\) of covariance \(C^0\), \(X-lQX\) has covariance \(C\) and zero cross covariance with \(QX\). Its joint Gaussian exponential factors, by completing the quadratic form, so the two are independent. Conditioning on \(QX=0\) thus gives (10). This is the covariance of the *linearized* map. Conditioning on the quaternion mean itself is not Gaussian conditioning.

**Discharged — local compression and its exact trace identity.** Let \(R_Z\) select independent physical near coordinates, with \(C^0_Z=R_ZC^0R_Z^T>0\). They may be local loop coordinates; they are not arbitrary Coulomb-gauge potentials. Define
\[
 T=(C^0_Z)^{-1/2}R_ZlSl^TR_Z^T(C^0_Z)^{-1/2}.
\]
Then \(0\le T\le I\), and
\[
 \operatorname{tr}T
  =E_{A\sim N(0,S)}\bigl[(R_ZlA)^T(C^0_Z)^{-1}(R_ZlA)\bigr].
                                                               \tag{11}
\]
The identity follows by expanding the square and using \(EAA^T=S\). For any prescribed \(s\),
\[
 s^T(C^0_Z)^{-1}s=\min_{R_Zv=s}v^THv:                    \tag{12}
\]
the minimizer is \(v=C^0R_Z^T(C^0_Z)^{-1}s\), and the cross term with a vector in \(\ker R_Z\) vanishes. This is the precise form needed for the proposed trace estimate.

**Implication — the scale gain in the trace.** Suppose, uniformly in the allowed regions and depths, the local radial-gauge extension in (12) costs at most \(K_E N\sup_Z|F|^2\); the curvature of \(lA\) obeys \(\sup_Z|F(lA)|\le K_L L^{-2}V(A)\); and \(EV(A)^2\le K_V\). Here the three constants are independent of \(L\). Then
\[
 \operatorname{tr}T\le K_0NL^{-4},\qquad K_0=K_EK_L^2K_V.\tag{13}
\]
This follows by substituting the two pointwise bounds into (11). It explains why a physical local coordinate is essential: a pure-gauge potential is not bounded by curvature. I's extension and lift estimates motivate these premises, but they do not by themselves specify a new unconstrained interacting reference. Any logarithmic loss in these premises must also appear in (13). Below (13) remains an explicit premise, not a new theorem about every region of I.

**Discharged — covariance comparison controls the quadratic response, not merely the law.** Whiten \(C^0_Z\), divide the field by \(g\), and let \(p_t\) be the Gaussian density of \(I-tT\), \(0\le t\le1\). Assume \(\operatorname{tr}T\le1/2\). For a fixed displacement \(h\), set
\[
 \beta_t(e;h)=\frac12\int e(y)\partial_h^2p_t(y)\,dy.
\]
This is exactly the coefficient of \(z^2\) in \(\int e(y)p_t(y-zh)dy\), even for bounded measurable \(e\). It has the familiar explicit integrand
\[
 \tfrac12e(y)\bigl[(h^T(I-tT)^{-1}y)^2
                         -h^T(I-tT)^{-1}h\bigr]p_t(y). \tag{14}
\]
Diagonalize \(T\) with eigenvalues \(\tau_i\ge0\). Direct Gaussian differentiation gives \(\partial_tp_t=-\frac12\sum_i\tau_i\partial_i^2p_t\). For a standard Gaussian density \(p\),
\[
 \frac{\partial_u^2\partial_h^2p}{p}
 =((u\cdot y)^2-|u|^2)((h\cdot y)^2-|h|^2)
       -4(u\cdot h)(u\cdot y)(h\cdot y)+2(u\cdot h)^2.
\]
Its \(L^1(p)\) norm is at most \(8|u|^2|h|^2\): the first product costs at most \(2|u|^2|h|^2\) by Cauchy–Schwarz, and the other terms cost at most four and two times that product. The Gaussian second and fourth moments are \(1,3\), obtained by integrating the derivatives of \(xe^{-x^2/2}\) and \(x^3e^{-x^2/2}\). Transforming from \(p_t\) to a standard Gaussian costs at most a factor four because \(I-tT\ge I/2\). Integrating \(\partial_t\beta_t\) proves
\[
 |\beta_1(e;h)-\beta_0(e;h)|
       \le8\|e\|_\infty\operatorname{tr}T\,|h|^2.       \tag{15}
\]
All differentiated integrands are bounded by a polynomial times a common integrable Gaussian on this compact interpolation interval, which justifies the integrations. The same second-derivative calculation gives \(\|p_1-p_0\|_1\le2\operatorname{tr}T\). Thus a score estimate such as (15), rather than a bare appeal to total variation, fills the Gaussian part of the proposed inference.

**Implication — comparison with (POS-beta).** If also \(|h|^2\le K_hN|F|^2/g^2\), then (13) and (15) give
\[
 |\beta_x^G(e)(F,F)-\beta_\infty^G(e)(F,F)|
 \le8K_0K_h\|e\|_\infty\,NL^{-4}\,rac{N|F|^2}{g^2}.\tag{16}
\]
Here \(\infty\) denotes *no constraint of the current step on the same finite physical space*, not an asserted infinite-volume limit. Once \(K_0NL^{-4}\le1/2\), this is at least as useful as the proposed \(\sqrt N L^{-2}\) factor, with the constants in (13) retained. A translate of the test must be compared to the corresponding translate of this reference. These facts give no freedom to choose an order-one profile \(b(x/L)\) for this Gaussian coefficient.

**Discharged — the first concrete old-factor test does not kill the Gaussian recursion.** At one plaquette, let \(X\) be its three-colour linearized curvature noise divided by \(g\), with covariance \(v_xI_3\), and test \(e_*(X)=1-e^{-|X|^2/2}\). This is the homogeneous limit of the bounded gauge-invariant test \(1-\exp[-|V_p-1|^2/(2g^2)]\), with a fixed matching convention for the quadratic norm. For a scalar curvature displacement \(zq/g\) in one colour, completing the three squares gives
\[
 E e_*(X+zq/g)=1-(1+v_x)^{-3/2}
        \exp[-z^2q^2/(2g^2(1+v_x))].
\]
Its quadratic coefficient is \(q^2/[2g^2(1+v_x)^{5/2}]\). For \(v_x,v_y\ge0\), its difference is at most \(5q^2|v_x-v_y|/(4g^2)\), by integrating the derivative of \((1+v)^{-5/2}\). A quadratic test \(e(X)=|X|^2\) is even simpler: its displacement coefficient is \(q^2/g^2\), independent of \(v_x\). These are actual linear-chart tests, not adjustable covariances; a variation bound on their actual \(v_x\) remains a premise. Neither supplies a gain-free term in the full nonlinear recursion.

## 6. Growing factors and the proposed corrected recursion

**Discharged — a weighted replacement for the supremum in (15).** The same fourth derivative polynomial has \(L^2(p)\) norm at most \(24|u|^2|h|^2\). Indeed its terms cost at most \((\sqrt{105}+6\sqrt3+3)|u|^2|h|^2<24|u|^2|h|^2\), by Cauchy–Schwarz and the Gaussian moments through order eight. Those moments follow recursively from \(\int (x^{n-1}e^{-x^2/2})'=0\), so the eighth is \(7\cdot5\cdot3=105\). Cauchy–Schwarz in the density integral and the same factor four then give
\[
 |\beta_1(e;h)-\beta_0(e;h)|
 \le24\operatorname{tr}T\,|h|^2
                     \sup_{0\le t\le1}(E_t|e|^2)^{1/2}.\tag{17}
\]
This is a genuine weighted estimate. Ordinary unweighted total variation alone would not control an unbounded test.

**Implication — the prescribed growth has the required Gaussian moment.** Suppose the standardized curvature components under all these Gaussian interpolants have variance at most \(v_0\), and shell \(r\) has at most \(B_0N(r+1)^4\) components. With the absolute rate \(\alpha>0\) from (TAIL), put \(U=\sup_x e^{-\alpha d(x,Z)/2}|X_x|\). Completing a scalar Gaussian square and applying Markov's inequality gives \(\Pr(|X_x|>u)\le2e^{-u^2/(2v_0)}\). The union over shells therefore gives, for \(u^2\ge2v_0\),
\[
 \Pr(U>u)\le C_\alpha N e^{-u^2/(4v_0)},\qquad
 C_\alpha=48B_0e^{-1/2}(1-e^{-\alpha/2})^{-5}.             \tag{18}
\]
Here \(e^{-u^2e^{\alpha r}/(4v_0)}\le e^{-1/2}e^{-\alpha r/2}\); sum \((r+1)^4q^r\le24(1-q)^{-5}\), obtained from five geometric series. Integrate \(6u^5\Pr(U>u)\), splitting at \(u_0^2=4v_0\log\max(e,C_\alpha N)\). Above that point, substitution \(s=u^2/(4v_0)\) and two integrations by parts bound the integral by a constant times \(\log(eN)^3\). Thus, for the actual stated envelope \(|e|\le cc\,\omega_Y(t_Y^2+t_Y^3)\) and \(t_Y\le c+\sqrt N U\),
\[
 (E_t|e|^2)^{1/2}\le C(v_0,B_0,\alpha,c)\,cc\,\omega_Y
                   N^{3/2}\log(eN)^{3/2}.                \tag{19}
\]
The same argument through the twelfth moment gives an \(L^4\) version if needed. No \(g^{-3}\) supremum occurs. This proves the moment substitution only under its Gaussian premises. Applying (19) to the interacting, cut-normalized density in (8) is still a gap.

**Gap — a marginal law does not contain the whole old factor.** The actual (TAIL) factors are functions of near real fields and of far field/site series. A comparison of the law on \(Z\) controls a fixed test of \(v_Z\); if the far variables are integrated out first, the conditional test itself can change with the position and with the law. Equality of two near marginals plainly gives no bound for a test of an extra far coordinate whose law is different. This logical observation is not an actual counterexample: I proposes to control the far dependence by its scaled radii and \(L^{-A}\) estimates. Those bounds must accompany (17)–(19) coefficient by coefficient. Likewise, “position enters only through the law” applies literally to a fixed test transported between positions; it does not prove that all inherited tile and support dependence of the actual family has disappeared. The proposed microscopic cancellation must handle that dependence in the family sum.

**Discharged — the valid microscopic cancellation.** If a tensor coefficient \(b\) has zero sum on each cell \(E\) of diameter at most \(d\), then
\[
 \left|\sum_{x\in E} b_x(F_x,F_x)\right|
 \le\sum_{x\in E}\|b_x\|\,
               |F_x-F_{x_E}|(|F_x|+|F_{x_E}|).           \tag{20}
\]
Subtract \(b_x(F_{x_E},F_{x_E})\) and use bilinearity. Under (2), (20) gains \((d/L)^a\). Here the \(P\) in the proposed repair is the tile length of I:3.1–3.2, not the noise operator also called \(P\). A partition into cells of size \(O(M+P)\), with cancellation on *each* cell, would suffice. Zero average on a whole new block would not. The hierarchical tiling is not asserted here to be a periodic translation orbit; establishing the required cancellation for the summed old family is a further premise.

**Implication — quantitative shape of a successful repair.** Let \(d=O(M+P)\), \(\delta_Z=\sqrt N L^{-2}\), \(P_Z=N^{3/2}\log(eN)^{3/2}\), and \(y_k=bb_k+cc_k\). Suppose (20) applies to the reference family, and the actual nonlinear, cut-inclusive comparison satisfies (POS-beta) with (19) in place of \(\sup|e|\), also in the required coefficient norm. With \(A_{\rm sum}(M)\) denoting the stated shape, volume-ratio and position constants *after* the \(L^4\) position factor has cancelled the \(L^{-4}\) from two curvatures, the linear old-factor coefficients would obey
\[
 \begin{split}
 q_2&\le A_{\rm sum}(M)P_Z
   \{K_{\rm mic}(d/L)^a+K_{\rm pos}\delta_Z
                                 +K_{\rm ng}(L,M)g^\eta\}
                                     (g'/g)^2,\\
 q_3&\le A_{\rm sum}(M)P_Z K_3L^{-2}(g'/g)^3.             \tag{21}
 \end{split}
\]
Each term has its distinct origin: (20), the position comparison, the non-Gaussian/cut estimate, and the cubic Taylor remainder. If the constants independent of \(g\) have the stipulated polynomial dependence on \(M=O((\log L)^5)\), and \(g'/g\le R\) uniformly, one can first make the first, second and fourth terms below \(1/16\) by taking \(L\) large, then make the third below \(1/16\) by choosing \(g\le\gamma\). A power of \(\log L\) times \(L^{-b}\) tends to zero: put \(x=\log L\) and bound \(e^{bx/2}\) below by a term of its power series with degree larger than that power. This explains how the repair could contract. Formula (21) is an implication with named, unproved inputs, not a corrected recursion proved at each term. It does not dispose of the other defects in the supplied files.

**Discharged — correction to the wording of D1.** Failure to prove a small upper bound does not prove \(cc_{k+1}\ge C P(M)y_k\).
