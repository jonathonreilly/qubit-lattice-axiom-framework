## 2. (Pre): the statement and its bookkeeping

**Result of this note (proved by Claude, not yet checked by the other model).** At every admitted pair, (Pre) is at most \(C g_k^{1/4}\), uniformly in level, depth, torus, history, frame and pair. In particular \(\sup_{\mathbf p}\lvert V_X^{\rm Pre}\rvert\le CN_0\). The proof reads only real estimates already checked as implications, together with P81's complex datum ball (9.2), Theorem C and Proposition S'. It does not use Lemma 6c\(^{\mathbb C}\), a complex T4, complex Hessian constants, or a Landau chart re-centred at a complex \(S(\mathbf p)\). These were the open items of `ref-chart.md` 9e-9f and of regate Sections 10-11.

**The mechanism.** Each localisation term is a sum over tops of holomorphic functions of the pair. Real decay at every real point of a short slice, together with a crude bound on the complex slice disc, gives complex decay at half the rate. This is `sa-2.13-2.md` Lemma 3.2. That bound does not change when the slice is rescaled, so a slice whose radius is comparable to the displacement is allowed. Corollary P withdrew such a slice only for Schwarz-type increment bounds, and Section 4 uses a fixed radius for those. The Gaussian block uses a fixed-radius disc and Cauchy estimates on the joint chart, which is centred at the real reference.

\[
 \sum_{p\text{ over }y}\lvert a_p(W)\rvert\le12(C_9\varepsilon^\sharp)^2 .
 \tag{3.2}
\]

*Proof.* Datum increments: \(\beta\) itself on the pair's rows. **Lemma T (the slice).** Let \(f\) be holomorphic on \(\{\lVert\beta\rVert_\infty<6\beta_A\}\) with \(\lvert f\rvert\le\mathsf B\). Suppose \(\lvert f(\beta)\rvert\le\epsilon\) at every real \(\beta\) with \(\lVert\beta\rVert_\infty\le5\beta_A\). Then \(\lvert f(\beta(\mathbf p))\rvert\le\sqrt{\mathsf B\epsilon}\).

*Proof.* Write \(\beta(\mathbf p)=\beta_R+i\beta_I\) row by row, with \(\beta_R,\beta_I\) Hermitian. If \(\beta_I=0\), the point is real and \(\lvert f\rvert\le\min(\epsilon,\mathsf B)\le\sqrt{\mathsf B\epsilon}\). Otherwise put \(s_I:=\lVert\beta_I\rVert_\infty\), \(R:=4s_I\), and \(\beta(z):=\beta_R+z\beta_I/s_I\). For Hermitian \(X,Y\), \(X=\frac12(Z+Z^*)\) with \(Z=X+iY\), so \(\lvert X\rvert,\lvert Y\rvert\le\lvert Z\rvert\). Hence \(\lVert\beta_R\rVert_\infty,s_I\le\beta_A\), and \(\lVert\beta(z)\rVert_\infty\le\beta_A+\lvert z\rvert\). This is at most \(5\beta_A\) on \(\lvert z\rvert\le R\), and below \(6\beta_A\) on \(\lvert z\rvert<R+\beta_A\). So \(h(z):=f(\beta(z))\) is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of the closed disc, with \(\lvert h\rvert\le\mathsf B\). For real \(t\), \(\beta(t)\) is Hermitian with norm at most \(5\beta_A\), so \(\lvert h(t)\rvert\le\epsilon\) on \([-R,R]\). `sa-2.13-2.md` Lemma 3.2, checked in Section 1, gives \(\lvert h(z)\rvert\le\sqrt{\mathsf B\epsilon}\) on \(\lvert z\rvert\le R/4\). If \(\epsilon>\mathsf B\), use \(\mathsf B\) instead. Finally \(h(is_I)=f(\beta(\mathbf p))\) and \(\lvert is_I\rvert=R/4\). \(\square\)

The disc has radius \(4s_I\), comparable to the displacement. The bound \(\sqrt{\mathsf B\epsilon}\) is unchanged when \(z\) is rescaled, so no fixed radius is needed. Corollary P withdrew this slice for a different use: a Schwarz increment \(\mathsf B\lvert z\rvert/(R-\lvert z\rvert)\) gains nothing on it. Section 4 uses a fixed radius wherever an increment bound is needed.
