## Step 4. Source A (DHY, arXiv:2611.04908v1), items (1)-(3). Mark: READ (pp. 1-6, 32-33, 39-43, 64-65, 70 in full; the rest by statement and anchor)

Page numbers are the PDF pages, which equal the printed ones. Credit: everything in this section is Ding, Huang and Yang's; I restate, I do not check their proofs.

### A(1) The theorem, exact

- **Model** (p. 1, (1.1)-(1.2)): finite G = (V, E) in Z^2, nearest-neighbour; spins sigma in (S^(N-1))^V; H^zeta_G(sigma) = - sum_(u~v in V) sigma_u . sigma_v - sum_(u in V, v in dV, u~v) sigma_u . zeta_v; Gibbs measure with weight exp(-beta H), product Haar measure.
- **Theorem 1.1** (p. 2). Fix N >= 3. For all beta > 0 there is c_beta > 0 such that for all L >= 1, <sigma_o>^zeta_(B_L, beta) <= c_beta^(-1) e^(-c_beta L) (1.3), for every boundary condition zeta that is constant on dB_L ("zeta^i_. are the same for all v in dB_L", every i). With the free boundary condition and u, v in B_(L/2): <sigma_u sigma_v>_(B_L, beta) <= c_beta^(-1) e^(-c_beta |u - v|_2) (1.4).
- **Uniformity** (from the proof, p. 39-40 and the constants convention p. 6): c depends only on (beta_0, N) and holds for all local-time conditions beta_v <= beta_0, so uniformly in L, in the finite box, and in beta in (0, beta_0]; Remark 1.2 (p. 2): the same for inhomogeneous couplings sqrt(J_u J_v) with 0 <= J_u <= 1. The stronger form Theorem 5.1 (p. 39) holds for every "xy boundary condition" xi of the loop representation, not only free.
- **Boundary conditions covered:** free, and constant spin boundary conditions; not general zeta (Remark 1.3, p. 2: "It is natural to expect that (1.3) holds for all boundary conditions xi, but it does not seem to be that immediate"). **Periodic boundary conditions and tori are not covered anywhere** (the loop soup is on the metric graph of a box with free or wired local-time boundary conditions, Section 2.2.2, p. 8). The infinite-volume limit is not stated as a theorem; (1.4) is uniform in finite boxes, which gives the free-boundary infinite-volume two-point function by monotone limits only if those limits are taken separately (not stated in the paper).
- **The generalisation** (Theorem 1.4, p. 3): for real 2 < N <= 3 (and verbatim N > 3), local times beta_v <= beta_0, the loop-soup model nu^(f,N) on the metric graph of B_L: P(u connected to v in the x-copy) <= c^(-1) e^(-c |u - v|_inf), with c = c(beta_0, N).

### A(2) The skeleton, step by step

1. **Loop-soup O(N) model** (pp. 2, 7-11, Section 2.2). Metric-graph loop soup of intensity N/2; each loop is put in copy i with probability 1/N for i <= floor(N), the rest to the last copy; condition on the total local time L*_v = beta_v at every vertex (1.5). For integer N, Lupu's isomorphism ([Lup16, Theorem 1]) couples it with the O(N) spin model: L^i_v = beta_v (sigma^i_v)^2 (1.6), signs of sigma^i = independent fair signs on the clusters of copy i. For 2 < N <= 3 write N = 2 + eps: the x and y copies are critical (intensity 1/2) soups, the z-copy has intensity eps/2 (p. 2; Section 5 p. 39: each loop goes to z with probability eps/N).
2. **Reduction to an XY model in a random environment** (p. 4 (1.9); p. 5). Condition on the z-copy local times L^z; the x, y copies are the loop-soup form of an XY model with local times beta - L^z (couplings K_uv = beta r_u r_v in spin language). Domain Markov property and copy reassignment (Sections 2.2.4-2.2.5, pp. 10-11) make this conditioning usable box by box.
3. **Spin correlation <= loop-cluster connection** (Section 2.3-2.4, pp. 11-19; Lemma 2.10, p. 18, for N = 2). Random signs on clusters give <sigma^i_u sigma^i_v> <= beta_0 P(u <-> v in copy i); a constant boundary spin is the same as a wired local-time boundary (proof of Theorem 1.1, p. 40, via (2.13), (2.15)).
4. **FKG for the XY loop model** (Section 3, Theorem 3.6, p. 29): positive association of (beta^x, omega^x, omega^y) for every copywise local-time boundary condition; Corollary 3.10 (p. 31): the classical spin-wave bound gives an annulus-crossing bound with c_3 -> 0 as the aspect ratio iota -> 0 or beta -> infinity (polynomial decay only; this is the McBryan-Spencer/Mermin-Wagner level).
5. **Gaussian tail of the number of crossing loops** (Section 4, Theorem 4.1, p. 32; Theorem 4.2, p. 33): for the XY loop model, <(cosh t)^(K_R)> <= exp(C' R^2 (cosh(t/R) - 1)), so P(K_R >= k) <= c^(-1) exp(-c k R log(1 + k/R)); Corollary 4.3 (p. 33): P(at least k loops of diameter >= delta diam D) <= c^(-1) exp(-c k^2). Proof by a complex-rotation (McBryan-Spencer) spin wave in the random-current representation, with test function h = 1 on A, 0 on B (E_b(t, h) of (4.4)).
6. **Good boxes** (Definition 5.4, p. 42): B is good for L^z if for every boundary condition on B(10), mu_(B(10), beta - L^z)(dB <-xy-> dB(2)) <= rho (no x- or y-cluster crosses the thin annulus). **Lemma 5.5** (p. 42; proof Section 5.3, p. 64, and p. 70): a box is good with probability at least c_5 = c_5(eps, beta_0, delta, rho) > 0, under every xy boundary condition. Proof: Theorem 4.1 picks k* with P(exactly k* crossing loops) >= delta and P(more) <= kappa delta/k*; with probability >= (eps/(2+eps))^(k*) delta all k* go to the z-copy, and then an x- or y-crossing loop has conditional probability O(eps); Proposition 5.27 (p. 64) turns "no macroscopic x-loop" into "no x-cluster crossing" (RSW, CBC and FKG of Section 3).
7. **Good boxes fill the annulus** (Definition 5.6, Lemma 5.7, pp. 42-43): adaptive multi-scale exploration from scale M^0.1 down to M^0.01, scale factor 2^t; with probability >= 1 - c^(-1) e^(-c iota M) the good boxes cover all but C_6 delta of the volume of Lambda_(M,(1+iota)M).
8. **Empowered spin wave** (Section 5.2, pp. 45-63: Definitions 5.8-5.23, Lemmas 5.11-5.25, Proposition 5.26 at p. 60): a spin-wave map on S^1 (angles of the XY copy) whose energy cost is paid only outside good boxes; types I and II of good boxes (Type II rare, Lemma 5.11, p. 46), control of the multiplicity of the map. Output **Theorem 5.3** (p. 40): there is c_3 = c_3(beta_0, N) > 0 such that for every iota > 0, M >= M_0(iota) and every xy boundary condition, P(dLambda_M <-xy-> dLambda_((1+iota)M)) <= 1 - c_3, **uniformly in iota** ("The key innovation in Theorem 5.3 is that c_3 is uniform for all iota", p. 41).
9. **Theorem 5.2 from 5.3** (p. 41): split Lambda_(M,2M) into C-bar thin annuli of ratio 1 + iota with (1 + iota)^C-bar = 2; the domain Markov property makes each crossing conditionally <= 1 - c_3; product (1 - c_3)^C-bar <= c_2.
10. **Theorem 5.1 from 5.2: coarse-graining at one scale** (pp. 39-40): with kappa = 10^(-100) and M >= M_0(kappa), annulus crossings around disjoint blocks are conditionally <= kappa; a connection from u to v forces a lattice animal of m >= |u - v|/(4M) blocks, at most 8^(2m) animals, kappa^(m/25) each; sum gives exponential decay with rate ~ 1/M. Theorem 1.1 follows by step 3 (p. 40).

### A(3) Where N > 2 is used, and what fails

- **The one place:** Lemma 5.5, through the z-copy. Quoted, p. 64: "with probability at least (eps/(2+eps))^(k*) delta, there exist k* crossing loops in the z-copy, and conditioned on this, the probability for the existence of a crossing loop in the x- or y- copy is at most O(eps)". Section 5 opens (p. 39): "we will fix N = 2 + eps for some eps in (0, 1]". Abstract: the mechanism "emerges as soon as N > 2 (i.e., as soon as beyond the range of the XY model) and does not necessarily rely on the rotation symmetry in the sphere S^2". Every other step (FKG, Gaussian loop tail, spin waves on S^1, coarse-graining) is a statement about the XY loop model and holds at N = 2.
- **N = 2:** eps = 0, the z-copy is empty, a box is never good, c_5 = 0, and Theorem 5.3 degrades to Corollary 3.10 (c_3 -> 0 as iota -> 0): polynomial decay only. This is consistent with the BKT phase (1.8), p. 4. The text marks the dependence: c_5 depends on eps (Lemma 5.5), so c_beta degenerates as N decreases to 2.
- **A massless factor.** The argument is internal to one loop soup: it needs a third copy of the *same* soup whose loops are exchangeable with the XY loops (copy reassignment, Section 2.2.5, Lemma 2.5, p. 9) so that a crossing loop moved to the z-copy removes it from the XY copies. A product model O(N) x (massless factor) has no such loop exchange across factors; DHY applied to the O(N) factor gives decay of that factor's spin correlations only, and says nothing about the other factor. So DHY is selective in the lane's sense: it does not prove a gap for a product with a massless factor (it never reads the whole state space of a product). The DHY Remark 1.5 / [GdM26] contrast (p. 3) shows the selectivity inside the class of rotators too: for the anisotropic model with lambda in (-1, 1) there is a BKT phase at large beta, and DHY's z-copy mechanism needs the full O(N)-symmetric loop soup (isotropy gives the equal 1/N copy assignment).

## Step 5. Source A (DHY), items (4)-(6)

### A(4) Kind under lead rule W2; Lemma PT; Proposition NP

- **Kind: (K-one) seed with a one-scale engine; the seed is proved directly at every large scale, not carried by a flow.** The decay engine (Theorem 5.1, pp. 39-40) is a coarse-graining at one fixed block size M: its only input is the annulus bound P(dLambda_M <-xy-> dLambda_(2M) | outside) <= kappa = 10^(-100) under every xy boundary condition (Theorem 5.2 with c_2 = kappa). That is a property of the measure at one scale with a number in it, i.e. the (K-one) shape, and its engine is the lane's kind (compare Theorem S / Corollary S2: a seed at one size plus a dimension-free counting step). The seed itself (Theorems 5.2-5.3) is proved for every M >= M_0 at once, by a direct estimate inside one annulus; the multi-scale structure (good boxes from M^0.1 down to M^0.01, Definition 5.6) is a geometric covering inside that annulus, not an induction in scale that starts from small-scale data. There is no step from L to 2L and no use of first-passage data. So it is not (K-flow). Pei (arXiv:2611.01532, p. 5) describes the same shape for both 2D proofs: "a sum over coarse paths at one fixed scale N gives a positive mass".
- **Lemma PT: passes.** The proof reads one loop soup with N exchangeable copies; the gain comes from moving crossing loops into a third copy (step 6; copy reassignment, Section 2.2.5). A product with a massless factor offers no such exchange across factors; the proof says nothing about the product's other factor and does not claim a gap for it. Inside one model it is selective: at N = 2 it gives only Corollary 3.10 (no decay), matching the BKT phase. So it is not a blind tool: it fails where it should.
- **Proposition NP: passes trivially.** The argument never reads D, Phi, S or any free energy; its hypotheses are properties of the measure (crossing probabilities under all boundary conditions). NP binds steps that read the model only through finitely many values of D; DHY is not of that form.
- Caveat on the classification: W2 asks a (K-one) input to be tested against Proposition V (`gap-crux-fp.md` L356) with p named; I did not run that test (reading only). DHY's seed number is non-explicit: kappa = 10^(-100) is fixed but M_0(kappa) comes through existence choices (k* from Theorem 4.1, c_5(eps, beta_0, delta, rho) of Lemma 5.5), so the seed size is not given as a formula.

### A(6) Bridge to the lane's seed T on periodic tori (Corollary S2), for the 2D model only

- **What DHY gives:** decay of the spin one-point function under constant boundary conditions and of the two-point function under free boundary conditions, in boxes, uniformly in the box; internally (Theorem 5.1-5.2), connection decay of loop clusters under every *xy local-time* boundary condition in a box.
- **What T needs:** S(n; 2n) <= 1/20 for one n, i.e. g(n, 2n) = log W(n, 2n) - n log lambda(2n) <= log(21/20), for the periodic n x 2n torus. This counts every excited state of the circle-2n transfer operator with multiplicity (the vector triplet alone contributes about 3 e^(-n m(2n))), so it needs n m(2n) >= log 60 plus control of all higher states.
- **Bridge: NOT WRITTEN, not in DHY.** The links that are missing, in order:
  1. (B1) The transfer-operator setting for O(3) on a circle (positive, trace class, simple top eigenvalue): the lane marks it "plausible ... not proved" (`gap-crux-restate.md` L205, row 698).
  2. (B2) DHY's argument on a torus or an infinite cylinder of circumference 2n: the paper works on boxes with free or wired local-time boundaries (Section 2.2.2). The annulus seed holds under every xy boundary condition, so by the domain Markov property it plausibly holds for annuli of size M << n embedded in a torus; clusters winding around the torus and Lupu's isomorphism with killing on a torus would have to be handled. Not done anywhere I read.
  3. (B3) From connection decay to exponential smallness of g(n, 2n). This is a statement about the whole spectrum, i.e. a mixing statement for all local observables with arbitrary boundary data (weak mixing in the sense of Martinelli-Olivieri-Schonmann, which they show implies strong mixing in 2D for finite spin spaces; RECALLED, and not known to me for continuous compact spins). DHY Remark 1.3 (p. 2) says that even the one-point bound under *all* spin boundary conditions "does not seem to be that immediate": general spin boundary conditions turn into conditioning on non-connection events (V^+ not connected to V^-, p. 40). So the uniform-in-boundary input that B3 needs is exactly the part DHY flag as not immediate.
- **For the 4D chain:** no bridge; DHY is a 2D spin-model statement (A(5)).
