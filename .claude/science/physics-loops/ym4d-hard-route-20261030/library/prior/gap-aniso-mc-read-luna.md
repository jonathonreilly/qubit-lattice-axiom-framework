## Anisotropic gauge studies

**Fu–Nielsen / abelian layered phase (accessible later account; original paper not read).** Fu and Nielsen's 1984 proposal is for pure anisotropic U(1), not SU(2) or SU(3). The open 1994 follow-up by Hulsebos, Korthals-Altes and Nicolis (arXiv:hep-th/9406003, pp. 1–2) states: “A layered phase is present” for abelian gauge theory in 4+D dimensions and says its second-order boundary exists for \(D\le3\). Its prototype has four-dimensional plaquette coupling \(\beta\) and transverse coupling \(\beta'\); within a layer loops are Coulomb/perimeter-like and those crossing the extra direction are area-law-like. This is a mean-field phase argument with one-loop/strong-coupling corrections discussed in the source; no lattice size, measured mass, mass error, or anisotropy-dependent spectrum is supplied. The earlier Fu–Nielsen 1984 full text was not found as an open copy in this pass; this is a later paper's summary, not a direct reading of Fu–Nielsen.


**Four-dimensional independent plaquette couplings (theoretical, not spectroscopy).** The accessible abstract for *Lattice gauge theory in four dimensions: Decoupling points and a dual symmetric perturbation theory* (Nucl. Phys. B 190 (1981) 519–541, DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(81)90446-6) describes a general compact gauge group with six independent plaquette energies \(E_{\mu\nu}\). At a limit with one pair of plaquette energies sent to infinity and the complementary pair to zero it derives an exact relation to two-dimensional systems; its explicit Ising illustration is for \(Z_2\). This is relevant to plane-type anisotropy as an exact endpoint construction, but no mass/string-tension measurements are given in the abstract, and the full paper was not available as an open copy in this pass. It does not supply a mass monotonicity result for SU(2)/SU(3).

**Pure SU(2), five dimensions.** Knechtli, Luz and Rago, *On the phase structure of five-dimensional SU(2) gauge theories with anisotropic couplings* (arXiv:1110.4210; Nucl. Phys. B 856 (2012) 74–94), is the closest open non-Abelian test. Its Wilson action has four-dimensional plaquettes weighted by \(\beta_4=\beta/\gamma\) and mixed fifth-direction plaquettes by \(\beta_5=\beta\gamma\); classically \(\gamma=a_4/a_5\). They report a line of first-order bulk transitions separating confined and deconfined phases. No second-order bulk transition supporting a pure-SU(2) layered continuum limit was found in the explored region. At confined-vacuum transition points \((\beta_4,\beta_5)=(2.33,0.937),(2.50,0.8697)\), periodic \(32^4\times16\), the measured static-potential string estimates are \(\sigma r_0^2=1.3(2),1.4(2)\), respectively. Their slope data trend from a five-dimensional-string value at the coarser point toward a four-dimensional-string value at the finer point; the authors explicitly say there is no scaling between the two points and they are probably not on a line of constant physics. This is a suggestion of dimensional reduction/localization, not a mass measurement at fixed coupling or a monotonicity test in one plane coupling. Their conclusion says their SU(2) Monte Carlo “did not find a second order bulk phase transition” for a continuum limit; the account's result is compatible with the absence of the predicted pure-gauge layered phase in 5D. No error bars are quoted for the visual slope trend.


**Larger-volume 5D SU(2) follow-up.** Del Debbio, Kenway, Lambrou and Rinaldi ([arXiv:1305.0752](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0752)) use the same anisotropic SU(2) Wilson action with \(\beta_4\) and \(\beta_5\), simulate \(16^5,20^4\times8,24^4\times8\), and check one point on \(32^4\times8\). At \(\beta_4=2.60\), the \(16^5\) susceptibility scan estimates \(\beta_5^c=0.8437(5)\); larger-volume scans at \(\beta_5=0.843,0.8435,0.844,0.8445\) show two-state/hysteresis signals increasingly clear on \(24^4\times8\) and separated hot/cold states at \(32^4\times8,\beta_5=0.844\). The authors conclude the transition is first-order at the point previously claimed as the first-to-second-order endpoint, making pure SU(2) 5D layer-phase dimensional reduction “unpromising.” This is plaquette/Polyakov-loop phase evidence, not a mass or string-tension monotonicity measurement.

**SU(3) approximation claim, not measured result.** An open-indexed 2001 VCE/mean-field paper, *The Layer Phase in the Non-isotropic SU(3) Gauge Model at Finite Temperature* (arXiv:hep-th/0101146), claims a layer phase for D=4 and D=5. It is an approximation study, not lattice Monte Carlo spectroscopy. The linked indexed PDF returned 404, so its action coefficients, lattices, observables and errors were not checked; do not treat the abstract-level prediction as measured evidence.

**SU(2) adjoint-Higgs exception in 5D.** Dimopoulos, Farakos and Koutsoumbas (arXiv:hep-lat/0111047) study a five-dimensional anisotropic SU(2) adjoint-Higgs model, by Monte Carlo and mean field, with a distinguished fifth direction and scalar self-coupling fixed at \(\beta_R=0.01\). Their abstract's claim is specifically a layered **Higgs** phase with unbroken U(1) within the layers and confinement in the extra direction; they contrast it with pure SU(2). Simulations are reported mainly on \(4^5,6^5\), with selected \(8^5\) runs, and scan gauge couplings \(\beta_g=0.5,1.2,1.8\) while varying Higgs coupling \(\beta_h\) for selected boundaries. The reported evidence is plaquette and order-parameter behavior/phase transitions, not a glueball mass or string-tension curve with errors versus anisotropy. It does not test monotonicity of mass in \(\beta_L\).

**Four-dimensional SU(2) “layered symmetry breaking” is not the same anisotropy.** Grady, *Evidence for layered symmetry breaking in SU(2) lattice gauge theory* (arXiv:hep-lat/0409163), reports a remnant-symmetry order parameter after partial axial gauge fixing on 4D SU(2) lattices; the average link pointing in the fixed fourth direction is tested in each transverse hyperplane. The source says weak-coupling symmetry breaking occurs independently in hyperplanes; the transition is studied on the Wilson axis and in the fundamental-adjoint plane at \(\beta_A=1.5\), with sizes up to \(16^4\). It reports susceptibility/Binder plots, but no mass/string-tension measurement with statistical errors and no independent plane-coupling scan. This is not the Fu–Nielsen pure U(1) layered phase, nor evidence for monotonicity of a gauge-invariant mass as one plaquette coupling is varied. It is a gauge-fixed remnant-symmetry claim and should be described as such.

**Standard anisotropic glueball spectroscopy (temporal/spatial anisotropy).** Morningstar and Peardon, *Efficient glueball simulations on anisotropic lattices* (arXiv:hep-lat/9704011) and *Glueball spectrum from an anisotropic lattice study* (arXiv:hep-lat/9901004), use an improved/tadpole mean-field SU(3) pure-gauge action with spatial and temporal couplings, bare aspect parameter \(\xi\), and \(a_s/a_t=\xi\) at tree level. The 1999 paper's \(\xi=5\) ensembles are \(6^3\times30\) at \(\beta=1.7,1.9\), \(8^3\times40\) at \(\beta=2.2,2.4\), and \(10^3\times50\) at \(\beta=2.5\); the additional \(\beta=3.0,\xi=3\) ensemble is \(15^3\times45\). In units of \(a_t^{-1}\), the scalar A1++ mass is 0.303(3) at (2.4,5), 0.288(4) at (2.5,5), and 0.318(4) at (3.0,3); the paper tabulates errors for all channels. Its explicit statement “In all cases, glueball effective masses are seen to converge monotonically from above” concerns effective-mass plateaus as Euclidean time increases, **not** monotonicity in a bare coupling. These simulations mostly hold \(\xi=5\) while changing \(\beta\); the one \(\xi=3\) result also changes \(\beta\), so the tables do not isolate a fixed-\(\beta\) derivative in \(\xi\). Temporal/spatial anisotropy buys time resolution; it is not the L/M/T plane split.

The 1997 study uses two temporal anisotropies and coarse spatial spacings about 0.2–0.4 fm; it reports SU(3) low-lying 0++, 2++ and 1+- spectroscopy, not different couplings among spatial plane types. These are method/scale-setting controls only. No quoted result establishes monotonicity of the lightest mass under an isolated plane-coupling change.

Teper's review *Glueball masses and other physical properties of SU(N) gauge theories in D=3+1* (arXiv:hep-th/9812187) is a synthesis, not a new fixed-coupling anisotropy study. Again all plaquettes share the isotropic coupling. This is a useful modern control, not a monotonicity theorem.

## Two-dimensional O(4) / SU(2) principal-chiral anchor

The SU(2) principal chiral model is equivalent to the O(4) sigma model after identifying SU(2) with unit quaternions. The exact coupling convention must be matched: the face paper uses a nearest-neighbour quaternion dot product with coefficient \(\beta_M\); many PCM papers instead write a prefactor such as \(-2N\beta\operatorname{ReTr}\) and so their printed \(\beta\) is not directly the face \(\beta_M\).

An open searchable table excerpt for the standard 2D O(4) sigma model reports at \(\beta=2.50\): \(\xi=34.85(9)\), and two-/three-/four-loop prediction ratios 0.911(2), 0.930(2), 0.949(2), respectively. At \(\beta=2.80\), it reports \(\xi=86.07(37)\). This matches the face coupling only if the source's action convention is the standard unit-vector bond action \(S=-\beta\sum_{\langle xy\rangle}\phi_x\cdot\phi_y\); it is near, not across, the full [2.3,2.6] window. However, the indexed PDF copy for that table returned 404 in this pass, and Heller's 1988 primary O(4) measurement paper was not readable: the APS PDF request returned 401 and the attempted DESY mirror returned 404. Treat the quoted 34.85(9) as an unverified search-index lead, not a source-checked value. Heller's accessible abstract says new correlation-length and susceptibility measurements supported asymptotic freedom, but gives no numeric data in the abstract.

A directly read open SU(N)×SU(N) PCM paper by Rossi and Vicari (arXiv:hep-lat/9401029) uses \(S_L=-2N\beta\sum_{x,\mu}\operatorname{ReTr}[U_xU_{x+\mu}^\dagger]\). Its numerical table is for N=3, 9, 15, 21, 30, not N=2; e.g. N=3, \(\beta=0.30,L=90\) gives \(\xi_G=10.41(10)\), while \(\beta=0.315,L=120\) gives 15.5(3). It cannot be used as a numerical O(4) benchmark by identifying N=3 with SU(2). The authors explicitly note N=2 equivalence to O(4), but this particular table does not list N=2 measurements. Therefore the anchor's exact coupling-specific O(4) correlation length still needs a directly readable, convention-matched primary source.
