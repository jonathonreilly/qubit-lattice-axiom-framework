## Sources and what they establish

### Perturbative anisotropy coefficients and scale ratios

- **Frithjof Karsch, “SU(N) Gauge Theory Couplings on Asymmetric Lattices” (1982).** For the Euclidean Wilson action with distinct spatial and temporal couplings and asymmetry `xi=a/a_t`, background-field perturbation theory gives the weak-coupling derivatives at `xi=1`: for SU(2), the spatial/time derivatives are `0.11403` and `−0.06759`; for SU(3), `0.20161` and `−0.13195`. Their sum is `b0=11N/(48 pi^2)`. The paper computes the one-loop anisotropic Lambda parameter for general finite asymmetry and recovers Hasenfratz–Hasenfratz in the Hamiltonian limit `xi -> infinity`. **Status/domain:** perturbative, weak bare coupling, with a fixed geometric anisotropy; it is not a strong-coupling interpolation theorem. Karsch explicitly says that the tuned, universal invariance of all physical quantities under changing anisotropy does *not* hold in the strong-coupling regime. This is the direct limit on using the one-loop coefficients in row 1053's strong-transverse-coupling region. I read the open scan, especially its introduction, equations (2.4)–(2.7), (2.27)–(2.29), and (3.9)–(3.10). [Paper scan](https://noah.nrw/ubbihs/download/pdf/5114815), [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(82)90390-X).

- **Anna and Peter Hasenfratz, “The Connection Between the Lambda Parameters of Lattice and Continuum QCD” (1980).** Their one-loop comparison of lattice and continuum MOM schemes reports `Lambda_MOM/Lambda_latt = 57.5` for SU(2), `83.5` for SU(3), at gauge parameter `alpha=1`. **Status/domain:** perturbative scheme conversion, not a nonperturbative matching or gap result. The open CERN/INSPIRE record gives the result; I did not obtain/read the full paper. [CERN record](https://cds.cern.ch/record/133703).

- **Anna and Peter Hasenfratz, “The Scales of Euclidean and Hamiltonian Lattice QCD” (1981).** They recalculate the MOM-to-Euclidean-lattice ratio and obtain the Hamiltonian-to-Euclidean lattice Lambda ratio `0.968 exp(−0.5495/N^2)`, quoted as about `0.91` for SU(3), `0.84` for SU(2). **Status/domain:** perturbative scale conversion in the Hamiltonian/continuous-time limit. It identifies scales, not an isotropic continuum path from a strongly coupled layered phase. I read the INSPIRE/journal abstract only; no open full copy was found. [INSPIRE record](https://inspirehep.net/literature/165092), [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90526-5).

### Renormalized anisotropy calibration

- **T. R. Klassen, “The Anisotropic Wilson Gauge Action” (1998).** In quenched four-dimensional pure SU(3) Wilson gauge theory with temporal spacing finer than spatial spacing, Klassen determines the map from bare anisotropy `xi0` to renormalized `xi` by matching spatial and temporal heavy-quark potentials. Data cover five anisotropies `1.5 <= xi <= 6`; the fit incorporates the one-loop result and is advertised for `1 <= xi <= 6`, `5.5 <= beta <= infinity`. Agreement is at a fraction of one percent at weak coupling and about one percent at strong coupling. **Status/domain:** Monte Carlo calibration plus a fitted interpolation, not a theorem; the strong-coupling accuracy is empirical and for this temporal/spatial SU(3) setup. It shows that holding *renormalized temporal anisotropy* fixed is practically possible on the studied range. It does not calibrate the row-1053 2+2 coupling family. Full arXiv copy read. [arXiv:hep-lat/9803010](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9803010).

- **Later methods.** Sakai, Saito and Nakamura (2000) calculate one-loop anisotropy and coupling coefficients for plaquette-plus-rectangle improved gauge actions and report numerical agreement in weak-coupling simulations; this is perturbative/improved-action work, not a strong-coupling Wilson theorem. Carena, Gustafson, Lamm, Li and Liu (2022) derive bare/renormalized relations for SU(N) and U(N) at any temporal anisotropy and spatial dimension, then compare with existing nonperturbative SU(2)/U(1) determinations, finding discrepancies below 10%. Their relations remain perturbative; their discrete-group Monte Carlo checks are separate. [Sakai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0002029), [Carena et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10417). I read the full 2022 preprint; Sakai's open abstract only.

### Anisotropic glueball calculations

- **Colin Morningstar and Mike Peardon, “The Glueball Spectrum from an Anisotropic Lattice Study” (1999).** Monte Carlo simulations of pure, quenched SU(3) use spatial spacings about `0.1–0.4 fm` and a finer temporal spacing, with improved action/operators. The paper extracts low-lying glueball masses, studies finite-volume and discretization errors, and separates single-glueball states from two-glueball and torelon-pair contamination. **Status/domain:** numerical spectroscopy at finite cutoffs, with continuum spin assignments and extrapolation; it is not a proof of a mass gap or a trajectory out of a 2+2 strong-coupling layer. Full arXiv copy read. [arXiv:hep-lat/9901004](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9901004).

### Orland and the Hamiltonian/strongly anisotropic limits

- **Peter Orland (2006), “Integrable Models and Confinement in (2+1)-Dimensional Weakly-Coupled Yang-Mills Theory.”** In axial gauge, the anisotropic Hamiltonian has `H=H0+e'^2 H1`; `H0` is a collection of decoupled `(1+1)`-dimensional SU(2) principal-chiral/O(4) models. Form factors give the horizontal string tension at small `e'`, with both gauge couplings weak. Orland explicitly notes that its coupling dependence does not follow the usual dimensional estimate. **Status/domain:** controlled physics calculation around the extreme anisotropic, weak-coupling limit; not a four-dimensional Wilson-lattice theorem and not an isotropic-trajectory construction. Full arXiv copy read. [arXiv:hep-th/0607013](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0607013).

- **Orland (2007), “Glueball Masses in (2+1)-Dimensional Anisotropic Weakly-Coupled Yang-Mills Theory.”** For SU(2), the light states are pairs of fundamental particles of the `(1+1)`-dimensional `SU(2) x SU(2)` principal-chiral model, bound by a linear potential; the overlap matching condition is fixed by its exact S-matrix. The calculation is near the weakly coupled anisotropic limit (`g'_0` small), and gives the lightest singlet spectrum in that approximation. **Status/domain:** analytic/integrability-based physics calculation, not rigorous construction; dimension is 2+1. Full arXiv copy read. [arXiv:0704.0940](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0940).

- **Orland (2008), “Near-Integrability and Confinement for High-Energy Hadron-Hadron Collisions.”** This is an effective large-`s` QCD Hamiltonian after longitudinal rescaling (`lambda << 1`), with coupled `(1+1)`-dimensional principal-chiral models after a transverse lattice construction. It computes string tensions in that eikonal/high-energy approximation. Orland says the approximation is not established as QCD. **Status/domain:** effective-model analysis, not an isotropic Wilson continuum limit. It is useful ancestry for the coupled-chiral anchor, but does not prove the specific 2+2 lattice region or its bridge. Full arXiv copy read. [arXiv:0801.0389](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0389).

- **Kogut and Susskind (1975), “Hamiltonian Formulation of Wilson’s Lattice Gauge Theories.”** Wilson’s model is rewritten as a canonical Hamiltonian of coupled rigid rotators with electric-flux strings; strong-coupling physics is described by these strings. The Hamiltonian limit takes time continuous while space remains lattice-discretized. **Status/domain:** formal Hamiltonian construction and strong-coupling interpretation, not a result about a 2+2 anisotropic Euclidean region or its isotropic continuum connection. APS provides the abstract; I could not read an open full copy. [Phys. Rev. D 11, 395](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395).

### Layer phases and dimensional reduction

- **Fu and Nielsen (1984), “A Layer Phase in a Non-Isotropic U(1) Lattice Gauge Theory.”** Their mean-field treatment, with one-loop and strong-coupling corrections, predicts a phase Coulomb-like within `d`-dimensional layers and strong-coupling in the other directions for `D >= 5`, `d=D-1`. Thus the canonical example is five-dimensional U(1) with four-dimensional Coulomb layers and confinement in the fifth direction. This is dimensional reduction/localization, but the layers are *massless Coulomb*, not gapped Yang-Mills. **Status/domain:** mean-field/approximation, not a rigorous phase theorem; it does not predict the same phase in four-dimensional pure SU(2). Full paper was not available openly; I used bibliographic metadata and the reported abstract only. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90529-7).

- **Later five-dimensional U(1).** Dimopoulos, Farakos and Vrentzos (2006) study the pure anisotropic five-dimensional Wilson U(1) model by Monte Carlo and mean field. With four-dimensional coupling `beta4` near `1.01` and transverse `beta5=0.01` or `0.2`, they find a layer phase: four-dimensional Coulomb behavior, fifth-direction confinement. Their finite-size analysis favors a weak first-order strong-to-layer transition; the layer-to-five-dimensional-Coulomb transition has evidence for second order. The values are simulation estimates, not exact critical couplings. **Status/domain:** numerical plus mean field; no rigorous dimensional-reduction limit, and no mass gap inside the Coulomb layers. Full arXiv copy read. [arXiv:hep-lat/0607033](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0607033).

- **Pure five-dimensional SU(2).** Mean-field studies predicted a layer phase for anisotropic Wilson couplings with `a5>a4` (`gamma<1`). Knechtli, Luz and Rago (2011) simulate the Wilson action and find the bulk confinement/deconfinement line first order; they report no evidence for the proposed continuous transition in their studied region. Del Debbio, Kenway, Lambrou and Rinaldi (2013), on larger lattices, find the first-order line persists to `beta4=2.60`, with no trace of the layer phase there; they say a continuum limit cannot be taken up to that point, while explicitly not excluding a second-order point at higher `beta4`. **Status/domain:** Monte Carlo in five dimensions, not proof; it weakens this route to a four-dimensional continuum theory. Full arXiv copies read. [2011 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.4210), [2013 paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0752).

- **Four-dimensional SU(2) qualifications.** “Evidence for Layered Symmetry Breaking in SU(2) Lattice Gauge Theory” (2004) reports simulations of four-dimensional SU(2) after partial axial gauge fixing; the residual symmetry, global in three directions and local in one, appears to break at weak coupling. This is a gauge-fixed residual-symmetry signal, not a gauge-invariant proof of a four-dimensional layer phase or a gap. A 1995 finite-temperature SU(2) layer-phase paper by Huang, Fu and Chen is cited in later literature, but I could not access its full text or verify its precise coupling region; I do not use it as evidence for a pure four-dimensional continuum bridge. [2004 arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0409163).

- **Matter-coupled variants are distinct.** Five-dimensional anisotropic SU(2)-adjoint-Higgs simulations find layered/Higgs structure associated with residual U(1), but this changes the theory by adding scalar matter. It does not establish a pure Wilson SU(2) layer phase. [Dimopoulos, Farakos and Koutsoumbas](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0111047).

### Rigorous results and the endpoint

- **Osterwalder and Seiler (1978), “Gauge Field Theories on a Lattice.”** The paper proves existence and analyticity of the infinite-volume limit for strongly coupled lattice Yang-Mills and verifies Wilson’s confinement bound, alongside reflection positivity/positive transfer-matrix results. The coupling region is a sufficiently small neighborhood of strong bare coupling. **Status/domain:** rigorous strong-coupling result. The accessible abstract does not state an anisotropic layer theorem; it does not connect this region to weak coupling or an isotropic continuum limit. I could access only the abstract, not a full open copy. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8).

- **Exact decoupled 2+2 endpoint.** At the formal face `beta_L -> infinity`, `beta_T=0`, the remaining mixed plaquettes give a product of 2D O(4) principal-chiral models (the row-1053 `ANCH` identity). This factorization is an exact model identity. Orland’s anisotropic continuum analysis is related in structure, but in 2+1 dimensions and with different variables/scaling. The 2D endpoint does not by itself establish a neighborhood at nonzero transverse coupling, an all-observable singlet gap, or continuation to isotropic 4D. Row 1061 also identifies the proposed matching at row-1053 line 183 as unsupported: assigning the sheet coupling at the transverse physical scale is an extra matching premise. Row 1061 therefore leaves V2 open; it corrects row 1053’s earlier claim that the one-loop exclusion had established that V2 merely renames the band.

## What is known about the isotropic connection

There is an established perturbative and numerical framework for *temporal* anisotropy: one-loop matching near weak coupling (Karsch), nonperturbative calibration of `xi0` to renormalized `xi` over a stated SU(3) beta range (Klassen), and anisotropic simulations that use the finer time spacing to resolve spectra (Morningstar–Peardon). These show how practitioners tune a fixed renormalized temporal anisotropy and extrapolate observables. They are not a theorem about all anisotropies, and their regime is not the 2+2 face with strong transverse coupling.

For the strongly anisotropic 2+2 region, I found no published result proving that a family with fixed renormalized anisotropy reaches the isotropic continuum, and no nonperturbative transport theorem carrying the anchor’s gap to that limit. Karsch’s perturbative coefficients apply in weak coupling and Karsch expressly limits universality to that regime. Orland’s models support an anisotropic mechanism and compute in selected effective limits; they do not provide the missing Wilson-lattice matching. The later U(1)/SU(2) layer literature is mostly mean-field or numerical and, where it has a layer phase, often gives Coulomb/massless layers rather than the required SU(2) gap. The bridge remains open in the literature reviewed here; this is a bounded search result, not a no-go theorem.
