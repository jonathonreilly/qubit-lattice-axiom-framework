### (1) Interpolations and twist/flux free energies

- **Discrete center sectors and twisted-boundary free energy.** de Forcrand–Jahn, [arXiv:hep-lat/0209060](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0209060), full open text, §§2–4. They define SU(2) twisted boundary conditions by anticommuting transition functions, which change the parity of center vortices through a plane; their vortex free energy is the ratio (Z^{tbc}/Z^{pbc}). They measure electric-twist free energies in SU(2) and SO(3). The exact text says the free energy is an order parameter; it does not define a continuous SU(2) family of measure-preserving link maps. **Lane:** twist-sector framework already covered by FINISH 739 and its linked prior; the exact link-map path is new in FINISH 1556(v).
- **Continuous flux path in compact (U(1)).** Vettorazzo–de Forcrand, [arXiv:hep-lat/0311007](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0311007), full open text, §§2–4. They add an arbitrary real flux φ to a plaquette stack, spread it over all parallel plaquettes by a change of variables, and study (Z(\phi)), which is (2\pi)-periodic. Their Coulomb-phase fit is a sum over flux sectors with quadratic cost β_R(\phi-2\pi k)^2/2; they define the helicity modulus as (F''(0)). The paper explicitly calls this the Abelian extension of the non-Abelian twist free energy. This is a continuous action/background-flux path, not the proposed deterministic SU(2) action on a cut and not a two-boundary mixed derivative. **Lane:** U(1) flux and helicity-modulus literature is already within the broader FINISH 739 flux scan; the specific covariance target is absent there. FINISH 930 is instead a Gaussian toron/pi-twist test.
- **Non-Abelian Abelian-projection extension.** The same Vettorazzo–de Forcrand paper, §5, treats SU(2) only after Abelian projection and under two explicit assumptions: effective Abelian long-range behavior and a Wilson-like effective action. It reports numerical helicity-modulus measurements, with gauge dependence. It is not a result for the unprojected SU(2) Wilson measure. **Lane:** projection/flux phenomena fall under FINISH 739; no matching map or boundary-covariance estimate appears there.
- **Vortex insertion and Fourier electric flux.** Kovács–Tomboulis, [arXiv:hep-lat/0002004](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0002004), full open text, abstract and definitions. They define magnetic-vortex free energy as the ratio of partition functions with and without a topologically trapped vortex, inserted by a singular gauge transformation; its center Fourier transform defines electric-flux free energy and yields a rigorous Wilson-loop upper bound. Their reported weak-coupling thick-vortex result is from multihistogram simulations. No continuous path or boundary-slice covariance is estimated. **Lane:** already recorded in FINISH 739 / `gap-lit-flux.md` (including that the measurement is numerical).
- **Reflection-positivity/Tomboulis–Yaffe family.** Kanazawa, [arXiv:0808.3442](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3442), full open text, abstract and §§1–2. It extends the SU(2) Tomboulis–Yaffe inequality to SU(N) and classical spin systems by reflection positivity. The theorem links twisted-boundary sensitivity to Wilson-loop correlations; it is not a bound on a mixed derivative of two separated boundary data. Kanazawa states that sufficient boundary insensitivity implies a nonzero mass gap. **Lane:** already covered in FINISH 739 / `gap-lit-flux.md`, which records the theorem and its limitations.
- **Volume relation for twisted/untwisted ratios.** Kanazawa, [arXiv:0805.2742](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2742), full open text, abstract and §§1–3. It derives an equality relating linear combinations of vortex free energies on different lattice sizes, with a numerator/denominator coupling discrepancy that the paper argues vanishes in the thermodynamic limit. The paper also examines the Tomboulis proof. This is a partition-function size relation, not a differentiable interpolation or a covariance decay bound. **Lane:** already represented in FINISH 739 / `gap-lit-flux.md`.
- **Mack–Petkova and ’t Hooft context.** Mack–Petkova, DOI [10.1016/0003-4916(79)90346-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(79)90346-4), is represented here through Kanazawa’s open 2009 discussion and the open DESY account [DESY 81-050](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1981/desy81-050.pdf): the disorder/vortex free energy compares twisted and untwisted partition functions, with twist produced by a singular gauge transformation on boundary values; Mack–Petkova compare SU(2) and Z2 Wilson-loop expectations. ’t Hooft’s original paper is DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(78)90153-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(78)90153-0); the open Kanazawa account describes its center-vortex/twisted-boundary construction. I did not read the closed original journal texts. These sources concern endpoint sectors and defect free energy, not the target path. **Lane:** already in the FINISH 739 literature map; no exact map/covariance analogue is listed.
- **Finite-temperature inequality family.** Borgs–Seiler, *Lattice Yang-Mills theory at nonzero temperature and the confinement problem*, CMP 91 (1983), 329–380, DOI [10.1007/BF01208780](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01208780). The open INIS record gives the authors’ abstract: inequalities relate Wilson, Polyakov, and ’t Hooft confinement criteria; infrared bounds imply a weak-coupling nonconfining regime at nonzero temperature in at least three spatial dimensions. This is finite-temperature/string-tension material, not the zero-temperature cylinder covariance. **Lane:** cited in the FINISH 739 source map; no matching boundary mixed derivative.
- **Forward citations through 2026.** Hayashi–Tanizaki, [arXiv:2606.17708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17708), abstract only. They propose torus and lens-space symmetry-twisted partition functions as center-vortex and monopole-condensation criteria, then study the leading contributions in an adjoint-Higgs phase and prove an implication between condensations in a gapped phase. Morikawa, [arXiv:2609.03525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03525), abstract only. It computes a continuum one-loop Gaussian response to a global center twist in a refined Gribov–Zwanziger framework on (T^4), while stating that an unfixed relative zero-mode/stabilizer normalization prevents the Gaussian calculation from establishing the vortex-condensation criterion. Both are relevant 2026 forward developments of twisted partition functions, but neither is a lattice Haar-preserving interpolation or cylinder covariance estimate. **Lane:** not present in FINISH 739 (which predates them); they add context only, not the target lemma.

**Answer to (1).** Yes, there is a continuous external-flux interpolation in compact (U(1)), and the literature extensively studies discrete center-twisted versus untwisted SU(N) sectors, vortex free energies, electric/magnetic fluxes, ’t Hooft loops, and interface/free-energy criteria. The sources read do not give the specific deterministic cut-link circle in FINISH 1556(v), nor a center-equivariant Haar-preserving link map with that exact endpoint. The fact that the SU(N) center is \(\mathbb Z_N\) makes its twist labels discrete; this does not prohibit choosing a continuous path of actions or configurations between endpoint ensembles. I found no such path with the claimed two-end derivative identity/estimate in the primary sources searched. This is a scoped search result, not a proof of absence.

### (2) Bounds on opposite-boundary covariances

- The Tomboulis–Yaffe inequality and its SU(N) extension bound Wilson-loop expectations in terms of center-vortex free energy, under reflection positivity. This is an endpoint partition-ratio implication, not a connected covariance between separated boundary slices. Kanazawa’s open theorem is the source statement; see FINISH 739 for the lane’s prior theorem-level review.
- Mack–Petkova comparison inequalities relate SU(2) and Z2 Wilson observables/disorder parameters; Borgs–Seiler derive finite-temperature inequalities among Wilson, Polyakov, and ’t Hooft criteria. The hypotheses and observables differ from local derivatives of a finite-temperature-free cylinder kernel. Both are already in the FINISH 739 map.
- Strong-coupling cluster expansions do give exponential control of vortex free energy in the regimes stated by the source literature (Kanazawa 0805.2742, §1, describing the cluster-expansion result). This is regime-specific and for a global twist free energy. It does not give a uniform all-coupling bound on the proposed mixed boundary covariance. Simulations of vortex/flux response are evidence only.
- A rigorous correlation result for **finite** gauge groups, Adhikari–Cao, [arXiv:2202.10375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10375), DOI [10.1214/24-AOP1702](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-AOP1702), proves exponential covariance decay for gauge-invariant rectangular-loop observables in a specified large-β regime. The group is finite and the observable is a loop pair. It neither covers compact continuous U(1) or SU(2) Wilson theory at the target coupling nor provides the target boundary derivative. **Lane:** already recorded in FINISH 739 / `gap-lit-flux.md`.

I found no rigorous all-coupling or order-one-coupling estimate for connected covariances of local boundary-slice derivatives across a lattice-gauge cylinder, and no twist free-energy identity controlled by the proposed mixed derivative. In particular, I found none with the uniform (v^{-2-\epsilon}) bound, suprema over the two cut rotations, expectation of the absolute derivative, and sum over an opposite cut required in FINISH 1556(v). This is not a no-go theorem. The known results do not, on the evidence found, distinguish this estimate for 4D SU(2) from 4D compact U(1), or establish a 4D-versus-5D distinction. FINISH 936’s ORBIT result is a nearby cut-rotation estimate at the rotor scale; its own result leaves the averaged force-covariance target open. FINISH 937 checked that status. The new question is therefore a refinement, not a duplicate of that result.

### (3) Four-dimensional compact (U(1)) Coulomb comparator

- **Rigorous nonconfinement, Villain action.** Guth, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.21.2291](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.21.2291), full open mirror text/abstract. For 4D Villain U(1), (g^2<0.168), he proves a Coulomb-form lower bound on Wilson-loop expectation, guaranteeing nonconfinement and existence of its infinite-volume limit. This is a rigorous weak-coupling result for the Villain action, not a field-strength two-point decay theorem or a bound for the Wilson-action mixed derivative. **Lane:** the U(1) fact tests in FINISH 930 and 936 are Gaussian/rotor and cut-gap tests, respectively; the broad FINISH 739 literature review includes the known U(1) Coulomb comparison but not this new correlation question.
++- **Flux free-energy size behavior.** In Vettorazzo–de Forcrand 0311007, §3, the numerical data are on a (4^4) box. The Coulomb-phase profile is fitted by the flux-sector sum with renormalized β_R; each sector has a quadratic cost. For a Gaussian Maxwell field, the classical cost of fixed total flux through a plane is proportional to the aspect ratio (L_\rho L_\sigma/(L_\mu L_\nu)), hence order one on symmetric 4-tori; the paper’s symmetric-box profile is consistent with that. This is a Maxwell/sector-sum scaling comparison, not a proved large-volume theorem for compact Wilson U(1), and it is not a decay of the twist free energy to zero. **Lane:** flux profile/order parameter is already part of FINISH 739; no boundary covariance estimate is recorded.
+- **Field-strength correlator scale.** In the free 4D Maxwell theory, the massless gauge propagator scales as (r^{-2}); two derivatives give a generic nonzero connected (F_{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\rho\sigma}(0)) component of order (r^{-4}). Summing that kernel over a two-dimensional opposite slice at distance (v) gives, in the continuum approximation, \(\int_{\mathbb R^2}d^2y\,(v^2+|y|^2)^{-2}=\pi v^{-2}\). This is a direct Gaussian calculation, not a theorem for compact Wilson U(1); angular/tensor cancellations can occur for particular components. The compact-U(1) Coulomb phase is expected and numerically treated as a massless-photon phase, but the sources located here do not prove this pointwise gauge-invariant (F)-(F) asymptotic in the interacting compact model. Consequently (v^{-2-\epsilon}) is sharper than the generic Maxwell slice-sum scale; comparison alone neither proves nor refutes the proposed expectation bound, because its derivative observable and absolute value may change the scaling.
+- **Numerical photon/field evidence.** Vettorazzo–de Forcrand’s flux/helicity-modulus work is numerical. Cella et al., [arXiv:hep-lat/9704012](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9704012), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.56.3896](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.56.3896), numerically study the heavy-charge potential and finite-size effects in the compact-U(1) Coulomb phase on a (16^3\times32) box. These are standard computations of a Coulomb response/potential, not direct rigorous estimates for the local connected field-strength correlator in question. **Lane:** existing Gaussian U(1) comparisons are FINISH 930 and 936; the compact-U(1) observable distinction was not previously stated for this new covariance target.

**Answer to (3).** The firm rigorous comparison found is nonconfinement/masslessness for 4D Villain U(1) at weak coupling, not the requested Wilson-action covariance rate. Standard free-Maxwell behavior predicts an unsummed field-strength correlator of order (r^{-4}), a two-dimensional opposite-slice sum of order (v^{-2}), and an order-one flux free-energy profile at fixed aspect ratio. These are a comparator, not a theorem about the compact interacting model. Thus (v^{-2-\epsilon}) would be faster than the generic massless Maxwell scale and could be selective if the proposed derivative has nonzero Maxwell overlap; this remains a testable inference, not an established SU(2)/U(1) theorem.
