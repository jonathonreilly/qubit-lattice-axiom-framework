## 1. Purity, replica swaps, period/coupling dependence, and interpolation

### Exact finite-volume facts

For a finite-dimensional Hamiltonian, let `Z(β)=Tr(e^{-βH})` and `ρβ=e^{-βH}/Z(β)`. Then `Tr(ρβ²)=Z(2β)/Z(β)²`. Direct differentiation gives

`dβ log[Z(2β)/Z(β)²] = 2 (⟨H⟩β − ⟨H⟩2β) ≥ 0` for `β ≥ 0`,

because `dβ⟨H⟩β = −Varβ(H) ≤ 0`. Thus this finite-volume Gibbs purity is nondecreasing with inverse temperature. This elementary identity is not a claim of coupling monotonicity, nor does it provide a continuous interpolation between distinct replica geometries. For a positive transfer spectrum `λj ≥ 0`, the period-`T` trace ratio is `Σj pj(T)²`, where `pj(T)=λj^T/Σk λk^T`; the same power-concentration argument makes it nondecreasing in `T`. These statements require the stated positive spectral weights. They do not justify applying the conclusion to signed/projected transfer traces or to a coupling parameter without additional hypotheses. The curvature is `d²β log P = 4 Var2β(H) − 2 Varβ(H)`, with no fixed sign from this identity alone.

A simple finite-size converse illustrates the assumptions needed for gap extraction: if `P=Tr(ρβ²)>1/2`, then the largest Gibbs eigenvalue is unique and the ratio of the second-largest to largest is at most `(1−P)/P`; hence the finite-volume energy gap is at least `β⁻¹ log(P/(1−P))`. A uniform thermodynamic gap needs a size-uniform purity bound at a fixed positive `β` and control of the relevant sector/ground-state degeneracy. This is not itself a theorem about the lane's Wilson transfer operator.

### Purity-doubling results in spin systems

- **OpenAI, “The periodic spin-one Haldane gap” (public 2026 author manuscript; no arXiv id/DOI located),** [public manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-periodic-spin-one-Haldane-gap-September-24-2026). The theorem proves a uniform thermodynamic gap for the periodic spin-one chain from finite-size partition-function bounds. The proof uses physical and spatially rotated purities, a Hermitian spatial transfer, and a discrete doubling/renormalization inequality; the exact cancellations are model-specific. This is a direct rigorous example of ratio/purity information yielding a gap in a spin model, but does not give a general theorem from one arbitrary ratio, nor a lattice-gauge result. The lane already has its own Fact PUR step (FINISH 1373/1461; check 1465), but not this spin-chain theorem as the proof of its YM claim. Exact phrase read: “self-adjoint transfer matrix”.

- **Tarun Grover, arXiv:2611.07400,** [“Extensions of the Haldane gap proof: higher dimensions, near degeneracy, fermions and tensor-network states”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07400), especially §§II–IV and the transfer/projection discussion. For a 1D spin system with a Hermitian spatial transfer, the paper defines physical and spatial purities from `Z(L,β)`, `Z(L,2β)`, and `Z(2L,β)`, then iterates a discrete squaring inequality. Bounds on both purities near one imply a positive thermodynamic gap under its hypotheses; extensions treat higher-dimensional systems, symmetry sectors, fermions, and tensor-network norms. It is the closest forward-cited theorem to the requested purity-to-gap question. The spatial-transfer representation and positivity conditions matter. In the symmetry discussion, a sector-projected trace after rotation need not itself be a purity: the paper gives a projected Ising ratio exceeding one, and explains that an appropriate sum over boundary twists is needed in its construction. Thus `P_K=Z_+(2u)/Z_+(u)^2` cannot simply be substituted into the bootstrap without proving the matching transfer representation and hypotheses. The argument is discrete doubling, not a continuous deformation from one `2T` torus to two `T` tori. It treats spin/fermion/tensor settings and provides no YM4D-versus-YM5D or non-Abelian-versus-Abelian theorem. Exact phrase read: “there exists a Hermitian spatial transfer matrix”. **Lane relation:** separate from, and potentially useful background for, its Fact PUR; no such identification for the lane's neutral Wilson trace was found in the checked sources.

- **Sirui Lu, arXiv:2611.11049,** [“Bridging Gaps with Tensor Networks: From the Haldane Gap and Beyond”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11049). The abstract says the author verifies, digests, and formalizes the OpenAI spin-chain argument using tensor-network diagrams and Lean 4, and reports reflection positivity as the general condition behind the proof, with extensions to seven spin chains/ladders. This is a formalization/generalization of that spin result, not an independent gauge-theory purity theorem or a continuous replica interpolation. Exact phrase read: “formalize OpenAI's computer-assisted proof”. **Lane relation:** no such result found in the checked lane files; it does not establish Fact PUR for this Wilson operator.

These papers support a useful distinction: ratio bounds can certify a gap under transfer positivity, geometry, and uniformity assumptions, but there is no model-independent implication from an unqualified value of `Z(2T)/Z(T)^2` to a uniform gap. Grover's twist-sector caveat is especially pertinent to a centre-neutral projection.

## 2. What purity says about gaps; other quantum-information bounds

- The finite-dimensional `P>1/2` implication and its uniformity caveat are derived above. The OpenAI/Grover spin-chain bootstrap is a stronger thermodynamic example using *two* compatible purities plus a Hermitian spatial transfer and discrete recursion. **Lane relation:** the lane already records Fact PUR, a one-direction purity-to-gap implication, at FINISH 1373/1461/check 1465; that closes only the lane's stated formal step, not the new neutral-purity bound or its physical premises.

- **Matthew B. Hastings and Tohru Koma, arXiv:math-ph/0507008,** [“Spectral Gap and Exponential Decay of Correlations”](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0507008). For short-range quantum spin/fermion systems, they prove exponential clustering from a nonzero spectral gap (with stated variants for interactions and symmetries). This is a gap-to-correlations implication, not a purity-ratio-to-gap converse. Exact phrase read: “the nonvanishing spectral gap implies exponential decay”. **Lane relation:** it cannot replace Fact PUR or establish the neutral Wilson ratio.

- **Hongrui Chen and Zhiyan Ding, arXiv:2610.05848,** [“Spectral Analysis Beyond the Spectral Gap: Rapid Mixing of Quantum Gibbs Samplers in One Dimension”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05848). For general noncommuting 1D local spin systems at fixed positive temperature, they prove rapid trace-norm mixing of a KMS detailed-balanced Gibbs sampler, with decay equivalent to exponential convergence in sandwiched Rényi-2 divergence and a modified log-Sobolev bound. This is a statement about convergence of a quantum Markov semigroup from arbitrary initial states toward a Gibbs state; its Rényi-2 divergence is not the static thermal purity `Tr(ρβ²)` and its Lindbladian rate is not the Wilson transfer gap. Exact phrase read: “equivalent to exponential convergence in sandwiched Rényi-2 divergence”. **Lane relation:** none of the checked lane results invoke this dynamics; it gives no neutral Wilson estimate.

These Lieb–Robinson/clustering and quantum-Markov results do not bridge the requested static ratio to a transfer gap without additional model-specific structure. The inspected quantum-information sources are spin-system results, not lattice-gauge transfer estimates.

## 3. Group and dimension selectivity

The sources above do not establish that thermal purity bounds distinguish non-Abelian from Abelian gauge theories, or four from five spacetime dimensions. Grover's extension to arbitrary spatial dimensions is a transfer-bootstrap scope result, not evidence of a dimension-selective YM inequality. The SU(2/3/4) Rényi lattice study is numerical and concerns subregion entanglement; it does not compare to an Abelian model under matched conditions.

A nearby rigorous statistical-mechanics comparison is **Jian Ding, Fenglin Huang, and Peng Yang** [arXiv:2611.04908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04908), “Exponential decay for the two-dimensional Heisenberg model.” The primary text proves exponential spin-correlation decay in the 2D O(N) model for integer `N≥3` at every positive inverse temperature, and extends the loop-soup argument to every real `N>2`. This separates that result's scope from the O(2)/XY case, but it is neither a lattice-gauge purity theorem nor a distinction between Abelian and non-Abelian gauge groups or between `d=4` and `d=5`. Exact phrase read: “every real-valued N>2”. **Lane relation:** no such loop-soup/correlation result is used by the lane's neutral-purity route.

## Assessment

The strongest relevant outside result is the 2026 spin-chain purity bootstrap (OpenAI, then Grover and Lu): it rigorously turns compatible finite-volume purity information into a thermodynamic gap under explicit Hermitian-transfer/positivity and iteration hypotheses. It is useful as a template to compare against, not as a proof of the YM4D neutral statement. Static thermal purity is inverse-temperature monotone for any finite Hamiltonian, and positive transfer power purity is period monotone under nonnegative spectral weights. I found no open primary source proving the requested neutral-Wilson coupling monotonicity, convexity, continuous two-torus/one-torus interpolation, or a YM4D/YM5D or Abelian/non-Abelian selector. The gauge-theory Rényi literature located concerns subregion entanglement and replica geometries, often numerically, and does not supply those claims.
