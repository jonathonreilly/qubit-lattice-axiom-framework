## Results by literature family

### Higgs–confinement analytic connection

- **Osterwalder–Seiler (1978), “Gauge field theories on a lattice.”** The accessible publisher abstract reports a positive self-adjoint transfer matrix from reflection positivity, existence and analyticity of the infinite-volume limit for strongly coupled lattice Yang–Mills, verification of the Wilson confinement bound, and a rigorous Higgs mechanism. This is a strong-coupling result; the abstract does not state a bridge to weak coupling or a uniform gap along an interpolation. The source was abstract-only: the publisher full text was inaccessible. [Publisher record and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8). The abstract does not let me certify a compact-U(1)-specific theorem or exact group range. **Rigorous paper; source inspected only at abstract level.**

- **Fradkin–Shenker (1979), “Phase diagrams of lattice gauge theories with Higgs fields.”** For fixed-length Higgs fields in the fundamental representation, they establish a region in the finite-lattice-spacing `(β,K)` plane where the free energy and all Green functions are analytic and which connects Higgs-like and confinement-like regimes; observables have no qualitative phase boundary within that region. Their expansion appendix also gives analyticity and local exponential clustering in its convergence region. The paper treats Z₂, U(1), and SU(N) examples; for continuous groups the analyticity region narrows as the Higgs hopping parameter K tends to infinity. Nonfundamental representations can have separating phase boundaries (their examples include adjoint SU(N) and charge-N U(1)). This is a rigorous analyticity/continuity result for gauge–Higgs models, not pure Wilson Yang–Mills and not a theorem that the physical Hamiltonian gap stays uniformly positive on a path. It includes compact U(1) in the fundamental-Higgs setting, with the stated representation/parameter qualifications. [Open SLAC preprint](https://www.slac.stanford.edu/pubs/slacpubs/2000/slac-pub-2238.pdf), especially its abstract, analyticity discussion, and Appendix. **Rigorous.**

### Complete analyticity and finite-volume mixing

- **Dobrushin–Shlosman (1987), “Completely Analytical Interactions: Constructive Description.”** Complete analyticity is characterized through equivalent uniform conditions on finite-volume partition functions, correlations, and their responses to small complex perturbations. The finite-volume bounds are uniform over volumes and boundary conditions. Their finite-size/constructive criterion reduces verification to finitely many box sizes under the paper’s finite-range, finite-spin setup; it is a criterion for establishing complete analyticity, not propagation of a gap between different coupling values. The stated setup does not directly include continuous compact link variables such as U(1). It is group-blind only when a gauge model has first been represented in a model satisfying those assumptions. [Full open paper](https://www.cpt.univ-mrs.fr/~shlosman/papers/132.pdf). **Rigorous.**

- **Martinelli–Olivieri (1992), “Finite Volume Mixing Conditions for Lattice Spin Systems and Exponential Approach to Equilibrium of Glauber Dynamics.”** The accessible mp_arc record says the paper studies finite-volume mixing conditions and their consequences for Gibbs states and Glauber dynamics, improves the Holley/Aizenman connection, and proves fast convergence above the Ising critical point, with or without field. I could not read the linked full paper copy (the link failed); I therefore do not assert a sharper theorem or extend it to gauge fields. The abstract’s spin-system setting does not establish compact-U(1) applicability. [mp_arc record/abstract](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/mp_arc-bin/mpa?yn=92-174). **Rigorous paper; abstract only read here.**

- **Martinelli–Olivieri–Schonmann (1994), “For 2-D lattice spin systems weak mixing implies strong mixing.”** For finite-range discrete-spin systems on `ℤ²`, weak mixing with exponential rate implies a stronger boundary-mixing estimate for squares: changing boundary data affects a region at a rate exponential in the distance to the changed boundary sites. The result applies on squares (and the paper describes unions of sufficiently large squares), not as a parameter-coupling interpolation theorem. It is dimension-specific and discrete-spin; compact U(1) link variables are outside its direct hypotheses. It propagates spatial mixing across box scales, not a Hamiltonian mass gap along a coupling path. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099735); theorem statement reproduced in this [later open mathematical source](https://content.ems.press/assets/public/full-texts/serials/aihpd/13/1/14298048/online/10.4171-aihpd-195.pdf). **Rigorous.**

### Lee–Yang / Lieb–Sokal zero-free methods

- In the scalar ferromagnetic setting, Lee–Yang and Lieb–Sokal theorems constrain zeros of finite-volume partition functions in complex external fields, under hypotheses on the single-site measure and nonnegative ferromagnetic couplings. They can yield analyticity and correlation information in the corresponding field domain. Those hypotheses are not a general theorem about zeros as a function of the plaquette coefficient β in a non-Abelian Wilson gauge partition function. I found no applicable gauge-theory zero-free region in β that transports a mass gap along the row-1072 path. Fradkin–Shenker’s analyticity region above is obtained in the specific gauge–Higgs model/expansion setting and should not be relabeled a general Lee–Yang theorem. [Lieb–Sokal bibliographic record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01011709). The theorem statement was checked in a secondary exposition, not the original paper; no gauge application is claimed. **Rigorous spin theorems; no qualifying gauge path theorem found.**

### Correlation inequalities and coupling monotonicity

- **Ginibre inequality.** The Wilson-action correlation inequality gives monotonicity of Wilson-loop expectations with respect to plaquette couplings for the relevant positive Wilson-loop observables. The result is stated for Wilson lattice gauge theory on graphs and is not restricted to SU(2), so compact U(1) is included at this level. It controls those expectations, not connected-correlation decay rates, transfer-matrix spectral support, or a spectral gap; monotonicity of loops does not imply monotonicity of a mass gap. The source inspected describes Ginibre’s result and an extension of its methods to Villain interactions only for Abelian gauge theory. [Chevyrev–Garban, “Villain action in lattice gauge theory”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09928). **Rigorous inequality; no gap monotonicity.**

- **Tomboulis–Ukawa–Windey (1981), “Correlation inequalities and quark confinement in lattice gauge theories.”** Their abstract reports inequalities for Wilson loops in Z₂ and U(N), and an area-decay/confinement consequence in a strong-coupling/temperature regime. The discussion of possible implications for SU(2) is not an SU(2) theorem. U(1) is within U(N) for N=1, but the result is about Wilson-loop bounds, not propagation of a transfer-matrix gap. [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90421-1). **Rigorous inequalities; abstract inspected.**

- **Simon–Lieb comparisons.** The known comparison method concerns correlation bounds in ferromagnetic spin systems. I found no general Simon–Lieb inequality for SU(2) Wilson theory, nor a version monotone in β that controls its gap. Such spin inequalities are not automatically group-blind gauge inequalities. No claim for compact U(1) gauge theory follows here.

- **Center-group comparisons.** A modern center comparison relates certain double-winding Wilson-loop area behavior for a gauge group G to that of its center Z(G), subject to its hypotheses and coupling rescaling. It is a center/loop comparison, not a gap estimate; it is not a general monotonicity theorem for SU(2) spectral gaps. [Ikeda–Kondo (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptep/ptab114). This does not provide a compact-U(1) mass-gap bridge.

### Layered and anisotropic models

- The already-read `gap-aniso-read-luna.md` covers temporal anisotropy calibration, perturbative and numerical work, and layer-phase studies. The additional layered papers found in this search concern different models and are not rigorous gap bridges. For example, the 4+1D anisotropic pure U(1) layer-phase study uses mean-field analysis and Monte Carlo; it reports a four-dimensional Coulomb-like layer and confining fifth direction, with transition-order evidence. That is explicitly nonrigorous numerical/mean-field evidence and has a massless Coulomb-like layer, not a four-dimensional SU(2) spectral-gap propagation theorem. [Dimopoulos–Farakos–Vrentzos (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0607033). ### Differential inequalities and sharpness

- **Aizenman–Barsky (1987), percolation sharpness.** For independent percolation in their translation-invariant class, the percolation threshold equals the threshold for onset of exponential cluster decay; the proof uses differential inequalities. This is a statement about percolation connectivity, not a gauge-theory transfer-matrix gap. [Full open paper](https://math.bme.hu/~balint/oktatas/perkolacio/percolation_papers/aizenman_barsky.pdf). **Rigorous; not gauge-group applicable by itself.**

- **Aizenman–Barsky–Fernández (1987), Ising-type sharpness.** For a general class of one-component ferromagnetic spin models, the transition occurs directly from the high-temperature regime with exponential correlation decay to the low-temperature regime with spontaneous magnetization, with no intermediate phase. Hypotheses are ferromagnetic scalar-spin interactions of their specified class; this is a spin-correlation theorem, not a gauge-coupling gap theorem. [Full open paper](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~ferna107/papers/_87_ABF_JSP.pdf). It does not directly cover compact U(1) link variables.

- **Duminil-Copin–Tassion and coauthors.** Their sharpness methods prove subcritical exponential decay for Bernoulli percolation and, in the later Potts/FK work, for the subcritical FK random-cluster model with q≥1 on transitive graphs under the stated assumptions. These are rigorous finite-size/differential or decision-tree arguments for those probability models. The hypotheses do not identify a Wilson gauge observable with the required FK model or transfer their exponential rate to a gauge Hamiltonian gap. [Duminil-Copin–Tassion (2016)](https://unige.ch/~duminil/publi/sharpness%20perco%20Ising.pdf); [Duminil-Copin–Raoufi–Tassion (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.03104). **Rigorous for the stated percolation/Potts/FK models; no gauge bridge.**

### Dimensional crossover

- I found rigorous results on spatial mixing within two-dimensional finite-range discrete-spin systems (the Martinelli–Olivieri–Schonmann result above), and sharpness results in percolation/Potts/FK models. I did not find a rigorous theorem that starts from a gapped two-dimensional layer or decoupled stack and carries a uniform spectral gap through nonzero interlayer coupling into a higher-dimensional isotropic gauge model. The 2+2 product endpoint in this repository’s prior anisotropy work does not by itself furnish such an open coupling interval or an isotropic endpoint. The methods above are not directly group-blind results for compact U(1) link fields.
