## 1. Two-dimensional lattice gauge theories with a fixed-length fundamental Higgs field

**Finding.** I found no rigorous theorem proving a gap or clustering throughout the non-Abelian two-dimensional fixed-length fundamental-Higgs model, or in a region beyond the available expansion domains. The nearby results concern analyticity of thermodynamic observables, or different Abelian models. None proves (GH2-MIX) or (P4-MIX).

- **Fradkin and Shenker (1979), “Phase Diagrams of Lattice Gauge Theories with Higgs Fields.”** The lattice field has fixed length. For Higgs matter in the fundamental representation, their strong-coupling and Higgs regions are analytically connected for a class of local gauge-invariant quantities; there is no phase boundary separating those regimes. The paper explicitly studies $Z_2$, U(1), and SU($N$); its non-Abelian analysis is for compact SU($N$), with enough fixed-length fundamental Higgs fields to break the local symmetry completely in unitary gauge. The proof uses the Osterwalder–Seiler analyticity argument for fixed-length fields and gives an analytic strip connecting strong-coupling and Higgs regions. In the SU($N$) discussion it says that all local Green functions are analytic and that the spectrum evolves smoothly, and describes the connected phase as completely massive. It supplies no quantitative gap lower bound or exponential-clustering estimate for that model, and does not prove the checkerboard singlet-mixing estimate. It also does not cover the four-sheet on-site-potential singlet estimate. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.19.3682); the authors’ [SLAC preprint](https://www.slac.stanford.edu/pubs/slacpubs/2000/slac-pub-2238.pdf) is open.
- **Brydges, Fröhlich, and Seiler (1979), “On the Construction of Quantized Gauge Fields I: General Results.”** Their general Theorem C treats two scalar fields in the same representation, one with arbitrary local gauge-invariant self-interactions and a massless bare field after unitary-gauge removal of angular modes. It proves exponential clustering of the second field’s two-point function only in dimension $\nu\geq3$. It does not give the requested two-dimensional result. In their two-dimensional lattice Abelian Higgs discussion, they establish inequalities, reflection positivity, and properties of $\theta$ vacua, but no mass-gap theorem. [Open author preprint](https://repo-archives.ihes.fr/FONDS_IHES/I_Prepublications/FROHLICH/1978-1980/P_78_237/P_78_237_web.pdf).
- **Brydges, Fröhlich, and Seiler (1981), “The Two-Dimensional Abelian Higgs Model Without Cutoffs.”** The paper constructs the continuum two-dimensional Abelian Higgs model and verifies the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms for gauge-invariant local fields, while explicitly allowing that clustering may be unproved. Thus it does not establish a gap. Its Higgs field is not the fixed-length SU(2) field in GH2. The accessible full-text copy states that clustering is possibly missing and says the authors believe it holds. [Open full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258230357_On_the_construction_of_quantized_gauge_fields_III_The_two-dimensional_abelian_Higgs_model_without_cutoffs).
- **Balaban, Imbrie, Jaffe, and Brydges (1984), “The Mass Gap for Higgs Models on a Unit Lattice.”** Theorem 4.1 is a rigorous result for the Abelian lattice Higgs model, with gauge group U(1), in every dimension $d\geq2$. The Higgs variable is a complex scalar with a quartic potential and fluctuating length. For fixed positive scalar parameter $\mu$ and positive vector mass $m_A=\mu e/\sqrt{8\lambda}$, sufficiently small $\lambda$ gives infinite-volume limits and exponential clustering for local integral-charge observables, with a positive rate independent of the observables. This is precisely a weak-coupling cluster-expansion neighborhood of a massive Gaussian theory. It is not a fixed-length or non-Abelian result, and does not reach outside its expansion region. [Open paper](https://uva.theopenscholar.com/files/john-imbrie/files/massgaphiggs.pdf), Theorem 4.1 and the discussion at lines 18–21, 305–347.
- **Borgs and Nill (1987), “The Phase Diagram of the Abelian Lattice Higgs Model: A Review of Rigorous Results.”** This is a review of rigorous phase-structure results for Abelian models, compact U(1) and noncompact real gauge group. It studies the Higgs mechanism, gauge-fixed symmetry breaking, Gribov copies, and analyticity/phase regions. It does not supply a two-dimensional non-Abelian fixed-length Higgs gap theorem. I could access the bibliographic record and article text excerpt, but not a complete open paper without an account. [Article record and abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01206163).
- **Seiler’s 1982 notes, “Gauge Theories as a Problem of Constructive Quantum Field Theory and Statistical Mechanics.”** I found the Springer book record and contents (the lattice-gauge chapter spans pages 3–98), but no open full copy that I could read without a sign-in. I therefore do not attribute a theorem to the notes. [Publisher record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/3-540-11559-5).

These results separate three claims. The Fradkin–Shenker theorem gives analyticity in a connected region. Balaban et al. prove exponential clustering in a different Abelian model near a Gaussian expansion point. The two-dimensional Abelian continuum construction leaves clustering open. None transfers to the row 1076 fixed-length SU(2) models by group substitution.

## 2. Transverse-lattice Hamiltonians and the Gauss-law reduction

**Finding.** Yes: the Gauss-law elimination and a quadratic Casimir of partial color charge are written explicitly on a finite spatial lattice in Orland’s anisotropic (2+1)-dimensional Hamiltonian treatment. The hypotheses and geometry matter. The papers do not prove the target four-dimensional Wilson descent inequality.

- **Bardeen and Pearson (1976), with the later Bardeen–Pearson–Rabinovici transverse-lattice work.** Their transverse lattice discretizes the two transverse spatial coordinates while leaving longitudinal space and time continuous. Link variables live on transverse links; longitudinal gauge fields and quarks live at transverse sites. Gauss’s law leaves a residual local transverse gauge charge at each transverse site. In the 1976 proposal the electric Coulomb term is tied to that site charge and a linear longitudinal potential. Their later Hamiltonian calculations study the transverse-lattice QCD spectrum, including large-$N$/planar approximations. This is a light-front or longitudinally continuous Hamiltonian framework, not the two-dimensional Euclidean Higgs model. [Bardeen and Pearson’s open 1976 paper](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/1976/conf/fermilab-conf-76-208.pdf); [Bardeen, Pearson, and Rabinovici’s 1980 paper record/PDF](https://digital.library.unt.edu/ark:/67531/metadc1093958/).
- **Burkardt (1999), “Gauge Field Theories on a Transverse Lattice.”** These notes explain the light-front transverse-lattice method. They focus on zero-mode difficulties in compact and noncompact canonical formulations and motivate a color-dielectric approximation that linearizes link fields. They are review/proceedings notes, not a proof of a spectral gap or a derivation of the target gap monotonicity. [Open arXiv copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9908195).
- **Dalley and collaborators.** The open transverse-lattice papers formulate effective light-front Hamiltonians, typically in large-$N$ and Eguchi–Kawai reductions, and calculate string tensions or glueball spectra. Their computational and approximation framework does not provide a rigorous correlation-gap theorem. [Dalley and van de Sande, “Transverse Lattice QCD in 2+1 Dimensions”](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9602291); [Dalley, “Color-dielectric gauge theory on a transverse lattice”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.56.7917).
- **Orland (2006), “Integrable Models and Confinement in (2+1)-Dimensional Weakly-Coupled Yang–Mills Theory.”** This is the clearest exact lattice answer. On a finite cylinder, open in $x^1$ and periodic in $x^2$, Orland uses the SU(2) Kogut–Susskind Hamiltonian and axial gauge $U_1=1$. The lattice Gauss law gives
  $l_1(x^1,x^2)=\sum_{y^1\leq x^1}q(y^1,x^2)-\sum_{y^1\leq x^1}(\mathcal D_2l_2)(y^1,x^2)$,
  with the residual total-charge constraint retained. The electric term is $H_1=(e')^2\sum_x\mathrm{Tr}(l_1(x)^2)/2$. Thus it is the Casimir of a cumulative, covariantly corrected partial color charge. This is written at finite lattice spacing, not inferred only from continuum Coulomb gauge. [Full open arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0607013v6), equations (2.4)–(2.5), (3.1)–(3.4); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0607013).

### Orland’s three papers, read in full

The three papers are exact citations requested in R1. Each PDF was fetched to a distinct fresh empty scratch directory, and I read the full open arXiv text. Their dimension, limiting regime, and approximation are not interchangeable.

1. **hep-th/0607013 (2006), “Integrable Models and Confinement in (2+1)-Dimensional Weakly-Coupled Yang–Mills Theory.”** The model has two anisotropic couplings $e,e'$ and gauge group SU(2) for the quantitative computation (the setup is described for SU($N$)). After axial gauge, $H_0$ is a set of decoupled 1+1-dimensional principal-chiral sigma models, while $H_1=(e')^2 E_1^2/2$ couples their charge densities. Orland treats $H_1$ perturbatively for small $e'$, uses exact O(4) sigma-model current form factors, and integrates out the transverse field to obtain the leading effective action for $A_0$. For the leading derivative term he truncates the form-factor spectral expansion to two Faddeev–Zamolodchikov particles; higher-derivative terms need higher-particle form factors. This repairs an earlier infrared-divergent Rayleigh–Schrödinger treatment. It computes the horizontal string-tension correction; the vertical tension and the gauge-theory mass gap are expressly left unresolved. There is no derivation of a universal monotonic gap in $e'$.
2. **0704.0940 (2007), “Glueball Masses in (2+1)-Dimensional Anisotropic Weakly-Coupled Yang–Mills Theory.”** The gauge group is SU(2) for the spectrum calculation; the setup and confinement discussion include SU($N$). It studies low-lying glueballs as pairs of fundamental particles of the 1+1-dimensional SU(2)$\times$SU(2) principal-chiral model bound by the linear potential generated by the small nonzero anisotropic coupling. The paper uses a nonrelativistic approximation, the low-energy expansion of the exact singlet S-matrix, and WKB/Airy quantization, with a matching condition at particle overlap. Its validity requires the anisotropic string scale to be small compared with the sigma-model mass. It derives approximate low-lying masses and corrections to $2m_1$, not a rigorous full-Hamiltonian gap theorem.
3. **0801.0389 (2008), “Near-Integrability and Confinement for High-Energy Hadron–Hadron Collisions.”** This is a longitudinally rescaled 3+1-dimensional effective theory for high-energy scattering, not ordinary 4D Wilson Yang–Mills. A small rescaling parameter $\lambda$ suppresses longitudinal couplings, but does not set them to zero. The spatial theory is first regularized on a lattice; Orland fixes axial gauge and writes Gauss’s law. Eliminating the longitudinal electric field produces a nonlocal quadratic charge term with the $|y^3-z^3|$ kernel (lattice sums before taking the $x^3$ continuum limit; current expression afterward). The leading string tensions are obtained in an anisotropic/small-$\lambda$ approximation with decoupled 1+1-dimensional principal-chiral models. The paper itself calls the eikonal justification heuristic, says the effective theory is not certain to describe QCD, and leaves stronger-coupling corrections open. This does write a longitudinal electric-energy term in lattice variables, but does not prove the lattice spectral-gap monotonicity conjectured by row 1076.

## 3. Monotonicity of a spectral gap in a kinetic coefficient

**Finding.** I found no general theorem that the gap of $H_t=-t\Delta+V$ is monotone in $t$ for arbitrary $V$. The named one-dimensional results provide lower bounds under shape hypotheses; they do not supply the needed comparison between two kinetic coefficients. Transfer-operator positivity alone also gives no gap-order theorem.

- For a discrete simple eigenvalue, the Hellmann–Feynman derivative is $\lambda_k'(t)=\langle\psi_k,-\Delta\psi_k\rangle$. Therefore the derivative of the gap is the difference of these two kinetic expectations. The available cited results do not order that difference without additional structure.
- **Ashbaugh and Benguria.** Their one-dimensional Dirichlet theorem concerns a symmetric single-well potential on a fixed interval. It yields the sharp fundamental-gap lower bound (for the normalized interval, gap at least 3; after rescaling, $3\pi^2/L^2$) and equality only for constant potential. It is a lower bound, not monotonicity in the coefficient of $-\Delta$.
- **Andrews and Clutterbuck.** For a bounded convex Euclidean domain of diameter $D$, Dirichlet boundary conditions, and convex (more generally suitably semiconvex) potential, they prove $\lambda_1-\lambda_0\geq3\pi^2/D^2$; their general bound also uses a modulus of convexity. This compares a gap with a geometric one-dimensional model; it does not assert that $\lambda_1(t)-\lambda_0(t)$ is monotone in $t$.
- **Ashbaugh and Kielty (2020)** prove the gap increases with the Robin boundary parameter for symmetric potentials in their 1D setting. The monotone parameter is the boundary condition, not the kinetic coefficient. [Open arXiv abstract/full paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00308).
- For a transfer operator, Perron–Frobenius/Jentzsch positivity identifies a simple leading eigenvalue under the relevant positivity and compactness assumptions. It does not order the ratio $\lambda_1(T_t)/\lambda_0(T_t)$, or the logarithmic gap, as $t$ varies. I found no transfer-operator theorem that supplies row 1076’s needed sign under its gauge constraints.

Thus the formal partial-charge Casimir is established in Orland’s lower-dimensional anisotropic setting, while the sign needed to infer a gap inequality remains a new mathematical input.

## 4. Two-dimensional sigma models beyond spheres; general O(N) bond weights

- **Spheres / O(N).** The open 2026 theorem “Exponential decay in two-dimensional classical O(n) models” proves exponential decay of spin two-point correlations for every integer $n\geq3$ and finite positive inverse temperature on square-lattice finite free subgraphs, uniformly for bounded nonnegative nearest-neighbor edge strengths. Its bond law is the standard exponential ferromagnet $\exp(\beta_{xy}\,s_x\cdot s_y)$; the result includes non-translation-invariant strengths but not an arbitrary function of $s_x\cdot s_y$. The result is for sphere targets $S^{n-1}$ and spin correlations. It does not establish the singlet bound for P4’s invariant on-site interaction or GH2’s non-exponential effective bond $\omega(\sqrt{2b^2+2b^2\rho\cdot\rho'})$. [OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Exponential-decay-in-two-dimensional-classical-On-models-September-23-2026).
- **Other homogeneous spaces.** I found no comparably general rigorous all-finite-coupling lattice gap theorem for compact homogeneous targets $G/H$ other than spheres. Exact integrable continuum spectrum statements, large-$N$ saddle points, and perturbative asymptotic freedom are not such a theorem. In particular, the exact supersymmetric $\mathbb{CP}^{N-1}$ mass formula is derived from an exact S-matrix and thermodynamic Bethe ansatz plus perturbation theory; it is not a constructive lattice proof for general finite $N$ and all couplings. $\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2$ is the sphere exception, and its $\theta$ dependence matters.
- **Broader O(N) interactions.** Lees and Taggi prove exponential decay of transverse correlations for Spin O(N) models for any $N>1$, inverse temperature, and nonzero external field. Their result extends to bounded-degree graphs and a specified class of continuous-symmetry models, including interactions not expressible as $e^{-\mathcal H}$ in the usual Hamiltonian form. But the nonzero field selects a direction and the theorem concerns transverse correlations. It does not prove zero-field singlet mixing for a general ferromagnetic bond weight. [Open access article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00440-021-01053-5).
