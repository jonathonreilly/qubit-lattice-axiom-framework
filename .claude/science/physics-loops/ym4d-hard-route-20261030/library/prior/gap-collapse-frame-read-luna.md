## What the sources state

### Balaban: axial gauge on a finite box

Sussman–Alpoge, CA-I, Lemma 4.12 (“The axial gauge of a box”), lines 43787–43865, states the following. On a lattice box of spacing `eta` and edge lengths at most `Delta`, assume every plaquette satisfies `|∂U(p)-1| ≤ phi` and `(d-1)(Delta/eta)phi ≤ 1`. Fix the minimal-coordinate corner, and gauge-transform using the holonomies along lexicographic paths from that corner. The resulting link is the identity on those paths, and on every bond

`|U^a(b)-1| ≤ (d-1)(Delta/eta) phi`,

with logarithmic potential `a(b)=(i eta)^(-1) log U^a(b)` bounded by

`|a(b)| ≤ (pi/3)(d-1) Delta phi eta^(-2)`.

This is a pointwise plaquette hypothesis and an explicit estimate, but it grows linearly with the box diameter `Delta` (and the potential also has the lattice-spacing factor). It is not uniform in box size. The construction fixes a root and a coordinate ordering; the resulting gauge is not a gauge-covariant unit adjoint boundary frame. This lemma is stated and proved in the Sussman–Alpoge/Codex/Claude account, not in a refereed publication.

### Balaban CMP 99 (1985): multiscale axial and block Landau gauge

Balaban, “Spaces of Regular Gauge Field Configurations on a Lattice and Gauge Fixing Conditions,” *Comm. Math. Phys.* **99** (1985), 75–102, especially pp. 82–83 (Theorem 2 and hypotheses (1.33)–(1.35)), gives a more relevant non-Abelian block result. In nested regions `Omega_0 ⊃ ... ⊃ Omega_k`, with block scales `ell_j=L^j eta` and separation/floor conditions, take a regular background `U_0` satisfying the paper’s small-plaquette and derivative regularity package (1.33). The field `U' U_0` must first be in the prescribed axial gauge, and its block averages must satisfy the smallness condition (1.34)–(1.35); the combined radii `alpha_0+alpha_1` must be below the stated threshold. Theorem 2 then selects a unique allowed gauge transformation (with the paper’s block-average restrictions on the gauge transformation) for which the logarithmic potential `A=-log(U_1)/(i eta)` satisfies the block-average constraints and the background-covariant block Landau condition `R(U_0)D^*_{U_0}A=0`. It also gives, on level-`j` regions, `|A|, |nabla_{U_0}A| ≤ B_1(alpha_0+alpha_1) ell_j^(-1)` and stated higher-difference estimates at corresponding powers of `ell_j`.

The constants are controlled by the dimension and block factor (and the stated higher-regularity parameter); the theorem is formulated with explicit torus floors and bounds independent of the number of levels once those floors hold. This is uniform in the count of scales under those hypotheses, not a fixed-radius local estimate independent of cell size: the gauge choice and conditions use block averages, nested domains, and a background, and the displayed potential bound itself carries `ell_j^(-1)`. The input `alpha_1` is a block-average mismatch, not merely the local plaquette curvature. The output is a gauge-fixed Lie-algebra potential `A`, not a covariant section `n` of the adjoint unit sphere with `|D_l n|` controlled by nearby curvature. Refereed.

### Balaban CMP 95, 96, and 98: averaging/propagator gauges

I checked Balaban, “Propagators and Renormalization Transformations for Lattice Gauge Theories I,” *CMP* **95** (1984), 17–40, in particular the gauge/averaging discussion at printed pp. 19 and 26; “II,” *CMP* **96** (1984), 223–250, especially pp. 225–226; and “Averaging Operations for Lattice Gauge Theories,” *CMP* **98** (1985), 17–51, especially printed p. 19. In I and II, the displayed axial/Landau gauge choices concern the vector-potential/propagator and constrained Gaussian minimization used in the renormalization transformation. The Landau condition is the linear divergence-free condition for that potential. The averaging paper defines a block operation on link variables that respects gauge transformations; near the identity its logarithm is compared with the linear average.

These papers establish gauge fixing or covariance of the averaging operation in their stated block/propagator constructions. The passages read do not state a lattice `SU(2)` adjoint boundary frame whose link defect is bounded by curvature in a fixed neighborhood, nor a range-uniform local estimate of that form. Their averaging/propagator setup and small-field/near-identity hypotheses should not be conflated with the requested frame statement. All three are refereed.

### Uhlenbeck’s Coulomb gauge and the lattice form

Uhlenbeck, “Connections with `L^p` Bounds on Curvature,” *CMP* **83** (1982), 31–42, DOI [10.1007/BF01947069](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01947069), is refereed. The publisher’s accessible abstract says that sufficiently small `L^(n/2)` curvature on a ball admits a Coulomb gauge, and that compactness on compact manifolds follows for `L^p`-bounded curvature with `p>n/2`. Terry Tao’s open exposition of the theorem describes a connection on the unit ball with small critical `L^(n/2)` curvature and a Coulomb-gauge representative with a natural boundary condition and a scale-invariant Sobolev bound. The original full paper was not available as an open copy in this read, so I do not assert more exact original hypotheses or quote a precise theorem number here. This is a ball-wide integral-curvature hypothesis and a gauge-fixed connection potential; it is not a pointwise local-curvature estimate for a lattice boundary frame.

The project’s `library/lattice-uhlenbeck.md` gives the lattice counterpart already checked in `gates/share-check-199fac1cb.md`. On a four-dimensional box `P` of comparable side lengths, `U=e^{ia}`, and total plaquette logarithm norm `||theta||_P ≤ epsilon_0`, it states existence of a gauge with `delta a=0`, `||grad a||^2 ≤ 4||theta||^2`, `||a||_(ell^4)+N^(-1)||a||_(ell^2) ≤ C_U||theta||`, and `sup|a|≤C_U||theta||`. The constants do not depend on box side `N`. It is therefore uniform in box size for that total `L^2` smallness assumption. It provides a Coulomb-gauge potential, not a covariant adjoint frame, and its hypothesis is not the requested sum of local plaquette curvatures. This project lemma is research work, not a refereed publication.

## Covariant-section obstruction and scope

I found no statement in the sources read asserting the requested gauge-covariant boundary frame with `|D_l n| ≤ C sum_{p within r of l}|s(U_p)|`, where the neighborhood radius `r` and constant are independent of the cell side.

There is a direct stabilizer obstruction at the exactly trivial connection, under the usual meaning of a frame rule that depends only on the links and is equivariant under every gauge transformation. If all links are identity, every constant `g in SU(2)` stabilizes the connection. Equivariance would require the returned unit adjoint vector at any site to satisfy `n=Ad_g n` for every `g`. The adjoint representation has no nonzero vector fixed by all of `SU(2)`, contradicting `|n|=1`. Thus such a rule cannot assign a covariant unit frame at the trivial connection; a rule on a neighborhood that includes it is likewise impossible. This elementary argument is a derivation here, not a claim attributed to a paper. It does not rule out a rule using extra transforming data, a gauge-fixed/rooted choice, or a rule defined only away from the trivial orbit. It also does not establish the broader negative claim needed for row 1038.

**Conclusion.** Among the open copies and project sources read, none states a gauge-covariant boundary frame with a curvature-controlled defect at a fixed range uniform in cell size. Balaban’s finite-box axial estimate loses the box diameter; his multiscale Landau result controls a potential under stronger block/background hypotheses; Uhlenbeck and the checked lattice form control Coulomb-gauge potentials from box-wide curvature norms. The stabilizer argument rules out an equivariant unit-frame selection at the trivial connection itself, but leaves punctured-neighborhood and extra-data formulations open.

RESULT: No read source supplies the requested uniform-range covariant boundary-frame estimate; the closest gauge-fixing bounds concern potentials and carry different hypotheses, while the trivial connection has a pointwise stabilizer obstruction.
