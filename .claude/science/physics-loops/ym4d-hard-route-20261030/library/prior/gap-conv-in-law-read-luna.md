## Answer to CONV

There are published *restricted* law-averaged criteria. The closest exact matches are for **random interactions/disorder**: a finite-cell mixing quantity averaged over the disorder law, or a random local disagreement parameter whose rare bad sites are subcritical. These prove almost-sure uniqueness and exponential quenched correlations for classical spin systems. They do not establish a criterion for a deterministic interaction merely by sampling boundary conditions from its own Gibbs law, and they do not cover continuous SU(2) gauge fields. Their local influence quantities still take a worst case over local boundary values before averaging over disorder.

There is also a useful two-dimensional bridge: weak mixing in law for independent random finite-range interactions implies stronger mixing-in-expectation on large squares and almost-sure uniqueness. The deterministic Martinelli–Olivieri–Schonmann theorem is a worst-case boundary theorem; it does not turn one typical-boundary test for a fixed model into full mixing.

The proposed first-order filter needs a qualification. Uniform complete analyticity / all-boundary exponential mixing excludes phase coexistence. A *single pure phase* may nevertheless have exponential clustering at a first-order transition. A proposed condition on the model's full finite-volume law or on two independent samples from the full coexistence law should detect persistent order-parameter covariance; a one-sample, phase-conditioned typical-boundary test need not. Thus “fails at a first-order point” is a needed design property of CONV, not a theorem about every typical-boundary condition.

## Consequence for the proposed CONV

A publishable analogue exists for random interactions (van den Berg WME; Gielis–Maes rare bad sites) and supports the design principle “average a finite-cell mixing/influence test, then control bad cells by subcritical geometry.” It does **not** supply the desired deterministic Wilson/SU(2) implication. The closest route requiring new proof is to replace random interaction disorder by the actual two-sample law of the model's cell boundaries while retaining a mechanism that (a) controls all local observables, including neutral ones, (b) propagates across good cells despite arbitrary rare bad cells, and (c) is stated so the full coexistence law at a first-order point violates it. Existing deterministic Dobrushin–Shlosman and 2D weak-to-strong criteria keep a supremum over boundary data; the finite-group gauge theorem is a specific swap proof, not a general criterion.

## RESULT

RESULT: Published law-averaged finite-cell/rare-bad-region criteria exist for quenched random finite-range spin interactions (including van den Berg's 2D WME and Gielis–Maes's `E q_0 < 1/(2d−1)` criterion); they are not a theorem for a deterministic Wilson law with typical/two-sample Gibbs boundary conditions and do not cover continuous SU(2) gauge fields. The standard all-boundary criteria fail at coexistence by uniqueness, while a pure-phase typical-boundary test need not; CONV must be designed against the full two-sample/coexistence law.
