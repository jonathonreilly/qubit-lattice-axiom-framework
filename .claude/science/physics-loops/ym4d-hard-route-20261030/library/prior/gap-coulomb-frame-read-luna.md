## Literature findings

### Continuum Gribov and Singer obstructions

**Gribov (1978).** V. N. Gribov, [“Quantization of non-Abelian Gauge Theories”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(78)90175-X), *Nuclear Physics B* **139** (1978), 1–19, refereed. In the continuum Euclidean non-Abelian theory, the condition ∂·A=0 does not select one representative per orbit: Gribov exhibits nontrivial gauge-equivalent transverse fields (his explicit construction is for SU(2), with decay conditions at infinity). Restricting to positive Faddeev–Popov operator removes some copies but does not turn the condition into a global unique gauge. This is a continuum result on noncompact space with decay, not a finite lattice box estimate; it supplies no bound of a chosen gauge transformation by plaquette curvature. The complete open text was read through a parsed mirror. A direct mirror `curl` failed certificate validation, which was not bypassed; the publisher copy is not open.

**Singer (1978).** I. M. Singer, [“Some Remarks on the Gribov Ambiguity”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01609471), *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **60** (1978), 7–12, refereed; [Project Euclid open copy](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-60/issue-1/Some-remarks-on-the-Gribov-ambiguity/cmp/1103901556.full). For compact nonabelian gauge group and the relevant bundle of irreducible connections, Singer proves there is no continuous global choice of one connection on each orbit over `S^4`; the paper also gives the corresponding obstruction over `S^3`. The usual decay-at-infinity formulation on `R^4` is compactified to `S^4`. He treats gauge transformations fixed at a point (the based gauge group) as well: that condition does not remove the `S^4` obstruction. This is a topological nonexistence of a continuous global selector, not a quantitative estimate for a small-curvature local box, and makes no box-size-uniform claim. The Project Euclid text was readable through the web text view; a direct PDF fetch returned a bot page, not a readable copy, and was not bypassed.

**Killingback (1984).** T. P. Killingback, [“The Gribov ambiguity in gauge theories on the four-torus”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(84)91878-1), *Physics Letters B* **138** (1984), 87–90, refereed. The publisher abstract states that gauge fixing is impossible for non-Abelian theory on the four-torus (and gives an SU(2) result on `S^2 × S^2`). The source available here was the abstract only; the full paper was not readable open access. The abstract gives periodic compact-manifold scope, but not enough detail to report the proof hypotheses more finely, make a box-size-uniformity claim, or infer anything about a pinned lattice face.

### Lattice copies, minimal gauges, and BRST

**Lattice Landau/Coulomb copies and the fundamental modular region.** D. Zwanziger, [“Vanishing of zero-momentum lattice gluon propagator and color confinement”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(91)90581-H), *Nuclear Physics B* **364** (1991), 127–161, refereed; open text [here](https://www.infomall.org/sites/dsc/jpac/QCDRef/1990s/Vanishing%20of%20zero-momentum%20lattice%20gluon%20propagator%20and%20color%20confinement%20-%20Zwanziger%20-%201991.pdf). On a finite periodic hypercubic lattice with compact SU(N) links, the minimal Landau gauge maximizes the sum of link traces over all links; minimal Coulomb gauge does so over spatial links on each time slice. Stationary points give the lattice transversality condition; local minima of the corresponding negative-trace functional have nonnegative Faddeev–Popov Hessian and lie in the first Gribov region. Compactness of each gauge orbit gives an absolute minimum, hence at least one representative in the fundamental modular region (FMR). The FMR is contained in the first Gribov region, which is contained in the region of nonnegative Faddeev–Popov operator. The paper exhibits an SU(2) periodic-lattice orbit with distinct gauge-equivalent minima, so the minimum prescription is not a globally unique selector. These are finite-lattice statements on the stated periodic lattice; there is no small-plaquette hypothesis, local curvature estimate, or box-size-uniform bound on the selected transformation. Van Baal’s [“More (thoughts on) Gribov copies”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(92)90232-3), *Nuclear Physics B* **369** (1992), 259–275, is a refereed continuum Hamiltonian-gauge account for compact three-dimensional spatial manifolds (or fields compactifiable to `S^3`): the FMR interior has no copies, while boundary points can be identified as copies. It does not establish a local lattice frame theorem. Zwanziger’s separate 1994 FMR article was not read in full; it was not needed for the claims above.

“Minimal Landau gauge” commonly means choosing a local minimum, and “absolute Landau gauge” an absolute minimum. The latter gives an FMR representative but does not canonically choose one when the orbit has multiple absolute minima. The analogous Coulomb minimization is slice-wise in time and retains residual time-dependent spatially constant transformations unless separately fixed. None of these prescriptions alone supplies a measurable, unique, gauge-covariant frame.

**Neuberger zero.** H. Neuberger, [“Nonperturbative BRS invariance and the Gribov problem”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(87)90974-9), *Physics Letters B* **183** (1987), 337–340, refereed. The original was not available open in full. The open review by M. Testa, [“Lattice gauge fixing, Gribov copies and BRST symmetry”](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9803025) (published in *Physics Letters B* **429** (1998), 349–353), explains the finite-lattice result: for standard nilpotent BRST gauge fixing with BRST-exact gauge-fixing term and compact lattice gauge group, the gauge-fixing partition function is an Euler-characteristic-type sum that vanishes; consequently the usual gauge-fixed expectation is (0/0). The finite-volume argument uses compactness and BRST-exactness, not a small-field hypothesis. It is an obstruction to that standard nonperturbative BRST integral, not a theorem that ordinary gauge representatives do not exist. A boundary-pinned finite lattice with a nontrivial compact group of interior gauge transformations still falls under the same zero mechanism if standard BRST is applied to that residual compact group; this last sentence is an inference from the finite-dimensional argument, not a separate theorem stated by Neuberger.

**Fujikawa.** K. Fujikawa, [“BRST Symmetric Formulation of a Theory with Gribov-type Copies”](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9510111) (1995 preprint; the full text was read, but no refereed publication was identified). It gives a formal BRST-symmetric summation over copies conditional on a globally single-valued correspondence between the gauge-fixing value and gauge parameter, and illustrates the condition in a soluble model. It discusses QCD implications but states no lattice Landau/Coulomb selector, small-plaquette theorem, or pinned-frame bound. Fujikawa and Terashima’s [“Note on the Gauge Fixing in Gauge Theory”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0550-3213(00)00102-4), *Nuclear Physics B* **577** (2000), 405–415, refereed, was retrieved as an open preprint and read. It compares a modified gauge-fixing formula with Faddeev–Popov when copies are absent and discusses nonlocality when copies occur; its lattice remarks do not prove a local frame selector.

### Local small-field lattice Landau theorem

**Balaban (1985).** T. Balaban, [“Spaces of Regular Gauge Field Configurations on a Lattice and Gauge Fixing Conditions”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01466594), *Communications in Mathematical Physics* **99** (1985), 75–102, refereed. This is the closest positive result read. Theorem 2 is relative to a regular background `U0`, nested multiscale domains/blocks, and an axial representative. Its hypotheses include `U0 ∈ U_k({Ω_j}, α0)`, the paper's additional regularity condition (3.35) of its reference [4], `U'U0 ∈ U_k({Ω_j}, α0) ∩ Ax_k(B_k,U0)`, and slice-average discrepancies `|(U'U0)_j − Ū_j| < α1` on each `Λ_j`; thus small plaquettes alone do not suffice. Under `α0+α1 ≤ c1`, the theorem claims exactly one gauge transformation obeying the prescribed average constraints for which the transformed link field is `exp(iηA)` and `R(U0)D*_{U0}A = 0`. It bounds `|A| ≤ B1(α0+α1)(L_jη)^−1` and `|∇_{U0}A| ≤ B1(α0+α1)(L_jη)^−2`, with a corresponding Hölder bound. The constants are independent of hierarchy depth `k` but depend on dimension and scale factor (the Hölder constant also on the Hölder exponent). The theorem also imposes block-size, separation, and sufficient-torus-room conditions, so this depth-uniformity is not a result for arbitrary boxes uniformly in their size. It does not state a pinned-face measurable colour frame or a defect bound by local curvature alone. I read the paper PDF and visually checked the theorem page; OCR text was not used as the formula source.

The local Balaban source copy was `LOCAL:ym4d-papers/balaban_cmp99_75.pdf`. ## Pinned face and the requested frame

Singer’s theorem is **not directly a no-go theorem for a finite lattice box with gauge transformations fixed on one face**. Its hypotheses concern continuous sections over continuum connection-orbit spaces on `S^3`/`S^4`, including the based-at-a-point case. A finite box with transformations equal to identity on a pinned face has a finite product of compact groups at its remaining vertices. Choosing a spanning forest rooted on that face gives a continuous tree gauge on the finite graph. This only shows that some global lattice gauge selector exists; it does not make that selector Landau or Coulomb, establish uniqueness of a Landau/Coulomb representative, or give a curvature-controlled frame defect. The tree-gauge observation is elementary finite-graph reasoning, not a cited Singer theorem.

If instead “pinned boundary” means the entire boundary of a continuum four-ball, the relative transformation group has the topology of based maps `S^4 → G`, and Singer’s based-group obstruction can persist. That is a topological inference for a different domain/pinning condition; it should not be identified with a single pinned lattice face.

Among the sources read, **none states** a measurable, gauge-covariant colour frame built from Landau or Coulomb gauge on a pinned face whose defect is bounded by local curvature. Balaban gives a genuine nearby small-field relative Landau representative with quantitative potential/derivative bounds, but its hypotheses and output do not identify the requested frame or defect. The SA outline sketches a related theorem while retaining an open dependency. No read source gives a standalone theorem that every sufficiently small-plaquette lattice field on an arbitrary box has a unique Landau/Coulomb gauge with a box-uniform transformation/potential bound.
