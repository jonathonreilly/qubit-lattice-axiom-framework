## C3. Polchinski-flow log-Sobolev criteria

**Bauerschmidt–Bodineau.** Roland Bauerschmidt and Paul Bodineau, [“Log-Sobolev Inequality for the Continuum Sine-Gordon Model”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12308), arXiv:1907.12308; published in *Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics* 74 (2021), DOI 10.1002/cpa.21926. Abstract and HTML read. Theorem 1.2 (HTML §1.2) states: “Then $\nu_0$ satisfies the Log-Sobolev inequality” under the flow hypothesis (1.8), namely $Q_t\operatorname{Hess}V_t(\varphi)Q_t\ge \dot\mu_t I$ for every $t\ge0$, with $Q_t=e^{-tA/2}$ and $\mu_t=\int_0^t\dot\mu_s ds$, plus setup assumptions (1.6) and positive smallest eigenvalue of $A$. This is the exact Hessian lower-bound criterion requested; it is for a finite-dimensional Gaussian reference/continuum sine-Gordon construction, not a Wilson gauge measure. Theorem 1.1 gives massive continuum sine-Gordon for $\beta<6\pi$ with asymptotically optimal LSI under stated suitable conditions. The paper says the Hessian need not be positive definite and its proof controls the Polchinski PDE; the exact LSI criterion needs the weighted Hessian lower bound along the entire flow, not only at the initial scale.

U(1) Wilson test: **not applicable as stated**; the paper has no gauge-field theorem and supplies no Wilson-action Hessian. Even if an analogous functional inequality were proved for U(1), U(1) in 4D is gapless at weak coupling, so it could not alone prove the desired SU(2) gap. It would need an SU(2)-specific input stronger than this common convexity/functional-inequality mechanism. Z2 test: no exclusion of a phase transition is built into the abstract criterion; the theorem does not cover a discrete gauge model. Small parameter: the sine-Gordon application has the explicit $\beta<6\pi$ regime and continuum construction hypotheses; the abstract criterion itself is conditional on the full flow bound. Open issue stated: the authors call verification of (1.8) for their non-log-concave example challenging; no gauge or sphere-valued application is stated. **GUIDE** for a proposed (HS-sup) only if a gauge-compatible flow and all-scale Hessian bound can be supplied; not USE at order-one Wilson coupling.

**Bauerschmidt–Dagallier.** Roland Bauerschmidt and Benoît Dagallier, [“Log-Sobolev inequality for near critical Ising models”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02301), arXiv:2202.02301; published *Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics* 77 (2024), 2568–2576. Abstract and HTML read. Theorem 1.1 for finite ferromagnetic Ising model (1.1) states the LSI constant obeys (1.5), $1/\gamma_{\beta,h}\le 1/2+\int_0^\beta \exp(2\int_0^t\chi_s ds)dt$, with susceptibility $\chi_s$ defined in the paper. The abstract says bounded spectral radius of the coupling matrix suffices; uniform volume control follows when susceptibility is bounded, including up to the critical point when the susceptibility stays bounded. This uses ferromagnetic Ising correlation structure and susceptibility, not a Wilson-action Hessian along a Polchinski flow. U(1) test: the Ising positivity/susceptibility theorem does not furnish an input for U(1) Wilson theory; it cannot be transferred by symmetry alone. Z2: yes, the Ising setting expressly includes a critical point and the estimate can deteriorate with susceptibility; it does not forbid a finite-coupling transition. Small parameter: no perturbative small coupling in the theorem; its control parameter is susceptibility. Verdict: **GUIDE**, for functional-inequality reasoning only; no direct (HS-sup) or (AC'-lat) supplier.

Reference follow-up: the Semantic Scholar references/citations endpoints for both arXiv papers were queried. Bauerschmidt–Bodineau cite the RG/Polchinski and earlier 2D stochastic sine-Gordon spectral-gap literature; citation results do not identify a gauge-theory extension. ## C7. Bootstrap and positivity

**Vladimir Kazakov and Zechuan Zheng**, [“Bootstrap for Lattice Yang-Mills theory”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11360), arXiv:2203.11360; arXiv preprint in the metadata read; HTML read. The page identifies this as the numerical bootstrap for SU(∞), dimensions 2, 3, 4, combining Makeenko–Migdal loop equations truncated at maximal loop length with positive-semidefinite Wilson-loop matrices. It reports rigorous upper/lower bounds on the plaquette average, not on a correlation length or transfer gap. Here “rigorous” is conditional on exact finite-dimensional inequalities; the numerical convex optimization is a computer step and is guide-only under this task. The authors say the D=4 lower bounds track Monte Carlo in the strong-coupling phase, while weak-coupling upper bounds reproduce three-loop perturbation theory. It is the planar limit, not finite-N SU(2); no SU(2) beta 1–3 result is claimed from this paper.

**Vladimir Kazakov and Zechuan Zheng**, [“Bootstrap for Finite N Lattice Yang-Mills Theory”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16925), arXiv:2404.16925; arXiv preprint in the metadata read; abstract and HTML read. The abstract says SU(2) loop equations close linearly on single-trace Wilson loops, and reports numerical bootstrap bounds using loops through length 24 with roughly 0.1% precision for the plaquette average in the “physically relevant range” in D=3,4; string-tension estimates are only in qualitative agreement with Monte Carlo. Its exact positivity constraints are finite Gram-matrix positive semidefiniteness of Wilson-loop averages combined with lattice loop equations and SU(2) trace identities. No theorem bounds correlation length or a spectral gap. The page does not provide a verified beta=1-to-3 numeric table in the text I read; **I do not transcribe plot values**. This is the paper’s SU(2) guide for that range: bounds on local plaquette/free-energy data and qualitative string-tension estimates, not a gap result.

The SU(2)-specific mechanism is the trace identity $\operatorname{tr}U\operatorname{tr}V=\operatorname{tr}(UV)+\operatorname{tr}(UV^{-1})$, which makes the loop equations linear/closed on single traces (HTML §I and equation (2.2)). It is false as an identity for U(1) Wilson traces in that SU(2) form, but U(1) has its own loop identities; therefore the bootstrap’s SU(2) simplification is not by itself a physical mechanism distinguishing SU(2) from U(1), and no gap follows. The method can accommodate finite-coupling phase transitions; it imposes no analyticity or uniqueness across beta. Small parameter: no analytic small parameter is claimed, but the finite loop-length cutoff and numerical optimization are essential computational truncations. Open in the paper: larger-loop/continuum bootstrap and selecting the physical loop-equation solution; no continuum gap theorem. Verdict: **GUIDE** only, for exact loop-equation and positivity constraints; it cannot be a Lean-discharge route under the no-computer rule and supplies none of (HS-sup), (ZF_B), (TM_B), (SC_B), (AC'-lat).

### C2 follow-up: gauge random-cluster result found through citations

The Semantic Scholar reference/citation records for Forsström–Viklund led to Paul Duncan and Benjamin Schweinhart, [“A Sharp Deconfinement Transition for Potts Lattice Gauge Theory in Codimension Two”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07534), arXiv:2308.07534; arXiv preprint (no journal reference was present in the API record); abstract and HTML read. The theorem-level statement is Theorem 10: “For $(d-1)$-dimensional Bernoulli plaquette percolation on $\mathbb Z^d$” the normalized negative log probability of the spanning-surface event has an area-law limit below the dual critical threshold and a perimeter-law limit above it. The paper’s abstract states the gauge consequence: a sharp transition for Wilson-loop expectations in $(d-2)$-dimensional q-state Potts hyperlattice gauge theory on $\mathbb Z^d$, dual to the Potts transition. It is unconditional for Ising lattice gauge theory; for q>2 it relies on a slab-regularity conjecture. This is a sharpness/area-vs-perimeter statement, not a mass-gap theorem for local plaquette correlations.

The input is the plaquette random-cluster representation plus FKG/Holley monotonicity and slab percolation sharpness; Theorem 36 imports Duminil-Copin–Raoufi–Tassion exponential decay for the supercritical random-cluster model, and the proof explicitly says it adapts Aizenman–Chayes–Chayes–Fröhlich–Russo to dependent plaquette percolation. It is not a non-Abelian symmetry representation: the coefficient group is finite $\mathbb Z_q$ / Potts. U(1) test: these finite-coefficient homology and FKG random-cluster inputs are not a representation of U(1) Wilson SU(2) or U(1) Wilson theory. Z2 test: yes; the theorem describes sharp deconfinement/area-perimeter change at a finite critical parameter. Small parameter: no perturbative parameter in Theorem 10; the sub/supercritical regimes and (for q>2) the explicit slab-regularity conjecture are the controlling inputs. Verdict: **GUIDE**, best gauge-specific structural lead found for what a Wilson-loop random-cluster representation would need; not USE for (HS-sup), (ZF_B), or (AC'-lat).

Citation follow-up for Duncan–Schweinhart lists the underlying random-cluster/Potts and plaquette-percolation papers, including Duncan–Schweinhart, “Some Properties of the Plaquette Random-Cluster Model” (arXiv:2406.08043), which I read through its abstract and HTML. Its abstract theorem says the $i$-dimensional plaquette random-cluster model with coefficients in $\mathbb Z_q$ is dual to a $(d-i)$-dimensional one and develops boundary conditions, infinite-volume limits and uniqueness. It establishes a representation/duality structure, not the SU(2) gap. The citation graph also surfaced Michael Aizenman, “Geometric Analysis of Ising Models, Part III” (arXiv:2509.02850), HTML read: it extends random currents to mild frustration and applies the geometry to the 3D $\mathbb Z_2$ lattice-gauge deconfinement transition. This strengthens the finite-$\mathbb Z_2$ guide, not a non-Abelian result. Neither paper is a direct SU(2) theorem.

- **One-link Dobrushin contraction is only strong coupling.** The prior `gap-zf-box-x.md` gives its explicit SU(2) finite-volume tube only through $\beta=1/10$; the contraction condition is lost near $0.102$. This does not close the order-one interval.
- **A zero-free radius shrinking as a power of the gap cannot cross a finite interval.** `gap-skeleton-final.md` §12.2 reports the exact continuation obstruction already derived in `gap-zf-box-x.md`: the differential lower bound for $m^p$ reaches zero at finite coupling. A fixed-width tube or a different mechanism is required.
- **Finite-group weak-coupling decay is not an SU(2) compact-Lie-group theorem.** Adhikari–Cao, already documented in `gap-lit-2.md`, is for finite groups; it cannot be transferred to SU(2) without a new representation/estimate.
- **Random-cluster sharpness is limited by the model and slab condition.** Duncan–Schweinhart gives an unconditional Ising lattice-gauge result and a q>2 result conditional on slab regularity; it does not provide a non-Abelian SU(2) random-cluster model or a gap theorem.
- **Polchinski LSI requires the full flow Hessian bound.** Bauerschmidt–Bodineau Theorem 1.2 is conditional on (1.8) at every scale. No paper found verifies it for Wilson gauge theory; the criterion alone would also not distinguish weak-coupling U(1) from SU(2).
- **The two-scale source does not establish a nondegrading many-scale iteration.** No read theorem gives the requested iterated all-scale uniform constants; this route remains unverified, not disproved.
- **Loop bootstrap is local-observable data, not a gap bound.** Kazakov–Zheng’s finite-N numerical bootstrap constrains plaquette averages and estimates string tension; it proves no correlation length or spectral-gap lower bound and uses a computational truncation disallowed as proof evidence here.
- **2D Langevin invariance is not exponential ergodicity.** Chevyrev–Shen proves invariance/universality on the two-dimensional torus; it says nothing about a volume-uniform 4D mixing rate.

The literature pass found directions and exact restrictions, but no new result that closes (HS-sup), (ZF_B), (TM_B), (SC_B), or (AC'-lat) for SU(2) Wilson theory at order-one coupling. This note is research text only; it establishes no proof, audit status, or mathematical status.
