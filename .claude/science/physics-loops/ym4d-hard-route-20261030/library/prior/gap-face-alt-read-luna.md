## 1. Plane-dependent couplings, faces, and layered phases

I found no rigorous theorem for a four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson-family action with independent couplings βμν on the six plane orientations that proves a uniform gap throughout a layered or dimensionally reduced phase, or a monotone gap along a path to the isotropic point. The exact endpoint limits are not such a bridge:

- If a set of plaquette couplings is exactly zero, the corresponding plaquette terms disappear. The resulting measure factorizes only when the remaining plaquette/link incidence graph separates; at the fully decoupled-plane endpoint this is a product of lower-dimensional gauge systems. A product inherits a gap only if each factor has one in the relevant infinite-volume observable sector. No positive lower bound uniform as one restores the missing couplings follows from this identity.
- If one or more couplings tend to (+infty), the Gibbs weight concentrates on flatness constraints in those planes. This is a singular constrained limit, not a finite-coupling cluster-expansion theorem. A reduced theory can retain massless modes or have a gap scale that tends to zero. I found no theorem uniform in the diverging couplings that controls this limit and connects it to isotropic β.
- The Sussman–Alpoge text itself treats a mass-gap estimate as a separate obligation: the index’s chunk-29 rows 260–261 and chunk-30 row 11 point to YM4 sketch lines 31871–31882 and 32487–32490. I read those lines in the fetched outline. It gives the example ∫ E e⁻ᵉ e⁻ᵗᵉ dE=(1+t)⁻² to show that polynomial clustering does not exclude spectral weight arbitrarily close to zero; its gap question remains separate. This does not add an anisotropic theorem. Credit Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text.

**Fu–Nielsen (1984), U(1), approximate result.** Their “A Layer Phase in a Non-Isotropic U(1) Lattice Gauge Theory” studies a (D)-dimensional anisotropic Wilson-type U(1) action with intra-layer and inter-layer plaquette weights. The reported prediction for D ≥ 5, with d = D−1, is a Coulomb phase within (d)-dimensional layers and strong coupling/confinement between layers. This is a mean-field proposal, with perturbative corrections, not a rigorous theorem. In particular, it predicts massless Coulomb layers rather than a positive layer mass gap. I found only the bibliographic/journal record and secondary reports, not an open full copy of Fu and Nielsen; the exact derivation and parameter inequalities are **NOT READ**, so no sharper threshold is attributed to them. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90529-7).

**Later non-Abelian evidence is numerical.** Del Debbio, Kenway, Lambrou and Rinaldi’s open 2013 paper studies the five-dimensional anisotropic SU(2) Wilson plaquette action with periodic boundaries. It reports a first-order confinement/deconfinement transition at the point β₄=2.60 that an earlier small-lattice study had proposed as an endpoint to a second-order transition. Its stated conclusion is that a continuum 4D reduction through the claimed layer phase is “unpromising”; it explicitly does not rule out a later second-order point. This is Monte Carlo phase-structure evidence, not a theorem, and has no clustering-rate estimate. [Open arXiv copy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.0752). The older Fu–Nielsen layer phase was U(1); it does not imply one for pure 4D SU(2). The 5D U(1) and 5D SU(2) literature summarized in `gap-aniso-read-luna.md` is reused, not recounted as a fresh result.

**Rigorous strong coupling permits anisotropy only near the origin.** The Osterwalder–Seiler cluster expansion is stated for sufficiently strong coupling and yields volume-uniform analyticity/clustering. A standard multivariate high-temperature expansion applies when every plaquette activity is sufficiently small, so it covers an anisotropic small-coupling neighborhood, not a face with some couplings large. I did not find in an open primary source an exact anisotropic threshold in the six βμν. There is no read source establishing a non-Abelian layered phase with a mass gap, decoupled-plane gap uniform in volume, or interpolation from any zero/infinite-coupling face to isotropic β around 2.

## 2. Two-dimensional gauge, spin, and principal-chiral models

**Pure 2D SU(2) lattice gauge theory.** It is exactly solvable by character expansion. On a simply connected lattice, plaquette holonomies are independent after gauge fixing; Wilson loops have an exact area law, with the fundamental loop factor the fundamental character coefficient to the enclosed area. This gives exact exponential area decay for the loop observable at each fixed finite Wilson coupling. It is not the same statement as a nontrivial, volume-uniform 4D glueball gap: local gauge-invariant plaquette algebras in the infinite plane have no propagating gauge degrees of freedom, while the cylinder transfer spectrum depends on circumference. I found no open primary text in this pass that states a uniform all-local-observable infinite-volume mass-gap theorem in the exact 4D sense. Gross–King–Sengupta, Driver and Lévy construct 2D continuum Yang–Mills measures; their existence/area-law results do not give the desired 4D family bridge. Hexagon preprint v5 likewise distinguishes 2D construction from a mass gap in physical units. No one should import “2D gauge theory is gapped” without naming which transfer direction, volume limit, and observable sector is meant. **NOT READ:** full primary 2D SU(2) gauge proof; accessible source found was a review/bibliographic mention only.

**2D O(n), n ≥ 3, spin correlations.** OpenAI’s September 2026 preprint *Exponential decay in two-dimensional classical (O(n)) models*, Theorem 1.1, states: “For every integer n ≥ 3 and every finite β > 0, there are constants A = A(n, β) < ∞ and m = m(n, β) > 0 such that, for every finite nearest-neighbor square-lattice subgraph G, every strength array b ∈ [0, β]^E, and all x, y ∈ V, 0 ≤ ⟨σ_x · σ_y⟩_{G,b}^{(n)} ≤ A exp(−m‖x−y‖₂).” Corollary 1.2 passes this bound to every local weak limit of free-boundary square boxes. This is for the unit-sphere ferromagnet with no external field and no fixed spins in the theorem’s boundary conditions. It proves exponential spin two-point decay, not a full transfer gap for all local observables and not uniqueness of every infinite-volume Gibbs state. O(2) is explicitly excluded: the cited Fröhlich–Spencer Kosterlitz–Thouless result gives a low-temperature regime where exponential decay fails. n = 1 is also not covered; it has its Ising critical point. [OpenAI Math preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Exponential-decay-in-two-dimensional-classical-On-models-September-23-2026); full PDF read from the pinned source snapshot in `/tmp/sa-gap-face-alt-read-luna.on.5k7DlJ/`.

**2D O(4), full periodic transfer spectrum.** The OpenAI preprint *Sharp mass bounds for the two-dimensional O(4) model*, Theorem 1.1, states: “There are finite constants β₀, c, C with 0 < c < C such that for every β ≥ β₀ the periodic square measures have a unique local limit, and its full transfer gap satisfies c√β e^(−πβ) ≤ m_lat(β) ≤ C√β e^(−πβ).” It also gives bounds on a tuned sequence of exact block transformations in (1.4). Corollary 1.2 strengthens the coupling range: for every finite β > 0, the periodic-square measures have a unique periodic local limit and m_lat(β) ≥ c exp{−πβ − C√((1+β) log(2+β))} > 0. The source scopes uniqueness to the periodic local limit; it does not assert uniqueness for all Gibbs states or a continuum gap without further convergence hypotheses. The theorem is for homogeneous O(4) periodic squares. Its preliminary Theorem 3.1 gives uniform mixing over nonnegative inhomogeneous bond strengths 0 ≤ β_e ≤ β on unpinned tori/free subgraphs, not arbitrary external fields. [OpenAI Math preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Sharp-mass-bounds-for-the-two-dimensional-O4-model-September-23-2026); full PDF read from the pinned source snapshot in `/tmp/sa-gap-face-alt-read-luna.o4.5k7DlJ/`. These are written research preprints, not an independent check by this reading.

**SU(2) principal chiral model (PCM).** As a lattice spin model, SU(2) is diffeomorphic to the unit 3-sphere: represent each group element as a unit quaternion q_x. Then (1/2) Re Tr(U_x U_y†) = q_x · q_y. Thus the nearest-neighbor ferromagnetic lattice SU(2) PCM is exactly the O(4) model up to coupling normalization. The O(4) periodic-square theorem above supplies a full lattice transfer gap at every finite positive coupling in that convention. It does not automatically cover a different PCM discretization, an external field, interactions between several PCM copies, or arbitrary SU(N) with N > 2. Continuum SU(N) PCM integrability and exact S-matrix mass formulas are often called a “mass-gap result” in physics accounts; they are not a rigorous proof of the lattice family or of all local correlation bounds in the present sense. The open older SU(2) vortex paper located in this search proposes a sufficient vortex-free-energy condition and tests it numerically; its own abstract does not prove that condition at every coupling. [Borisenko–Skala, abstract](https://inspirehep.net/literature/527971).

**External fields, pins, and coupling models.** No theorem found here proves a general full gap for O(n)/PCM systems coupled to one another or in arbitrary site-dependent external fields. The O(n) theorem permits weakened/removed nonnegative bonds, but no fields. The O(4) full-gap theorem is homogeneous and periodic; its proof has intermediate pinned/twisted cell estimates, which are not a theorem for arbitrary externally forced infinite-volume systems. The older Kunz–Pfister–Vuillermot inequalities apply to restricted common-axis fields/couplings (including a special O(4) cone), not arbitrary field directions. This distinction is recorded in `gap-fstn-read-luna.md` and reused here. I did not find an open result that upgrades these restricted statements to several interacting PCM copies.

## 3. Correlation inequalities, monotonicity, and reflection positivity

**What transfers.** For the ferromagnetic Ising model, FKG/GKS inequalities give positive association under their standard nonnegative coupling/field hypotheses. For plane rotators and selected O(4) component cones, Ginibre/Kunz–Pfister–Vuillermot inequalities give covariance positivity under the theorem’s restricted common-axis coupling and field assumptions. The O(4) mass paper uses Ginibre and FKG only after conditional decompositions into circle-rotator and Ising ferromagnets. These hypotheses do not hold automatically for noncommuting SU(2) link variables or plaquette traces.

**Non-Abelian gauge theory.** The checked literature notes report no general SU(2) GKS/Ginibre inequality making all Wilson-loop or plaquette correlations monotone in a bare plane coupling. This is a bounded negative search, not a theorem that no such inequality exists. What is available is more specific:

- Reflection positivity for Wilson-type actions follows from nonnegative character/Fourier coefficients of each reflected plaquette kernel. It gives a positive transfer operator and supports chessboard/reflection estimates for chosen nonnegative observables/events. It is not monotonicity in a coupling and it does not itself yield clustering.
- Tomboulis–Yaffe type inequalities compare twisted partition functions/center-flux observables and Wilson-loop bounds under reflection positivity and suitable character-positivity assumptions. They are not GKS ordering for arbitrary local observables and do not give the needed derivative sign of the gap in a plane coupling.
- For the SU(2) Wilson one-plaquette weight, character coefficients (c_j(β)=I_{2j+1}(β)/I_1(β)) are positive and strictly log-concave in the representation index by the Turán inequality. This is an exact one-plaquette property, not a correlation inequality for the interacting lattice and not a proof that the mass increases with β. The cited SU(2) nonabelian comparison attempts are model-specific; one-plaquette properties need not survive decimation.

Sources/reuse: `gap-sepinput-read-opus.md` sections 5.2–5.3; Tomboulis, [arXiv:0707.2179](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2179); Kanazawa’s extension of Tomboulis–Yaffe, [arXiv:0808.3442](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3442); and Ginibre’s original [CMP paper record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01609449). The latter was not fully available as an open copy in the cited prior search. The 2D O(4) and O(n) papers are read in full above. **No proved monotonicity of a non-Abelian mass gap in any one plane coupling was found.**

## 4. Four-dimensional SU(2) strong-coupling gap and the range toward β≈2

**Osterwalder–Seiler (1978).** Their paper *Gauge Field Theories on a Lattice* proves reflection positivity/positive transfer structure and, at sufficiently strong coupling, the infinite-volume limit and Wilson confinement bound by a convergent cluster expansion. The local-observable cluster expansion converges uniformly in volume; exponential clustering/mass gap and analyticity follow from it. The statement uses compact gauge group, including SU(2), and a strong-coupling neighborhood of β=0. The accessible publisher copy gives only an abstract; the open full text of the 1978 article was **NOT READ**, and the article does not give a numeric SU(2) Wilson β threshold in the readable abstract. I therefore do not assign it a numerical endpoint. [Annals of Physics record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8). Shen–Zhu–Zhu’s open paper, introduction lines 91–103, restates OS’s “sufficiently strong” regime and its volume-uniform local-observable cluster expansion.

**Shen–Zhu–Zhu (2023), explicit all-observable clustering at SU(2) strong coupling.** Their action is dμ ∝ exp(Nβ Σ_p Re Tr Q_p) dQ, with G = SU(N) in dimension d. Assumption 1.1 is |β| < 1/[16N(d−1)]. Corollary 1.6 states that, under this assumption, for disjointly supported smooth cylinder functions f and g,

\[
|\operatorname{Cov}_{\mu^{ym}_{N,\beta}}(f,g)| \le c_1 d(g) e^{-c_N d(\operatorname{supp}f,\operatorname{supp}g)}\left(\|\!\|\!\|f\|\!\|\!\|_\infty\|\!\|\!\|g\|\!\|\!\|_\infty+\|f\|_{L^2}\|g\|_{L^2}\right),
\]

where c₁ depends on support sizes and c_N on the positive curvature constant, N and d. For d = 4, N = 2, the paper’s convention gives β < 1/48. Its exponent is 2β Re Tr, while the conventional SU(2) Wilson exponent is (β_W/2) Re Tr, so β_W = 4β and the sufficient Wilson range is 0 ≤ β_W < 1/12. The theorem covers SU(2) explicitly and proves uniqueness of the infinite-volume Gibbs state and exponential clustering of all smooth local observables. It is analytic, for homogeneous isotropic coupling, not plane-dependent endpoints. [CMP article, open full paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-022-04609-1); locally read in `/tmp/sa-gap-face-alt-read-luna.szz.r7tmRg/`.

**Explicit SU(2) range from a recent open preprint.** Bhattacharjee, Bhattacharya, Lockwood and Romanov, *Explicit Lattice Yang–Mills Mass Gap for Every Compact Simple Group Reducing the Continuum Gap to One Cube*, version 5 (31 Oct 2026), Theorem 1.1 states for G = SU(2) and 0 ≤ β_W < 1/3, free-boundary boxes exhausting ℤ⁴ have a unique exhaustion-independent gauge-invariant local limit; it is translation/hypercubic invariant and reflection positive; truncated Lipschitz-observable correlations decay at every rate below −log(3β_W), and the transfer matrix has gap m ≥ −log(3β_W) > 0. This is a fixed-lattice theorem, not a continuum mass gap. The source also states Theorem 1.3 for 0 ≤ β_W ≤ 0.345, for SU(2) with plaquette blocks and a positive transfer gap. However its endpoint depends on certified interval bounds (including an evaluation on 20,000 subintervals); that computational proof is outside this task’s no-computer-assisted rule and must not discharge a repository obligation here. The clean hand-analytic Theorem 1.1 reaches only βW<1/3. The paper itself says the gap between this strong-coupling result and the SU(2) scaling/crossover near βW≈2.2 remains open; it gives no mass-gap theorem near βW=2. [Open v5 PDF](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00341v5), locally read in `/tmp/sa-gap-face-alt-read-luna.hexagon.kyPgtB/`. This is a research preprint’s claimed result, not checked in this reading.

**Series versus theorem.** Münster’s *Strong Coupling Expansions for the Mass Gap in Lattice Gauge Theories* computes formal/controlled series coefficients for SU(2), SU(3), Z2 and Z3 to finite order. Decker computes SU(2) mixed-action coefficients through order β⁷. These are expansions and numerical extrapolations; the latter are not a proved convergence radius extending to the scaling window. No inspected rigorous SU(2) cluster expansion or series theorem reaches βW near 2. The finite-volume fact that a positive transfer kernel has a positive first gap at each fixed box and β does not provide a positive lower bound uniform in box size.

## 5. Hamiltonian/transfer-matrix limits, Higgs actions, and added terms

**Anisotropic-time limit.** The Wilson anisotropic action with spatial and temporal plaquette coefficients has the formal transfer-matrix limit a_t → 0, β_t → ∞, β_s → 0 with matched products, yielding the Kogut–Susskind electric-Casimir plus magnetic-plaquette Hamiltonian. Kogut–Susskind’s 1975 paper constructs the canonical Hamiltonian and explains the strong-coupling string basis. This is a derivation of the operator and a perturbative framework, not a theorem that the full 3+1D SU(2) Hamiltonian has a volume-uniform gap at a stated finite gauge coupling g. Strong-coupling expansions and anisotropic Monte Carlo spectra are not all-volume gap proofs. I did not find an open primary proof of a 3+1D Hamiltonian-limit gap covering the isotropic window. The cited overview is [Kogut (1979) review](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.51.659); Kogut–Susskind primary article: [Phys. Rev. D 11, 395](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.11.395), open proceedings copy [here](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1006302/contributions/4399120/attachments/2287752/3888668/lattice2021.pdf).

**Gauge–Higgs and Wilson-containing actions.** Osterwalder–Seiler also establish strong-coupling analyticity for lattice Higgs/gauge systems in their expansion domain. Fradkin–Shenker prove analyticity connection between confinement-like and Higgs-like regions for fixed-length fundamental Higgs models and a class of local gauge-invariant observables; analyticity is not a quantitative gap theorem. The Abelian Higgs mass-gap theorems (e.g. Balaban–Imbrie–Jaffe–Brydges, 1984) are U(1) with a fluctuating complex scalar and a weak-coupling cluster-expansion neighborhood; they do not imply non-Abelian SU(2) Wilson results. No theorem was found for the requested class “any local reflection-positive action that has Wilson at one parameter value” that supplies a gap near βW≈2 or carries a gap along an interpolation. A member action’s gap says nothing by itself about a different point in that family.
