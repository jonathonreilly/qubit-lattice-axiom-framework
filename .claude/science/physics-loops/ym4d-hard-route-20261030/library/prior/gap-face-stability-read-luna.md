## Complete analyticity and weak-to-strong mixing

**Dobrushin–Shlosman (1987), “Completely Analytical Interactions: Constructive Description.”** I read the open journal PDF, pp. 983–1014, especially §§1–2. They define complete analyticity by twelve equivalent conditions “formulated in terms of … correlation decay, or truncated correlation bounds,” with uniformity over every finite volume and boundary condition. Their worked setting is finite spin space, translation-invariant finite-range interactions on `Z^d`; their conditions include complex-perturbation zero-freeness and all-order semi-invariant/tree bounds. The analytic conditions contain an interaction-norm neighborhood, so once an interaction is proved CA, sufficiently small local perturbations remain in that neighborhood. This is the direct stability mechanism sought. It could fit the sheet product only if the unperturbed compact-spin sheet law first satisfies a suitable CA criterion; the OpenAI O(4) theorem below does not establish that stronger, all-boundary hypothesis. The 1987 paper says it restricts to finite spin for simplicity, so its stated theorem is not itself a compact-continuous-spin application. [Dobrushin–Shlosman, DOI 10.1007/BF01011153](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01011153).

**Martinelli–Olivieri–Schonmann (1994), “For 2-D Lattice Spin Systems Weak Mixing Implies Strong Mixing.”** The published abstract states the theorem for “finite range discrete spin systems on the two dimensional lattice `Z^2`”: weak mixing implies a stronger DS/complete-analyticity-like condition restricted to squares (or unions of sufficiently large squares). Their argument uses the one-dimensional boundary to stop propagation along the boundary. This is not a theorem for compact continuous spins; no extension to `S^3` was found. It starts from weak mixing with boundary-uniform control, which cannot be inferred from the OpenAI O(4) free-subgraph estimate alone. Full text was not available in an open copy in this pass; I rely only on the primary publisher abstract/metadata and do not attribute finer constants or extra hypotheses. [Martinelli–Olivieri–Schonmann, DOI 10.1007/BF02099735](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099735).

## Log-Sobolev and mixing for continuous spins

**Stroock–Zegarlinski (1992), continuous-spin papers.** The open publisher abstract for *The logarithmic Sobolev inequality for continuous spin systems on a lattice* says Theorem 1.6 gives a sufficient condition on a local specification for its Gibbs state to satisfy LSI, and Theorem 3.10 relates that condition to DS mixing. Yoshida's open paper identifies the earlier compact-spin results as equivalence, when properly defined, between DS mixing and rapid Gibbs/Glauber relaxation. Bibliographic records identify a compact continuous spin space. The full SZ article and the companion compact-spin equivalence paper were not available as open full-text copies here; therefore I do not claim their exact differential criterion, coupling threshold, or a model-specific application. Even if their criterion applies to compact `S^3`, it must be verified uniformly for every sheet box/boundary; the free-boundary O(4) two-point theorem is not that verification. [Stroock–Zegarlinski, JFA 104 (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(92)90003-2); [Stroock–Zegarlinski, CMP 144 (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02101094).

**Yoshida (2001), “The Equivalence of the Log-Sobolev Inequality and a Mixing Condition for Unbounded Spin Systems on the Lattice.”** I read the open Numdam PDF, pp. 223–243, Theorem 2.1. For finite volumes, the theorem makes the listed mixing conditions uniform over all volumes and boundary configurations. With (U0), it proves uniform LSI ⇒ Poincaré ⇒ exponential decay of spin covariance ⇒ single-site boundary influence decay; with stronger (U1), these conditions and a constructive criterion are equivalent. The system is a *ferromagnetic unbounded real-spin* lattice gas with finite-range nonnegative couplings and a confining on-site potential `U=V+W`, `inf V''≥m`, `W,W'` bounded; (U1) allows every `m>0`. The paper explicitly says this extends Stroock–Zegarlinski's compact-spin result, but Yoshida's own hypotheses are not the compact O(4) sheet model. The high-temperature/small-coupling alternative noted there is not a bound at the target couplings. [Yoshida, DOI 10.1016/S0246-0203(00)01066-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0246-0203(00)01066-9).

## Cluster expansions and truncated correlations

**Kotecký–Preiss (1986), abstract polymer expansion.** The published abstract says it gives a direct convergence criterion for abstract polymer/contour models. The criterion applies after a polymer representation and a summable incompatibility majorant have been supplied; it does not take “exponential two-point decay of a reference measure” as its hypothesis and turn that alone into a polymer gas. The outline's explicit rooted-tree form (above) makes this gap especially clear. The open publisher PDF endpoint returned an HTML page rather than a readable paper, so the paper's full theorem was not read; I use the abstract plus the explicit criterion stated in the outline, not a claimed detailed K-P theorem. [Kotecký–Preiss, DOI 10.1007/BF01211762](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211762).

**Duneau–Iagolnitzer–Souillard (1973) and Duneau–Iagolnitzer–Souillard (1974), strong cluster properties.** The 1973 paper's bibliographic/abstract record was found, but its full text was not available as an open copy. I read the authors' open copy of the 1974 sequel. It states an N-point truncated-correlation estimate as a sum over spanning trees, each edge weighted by a decaying function `u(distance)`. The sequel proves such “strong cluster” properties for finite-range interactions in specified regimes: low activity for lattice and continuous systems; arbitrary activity at high temperature for lattice systems; and selected ferromagnets at field/temperature conditions. The continuous-spin extension is real, but is conditional on those regimes and is not a blanket consequence of two-point decay. It could apply if the O(4) sheet couplings were in one of those proven regimes; the literature read does not place beta 2.3–2.6 there, and it does not address a general transverse plaquette perturbation. [1973 paper, DOI 10.1007/BF01646265](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01646265); [1974 sequel, DOI 10.1007/BF01646352](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01646352).

**Brydges/Federbush-type expansions.** The open abstract for Brydges–Federbush's finite-range particle expansion gives convergence and exponential clustering at low enough density under stability and technical potential assumptions. That is a gas/low-density expansion, not an expansion around an arbitrary compact-spin measure known only to have exponentially decaying two-point functions. The attempted open PDF endpoint returned HTML and the full paper was not read. Together with the Duneau tree estimate and the outline's abstract polymer lemma, this gives the standard route *if* one has summable activities or base-measure cumulants with the required tree majorant; no source found derives that majorant from the O(4) anchor's two-point estimate alone. [Brydges–Federbush, 1976](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01608729).

**Laslier–Lubetzky (arXiv:2611.17193, 12 Nov 2026), “Radius Decay and Boundary Polymers.”** I read the open 29-page PDF, Theorem 1 and examples. For a generic nearest-neighbor Gibbs measure on a finite simply connected subset of `Z^2`, with finite spin set `S`, arbitrary inhomogeneous edge/site potentials and boundary conditions, it handles added many-body terms `g_{x,r}` on radius-r balls when `||g_{x,r}||∞ ≤ exp(-C*(r+1))`, `C*≥35`. It constructs a boundary-decorated polymer representation with weights ≤ `exp(-C*|γ|/10)`. The finite-spin restriction rules out direct use for `S^3` O(4) sheets. Its exponential-correlation corollaries are proved for particular high-temperature Ising (arbitrary BC) and low-temperature Ising (plus BC) cases; the theorem's polymer representation alone does not give an O(4) cumulant estimate. A small plaquette term has fixed radius, but the missing continuous-spin extension remains decisive. This is a recent reusable architecture, not a result that closes row 1091. [Laslier–Lubetzky, arXiv:2611.17193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17193).

**Capel–Moscolari–Teufel–Wessel (2023), “From Decay of Correlations to Locality and Stability of the Gibbs State.”** I read the open arXiv manuscript. Theorem 22 says exponential covariance decay in an *unperturbed quantum Gibbs state* implies that a perturbation `V` supported on one finite region changes a distant local expectation by a distance-decaying amount (under uniform Lieb–Robinson/QBP locality). Its hypotheses are finite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces and short-range Hamiltonians. This is local response to a localized defect; it neither proves decay for the perturbed state nor covers a bulk sum of transverse plaquettes. It is also not a direct theorem for classical continuous `S^3` spins. Thus it does not discharge the needed stability of the bulk perturbed covariance. [Capel et al., arXiv:2310.09182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09182).

## O(N) results and recent perturbations

**OpenAI, “Exponential decay in two-dimensional classical O(n) models” (23 Sep 2026), Theorem 1.1.** I read the open PDF. For integer `n≥3` and every finite `β>0`, it proves `0 ≤ <σ_x·σ_y> ≤ A(n,β)e^{-m(n,β)||x-y||_2}` on every finite square-lattice subgraph, uniformly over all nonnegative nearest-neighbor pair strengths `b_e≤β`, with free (unpinned) boundary. It passes to subsequential free-boundary local limits; the paper explicitly says it does not prove uniqueness of all infinite-volume Gibbs states. It is a two-point spin bound, not all-local-observable CA or a tree bound for arbitrary cumulants. It applies to each decoupled sheet, but a transverse plaquette creates multi-sheet, multi-spin interactions outside the theorem's pairwise O(n) class. [OpenAI O(n) paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Exponential-decay-in-two-dimensional-classical-On-models-September-23-2026/paper.pdf).

**OpenAI, “Sharp mass bounds for the two-dimensional O(4) model” (23 Sep 2026), Theorem 3.1.** I read the open 102-page PDF, §§3.1, 3.11–3.12. This is closer to the anchor needed by row 1091: for unpinned rectangular tori above a minimum side and every unpinned free subgraph, it bounds covariance of *arbitrary local Lipschitz observables* by a polynomial support/distance prefactor times exponential decay, uniformly for nonnegative pair couplings `β_e≤β`. The proof uses two conditional decompositions into ferromagnetic circle/Ising systems, association, and two localizations. It explicitly restricts the all-observable conclusion to untwisted zero-field ferromagnetic pair couplings. The paper says uniqueness only for the homogeneous periodic local limit and not uniqueness of all Gibbs states. Theorem 3.1 is not a uniform perturbation theorem: adding transverse plaquettes leaves its pairwise model class. The fact it controls arbitrary *two-block covariance* still does not state uniform third cumulants or a tree bound for all orders. [OpenAI O(4) paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Sharp-mass-bounds-for-the-two-dimensional-O4-model-September-23-2026/paper.pdf).

**Ding–Huang–Yang (arXiv:2611.04908, 3 Nov 2026), “Exponential decay for the two-dimensional Heisenberg model.”** I read the open PDF, Theorems 1.1 and 1.4. For integer `N≥3`, it proves exponential spin two-point decay in free boxes for interior sites; its boundary one-point statement requires coordinatewise constant boundary data. The proof constructs a generalized loop-soup O(N) model and gets exponential loop connectivity for real `N>2`; the model is nearest-neighbor and ferromagnetic, with site-varying bounded nonnegative strengths. It is a complementary proof of the isotropic two-point result, not a stability theorem for general local perturbations or a multipoint-cumulant estimate. [Ding–Huang–Yang, arXiv:2611.04908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04908).

**Pei (arXiv:2611.01532v2, 11 Nov 2026), “The Massive Phase of the Two-Dimensional Heisenberg Model Is Stable under Small Anisotropy.”** I read the open v2 PDF, Theorems 1.1, 1.3, 1.6 and the annulus-transfer proof. For the `S^2` nearest-neighbor anisotropic pair model with couplings `b_e Σ_{i=1}^3 J_i σ_x^i σ_y^i`, `b_e∈[0,β]`, there is `δ(β)>0` such that every finite free subgraph has exponentially decaying spin two-point functions when `|J_i−1|≤δ(β)`; the same method handles near-isotropic `O(n)` componentwise pair couplings and aligned single-site quadratic anisotropy. Its proof transfers an isotropic annulus crossing estimate to anisotropic couplings by a finite-volume Radon–Nikodym comparison, then uses sign clusters and coarse paths. It uses DHY's annulus input for Theorem 1.1; Theorem 1.6 gives an explicit lower bound `δ(β)≥exp(−C(1+β²))` using the OpenAI O(4) source. This is a genuine recent small-perturbation result, but its perturbations preserve pairwise component structure and the proof's sign/planar-correlation inequalities. A transverse plaquette term coupling sheets is a four-spin interaction and is not covered; the theorem gives two-point spin decay only, not all-observable or multi-point tree decay. [Pei, arXiv:2611.01532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01532).

**Garban–de Montgolfier (arXiv:2610.16824, 12 Oct 2026), “Small perturbations of the 2d classical Heisenberg model.”** The paper proves power-law lower bounds at sufficiently large β for an easy-plane `S^2` model with pair coupling `σ^xσ^x+σ^yσ^y+λσ^zσ^z`, for every fixed `λ∈(−1,1)` (and a hemisphere variant). This identifies a different perturbation regime and is discussed by Pei; it does not contradict Pei's `δ(β)` statement because its low-temperature threshold depends on λ. It is not a transverse-sheet perturbation and does not give a multi-point decay theorem. [Garban–de Montgolfier, arXiv:2610.16824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16824).

## Applicability to weakly coupled O(4) sheets and source limits

For the decoupled anchor, the O(4) Theorem 3.1 is the strongest direct input found: it gives two-block covariance decay for free subgraphs of each sheet. The recent Pei theorem shows that a finite-scale crossing argument can prove stability for a special internal anisotropy, and Laslier–Lubetzky gives a boundary-polymer construction for finite-spin 2D bases. Neither handles a bulk four-spin transverse plaquette term on continuous `S^3`-valued sheets. DS complete analyticity would be the clean all-order stability route if its boundary-uniform/complex-neighborhood hypotheses were established for this anchor; they are not established by the cited OpenAI theorem. The MOS theorem cannot be directly extended from finite to compact continuous spins from its statement, and no such extension was found here. Continuous-spin LSI/mixing theory offers a possible criterion, but the relevant compact-spin SZ full text was inaccessible and no cited estimate verifies it at the target β range.

For row 1091, the literature therefore narrows but does not close the bridge: a route needs (i) a uniform all-boundary or suitable face-state mixing/analyticity criterion for the compact continuous O(4) sheet product, or (ii) a direct summable third-cumulant bound along the perturbation path and a uniform tree cumulant/activity bound for all orders. Existing pairwise two-point decay, including OpenAI's all-observable pair covariance estimate, is not by itself a written proof of either input. RESULT: No published result found transfers the O(4) sheet pair-covariance estimate to a size-uniform positive interval under a transverse plaquette perturbation or supplies the required uniform third/tree cumulant bounds. Dobrushin–Shlosman gives the right conditional stability mechanism if complete analyticity is proved; Pei gives a genuine but model-specific pair-anisotropy stability theorem; MOS remains stated for finite discrete spins.
