## Correlation inequalities and comparison results

### Ginibre and Kunz–Pfister–Vuillermot (KPV)

Ginibre’s “General formulation of Griffiths’ inequalities” (1970) is cited by KPV as the source of the D=2 cone inequality; I could not read an open full copy of Ginibre’s paper. The KPV 1976 full paper was readable from the open PDF mirror, although downloading a local copy failed (the mirror timed out and IOP returned HTTP 500). The paper defines a classical D-component spin as a unit vector with uniform spherical a priori measure and uses pair couplings plus an inhomogeneous linear field [KPV, DOI/full text](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/9/10/019).

For the plane rotator (D=2), KPV define (F_2) as functions with nonnegative coefficients in the Fourier basis — their phrase is “powers of \(\cos(m\cdot\theta)\) with positive coefficients only.” Their Proposition 2 says that if (-H_2\in F_2) and (f,g\in F_2), then \(\langle f\rangle\ge0\) and \(\langle fg\rangle\ge\langle f\rangle\langle g\rangle\). Their displayed sufficient field condition is (J^1\ge|J^2|, h^1\ge0, h^2=0), selecting one common field axis. KPV’s O(4) example (their §3.3) gives the analogous proposition only under (J^1=J^2\ge J^3=J^4\ge0) and (h^1\ge0, h^2=h^3=h^4=0). KPV also give an O(2) covariance ordering when the two component couplings are equal and (h^1\ge|h^2|); that is a restricted positive quadrant, not arbitrary sitewise field directions. These are finite-volume correlation inequalities with common coordinate axes; no arbitrary-direction O(4) pins or field comparison follows from the stated hypotheses. Spin spaces: (S^1) for D=2, (S^3) for D=4. Applicability to target: no for arbitrary directions.

### Messager–Miracle-Solé–Pfister (MMP)

The full text of “Correlation inequalities and uniqueness of the equilibrium state for the plane rotator ferromagnetic model” was not openly readable; I used its publisher abstract only [MMP, 1978](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01624786). The abstract’s exact condition/conclusion includes: “the derivability of the free energy as function of the magnetic field h implies the existence of a unique translation invariant Gibbs state.” In the isotropic model, at (h=0), all Gibbs states are rotation invariant; in the anisotropic model the uniqueness assertion holds for nonzero (h), and there are at most two extremal translation-invariant states for almost all anisotropy parameters. Spin space is (S^1). The abstract gives no quantitative finite-volume boundary influence, no O(4) result, and no arbitrary direction or spatially varying pin theorem. Because the inequalities and their detailed hypotheses are behind the publisher paywall, those details remain unread.

### Lebowitz, Pfister, and Bricmont–Lebowitz–Pfister (BLP)

Lebowitz’s open 1972 correlation paper is specifically a ferromagnetic Ising/lattice-gas system with scalar spins (\{\pm1\}). It uses FKG to bound correlations between separated sets by pair correlations and derives smoothness when pair correlations decay exponentially uniformly in volume [Lebowitz, 1972](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~lebowitz/PUBLIST/jll.pub_90.pdf). It is not a continuous O(4) inequality.

The relevant boundary-comparison theorem I could read is Bricmont, Lebowitz and Pfister, “On the equivalence of boundary conditions” (1979), for general spin systems and generalized boundary conditions [BLP, open full text](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~lebowitz/PUBLIST/bricmont_pfister_176.pdf). Its hypothesis is that the supremum, over volumes and interior configurations, of the boundary-induced energy difference is finite, (C_{b,b'}<\infty). For any common exhausting volume sequence along which the two finite-volume laws converge to (ho,ho'), the theorem says the limits are equivalent (mutually absolutely continuous); its exact conclusion includes “if ρ is extremal, ρ=ρ'.” At finite volume the proof bounds event probabilities by multiplicative factors (e^{\pm 2\beta C_{b,b'}}). Spin space is general. This is not a bound on how a fixed local expectation changes as the boundary is moved farther away. For arbitrary sitewise boundary changes along a growing perimeter, (C_{b,b'}) need not stay bounded, and the theorem has no exponential-distance rate. So it does not imply target FST-N.

I found no separate open primary paper by Lebowitz and Pfister that yields a comparison of arbitrary O(4) directions with aligned ones. The BLP theorem above is the readable general boundary-equivalence result and has the finite-energy-difference limitation just stated. MMP is a distinct plane-rotator uniqueness result.

### Bricmont–Fontaine–Landau (BFL)

The full BFL paper “On the uniqueness of the equilibrium state for plane rotators” was not openly readable; the publisher abstract was [available](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01614213). Its exact claim begins: “there is a unique translation invariant equilibrium state in zero external field, if there is no spontaneous magnetization.” It further identifies a unique phase in two dimensions and says a sufficiently large external field implies uniqueness by Lee–Yang. Spin space is (S^1). These are uniqueness statements, not uniform finite-volume decay of boundary influence; the large-field conclusion is not an arbitrary weak or sitewise field result and neither statement extends here to O(4) arbitrary pins.

### Aligned-boundary domination

The closest explicit pin/boundary domination found is OpenAI’s 2026 “Exponential decay in two-dimensional classical O(n) models” [official preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Exponential-decay-in-two-dimensional-classical-On-models-September-23-2026). Its base spin law is on a finite square-lattice subgraph, σ at each vertex lies on (S^{n-1}), (n\ge3), edge strengths satisfy (0\le b_e\le\beta<\infty), and there are no fixed boundary spins, external fields or added interactions. Theorem 1.1 is exactly (0\le\langle\sigma_x\cdot\sigma_y\rangle\le A(n,\beta)e^{-m(n,\beta)|x-y|_2}), uniformly over such free-boundary finite subgraphs and strength arrays. The paper explicitly says its result is a spin two-point estimate, does not prove Gibbs-state uniqueness, and a full transfer gap controlling all local observables (including rotation-invariant bond energies) needs a separate argument.

Its Proposition 4.5 adds a limited comparison: for the three-component (O(3)) free spin law on a finite graph with nonnegative edge strengths, pinning any subset to the same pole (s_*=(1,0,0)) cannot decrease the probability of any increasing event in the auxiliary bond law. The spin space for this proposition is (S^2), and the comparison concerns increasing bond events, not expectations of arbitrary spin observables. This aligned common-pole pinning does not dominate arbitrary O(4) boundary configurations or multiple field directions. Thus it is not the requested inequality “general boundary conditions \(\le\) aligned boundary conditions” for neutral O(4) observables. For the exact model, hypotheses, pinning proposition and scope limits see [the paper text, §§1 and 4.2](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Exponential-decay-in-two-dimensional-classical-On-models-September-23-2026).

## Neutral-observable boundary influence and uniqueness

I found no readable O(N) or principal-chiral-model theorem giving exponential decay, uniform over arbitrary boundary directions, of the response of a far neutral/rotation-invariant local observable. The OpenAI O(n) theorem above supplies only the free-boundary vector two-point estimate and expressly leaves local invariant observables and uniqueness outside its conclusion. The two-dimensional principal chiral model literature surfaced in this search concerned mass-gap mechanisms, correlation functions, or non-statistical boundary scattering; I found no open result for the requested arbitrary-boundary neutral-observable influence. This is a bounded search result, not a proof that no such theorem exists.

There is a general sufficient uniqueness route for compact continuous spins, but it is conditional. Conache–Kondratiev–Kozitsky–Pasurek (2015) treat Gibbs fields on bounded-degree graphs with general standard-Borel single-site spaces (hence the compact (S^3) spin space is allowed). Under their one-site total-variation estimate with influence matrix (\kappa) satisfying \(\sup_x\sum_y\kappa_{xy}<1\), their Theorem 2.6 gives at most one Gibbs state; their Theorem 2.7 gives exponential decay of covariances for bounded local observables when their full hypotheses (including the moment/control conditions for (h,K,c)) hold [Dobrushin–Pechersky criterion, full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.00673). The usual Dobrushin comparison iteration under a summable finite-range influence matrix gives a volume- and boundary-uniform bound on the effect of changing exterior spins, with an exponential rate when the weighted influence sum is also strictly below one. That is a sufficient route for O(4) pins/fields if verified. I found no verification of this contraction or the paper’s hypotheses for the nearest-neighbor O(4) model at β in [2.3,2.6]; the general criterion therefore does not settle the target interval.

BFL gives two-dimensional plane-rotator uniqueness under its zero-magnetization premise and at sufficiently large uniform field, but no uniform boundary-influence rate. MMP gives uniqueness from differentiability in the field, again for the plane rotator and without a boundary-distance estimate. These do not combine into FST-N for O(4).

## Recent extensions with pins, fields, or boundary conditions

### Ding–Huang–Yang (DHY)

Ding, Huang and Yang, “Exponential decay for the two-dimensional Heisenberg model,” arXiv v1 dated 3 Nov 2026, prove Theorem 1.1 for O(N), N\(\ge3\), and every β>0 [open full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04908). In a square box (B_L\subset\mathbb Z^2), for the unit-sphere spin model with fixed exterior spins ζ and nearest-neighbor interaction, if for each coordinate (i), ζ^i is constant over all boundary vertices, then the center one-point vector has norm at most (c_\beta^{-1}e^{-c_\beta L}). In the free case it gives central two-point exponential decay. Thus for O(4) it includes boundary spin vectors that are constant across the whole boundary (the vector can point in any single direction); it does not allow different pinned directions at different sites. The authors explicitly say they expect the one-point estimate for all boundary conditions but that it is not immediate, and that the OpenAI free-boundary theorem does not directly yield it. Their Theorem 5.3 is uniform over arbitrary XY *local-time* boundary conditions for an auxiliary loop model, but only bounds an annulus crossing event. It is not spin-observable boundary response. DHY therefore does not imply FST-N.

### Pei: recent pinned O(4) result, but odd observables only

Yan Ru Pei, “The massive phase of the two-dimensional Heisenberg model is stable under small anisotropy,” arXiv:2611.01532v2 (11 Nov 2026) [open full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01532), has a directly relevant Theorem 1.5. It assumes estimates (I1)–(I3) imported from a recent O(4) OpenAI paper: stiffness at a coarse scale, a deterministic profile construction, and a conditional bad-site bound. For each ε in (0,1), sufficiently small anisotropy width δ, (0<\beta\le(1-\varepsilon)|\log\delta|/(2\pi)), (n\ge4), finite (V\subset\mathbb Z^2), nearest-neighbor edge set, arbitrary pin subset (P) and arbitrary values σ-bar in ((S^{n-1})^P), and edge couplings (b_e\in[-\beta,\beta]), (m_e^k\in\mathbb R) with |m_e^k-b_e|\le\beta\delta), it proves

\[
|\mathbb E\sigma_x^i|\le A_*e^{-\mathrm{dist}_\infty(x,P)/\Xi(\beta)},\qquad
|\mathbb E\sigma_x^i\sigma_y^j|\le A_*e^{-\min(\|x-y\|_\infty,\mathrm{dist}_\infty(x,P))/\Xi(\beta)}
\]

for unpinned (x,y), with (\Xi(\beta)=\exp(\pi\beta+C_\Xi\sqrt{(1+\beta)\log(2+\beta)})). For n=4 the paper further transfers this to every odd one-spin observable for near-isotropic anisotropy matrices. This is a real arbitrary-direction pin result for charged/odd O(4) observables, under the stated imported assumptions and beta–delta threshold; it is not a neutral-observable theorem. The author explicitly says the theorem holds for every boundary condition “but only for odd observables,” and gives an exact two-site derivative showing that ((\sigma^1)^2-1/4), an even single-site function with zero S^3 average, has nonzero expectation under a small anisotropic perturbation. The paper further says a small odd single-site term such as h\sum_x\sigma_x^1, even with |h|\le\beta\delta, does not satisfy a needed lemma and the conclusion of its pinned theorem fails. Thus this result does not cover arbitrary small fields. The note says the recent input papers used for Theorem 1.5 had not been refereed as of this version. These limitations do not refute the isotropic FST-N claim. For the exact theorem and limitation see [Pei, Theorem 1.5 and Remark 7.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.01532).

This corrects the scope summary in the existing row-1092 note: Pei v2 does have an arbitrary-pin theorem, but only for odd O(4) observables and under (I1)–(I3)/small-anisotropy assumptions. It leaves FST-N’s neutral sector open. Pei’s free-boundary Theorem 1.1 is a separate result for S²-valued (O(3)) near-isotropic couplings, not an O(4) arbitrary-boundary result.

## Finding

The classical correlation inequalities located constrain fields to common coordinate directions or restricted cones, and the boundary-equivalence theorem requires uniformly finite energy difference without a distance rate. The newer O(4) result handles arbitrary pin values for odd observables, while its own two-site example blocks extending that perturbative statement to all mean-zero even observables. The available free-boundary O(4) exponential results either concern vector two-point functions or require an additional transfer-gap argument for neutral observables, and none located proves that gap uniformly over arbitrary pins or small arbitrary-direction fields. Therefore these sources do not establish FST-N for the two-dimensional O(4) model over β in [2.3, 2.6]. This is a literature finding only; it assigns no proof/audit status.

RESULT: No located source establishes uniform decay of the response of far rotation-invariant local O(4) observables to arbitrary-direction pins or small fields for β in [2.3, 2.6]; Pei v2 establishes a conditional arbitrary-pin estimate for odd observables, and the neutral-observable case remains uncovered by the sources read.
