## 2. Rothe (1995), “A Novel Look at the Michael Lattice Sum Rules”

**Read in full:** yes; see the source and exact statements below.

**Read in full:** Rothe's arXiv source `hep-lat/9504012v1`, 453 TeX lines (downloaded as `source.tar.gz`, decompressed to `source.tex` in the paper folder). No PDF was needed. **Main statements:** on an isotropic lattice the subtracted static quark-pair potential satisfies `∂β V̂ = lim_{T→∞} T^{-1}<Pτ+Ps>_{q q̄-0}` (eqs. (5a–b), pp. 15–16). The corrected action sum rule is `V̂ + R̂ ∂_{R̂}V̂ = (∂β/∂ln a)<Pτ′+Ps′>_{q q̄-0}` near the continuum limit (eq. (12), p. 18), hence for a linear confining potential its left side is `2 σ̂ R̂` (eq. (13), p. 18); the factor two omitted in Michael's original 1987 form is identified at the end of p. 18. For anisotropic regularization, the exact coupling-response identity is (19)–(22), pp. 20–21. The comparison with continuum field energy uses weak-coupling relations (16), (23)–(25), pp. 19–22; the apparent contradiction with the continuum energy sum rule leads the author to call that identification inadequate and to request further study, not to claim a settled theorem (pp. 22–24). The paper uses analytic lattice and renormalization-group manipulations; it is not a numerical proof, but its continuum interpretation is conditional/approximate at the weak-coupling stage.

**Crux:** this does give a coupling derivative of the static potential, but its exact derivative is an expectation of the action in the Wilson-loop state minus vacuum. It does not identify or bound the derivative of an isolated transfer-matrix excitation level, nor compare a level's action deficit with its energy uniformly in box size. It therefore does not prove (VIR_K), (LEV_K), or (VIR-log). It is a useful warning about action-sum-rule normalization, not a usable seed tool.

## 4. Rinaldi et al. (2012), arXiv:1212.0635

**Read in full:** yes; the full arXiv source and figures were read.

**Read in full:** Rinaldi, Lacagnina, Lucini, Patella and Rago, arXiv `1212.0635v1`, 516 TeX lines (full e-print source and figures downloaded in this paper's directory). **Statements:** Eq. (1), §1 defines the four-dimensional fundamental–adjoint SU(2) action. §2 says the Wilson axis `β_adj=0` has a crossover around `β_fund≈2.30`; turning on the adjoint coupling produces a bulk first-order line that strengthens with increasing `β_adj` (article pp. 2–3). From hot/cold hysteresis and the plaquette branch separation, the authors infer the separation tends to zero at about `β_adj≲1.25`; `β_adj=1.25` required `40^4`, with systematic and statistical uncertainties “not very good” (eq. (2), §2, p. 3). They explicitly do not precisely locate the endpoint, and describe its nature as controversial (§3, p. 7). These are Monte Carlo observations/extrapolations, not rigorous phase-transition results. The paper also studies spectrum values at `β_adj=1.00,1.05,1.10,1.16` on five finite volumes; some tensor masses could not be extrapolated to infinite volume (§3, pp. 4–7). No boundary-condition comparison theorem is given.

**Crux:** the first-order line ends near an adjoint coupling of about 1.25, in the authors' finite-size estimate. The fundamental Wilson axis is described as a crossover near βfund≈2.30, not as lying on that first-order line. This supports K7 as a warning that the same broad group and positivity features can coexist with a transition for a modified weight; it neither proves nor refutes (NOCRIT) on the Wilson axis. No usable gap estimate, two-volume bound, or proof about the Wilson-axis transition follows.

## 5. Kazakov and Zheng (2024), “Bootstrap for Finite N Lattice Yang-Mills Theory”

**Read in full:** yes; the complete article and appendices were read.

**Read in full:** Kazakov and Zheng, arXiv `2404.16925v1`, complete 1,074-line TeX source (article plus appendices). The exact algebra is based on Haar integration by parts, finite-N trace identities and positivity of moment matrices. For SU(2), `tr U tr V = tr(UV)+tr(UV†)` (Main results, eq. (2)); the lattice Makeenko–Migdal equations then close on single-trace loops (§2, eqs. (15)–(20)). The basic positivity consequence is `⟨O†O⟩≥0`, equivalently a positive-semidefinite matrix of Wilson-line inner products (§3, eqs. (22)–(24)); reflection-positivity matrices are also described (§3, eqs. (25)–(27)). These are exact necessary constraints for the specified measure/operator family and are hand-checkable at a small truncation.

The quantitative 3D/4D plaquette bounds instead come from truncated semidefinite optimization: loops are cut off at length 24; in 4D the Λ=3 calculation uses only reflection positivity and only half the Λ=3 Wilson lines; the article reports 343,851 variables, 152,149 loop equations, 40 PSD blocks, roughly 1 TB memory and about 20 days (§4, Table 1 and Fig. 4 discussion). The authors describe roughly 0.1% margins for the plaquette and qualitative string-tension estimates, but give no hand-checkable dual certificate or rounding/error proof. These numbers are numerical outputs, not proof evidence under our rule. In Discussion they state the method remains “confined to a local region of the infinite lattice”; they propose coarse graining as future work. No inequality compares two volumes or two boundary conditions, and no bound concerns an isolated transfer level.

**Crux:** the exact finite-N loop equations and PSD constraints are usable identities for a future hand certificate specific to the Wilson weight. The paper supplies no certificate for (BOOT_eps), no two-volume comparison, and no mass-gap tool. Its measured/computed plaquette margins do not close the seed. Credit Kazakov and Zheng for the loop-equation and bootstrap framework.

## 8. Lüscher, Narayanan, Weisz and Wolff (1992), arXiv hep-lat/9207009

**Read in full:** partial; main text §§1–8 and Appendix A were read, while Appendices B–D were not.

**Read in full for the requested claim:** Lüscher, Narayanan, Weisz and Wolff, arXiv `hep-lat/9207009v1`; the full main text §§1–8 and Appendix A were read. Appendices B–D were not read, so the full source including technical appendices was **not** read in full. The article studies pure SU(N) Schrödinger-functional couplings in finite spatial volume with periodic spatial boundary conditions; it explicitly excludes twisted periodic boundary conditions (§2.1). It proves a lattice stability theorem for a chosen Abelian background at finite lattice spacing (Theorem 1, §5.2): for boundary angles in the stated fundamental domain and `TL/a²>(N−1)π² max{1,N/16}`, that background minimizes the action, uniquely up to gauge transformations. It argues that the Schrödinger functional is finite after ordinary coupling renormalization (§2.5) and verifies this through one-loop perturbation theory (§3.5).

For the running coupling, §2.6 defines `ḡ²(L)` by differentiating the effective action in a background parameter and scaling all dimensionful quantities with `L`. For SU(2), §5.3 specifies `T=L`, `0<η<π`, and `L/a≥4`. Section 7 computes the one-loop coefficient at individual box sizes `L` and gives a perturbative expansion (eq. (7.43)): `ḡ²(L)=ḡ_MS²(L)+c₁ ḡ_MS⁴(L)+…`; the derivation uses determinants and a numerical extrapolation in lattice size (§7.5–7.6). The introduction describes recursive finite-size scaling from large to small `L` as the intended programme, but states numerical scaling results would appear elsewhere. This paper does **not** prove a finite-coupling inequality or exact step-scaling relation between `L` and `2L`; its one-loop asymptotic formula is perturbative and the finite-size table is a numerical calculation. **Crux:** no seed or mass-gap input. It supplies a definition and perturbative running-coupling normalization, not a two-volume bound.

## 9. Anderson and Kruczenski, “Loop Equations and Bootstrap Methods in the Lattice”

**Read in full:** yes; the complete article source was read.

**Read in full:** Anderson and Kruczenski, arXiv `1612.08140`, full TeX source `LoopEqSdp_07s.tex` (all 1,959 lines including preamble, article, appendix, and references) in the paper folder. The actual article begins at source line 450; its main results are §§2–4. The theory is Wilson lattice gauge theory, with the exact single-loop Makeenko–Migdal closure specifically in the large-N limit (§2, eqs. (11)–(23), pp. 3–6). For finite N, the paper gives the loop equation but does not claim single-loop closure because expectation values of products remain (§2.2).

**Exact positivity:** for any matrix A, `tr(AA†)≥0` (eq. (30)); choosing a linear combination of powers of a unitary link gives the Toeplitz moment matrix `ρ^(L)⪰0` (eqs. (31)–(34)). Its principal minors imply `|W_n|≤1` and the explicit increment estimate `(W_n−W_{n−1})²≤4(1−W_1)` (eqs. (36)–(39), §3.1). These are algebraically checkable necessary constraints. In four dimensions, the analogous open-path Gram matrix is positive semidefinite because it is an expectation of a color-traced `AA†` (§4.2, eqs. (63)–(67)); its consequences include inequalities and the flat-configuration implication when the plaquette loop equals one. These are exact finite-matrix statements under the stated Wilson-loop expectation/measure setup, independent of optimizing an SDP. The paper’s broader dynamical use combines them with loop equations.

**Numerical versus rigorous scope:** the two-dimensional finite truncation and four-dimensional bounds are solved by numerical optimization; the authors state that their 4D loop enumeration reaches length 18, with most calculations through length 14 (§4 opening, p. 19). They explicitly say the 4D bounds are not close in the studied truncations (§4.7, p. 27), and the conclusions describe the higher-dimensional method as a potential extension, with finite-loop numerical work (§5). The SDP formulation is explained in Appendix, but there is no exact dual certificate or numerical error analysis establishing a theorem about the infinite-volume gap. The model is an infinite cubic lattice in the loop-equation discussion; I found no estimate comparing two finite volumes or two boundary conditions, and no statement about an isolated transfer level.

**Crux:** the exact PSD constraints are a usable algebraic ingredient for a future hand-checkable Wilson-loop certificate; the paper provides no such certificate for (VIR_K), (LEV_K), (VIR-log), (NOCRIT), (BOOT_eps), MIX or K7. The numerical bootstrap results do not prove a volume-uniform mass gap. Credit Peter D. Anderson and Martin Kruczenski for the method and conclusions.
