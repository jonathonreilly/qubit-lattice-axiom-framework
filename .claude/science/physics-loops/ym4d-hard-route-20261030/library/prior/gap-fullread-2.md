## 1. Piazza–Shen: two-dimensional SU(N) LSI at all fixed couplings

**Source:** Jack Piazza and Hao Shen, “Log-Sobolev inequality for 2D SU(N) lattice gauge theory at all couplings,” arXiv:2610.35286v1, full 13-page PDF read, pp. 1–13. [arXiv abstract and source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35286); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35286).

**Exact theorem and constants (Theorem 1.1, p. 1, (1.1)–(1.2)).** For every integer `N>=2` and every real inverse coupling `beta`, there is `C_{N,beta}>0`, depending only on `N,beta`, such that on every periodic square lattice `Lambda_L=(Z/LZ)^2`, every smooth `F:SU(N)^{E_L^+}->R` satisfies

`Ent_mu(F^2) <= 2 C_{N,beta} sum_e integral |grad_e F|^2 dmu`.

Here `mu` has density proportional to `exp{N beta sum_p Re Tr(U_p)}` against product normalized Haar measure (p. 1, (1.1)). The constant is independent of side length / volume. The statement is for the full edge-variable law, not only gauge-invariant observables. No boundary-condition quantifier is needed because the theorem is periodic-torus; it does not assert a finite-box conditional LSI uniform in arbitrary exterior fields.

**Two-scale disintegration and dependence (pp. 2–10).** Normalize one-plaquette density as `w(U)=exp(N^2 beta chi(U))/z`, with `chi=N^{-1} Re Tr U` and `t=N^2 |beta|`; then `e^{-2t}<=w<=e^{2t}` (p. 2, (2.1)). Put `r=1-(1/2)e^{-2t}` and write `w=(1-r)+r v` with `v` a probability density. The convolution identity `w^{*k}=1+r^{k-1}(w-1)*v^{*(k-1)}` (p. 3, Lemma 2.1) gives decay of the density, gradient and Hessian to Haar: their sup norms are bounded by `M r^{k-1}` (pp. 3–4, Lemma 2.2), where `M` depends explicitly on `N,|beta|` through the displayed bounds. Thus `r` is independent of `N` except through `t`, tends to 1 as `|beta|` grows, and the smoothing is weaker at large coupling.

For an `m x m` block (`n=m^2`), an axial tree of plaquette centers is used to integrate its `n-1` internal tree edges. This turns the block weight into the `n`-fold convolution evaluated at its boundary holonomy, `w^{*n}(W_{partial B})` (pp. 4–5, (3.1)); the remaining edge law is the product over blocks of these boundary weights (p. 5, (3.2)), and the internal conditional laws are independent blockwise (pp. 5–6, (3.3)). The boundary log-density has Hessian bounded by a constant times `sqrt(n)(||grad log w^{*n}||+||Hess log w^{*n}||)` (p. 6, Lemma 4.1), and each edge is in at most two blocks. Choose `n` large enough, depending on `N,beta`, that `M r^{n-1}<=1/3` and the resulting marginal Hessian is at most `1/2` in norm (pp. 4, 7). Since `Ric_{SU(N)}=(N/2)g` in their Hilbert–Schmidt metric, Bakry–Émery gives marginal LSI constant 4 (p. 7, (4.3)).

Conditioned on the boundary edges, each block’s internal law is tilted product Haar; the product Haar LSI follows from the same positive Ricci curvature, and Holley–Stroock gives constant `K=e^{4tn}` (p. 8, (5.1)–(5.5)). Entropy chain rule plus the derivative bound for the conditional expectation (Lemma 6.1, pp. 8–10, (6.3)–(6.7)) yields Theorem 1.1. Therefore the final finite constant can be chosen from the explicit displayed ingredients as a finite function of `N,beta` via the chosen block area `n`, `K`, and the absolute constant in Lemma 6.1; the paper does not optimize or give a closed best constant. Small tori (`L<2m`) are handled directly by Holley–Stroock with finite volume (footnote 3, p. 5; p. 8). The theorem’s uniform-in-volume conclusion follows because `m` is fixed once `N,beta` are fixed.

**Where dimension enters.** The authors identify the essential 2D geometry (p. 2): inside each block, integrating internal tree edges collapses the product of plaquette weights to a convolution evaluated on the block boundary; conditioned on the block boundaries, the small-scale variables are independent across blocks. They state that in higher dimensions the conditional integrations naturally produce nonlocal larger Wilson loops, which are much harder to analyze. Thus the step that yields a boundary-only marginal and blockwise conditional product uses two dimensions essentially; this proof does not extend by simply changing a constant. The argument also uses positive Ricci curvature of SU(N), and the authors say it does not cover U(1)/SO(2) without new ideas (Remark 1.2, p. 2).

**Relevance to the four-dimensional target.** This is a genuine all-volume functional inequality for a compact non-Abelian lattice gauge measure and arbitrary fixed beta, but it is for two dimensions. In 4D, the stated tree integration leaves larger Wilson loops and removes the blockwise conditional independence used in the proof. To inform (PHYS-SEED), a new four-dimensional disintegration would need to control those induced interactions and prove a volume-uniform LSI or another estimate for the actual transfer law, then establish a quantitative bridge from that LSI to the transfer thermal sum `S`. Neither bridge is in this paper. Theorem 1.1 itself concerns the full configuration-space gradient Dirichlet form, not the one-direction transfer operator; no 4D Wilson SU(2), aspect-ratio-four transfer ratio, or order-one-coupling (PHYS-SEED) statement follows. The paper explicitly notes that a positive mass gap is essentially trivial for 2D Yang–Mills (p. 2), underscoring that its LSI result is not the missing 4D mechanism.

**Disposition:** conditional tool / dimensional closed door. Exact closed door: its theorem assumes `Lambda_L subset (Z/LZ)^2` and its proof’s boundary-holonomy convolution/block product (pp. 4–6) is the 2D step. It does not prove or refute the 4D statement.

## 3. Bauerschmidt–Bodineau–Dagallier: integrated Hessian criterion

**Source:** Roland Bauerschmidt, Thierry Bodineau, and Benoît Dagallier, “Stochastic dynamics and the Polchinski equation: an introduction,” arXiv:2307.07619 (published *Probability Surveys* 21 (2024), 200–290). Read Theorem 3.6 and all of §6 (printed pp. 46–59; PDF pp. 47–60), including its examples and qualifications. [arXiv abstract/source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07619); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07619).

**Theorem 3.6 (printed p. 18, §3.3, (3.31)–(3.34)).** For a measure `nu_0` of the Gaussian-perturbation form (3.10), with differentiable covariance decomposition `dot C_t`, assume the continuity condition (3.31): for bounded smooth `F` and `g`, `E_{nu_t}[g(P_{0,t}F)] -> g(E_{nu_0}F)` as `t -> infinity`. Suppose there are real `dot lambda_t` (possibly negative) such that, for all fields `phi` and `t>0`,

`dot C_t Hess V_t(phi) dot C_t - (1/2) ddot C_t >= dot lambda_t dot C_t`.

Set `lambda_t=integral_0^t dot lambda_s ds` and require `1/gamma=integral_0^infinity exp(-2 lambda_t) dt < infinity`. Then

`Ent_{nu_0}[F] <= (2/gamma) E_{nu_0}[(grad sqrt(F))^2_{dot C_0}]`.

The input `V_0` need not be convex. The criterion is an integrated lower bound along the *renormalised potentials* `V_t`, not a conclusion from a Hessian bound on the original action alone. `dot C_t` acts as an inverse metric and becomes coarser under the RG interpolation. The criterion itself is abstract; in the authors’ words, checking it in concrete models is often the specific problem (printed p. 45, §6 opening discussion of (6.1)).

**Section 6 applications and their exact regimes.**

- For massive continuum sine-Gordon in `d=2`, Theorem 6.1 (printed p. 48, (6.13)–(6.14)) proves an integrated Hessian bound uniformly in mesh `epsilon,t,phi` when `beta<6 pi`; the bound may depend on `beta,z,m,L`. Uniformity in torus side `L` additionally requires `m>=1/L` and a smallness condition `|z|<=delta_beta m^(2+beta/(4 pi))`. This is not all parameter values in the continuum construction, and no gauge field is involved.
- For continuum `phi^4_d`, `d=2,3`, Theorem 6.2 (printed pp. 48–50, (6.22)–(6.24)) uses the susceptibility `chi(g,r+1/t)` in `dot lambda_t=1/t-chi(g,r+1/t)/t^2`. Its integrated bound is uniform in mesh for fixed `L`; it is uniform in `L` in the single-phase region `r>r_c(g)`. The proof needs a covariance/susceptibility estimate for the renormalised fluctuation measure, not just formal RG evolution.
- For lattice `phi^4` (Example 6.3) the same covariance formula yields LSI if `r>r_c(g)`; a mean-field susceptibility estimate yields polynomial deterioration near criticality (Example 6.4, (6.29)–(6.35)). The transport-map applications in §6.3 (Examples 6.5–6.7) propagate the same established Hessian estimates; they do not remove their hypotheses.
- For Ising, Theorem 6.11 (printed p. 55, (6.66)) gives a spectral high-temperature condition `beta<1` after normalizing the coupling matrix spectrum to `[0,1]`. For ferromagnetic interactions, the Hessian condition is reduced to a uniform-field covariance bound; FKG, Perron–Frobenius, and the Ding–Song–Sun inequality identify the maximal susceptibility at zero field (printed pp. 56–58, (6.71)–(6.82)). Theorem 6.17 gives an LSI bound up to the critical point when the zero-field susceptibility integral is finite. These arguments depend on Ising order/correlation inequalities and do not cover Wilson plaquette variables. Section 6.5 only notes that the criterion can in principle apply to conservative dynamics, with a constrained-magnetization example; it supplies no gauge application.

**What a version for four-dimensional Wilson SU(2) would require.** It would have to define a suitable scale interpolation and renormalised potentials for the actual compact link law (or for a proved Euclidean representation of it), and prove the integrated Hessian lower bound uniformly in the field, cutoff and torus size at the target running coupling. In addition, the resulting configuration-space LSI would need a separate quantitative implication for the one-direction transfer thermal sum in (PHYS-SEED). Section 6 supplies examples only where these model-specific Hessian/covariance inputs are available. It gives no interpolation, curvature flow, or susceptibility estimate for 4D Wilson SU(2), and no transfer-trace bridge. A possible *conditional tool* is the abstract criterion if those inputs are independently proved; applying it to the target is the crux again.

**Disposition:** conditional tool. No use established for (PHYS-SEED) without a 4D compact-link integrated Hessian estimate and a transfer-gap implication. Closed-door scope: the actual theorem requires the Euclidean Gaussian covariance decomposition (3.10) and its pointwise-in-field flow inequality (3.32); Section 6’s examples use scalar Euclidean fields or discrete Ising spins with model-specific covariance/order structure, not Wilson SU(2) plaquette interactions.

## 4. Gosset–Mozgunov: local gap thresholds

**Source:** David Gosset and Evgeny Mozgunov, “Local gap threshold for frustration-free spin systems,” arXiv:1512.00088v1 (2015; published *J. Math. Phys.* 57, 091901 (2016)). Full 23-page preprint read, pp. 1–23, including the 1D and 2D proofs and autocorrelation lemmas. [arXiv abstract/source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.00088); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.00088).

**1D exact threshold (Theorem 3, printed p. 5; proof pp. 6–11).** Let `H_m^circ` be a translation-invariant nearest-neighbour periodic chain of `m` qudits, each interaction a projector, and assume frustration-freeness. Let `H_n` be the open chain of `n` qudits, with lowest nonzero gaps `epsilon_m^circ` and `epsilon_n`. For `n>2`, `m>2n`,

`epsilon_m^circ >= (5/6) ((n^2+n)/(n^2-4)) (epsilon_n - 6/[n(n+1)])`.

Thus a single open-chain local gap strictly above `6/[n(n+1)]` yields a positive gap uniform for all periodic sizes `m>2n`. This is an inverse-square local threshold. The assumptions include a common frustration-free zero-energy space for local projector terms, translation invariance, and nearest-neighbour range. The Hilbert space is a finite tensor power of a finite-dimensional local qudit space `C^d`; `d` is arbitrary but finite. The proof compares squared Hamiltonians to squares of weighted local subchain operators and uses translation eigenstates, positivity of separated projector products, and a monotone autocorrelation lemma. The bound is sharp in order: the ferromagnetic Heisenberg chain has `epsilon_n=1-cos(pi/n) ~ pi^2/(2n^2)`, so the threshold coefficient cannot be lowered below `pi^2/2` by this criterion (p. 6).

**2D exact threshold (Theorem 5, printed p. 12; proof pp. 13–18).** For a translation-invariant, frustration-free nearest-neighbour projector Hamiltonian on a square-lattice torus, the isotropic case obeys, for even `n>2` and `m>2(n+2)`,

`epsilon_m^T >= (3/4)(epsilon_n^P - 8/n^2)`.

Here `epsilon_n^P` is the gap on a specified `n x (n+2)` patch: start with an `n`-row, `n+2`-column grid and remove vertical edges in the first and last columns (two rough and two smooth boundaries). So `epsilon_n^P>8/n²` yields a size-uniform periodic-torus gap. In the anisotropic case the local input is `min(epsilon_n^P, epsilon_n^Q)`, for the patch and its rotated form. The paper expressly does not prove the criterion for arbitrary connected subgraphs or general lattices; the patch shape is chosen to cancel the two types of adjacent-edge terms on the square lattice. The proof uses weighted patch projectors, translation eigenvectors, and a 2D autocorrelation inequality.

**Continuous-variable / transfer-operator analogue.** The paper does not state a theorem for continuous compact spins or a positive transfer operator. Its proof is algebraic in bounded local projections and operator products, and its numerical constants do not depend on `d`; this makes an abstract extension to tensor products of infinite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces with bounded projectors and the same exact hypotheses plausible. That is an inference from the proof, not a theorem stated in the paper. Such an extension would still require a well-defined common local kernel, a positive local spectral gap above that kernel, finite interaction range and the same translation/patch structure. No step identifies a Wilson SU(2) Euclidean transfer operator with such a sum of local frustration-free projectors. The positive transfer operator of `gap-crux-restate.md` §1 is a different object, and a logarithm of a transfer operator is not shown to have this local projector form.

**Relevance to (PHYS-SEED).** The local criteria propagate a *Hamiltonian* gap from a finite open chain or a shaped square-lattice patch to larger periodic quantum-spin Hamiltonians. (PHYS-SEED) instead asks for a small non-vacuum trace sum of an operator acting along one direction of a 4D periodic Wilson lattice, with compact continuous link variables and order-one coupling. To use this result one would need (i) a proved local frustration-free projector Hamiltonian representing the relevant transfer problem, (ii) a local patch gap above its common kernel with the numerical thresholds above, and (iii) a proved relation from its periodic Hamiltonian gap to the thermal sum for the actual Wilson transfer operator. None is given. The threshold is useful as a conditional criterion in its own quantum-spin setting, but is **no use established** for Wilson SU(2) as stated.

**Disposition:** conditional tool for finite-range frustration-free projector Hamiltonians; no Wilson transfer analogue supplied. The 2D `8/n²` threshold is not a four-dimensional gauge estimate.

## 5. Lemm–Mozgunov: finite-size criteria with boundary gaps

**Source:** Marius Lemm and Evgeny Mozgunov, “Spectral gaps of frustration-free spin systems with boundary,” arXiv:1801.08915v2 (2019; published *J. Math. Phys.* 60, 051901 (2019)). Read the theorem statements and their proof sections for Theorems 2.5 and 3.10 (printed pp. 10–11, 18–19, 20–25, 43–45), plus the boundary/setup discussion (pp. 1–7, 9, 16–19). [arXiv abstract/source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08915); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.08915).

**1D boundary criterion (Theorem 2.5, printed p. 10, (2.5); proof §§4.2–4.6, printed pp. 20–25).** The model is a finite open chain of `m` sites with

`H_m = sum_{i=1}^{m-1} h_{i,i+1} + Pi_1 + Pi_m`,

where each bulk interaction and each endpoint term is a projection and `ker H_m` is nonempty (Assumption 2.1). Bulk projectors use a fixed translation-invariant two-site interaction. For `m>=8` and `4<=n<=m/2`,

`gamma_m >= [1/(28 sqrt(6n))] ( min{gamma_n^B, gamma_{n-1}^E} - 2 sqrt(6) n^(-3/2) )`.

`gamma_n^B` is the gap of the length-`n` bulk Hamiltonian with no boundary projectors. `gamma_{n-1}^E` is the minimum of 1 and the left/right endpoint gaps over every length `2<=n'<=n-1`. Thus the strict local threshold `min{gamma_n^B,gamma_{n-1}^E}>2 sqrt(6)n^(-3/2)` supplies a positive lower bound for every eligible global open-chain size. The theorem gives an optimal finite-size threshold `G(n)` smaller for finite n, with `G(n) < 2 sqrt(6)n^(-3/2)` and asymptotic ratio `G(n)/(2 sqrt(6)n^(-3/2))->1` (Remark 2.6(iii)). The prefactor in the displayed bound is itself size-dependent, so a fixed `n` local certificate yields a positive global lower bound independent of `m`, not a lower bound independent of the chosen seed size `n`.

The proof compares `H_m^2` with weighted squares of overlapping local subchain operators. Interior blocks use the bulk gap; blocks touching a boundary split into commuting left/right pieces and require the minimum edge gap at all shorter lengths. The weighted overlapping-block argument loses the `n^-3/2` threshold because the open-boundary Hamiltonian is not translation invariant; the authors explicitly contrast this with the `n^-2` periodic result of Gosset–Mozgunov (Remark 4.3, printed p. 21). The result extends to finite interaction range with changed constants (Remark 2.6(iv)); no dimension-independent continuum theorem is stated.

**2D finite-range boundary criterion (Theorem 3.10, printed p. 18, (3.12); definitions and proof §§3.3, 6.1–6.6, printed pp. 16–19, 37–45).** For `R`-range local projector interactions on a 2D lattice, define rhomboidal patches `R_{n_1,n_2}` (Def. 3.8, printed pp. 17–18) and include only interactions whose full labeled shape lies in the patch. Under the specified bulk finite-range interaction labeling and frustration-freeness for all rhomboidal boxes (Assumption 3.9), for even `n` with `2<=n<=min(m_1/2,m_2/2)`,

`gamma_{R_{m_1,m_2}} >= C_1 ( min_{l_1,l_2 in [n/2,n]∩Z} gamma_{R_{l_1,l_2}} - C_2 n^(-3/2) )`.

The constants `C_1=C_1(n,R,{P^S})` and `C_2=C_2(R,{P^S})` are explicit in the proof; in particular `C_1>0`, and the threshold coefficient is independent of the global side lengths. Therefore local gaps on every macroscopic patch in the indicated interval, uniformly above `C_2 n^-3/2`, give a positive global gap independent of `m_1,m_2`. For other reasonable global boundaries, the local set must include all patch intersections with that boundary; the authors say the same `Theta(n^-3/2)` threshold remains (printed p. 19). The shape of the local patches is a real proof input, not arbitrary: rhomboids solve a counting condition after a one-step coarse graining (Prop. 6.1 and proof of Thm. 3.10).

**Compact continuous variables / transfer analogue.** The results are for finite-dimensional local spin spaces and frustration-free sums of local projections. The paper notes that replacing projections by general nonnegative terms with positive spectrum bounded in `[a,b]` is only a normalization issue under frustration-freeness (printed p. 6); it also expects an algebraic extension to local fermion systems when disjoint even terms commute. It does not state an analogue for a positive integral transfer operator with compact continuous link variables. An abstract extension to `L^2(SU(2)` local spaces with bounded projections and the same frustration-free finite-range hypotheses appears plausible from the algebraic proof, but this is an inference and still would be a Hamiltonian theorem, not a transfer-operator theorem. For the Wilson transfer problem one would need an explicit local decomposition with a common kernel and a local spectral gap, a compatible patch family, and a proof relating its open/periodic Hamiltonian gaps to the non-vacuum transfer trace sum. No such representation or implication is supplied.

**Relevance and disposition.** This source makes boundary quantifiers especially important: the local input includes edge Hamiltonians and macroscopic boundary-intersected patches. The target (PHYS-SEED) is a periodic four-torus transfer trace ratio; it has no supplied frustration-free projector Hamiltonian or edge-gap data. No use for the Wilson SU(2) seed is established. The exact closed door is the theorem’s frustration-free local-projector Hamiltonian hypothesis and its boundary-sensitive local gaps; the desired transfer operator is not shown to meet them.

## 6. Aharonov–Arad–Landau–Vazirani: detectability

**Source:** Dorit Aharonov, Itai Arad, Zeph Landau, and Umesh Vazirani, “The Detectability Lemma and Quantum Gap Amplification,” arXiv:0811.3412v3 (2009). Read the local-projector/layer setup (§2), the finite-constraint overlap parameter (§3.3), the two-layer lemma and its proof (§4), and the general lemma statement (§5.2). [arXiv abstract/source](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3412); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0811.3412).

**Exact statement.** In the paper’s setup, `H=sum_{j=1}^M Q_j` is a `k`-local Hamiltonian on `n` qubits, each `Q_j` a projector, with each projector intersecting only a bounded number of others. The constraints can therefore be partitioned into a constant number `g` of nonintersecting (commuting) layers (§2, printed p. 4). A finite family of allowed local constraints supplies a parameter `0<theta<1`: in each pyramid, every ordered product of its constraints or complements, sandwiched by the noncommuting-subspace projector, has norm at most `theta` (Def. 3.1, printed p. 6). These are substantial structure hypotheses, not consequences of positivity alone.

For two layers and `ell=0`, Lemma 4.1 (printed p. 11, (34)–(36)) assumes ground energy `epsilon_0>0` and gives, for every normalized `psi`,

`max{||(I-Pi_red)psi||^2, ||(I-Pi_blue)psi||^2} >= Delta_2(0)/8`,

where `Pi_red, Pi_blue` project onto the simultaneous zero-violation spaces of their layers and

`Delta_2(0)=1-1/(1+(epsilon_0/f(k))*((1-theta^2)^3/theta^2))`.

Equivalently, the product of the two layer ground-space projections contracts every normalized state: `||Pi_red Pi_blue psi||^2 <= 1-Delta_2(0)` (Lemma 4.2). The general detectability statement (Lemma 5.1, printed p. 17, (62)) considers `g` layers and a state with nonzero ground energy `epsilon_0`; there are functions `r(theta,k,g), f(k,g)>1`, independent of system size, such that if `0<=ell<(1/r)(epsilon_0/f - 1/(1-theta))`, then at least one layer has probability at least `Delta^2(ell)/(2g)^2` of more than `ell` violated constraints. Here `Delta` depends on `ell,epsilon_0,theta,k,g` as specified in (63). This is a detectability / constraint-violation statement for a nonzero ground-energy instance. The paper distinguishes this “gap amplification” from amplification of a spectral gap (intro, printed p. 3).

**Volume and scope.** The constants depend on locality `k`, layer count `g`, and the finite constraint family through `theta`; the lemma does not require a small coupling. With bounded participation and fixed local constraint family, these parameters can be uniform in the number of qubits. But the statement assumes local projector constraints and a nonzero *ground energy*. It does not provide a local spectral-gap lower bound for a frustration-free Hamiltonian, nor a bound on a transfer-operator thermal trace. The zero-ground-energy setting of the Wilson Gibbs law would not satisfy the lemma’s `epsilon_0>0` premise as a Hamiltonian constraint problem without an additional construction, and even then the conclusion would concern layer violations, not (PHYS-SEED).

**Continuous compact variables and the crux.** The source is formulated for finite-dimensional qubit spaces and a finite family of projector constraints. An abstract operator version on Hilbert tensor factors might be possible if bounded local projections, bounded overlap degree, a uniform finite family/`theta`, and all pyramid decompositions are available; this would be an extension, not a statement in the paper. A compact continuous variable with a smooth Wilson plaquette weight is not itself a finite-family projector constraint. ## 7. Nachtergaele: martingale/projector criterion for GVBS chains

**Open copy and source scope:** Bruno Nachtergaele, “The spectral gap for some spin chains with discrete symmetry breaking,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 175 (1996), 565–606. Read the author’s open arXiv preprint cond-mat/9410110 (1994), including Theorems 2 and 3 and §6’s proof and explicit bound (printed pp. 7–11, 30–33). The final 1996 journal copy was not separately compared; wording below is from the open preprint. [arXiv record/source](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9410110); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9410110).

**Exact GVBS theorem (Theorem 2, printed p. 7).** Let `h` be a finite-range interaction with an associated GVBS state `omega`, and assume its infinite-volume ground-state face equals the face `F_omega` of that state, `F_h=F_omega`, and that for every sufficiently long interval `[M,N]` the finite-volume zero-energy space is exactly the local support space `ker H_[M,N]=G_[M,N]`. Then a constant `gamma>0` exists such that the second eigenvalue of `H_[M,N]` is at least `gamma` for every sufficiently long interval, and `gamma` also lower bounds the gap in every pure ground state. The theorem itself is stated for the special 1D GVBS chain setting; finite-range locality is assumed. For a finite mixture of pure GVBS states, the component count is finite. The exact local-kernel equality is a substantive condition; the authors give an example where the ground-state face equality alone holds but the finite-volume kernels do not stabilize and no gap follows (Theorem 2 discussion, pp. 7–8; §7, Example 3).

**General criterion and constants (Theorem 3, printed pp. 10–11; Proposition proving Theorem 2, printed p. 33).** The general projector/martingale strategy requires: (C1) an energy-comparison bound `0 <= sum_{n=l}^N H_{Lambda_n\Lambda_{n-l}} <= d_l H_{Lambda_N}`; (C2) a nontrivial common local zero space and a local gap `H_{Lambda_n\Lambda_{n-l}} >= gamma_l (I-G)`; and (C3) an overlap/mixing bound `||G_{Lambda_{n+1}\Lambda_{n-l} E_n|| <= epsilon_l < 1/sqrt(l+1)`. Then any state orthogonal to the global ground space has energy at least

`(gamma_{l+1}/d_{l+1}) (1 - epsilon_l sqrt(l+1))^2`.

The stronger version (C1′, C2, C3′), with a block-overlap bound `eta_l<1/sqrt(2)`, gives `gamma_{2l_0}/d * (1-sqrt(2) eta_{l_0})^2`. In the GVBS application with finite pure components `omega_alpha`, the preprint gives (6.10)–(6.11):

`gamma >= (gamma_{2m}/2)(1-sqrt(2) eta_m)^2`,

`eta_m <= 4(n-1) epsilon_m/[1-2(n-1)epsilon_m] + sum_alpha A_m^alpha`,

where `epsilon_m=max_{alpha!=beta} O(G^alpha_[1,m],G^beta_[1,m])` is the maximum overlap of distinct component support spaces, and `A_m^alpha` measures failure of the local support projections of component `alpha` to commute across an overlap. The component estimate (6.1) is `A_m^alpha <= C lambda^m (1+C lambda^m)/(1-C lambda^m)`, with `C` bounded by the square of the auxiliary-space dimension and `lambda` chosen above the nontrivial transfer eigenvalues and below 1. For distinct pure states, the component overlap also decays with interval length. Thus fixed local gap plus sufficiently small overlap/commutation defects yields a volume-independent gap. The constants depend on the local interaction, finite component set, auxiliary dimensions, transfer spectral data, and the selected block length; no four-dimensional Wilson coupling estimate is given.

**Relevance to a compact-variable transfer problem.** This is a serious conditional mixing-to-gap mechanism for one-dimensional quantum spin chains with a frustration-free common ground space. It does not begin with a positive integral transfer operator and does not state a theorem for compact continuous local variables. To use it for the Wilson system one would need to build a one-dimensional quantum Hamiltonian with the exact local ground-space/kernel property, prove block overlap and commutation bounds uniform in the three transverse directions and cutoff, and relate its gap to the thermal sum of transfer eigenvalue ratios. No such representation or bridge is in the source. The result can guide the form of a possible transfer criterion (control of local support overlap), but it does not supply (PHYS-SEED); for this target its status is **no use established**. The exact closed door is the GVBS/local-kernel and overlap hypotheses of Theorem 2/conditions C1–C3′.
