## 1. Two-dimensional gauge theory with matter

**Exact pure-gauge control.** Two-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory with compact SU(2) has an exactly solvable character expansion. With action `beta sum_p(1 - Tr(U_p)/2)` and free boundary on a simply connected planar lattice, the exact normalized fundamental Wilson loop around a loop enclosing A plaquettes is `u(beta)^A`, where `u(beta)=I_2(beta)/I_1(beta)` and I_j are modified Bessel functions. For every finite beta>0, 0<u(beta)<1; the lattice string tension is `-log u(beta)>0`, and it tends to zero as beta→infinity. This gives area-law confinement of external fundamental charges in pure gauge theory. This is a statement about pure gauge theory and external probes. It does not prove a gap for local gauge-Higgs observables or for dynamical fundamental matter. A dynamical fundamental field can screen the external charge, so asymptotic string tension is not a confinement criterion in the matter model.

**SU(2) fixed-length gauge-Higgs, numerical only.** Gongyo, “Phase structure and the gluon propagator of SU(2) gauge-Higgs model in two dimensions” ([arXiv:1402.7124](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.7124)), studies the action
`S = beta sum_P(1 - Tr(U_P)/2) - (gamma/2) sum_(x,mu) Tr(phi*(x) U_mu(x) phi(x+mu))`, with `U_mu, phi in SU(2)` and fixed-length Higgs field. This is fundamental-representation matter written as an SU(2) matrix, not a theorem about every fundamental-Higgs potential. The paper reports Monte Carlo, not a proof or all-coupling theorem. At beta=120, gamma=2 shows a linear static potential followed by string breaking near r/a≈20; gamma=8 has a Yukawa-like potential and almost-zero fitted string tension. The potential fits use `-A exp(-m r)+sigma r+B`, over r/a<20. Reported sigma is 0.01221(5) at (beta,gamma)=(120,2), 7(9)×10^-5 at (120,8), and 3(4)×10^-5 at (200,8). The fitted gauge-invariant W mass has a minimum near gamma≈5 and remains visibly nonzero at the finite volumes studied (256²–512²); this is a numerical observation, not a lower bound. It is a charged-vector screening observable; the paper supplies no monotonicity theorem for the neutral spectrum as beta changes. The authors interpret the gamma=8 potential as massive-W exchange and the observed behavior as crossover or possible first-order behavior. No uniform exponential-clustering constant, coupling-uniform gap, or theorem at every beta and gamma is established.

Gongyo's shorter conference report ([PoS LATTICE2014, 362](https://pos.sissa.it/214/362/pdf)) gives the same frozen-length SU(2) action and numerically compares beta=7.99 and 120 on 256². It says the plaquette changes smoothly with gamma in the displayed range. It is not an independent theorem or a general analyticity proof. Its spectrum and potential evidence remains numerical.

**SU(2) versus O(4), and what is proved.** A unit quaternion is S³, with SU(2) acting by left multiplication. Accordingly, the fixed-length Higgs field above has four real components/S³, but its local gauge symmetry is SU(2); it is not the ungauged O(4) ferromagnet. The cited simulations use SU(2), not an O(4) proof. I found no open source proving a box-uniform mass gap for the two-dimensional SU(2) gauge-Higgs or gauged principal-chiral/O(4) lattice model for all couplings. I also found no theorem that the gauge-invariant neutral mass increases or decreases monotonically with the gauge coupling. Numerical spectra in the cited paper do not answer that derivative question.

**General gauge-Higgs analyticity is not the requested gap.** Fradkin–Shenker analyticity results concern continuity between confinement-like and Higgs-like regions for specified lattice gauge-Higgs actions; analyticity of local thermodynamic quantities does not imply a mass gap, screening rate, or monotone neutral spectrum. The 2D SU(2) study above is numerical and should not be upgraded to that theorem. No exact theorem with the requested all-coupling correlation constants was located for gauged principal chiral or O(4) models.

## 2. Coupled O(N), principal-chiral, and O(4) copies

A new standalone result gives the nearest rigorous comparison: OpenAI, “Exponential decay in two-dimensional classical O(n) models” (23 September 2026; [paper/PDF and source repository](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/Exponential-decay-in-two-dimensional-classical-On-models-September-23-2026)). Theorem 1.1 states: for every integer n≥3 and finite beta>0, there are finite A(n,beta) and positive m(n,beta), such that every finite nearest-neighbor square-lattice subgraph G, every nonnegative edge-strength array b with each b_e≤beta, and all vertices x,y satisfy `0 ≤ <sigma_x·sigma_y> ≤ A exp(-m ||x-y||_2)`. The free-boundary thermodynamic limits inherit this two-point bound. This is a theorem for one classical O(n) spin field, including O(4), at each finite beta; it gives no explicit numerical values for A or m and no uniformity as beta→infinity. The source has a Lean formalization, but this reading did not inspect or use that formalization. The theorem says nothing directly about gauge fields, several interacting copies, arbitrary invariant on-site interactions, torus boundary conditions, or all local observables in the target P4 model.

For the SU(N) principal chiral model, Rossi and Vicari’s lattice study ([Phys. Rev. D 49 (1994) 1621](https://osiris.df.unipi.it/~rossi/PhysRevD.49.1621.pdf)) gives strong/weak-coupling expansions and simulations, especially N=6, and compares mass ratios with the integrable continuum prediction `M_r/M = sin(r pi/N)/sin(pi/N)`. It does not prove a coupled-copy gap or a theorem on a product interaction. The continuum integrable PCM prediction is not a proof for four coupled lattice O(4) sheets.

For the proposed four-sheet interaction `kappa/2 tr(Phi_1 Phi_2 Phi_3^{-1} Phi_4^{-1})`, I found no exact theorem or numerical study giving a gap, massless mode count, or coupling interval. The single-copy O(n) result cannot be applied by conditioning or relabeling: the interaction changes the joint law and its symmetry sectors. In particular, it is not evidence for the target P4(beta_M,kappa) at kappa in (0,beta_M]. No SU(2)-specific proof is available for this four-copy interaction.

## 3. Compact U(1), XY, and massless comparison

For one two-dimensional XY model, the Berezinskii–Kosterlitz–Thouless result supplies a genuine massless/quasi-long-range regime: low-temperature spin correlations decay algebraically rather than exponentially. The standard nearest-neighbor XY model has no spontaneous magnetization at positive temperature, which does not imply exponential clustering. Fröhlich and Spencer’s “The Kosterlitz–Thouless transition in two-dimensional Abelian spin systems and the Coulomb gas” ([CMP 81 (1981), 527–602](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01208273)) is the rigorous source for the 2D Abelian spin-system transition. Their later “Massless phases and symmetry restoration in Abelian gauge theories and spin systems” ([CMP 83 (1982), 411–454](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01213610); [open IHES copy](https://archives.ihes.fr/document/P_81_40.pdf)) proves a massless phase for 4D compact U(1) gauge theory and results for 3D/4D XY and Abelian-Higgs systems; it is not a theorem for 2D compact U(1) gauge-Higgs. I did not infer a 2D gauge-theory massless phase from its higher-dimensional result.

Bighin, Defenu, Nándori, Salasnich and Trombettoni, “BKT-paired phase in coupled XY models” ([arXiv:1907.06253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06253)), study two 2D XY systems with linear interlayer tunneling. They report numerical simulations and an RG phase diagram: when uncoupled, each layer has algebraic and exponential regimes; with coupling, a paired BKT phase appears with exponentially decaying one-body correlators but power-law two-body correlations. They report the paired phase for every finite interlayer coupling in their simulated setup. This is not a rigorous all-coupling theorem and not the four-copy quartic invariant in P4. It illustrates that some charged/one-copy correlations can be short-range while a neutral/composite mode stays massless.

For gauged compact U(1) with matter, the exact phase depends on dimension, charge, radial potential, and topological angle. The 2D U(1) gauge-Higgs literature I located includes numerical and continuum-critical-endpoint work, not an all-coupling screening theorem for the frozen-length gauged XY model. In 2D, do not transfer the pure XY BKT conclusion to a gauge-invariant local observable without checking the gauge action and matter coupling. No SU(2) analogue is supplied by these U(1) results.

## 4. Bardeen–Pearson–Rabinovici and transverse lattice

Bardeen and Pearson’s transverse-lattice construction, followed by Bardeen, Pearson and Rabinovici’s “Hadron Masses in Quantum Chromodynamics on the Transverse Lattice” ([FERMILAB-Pub-78/60-THY, open PDF](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/1978/pub/Pub-78-060-T.pdf); published as Phys. Rev. D 21 (1980) 1037), keeps two longitudinal coordinates continuous and discretizes the two transverse directions. It is a Hamiltonian/approximation program for QCD spectra, not a theorem about a two-dimensional SU(2) gauge-Higgs Gibbs measure. The longitudinal dynamics of a transverse link is described through a nonlinear chiral/sigma-model sector with gauge interactions; transverse motion is treated through link interactions and approximations.

Bardeen, Lee and Shrock, “Phase transition in a nonlinear O(N) sigma model with gauge interactions” ([FERMILAB-Pub-76/38-THY](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/1976/pub/Pub-76-038-T.pdf)), is the closest explicit coupling-sign calculation in that family. It is a leading-large-N analysis of a two-dimensional O(N) sigma model with O(N) gauge interactions, not finite-N SU(2) or a rigorous lattice theorem. In its notation the symmetric phase can exist for all couplings; the broken phase exists only when the sigma coupling h is sufficiently small. Their phase diagram says that decreasing the gauge coupling shrinks the allowed broken-phase region; zero gauge coupling leaves only the symmetric phase. Thus stronger gauge interaction can stabilize the broken branch in that model. The paper does not establish a universal sign for a gauge-invariant neutral mass derivative. Its scalar/vector masses are calculated in a controlled large-N approximation and exhibit phase-branch changes; the sign cannot be carried over to P4 or GH2.

Later work remains model- and truncation-dependent. Chakrabarti, Harindranath and Vary ([arXiv:hep-ph/0309317](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0309317)) numerically study light-front transverse-lattice q-qbar spectra in a one-link approximation using DLCQ. They explicitly say the approximation is crude and they explore parameter dependence rather than make a precision fit. Their longitudinal dynamics are handled by DLCQ; varying helicity-flip couplings, link mass, and hopping changes low-lying levels and wavefunctions, with effects depending on the sector and formulation. No general monotonicity theorem for mass versus longitudinal coupling follows. Patel’s transverse-lattice meson model ([arXiv:hep-lat/0309063](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0309063)) obtains a solvable limit at large N and strong transverse gauge coupling; it explicitly notes that this limit is outside the physical weak-coupling scaling region. Neither source supports a sign claim for the target's neutral mass as beta_L changes.
