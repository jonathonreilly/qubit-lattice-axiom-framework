## Findings

### Stationary-measure averaged coupling: a close general precedent

**Jean-René Chazottes, Pierre Collet and Frank Redig (2008), “Coupling, concentration inequalities and stochastic dynamics,” Theorem 4.1 and §3.3.3.** For an interacting particle semigroup (S_t), their (D_t^\sigma(i,k)) is the conditional-distribution average of a coupled-process disagreement probability at site (k), where the two coupled starts differ at site (i) and the common exterior is sampled according to the stationary Gibbs law. They first prove

\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu}(S_t f)\leq \int\|D_t^\sigma\delta f\|_2^2\,\mu(d\sigma),
\]

then define \(\mathcal D_t^p(i,j)=(\mathbb E_\mu[(D_t^\sigma(i,j))^p])^{1/p}\) and Theorem 4.1 gives, for every (p\in\mathbb N),

\[
\|S_t f-\mathbb E_\mu(S_t f)\|_{L^{2p}(\mu)}\leq20p\,\|\mathcal D_t^{2p}\|_2\,\|\delta f\|_2.
\]

The authors explicitly explain that exceptional starting configurations with poor disagreement decay are integrated against \(\mu\). In §3.3.3, however, their heat-bath/Glauber example assumes the **uniform** Dobrushin row-sum condition \(\|C\|_\infty<1\); the non-uniform stationary-moment example is asymmetric exclusion, not Glauber. The paper also proves a Poincaré inequality from conditional-distribution couplings for one-dimensional Gibbs measures (Theorem 5.1), under a summability condition on coupling tails. **Closeness:** this is the strongest direct precedent for stationary-law averaged coupling/moment control in interacting dynamics. It supplies a semigroup/conditional-start disagreement matrix and norm-decay route, not AN1's products of continuous transport responses at selected jumps conditional on every finite block schedule. Its model class is discrete-spin dynamics / 1D Gibbs chains, with no non-Abelian gauge application. Sources: [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2152), §§3.3.3, 4–5, especially Eqs. (34)–(35), Theorems 3.3, 4.1, 5.1; [published article, J. Math. Phys. 49 (2008), 125214](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.2995833).

**G. Gielis and C. Maes (1995), “The Uniqueness Regime of Gibbs Fields with Unbounded Disorder.”** For a stationary one-dependent random field of local worst-case total-variation influences (q_x), they prove almost-sure quenched uniqueness and exponential decay of quenched correlations if \(\mathbb E q_x<(2d-1)^{-2}\); they give a sharper random-field Ising condition \(\mathbb E q_x<p_c(\mathbb Z^d)\). A bad site has \(q_x=1\). The argument is disagreement-percolation based, with the disorder's finite-range dependence explicitly controlled. **Closeness:** an annealed expectation of local influence controls quenched Gibbs specifications, but it averages an externally sampled, one-dependent *spatial disorder field*, not the dependent Wilson configuration and its temporal heat-bath response sequence. The one-dependent hypothesis and TV influence do not match the target's S³ transport fields. This is already summarized in `library/prior/gap-lit-dp.md`; reused here, not re-searched as a new result. Source: [Journal of Statistical Physics 81 (1995), 829–835](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02179259); [author-copy PDF](https://fys.kuleuven.be/english/staff/christ/files/pdf/jspuniqueness.pdf).

**G. Gielis, C. Maes and K. van de Velde (1999), “Randomly Interacting Particle Systems: The Uniqueness Regime.”** For discrete-time dynamics with spatial disorder, they prove a unique invariant Gibbs measure and exponential spatial correlation decay; for directed probabilistic cellular automata (PCA), Theorems 1–2 give almost-sure and disorder-averaged relaxation bounds. For continuous-time dynamics they establish the infinite-volume limit and uniform-in-time approximation by discrete-time updating. **Closeness:** a dynamic, random-environment precedent with averaged relaxation, but the disorder is spatial and the results are for PCA / spin-flip systems under their scarcity assumptions, not stationary Wilson heat-bath response products or a gauge field. Source: [Annales de l’I.H.P. Physique théorique 70 (1999), 445–472](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPA_1999__70_5_445_0.pdf) (journal record and PDF are linked from the [1999 article page](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPA_1999__70_5_445_0/)).

### Existing contraction criteria that remain worst-case or deterministic-law criteria

**R. L. Dobrushin and S. B. Shlosman (1987), “Completely Analytical Interactions: Constructive Description.”** The paper defines complete analyticity by any of twelve equivalent conditions on finite-volume partition functions, correlations or truncated correlations; each bound is required for every boundary condition and uniformly in volume. The constructive result reduces verification to a finite family of bounded-diameter volumes with specified constants. Its original setup is finite-range finite-spin interactions. **Closeness:** block/local finite-volume criterion and dynamics implications are relevant, but its defining tests are uniform over boundaries, not averaged over a stationary environment or along a stationary chain. No Yang–Mills heat-bath application is supplied. Source: [J. Stat. Phys. 46 (1987), 983–1014](https://www.cpt.univ-mrs.fr/~shlosman/papers/132.pdf), pp. 983–984 and §4.

**Nima Anari, Kuikui Liu and Shayan Oveis Gharan (2020/2021), spectral independence; Zongchen Chen, Kuikui Liu and Eric Vigoda (2021), entropy factorization.** Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan define spectral independence by bounded largest eigenvalue of the pairwise influence matrix for the measure and all conditional measures, and derive rapid Glauber mixing in their finite-spin/hard-core setting. Chen–Liu–Vigoda prove that, on a bounded-degree graph, a totally connected spin-system Gibbs law that is both (b)-marginally bounded and \(\eta\)-spectrally independent has modified log-Sobolev constant at least \(1/(C_1n)\), with \(C_1=(\Delta/b)^{O(\eta/b^2+1)}\), and corresponding \(O(C_1 n\log(n/\varepsilon))\) mixing. Their entropy factorization is derived from local spectral expansion / conditional influence control; it is not an average over a random environment. **Closeness:** broad static-to-dynamic tools for finite-alphabet Gibbs laws; they require uniform bounds over conditionings, bounded degree and marginal assumptions. They do not directly cover continuous SU(2) links modulo gauge or a stationary-chain average of block response products. Sources: [Anari–Liu–Oveis Gharan, arXiv:2001.00303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.00303); [Chen–Liu–Vigoda, SIAM J. Comput. 51 (2022), STOC version 2021](https://doi.org/10.1137/21M1443340), Definitions 1.10–1.11 and Theorem 1.12.

### Space-time contour / disagreement methods for persistent bad regions

**Jan M. Swart, Réka Szabó and Cristina Toninelli (2022; expanded companion 2025/2026), “Peierls Bounds from Toom Contours” and “Peierls Bounds from Random Toom Contours.”** In monotone cellular automata with random update rules assigned independently to space-time points, Theorem 24 of the latter paper gives the literal Peierls bound

\[
\mathbb P[\overline X^p(0)=0]\leq\sum_{T\in\mathcal T_0}\mathbb P[T\text{ is present}],
\]

where \(\mathcal T_0\) is the set of Toom contours rooted at the target point; their contour sum estimates the chance that a defect survives in the maximal trajectory. The 2022 paper develops the Peierls contour argument for deterministic and some intrinsically random monotone cellular automata; the 2025 paper introduces random contours to control the Peierls sum for further random-update cases. **Closeness:** a real space-time Peierls/contour treatment of persistent defects. Its bad events are defective local update rules/noise in an i.i.d. space-time field and stability relies on monotone eroder structure. It does not treat a reversible heat-bath chain with dependent stationary Gibbs updates, continuous gauge variables, or disorder drawn from the Wilson law. Sources: [2022 paper, arXiv:2202.10999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10999); [2025 paper, arXiv:2501.09660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09660), Theorem 24 and Theorems 10–11.

**Antonio Blanca and Reza Gheissari (2025), “On the Tractability of Sampling from the Potts Model at Low Temperatures via Random-Cluster Dynamics.”** They use a low-temperature disagreement-percolation framework with space-time recursions to prove fast mixing of random-cluster Glauber and Swendsen–Wang dynamics on graph families with a strongly supercritical independent-edge-percolation phase (including stretched-exponential-growth graphs and locally treelike graphs under their hypotheses). **Closeness:** explicitly dynamical, space-time disagreement percolation, with typical random-cluster configurations and mixing bounds. It is a finite discrete Potts/FK model and its random-cluster phase geometry; it does not provide a stationary Wilson-environment contraction criterion or a Peierls estimate for persistent high-field regions in gauge dynamics. Source: [Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 191 (2025), 1121–1168](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-024-01289-x), especially §1.4 and the low-temperature disagreement-percolation argument.

## Coverage and disposition

Known in the abstract: stationary-measure averaged moment control of non-uniform coupling for stochastic dynamics (Chazottes–Collet–Redig, 2008); annealed random-influence/disagreement-percolation criteria for quenched random Gibbs specifications (Gielis–Maes, 1995); and space-time Peierls/disagreement frameworks for random cellular automata and Potts/FK dynamics (Swart–Szabó–Toninelli, 2022/2025; Blanca–Gheissari, 2025). Spectral independence, entropy factorization and Dobrushin–Shlosman criteria are established contraction/mixing tools but require uniform finite-spin conditional-law controls.

Not found in this bounded search: an exact theorem for annealed products of block transport responses along the stationary Wilson SU(2) heat-bath chain; an averaged-environment Dobrushin–Shlosman/block criterion for continuous non-Abelian gauge variables at intermediate coupling; or a space-time Peierls estimate for persistent bad Wilson gauge blocks under that chain.
