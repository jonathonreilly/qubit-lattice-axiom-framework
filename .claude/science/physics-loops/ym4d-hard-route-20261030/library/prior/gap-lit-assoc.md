## Results, closest first

### A. A signed non-orientable surface contribution is already in the literature

**Drouffe and Zuber (1983), direct predecessor.** J.-M. Drouffe and J.-B.
Zuber, “Strong coupling and mean field methods in lattice gauge theories,”
*Physics Reports* 102 (1983), 1–119, DOI
[10.1016/0370-1573(83)90034-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-1573(83)90034-0),
open author-hosted PDF:
https://www.lpthe.jussieu.fr/~zuber/MesPapiers/dz_PR83.pdf . Read status: the
full review was opened in searchable PDF form; the strong-coupling character
expansion and topology passages (printed pp. 34–36, 48–49) were read.

Their exact result is stronger than a generic surface analogy: in the
strong-coupling character expansion of the lattice-gauge partition function,
closed plaquette diagrams can be regular surfaces or have singular lines.
For a non-orientable regular component, the link integral uses the twisted
contraction (their (3.33)); it contributes only for self-conjugate
representations and carries the representation-theoretic indicator in that
contraction. They explicitly identify the projective plane as the
non-orientable genus-one closed surface and report the first non-orientable
diagram at order 24 in their expansion, with projective-plane topology (Fig.
15). Their account therefore already identifies non-orientable projective
planes as actual character-expansion terms. The SU(2) fundamental is
pseudoreal, so its twisted contraction has the negative Frobenius–Schur sign;
this identifies the mechanism behind a negative term. The paper does not give
the row-737 embedded 92-plaquette surface or its coefficient.

Relation to the lane: **states the general sign mechanism and a projective
plane term; supplies prior credit** for the possibility of negative
non-orientable character-expansion terms. It is lattice gauge theory with
Wilson-type character expansion, including SU(2), in the strong-coupling
series, not an assertion that the whole resummed partition function is
negative. Its first projective-plane diagram is smaller in series order than
row 737’s explicit embedding, but the diagrams are not shown to be identical.
No implication for a positive summed class weight follows. The surface count
and coefficient in row 737 remain a separate concrete claim.

**Important qualification.** Drouffe–Zuber state positive one-plaquette
character coefficients for their Wilson action and separately derive
representation-dependent link contractions. Positive coefficients do not
make every closed diagram’s group contraction nonnegative. That distinction
is exactly the one needed here: a positive expansion coefficient is not a
positive-measure graphical expansion term by term.

**Witten (1991), the two-dimensional topological precursor.** Edward Witten,
“On quantum gauge theories in two dimensions,” *Communications in Mathematical
Physics* 141 (1991), 153–209, DOI
[10.1007/BF02100009](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02100009), open Project Euclid
PDF: https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-141/issue-1/On-quantum-gauge-theories-in-two-dimensions/cmp/1104248198.pdf .
Read status: full searchable PDF; §4.6 on unoriented surfaces and the RP²
calculation (printed pp. 198–201) read.

For flat-connection/moduli-volume calculations on non-orientable surfaces,
Witten reduces the projective-plane contribution to the SU(2) character
integral ∫ dU χ_j(U²). Its value is the Frobenius–Schur indicator: +1 for
real/integer-spin irreducibles, -1 for pseudoreal/half-integer-spin
irreducibles, and 0 for complex irreducibles. Thus signed RP² terms from
pseudoreal SU(2) representations were known in two-dimensional gauge theory.
This is a continuum/topological 2D result, not a 4D Wilson-lattice
strong-coupling coefficient or a construction of the 92-plaquette surface.
It supports the sign mechanism, not a direct four-dimensional application.

**Münster (1981), adjacent but not the sign result.** Gernot Münster,
“Strong coupling expansions for the mass gap in lattice gauge theories,”
*Nuclear Physics B* 190 (1981), 439–453, DOI
[10.1016/0550-3213(81)90570-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90570-8),
open ScienceDirect PDF link returned article metadata/abstract only. Read
status: abstract only. The abstract says strong-coupling mass-gap series are
derived in any spacetime dimension; the series for SU(2), SU(3), Z2 and Z3
are computed through order g^-16, and used for rough continuum mass estimates
under an assumed sudden crossover. It does not state a non-orientable surface
sign, an RP² term, or a 4D continuum proof. The broad coupling/dimension
scope is relevant background, but it is not evidence that the particular
negative term was independently identified there.

**Cao, Park and Sheffield (2025; preprint 2023).** Sky Cao, Minjae Park, and
Scott Sheffield, “Random surfaces and lattice Yang–Mills,” *Communications of
the American Mathematical Society* 5 (2025), 774–896, DOI
[10.1090/cams/59](https://doi.org/10.1090/cams/59), arXiv:[2307.06790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06790), open PDF at
https://inspirehep.net/files/39c08fe8eb2423adca2b50020669e656 . Read status:
full paper searchable PDF; abstract, the lattice-model definition, the
surface-sum theorem, and group sections were read.

Their theorem represents Wilson-loop expectations in lattice Yang–Mills as
sums over embedded maps with edge/plaquette faces. The abstract’s range is
compact Lie groups, all matrix dimensions N≥1, inverse temperature beta>0,
and lattice dimension d≥2; for SU(N), O(N), SO(N) and Sp(N/2), the embeddings
and weights differ. The terms carry Euler-characteristic factors and
Weingarten/link-integration weights. This is a rigorous all-beta Wilson-loop
surface-sum construction, not the standard plaquette character expansion of
the vacuum partition function. It neither asserts that each term is positive
nor identifies the specific RP² coefficient of row 737. For SU(2), it offers
surface-sum tools but does not resolve the 4D character-term sign or (EP).
The paper says its particular surface expansions are new to its authors’
knowledge; that does not erase Drouffe–Zuber’s earlier character-diagram
surface expansion because these are different expansions and observables.

**Chatterjee and Jafarov (2016), orientable-string expansion in another
group.** Sourav Chatterjee and Jafar Jafarov, “The 1/N expansion for SO(N)
lattice gauge theory at strong coupling,” arXiv:[1604.04777](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04777), open arXiv HTML. Read status: full HTML, the theorem and surface-expansion portions read. For SO(N) Wilson lattice gauge theory in arbitrary lattice dimension and sufficiently small coupling (the threshold depends on dimension and expansion order), they rigorously construct a 1/N expansion for Wilson-loop expectations as a string-trajectory sum: genus-zero surfaces give the first term and higher-genus surfaces give higher-order corrections. This is a non-Abelian surface expansion and a controlled strong-coupling result, but it is SO(N), not SU(2); its oriented genus expansion does not supply the pseudoreal SU(2) projective-plane sign or a four-dimensional order-one inequality. It is not the same object as row 737’s character expansion.

### B. Correlation inequalities: positives, counterexamples, and the remaining cone

**Ginibre (1970), general framework; already read locally.** Jean Ginibre,
“General formulation of Griffiths’ inequalities,” *Communications in
Mathematical Physics* 16 (1970), 310–328, DOI
[10.1007/BF01646537](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01646537). Open Springer
abstract: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01646537 . Read status:
abstract only (reuse `gap-crux-lit-2.md` §T7). It gives a general sufficient
framework for Griffiths inequalities, including Ising and plane-rotator
(O(2)) examples; the abstract says no noncommutative/quantum example
satisfying all of its hypotheses was then known. It is a framework, not a
claim that every non-Abelian model satisfies association. No gauge-group,
plaquette, or SU(2) conclusion follows.

**Sylvester (1980), sharp counterexample to the broad Ginibre tool.** Garrett
Sylvester, “The Ginibre inequality,” *Communications in Mathematical
Physics* 73 (1980), 105–114, DOI
[10.1007/BF01198120](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01198120), full-text page:
https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38328963_The_Ginibre_inequality .
Read status: part of full-text page (abstract and counterexample/conclusion
passages); proof details not reconstructed. For classical vector-spin
models on graphs, the Ginibre positive-definiteness inequality holds in
spin dimensions 1 and 2 (Ising and plane rotator) but Sylvester gives graph
counterexamples for every spin dimension ≥3; its large-dimension leading term
is nonnegative for any graph. Sylvester explicitly cautions that failure of
the Ginibre inequality does not itself imply failure of Griffiths
inequalities; it means this proof tool does not apply in general. O(4) is
covered by the broad failure statement for the Ginibre kernel, but not every
O(4) correlation inequality is thereby false. The SU(2) gauge model is not
an O(4) site-spin model: SU(2) links have local gauge symmetry and plaquette
interactions. It therefore neither proves nor refutes (EP).

**Orland (2002), a restricted positive result for some O(n) models.** Peter
Orland, “Griffiths Inequalities for some O(n) Classical Spin Models with
n≥3,” arXiv:[hep-lat/0205028](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0205028), open
arXiv HTML. Read status: full HTML around Theorems 1–2 and their hypotheses.
For a finite lattice spin system with a nonnegative even single-site measure,
Theorem 1 proves GKS I, ⟨s(x)·s(y)⟩≥0. Theorem 2 proves GKS II,
Cov(s(x)·s(y), s(u)·s(v))≥0, under the stronger condition that the
single-site Laplace transform is exp(Ξ(q·q)) with Ξ analytic near zero and
having nonnegative Taylor coefficients. It covers certain O(n)-invariant
models for arbitrary n, including examples described by the paper; the
condition is additional and is not automatically met by the fixed-length
Heisenberg/O(n) sigma measure. This is a genuine invariant-observable
positive result in a restricted O(n) class, not a general theorem for O(4)
spins or gauge fields. It supplies no SU(2) plaquette association.

**Lees (2022), component-observable O(N) Griffiths inequality.** Benjamin
Lees, “Griffiths inequalities for the O(N)-spin model,” arXiv:[2205.12928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12928), open arXiv HTML. Read status: abstract and full HTML introduction/model setup; the theorem display was not fully rendered in the extract, so I report the paper's stated result and do not reconstruct a more specific formula. It claims Griffiths inequalities for unit-sphere O(N) spins on arbitrary finite simple graphs, N≥2, with nonnegative edge couplings that may vary by spin direction, and free or aligned-plus boundary conditions; it also treats inhomogeneous external fields. Its random-path/switching-lemma method is described as treating component-spin correlations. This is a stated O(4) positive result, but not a proof of GKS2 for diagonal-O(N)-invariant dot-product observables: the 2022 paper by Marvian et al. below expressly distinguishes the known component inequalities from the still-open invariant-observable GKS2 case for N≥3. Gauge-invariant SU(2) Wilson plaquettes are not component spins and the gauge quotient/action is different, so it does not prove (EP).

**Messager, Miracle-Sole and Pfister (1978), O(2) only.** A. Messager,
S. Miracle-Sole, and C. Pfister, “Correlation inequalities and uniqueness of
the equilibrium state for the plane rotator ferromagnetic model,”
*Communications in Mathematical Physics* 58 (1978), 19–29, DOI
[10.1007/BF01624786](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01624786). Bibliographic and
secondary theorem descriptions were checked; article body was not read in
this pass. Their stated result derives new inequalities for the plane-rotator
ferromagnet and uses differentiability of free energy to obtain uniqueness
and rotation-invariance conclusions under the listed isotropic/anisotropic
cases. It reinforces the useful O(2) case but gives no O(4)/SU(2) extension.
No direct Wilson-gauge conclusion.

**Marvian et al. (2022), zero-coupling invariant-observable program.** “Non-
Abelian correlation inequalities and stable determinantal polynomials,”
arXiv:[2207.07603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07603), open arXiv HTML.
Read status: full HTML introduction and theorem context. The paper considers
invariant observables for O(N) and CP^(N−1) at zero coupling, and proves
GKS2/Ginibre-type inequalities for inverse half-integer powers of certain
stable determinantal polynomials (including Kirchhoff polynomials). It states
that for interacting O(N) invariant observables the GKS2 inequalities are
known for N=1,2, while N≥3 remains open; component-observable results are a
different direction. This is a real restricted non-Abelian inequality/tool,
but its zero-coupling asymptotic model and determinant hypotheses are not the
4D Wilson SU(2) plaquette measure. It neither settles nor directly supplies
(EP).

**De Angelis and de Falco (1977), unresolved bibliographic lead.** G. F. De
Angelis and D. de Falco, “Correlation inequalities for lattice gauge fields,”
*Lettere al Nuovo Cimento* 19 (1977), 536–538, DOI
[10.1007/BF02785102](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02785102). Springer metadata
only; abstract and paper text were not exposed. This was already recorded at
`gap-crux-lit-2.md` §T7 and is not re-read. The title alone cannot establish
which group, which observable cone, or any inequality. It remains a lead,
not evidence for SU(2) association.

**Known gauge inequalities are not association.** The SU(2)/Z2 Wilson-loop
comparison and twist/disorder inequalities in Tomboulis’s accessible 1980
report, and the Tomboulis–Yaffe/Kanazawa finite-volume twist inequality
already recorded in `gap-crux-lit-2.md`, relate Wilson loops or twisted
partition functions. They are useful gauge-specific implications, but they
do not assert nonnegative covariance for products of elementary SU(2)
plaquette traces. Do not relabel them FKG/Ginibre association.

**Finite-group sharpness is a model-specific success.** Duncan and
Schweinhart (arXiv:[2308.07534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.07534), already
read in `gap-crux-lit-cross.md` §C2) prove sharp area/perimeter transition for
Ising lattice gauge theory and a q-state Potts lattice-gauge result with the
stated slab-regularity condition for q>2. The mechanism is a plaquette
random-cluster representation, FKG/Holley monotonicity and percolation
sharpness; Forsström–Viklund (arXiv:[2502.19942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19942), prior abstract-only reading) give random currents and switching for Ising lattice gauge theory. These are exact positive results and useful templates for a dichotomy, but their gauge groups are finite/Abelian (ℤq), not non-Abelian SU(2). No quotient-preserving random-cluster or current representation with the needed SU(2) plaquette association was found. The q>2 condition remains conditional as reported in the prior note.

**Repository counterexamples do not close (EP).** Row 738’s two-loop sign
calculation refutes association for its broader cone even at small positive
coupling and in the centre-even state; its exterior-field example refutes
all-exterior elementary-plaquette association. Both remain strictly narrower
than a theorem about unconditioned periodic elementary sources on every
finite 4D torus. I found no published theorem in the sources read that
proves or contradicts (EP) itself. Status: **not found**, not a proof of
absence.

### C. Sharpness/dichotomy assessment

No source read gives a sharpness or dichotomy theorem for four-dimensional
Wilson SU(2), or a result for a non-Abelian gauge group that yields an
association-driven seed. The closest rigorous dichotomy is for finite-group
Potts/Ising gauge systems through random-cluster percolation; the O(N) spin
results give correlation monotonicity in their specified observables, but do
not provide the lattice-gauge order parameter, seed alternative, or a
Wilson-SU(2) sharpness theorem. The general Ising sharpness theorems of
Aizenman–Barsky/Fernández and Duminil-Copin–Tassion are important inputs to
the finite-group lineage already noted in `gap-crux-lit-cross.md`; they are
not independent non-Abelian results and are not repeated here.

## Result and limits

**Established by the sources read:** (i) Sylvester’s counterexample is to the
general Ginibre positive-definiteness tool for O(N) spin dimension N≥3; it
does not prove all O(4) Griffiths inequalities fail. Later restricted
O(N) Griffiths inequalities exist, including component-spin inequalities
for N≥2 and conditional invariant-observable inequalities. (ii) Drouffe and
Zuber already describe non-orientable/projective-plane diagrams in the
strong-coupling character expansion, and the pseudoreal SU(2) sign mechanism
is the Frobenius–Schur indicator; Witten’s 2D RP² calculation is a related
prior instance. (iii) finite-group gauge models have rigorous sharpness and
switching tools, but they do not transfer as stated to Wilson SU(2).

**Open:** periodic unconditioned elementary-plaquette association (EP), any
SU(2) FKG/Ginibre/Griffiths cone that closes under the required gauge
integrations, and any non-Abelian gauge sharpness dichotomy producing the
row-738 seed. The broad negative-term mechanism is prior art; the exact
92-plaquette embedded SU(2) surface and its displayed coefficient remain
specific to row 737 and were not independently counted here.

RESULT: Literature search finds no theorem establishing or refuting (EP) for
four-dimensional periodic Wilson SU(2). Sylvester rules out the general
Ginibre positivity method at O(4) spin dimension, while later O(N) results
prove narrower inequalities for specified observables. Drouffe–Zuber already
identify non-orientable/projective-plane character diagrams and their
representation-dependent sign mechanism, so row 737’s negative-term
principle is not new; the 92-plaquette embedded example is a separate
explicit construction. No non-Abelian SU(2) sharpness/dichotomy theorem was
found in the two forward-citation rounds.
