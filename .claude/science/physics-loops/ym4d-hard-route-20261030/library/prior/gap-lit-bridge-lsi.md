## Literature sources


### Brownian bridges, heat kernels, and path/loop spaces

- **Leonard Gross (1991), “Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on loop groups,” JFA 102, 268–313, DOI [10.1016/0022-1236(91)90123-M](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(91)90123-M).** The primary journal abstract says: for compact Lie (G), the based-loop Brownian-bridge measure on (Y=\{g:[0,1]\to G:g(0)=g(1)=e\}) satisfies an LSI for the finite-energy gradient, with an added potential quadratic in the associated Lie-algebra Brownian motion; it is inherited from the (G)-valued Brownian-motion inequality. **Scope:** a path measure on loops, not a finite lattice two-slice law; the potential term matters. No lattice coupling parameter. The journal full text returned HTTP 403, so the exact displayed inequality and its constant were not read. [Publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002212369190123M).
- **Bruce Driver and Terry Lohrenz (1996), “Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities for pinned loop groups,” JFA 140, 381–448.** Theorem 6.4 states that for connected compact-type (G) with an Ad-invariant inner product, the loop-group heat-kernel measure at every (T>0) obeys
  \[
  \int f^2\log f^2\,d\nu_T\le \frac{2}{C}(e^{CT}-1)\int\|\vec\nabla f\|^2\,d\nu_T,
  \]
  for bounded smooth cylinder (f) with bounded gradient; (C\) depends on the group metric. This measure is the heat-kernel law generated by the loop-group Laplace–Beltrami operator (Theorems 6.2, 6.4), with pinned loops. Their introduction says the LSI for pinned Wiener measure alone had an added potential and that whether it is needed remained open. **Closeness:** the group and loop geometry are close, but its Dirichlet form is on an infinite-dimensional loop group, not the gauge-invariant gradient on a pair of spatial slices. There is no lattice or β range. [Full primary paper](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~bdriver/DRIVER/Papers/Drivers_Papers/A17-Loop-Log-Sob.pdf), especially Theorems 1.2, 6.2 and 6.4.
- **Elton Hsu (1997), “Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on path spaces over Riemannian manifolds,” CMP 189, 9–16.** For a connected complete Riemannian manifold with ∥Ric∥≤c, the unit-time Wiener measure of Brownian motion starting at a fixed point obeys the path-space LSI with Bismut tangent gradient (D): \(\mathrm{Ent}(F^2)\le 2e^{3c}\,\mathbb E\|DF\|^2\) (Theorem 1.1; equivalent normalization in the paper's display). This is an unpinned-endpoint Brownian motion law, not a bridge. No compactness or coupling parameter is needed beyond the stated curvature bound. **Closeness:** a useful smooth path-space inequality, but not for a joint Wilson slice law or its gauge-invariant gradient. [Primary paper](https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~ehsu/Logarithmic%20Sobolev%20Inequality%20on%20Path%20Space%20Over%20Riemannian%20Manifolds.pdf).
- **Mireille Capitaine, Elton Hsu, and Michel Ledoux (1997), “Martingale representation and a simple proof of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on path spaces,” ECP 2, 71–81, DOI [10.1214/ECP.v2-986](https://doi.org/10.1214/ECP.v2-986).** For Brownian motion on a complete connected manifold with bounded Ricci curvature operator (K=\sup\|\mathrm{Ric}\|<\infty), their Clark–Ocone/Fang representation gives \(\mathrm{Ent}(F^2)\le2e^K\mathbb E\|DF\|^2\) for path functionals. Theorem 1 extends this to a diffusion with generator (\tfrac12\Delta+V) on a metric-compatible connection when torsion is totally antisymmetric and the displayed combined curvature/drift bound (K<\infty) holds. Again this is a path law, not an endpoint bridge or lattice marginal. **Closeness:** analytic technique only. [Full primary paper](https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~ehsu/Martingale%20Representation%20and%20Logarithmic%20Sobolev%20Inequality.pdf).
- **Aida/Fang/Malliavin search.** Capitaine–Hsu–Ledoux identify Aida–Elworthy, “Differential calculus on path and loop spaces I: Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities on path spaces” (1995), and Fang's 1994 result as earlier path-space inputs. The primary Aida–Elworthy note is only a two-page C. R. Acad. Sci. report; its theorem details were not accessible in a primary full text here. Fang's cited 1994 paper is a Poincare/spectral-gap result, not itself an LSI theorem. Malliavin calculus supplies the gradient and integration-by-parts framework in these works; I found no separate Malliavin theorem for the finite two-slice Wilson measure. These are marked **not fully read**, not silently imported.

### Gibbs marginals, bridge interpolation, and ground-state processes

- **No direct published theorem found** in the bounded search for a log-Sobolev inequality of the two-time marginal of a Gibbs process or a ground-state process that matches the finite two-slice Wilson vacuum law and the gauge-invariant gradient. Existing functional-inequality results for an invariant Gibbs measure concern that measure and its own Dirichlet form. A path-space or stationary-measure LSI does not by itself identify the LSI constant of a two-time marginal under a different gradient.
- **Bauerschmidt–Bodineau–Dagallier (2024), arXiv:2307.07619, Theorem 3.6 and §6** is the closest multiscale tool already read in `research/mass-gap/library/prior/gap-fullread-2.md` §3. Under a Gaussian covariance decomposition, continuity of the interpolation and an integrated Hessian lower bound along all renormalized potentials, it gives an LSI for the initial measure. Its applications are to sine-Gordon, \(\phi^4\), and Ising in their stated regimes; it has no Wilson-link, quotient, bridge-marginal, or transfer-operator theorem. No direct β range for gauge theory is established. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07619).
- **Ground-state transform.** The bounded primary-source search found treatments of the ground-state transformed *invariant measure* and semigroup, but no theorem for a two-time marginal matching this target. A ground-state transform changes the measure and generator; an LSI for the transformed invariant measure would still require an explicit map to (J) and to its gradient. I found no such map for lattice gauge theory. No ground-state-marginal theorem is imported.

More fundamentally, their result is for the full infinite-volume measure and all edge gradients, not the finite joint two-slice vacuum marginal. It does not turn a transfer-operator gap into an LSI. [Primary full paper](https://www.sfb1283.uni-bielefeld.de/preprints/sfb22100.pdf), Assumption 1.1 and Theorem 1.4.
- **Jack Piazza and Hao Shen (2026), arXiv:2610.35286, Theorem 1.1.** Reusing the full primary read recorded in `research/mass-gap/library/prior/gap-fullread-2.md` §1: for periodic square lattices and every (N\ge2), real β, and side (L\ge1), the 2D SU(N) Wilson edge law satisfies an LSI with constant (C_{N,\beta}) independent of (L). The proof uses two-scale disintegration and positive Ricci curvature. The authors say the essential two-dimensional step collapses each block to a boundary-holonomy convolution; in higher dimensions the corresponding integrations produce larger Wilson loops and lose the blockwise conditional product. **Closest group/coupling match:** arbitrary fixed β and compact non-Abelian gauge links. **Gap:** dimension two, full edge measure, not the 4D SU(2) two-slice vacuum marginal. [Primary arXiv page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35286); full theorem read recorded in `origin/main:research/mass-gap/library/prior/gap-fullread-2.md` §1.

### Poincare, spectral gaps, and transfer operators

- For a fixed probability measure and the same Dirichlet form, an LSI implies its Poincare inequality (by linearization). That one-way implication is standard and also explicit in the lattice Yang–Mills Theorem 1.4 above. A Poincare or transfer spectral gap alone is weaker in general; obtaining LSI needs extra regularity/tail/curvature or finite-state hypotheses. I found no primary result that converts a **Wilson lattice-gauge transfer-operator gap** into an LSI for its two-slice law, nor a gauge-specific converse with constants at intermediate coupling.
- The nearest earlier gauge result, Shen–Zhu–Zhu, proves LSI by a Bakry–Émery estimate for the stochastic-quantization Dirichlet form under strong coupling; its Poincare inequality follows from LSI. This is not an LSI deduced from a transfer gap. Piazza–Shen also prove LSI directly by 2D disintegration, not from a transfer gap.
- The 2026 Bhattacharjee–Bhattacharya manuscript gives LSI and a transfer gap in overlapping but nonidentical strong-coupling ranges; its transfer theorem uses reflection positivity and time-correlation decay. It does not derive LSI from the gap, and neither range includes target β_W. The AIRR Poincare/LSI proposals are conditional RG arguments, not a proved gap-to-LSI theorem.
- Generic continuum compact-spin, Gibbs, or ground-state semigroup equivalences do not settle this: the relevant state space, invariant law, gradient, and operator must coincide, and no source read supplies those identifications for (J).
