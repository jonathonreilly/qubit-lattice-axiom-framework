## Gap, thermal weight, and periodic vacuum-energy defects

Search completed 2026-11-11. Scope: ES-1 in FINISH row 749: whether a gap or small excited thermal weight on a periodic box controls `D(L)=E0(2L)-2^d E0(L)` or a Casimir contribution. ## Result first

There is an exact, model-specific thermal representation of the periodic Casimir energy for a free massive scalar on a one-dimensional periodic lattice (GUIDE dimension): the mirror-Hamiltonian identity below. It is not a general bound from an arbitrary small thermal excited weight to `D(L)`. For any finite-volume Hamiltonian with a trace-class Gibbs operator, the thermal excited weight controls the difference between `E0(L)` and the free energy at the *same* size exactly. To control `D(L)`, one must additionally control the corresponding finite-temperature free-energy defect. A gap alone does not supply that input; it gives a Boltzmann bound only when the excited-state count or density of states is also controlled.

For finite-range bounded local interactions, a simple seam comparison gives a direct size-defect bound by the operator norm of the seam change. That bound is geometric and interaction-dependent, not gap-derived. The literature reviewed here does not establish an ES-1 theorem for the four-dimensional Wilson SU(2) Hamiltonian/lattice theory. No general exponential finite-size vacuum-energy theorem with constants applicable to it was located.

## Exact thermal identity and what it does not say

This is an elementary spectral identity, derived here; it is not attributed to a cited paper and no computation is involved. Let `H_L` be a finite-volume Hamiltonian with a trace-class Gibbs operator, ground energy `E0(L)`, ground-space multiplicity `g_L`, inverse temperature `β>0`, and

`S_L(β) = Σ_{n:E_n(L)>E0(L)} exp[-β(E_n(L)-E0(L))]`.

Then `F_L(β) = -β⁻¹ log Tr(exp[-βH_L]) = E0(L) - β⁻¹ log(g_L+S_L(β))`. In the unique-ground-state case this becomes the familiar `log(1+S_L)` formula. Hence

`0 ≤ E0(L)-F_L(β) = β⁻¹log(g_L+S_L(β))`.

For `D_F(L,β)=F_{2L}(β)-2^dF_L(β)`, the exact comparison is

`|D(L)-D_F(L,β)| ≤ β⁻¹[log(g_{2L}+S_{2L}(β))+2^d log(g_L+S_L(β))]`.

In the unique-ground-state case, if both thermal weights are at most `ε`, this reduces to `β⁻¹(1+2^d)log(1+ε)`. With degeneracy, the ground multiplicities contribute the displayed `log(g_L+S_L)` terms even when the excited weights vanish. This is the sharp structural answer to the thermal-weight part of ES-1: the remainder is `D_F`; small excited weights do not by themselves bound it. A gap lower bound `Δ` yields only `S_L≤(N_L-g_L)e^{-βΔ}` when the number `N_L` of states is finite; in a volume-growing state space this needs an independent density-of-states estimate. For infinite-dimensional lattice gauge fields, trace-classness and a usable spectral counting bound also require separate justification.

There is also an elementary locality comparison. If the periodic `2L` Hamiltonian is cut into `2^d` periodic `L` cells and `V` denotes the difference between the large-box Hamiltonian and the sum of those cell Hamiltonians, the variational eigenvalue bound gives `|D(L)|≤||V||`. This holds without a gap. For finite-range bounded interactions, `||V||` is bounded by the sum of norms of the seam terms and so is typically a boundary-order estimate (**GUIDE**, with coefficient set by interaction range, strength, and the chosen cell decomposition). It is not an exponential Casimir estimate. Applying this comparison to a canonical Wilson Hamiltonian still requires explicitly checking the gauge constraint, operator-domain, and seam-decomposition details; I have not made that model-specific derivation here.
