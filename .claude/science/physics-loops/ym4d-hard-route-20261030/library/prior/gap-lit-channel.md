## Answer for row 771

For a finite periodic spatial box, exact lattice symmetries permit the transfer operator to be block-decomposed by spatial momentum, cubic-group irrep (with parity and charge conjugation where present), and center charge. If the transfer operator is positive and commutes with the orthogonal channel projections, each block is positive and its autocorrelation has nonnegative spectral weights. This is a structural consequence of transfer-matrix positivity and symmetry. It gives no lower bound on the bottom energy of an individual block, no ordering between blocks, and no comparison of the same channel at different spatial sizes. The task's channel sums reorganize traces; symmetry alone does not make their summands small. For twisted boxes, the same conclusion needs a positive transfer operator and symmetry actions in the specific twisted sector; the existence of a twist trace identity alone gives no channel energy estimate.

### Exact gap and reflection-positivity results

**Osterwalder–Seiler (1978), “Gauge field theories on a lattice,” Ann. Phys. 110, 440–471, DOI [10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8).** The paper proves physical positivity for lattice Yang–Mills Schwinger functions, giving a positive self-adjoint transfer matrix, and proves existence and analyticity of the infinite-volume limit at sufficiently strong coupling and Wilson's confinement bound. The strong-coupling cluster-expansion literature also gives exponential decay of gauge-invariant connected correlations and hence a positive transfer gap in that regime. A full-spectrum gap, when established, implies the same lower bound on every invariant channel, but this is not a separate irrep-, momentum-, or center-charge estimate. It is not an order-one-coupling result along the continuum trajectory. The classic article was not available here as a full text; its published abstract and the strong-coupling result as recorded in the existing lane literature reviews were checked. Applies to compact-group Wilson lattice gauge theory in its strong-coupling range, including SU(2); no channel-resolved finite-periodic-box statement was located.

**Positive transfer matrix for Wilson action.** The finite-volume construction gives a positive transfer operator (Osterwalder–Seiler; Lüscher, “Construction of a selfadjoint, strictly positive transfer matrix for Euclidean lattice gauge theories,” Comm. Math. Phys. 54 (1977) 283, DOI [10.1007/BF01614090](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090)). Together with commuting symmetry projections this yields positivity of each projected autocorrelation, not a positive *lower* energy bound in each block. This is the exact input relevant to the channel-by-channel reflection-positivity part of row 771. The Lüscher bibliographic record and the result as used in the existing transfer-matrix notes were checked; the paper itself was not newly read in full.

### Which glueball channel is lowest?

**Strong-coupling effective transfer matrix — Münster (1985), DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(85)90385-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(85)90385-2).** For four-dimensional pure lattice gauge theory with standard Wilson action, the paper constructs an effective transfer matrix in the leading strong-coupling eigenspace, whose eigenvalues match the corresponding full transfer-matrix eigenvalues order by order. It calculates through the eighth nontrivial order. The lowest particle states in this strong-coupling expansion are spin-zero and spin-two glueballs and are degenerate at leading order; at zero momentum these transform in cubic irreps. This is a controlled strong-coupling series calculation, not a channel-wise lower-bound theorem for all couplings or finite-volume energy ordering. The open DESY copy was read at the abstract, setup, symmetry classification, and leading-state discussion. For the A1/E/T2 labels, continuum spin subduces to cubic irreps; the spin-2 multiplet subduces as E plus T2, not one unique cubic irrep.

**Hamiltonian strong-coupling spectroscopy — Kimura (1984), Nucl. Phys. B 232, 127–146, DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(84)90118-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(84)90118-4).** Uses cubic-group irreps and ordinary Hamiltonian strong-coupling perturbation theory to approximate the SU(3) glueball spectrum. It explains the symmetry classification and constructs operators; it is not a convergence-backed lower bound on the lowest eigenvalue in each channel. The open DESY copy was read at the Hamiltonian, irrep decomposition, and scope. SU(3), and perturbative rather than a theorem for Wilson SU(2).

**Berg, Billoire and Vohwinkel (1986), “0++–2++ Glueball Mass Ratio in Non-Abelian Gauge Theories,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 57, 400, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.400](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.57.400).** High-statistics Monte Carlo on pure SU(2) and SU(3) reports 2++ as the lowest observed excitation, with the 0++/2++ ratio slightly above one. This differs from the familiar SU(3) continuum ordering and shows why a channel identification must specify lattice spacing, volume, operator basis, and extrapolation. It is simulation evidence (**GUIDE**), not a rigorous channel bound. APS abstract read.

**Morningstar and Peardon (1999), “The glueball spectrum from an anisotropic lattice study,” [arXiv:hep-lat/9901004](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9901004), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.60.034509](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.60.034509).** Constructs operators in cubic irreps and measures the low-lying pure SU(3) glueball spectrum; it identifies continuum spin assignments while accounting for finite-volume and discretization errors and separating single glueballs from two-glueball and torelon-pair contamination. It is a spectroscopy method and numerical spectrum, not a rigorous theorem selecting a lowest channel. Abstract and methods/scope read. SU(3), not SU(2), and numerical.

### Winding flux and torelons

**Center-charge classification.** On a periodic spatial torus, a spatially winding fundamental Wilson line (spatial Polyakov loop) is gauge invariant and transforms nontrivially under the corresponding global center symmetry. Its transfer-matrix correlator propagates a center-charged winding-flux state; center-neutral local glueball operators lie in a different sector. This identifies the symmetry quantum number of a torelon, but does not estimate its energy. The exact center-sector trace structure is in the lane's `gap-transfer.md`; the qualitative operator/state correspondence is also described in the spectroscopy sources below.

**Kavaki and Lewis (2025), “False vacuum decay in triamond lattice gauge theory,” Phys. Rev. D 112, 014502, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.112.014502](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.112.014502).** On a small, nonstandard triamond lattice with SU(2) Hamiltonian and periodic boundaries, identifies winding torelon states and center-charge quantum numbers; a twisted boundary condition permits a specified torelon configuration to decay. The work computes a finite toy spectrum, not an analytic lower bound for a cubic Wilson lattice, and does not establish an all-volume or order-one-coupling theorem. Full open APS PDF read at the model and torelon/twist discussion. SU(2), but wrong lattice/action geometry for row 771 and computational finite-spectrum evidence (**GUIDE**).

**Athenodorou et al. (2021), “The torelon spectrum and the world-sheet axion,” [arXiv:2112.11213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.11213).** Studies closed flux-tube excitations on periodic 3+1D SU(N) lattices for N=3,5,6 at multiple lattice spacings; reports the torelon spectrum and fits string/world-sheet descriptions. The energy is in a nontrivial N-ality/center-charge winding sector, with string tension inferred from the lowest torelon energy. This is numerical spectroscopy and effective-string analysis, not a rigorous lower bound; it does not cover SU(2). Abstract and scope read.

**Strong-coupling Polyakov-loop/area-law route.** A rectangular Wilson-loop area-law estimate in a convergent strong-coupling expansion is the nearest rigorous route to a winding-energy estimate: after exchanging Euclidean axes, a Polyakov-loop two-point function is a winding-flux transfer correlator. If a uniform bound of the form `C(L) exp(-sigma*L*r)` is established for all separations `r`, spectral positivity bounds the bottom of the spectral support seen by that winding-loop operator by `sigma*L`. This inference is elementary from a positive spectral sum, but it is only an operator-overlap bound unless the chosen winding operators are shown to detect the whole center-charged sector. The sources reviewed here do not state a direct theorem `E_torelon(L) >= c*L` for periodic or twisted 4D Wilson SU(2); no such theorem was found in the prior winding/flux reviews either. The all-coupling Tomboulis–Yaffe/Kanazawa inequalities concern twist free energies versus Wilson-loop expectations, with twist sensitivity as input; they do not supply this energy estimate. The existing `gap-lit-flux.md` and `gap-lit-wind.md` cover those results and are not repeated here.

### Box-size splittings

**Lüscher finite-size methods.** Finite-size corrections to isolated particle masses in a massive theory are exponentially small at large box size and depend on scattering data; Lüscher's finite-size formulae are the relevant shape of result. Their hypotheses include a mass gap and control of the infinite-volume spectrum/scattering amplitudes. They do not compare the row-771 vacuum-energy defect, and no channel-specific theorem relating the same `O_h`/momentum/center sector at sizes `L` and `2L` for order-one 4D Wilson SU(2) was identified. ## RESULT

**RESULT:** No separate rigorous channel-wise mass-gap bound or order-one-coupling channel splitting for 4D Wilson SU(2) was found. Strong-coupling cluster expansion gives a channel-uniform gap, which trivially restricts every channel only within that regime; reflection positivity gives channel positivity, not channel energy lower bounds. Periodic torelons have an exact nontrivial center-charge label, and strong-coupling area-law reasoning is the closest route to an energy growing with winding length, but no direct theorem for periodic or twisted 4D Wilson SU(2) torelon energies was identified. Published glueball/torelon channel orderings are perturbative guides or numerical spectroscopy. No result found closes or quantitatively reduces row 771's (ES) obligations.
