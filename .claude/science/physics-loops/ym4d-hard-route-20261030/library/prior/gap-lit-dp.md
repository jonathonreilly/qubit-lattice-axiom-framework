## Findings

### Disagreement percolation and typical/bad environments

**Van den Berg–Maes (1994), “Disagreement Percolation in the Study of Markov Fields,” Annals of Probability 22(2), 749–763.** Their Theorems 1–2 construct a coupling of two finite-volume Gibbs fields with distinct boundary conditions. The disagreement set is connected to the boundary; for each site its probability is bounded by a percolation probability built from the local conditional laws. Corollary 2 gives a uniqueness criterion from absence of an infinite disagreement path. The paper treats a general finite single-site state space and says essentially all of its argument extends to countable state spaces; it does not establish the corresponding theorem for continuous S^3 link variables. There is no universal coupling interval: the condition is that the model-specific dominating/disagreement percolation be subcritical. Its Ising examples compare this criterion with Dobrushin and show improved parameter regions in some cases. This is a proved general theorem, not an estimate for Yang–Mills. Source: [paper PDF](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1472/1472D.pdf), especially pp. 749–751 and Theorems 1–2 / Corollary 2.

**Van den Berg (1993), “A Uniqueness Condition for Gibbs Measures, with Application to the 2-Dimensional Ising Antiferromagnet,” CMP 152, 161–166.** A two-independent-realization disagreement-percolation criterion yields uniqueness when its disagreement process has no infinite cluster. For the square-lattice Ising antiferromagnet, the paper proves uniqueness when $\beta(4-|h|)<\frac12\log(p_c/(1-p_c))$, improving the computer-assisted Dobrushin–Kolafa–Shlosman range by using $p_c>1/2$. This is a finite-spin, model-specific theorem; it supplies neither a continuous-spin estimate nor a gauge-theory criterion. Source: [paper PDF](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/2298/2298D.pdf), abstract and theorem statement.

**Gielis–Maes (1995), “The Uniqueness Regime of Gibbs Fields with Unbounded Disorder,” J. Stat. Phys. 81, 829–835.** This is the closest precise precedent for “typical environment” and “bad sites.” For a stationary one-dependent random field $q_x$ of local worst-case total-variation influences (the maximum over pairs of neighboring boundary assignments of the one-site conditional-law variation), if $\mathbb E q_x<(2d-1)^{-2}$ then almost surely the quenched Gibbs specification has a unique Gibbs measure, and the disorder-averaged absolute truncated correlations decay exponentially in distance. They also give a sharper random-field Ising condition $\mathbb E q_x<p_c(\mathbb Z^d)$; a bad site is one where $q_x=1$, and their examples show a sufficiently sparse bad-site probability can suffice at sufficiently small temperature parameter. The explicit spin-glass/random-field/hard-core applications are finite-state; the argument is based on a percolation coupling and an independence assumption (stated one-dependence; finite-range dependence is discussed as an extension). No numerical or fixed universal coupling range is claimed: the input is a disorder-law bound on $q$. This is proved for quenched random specifications, not for links sampled from a Wilson Gibbs measure; it is not a theorem that an annealed set of bad links is subcritical. Source: [author PDF](https://fys.kuleuven.be/english/staff/christ/files/pdf/jspuniqueness.pdf), pp. 829–835, Theorem and Remarks 1–2.

**Georgii, Häggström and Maes (1999), “The Random Geometry of Equilibrium Phases,” survey, Section 7 and Section 9.** The general agreement-percolation theorem (Theorem 7.2) says that if its dominating independent site-percolation process has no infinite open cluster, then the Gibbs measure is unique; on regular graphs such as $\mathbb Z^d$, subcriticality also gives exponential mixing. The random-interaction section treats iid random bond/site fields, including diluted Ising/Potts models, and proves almost-sure uniqueness and mixing in stated subcritical regimes (e.g. Theorem 9.7’s explicit disorder expectation bound for the random Ising model). It analyzes Griffiths/intermediate regime phenomena for diluted ferromagnets, not a continuous non-Abelian gauge model. It is a review of proved results; no parameter interval carries over without matching its field law and comparison. Source: [survey PDF](https://www.mathematik.uni-muenchen.de/~georgii/papers/ghm.pdf), Theorem 7.2 and Section 9, especially Theorems 9.2 and 9.7.

**Dobrushin–Shlosman complete analyticity (1985, 1987).** The 1985 constructive uniqueness criterion and the 1987 “Completely Analytical Interactions: Constructive Description” formulate finite-volume conditional/correlation/analyticity conditions, uniform over boundary conditions, and show constructivity by reducing the verification to bounded-size volumes once constants are supplied. The 1987 paper’s stated setup simplifies to finite-range, finite-spin systems, and its construction is a finite-size criterion, not an environment-averaged theorem. There is no universal beta range: one must verify the model-specific block bounds. The continuous-spin Wasserstein/block analogies are method adaptations, not automatic consequences of the original finite-spin theorem. No intermediate-coupling SU(2) block influence bound was found in these sources. Source: [1987 paper](https://www.cpt.univ-mrs.fr/~shlosman/papers/132.pdf), abstract, §§1–2 and constructive criteria; 1985 citation is also given in that paper’s references.

### Continuous-spin and O(N)/S^3 models

**Continuous-spin disagreement coupling.** The 1994 van den Berg–Maes paper explicitly assumes finite single-site state space for the results it applies; it notes most arguments survive for countably infinite state spaces, not arbitrary continuous spaces. A continuous-state transport metric can replace total variation in Dobrushin comparison (e.g. Wasserstein-Dobrushin theorems), but this is a different criterion and requires an explicit metric Lipschitz bound for the conditional law. No direct theorem was found applying the 1994 percolation construction to SU(2) link variables on S^3 at order-one Wilson beta.

**Wasserstein/Dobrushin for continuous spins.** Föllmer (1982), “A Covariance Estimate for Gibbs Measures,” JFA 46, 387–395, gives a comparison/covariance theorem under a contraction matrix of one-site conditional-law transport coefficients: covariance is bounded by the resolvent $D=I+C+C^2+\cdots$ acting on coordinate oscillations. Its metric version applies once the conditional kernels have a proved Wasserstein modulus and the influence operator contracts. Wu (2006), “Poincaré and Transportation Inequalities for Gibbs Measures under the Dobrushin Uniqueness Condition,” Ann. Probab. 34, 1960–1989, derives Poincaré/spectral-gap and transportation inequalities under the same type of spectral-radius-below-one hypothesis. These are proved general continuous-metric tools, not coupling windows for S^3 or an automatic extension of disagreement percolation; the model-specific influence bound remains the obligation. The local lane review `gap-lit-vmf-tor.md` §A has the citations and scope; see [Föllmer DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(82)90053-2) and [Wu preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611635).

**O(N) / sphere spins.** Aizenman–Simon (1980), “Local Ward Identities and the Decay of Correlations in Ferromagnets,” CMP 77, 137–143, prove a mass gap above the mean-field temperature for isotropically coupled O(N) *ferromagnets*. At $d$ dimensions the relevant mean-field threshold in the standard normalization is $\beta<N/(2d)$; for O(4) in three dimensions this is $2/3$. This is the ferromagnetic comparison already summarized in `gap-lit-vmf-tor.md`: it does not cover arbitrary signed/frustrated background couplings, random link staples, or a uniform all-exterior conditional estimate. Dobrushin/transport criteria can handle signs using absolute influences when the relevant row sum is below one, but that high-temperature sufficient condition does not prove the ferromagnetic mean-field interval for all backgrounds. I found no published result extending the O(N) theorem to the required random frustrated S^3 layer at intermediate coupling. Source: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01982713); local prior comparison: `library/prior/gap-lit-vmf-tor.md` §A.

### Lattice gauge influence estimates and numerical coefficients

**Current SU(2), 4D Wilson single-link criterion.** In the normalization $\prod_p \exp[(\beta_W/2)\operatorname{tr}U_p]$, a one-link conditional is von Mises–Fisher on $S^3$. Its chordal-Wasserstein response modulus is $1/4$; in four dimensions the link has 18 distinct neighboring links sharing plaquettes. Thus the direct single-site row sum is bounded by $18\beta_W/4$, giving Dobrushin contraction for $\beta_W<2/9$. The response constant is sharp/attained in the worst exterior, so this particular worst-case single-link criterion cannot be extended past that endpoint. This is a proved estimate, not numerical evidence. It is already represented in row 819’s literature/prior material (`gap-lit-vmf-tor.md` §A and `gap-lit-recent.md`); row 820’s Monte Carlo observation that typical row sums are 3–4 at order-one beta is a separate guide, not an upper bound or proof. Source: the present lane’s earlier strong-coupling literature note; Deep Bhattacharjee, Ushashi Bhattacharya and James Montgomery Lockwood, “Mass Gap of Four-Dimensional Lattice Yang–Mills Theory for Every Compact Simple Group,” [v4 manuscript](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00341v4), lines 0–11 and 63–73. It is an author preprint; the statement is recorded, not independently proof-checked here.

**Gauge fixing and gauge-invariant/block criteria.** The same recent SU(2) analysis uses a straight-line/forest gauge that fixes exactly one link in every plaquette, reducing each free link to 12 free partners; this improves the single-link row count but remains a worst-case influence argument. Its block section examines a plaquette-block Dobrushin–Shlosman condition, not a proved order-one typical-environment result. The existing `gap-lit-recent.md` records a proposed one-cube criterion whose continuum-tuned input is explicitly still open. Gauge fixing can remove redundant directions but changes the conditioning geometry and boundary variables; a gauge-fixed coefficient is not automatically a gauge-invariant conditional influence estimate. Neither cited work proves that conditioning only on gauge-invariant observables yields a contractive one-site or block specification at order-one beta.

**Borgs; Brydges–Fröhlich–Seiler; Seiler.** The located Borgs high-temperature lattice-gauge result is a cluster-expansion area law for spatial Wilson loops at sufficiently high temperature, not a Dobrushin coefficient (`Area law for spatial Wilson loops in high-temperature lattice gauge theories`, NPB 261 (1985), 455–460; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(85)90582-6)); its abstract gives no numerical coupling threshold. Brydges–Fröhlich–Seiler, “On the Construction of Quantized Gauge Fields I: General Results,” Ann. Phys. 121 (1979), 227–284, establish general constructive gauge-theory inequalities, including diamagnetic, chessboard and infrared bounds; neither its abstract nor the inspected theorem excerpts state an intermediate-coupling link-influence bound. Seiler’s 1982 monograph, *Gauge Theories as a Problem of Constructive Quantum Field Theory and Statistical Mechanics*, is a broad lattice/continuum gauge-theory treatment; the existing lane bibliography says its detailed theorem ranges were not read, so I do not attribute an influence criterion to it. Osterwalder–Seiler (1978) establish physical positivity, a positive transfer matrix and strong-coupling infinite-volume/analyticity/confinement results; this is not a typical-environment influence bound. These are relevant context/citation trails, not suppliers for row 821’s proposed route. Sources: [BFS I](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(79)90098-8), [Seiler monograph](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-11559-5), [Osterwalder–Seiler](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8), and existing lane `gap-lit.md` lines 19–21 / `gap-lit-recent.md` § strong-coupling.

**Shen–Zhu–Zhu (2022).** Their lattice gauge Langevin paper proves uniqueness/ergodicity and Poincaré/log-Sobolev inequalities, hence exponential correlation decay, under a Bakry–Émery curvature condition; it is not a Dobrushin/disagreement-percolation argument. In their ’t Hooft normalization $\exp(N\beta\operatorname{ReTr}U_p)$ the SU(N) range is $|\beta|<1/[16(d-1)]$. For SU(2), $d=4$, and the conventional Wilson weight $\exp[(\beta_W/2)\operatorname{tr}U_p]$, $\beta_W=4\beta$, so this is $|\beta_W|<1/12$. The proof’s scope is strong coupling, with $N$ fixed; it does not give an order-one typical-link influence theorem. See [arXiv:2204.12737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12737) and `gap-lit-recent.md`. A separate 2026 manuscript reports an un-gauge-fixed Hessian bound and forest-gauge/block improvements in its own conventions; those are not Shen–Zhu–Zhu results and do not constitute a typical-environment theorem.

**Numerical SU(2) coefficients.** I found no published numerical study reporting the Dobrushin row-sum coefficient $\alpha_e$ (or its typical/bad-link distribution) for pure Wilson SU(2) on a 4D periodic lattice. Standard simulations report plaquette expectations, Wilson loops, Polyakov loops, spectra and related observables, not this conditional-law Wasserstein coefficient. Row 820 is the identified computation: for its finite $8^4$ sample protocol, median single-link coefficient row sums lie around 3–4 over the sampled order-one beta values and every sampled link is marked bad. This is simulation evidence for that finite-volume protocol only; it does not establish a thermodynamic percolation result, and it cannot discharge any proof step under the lane’s no-computer-assisted-steps rule.

## Assessment for row 820

The literature gives two distinct, rigorous templates: (i) worst-case Dobrushin/Wasserstein contraction, which for the direct SU(2) link specification reaches only $\beta_W<2/9$; (ii) quenched finite-spin disagreement percolation, where the *environment’s distribution* makes bad sites sparse enough and independent enough that the induced comparison percolation is subcritical almost surely. Row 820’s measured links do not instantiate template (ii): the proposed “environment” is a correlated Gibbs configuration, while the criterion’s coefficients are uniform over all local boundary assignments, and the measurement reports most links bad at the sampled couplings. A possible research route must therefore prove a new model-specific block/quotient coupling theorem under the *Wilson Gibbs law*, or bound a gauge-invariant conditional influence that cancels gauge directions. The cited literature supplies no such estimate or intermediate-coupling range for SU(2).

This conclusion is a scoped literature search, not an absence theorem or proof result. No calculation was run for this note. The row-820 run is cited only as guide evidence. No computer-assisted step is used to establish a claim.

RESULT: Classical bad-set/disagreement percolation has a proved almost-sure uniqueness template for quenched finite-spin random specifications (not annealed Wilson link configurations); continuous-spin transport/Dobrushin and SU(2) Wilson single-link results remain worst-case and stop at $\beta_W<2/9$, with no located published typical SU(2) coefficient study or order-one block/gauge-invariant influence bound.
