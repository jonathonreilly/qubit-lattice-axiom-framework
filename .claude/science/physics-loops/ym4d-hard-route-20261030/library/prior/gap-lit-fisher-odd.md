## Literature findings

### Twist ratios, Fisher information, and Dirichlet forms

No primary source located in this bounded search defines Fisher information of a centre-twist ratio, or identifies its parameter derivative with the Dirichlet form of a transfer-chain Markov operator. These are distinct notions: Fisher information is a quadratic score expectation for a parameterized probability law; a Markov Dirichlet form is the quadratic form of a specified generator and reference measure. Equating them needs an explicit construction and normalization. The exact two-slice variance/Dirichlet identity recorded in FINISH row 922 is therefore the closest item found to the proposed connection, and is a lane derivation rather than a published precedent.

Eriksson, “DLR-Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequality and Unconditional Mass Gap for Lattice Yang–Mills at Weak Coupling” (2026, viXra preprint 2602.0053), states a DLR-uniform log-Sobolev inequality for finite regions and boundary conditions and claims a uniform physical transfer-matrix gap by an LSI-to-mixing-to-reflection-positivity argument. The paper defines a Dirichlet form for its Gibbs measure. **Closeness:** generic LSI/Dirichlet-to-gap route, but no twist ratio, vortex free energy, or odd flux sector. This is a self-archived preprint claim, not an independently checked theorem; it is a lead only, not evidence that the claim or its hypotheses hold for the present target. [Source](https://www.ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0053v1.pdf).

### Electric flux, twisted partition functions, and sector energies

’t Hooft, “A Property of Electric and Magnetic Flux in Non-Abelian Gauge Theories,” *Nuclear Physics B* **153** (1979), 141–160, [doi:10.1016/0550-3213(79)90595-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(79)90595-9). In the periodic pure-gauge theory, fixed electric/magnetic flux free energies are defined using projectors onto flux sectors; the electric-sector partition function is a trace of the projected transfer evolution. At zero temperature the free energy becomes the lowest energy in that flux sector (relative to the vacuum when taking the flux cost). The paper also gives an exact Euclidean rotation/duality relation between electric and magnetic flux sectors. **Closeness:** establishes the finite-volume flux-sector and ground-energy interpretation. It does not state the proposed odd spectral gap of a ground-state-transformed chain or a Poincaré estimate for a twist ratio. The article text was consulted through an OCR mirror, so the source attribution and formulas were cross-checked against the paper citation and later primary accounts; OCR details are not used here.

Tomboulis and Yaffe, “Finite Temperature SU(2) Lattice Gauge Theory,” [*Communications in Mathematical Physics* **100** (1985), 313–341](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01206134). Their magnetic-flux free energy is defined by the ratio/expectation for a coclosed stack of centre-flipped plaquettes. Their electric-flux weight is the expectation of the electric-flux-sector projector and equals one half of one minus the magnetic twist expectation in their notation. Appendix I proves reflection-positivity inequalities for ’t Hooft loops: the normalized loop expectation is monotone in transverse sizes, and a bound on the flux free energy yields a corresponding loop bound. **Closeness:** exact twist/flux-sector relations and monotonicity, but not a Dirichlet or Fisher-information formula, nor an odd-sector Poincaré inequality. This is the original primary source, read through a full-text mirror; the DOI is the stable citation.

A. Hasenfratz, P. Hasenfratz, and Niedermayer, “Electric fluxes and twisted free energies in SU(3),” *Nuclear Physics B* **329** (1990), 739–752, [doi:10.1016/0550-3213(90)90080-W](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(90)90080-W). The paper expresses fixed electric-flux partition functions as traces over projected transfer sectors and as discrete Fourier transforms of twisted partition functions; it extracts flux energies from the large-time decay and reports numerical measurements. **Closeness:** a direct computational realization of the twist-to-electric-sector-energy connection, but the numerical work is not a proof and contains no Fisher/Dirichlet/Poincaré identification.

### Vortex free energies and inequalities

Yoneya, “Inequalities for magnetic-flux free energies and confinement in lattice gauge theories,” *Nuclear Physics B* **205** (1982), 130–140, [doi:10.1016/0550-3213(82)90470-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(82)90470-9). The publisher abstract reports rigorous inequalities among magnetic-flux free energies on tori of varying diameters and a conditional implication from vanishing free energy under large uniform dilation to exponential decrease in cross-section area, sufficient for permanent static-quark confinement. The publisher full text was inaccessible (403), so only the abstract was read. **Closeness:** size monotonicity/conditional confinement criterion, not a Poincaré inequality or Dirichlet-form bound.

Tomboulis and Yaffe (1985), above, prove reflection-positivity inequalities linking magnetic twist/flux free energies and ’t Hooft-loop expectations. Kanazawa, “The Tomboulis-Yaffe Inequality for U(N) Lattice Gauge Theories,” arXiv:0808.3442, *Annals of Physics* **324** (2009), 1634–1665, Theorems 1 and 4, gives the periodic-lattice loop bound in terms of the centre-twist ratio for SU(2), U(N), and U(1). This is the exact inequality already transcribed in the repository's `gap-skeleton-final.md`; it does not supply smallness of the twist ratio and is not a gap estimate. **Closeness:** published twist-to-loop inequality, with no Fisher/Poincaré link.

The pre-existing `library/prior/gap-lit-2.md` already read Tomboulis (arXiv:0707.2179) and Ito–Seiler (arXiv:0711.4930), so those papers were not reread. Its recorded finding is that Tomboulis claims all-coupling confinement while Ito–Seiler identify a key inequality as established only in the high-temperature/strong-coupling expansion, with analytic continuation not justified there. This is a disputed vortex-free-energy argument, not an odd electric-flux gap or a Dirichlet-form theorem. See that prior note for its source passages; this entry does not repeat them.
