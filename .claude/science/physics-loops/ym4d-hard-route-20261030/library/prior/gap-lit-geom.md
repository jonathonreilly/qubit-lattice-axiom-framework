### Closest 4D lattice Yang–Mills claims

**Eriksson, “The Yang–Mills Mass Gap on the Lattice: a Self-Contained Proof” (February 2026), viXra 2602.0033 v1.** The paper states for Wilson SU($N_c$), $d=4$, small $g^2$, a positive gap in lattice units with a lower bound of order $e^{-C/g^2}$. Its Theorem 1.1's actual range is $L_0\le L\le C_0 e^{C(N_c)/g^2}$, not all volumes; §6 says larger volumes require a spatial cluster expansion not supplied. It assumes compatibility of Balaban's partition-function identity with temporal transfer-matrix factorization (Theorem 4.1). The proof claims a terminal-scale transfer gap by applying Holley–Stroock and a Ricci lower bound to a coarsened lattice with $O(1)$ sites, then transports the gap through the RG. **Relation:** strongest close precedent for “terminal-scale spectral argument”; it is a claimed direct transfer-gap conclusion in a finite volume window, but not M5-G's fixed-physical-time Doob Dirichlet form or cutoff-uniform all-large-cube statement. It presupposes precisely a transfer/RG compatibility which is not established in the paper. **Wilson SU(2):** same nominal model/group, but this compatibility and volume-range limitation block it as an application. **Read:** full six-page PDF (pp. 1–6; theorem, RG, terminal estimate, transfer identity and caveats). This is an unrefereed viXra manuscript; “the paper states” is not a correctness verdict. URL opened: https://www.ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0033v1.pdf . No arXiv ID; viXra:2602.0033.

**Eriksson, “Uniform Poincaré Inequality for Lattice Yang–Mills Theory via Multiscale Martingale Decomposition” (February 2026), viXra 2602.0040 v1; archived as AIRR ARR-2026-6RKVSY9K529RTVRV.** It claims for $SU(N_c)$, $d=4$, Wilson lattice YM at sufficiently large $\beta=2N_c/g^2$, a configuration-space Poincaré inequality uniform in lattice size, conditional in v1 on Balaban constructive RG and an RG-normalized disintegration hypothesis. The stated inputs include a Ricci lower bound for gauge-orbit geometry, conditional fast-mode Poincaré bounds, polymer derivative/cross-scale estimates, and martingale commutator summability; its Theorem 5.3 explicitly assumes Theorems 3.1, 4.2, 4.3 and 2.6. The terminal marginal still needs a Poincaré constant, obtained in the manuscript from a bounded-site terminal lattice and a large-field patching estimate. **Relation:** the closest geometric/multiscale configuration-space inequality found; its Dirichlet form is the gradient form for the Gibbs measure, not $\langle f,(I-P_{j,L})f\rangle_{\psi^2}$ for physical-time transfer. No transfer bridge is part of this Poincaré statement. **Wilson SU(2):** it purports to include SU(2), but its RG and disintegration inputs and the mismatch of generators prevent direct use for M5-G. **Read:** full downloaded 17-page v1 PDF; abstract, hypotheses and Theorem 5.3 inspected (not a line-by-line proof check). It is an unrefereed manuscript; AIRR's record explicitly says the historical import is not assessed. URLs opened: https://www.ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0040v1.pdf and https://airr.science/papers/ARR-2026-6RKVSY9K529RTVRV/versions/v1/ . No arXiv ID; DOI unavailable.

### Stochastic quantization and strong coupling

**Shen, Rongchan Zhu, Xiangchan Zhu, “A Stochastic Analysis Approach to Lattice Yang–Mills at Strong Coupling,” CMP 400 (2023), 805–851, arXiv:2204.12737, DOI 10.1007/s00220-022-04609-1.** Theorem 1.4 gives LSI and Poincaré inequalities for the infinite-volume invariant measure of their lattice Langevin dynamic under Assumption 1.1; for SU($N$), their sufficient small-coupling parameter is $|\beta|<1/[16(d-1)]$ in their action convention. They also prove exponential ergodicity and exponential correlation decay for a stated class of observables, hence their lattice “mass gap.” The Poincaré form is the Langevin generator's sum of edge-gradient energies. **Relation:** exact lattice-gauge Dirichlet-form result, but for stochastic-quantization dynamics on configuration space, not the time-direction transfer kernel or its ground-state transform. It works at strong coupling (small $\beta$), not across the large-$\beta$ weak-coupling path, and its statement does not say a correlation length many lattice spacings in the scaling regime. **Wilson SU(2):** the Wilson action is in scope in that parameter range; it does not reach M5-G's weak-coupling/continuum regime and has no transfer-kernel comparison. **Read:** full 43-page arXiv PDF downloaded; Theorems 1.1–1.4, §§3–5 and the generator/LSI definitions inspected. ### Ground-state LSI and hypercontractivity: what transfers, and what does not

**Gross (1975), “Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities,” Amer. J. Math. 97, 1061–1083, DOI 10.2307/2373688; Federbush (1973), “A partially constructive proof of Nelson's conjecture,” J. Funct. Anal. 12, 1–27, DOI 10.1016/0022-1236(73)90028-7; Gross (1975), “Existence and uniqueness of physical ground states,” J. Funct. Anal. 20, 52–109, DOI 10.1016/0022-1236(75)90002-9.** The general theorem is that an LSI for a specified symmetric Markov Dirichlet form is equivalent to hypercontractivity of that form's semigroup, and implies its Poincaré/spectral-gap inequality. Federbush-type bounds control lower spectral energies under exponential-integrability assumptions on a perturbing potential. These are tools/criteria, not a dimension-free theorem for arbitrary interacting ground-state measures. A ground-state transform transfers the Schrödinger semigroup to a Dirichlet form with invariant density $\psi^2$; it does not itself give a uniform LSI constant. **Relation:** if one proves LSI for the *physical-time Doob semigroup*, it is stronger than M5-G. The older QFT literature uses specific scalar-field hypotheses, vacuum/Markov structure, and perturbation estimates; no result found supplies a cutoff- and volume-uniform bound for the 4D Wilson transfer ground-state law. **Wilson SU(2):** no direct applicability; compact nonabelian gauge orbit geometry, transfer time vs auxiliary Langevin generator, and weak-coupling uniformity are all missing. **Read:** Gross 1975 and Federbush metadata plus theorem-level secondary/open sources; original papers not downloaded here (partial/abstract/bibliographic, not full). URLs opened: https://doi.org/10.2307/2373688 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(73)90028-7 ; https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(75)90002-9 .

**Simon and Høegh-Krohn (1972), “Hypercontractive semigroups and two dimensional self-coupled Bose fields,” J. Funct. Anal. 9, 121–180, DOI 10.1016/0022-1236(72)90008-0; Guerra, Rosen, Simon (1975), “The P($\phi$)$_2$ Euclidean quantum field theory as classical statistical mechanics,” Ann. Math. Studies 94, Princeton Univ. Press.** Simon–Høegh-Krohn give an abstract perturbation theorem for $H_0+V$ under four hypotheses: $H_0\ge0$ self-adjoint on $L^2(M,\mu)$ with $e^{-tH_0}$ an $L^1$ contraction; some $e^{-TH_0}:L^2\to L^4$ bounded; $V\in L^p(\mu)$ for some $p>2$; and $e^{-tV}\in L^1(\mu)$ for every $t>0$. It yields hypercontractive semigroups in a 2D self-coupled boson-field setting. The QFT results connect hypercontractivity plus the Markov property to decay/mass-gap statements for $P(\phi)_2$. **Relation:** powerful continuum scalar-field precedent and proof technique. It relies on a supplied hypercontractive free semigroup and scalar interaction integrability; it does not yield a Wilson SU(2) physical-time Doob LSI. **Wilson SU(2):** no; dimension, group, and input semigroup differ. **Read:** Simon–Høegh-Krohn article abstract and theorem setup (open full text page; part, pp. 121–124 indexed); Guerra–Rosen–Simon book-level citation/limited open excerpt only. URLs opened: https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(72)90008-0 and https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(73)90312-2 .

**Bauerschmidt and Dagallier (2023), “Log-Sobolev inequality for the $\varphi^4_2$ and $\varphi^4_3$ measures,” Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 77 (2024), 2579–2612, DOI 10.1002/cpa.22173, arXiv:2202.02295.** They prove LSI for continuum $\varphi^4_2$ and $\varphi^4_3$ under the optimal condition that susceptibility is bounded; the LSI is uniform in lattice regularization, and uniform in volume throughout the models' high-temperature phases. **Relation:** a strong multiscale continuum-field result whose assumption reaches a correlation scale controlled by susceptibility, not a uniform transfer-time estimate through a nonperturbative 4D gauge regime. It is an LSI for the scalar field measure/dynamics. **Wilson SU(2):** no scalar convexity/Polchinski flow or susceptibility control for Wilson orbit-space measure is supplied; no Doob-kernel comparison. **Read:** full arXiv PDF's abstract and main theorem/assumption pages (pp. 1–4; proof not full). URLs opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02295 and https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.22173 .

**Bauerschmidt and Bodineau (2021), “Log-Sobolev inequality for the continuum sine-Gordon model,” CPAM 74, 2064–2113, DOI 10.1002/cpa.21926, arXiv:1907.12308.** Their multiscale Bakry–Émery/Polchinski-equation criterion proves asymptotically optimal LSI for the massive continuum sine-Gordon model for $\beta<6\pi$, for Glauber and Kawasaki dynamics under the paper's stated conditions. The criterion handles measures that need not be log-concave. **Relation:** a nontrivial multiscale LSI tool reaching the sine-Gordon model's parameter threshold, but for those specified scalar dynamics. It gives no direct bound for the Wilson transfer ground state and has no “small number at the correlation length” that can be imported to the gauge model. **Wilson SU(2):** not directly; no matching measure, flow equation, or physical-transfer generator. **Read:** full 50-page open author repository PDF; Theorem 1.1, §1.2–1.4 and criterion read; proof not read line by line. URLs opened: https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12308 and https://api.repository.cam.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/c646097a-2930-4553-9452-9c2d72d9d173/content .

**Bauerschmidt, Bodineau, Dagallier (2025), “A criterion on the free energy for log-Sobolev inequalities in mean-field particle systems,” arXiv:2503.24372.** Abstract states a free-energy criterion for mean-field systems; examples of double-well quadratic-interaction models reach their critical temperature, with asymptotics of the LSI constant near criticality. ### General geometric and lattice-spin criteria

**Lawler–Sokal (1988), “Bounds on the $L^2$ spectrum for Markov chains and Markov processes: a generalization of Cheeger's inequality,” Trans. AMS 309, 557–580, DOI 10.1090/S0002-9947-1988-0930082-9.** Theorem-level result: for reversible discrete-time Markov chains or continuous-time jump processes on general state spaces, an isoperimetric/conductance lower bound controls the bottom nonzero $L^2$ spectrum (with the usual squared-Cheeger form and hypotheses on irreducibility/reversibility). **Relation:** this is precisely the abstract type of geometric producer suggested for the Dirichlet form in M5-G. It gives no model conductance estimate. For M5-G the needed input would be a cutoff- and cube-uniform lower bound on conductance of the *actual* joint law $\pi_{j,L}(dx)P_{j,L}(x,dy)$, after the entire fine slab is integrated out. **Wilson SU(2):** theorem applies abstractly if the Doob kernel meets its Markov/reversibility hypotheses; no lower bound for this kernel follows from the theorem. **Read:** full 24-page open AMS PDF; theorem and assumptions inspected, proof not checked line by line. URL opened: https://www.ams.org/journals/tran/1988-309-02/S0002-9947-1988-0930082-9/S0002-9947-1988-0930082-9.pdf .

**Stroock–Zegarlinski (1992), “The logarithmic Sobolev inequality for continuous spin systems on a lattice,” JFA 104, 299–326, DOI 10.1016/0022-1236(92)90003-2; and “The logarithmic Sobolev inequality for discrete spin systems on a lattice,” CMP 149 (1992), 175–193, DOI 10.1007/BF02096629.** For continuous spins, Theorem 1.6 gives a sufficient local-specification condition for an LSI; Theorem 3.10 relates it to Dobrushin–Shlosman mixing. For finite-spin finite-range lattice gases, their other paper shows equivalence of Dobrushin–Shlosman mixing and an LSI for the unique Gibbs state. These concern the associated Glauber dynamics/Gibbs Dirichlet form. **Relation:** conditional way to get uniform mixing and a gap from a strong spatial-mixing estimate; it does not supply mixing at a gauge-theory correlation scale, nor does it identify the transfer Doob dynamics. **Wilson SU(2):** compact-link continuous spins resemble the setting, but the gauge constraints, non-product conditional geometry, and missing Dobrushin bounds block an application. A DLR-LSI extension would require boundary-uniformity explicitly. **Read:** continuous-spin full article page and theorem abstracts; discrete-spin abstract/metadata only. URLs opened: https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-1236(92)90003-2 and https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02096629 .

**Eriksson, “Spectral Gap and Thermodynamic Limit for SU(N) Lattice Yang–Mills Theory via Log-Sobolev Inequalities and Complete Analyticity,” viXra 2602.0089 v1 (February 2026).** Abstract claims, at fixed lattice spacing $\eta>0$, four-dimensional SU($N$) Wilson theory at weak coupling $g_0\le g_*$: (A) an L-uniform Gibbs-measure LSI; (B) an L-uniform Osterwalder–Seiler Hamiltonian gap; (C) uniqueness/thermodynamic limit. The scale is fixed lattice spacing and the asserted volume limit is at weak bare coupling; it gives no independent estimate of the centre-even ground-state physical-time kernel. **Wilson SU(2):** nominally included for N=2; the load-bearing CA verification and the transfer/Doob-form distinction remain. **Read:** full 16-page open PDF; abstract, theorem statements, assumptions and proof dependencies read; no line-by-line correctness check. ## Answers for the lane

1. **Transfer/Doob spectral gap by geometry:** no independently verified published theorem found that proves M5-G for the 4D Wilson SU(2) ground-state Doob kernel. The closest applicable abstract method is Cheeger/Lawler–Sokal: prove a conductance bound for that exact kernel, then infer a Dirichlet spectral gap. Finite-cutoff positivity/simple Perron eigenvalue gives only a cutoff-dependent gap. The close 4D Eriksson manuscripts claim a terminal-scale gap or an L-uniform transfer gap through RG compatibility or complete analyticity, not M5-G's direct physical-time form; both are unrefereed and carry substantial named inputs.
2. **LSI/hypercontractivity:** Gross/Federbush/Simon theory gives powerful criteria once the *same* ground-state or Markov semigroup has an LSI/hypercontractive bound. Bauerschmidt–Bodineau reach massive sine-Gordon for $\beta<6\pi$; Bauerschmidt–Dagallier reach continuum $\varphi^4_2,\varphi^4_3$ when susceptibility is bounded, uniformly in the lattice cutoff and in high-temperature volume ranges. Shen–Zhu–Zhu obtain a lattice YM Langevin LSI/Poincaré and clustering at strong coupling. None furnishes a uniform physical-time transfer estimate for a correlation length far above $a$ in 4D Wilson SU(2) without a small interaction parameter or an independently established mixing/curvature hypothesis.
3. **Conditional 4D reductions:** Eriksson 2602.0033 assumes compatibility of Balaban partition-function identity with temporal transfer factorization, applies a terminal-scale Holley–Stroock/Ricci gap on a bounded-site coarse torus, and its theorem's volume range ends at order $e^{C/g^2}$; larger volumes explicitly require an unproved spatial cluster expansion. Eriksson 2602.0040 v1 assumes Balaban RG and an RG-normalized disintegration; Theorem 5.3 additionally assumes its named conditional Poincaré, martingale commutator, and terminal estimates (Theorems 3.1, 4.2, 4.3, 2.6). Eriksson 2602.0054 v2 claims its former Assumption 5.4 is verified in companion papers, with integrated cross-scale derivative bounds and named companion hypotheses. Eriksson 2602.0089 conditions the LSI and transfer-gap route on Dobrushin–Shlosman complete analyticity as verified by the RG. These are source-stated claims, not checked proofs here.

**Use for M5-G/M5-H.** The literature suggests three different suppliers: (i) prove conductance for the physical-time joint law (Cheeger); (ii) prove a uniform LSI for the *transfer ground-state* Dirichlet form; or (iii) prove a spatial-mixing/CA criterion and separately establish its bridge to transfer. No located source supplies (i) or (ii) for 4D Wilson SU(2). The Eriksson claims are close conditional reductions, but their data do not remove the task's open physical-scale input. No source located proves (M5-H), a uniform even-sector thermal state count, from an LSI alone. A separate counting/entropy estimate remains necessary, as the existing lane states.
