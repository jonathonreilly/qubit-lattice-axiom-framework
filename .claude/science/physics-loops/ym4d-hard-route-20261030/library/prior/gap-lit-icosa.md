## Findings

### 1. Klein invariant theory, Molien series, and Haar moments

For the binary icosahedral group `2I` (order 120) in its defining two-dimensional representation, Klein's invariant ring has basic invariant degrees 12, 20, and 30, with one relation in degree 60. Equivalently its Hilbert/Molien series is

$$
H(t)=\frac{1-t^{60}}{(1-t^{12})(1-t^{20})(1-t^{30})}
     =\frac{1+t^{30}}{(1-t^{12})(1-t^{20})}.
$$

The degree-12 invariant is the binary form vanishing on the 12 vertices (in a suitable coordinate choice, the Klein polynomial is `uv(u^10+11u^5v^5-v^10)`). These are exact invariant-theory statements; a readable derivation applying Molien's theorem is Mukai, *An Introduction to Invariants and Moduli*, Example 1.16, [pp. 17–20](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~obenoist/refs/Mukai.pdf). It gives the class average, the simplified Hilbert series, the degree-12 Klein form, and the degree-20/30 forms and relation. Klein's original *Lectures on the Icosahedron* is the classical source.

For a compact group, averaging `g^{⊗n}` is the orthogonal projection onto invariant tensors. Thus the absence of `2I`-invariants in symmetric degrees 1 through 11, followed by the first invariant line in degree 12 (spin 6), gives the moment match and first discrepancy. Row 804 states the stronger full tensor identity: the Haar averaging operators on `V^{⊗n}` agree for `n≤11`, and at `n=12` differ by the rank-one invariant projection in `Sym^12 V`. Its displayed character-count proof (G12–G13) is hand-checkable. The invariant-theory literature supports the degree-12 mechanism; the tensor-operator equality also uses the usual invariant-projector identity and is explicitly established in row 804.

**Scope / relevance:** exact algebra, not a Wilson-measure estimate. The agreement is for unweighted Haar averages; it does not imply agreement of positive-β Wilson-tilted laws. The degree-12 distinction is the input to the charged high-spin filter in (G15)–(G17a), but invariant theory supplies neither its insertion into the original Wilson integral nor (G22).

**Coupling values need care.** Horn–Karliner, “Mean Field Analysis of Hamiltonian SU(2) Lattice Gauge Theory,” [Fermilab-Pub-83/61-THY](https://lss.fnal.gov/archive/1983/pub/Pub-83-061-T.pdf), reports a freezing/difference near `β_F≈2.9` (PDF lines 436–440 and figure caption line 576) for a 3D Euclidean chiral SU(2) problem used to evaluate a mean-link ansatz for the 3+1D Hamiltonian theory. This is numerical and is **not** a measured critical coupling for the 4D pure Wilson lattice action. Petcher–Weingarten's abstract establishes first-order transition behavior and the trend in critical couplings but gives no numerical value in the abstract; I do not assign it a specific β here.

The historical results are consistent with row 804's algebra: low-order averages can coincide while fixed-group Wilson dynamics departs at larger coupling due to discreteness. These are separate mechanisms: (G12) concerns exact Haar tensors at low degree; freezing is a finite-coupling phenomenon observed in simulations. Neither gives a rigorous beta interval for G22.

Recent quantum-simulation literature uses finite groups as digitizations or finite-dimensional encodings. Examples include the binary-octahedral gate construction [Phys. Rev. D 109, 054503 (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.109.054503), and reviews of finite-subgroup and representation truncations [Byrnes & Yamamoto, *Quantum simulation of lattice gauge theories in more than one space dimension*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/ac4a6b). These discuss resource costs, Gauss-law constraints, and how finite truncations depart from continuous groups; they are quantum-algorithm/design results, not rigorous bounds on continuum Wilson twist free energies. The recent quantum-simulation route does not appear to supply an exact Wilson block resummation on the charged boundary space.

### 3. High spin, Casimir projections, recoupling, and operator norms

The closest exact literature is representation theory and the standard Peter–Weyl/spin-network expansion: link functions decompose into irreducible representations, Casimir eigenvalues label electric energy in Hamiltonian formulations, and recoupling coefficients organize gauge-invariant contractions. This supplies a language and exact kinematic decompositions, not a uniform estimate on the normalized Wilson transfer operator in a twisted charged sector. For a concrete nearby use, Horn–Karliner (above, PDF Appendix B, lines 497–535) derives the SU(2) character/Casimir eigenfunction equations and notes higher-rank tensors can be built by symmetrized generator products. This feeds a variational mean-field ansatz for a Hamiltonian vacuum energy; it does not estimate a twist, a Wilson loop, a mass gap, or a charged-boundary operator norm.

Row 804's own formulas are closer to the requested use than the literature located: the convolution eigenvalue estimate and degree-12 invariant give an SU(2)-specific high-spin charged filter, with explicit comparison eigenvalues for `2I` and U(1). It then proves two limitations: the bare four-strand Haar tensor is identity on its invariant space, so free filter insertion has no contraction; and a flat wrapping plane of spin-1/2 plaquettes contributes without encountering that filter. The note does not claim these as literature results. I found no primary source stating a high-spin/Casimir projection or spin-network norm bound that controls a twisted free energy, Wilson-loop expectation, or mass gap for four-dimensional Wilson SU(2) at order-one coupling. Examples found were formal spin-network identities, Hamiltonian electric-Casimir definitions, or model-dependent/numerical Casimir scaling of static potentials. None has the same normalized boundary-sector statement.
