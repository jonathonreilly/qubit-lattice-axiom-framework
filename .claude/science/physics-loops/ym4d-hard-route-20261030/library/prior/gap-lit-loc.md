# Literature on localized trial states for flux-sector energies

## Scope and answer

Target: **GUIDE** four-dimensional Wilson SU(2), electric centre sectors of the seed transfer operator, and a trial function whose support stays localized as the cutoff changes. Row 754 requires both

`Dir(f) <= c1 (1-r_chi) ||f||_2^2`

and

`||f||_inf^2 ||f||_2^2 <= c2 Var_pi(f^2)`,

with `c1`, `c2`, and `Theta_chi` uniform in the cutoff. These are stronger than merely finding a trial state with finite energy. The first says its Rayleigh quotient is within a fixed factor of the unknown sector minimum. The second prevents its square from having too little variance relative to its supremum and norm.

**Answer:** the sources below provide variational principles, perturbative sector spectra, numerical electric-flux energies, and comparison tools for reversible chains. I found no published proof that supplies both LOC inequalities for a bounded localized electric-flux trial function in four-dimensional Wilson SU(2), uniformly in the cutoff. The standard variational principle gives a sector-energy upper bound once such a state and its quadratic form are controlled; it does not supply the state or the uniform estimates.

## Closest gauge-theory results

| Result and exact scope | Bearing on LOC and Wilson SU(2) | Read status and identifier |
|---|---|---|
| **Rayleigh–Ritz in a symmetry sector.** For a self-adjoint, lower-bounded Hamiltonian preserving a closed sector subspace, its lowest sector energy is at most the quadratic-form quotient of any nonzero trial vector in that subspace's form domain. The same min–max statement applies to `1-P` for a self-adjoint Markov transfer operator restricted to an invariant sector. | This is the rigorous variational upper bound in general. In the transfer formulation, every `f` in the sector has `Dir(f)/||f||² >= 1-r_chi`; LOC needs the reverse comparison up to a uniform constant for one bounded, localized `f`. The principle alone provides neither that approximate-minimizer property nor the variance estimate. Applying it to the Wilson Hamiltonian also requires the actual Gauss-law/centre-sector domain and a normalized admissible trial vector. | Exact functional-analysis principle, not a model-specific literature estimate. |
| **Horn–Weinstein, gauge-invariant Hamiltonian variational method.** They project trial states onto the locally gauge-invariant subspace and minimize a Rayleigh–Ritz quotient. The construction is formulated for continuous and non-Abelian gauge groups in spatial dimensions two and three. Their explicit calculations concern periodic U(1) lattice electrodynamics; the non-Abelian effective partition function is formulated but not evaluated. | Relevant method for constructing gauge-invariant Hamiltonian trial states. The paper does not prove a localized centre-odd winding-flux trial state, a twisted-sector energy bound, or a cutoff-uniform Dirichlet/variance pair for Wilson SU(2). ## Dirichlet-form and spectral-gap comparisons

| Result and exact scope | What it supplies; why it does not supply LOC | Read status and identifier |
|---|---|---|
| **Diaconis–Saloff-Coste comparison.** For reversible Markov chains on the same finite state space, geometric comparison bounds eigenvalues of one chain using another chain and the comparison graph/congestion. Their principal example is the symmetric exclusion process compared with Bernoulli–Laplace diffusion. | It can transfer a spectral-gap estimate after a same-space comparison of stationary laws and Dirichlet forms has been proved. It is not an automatic comparison between distinct electric or magnetic transfer sectors, whose kernels, measures, or Hilbert spaces may differ. No application to Wilson SU(2) flux sectors was found. | Full paper text accessed; read abstract, hypotheses, and opening example, not every proof. P. Diaconis and L. Saloff-Coste, *Ann. Appl. | **Lawler–Sokal Cheeger inequality.** Gives conductance-based spectral bounds for general-state Markov chains/processes, including reversible forms and killed processes; for reversible chains the standard form bounds the gap below by a constant times conductance squared and above by a constant times conductance. | A bottleneck estimate can bound a chain's gap once the stationary measure and boundary flow are known. This does not produce the required localized near-minimizer: a Cheeger lower bound is not the cutoff-uniform upper comparison on `Dir(f)` required by LOC. No sector-specific lattice-gauge application was found. | Full paper text accessed; abstract and theorem scope read. G. F. Lawler and A. D. Sokal, *Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 309 (1988), DOI [10.2307/2000925](https://doi.org/10.2307/2000925). |
| **Eldan–Koehler–Zeitouni, high-temperature Ising.** For Ising measures on a finite hypercube with general quadratic interaction matrix, if the interaction matrix operator norm is below **GUIDE 1**, they prove a Poincaré inequality for the natural Glauber-dynamics Dirichlet form and obtain mixing bounds. | This is a rigorous spin-model Dirichlet-gap result, but it concerns stochastic spin-flip dynamics, not the row 754 transfer operator or a twisted electric sector. Its high-temperature hypothesis does not identify the order-one effective-coupling Wilson SU(2) regime. | Abstract and theorem condition read; full proof not read. R. Eldan, F. Koehler, O. Exact transfer spectra for the two-dimensional Ising model with twisted or other boundary conditions are also known. They give sector eigenvalues in that solvable model, not comparison estimates or localized trial functions for four-dimensional gauge transfer operators. See Abraham, *On the Transfer Matrix for the Two-Dimensional Ising Model*, DOI [10.1002/sapm197150171](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm197150171), and the Ising/Potts boundary-condition analysis, DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(86)90596-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(86)90596-1); abstract-level reads.
