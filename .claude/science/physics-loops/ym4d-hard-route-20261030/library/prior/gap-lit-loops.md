## Results by method

### Exact equations and rigorous expansions

**Makeenko–Migdal (1979), “Exact equation for the loop average in multicolor QCD,” DOI [10.1016/0370-2693(79)90131-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(79)90131-X).** The closed loop equation is derived in the infinite-colour limit and its lattice regularization considered. It is an equation for large-N Wilson-loop expectations, with splitting at intersections; it is not a finite-N estimate. It supplies neither a coupling-uniform bound nor a mass gap. This is the historical large-N starting point (abstract/metadata read; paper body not read).

**Chatterjee (2015), “Rigorous solution of strongly coupled SO(N) lattice gauge theory in the large N limit,” [arXiv:1502.07719](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.07719), DOI [10.1007/s00220-019-03353-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-019-03353-3).** Theorem 3.1 gives an absolutely convergent string-trajectory expansion for normalized Wilson-loop expectations in the SO(N) ’t Hooft limit, for sufficiently small inverse-coupling parameter `|β|≤β0(d)` (GUIDE), in any dimension. Corollaries include factorization and an area-law upper bound in that regime. It is a rigorous strong-coupling/large-N result for arbitrary loop sequences, including intersecting paths; it is not finite-N SU(2) at order-one effective coupling and says nothing about a continuum mass gap. PDF theorem and scope read.

**Chatterjee–Jafarov (2016), “The 1/N expansion for SO(N) lattice gauge theory at strong coupling,” [arXiv:1604.04777](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04777).** Theorem 3.1 proves, to every fixed order, an absolutely convergent string-trajectory formula for the 1/N expansion of Wilson-loop expectations in SO(N), for `|β|≤β0(d,k)` (GUIDE), any dimension. This extends the strong-coupling large-N formula systematically, but its constants depend on expansion order and it is not a 4D SU(2) order-one estimate. All loop shapes are admitted as inputs; special shapes do not evade the coupling restriction. PDF theorem and proof setup read.

**Jafarov (2016), “Wilson loop expectations in SU(N) lattice gauge theory,” [arXiv:1610.03821](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03821).** Theorem 8.1 proves a finite-N master loop equation for SU(N) on a finite Λ, for every `N≥2` and real β, for loop sequences with the one-neighbourhood of their supports inside Λ. It includes splitting, merger, deformation and expansion terms and therefore applies to crossings and arbitrary paths. This is an exact Schwinger–Dyson identity, not a decay bound. Theorem 4.1 separately proves the 1/N expansion only for `|β|≤β0(d,k)` (GUIDE), i.e. strong coupling. It does not prove a finite-N gap. Theorems 4.1, 4.2 and 8.1 read.

**Shen–Smith–Zhu (2022), “A new derivation of the finite N master loop equation for lattice Yang–Mills,” [arXiv:2202.00880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00880), DOI [10.1007/s00220-023-04638-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-023-04638-w).** Gives a Langevin/Itô derivation of the finite-N equation for SO(N), U(N) and SU(N). The equation is an exact identity at finite N and arbitrary coupling, not a solution or quantitative bound. It is relevant to SU(2) only as identity-level infrastructure; it supplies no mass-gap or correlation-length estimate. Abstract and theorem/formula discussion read.

**Basu–Ganguly (2017), “SO(N) Lattice Gauge Theory, planar and beyond,” [arXiv:1608.04379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04379), DOI [10.1002/cpa.21788](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.21788).** Gives a geometric/string-trajectory treatment of Chatterjee's strong-coupling expansion and an example showing that a Wilson-loop area-law lower bound does not hold in full generality. For SO(N) and strong coupling/large N; not SU(2) at the target coupling. The existing `gap-lit-ineq.md` already records the boundary result; source abstract, theorem statements and coupling discussion read.

**Cao–Park–Sheffield (2025), “Random surfaces and lattice Yang–Mills,” [arXiv:2307.06790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06790), DOI [10.1090/cams/59](https://doi.org/10.1090/cams/59).** Theorem 1.8 gives an exact surface/map-sum representation for Wilson-loop expectations on a finite lattice for U(N), with analogues for SU(N), O(N), SO(N) and Sp(N/2); the stated setup allows every β>0 and d≥2 (GUIDE). It covers arbitrary loop collections, including self-intersections, but is a representation formula, not a positivity estimate or a convergent decay theorem in 4D. The authors explicitly leave the d≥3 continuum problem open. It is useful for organizing shape dependence, not a 4D SU(2) gap input. Theorem 1.8, Theorem 2.16 and continuum-scope discussion read.

**Cao–Nissim–Sheffield (2025), “Expanded regimes of area law for lattice Yang–Mills theories,” [arXiv:2505.16585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16585).** This is the important hand-proof exception to a blanket “all loop-equation results are large-N or numerical” statement. Theorem 4.1 proves an area-law upper bound for every rectangular loop, uniformly over every finite lattice Λ, for U(N) when `N≥10^10 d^10` and `β≤10^-10/d` (GUIDE); β is the paper's small, strong-coupling parameter. The proof analyzes the finite-N master equation by a truncated model and a contraction/rare-merger estimate; it is a written analytic proof, not a numerical SDP certificate. Theorem 1.2 extends the conclusion to every N at some dimension-only small-β threshold, combining Theorem 4.1 with Osterwalder–Seiler for the finitely many smaller N. For fixed N=2 this gives only a strong-coupling range; it does not reach order-one coupling. Its output is an area-law bound, not a two-sided bootstrap interval, volume comparison, or local correlation-length estimate. Theorem 1.2, parameter assumptions (2.1)–(2.3), Theorem 4.1 and proof read. Numerical thresholds are GUIDE; the proof route itself is by hand.

### Positivity bootstrap and computed bounds

**Anderson–Kruczenski (2017), “Loop equations and bootstrap methods in the lattice,” [arXiv:1612.08140](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.08140), DOI [10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2017.06.009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2017.06.009).** Proposes positivity of Wilson-loop/open-path matrices together with the loop equation and studies numerical methods. The large-N loop equation closes on single loops; in 2D the exact solution gives a check, while their 4D weak-coupling results complement strong-coupling expansions. Their truncated bootstrap/SDP values are numerical estimates (e.g. loop lengths up to 10 and finite-lattice comparisons are GUIDE), not hand inequalities and not certified here as Lean-formalizable certificates. No mass gap or volume-to-volume comparison. PDF method and results sections read.

**Kazakov–Zheng (2023), “Bootstrap for lattice Yang–Mills theory,” [arXiv:2203.11360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11360), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.107.L051501](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.107.L051501).** SU(∞), d=2,3,4. Combines length-truncated Makeenko–Migdal equations with positivity matrices and computes rigorous upper/lower plaquette bounds at sampled couplings; in 4D at `Lmax=16` (GUIDE), lower bounds track the strong-coupling branch and upper bounds track weak-coupling perturbation theory. The paper notes the unresolved benchmark interval λ∈(2.4,2.8) (GUIDE) and calls for larger cutoffs. The method solves SDPs with MOSEK; the presented coupling-dependent margins are solver outputs, not hand-derived certificates. It is large N, plaquette/free-energy information only, and has no volume comparison or gap conclusion. Main text and supplement's solver statement read.

**Kazakov–Zheng (2025), “Bootstrap for finite N lattice Yang–Mills theory,” [arXiv:2404.16925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16925), DOI [10.1007/JHEP03(2025)099](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP03(2025)099).** Finite-N SU(2), d=2,3,4. The linear single-trace SU(2) loop equation and positivity constraints are used in convex SDPs; their output gives upper/lower margins for the plaquette/free energy and string-tension estimates at sampled couplings, with reported sub-percent precision in the physically relevant 3D/4D range (GUIDE). In 4D the paper plots the Lmax≤16 (GUIDE) bound curves and reports three upper/lower pairs at λ=3.19, 3.48, 3.64 (GUIDE) with Lmax≤24 (GUIDE); λ=2N²/β in their convention. The calculation uses MOSEK, and the paper notes limited computation at the highest truncation and a local-infinite-lattice bootstrap bottleneck. The abstract calls the margins exact, but the concrete margins are produced by optimization software and are not hand proofs in the paper. This is the closest group/dimension match, but the solver-derived observable is not a volume-uniform estimate of a Wilson loop family, a seed, or a gap. Abstract, methods, 4D result section and MOSEK discussion read.

**Li–Zhou (2024), “Bootstrapping the Abelian lattice gauge theories,” [arXiv:2404.17071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17071), DOI [10.1007/JHEP08(2024)154](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP08(2024)154).** Solver-based two-sided Wilson-loop bounds for Z₂ and U(1); 2D U(1) plaquette estimates are reported near 10^-8 precision at `Lmax=60` (GUIDE), and 3D Z₂/U(1) at `Lmax=16` (GUIDE). It demonstrates that positivity plus loop equations can be numerically constraining, while also showing the method is not inherently SU(2)-specific. It does not establish a 4D SU(2) bound or mass gap. Abstract and reported scope read.

**Guo–Li–Yang–Zhu (2025), “Bootstrapping SU(3) Lattice Yang–Mills Theory,” [arXiv:2502.14421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14421), DOI [10.1007/JHEP12(2025)033](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP12(2025)033).** Combines Hermitian/reflection positivity, single- and multiple-trace loop equations to compute plaquette bounds for SU(3) in d=2,3,4. Its twist-reflection positivity is proved exact in 2D; higher-dimensional bounds are computed by SDP solvers (the paper explicitly reports Mathematica/MOSEK solves). Couplings are sampled in displayed strong/weak/nonperturbative regimes; no hand-derived 4D order-one interval or correlation-length/mass-gap theorem is given. This is a different group and solver-backed bound. Abstract, 4D SU(2)/SU(3) comparison, and solver passages read.

**Liu–Yang (2026), “Direct and indirect loop equations in lattice Yang–Mills theory,” [arXiv:2601.04316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04316), DOI [10.1007/JHEP04(2026)107](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP04(2026)107).** Gives a geometric method to enumerate independent direct equations and “indirect” equations generated by elimination for SU(2) in d=2,3,4, with asymptotic counting and numerical/statistical growth analysis. It is bootstrap infrastructure, not a theorem bounding expectations or proving decay. No special-loop estimate or mass gap. Abstract and 4D equation-counting/results sections read.

**Lemoine (2026 preprint), “Universal dualities for Wilson loops in lattice Yang–Mills,” [arXiv:2604.16252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16252).** Gives a finite-N U(N) representation, for smooth central plaquette actions and d≥2 (GUIDE), in terms of decorated spanning surfaces, a spin-foam/channel model, and a coefficient-side master equation. This is exact structural information, not an estimate or a result for SU(2); it gives no gap or decay statement. Abstract and theorem overview read; preprint, not a journal result as of the search date.

## Special loop shapes

- **Figure-eight/self-intersections.** The finite-N identities of Jafarov and Shen–Smith–Zhu apply to arbitrary loop sequences and explicitly split/merge paths at repeated edges or vertices. Chatterjee’s and Chatterjee–Jafarov’s convergent string expansions also admit arbitrary loop sequences, but only in strong coupling and/or the large-N expansion. Ashtekar, Lewandowski, Marolf, Mourão and Thiemann (1996), [arXiv:hep-th/9605128](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9605128), give exact continuum Wilson-loop functionals for all piecewise-analytic loops for pure SU(N) on R² and R×S¹, at arbitrary coupling, satisfying their proposed gauge OS axioms; intersecting contours are included. This is a two-dimensional continuum solution, not a 4D lattice result. I found no standalone 4D SU(2) theorem for a figure-eight expectation at order-one coupling.
- **Clovers.** A clover observable is a finite linear combination of small plaquette loops (or their traces); it is included algebraically among loop observables, but I found no published special clover bound from loop equations plus positivity that yields a 4D SU(2) correlation or gap estimate. In particular, no clover-specific computation upgrades the solver bounds above to a hand theorem.
- **Double winding.** Ikeda–Kondo (2021), “Center group dominance in quark confinement,” [arXiv:2106.14416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.14416), DOI [10.1093/ptep/ptab114](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptep/ptab114), studies coplanar and transversely shifted double-winding loops. Its leading strong-coupling formulas give the difference-of-areas pattern for SU(2), max-of-areas for SU(3), and sum-of-areas for SU(N≥4) in the strong-coupling branch (GUIDE); for the shifted loop it extracts an exponential-in-separation leading term and labels a positive Δ(β) “mass gap.” The paper itself says that its explicit double-winding result is exact for all coupling only in D=2 and only strong-coupling for D>2; its 4D claim is therefore not a proved order-one gap. It uses character expansion/center-group arguments, not the positivity bootstrap. The source and its domain caveat read; the existing `gap-lit-grok.md` summary is reused.
- **General shapes via maps.** Cao–Park–Sheffield's exact map representation encompasses complicated and intersecting contours, but supplies no sign/coercivity estimate for the maps. It is an organizational formula, not a shape-specific decay theorem.

## Mass gap and fit to the present crux

The closest rigorous loop-equation use toward decay is an area-law estimate, not a mass-gap theorem: Osterwalder–Seiler in strong coupling, the SO(N) strong-coupling/large-N trajectory expansions, and Cao–Nissim–Sheffield's finite-N master-equation argument for U(N) at small β (plus the fixed-N strong-coupling extension). These constrain large rectangular Wilson-loop expectations. They do not establish volume-uniform exponential clustering for local gauge-invariant observables, the finite-size seed/vacuum-energy comparison in `gap-skeleton-final.md` §13.19, or a continuum correlation length at effective coupling of order one.

Kazakov–Zheng and later SU(3)/Abelian bootstrap papers report string-tension or glueball-mass estimates, but the estimate tables are solver products, not hand proofs. The bootstrap constraints themselves apply to U(1) and Z₂ too; a certificate intended to distinguish SU(2) must exploit a genuinely SU(2)-specific relation and still be proved without the solver. The exact SU(2) trace identity can rewrite a crossing product into a sum of two loop traces, but none of the reviewed works turns it into a volume comparison or a gap estimate. No reviewed loop-equation/shape result proves the 4D Wilson SU(2) order-one seed, fusion bound, or physical mass gap.
