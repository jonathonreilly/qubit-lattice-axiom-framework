## Answer to the first question

**A construction assessed by an external working group now reaches a four-dimensional Wilson-lattice continuum theory, but it does not establish a gap.** The group's assessment says it obtains a Wightman QFT as a scaling limit for any simple compact gauge group, while leaving uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit and a mass gap unproved. For a physical-scale gap criterion, I found no unconditional method that verifies (G_ev) or a periodic/full/kappa seed along that flow. The closest explicit proposals are conditional: Eriksson's terminal-scale spectral route assumes its RG/transfer inputs, and Bhattacharjee et al.'s one-cube Dobrushin–Shlosman condition would imply a continuum gap if it held uniformly along tuning, but that estimate is open.

Balaban's established multiscale construction controls the ultraviolet flow while the running coupling stays below a sufficiently small threshold; strong-coupling cluster expansions prove gaps in separate lattice regimes. They do not by themselves bridge to a physical-scale gap. The other results below either retain an infrared cutoff, use finite gauge groups, prove only a conditional implication, or concern another model/dimension. The recent papers/manuscripts are classified by their stated claims and read status, not adopted as proof. The Clay page continues to label the problem “Unsolved” [Clay status](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/yang-mills-the-maths-gap/).

## Results closest to the crux

### Balaban: the ultraviolet RG route

**Statement/model.** The Balaban series (GUIDE) constructs the multiscale renormalization of four-dimensional pure lattice Yang–Mills, first for SU(2) (GUIDE), on finite tori. The small-field analysis generates effective actions and renormalizes the coupling; the large-field R-operation controls the large-field contribution. The final ultraviolet-stability estimate bounds the effective density uniformly in lattice mesh and torus size, with volume-exponential factors under the theorem's RG hypotheses. The account summarized above describes the running trajectory only up to a small-coupling ceiling, not through a coupling of order one.

**Means and crux fit.** Other than M1–M4 (GUIDE): multiscale RG plus small-field expansion, large-field decomposition/R-operation, and polymer estimates. It is the closest established construction to the ultraviolet side of the requested continuum theory. It reaches none of (G_ev), the periodic seed, the full seed, or the kappa seed at the correlation-length scale after strong coupling. Its running-coupling control assumes the effective coupling remains in the small regime; the later order-one flow and gap estimate are additional obligations.

**Read status/source.** Part: the prior local read checked CMP 119 (GUIDE), Corollary 3, p. 264 (GUIDE), against the PDF; it also inspected the CMP 109 (GUIDE) small-field scope. I did not reread the whole series. T. Balaban, “Renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories. I. Generation of effective actions in a small field approximation and a coupling constant renormalization in four dimensions,” *CMP* 109 (GUIDE), 249–301 (GUIDE), 1987 (GUIDE); “Convergent renormalization expansions for lattice gauge theories,” *CMP* 119 (GUIDE), 243–285 (GUIDE), DOI [10.1007/BF01217741](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01217741) (GUIDE); “Large field renormalization I–II,” *CMP* 122 (GUIDE), 175–202 (GUIDE), 355–392 (GUIDE), 1989 (GUIDE). The existence of the complete series and its scope are also summarized in J. Dimock, [“The Renormalization Group According to Balaban”](https://www.nsm.buffalo.edu/~dimock/Rome.final.pdf), a survey, not a new gap theorem.

### Magnen–Rivasseau–Sénéor: fixed infrared cutoff

**Statement/model.** They give a basis for constructing Schwinger functions of pure four-dimensional SU(2) (GUIDE), in the trivial topological sector, with a fixed infrared cutoff and no ultraviolet cutoff, using a regularized axial gauge. At large field they use axial-gauge positivity; at small field they use a perturbative gauge depending on lower-momentum backgrounds. Their abstract says they nonperturbatively control the combined small-field integral and gauge-restoring counterterms for a suitable class of momentum cutoffs, and verify Slavnov identities.

**Means and crux fit.** Other than M1–M4 (GUIDE): gauge choice, background-field expansion and counterterm control. This does not remove the infrared cutoff and does not state a mass gap or any seed/(G_ev) estimate at the correlation length. No order-one running-coupling theorem is stated in the abstract.

**Read status/source.** Abstract and opening model discussion only; the full proof was not read. J. Magnen, V. Rivasseau and R. Sénéor, “Construction of YM4 with an infrared cutoff,” *CMP* 155 (GUIDE), 325–383 (GUIDE), DOI [10.1007/BF02097397](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02097397) (GUIDE).

### Federbush's phase-cell series

**Statement/model.** The series develops a phase-cell/block-spin description with successive lattice fields, small modes, averaging corrections and large “chunks.” The accessible part VI exhibits a one-to-one correspondence, under smoothness and fall-off assumptions, between sufficiently smooth continuum Yang–Mills configurations and compatible group-valued bond configurations on an infinite sequence of refining lattices; the lattice Wilson actions approach the continuum field/action in that classical configuration statement. This is not a probability-measure construction or mass-gap theorem.

**Means and crux fit.** Other than M1–M4 (GUIDE): phase-cell variables and a block-spin/lattice-continuum correspondence. No estimate of (G_ev), any seed, or a physical correlation-length gap is stated. The classical smooth-field correspondence does not control the quantum effective action through order-one coupling.

**Read status/source.** Part: Federbush's open part VI and abstract; the rest of the series was not read here. P. Federbush, “A Phase Cell Approach to Yang-Mills Theory VI: Non-Abelian Lattice-Continuum Duality,” *Ann. Inst. H. Poincaré A* 47 (GUIDE), 17–23 (GUIDE), 1987 (GUIDE), [open copy and bibliographic record](https://www.numdam.org/item/AIHPA_1987__47_1_17_0/). For the phase-cell large-field method, part IV is [CMP 114 (GUIDE), 317–343 (GUIDE)](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/communications-in-mathematical-physics/volume-114/issue-2/A-phase-cell-approach-to-Yang-Mills-theory-IV-The/cmp/1104160585.pdf) (GUIDE).

### Strong-coupling lattice gaps and confinement

**Statement/model.** Osterwalder–Seiler establish physical positivity/positive transfer matrix for Wilson lattice gauge theory and an infinite-volume strong-coupling phase with Wilson's confinement bound. More recently, Hao Shen, Rongchan Zhu and Xiangchan Zhu prove log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities, exponential correlation decay and a positive lattice gap for SU(N) (GUIDE) when the Wilson inverse-coupling parameter satisfies `|β| < 1/[16(d−1)]` (GUIDE), and for SO(N) (GUIDE) when `|β| < (N−2)/[32(d−1)N]` (GUIDE), in dimensions `d>1` (GUIDE). These are strong-coupling lattice ranges, not continuum limits.

**Means and crux fit.** M1 in the lattice strong-coupling regime: convergent character/cluster expansion or functional inequalities give exponential decay/gap. They verify an actual gap in their regime, but do not transport it across the weak-to-strong crossover in four-dimensional SU(2) (GUIDE), and say nothing about the requested seed at physical scale in the continuum trajectory.

**Read status/source.** Osterwalder–Seiler: publisher abstract; Shen–Zhu–Zhu: abstract and theorem statement recorded in `gap-lit.md`, not a new reread. K. Osterwalder and E. Seiler, “Gauge field theories on a lattice,” *Ann. Phys.* 110 (GUIDE), 440–471 (GUIDE), DOI [10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(78) (GUIDE)90039-8). H. Shen, R. Zhu and X. Zhu, “A stochastic analysis approach to lattice Yang–Mills at strong coupling,” arXiv:[2204.12737](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12737) (GUIDE).

### Chatterjee: conditional confinement and finite-group decay

**Statement/model.** “Yang-Mills for probabilists” presents the four-dimensional continuum construction, mass-gap and confinement questions as open problems, not as theorems. In later work, Chatterjee proves that a strong mass-gap condition—uniform exponential decay of truncated correlations for bounded local edge observables under every finite-volume boundary condition—implies unbroken center symmetry and, for nontrivial center, an area law for suitable Wilson loops. Separately, Adhikari–Cao prove uniform-in-box exponential covariance decay for observables built from separated Wilson loops in four-dimensional finite-group gauge theory when `β ≥ Δ_G⁻¹(114+4 log|G|)` (GUIDE), with `Δ_G` as defined in their paper.

**Means and crux fit.** Chatterjee's implication is downstream of the gap and so supplies no way to verify a seed or (G_ev). Adhikari–Cao use a finite-group weak-coupling/cluster mechanism and prove correlation decay in that different group class; it does not extend to compact Lie group SU(2) (GUIDE) by the argument stated. Neither crosses the SU(2) order-one regime.

**Read status/source.** Existing local reads: abstract+HTML for Chatterjee's survey and conditional theorem; abstract+HTML for Adhikari–Cao. S. Chatterjee, [“Yang-Mills for probabilists,” arXiv:1803.01950](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.01950) (GUIDE); [“A probabilistic mechanism for quark confinement,” arXiv:2006.16229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16229) (GUIDE). A. Adhikari and S. Cao, [arXiv:2202.10375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10375) (GUIDE). Exact theorem statements and limitations are recorded in `gap-lit-2.md`; they are not duplicated as new reading here.

### Tomboulis's all-coupling claim and its reply

**Statement/model.** Tomboulis claims confinement for four-dimensional SU(2) (GUIDE) lattice gauge theory at all `0<g<∞` (GUIDE), via approximate RG decimations and interpolation inequalities for vortex free energies. Ito–Seiler identify a missing inequality at large inverse coupling and show that the claimed implicit-function continuation can fail at an intermediate parameter; they conclude the proof has not been established, not that confinement is false.

**Means and crux fit.** It directly purports to cross the coupling range and might look like M2/M4 (GUIDE) via twist/vortex free energies. The disputed inequality is exactly the missing bridge; neither paper verifies a gap criterion at the physical correlation scale or proves a continuum mass gap. It cannot be used as a rigorous method for the present crux.

**Read status/source.** Existing local reads: abstract+HTML of both papers and reply context; not a line-by-line independent audit. E. T. Tomboulis, [arXiv:0707.2179](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2179) (GUIDE); K. R. Ito and E. Seiler, [arXiv:0711.4930](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4930) (GUIDE), with follow-ups 0712.2620 (GUIDE), 0803.3019 (GUIDE), 0901.4246 (GUIDE). The dispute and exact cited obstruction are summarized in `gap-lit-2.md`.

### Gawędzki–Kupiainen, Ito–Tamura and hierarchical sigma models

**Statement/model.** Gawędzki–Kupiainen rigorously construct a continuum limit of a hierarchical O(N) (GUIDE) nonlinear sigma model in two Euclidean dimensions (GUIDE), establishing asymptotic freedom and a weak-coupling Wilson RG flow. Ito–Tamura's work instead bounds the critical inverse temperature for the ordinary two-dimensional O(N) spin model at large N (GUIDE); their introduction explicitly says the corresponding real-system mass-generation theorem was not then proved. These are not four-dimensional gauge theories.

**Means and crux fit.** The hierarchical sigma-model construction is a genuine multiscale weak-coupling RG analogue; asymptotic freedom keeps effective interactions small at the steps where their expansion is used. That is not a demonstration that one can continue an ordinary gauge action into an order-one regime, nor an estimate of (G_ev) or the three seeds for Wilson SU(2) (GUIDE). The hierarchy and target space are essential differences. No finite-N O(N) spin result found here gives an SU(2) gauge bridge.

**Read status/source.** Part: open author copy for Gawędzki–Kupiainen's abstract and theorem page; abstract/introduction for Ito–Tamura. K. Gawędzki and A. Kupiainen, “Continuum limit of the hierarchical O(N) nonlinear sigma-model,” *CMP* 106 (GUIDE), 533–550 (GUIDE), 1986 (GUIDE), DOI [10.1007/BF01463394](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01463394) (GUIDE). K. R. Ito and H. Tamura, “N Dependence of Upper Bounds of Critical Temperatures of 2D O(N) Spin Models,” *CMP* 202 (GUIDE), 127–168 (GUIDE), DOI [10.1007/s002200050577](https://doi.org/10.1007/s002200050577) (GUIDE). I did not find an Ito–Tamura theorem proving a physical two-dimensional sigma-model mass gap.

### Bauerschmidt–Brydges–Slade: rigorous RG in other models

**Statement/model.** Their rigorous RG framework is applied to four-dimensional weakly self-avoiding walk and n-component |φ|⁴ (GUIDE) spin models at criticality. Theorems give critical two-point asymptotics and logarithmic susceptibility corrections; these are critical, algebraically decaying, non-gauge models, not pure Yang–Mills. Their book/primer develops the RG method for hierarchical |φ|⁴ (GUIDE) and explains extensions to Euclidean models.

**Means and crux fit.** Their nonperturbative RG coordinate and finite-range covariance decomposition control the weak-coupling flow and its remainders. The cited results establish critical scaling, not an SU(2) (GUIDE) order-one gap. Importing the method would require a gauge-covariant measure, reflection-positive block map, control of the infinitely many gauge-invariant interactions and a verified strong-coupling base/bridge. None is supplied by these papers.

**Read status/source.** Abstracts and author/principal-source pages only; no full proof reread. R. Bauerschmidt, D. C. Brydges and G. Slade, [arXiv:1403.7422](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7422) (GUIDE), [arXiv:1403.7268](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7268) (GUIDE), and [arXiv:1907.05474](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05474) (GUIDE). These are method analogies, not four-dimensional Yang–Mills results.

### Recent partial results outside the target theory

**Piazza–Shen, two-dimensional lattice gauge theory.** Their 2026 (GUIDE) preprint proves a volume-uniform logarithmic Sobolev inequality for two-dimensional SU(N) (GUIDE) Wilson lattice gauge theory at arbitrary fixed inverse coupling, with a constant depending on the coupling and group. The method is a two-scale disintegration argument. This gives functional control in two dimensions, but does not establish a transfer-matrix mass gap in four dimensions, nor continue a four-dimensional non-Abelian flow to order-one coupling. Abstract only; the inequality and constants were not independently checked. J. Piazza and H. Shen, [arXiv:2610.35286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35286) (GUIDE).

**Faria da Veiga–O’Carroll, charge-conjugation sectors.** Their 2025/26 (GUIDE) preprint treats four-dimensional Euclidean Wilson lattice SU(N) (GUIDE), with two charge-conjugation sectors for N≠2 (GUIDE) and one for SU(2) (GUIDE), and proves correlation identities plus an Elitzur-type statement. It describes a multiplicity-two one-particle glueball at small β for N≠2 (GUIDE) as an expectation, not a proved gap theorem. It gives neither a verified SU(2) order-one bridge nor any (G_ev)/seed estimate. Abstract only. J. Faria da Veiga and M. O’Carroll, [arXiv:2509.03513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03513) (GUIDE), version 2 (GUIDE), 24 January 2026 (GUIDE).

### Bhattacharjee et al.: strong-coupling gap and a conditional one-cube route

**Statement/model.** The current version claims, for four-dimensional Wilson lattice Yang–Mills with compact simple gauge group, uniqueness, exponential clustering and a transfer-matrix gap at strong coupling; for SU(2) (GUIDE) its plaquette-block theorem states `0 ≤ β_W ≤ 0.348` (GUIDE). Its central continuum implication is precise: a Dobrushin–Shlosman correlation/uniqueness estimate on one cube of fixed physical size, uniformly along continuum tuning, implies a positive physical spectral gap for every nontrivial continuum limit. The authors also claim block variables approach Haar measure at the coarsest scale on tori up to `exp(2^k/(17k))` (GUIDE) after `k` (GUIDE) blockings, and state that for SU(N) (GUIDE) the cube side must be at least of order `(β_W/log β_W)^(1/4)` (GUIDE). They explicitly say that the cube estimate along the entire tuning and existence of a nontrivial continuum limit remain open.

**Means/fit.** The cube condition is a direct correlation-inequality route (M4-like or a fifth criterion) using forest gauge and block averaging, and the closest found match to the requested physical-scale verifier. The paper states the implication, but does not verify its one-cube input uniformly on the Wilson continuum trajectory; its strong-coupling gap starts in a separate lattice regime. Its Haar-at-coarsest-scale result does not close the whole-tuning estimate. Thus it gives a conditional bridge and finite-lattice gap claims, not the SU(2) (GUIDE) order-one-to-continuum bridge required here. The paper reports Lean formalization and certified numerical work in earlier versions; I ran no computer checks and count none as proof evidence. The current version says constants are proved by hand; that proof was not independently checked.

**Read status/source.** Part: current version abstract/provenance and theorem statements, plus selected pages of version 4 (GUIDE) for the explicit SU(2) (GUIDE) range and cube-to-gap implication; no proof audit. D. Bhattacharjee, U. Bhattacharya, J. M. Lockwood and T. Romanov, [version 7 (GUIDE), “One Cube Estimate Implies the Yang–Mills Mass Gap”](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00341v7) (no arXiv identifier stated); [Zenodo formalization release DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23992883 (GUIDE)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23992883). ### Eriksson: conditional weak-coupling lattice gap reduction

**Statement/model.** The current author page describes a conditional reduction for four-dimensional SU(N_c) (GUIDE) Wilson lattice Yang–Mills at sufficiently weak bare coupling: under four explicit hypotheses it claims a lattice gap at least `c(N_c) exp(−C(N_c)/g²)` (GUIDE), uniform on lattice sizes up to `C_0 exp(C/g²)` (GUIDE). The method combines Balaban RG to a terminal scale where the effective coupling is order one, a Witten-Laplacian/Holley–Stroock spectral estimate there, and a transfer-matrix trace identity. The published vixra paper states the key RG/transfer-matrix compatibility assumption explicitly; the current author page records additional necessary hypotheses and subsequent corrections, including withdrawal of an earlier unconditional-closure claim.

**Means/fit.** This is a proposed M2 (GUIDE)-style coupling bridge followed by M1 (GUIDE) at the terminal scale, but only conditional on its named inputs. The endpoint becomes order one only after assuming the RG flow and transfer factorization; the result is lattice-unit uniformity on a bounded volume window, not a physical-scale continuum gap. It neither proves the continuum-limit gap nor verifies (G_ev) or a seed along the physical-correlation-length trajectory. No computation was run or used to discharge a step. Later corrections in the same author programme withdraw earlier claims that the compatibility/large-field and Dobrushin-type inputs were discharged; the current public account keeps them as hypotheses.

The same author’s companion “From Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequality to Mass Gap for Lattice Yang–Mills at Weak Coupling” states a volume-uniform transfer gap for SU(N_c) (GUIDE), `d≥3` (GUIDE), at sufficiently large inverse coupling, via a periodic-torus LSI, frozen-boundary DLR-LSI, mixing and reflection positivity. The current author record makes the status conditional on cross-scale derivative and Dobrushin-type hypotheses (or another valid mixing route); neither is proved in that manuscript. This remains a lattice result, with no continuum-limit gap. Read status: abstract and author-page correction only. [vixra 2602.0054 (GUIDE)](https://www.ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0054v1.pdf); current [author-page status](https://airr.science/authors/lluis-eriksson/) (GUIDE).

**Read status/source.** Part: read the six-page vixra source abstract and Theorem 1.1 plus the current author-page summary/corrections; not an independent proof audit. L. Eriksson, [“The Yang–Mills Mass Gap on the Lattice: A Conditional Reduction via Witten Laplacian and Constructive Renormalization,” vixra 2602.0033 (GUIDE)](https://ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0033v1.pdf) (GUIDE); companion [vixra 2602.0054 (GUIDE)](https://www.ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0054v1.pdf) (abstract only); current conditional status and corrections at [AIRR.SCIENCE author page](https://airr.science/authors/lluis-eriksson/) (GUIDE). ## Other relevant boundaries

- **Gross–Witten large-N transition.** The paper suggests a weak-to-strong third-order transition in four-dimensional large-N lattice gauge theory; it does not prove a mass gap, confinement for SU(2) (GUIDE), or a continuum theorem. - **Weak-coupling area law for U(N).** Cao, Nissim and Sheffield prove an area-law bound for pure U(N) (GUIDE) lattice Yang–Mills in every dimension `d≥2` (GUIDE) when `β≤β_0(d)` (GUIDE), with `β_0(d)>0` (GUIDE) independent of N.
