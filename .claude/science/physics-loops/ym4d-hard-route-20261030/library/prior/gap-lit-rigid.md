## Findings

### The closest qualitative theorem: gapped 4D gauge phases and light fluxes

**Mendel Nguyen, Yuya Tanizaki, Mithat Ünsal (2023), “Study of gapped phases of 4d gauge theories using temporal gauging of the Z_N 1-form symmetry.”** The paper considers a 4D QFT with a ℤ_N one-form symmetry. Assuming a mass gap and Lorentz-invariant vacuum, temporal gauging gives a 3D theory with paired electric/magnetic one-form symmetries; its unbroken subgroup is order N and mutually local. In the T⁴ appendix, Euclidean rotation gives a finite Fourier self-duality identity for the ’t Hooft partition function. Under the separate assumption that every normalized flux-sector partition-function ratio tends to either 0 or 1 in the large-box limit, for each fixed third electric and magnetic flux pair there are either exactly N² or no light transverse flux pairs among N⁴ possibilities, and any two light pairs have zero symplectic pairing. Thus, for N=2, a nonempty fixed slice has four light pairs among sixteen and they are isotropic. The proof uses positivity and the Fourier duality.

This is the same *qualitative asymptotic* 0/1 and isotropic light-flux pattern that motivates Theorem R. It is not Theorem R's finite quantitative implication from the two smallness tests, its near-zero/near-one bounds, or its equivalence among the three seeds. Most importantly, the binary-ratio property is not proved for general gapped 4D topological states: the authors explicitly call it a technical assumption and say proving or disproving it generally remains open. The result is framed for 4D QFT with ℤ_N one-form symmetry and gives the conditional pattern for SU(2) when N=2; it does not establish a mass gap, the binary limit, or the finite estimate for 4D Wilson SU(2).

Read status: full open-access article, with the abstract, main text around temporal gauging/positivity, and Appendix A read (OSTI-rendered PDF, especially its pp. 4–5 and 19–21). e-print **arXiv:2306.02485**, DOI **10.1007/JHEP08(2023)013**. [Open arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02485); [open full-text PDF](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/2418982).

The same paper's §2.3 is a second close result: a strong-coupling surface expansion of the **4D lattice SU(N) Wilson action** with a background ℤ_N two-form field. The ’t Hooft partition function localizes to zero temporal electric flux in the large spatial-size limit, with corrections written as exponentially suppressed string-worldsheet contributions; this gives the confining electric-flux pattern. This is a strong-coupling expansion/asymptotic calculation in a lattice model, not an all-coupling theorem, not a proof of finite-size Theorem R, and it does not use the periodic-box additivity defect or classify all crossed SU(2) weights by a finite rigidity lemma. It does directly apply to the Wilson-action family at strong coupling, but not to the desired continuum/mass-gap conclusion. Read status: full article as above, §2.3 pp. 7–8 read. Same identifier and URLs.

### Foundational flux duality and confinement classification

**G. ’t Hooft (1979), “A property of electric and magnetic flux in non-Abelian gauge theories.”** For pure SU(N) on a periodic finite-temperature box, defines gauge-invariant electric/magnetic flux and obtains an exact free-energy duality from Euclidean invariance; the paper's abstract says that for N≤3 electric and magnetic confinement cannot both hold. This is the source of the torus duality used in the modern derivation, not a finite quantitative near-binary theorem and not by itself a proof that a massive phase has a binary flux partition function or a Lagrangian light set. Model/group: non-Abelian gauge theory, including SU(2), on periodic box; read status **abstract only** (publisher/index metadata, no full article text relied on). DOI **10.1016/0550-3213(79)90595-9**. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(79)90595-9); [INSPIRE record](https://inspirehep.net/literature/141136).

**G. ’t Hooft (1981), “Topology of the gauge condition and new confinement phases in non-Abelian gauge theories.”** Develops abelianization, magnetic monopole/electric dyon charges, and oblique-confinement phases governed by the theta angle. This supplies the Wilson–’t Hooft phase-classification background, but its abstract-level statement is not a finite-volume theorem on sixteen crossed weights, seed tests, or box-energy defects. Model/group: non-Abelian SU(N) gauge theories; read status **abstract only**. DOI **10.1016/0550-3213(81)90442-9**. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90442-9); [INSPIRE record](https://inspirehep.net/literature/166006).

### Rigorous relations involving twists, flux free energies, and spectra

**T. Tomboulis and L. G. Yaffe (1985), “Finite temperature SU(2) lattice gauge theory.”** This is a rigorous finite-temperature lattice SU(2) source in the cited line of work. Its abstract proves absence of confinement at sufficiently high temperature in the pure gauge theory. The practical inequality relating center-vortex/twist free energy to Wilson-loop bounds is stated in generalized form and with its hypotheses by Kanazawa (below); it controls Wilson loops through an electric-flux/twist partition-function quantity. It does not give a vacuum energy additivity defect or classify all crossed fluxes. Model: finite-temperature SU(2) lattice gauge theory; supports the flux/twist-to-confinement route, not finite Theorem R. Read status: **abstract only**, with the generalized inequality read in Kanazawa. DOI **10.1007/BF01206134**. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01206134); [open copy](https://scispace.com/pdf/finite-temperature-su-2-lattice-gauge-theory-530spmhqyk.pdf).

**C. Borgs and E. Seiler (1983), “Lattice Yang–Mills theory at nonzero temperature and the confinement problem.”** The paper studies finite-temperature lattice Yang–Mills, clarifies relations between confinement criteria based on Wilson, Polyakov, and ’t Hooft loops by string-tension inequalities, and proves a high-temperature deconfining phase in spatial dimension at least three (the abstract's scope). It supplies rigorous relations among finite-temperature confinement diagnostics, but not a crossed electric/magnetic light-set rigidity theorem or periodic-box vacuum-energy defect identity. Read status: **abstract plus related statement in Kanazawa**; not full paper. DOI **10.1007/BF01208780**. [Author/institution record](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/lattice-yang-mills-theory-nonzero-temperature-confinement-problem/); [bibliographic record with DOI](https://pascal-francis.inist.fr/vibad/index.php?action=getRecordDetail&idt=9401403).

**Takuya Kanazawa (2009), “Generalizing the Tomboulis–Yaffe Inequality to SU(N) Lattice Gauge Theories and General Classical Spin Systems.”** For SU(2), Theorem 1 treats a finite d-dimensional periodic hypercubic lattice, a Wilson one-plaquette action, a center-twisted partition function `Z^-_Λ`, and a rectangular fundamental Wilson loop `W(C)` of area `A_C`. It proves

`|<W(C)>| ≤ 2 { 1/2 (1 − Z^-_Λ/Z_Λ) }^(A_C/(L_μ L_ν))`.

The reflection-positivity proof gives a rigorous twist-free-energy-to-Wilson-loop bound; Theorem 2 generalizes the inequality to SU(N), with an electric-flux projection formulation. It does not link twist free energy to periodic-box vacuum-energy additivity defect, and does not infer a Lagrangian group or Theorem R's seed equivalence. It includes SU(2) Wilson gauge theory in any stated lattice dimension, but gives no four-dimensional mass-gap result. Read status: **partial full-text read**: theorem and proof, setup and conclusion. e-print **arXiv:0808.3442**, Ann. Phys. 324 (2009) 1634, DOI **10.1016/j.aop.2009.06.009**. [Full arXiv text](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3442); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aop.2009.06.009).

**M. Lüscher (1983), “Some analytic results concerning the mass spectrum of Yang–Mills gauge theories on a torus.”** For weak-coupling continuum Yang–Mills quantized on a small spatial T³, constructs the perturbative spectrum around classical vacua and treats electric-flux sectors; central sectors have degeneracies to all orders of perturbation theory. The article explicitly restricts to periodic connections and excludes twisted sectors. This is spectral/weak-coupling information on a finite torus, not a nonperturbative comparison of twist free energies, not a vacuum-energy additivity defect, and not the light/heavy crossed-sector pattern. Not applicable to establishing a gap for Wilson SU(2) at the coupling relevant to the task. Read status: **partial full PDF**; setup, electric-sector discussion, perturbative degeneracy result, and conclusion read. DOI **10.1016/0550-3213(83)90436-4**. [Open full-text copy](https://infomall.org/sites/dsc/jpac/QCDRef/1980s/SOME%20ANALYTIC%20RESULTS%20CONCERNING%20THE%20MASS%20SPECTRUM%20OF%20YANG-MILLS%20GAUGE%20THEORIES%20ON%20A%20TORUS%20-%20Luscher%20-%201983.pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(83)90436-4).

**What I found on the box-energy question:** the exact bridge present in Nguyen–Tanizaki–Ünsal is the trace/projector representation of the ’t Hooft partition function and its finite-torus Fourier/rotation duality. Kanazawa and Borgs–Seiler give twist/electric-flux-to-loop or confinement inequalities. Lüscher gives a separate perturbative finite-torus spectrum. None of the read sources states a rigorous general identity or bound taking a center-twist free energy to the additivity defect of periodic-box vacuum energies in a massive phase. The lane's `delta` smallness criterion in `gap-means-max.md` remains a lane construction, not a known literature equivalence found here. A broad local-massive-spin-system result on insensitivity to U(1)-twisted boundary conditions exists, but it is not a Yang–Mills flux-sector or Casimir/additivity theorem (listed below).

### Condensed-matter and exactly solvable lattice results

**Gaiotto, Kapustin, Seiberg, Willett (2015), “Generalized Global Symmetries.”** Establishes the language and structural framework of higher-form symmetry and its charged extended operators, anomalies, and gauging. It is foundational context for center one-form symmetry, but is not a theorem that a given lattice gauge model has a gapped phase with a specified light-flux set. Read status: **abstract/metadata and cited use only**. e-print **arXiv:1412.5148**, DOI **10.1007/JHEP02(2015)172**. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.5148); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP02(2015)172).

**Kitaev–Kong (2012), “Models for gapped boundaries and domain walls.”** Gives lattice realizations of gapped boundaries/domain walls for 2D topological phases based on a unitary tensor category and module categories. **Levin (2013), “Boundary degeneracy of topological order,”** applies boundary theory to toric-code/double-semion and related abelian models. These support electric/magnetic condensation and boundary classification in 2+1D topological order. They concern boundary excitations and boundary degeneracy, not a 3+1D SU(2) finite-torus crossed-flux theorem or a bulk vacuum-energy defect. Read status: **abstracts only**. Identifiers: Kitaev–Kong **arXiv:1104.5047**, DOI **10.1007/s00220-012-1503-8**; Levin **arXiv:1212.4863**, DOI **10.1103/PhysRevX.3.021009**. [Kitaev–Kong arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.5047); [Levin arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.4863).

**Exact toric-code/quantum-double examples.** Kitaev's ℤ₂ toric code is an exactly solvable 2D lattice gauge/topological-order model with four ground states on the spatial torus; its electric and magnetic noncontractible loop sectors are the archetype behind electric/magnetic condensation. More generally, quantum-double models have topology-dependent torus ground spaces. This is an exact result for those commuting-projector models, not a classification theorem saying every gapped ℤ₂ gauge theory has a unique light-flux Lagrangian subgroup, and not a 4D Wilson SU(2) result. Read status: **standard result checked in open teaching/review material; Kitaev original not read**. A primary source is A. Kitaev, “Fault-tolerant quantum computation by anyons,” **arXiv:quant-ph/9707021**, DOI **10.1016/S0003-4916(02)00018-0**. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9707021); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0003-4916(02)00018-0).

**Bravyi, Hastings, Michalakis (2010), “Topological quantum order: stability under local perturbations.”** Proves stability of a low-energy band and gap under weak local perturbations for geometrically local commuting-projector quantum-spin Hamiltonians satisfying their TQO conditions. This is rigorous stability of specified topological order, not a classification by flux sectors and not a derivation of the SU(2) Wilson flux weights. The hypotheses/model class do not cover continuum Yang–Mills or its Wilson lattice action as stated. Read status: **abstract and theorem setup only**. e-print **arXiv:1001.0344**, DOI **10.1063/1.3490195**. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0344); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.3490195).

**Córdova, Costa, Hsin (2025), “Non-invertible symmetries in finite-group gauge theory.”** Builds lattice topological operators, gapped boundaries/domain walls, and electric-magnetic duality examples for finite-group gauge theories across spacetime dimensions, including 3+1D ℤ₂ gauge theory. It gives exact lattice constructions and symmetry/defect algebra in topological finite-group models, not a dynamical classification of SU(2) Wilson flux-sector free energies by the additivity defect. Read status: **partial full PDF** (abstract, summary, selected lattice-construction sections). DOI **10.21468/SciPostPhys.18.1.019**. [Open full text](https://www.scipost.org/SciPostPhys.18.1.019/pdf); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.21468/SciPostPhys.18.1.019).

**Haruki Watanabe (2018), “Insensitivity of bulk properties to the twisted boundary condition.”** In a gapped finite-range U(1)-symmetric quantum-spin Hamiltonian, proves that local bulk observables, ground-state energy, excitation gap, and specified responses become insensitive to the U(1) boundary twist as system size grows; the paper states exponential suppression in the linear system size. This is a rigorous twist/ground-energy relation in a different setting. The boundary twist is a symmetry holonomy for a spin Hamiltonian, not a Yang–Mills center electric/magnetic flux sector; it does not bound box-energy additivity defects or classify crossed sectors. Read status: **abstract and opening setup**. e-print **arXiv:1803.06558**. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.06558).

I did not find a rigorous lattice theorem of Theorem R's shape on the condensed-matter side: exact models realize these patterns, and boundary/anyon theory classifies allowed condensates under its hypotheses, but that does not prove the finite SU(2) rigidity implication.

### Result for this path

**Answer to question 1:** the qualitative gapped-phase conclusion is already in ’t Hooft's duality argument as made explicit by Nguyen–Tanizaki–Ünsal: assuming the normalized flux ratios become binary, the light set on each fixed slice is empty or a maximal isotropic set (for SU(2), four among sixteen). The modern paper's appendix proves this conditional consequence; it expressly leaves the general binary-limit assumption unresolved. Thus the prior literature covers the *asymptotic qualitative shape conditionally*, while the lane's finite quantitative Theorem R appears to be a distinct result in this searched corpus. This makes the massive-phase sentence in the lane more than an unqualified recollection, but does not validate it without the missing hypothesis.

**Answer to question 2:** there are rigorous flux/twist-to-Wilson-loop or confinement inequalities, exact trace/projector and Fourier-duality relations, and perturbative finite-torus spectral results. I found no rigorous theorem identifying or bounding a center-twist free energy by the periodic-box vacuum-energy additivity defect in a general gapped phase. Strong-coupling surface expansions and local twist-insensitivity results are narrower and do not supply that missing link.

**Answer to question 3:** 2+1D toric-code/quantum-double and boundary/anyon theories rigorously realize and classify condensation patterns (with torus degeneracy and higher-form symmetry descriptions in their model classes). They are exact lattice models or topological effective theories, not a theorem for 4D Wilson SU(2) or the finite quantitative Theorem R. I found no condensed-matter lattice theorem of the same statement and hypotheses.

**Forward citation stopping rule:** the first round after the 2023 paper found the 2025 direct Monte Carlo sector computation; the next found its 2026 extension. These added measured SU(2) flux patterns and numerical caveats only. No new relevant rigorous statement appeared in those two rounds, so the forward search stopped there.

RESULT: Prior literature states the qualitative gapped-phase light-flux/Lagrangian pattern conditionally on binary asymptotic sector ratios; the finite quantitative Theorem R implication from even seed plus vacuum-energy defect, and its seed equivalence, were not found in the scoped literature search.
