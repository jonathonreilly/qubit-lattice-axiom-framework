## Findings

### A. Variational and relative-entropy bounds

**Gibbs/Donsker–Varadhan variational identity.** For a finite-volume reference law `mu` and bounded action difference `X`, the Gibbs variational principle gives `F = inf_nu { E_nu X + D(nu||mu) }`, with equality at the twisted Gibbs law. The exact action-plus-entropy identity in row 789, (E21), is this standard identity specialized to the untwisted and twisted Wilson laws. It is a proved measure identity, but unrestricted optimization chooses the unknown twisted law itself. It gives no explicit trial law, no small upper bound, and no `TR-F` construction. The generic principle does not itself supply (26). See the target's own derivation (E21)–(E23); that derivation is the closest exact match.

For the row-789 mixture of Haar-preserving maps, the entropy chain rule gives exactly `C(T,pi)=F+D(nu||mu')`. That construction is already derived in `gap-ext-astra.md`; it is not an independent literature result. No paper located gives a Wilson-action map family with this cost bounded by `TR-F`, or directly controls the weighted exterior mean in (26).

**Jarzynski et al., “Jarzynski's theorem for lattice gauge theory,” arXiv:1604.05544 (2016).** The paper applies the nonequilibrium-work identity to interface free energies and the equation of state. Its interface-free-energy demonstration is Monte Carlo in the three-dimensional `Z_2` gauge model, with periodic-to-antiperiodic boundary changes. The identity is exact; the reported interface values and algorithm are numerical. It shows that a prescribed path can encode a free-energy difference, but does not prove a path-independent upper bound, does not treat the 4D SU(2) vortex ratio in (26), and supplies neither `TR-F` nor (26). [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.05544).

### B. Thick vortices, twists, and group dependence

**Mack and Petkova, “Comparison of lattice gauge theories with gauge groups Z2 and SU(2),” Ann. Phys. 123 (1979), 442–467, DOI 10.1016/0003-4916(79)90346-4; Mack, “Properties of lattice gauge theory models at low temperatures,” DESY 80/03 (1980), §§8–9.** The article abstract states an inequality between SU(2) and restricted Z2 Wilson-loop expectations: confinement of static quarks in the corresponding Z2 model implies confinement in their SU(2) model. In the scanned §8, inequality (8.6) bounds a Wilson character by the maximum Fourier coefficient of the distribution of a vortex-container partition-function ratio; the text says confinement follows if vortex free energy decreases sufficiently rapidly as container thickness grows. The paper explicitly motivates thick vortices because continuous-group vortex plaquettes need not themselves equal a center element. This is a rigorous sufficient criterion within the paper's stated models and hypotheses, not an unconditional estimate of the torus twist ratio, and not a `TR-F` transport-map construction. Its §9 strong-coupling discussion is a high-temperature expansion, not an order-one-coupling estimate. [1979 article record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(79)90346-4), [DESY scan](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/scan/1980/desy80-003.kek.pdf).

**’t Hooft, “On the phase transition towards permanent quark confinement,” Nucl. Phys. B 138 (1978), 1–25, DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(78)90153-0.** The paper derives phase consequences from the algebra of topological and gauge-field operators and argues that permanent confinement is one allowed phase of a 3+1D non-Abelian theory. The vortex/free-energy criterion later called the ’t Hooft criterion is conditional: exponential suppression of the wrapped center-vortex free-energy ratio with transverse area is sufficient for an area law. It is not a proof that this suppression occurs for 4D SU(2) Wilson theory at order-one or every coupling. It gives the form of a target like (26), not its estimate. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(78)90153-0).

**Tomboulis–Yaffe, “Finite temperature SU(2) lattice gauge theory,” Commun. Math. Phys. 100 (1985), 313–341, DOI 10.1007/BF01206134; explicit extension by Kanazawa, arXiv:0808.3442 (2008).** For periodic SU(2) lattices, the TY inequality bounds a fundamental Wilson loop by `2 { (1/2)(1 - Z^(-)_Lambda/Z_Lambda) }^(A_C/(L_mu L_nu))`. Kanazawa proves the corresponding reflection-positivity inequality for SU(N), and for a broad class of classical spin systems. Thus a suitable *small twist deficit* yields a Wilson-loop area law. This is a consumer inequality: it does not upper-bound the twist free energy or provide the exponential smallness assumed by ’t Hooft. It supplies no `TR-F` and does not close (26). [Kanazawa source and theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3442); [TY DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01206134).

**Mack–Petkova and continuous versus finite groups.** The literature supports the thick-vortex picture and comparison inequalities, but I found no published quantitative entropy-versus-action theorem that distinguishes SU(2) from a finite subgroup in the exact Wilson-map sense of (TR-F). Algebraically, U(1) is Abelian, so every group commutator is the identity; a commutator-spreading mechanism cannot furnish a nontrivial U(1) twist. For finite groups, any fixed nonidentity group element has positive distance from the identity in a chosen faithful matrix norm, but that elementary discreteness fact is not an entropy estimate. Row 789's exact `[A_N,B_N]=exp(i pi sigma_3/N)` sequence and its finite-subgroup/U(1) tests are the route note's own construction; the literature search did not find this sequence used to prove a lattice twist bound. No inference from the continuum vortex pictures supplies its correlated Wilson-law cost.

**Kovács–Tomboulis, “Vortices and confinement at weak coupling,” arXiv:hep-lat/9711009 (1997); Kovács–Tomboulis, Phys. Rev. Lett. 85 (2000), 704, arXiv:hep-lat/0002004.** The 1997 abstract reports numerical simulations in SU(2) supporting thick-vortex dominance at weak coupling and states that excluding linked vortices removes the confining potential. The 2000 paper reports the first weak-coupling measurement of the vortex free-energy order parameter. These are numerical results and physical evidence, not proofs. They supply neither the all-frame weighted mean (26) nor a bound on map action minus information, hence neither requested estimate. [1997 source](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9711009); [2000 source](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0002004).

**Cornwall, “Relativistic center-vortex dynamics of a confining area law,” Phys. Rev. D 69 (2004), 065019, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.69.065019.** The abstract describes a “physicist’s proof” that the confining Wilson-loop area is extremal and relates contour forms to Abelian potentials. This is a continuum model/argument, not a rigorous lattice estimate for SU(2) or SO(3) twisted partition functions. It supplies neither (TR-F) nor (26). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.69.065019).

### C. Entropy versus energy and coupling regimes

The clearest rigorous energy/entropy competition located is the convergent **strong-coupling cluster expansion** below. The thick-vortex papers provide conditional comparison/criterion results; the weak-coupling thick-vortex evidence is numerical. I found no theorem that estimates the entropy of the row-789 correlated maps against their Wilson action cost at order-one coupling. General relative entropy alone is an exact variational representation, with the minimizer equal to the unknown twisted law; it does not establish the needed cost balance.

**Münster, “High-temperature expansions for the free energy of vortices and the string tension in lattice gauge theories,” Nucl. Phys. B 180 (1981), 23–60, DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(81)90153-X.** The paper states that it derives high-temperature cluster expansions for vortex free energies in SU(2) and Z2 lattice gauge theories in 3 and 4 dimensions, verifies area-law behavior, and identifies the area coefficient with the static-quark string tension. A contemporaneous statement records for pure 4D SU(2), in the high-temperature/strong-coupling regime, the asymptotic form proportional to `a^-2 exp(-alpha d1 d2)`, with `alpha` the string tension, to all orders of the expansion. Kanazawa later describes Münster's result as verified by the *convergent* strong-coupling cluster expansion. This is the positive rigorous hit for exponential-in-transverse-area vortex suppression, in its strong-coupling convergence regime. It may supply an analogue of (26) there when the twist definition and geometry are matched; it does not supply `TR-F`, the order-one beta interval, uniformity in the route's frames, or the claimed weak-coupling/continuum regime. The accessible abstract does not state an explicit convergence endpoint in beta, so none is asserted here. [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90153-X); [contemporaneous 4D statement](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/postpr/1981/desy81-050.pdf).

**Tomboulis, “Confinement for all values of the coupling in four-dimensional SU(2) gauge theory,” arXiv:0707.2179 (2007).** This paper claims a derivation of confining vortex free-energy behavior for SU(2), `0 < g < infinity`, in `d <= 4`, by potential-moving decimation and matching to strong coupling. If valid, it would imply the broad conclusion sought in (d), but the claimed proof is contested and cannot be treated here as an established theorem. Ito and Seiler, arXiv:0711.4930, identify a needed inequality/implicit-function step they say is not proved; Tomboulis replies in arXiv:0712.2620 that the inequality is used only in the strong-coupling region. Kanazawa's 2009 paper, arXiv:0805.2742, derives a different partition-function relation and argues that Tomboulis's interpolation argument has gaps, including an unproved existence of a matching parameter and unsupported flow behavior. These papers directly concern a route to (26), but the disputed claim does not provide an uncontested accepted supplier. Read the original and criticism before using any detailed verdict. [Tomboulis](https://arxiv.org/abs/0707.2179), [Ito–Seiler](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4930), [Tomboulis reply](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.2620), [Kanazawa](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2742).

**Kanazawa, “A theorem concerning twisted and untwisted partition functions in U(N) and SU(N) lattice gauge theories,” Phys. Lett. B 670 (2009), 421–427, arXiv:0805.2742.** Theorem 1 is an exact relation between a weighted sum of twisted partition functions and an untwisted partition function on a coarser lattice, with a small coupling-parameter mismatch. It applies to SU(N) and U(N), without a confinement assumption. The author says it does not incorporate the dynamics needed to prove confinement; the theorem works for U(1) as well. This is useful bookkeeping for ratios under blocking, not decay of an individual twist ratio, and it gives no (TR-F) or (26).

For SO(3), de Forcrand and Jahn, arXiv:hep-lat/0209060, measure electric-twist free energies in SU(2) and SO(3) and use them as transition observables. The paper is numerical. It does not prove exponential transverse-area decay for either group or give a correlated-map estimate. [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0209060).

For finite groups, Kanazawa's TY extension includes SU(N) and general spin models, but its conclusion is the implication from twist sensitivity to loop decay, not a small twist cost. Münster's strong-coupling expansion covers Z2, not the binary icosahedral subgroup. I found no theorem that supplies the exact finite-subgroup comparison needed to derive (26) or `TR-F`; row 789's binary-icosahedral action-gap calculation remains the binding counter-test for a proposed route.

## Disposition for row 789

1. **Positive theorem, restricted regime:** Münster's convergent high-temperature/strong-coupling expansion gives exponential-in-area vortex free-energy behavior for SU(2) in 3D and 4D (and Z2), with string-tension coefficient. It does not reach the route's order-one coupling interval or furnish its transport maps.
2. **Exact consumers/identities:** Gibbs/DV and Jarzynski identities express free-energy differences variationally or through work. They do not give the needed explicit low-cost correlated Wilson-variable maps. TY and Mack–Petkova inequalities use supplied twist/vortex control to infer Wilson-loop confinement; they do not prove that control.
3. **Weak-coupling vortex literature:** the cited SU(2) studies are simulations or conditional physical models. They are guidance only.
4. **All-coupling claim:** Tomboulis's 4D SU(2) proof claim would be highly relevant if validated. The published exchanges dispute essential steps, so it is not used as a proof of (26).
5. **Small commutator:** no located publication uses the exact sequence `[A_N,B_N]=exp(i pi sigma_3/N)` to bound a Wilson twist free energy. U(1)'s commutator is identically trivial; finite-group discreteness blocks a sequence of nontrivial elements tending to identity. These are algebraic comparisons, not a published entropy-cost estimate.

The literature search therefore does not close the row-789 gap. Its only rigorous exponential-area supplier is in the strong-coupling regime, outside the needed order-one window. The first missing item remains the route's own: an explicit Wilson-weighted correlated family satisfying (TR-F), or a direct proof of (26) on the required compact beta interval and in every required frame.
