### Shenfeld: exact renormalization groups and transport

**Primary source:** Yair Shenfeld, “Exact renormalization groups and transportation of measures,” arXiv:2205.01642, revised 2026-07-02. [Open arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.01642). The main body was read through the PDF text view at §§1.2–1.6, 2.1–2.2, and the Brownian-map discussion in §3 (PDF pp. 2–6, 10–11, 13); local retrieval was attempted but `curl` failed at DNS resolution.

**Exact statement used here.** For a smooth finite-dimensional scalar-field model `ν^{ε,L}(dφ)=e^{-V_0(φ)}γ^{ε,L}(dφ)` on the periodic lattice approximation `Λ_{ε,L}⊂R^d`, where `γ^{ε,L}` is the massive Gaussian free field with covariance `A_ε^{-1}`, define `Q_t=e^{-tA_ε/2}` and the renormalized potential `V_t(φ)=-log E_{C_t}[e^{-V_0(φ+ζ)}]` with `C_t=∫_0^t e^{-sA_ε}ds`. Definition 1.2 requires, for every `t≥0` and every field `φ`, a real (possibly negative) `λ̇_t` with `Q_t Hess(V_t)(φ) Q_t ⪰ λ̇_t I`. The unnumbered Main Theorem in §1.6 then gives a transport `T_{ε,L}` pushing `γ^{ε,L}` to `ν^{ε,L}` with Lipschitz constant `exp(-½∫_0^∞ λ̇_t dt)` (PDF p. 5, lines 315–343 in the arXiv extraction). The finite constant requires that integral to define a finite bound. Poincaré/LSI transfer follows from the reference Gaussian inequality and the map's Lipschitz constant; it does not itself state a transfer-eigenvalue action bound.

**Brownian map qualification.** Shenfeld's §3 identifies the special case `τ=1`, `Ċ_t=I` with the Brownian transport map of Mikulincer–Shenfeld and says that work proves Lipschitz control using a multiscale Bakry–Émery input. Shenfeld explicitly leaves the extension to general `Ċ_t` and incorporation of the multiscale criteria for future work (§3, PDF pp. 12–13). The theorem Shenfeld proves in this note is the finite-dimensional Langevin/Ornstein–Uhlenbeck map above, not a theorem for compact group-valued Gibbs fields.

**Fit to lattice SU(2).** No direct application: the measure is a smooth density on a real vector space relative to a Gaussian, and its key input is a uniform-in-field Hessian lower bound along the Polchinski covariance flow. A lattice SU(2) version would need a well-defined group-valued reference and flow on products of compact links (with gauge symmetry handled), a corresponding curvature/Hessian criterion and a uniform Lipschitz bound over volume and coupling. To reach (VIR-log)/(SUPER-D), it would also need a separate transfer-operator argument that converts transport control into a level-versus-vacuum magnetic-action bound with a signed remainder. Shenfeld's statement supplies none of these identifications. **Disposition: GUIDE, not a supplier.**

**Brownian-map primary text cited by Shenfeld:** Dan Mikulincer and Yair Shenfeld, “The Brownian transport map,” arXiv:2111.11521v4 (2026-08-27 version). [Open arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.11521). Its target is a probability measure `p` on Euclidean `R^d`; `X_1` is the endpoint of the Föllmer process, a Wiener-space map pushing Wiener measure to `p` (eq. (1.2), §1). Theorem 1.1 assumes `p` is `κ`-log-concave, meaning it has a smooth density with `−Hess log(dp/dx) ⪰ κ I` on the interior of its support, and writes `S=diam(supp p)`. It gives an almost-sure contraction: if `κ S²≥4`, the constant is `κ^{-1/2}`; if `κ S²<4`, it is `S[(exp(2−κ S²/2)+1)/8]^{1/2}`. Here `κ` may be negative. These are Euclidean-density/support hypotheses, not a theorem for compact-group Gibbs measures. The natural product of embedded SU(2) links is singular in ambient Euclidean coordinates, and the theorem's diameter depends on the full state-space metric. A group-valued version would need a Brownian/Föllmer process on the product group, Haar-relative density, gauge covariance, and volume-uniform contraction estimates. It would still need a separate spectral identity for (SUPER-D). **Disposition: no direct application.**

### Bauerschmidt–Bodineau–Dagallier: stochastic dynamics and Polchinski flow

**Primary source already read in full:** Roland Bauerschmidt, Thierry Bodineau, and Benoît Dagallier, “Stochastic dynamics and the Polchinski equation: an introduction,” arXiv:2307.07619, *Probability Surveys* 21 (2024). Reuse row 719's full reading of Theorem 3.6 and §6 (`origin/main:research/mass-gap/library/prior/gap-fullread-2.md:49–82`); no second reading is counted. [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.07619).

**Theorem 3.6, exact hypotheses and consequence (as recorded in the prior full read).** For `ν_0` of the Euclidean Gaussian-perturbation form (3.10), with differentiable covariance decomposition `Ċ_t` and the stated continuity condition (3.31) for the Polchinski semigroup, assume for all fields `φ` and `t>0` that `Ċ_t Hess(V_t)(φ) Ċ_t - ½ C̈_t ⪰ λ̇_t Ċ_t`, where `λ_t=∫_0^t λ̇_s ds`, and `1/γ=∫_0^∞ e^{-2λ_t}dt<∞`. Then (3.34) gives the log-Sobolev bound `Ent_{ν_0}[F] ≤ (2/γ) E_{ν_0}[|∇√F|²_{Ċ_0}]`. Convexity of the bare `V_0` is not assumed; the condition is imposed on all renormalized `V_t`. The source and theorem-number details are from the prior paper read, not a new verification here.

**Fit to lattice SU(2).** The criterion concerns Euclidean fields with a Gaussian covariance split; §6's concrete models are scalar `φ^4`, sine-Gordon, Ising, and a constrained magnetization example, not a compact gauge-link law. A compact-link version needs a group-compatible flow, a covariant Hessian/curvature inequality uniformly in field, volume, and coupling, plus a bridge from the resulting functional inequality to the transfer-level energy/action comparison. **Disposition: conditional architecture only; no Wilson SU(2) supplier.**

### Lüscher: trivializing maps and Wilson flow

**Primary source:** Martin Lüscher, “Trivializing maps, the Wilson flow and the HMC algorithm,” arXiv:0907.5491v3. [Open arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.5491). Read §§2.1–2.3, 3.1, and 4.1–4.2 (PDF pp. 2–8) through the primary PDF text view; local download failed at DNS resolution.

**Exact construction and hypotheses.** On a finite periodic hypercubic lattice, the explicit construction takes link variables in `SU(3)` with product normalized Haar measure and a smooth gauge action that is a sum of Wilson loops. A field map `U=F_t(V)` is generated by `U̇_t=Z_t(U_t)U_t`. If its generator satisfies `∫_0^t ds Σ_{x,μ} (∂^a_{x,μ}[Z_s(U)]^a(x,μ))|_{U=U_s}=t S(U_t)+C_t`, with `C_t` field-independent, then `S(F_t(V))-log det F_{t,*}(V)=(1-t)S(F_t(V))-C_t`; at `t=1` the transformed density is trivial. Lüscher constructs a solution by taking `Z_t=-∂S̃_t` and solving the elliptic equation `L_t S̃_t=S+Ċ_t`, choosing `Ċ_t` to remove the constant zero mode (eqs. (4.1)–(4.10), pp. 7–8). The Wilson flow is the leading-order approximation to the trivializing flow, not an asserted uniformly Lipschitz coupling transport or an action-energy inequality.

**Fit to lattice SU(2).** The finite-volume existence principle for trivializing maps is stated for any compact connected gauge group, so compact SU(2) is within that topological existence observation (p. 2). The paper's detailed construction and group constants are for SU(3). Neither statement gives a volume-uniform derivative bound, an estimate across the order-one coupling interval, or a comparison of individual transfer levels with the vacuum. A useful SU(2) version would need an explicit gauge-covariant flow and Jacobian with SU(2) constants, uniform regularity/invertibility as volume and coupling vary, and an additional spectral identity controlling the vacuum subtraction. **Disposition: map-construction context, not the row-913 inequality.**

## Findings

### A. Variational and relative-entropy bounds

**Gibbs/Donsker–Varadhan variational identity.** For a finite-volume reference law `mu` and bounded action difference `X`, the Gibbs variational principle gives `F = inf_nu { E_nu X + D(nu||mu) }`, with equality at the twisted Gibbs law. The exact action-plus-entropy identity in row 789, (E21), is this standard identity specialized to the untwisted and twisted Wilson laws. It is a proved measure identity, but unrestricted optimization chooses the unknown twisted law itself. It gives no explicit trial law, no small upper bound, and no `TR-F` construction. The generic principle does not itself supply (26). See the target's own derivation (E21)–(E23); that derivation is the closest exact match.

For the row-789 mixture of Haar-preserving maps, the entropy chain rule gives exactly `C(T,pi)=F+D(nu||mu')`. That construction is already derived in `gap-ext-astra.md`; it is not an independent literature result. No paper located gives a Wilson-action map family with this cost bounded by `TR-F`, or directly controls the weighted exterior mean in (26).

**Jarzynski et al., “Jarzynski's theorem for lattice gauge theory,” arXiv:1604.05544 (2016).** The paper applies the nonequilibrium-work identity to interface free energies and the equation of state. Its interface-free-energy demonstration is Monte Carlo in the three-dimensional `Z_2` gauge model, with periodic-to-antiperiodic boundary changes. The identity is exact; the reported interface values and algorithm are numerical. It shows that a prescribed path can encode a free-energy difference, but does not prove a path-independent upper bound, does not treat the 4D SU(2) vortex ratio in (26), and supplies neither `TR-F` nor (26). [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.05544).

### B. Thick vortices, twists, and group dependence

**Mack and Petkova, “Comparison of lattice gauge theories with gauge groups Z2 and SU(2),” Ann. Phys. 123 (1979), 442–467, DOI 10.1016/0003-4916(79)90346-4; Mack, “Properties of lattice gauge theory models at low temperatures,” DESY 80/03 (1980), §§8–9.** The article abstract states an inequality between SU(2) and restricted Z2 Wilson-loop expectations: confinement of static quarks in the corresponding Z2 model implies confinement in their SU(2) model. In the scanned §8, inequality (8.6) bounds a Wilson character by the maximum Fourier coefficient of the distribution of a vortex-container partition-function ratio; the text says confinement follows if vortex free energy decreases sufficiently rapidly as container thickness grows. The paper explicitly motivates thick vortices because continuous-group vortex plaquettes need not themselves equal a center element. This is a rigorous sufficient criterion within the paper's stated models and hypotheses, not an unconditional estimate of the torus twist ratio, and not a `TR-F` transport-map construction. Its §9 strong-coupling discussion is a high-temperature expansion, not an order-one-coupling estimate. [1979 article record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0003-4916(79)90346-4), [DESY scan](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/scan/1980/desy80-003.kek.pdf).

**’t Hooft, “On the phase transition towards permanent quark confinement,” Nucl. Phys. B 138 (1978), 1–25, DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(78)90153-0.** The paper derives phase consequences from the algebra of topological and gauge-field operators and argues that permanent confinement is one allowed phase of a 3+1D non-Abelian theory. The vortex/free-energy criterion later called the ’t Hooft criterion is conditional: exponential suppression of the wrapped center-vortex free-energy ratio with transverse area is sufficient for an area law. It is not a proof that this suppression occurs for 4D SU(2) Wilson theory at order-one or every coupling. It gives the form of a target like (26), not its estimate. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(78)90153-0).

**Tomboulis–Yaffe, “Finite temperature SU(2) lattice gauge theory,” Commun. Math. Kanazawa proves the corresponding reflection-positivity inequality for SU(N), and for a broad class of classical spin systems. Thus a suitable *small twist deficit* yields a Wilson-loop area law. This is a consumer inequality: it does not upper-bound the twist free energy or provide the exponential smallness assumed by ’t Hooft. It supplies no `TR-F` and does not close (26). [Kanazawa source and theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3442); [TY DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01206134).

**Mack–Petkova and continuous versus finite groups.** The literature supports the thick-vortex picture and comparison inequalities, but I found no published quantitative entropy-versus-action theorem that distinguishes SU(2) from a finite subgroup in the exact Wilson-map sense of (TR-F). For finite groups, any fixed nonidentity group element has positive distance from the identity in a chosen faithful matrix norm, but that elementary discreteness fact is not an entropy estimate. ### C. Entropy versus energy and coupling regimes

The clearest rigorous energy/entropy competition located is the convergent **strong-coupling cluster expansion** below. The thick-vortex papers provide conditional comparison/criterion results; the weak-coupling thick-vortex evidence is numerical. I found no theorem that estimates the entropy of the row-789 correlated maps against their Wilson action cost at order-one coupling. General relative entropy alone is an exact variational representation, with the minimizer equal to the unknown twisted law; it does not establish the needed cost balance.

**Münster, “High-temperature expansions for the free energy of vortices and the string tension in lattice gauge theories,” Nucl. Phys. B 180 (1981), 23–60, DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(81)90153-X.** The paper states that it derives high-temperature cluster expansions for vortex free energies in SU(2) and Z2 lattice gauge theories in 3 and 4 dimensions, verifies area-law behavior, and identifies the area coefficient with the static-quark string tension. A contemporaneous statement records for pure 4D SU(2), in the high-temperature/strong-coupling regime, the asymptotic form proportional to `a^-2 exp(-alpha d1 d2)`, with `alpha` the string tension, to all orders of the expansion. Kanazawa later describes Münster's result as verified by the *convergent* strong-coupling cluster expansion. **Tomboulis, “Confinement for all values of the coupling in four-dimensional SU(2) gauge theory,” arXiv:0707.2179 (2007).** This paper claims a derivation of confining vortex free-energy behavior for SU(2), `0 < g < infinity`, in `d <= 4`, by potential-moving decimation and matching to strong coupling. **Kanazawa, “A theorem concerning twisted and untwisted partition functions in U(N) and SU(N) lattice gauge theories,” Phys. Lett. B 670 (2009), 421–427, arXiv:0805.2742.** Theorem 1 is an exact relation between a weighted sum of twisted partition functions and an untwisted partition function on a coarser lattice, with a small coupling-parameter mismatch. It applies to SU(N) and U(N), without a confinement assumption. The author says it does not incorporate the dynamics needed to prove confinement; the theorem works for U(1) as well. This is useful bookkeeping for ratios under blocking, not decay of an individual twist ratio, and it gives no (TR-F) or (26).

For SO(3), de Forcrand and Jahn, arXiv:hep-lat/0209060, measure electric-twist free energies in SU(2) and SO(3) and use them as transition observables. The paper is numerical. ## Disposition for row 789

1. **Positive theorem, restricted regime:** Münster's convergent high-temperature/strong-coupling expansion gives exponential-in-area vortex free-energy behavior for SU(2) in 3D and 4D (and Z2), with string-tension coefficient. It does not reach the route's order-one coupling interval or furnish its transport maps.
2. **Exact consumers/identities:** Gibbs/DV and Jarzynski identities express free-energy differences variationally or through work. They do not give the needed explicit low-cost correlated Wilson-variable maps. TY and Mack–Petkova inequalities use supplied twist/vortex control to infer Wilson-loop confinement; they do not prove that control.
3. **Weak-coupling vortex literature:** the cited SU(2) studies are simulations or conditional physical models. They are guidance only.
4. **All-coupling claim:** Tomboulis's 4D SU(2) proof claim would be highly relevant if validated. The published exchanges dispute essential steps, so it is not used as a proof of (26).
5. **Small commutator:** no located publication uses the exact sequence `[A_N,B_N]=exp(i pi sigma_3/N)` to bound a Wilson twist free energy. U(1)'s commutator is identically trivial; finite-group discreteness blocks a sequence of nontrivial elements tending to identity. These are algebraic comparisons, not a published entropy-cost estimate.
