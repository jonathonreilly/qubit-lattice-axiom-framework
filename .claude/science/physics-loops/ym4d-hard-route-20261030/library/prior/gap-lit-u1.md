## Four-dimensional U(1): what is proved

**Fröhlich and Spencer (FS), “Massless Phases and Symmetry Restoration in Abelian Gauge Theories and Spin Systems,” Commun. Math. Phys. 83 (1982), 411–454, DOI [10.1007/BF01213610].** I read the full paper from the official IHES-hosted scan, extracting its text to scratch for inspection. This is the main source for the broad Coulomb/masslessness statement.

- **Weights.** The paper sets up positive U(1)-invariant one-plaquette weights (class functions), and proves its low-temperature results for the Villain/heat-kernel weight. It expressly says the Wilson result follows by adapting the direct argument, and that the methods/results extend to a “fairly large” class including the Wilson cosine weight \(w_\beta(\theta)=e^{\beta\cos\theta}\). This does **not** mean every positive class function is covered: the paper gives no exhaustive characterization of that larger class. Its Wilson-cosine claim is a published rigorous direct claim, not merely an appeal to universality or numerical evidence. However, the paper omits the lengthy Wilson-action derivation; the displayed detailed proof is for Villain. Thus the conclusion is asserted rigorously by FS, with less proof detail available in print than for Villain.
- **Volumes and boundary conditions.** The finite-volume setup uses link variables on finite rectangular subsets of \(\mathbb Z^4\), with boundary links fixed (the Dirichlet setup used in the finite-volume estimates); the infinite-volume statements are obtained on \(\mathbb Z^4\). FS invokes Ginibre inequalities for existence/selection of the infinite-volume expectation. It says the method is not tied to a special boundary condition and extends to a large class of U(1)-invariant boundary conditions, and that translation invariance is not needed in the estimates. This is not a theorem about arbitrary boundary conditions or about a periodic finite four-torus.
- **No area law / deconfinement.** For a rectangular Wilson loop with side lengths \(L,T\) in a coordinate plane, the low-temperature theorem gives a perimeter lower bound of the form \(\langle W(L,T)\rangle_\beta\ge e^{-d(\beta)(L+T)}\), with finite \(d(\beta)\), for sufficiently large \(\beta\). The infinite-volume Wilson-loop expectation has the same lower bound. This rules out a positive area-law string tension in that regime; it is not an equality or a full asymptotic perimeter law. In the Villain proof the sufficient condition is \(\beta>108\beta_1\), where \(\beta_1\) is a finite geometric constant, so it is existential rather than a useful numerical threshold. The Wilson-cosine theorem likewise states sufficiently large coupling without a numerical threshold. **GUIDE:** do not identify this theorem’s unspecified threshold with the Monte Carlo transition estimate \(\beta\simeq1.011\) recorded in `gap-tests-lit.md`.
- **Coulomb/massless statement.** FS studies gauge-invariant field-strength plaquette observables and gives a non-summability lower bound for their two-point correlations at large separation, compared with the lattice Green function. This is the precise operational masslessness claim behind its “massless photon” wording: the field-strength correlation in this channel cannot have summable/exponential falloff. Do not promote it to a full continuum spectral theorem, a general Osterwalder–Schrader mass-spectrum result, or an exact pointwise \(r^{-2}\) asymptotic. The Wilson-cosine extension is stated for the massless result too, with the paper noting that its proof needs a more involved direct argument.
- **Transition.** In the Villain model, the low-\(\beta\) strong-coupling estimate gives an area law, while the high-\(\beta\) theorem gives the perimeter lower bound; FS concludes there is at least one transition between confining and massless/deconfined regimes. Its corresponding low- and high-coupling statements also give a transition for Wilson cosine. No numerical critical \(\beta\), uniqueness of the transition, or order of transition is proved. Here \(\beta=1/g^2\) is FS’s convention; threshold values are existential.

**Guth (1980), “Existence proof of a nonconfining phase in four-dimensional U(1) lattice gauge theory,” Phys. Rev. D 21, 2291–2307, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.21.2291].** Read status: published APS abstract only; the full-text request to APS returned HTTP 403, and retrieval stopped there. The abstract says the **Villain-form** action has a rigorous Coulomb-form Wilson-loop lower bound for \(g^2<0.168\), hence no electric-charge confinement; the infinite-volume Wilson-loop limit exists, and together with Osterwalder–Seiler strong-coupling results this yields at least one phase transition. It does not establish the FS field-strength correlation result from the abstract alone. **GUIDE:** the reciprocal conversion is approximately \(\beta>5.95\) if this abstract’s \(g^2\) is converted via \(\beta=1/g^2\); keep Guth’s stated bound in \(g^2\) and do not compare it numerically with Wilson-action simulations without checking conventions.

**Forward follow-up checked:** Garban and Sepúlveda, “Improved spin-wave estimate for Wilson loops in U(1) lattice gauge theory,” IMRN 2023, DOI [10.1093/imrn/rnac356], arXiv:2107.04021. Read status: primary arXiv abstract and theorem text. For the Villain interaction on \(\mathbb Z^4\), their low-temperature bound is an **upper** perimeter-decay estimate for sufficiently large loops; the paper describes it as complementing Guth and FS. It refines Wilson-loop control and defect corrections, but is not a new proof of the Wilson-cosine massless phase and does not extend its loop estimate to the Wilson cosine weight.

## The comparison cases

**Three-dimensional U(1).** Göpfert and Mack, “Proof of confinement of static quarks in three-dimensional U(1) lattice gauge theory for all values of the coupling constant,” Commun. Math. Phys. 82 (1982), 545–606, DOI [10.1007/BF01961240]. Read status: publisher/institutional abstract and bibliographic record; not full proof. For the 3D **Villain** action, they prove positive string tension at every coupling (the source describes exact duality to a 3D integer-spin/Coulomb system and a massive continuum limit). This is area-law confinement, unlike the weaker general result below. Chatterjee, “A short proof of confinement in three-dimensional lattice gauge theories with a central U(1),” arXiv:2602.00436, DOI [10.48550/arXiv.2602.00436], read status: primary arXiv theorem/abstract. For Wilson lattice gauge theory on finite subsets of \(\mathbb Z^3\), free boundary conditions, and compact \(G\subseteq U(n)\) containing scalar U(1), rectangular loops obey an explicit upper bound implying a logarithmically growing static potential at every \(\beta>0\). For \(G=U(1)\), this rigorously proves confinement for the Wilson cosine action, but only a logarithmic potential; do not call it an area law. The paper identifies Göpfert–Mack as the stronger Villain result.

**Four-dimensional \(\mathbb Z_2\).** The nearby `gap-tests-lit.md` §(e) records the self-dual coupling and first-order transition claim from Bhanot–Creutz (1981); that is not by itself the rigorous loop-law result. Chatterjee, “Wilson loops in Ising lattice gauge theory,” Comm. Math. Phys. (2020), arXiv:1811.09770, DOI [10.48550/arXiv.1811.09770]. Read status: primary arXiv full text, including theorem and setup. For the Wilson \(\mathbb Z_2\) gauge action in finite boxes with free boundary conditions, it computes weak-coupling Wilson-loop expectations to leading order and constructs a translation-invariant infinite-volume Gibbs measure at sufficiently large \(\beta\). This is a rigorous weak-coupling calculation, not a uniform exact perimeter asymptotic for every large loop at each fixed \(\beta\).

Palö Forsström and Viklund, “Free energy and quark potential in Ising lattice gauge theory via cluster expansion,” arXiv:2304.08286, published in IMRN (2025). Read status: primary arXiv abstract and theorem. For Wilson \(\mathbb Z_2\) gauge theory on \(\mathbb Z^m\), \(m\ge3\), at sufficiently large inverse temperature \(\beta>\beta_0(m)\), they prove an infinite-volume free-energy limit and existence of the rectangular-loop static potential; the potential stays bounded as separation grows, yielding a strong perimeter-law result. This supplies a rigorous weak-coupling deconfined/perimeter regime in 4D; \(\beta_0(m)\) is existential in this report, with no numerical threshold claimed. The small-\(\beta\) Wilson area-law bound is the strong-coupling result of Osterwalder and Seiler, “Gauge field theories on a lattice,” Ann. Phys. 110 (1978), 440–471, DOI `10.1016/0003-4916(78)90039-8`; read status here: abstract and citation in Chatterjee’s article, proof not reread. Together these establish distinct confining and deconfined regimes. The exact transition point/order is not established by these loop results; the self-dual/first-order statement in the existing literature note should remain distinguished from this proof status.
