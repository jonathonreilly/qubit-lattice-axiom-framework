## A. vMF transport, covariance, uniqueness, and O(N) frustration

### The one-site target

Row 785 asks for the chord-metric estimate on $S^3\subset\mathbb R^4$: for $d\mu_F(h)\propto e^{\langle h,F\rangle}d\sigma(h)$, all $F$ in the stated ball and every chord-1-Lipschitz $\phi$,

\[
\left|\operatorname{Cov}_F\!\left(\phi(h),\langle h,e\rangle\right)\right|\le \tfrac14
\quad (|e|=1).
\]

By differentiating the exponential family and Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality, this is the claimed $W_1$-Lipschitz constant $1/4$ for the parameter map $F\mapsto\mu_F$. The exact general-direction vMF bound is not supplied by the literature hits below. The local `gap-vmf-lip.md` now proves a theorem of this form (row 785, not yet independently checked); that is the current proof route, not a published citation. Its orthogonal-direction reflection argument is elementary, while its nonorthogonal case is the crux. No numerical evidence is used here.

The correlation estimate of Föllmer and the dynamical gap results are downstream tools: if the target single-site (W_1) coefficient is supplied and the row sums contract, they can turn it into uniqueness, covariance decay, or a dynamics gap. That implication is the useful literature contribution; the sharp coefficient and its coupling range remain a separate input.

### Mean-field temperature and frustration

Michael Aizenman and Barry Simon, [“Local Ward Identities and the Decay of Correlations in Ferromagnets”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01982713), *Commun. Math. Phys.* 77 (1980), 137–143, use Ward identities and correlation inequalities for N-component, isotropically coupled, pair-interacting **ferromagnets**. They prove a mass gap above the mean-field temperature for every $N\ge2$ (and further critical-exponent/susceptibility results for selected N). In the 3D O(4) layer normalization used in row 785, the mean-field endpoint is $N/(2d)=4/6=2/3$. This proves the cited ferromagnetic comparison theorem, not the all-background (AS-bg) estimate.

The relevant obstacle is hypothesis-level: a gauge background makes bond couplings signed or frustrated, so the ferromagnetic correlation inequalities used by Aizenman–Simon do not apply as stated. General Dobrushin/Wasserstein criteria can cover signs if their absolute influence matrix contracts, but that gives a high-temperature sufficient condition, not the full $\beta<2/3$ range. I found no proved result extending the Aizenman–Simon mean-field-temperature gap to arbitrary frustrated or random O(N) couplings in the form required by R1. As nearby but non-supplying literature, S. Shlosman, [“First-order transitions for n-vector models in two and more dimensions; rigorous proof”](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0205455), proves first-order transitions for certain SO(n)-invariant n-vector interactions with a sufficiently deep and narrow local-energy minimum. It is a different interaction family and does not establish (or refute) a gap for the row-785 random-background layer model.

## B. Four-dimensional U(1): volume, boundary conditions, and dynamics

### Fröhlich–Spencer and Guth

J. Fröhlich and T. Spencer, [“Massless Phases and Symmetry Restoration in Abelian Gauge Theories and Spin Systems”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01213610), *Commun. Math. Phys.* 83 (1982), 411–454, prove a weak-coupling Coulomb/massless-phase result for four-dimensional U(1). The full source was already read and summarized in `library/prior/gap-lit-u1.md`: for the Villain/heat-kernel action, sufficiently large inverse coupling gives a perimeter lower bound for rectangular Wilson loops (no area law) and non-summable field-strength correlations, with finite boxes using fixed boundary links and an infinite-volume conclusion. They state that the argument extends to the Wilson cosine action, but omit the lengthy Wilson derivation; their theorem does not cover every positive class-function weight. The threshold is existential in the printed result. These are rigorous infinite-volume/fixed-boundary results, not a bound uniform in side $M$ for the periodic torus $(\mathbb Z/M)^4$.

Alan Guth, [“Existence Proof of a Nonconfining Phase in Four-Dimensional U(1) Lattice Gauge Theory”](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.21.2291), *Phys. Rev. D* 21 (1980), 2291–2307, proves for the Villain action a Coulomb-form lower bound on Wilson loops for $g^2<0.168$, and existence of the infinite-volume Wilson-loop limit; it implies nonconfinement and, with strong-coupling results, a phase transition. Read status in the existing local note: APS abstract and a separate scan excerpt, not a complete proof reading. Guth's statement is not a uniform periodic-torus correlation estimate and is not about Langevin/Glauber dynamics.

Driver's [“Convergence of the U(1)\(_4\) Lattice Gauge Theory to Its Continuum Limit”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01212424), *Commun. Math. Phys.* 110 (1987), 479–501, proves convergence in four dimensions to a renormalized free electromagnetic field on the current sector for sufficiently large coupling in the paper's convention; for Wilson energy it obtains convergence for arbitrary coupling with a judicious Gibbs-state choice, with state-independence except at at most countably many couplings. This is a continuum-limit/current-sector theorem, not the requested finite periodic (M)-uniform correlation bound or a spectral-gap estimate for lattice stochastic dynamics.

H. Göpfert and G. Mack, [“Proof of Confinement of Static Quarks in Three-Dimensional U(1) Lattice Gauge Theory for All Values of the Coupling Constant”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01961240), *Commun. Math. Phys.* 82 (1982), 545–606, prove 3D Villain confinement. The dimension and conclusion differ from 4D periodic Coulomb behavior; it does not settle the torus question.

### Periodic tori and dynamics

The literature found here does **not** establish that the Fröhlich–Spencer non-summable correlation lower bound holds uniformly in (M) on periodic 4D tori. The difference is material: the theorem cited above uses fixed boundary links before taking infinite volume, and a periodic torus has global holonomy/zero-mode sectors. Passing from the fixed-boundary infinite-volume theorem to a periodic finite-size sequence requires a separate argument. `library/gap-lit-u1.md` and `library/gap-u1-audit-opus.md` already identify this as an open bridge for the periodic seed; it must not be silently imported.

I found no rigorous result showing a weak-coupling vanishing of the spectral gap for Langevin or Glauber dynamics of the 4D Wilson U(1) measure on periodic $(\mathbb Z/M)^4$, nor a positive gap uniform in $M$. Wu's theorem above is conditional on Dobrushin contraction and does not reach this weak-coupling target. Moriarty's [1982 compact-U(1) Monte Carlo study](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.25.2185) reports observables on $4^4,5^4,6^4$ finite lattices and numerical evidence for a second-order transition; this is finite-size simulation evidence, not a proof about correlation summability or a dynamics spectral gap. The 2026 continuum-limit result for 3D U(1) Langevin dynamics is also wrong dimension and studies scaling convergence, not the 4D finite-torus gap.

## Result

The literature supplies general Wasserstein/Dobrushin covariance and dynamics machinery, and a rigorous O(N) mass-gap theorem above the mean-field temperature for ferromagnetic couplings. It does not supply the sharp vMF chord-(W_1) estimate or its frustrated-background extension for R1. Fröhlich–Spencer and Guth establish 4D U(1) Coulomb/nonconfinement results in infinite-volume settings built from fixed-boundary boxes, but I found no proof transferring the required non-summable correlation bound uniformly to periodic tori; I found no 4D periodic weak-coupling Langevin/Glauber gap theorem either. For row 783, global twist inequalities, strong-coupling expansions, weak-coupling SU(2) simulations, and finite-group local-clustering theorems fall short of the order-one Wilson-weighted exterior-average estimate. These are literature-search findings, not absence theorems and not proof closures. No computer-assisted calculation was run.
