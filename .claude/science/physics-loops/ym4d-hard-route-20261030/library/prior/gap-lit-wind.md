## Findings

### 1. Lattice SU(N) loop equations and winding paths

**Jafarov (2016), “Wilson loop expectations in SU(N) lattice gauge theory,” [arXiv:1610.03821](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.03821), DOI [10.48550/arXiv.1610.03821](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1610.03821).** Theorem 8.1 gives a finite-(N) SU(N) master loop equation for arbitrary real coupling and loop sequences whose one-lattice-neighborhood is contained in a finite subset of ℤ^d. It includes splitting, merging, deformation and expansion terms, and is an exact Schwinger–Dyson identity. The theorem’s stated domain is a finite subset of ℤ^d, so its loops are interior contractible paths; it does **not** state the corresponding equation on a periodic torus for a homologically winding loop, and it has no ’t Hooft twist. Separately, the paper’s convergent (1/N) string-trajectory expansion is in a sufficiently small strong-coupling regime. **Bearing on row 767:** this supports loop-equation identities for arbitrary shapes within a nonwrapping region, but not the missing winding sector or a volume comparison. **4D Wilson SU(2):** the exact identity is for SU(N), includes (N=2,d=4), but only on the stated nonwrapping domain; no order-one finite-size estimate follows. **Read status:** full HTML theorem statement and hypotheses read; the (1/N) expansion is read at the scope level, not used here. The repository’s `gap-lit-loops.md` already read the master-equation theorem and is credited rather than redone.

**González-Arroyo and Okawa (1983), “Twisted-Eguchi-Kawai model: A reduced model for large-N lattice gauge theory,” [Phys. Rev. D 27, 2397](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.27.2397), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.27.2397.** Their abstract states that the reduced model’s Schwinger–Dyson equations agree with infinite-lattice equations provided the [U(1)]⁴ symmetry is unbroken; for a particular twist they prove weak-coupling symmetry preservation and recovery of planar perturbation theory. The published follow-up derivation in García Pérez, González-Arroyo and Okawa’s *Methods of Contemporary Gauge Theory*, ch. 15, eqs. (15.70)–(15.75), writes the twist-phased TEK loop equation. The Wilson loop variable includes the centre-flux phase (surface product, independent of spanning surface by the Bianchi identity); the equation has twist phases multiplying the plaquette-deformed loop and a product of open segments. At large (N), unbroken centre symmetry makes the open-loop terms vanish and recovers the ordinary planar lattice equation. This is a reduced one-site model and a **conditional large-(N)** equivalence result. **Bearing on row 767:** it is the clearest published equation where twist/winding phases enter explicitly, and a useful source for what the winding equations look like; it offers no finite-(N) contraction or twisted-sector free-energy bound. **4D Wilson SU(2):** no—the equivalence and factorization argument is planar (N→∞), not (N=2). **Read status:** APS abstract and the textbook’s derivation of the TEK equation read; original 1983 article body not read. The book chapter is [DOI 10.1017/9781009402095.016](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009402095.016).

**García Pérez, González-Arroyo and Okawa (2017), “Perturbative contributions to Wilson loops in twisted lattice boxes and reduced models,” [arXiv:1708.00841](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.00841), DOI [10.1103/PhysRevD.97.014501](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.97.014501).** They derive the finite-box Wilson-loop perturbative expansion through (g^4), for SU(N), arbitrary dimension and irreducible ’t Hooft twist, including symmetric-twist 4D TEK. The results compute flux-dependent finite-volume corrections and quantify the approach to volume independence at large N. **Bearing:** useful twist-phase and finite-volume perturbation formulas, but only a weak-coupling expansion through fixed order; it neither solves a winding-loop SD system nonperturbatively nor controls order-one twisted free energies or plaquette means. **4D Wilson SU(2):** formulas are stated for SU(N) and specialize formally to (N=2), but the volume-independence conclusion is large-N and the truncated perturbative result is not the target bound. **Read status:** arXiv HTML abstract, twist setup, and conclusions read; the detailed coefficient derivation was not read.

**Scope distinction.** A link-integration-by-parts derivation on a finite periodic product Haar space should admit a winding loop as a test observable, with topology affecting the resulting loop words and centre phases but not the formal integration-by-parts identity. That is a direct mathematical inference from compact Haar integration, not a claim that Jafarov’s ℤ^d theorem already states the periodic/twisted version. I found explicit twist phases in the TEK equation, but no reviewed published finite-(N) theorem that writes and analyzes the full 4D Wilson SU(N) master equations on a general ’t Hooft-twisted torus for winding loops.

### 2. Polyakov-loop Schwinger–Dyson work and bootstrap

**Billó, Caselle, D’Adda and Panzeri (1996), “Effective actions for finite temperature Lattice Gauge Theories,” [arXiv:hep-lat/9609027](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9609027).** By character expansion and Schwinger–Dyson-type equations, the authors construct a Polyakov-loop effective action order by order in the spatial coupling β_s, exact to all orders in temporal coupling β_t; they give the first nontrivial order for (3+1)-dimensional SU(2) and use it to extract a deconfinement temperature. **Bearing:** equations explicitly involving Polyakov variables exist, but this is an anisotropic finite-temperature effective-action expansion; it yields no positivity bootstrap, rigorous volume comparison or twist free-energy bound. **4D Wilson SU(2):** yes, as the example at a truncated spatial-coupling order; no target estimate at isotropic order-one coupling. **Read status:** arXiv HTML abstract, construction scope, and opening construction discussion read; detailed derivation and numerical/model extraction not read.

**Dittmann, Heinzl and Wipf (2004), “Effective lattice theories for Polyakov loops,” [arXiv:hep-lat/0306032](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0306032), DOI [10.1088/1126-6708/2004/06/005](https://doi.org/10.1088/1126-6708/2004/06/005).** They derive SU(2) Polyakov-loop effective actions with inverse Monte Carlo and Haar-invariance Schwinger–Dyson identities; matching the effective ensemble to the underlying Yang–Mills one-point distribution and using a character expansion allows numerical extraction of 14 couplings, with a short-ranged effective action reported to reproduce Yang–Mills correlators. **Bearing:** a Polyakov-loop reduced effective model with SD identities, not a positivity-certified bound on winding moments. **4D Wilson SU(2):** the target group and thermal lattice setting are relevant, but the coupling extraction and correlator agreement are numerical and finite-temperature; no twisted-box or finite-size plaquette inequality. **Read status:** full HTML abstract and setup/SDE formulation read; extraction results are abstract-level only.

**Azuma and Morita (2024), “A Scaling Relation, (Z_m)-Type Deconfinement Phases, and Imaginary Chemical Potentials in Finite Temperature Large-N Gauge Theories,” [PTEP 2024, 093B03](https://doi.org/10.1093/ptep/ptae130).** In finite-temperature large-N SU(N) theories with adjoint matter, Schwinger–Dyson equations imply a temperature-scaling relation for the Polyakov-loop effective potential, with applications to (Z_m)-type phases and several models. **Bearing:** a genuine analytic Polyakov-loop SD result, but a large-N effective-potential relation, not lattice winding-loop positivity/bootstrap or a torus-size comparison. **4D Wilson SU(2):** no; the theorem’s large-N/adjoint-matter setting is different. **Read status:** full publisher HTML abstract and theorem-description/intro scope read, not the detailed derivation.

**Polyakov bootstrap answer.** The lattice Wilson-loop bootstrap papers do combine loop equations with positive moment/Gram constraints. Kazakov and Zheng (2025), [arXiv:2404.16925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16925), explicitly treat SU(2) in dimensions 2–4 and report solver-produced plaquette/free-energy margins and string-tension estimates. Their finite loop basis is for the lattice Wilson-loop variables; the paper does not formulate a Polyakov-loop/twisted-sector Gram bootstrap or a joint system for different torus sizes. Liu and Yang (2026), [arXiv:2601.04316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04316), supply an SU(2), 2D–4D algorithm/count of direct and indirect loop equations, not bounds. Guo, Li, Yang and Zhu (2025), [arXiv:2502.14421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14421), add Hermitian/reflection positivity and twist-reflection positivity to numerical SU(3) bounds; the paper states the twist-reflection condition is exact in 2D. That is a reflection operation in the bootstrap, not by itself a ’t Hooft-twisted torus or a winding-loop bootstrap. The finite-N SU(2) solver margins are directly group/dimension relevant, but all solver-produced values are **GUIDE** and cannot discharge this no-compute task. Read status: Kazakov–Zheng and Liu–Yang full HTML abstract/method/topology scopes read; their detailed result tables are reused from `gap-lit-loops.md`; Guo et al. abstract and scope read from the existing digest. No lattice positivity bootstrap with explicit Polyakov-loop variables or twisted winding sectors was located in this search.

A 2026 paper titled “Thermal Polyakov bootstrap,” [arXiv:2609.28627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28627), is a terminology false friend: it derives thermal CFT OPE/KMS bootstrap equations on (S^1_β\times\mathbb R^{d-1}), not equations for gauge-theory Polyakov holonomies. ### 3. Reflection positivity, twisted free energies, and plaquette means

These are the closest rigorous twisted-box results, but they are not bounds derived from a winding-loop bootstrap and their conclusions differ from row 767’s finite-size plaquette shift.

**Tomboulis–Yaffe (1985), “Finite temperature SU(2) lattice gauge theory,” [DOI 10.1007/BF01206134](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01206134); generalized by Kanazawa (2009), “The generalized Tomboulis–Yaffe inequality,” [arXiv:0808.3442](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.3442), DOI [10.1016/j.aop.2009.04.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aop.2009.04.002).** Reflection positivity gives a Wilson-loop upper bound in terms of the ratio (Z^-/Z) of a centre-twisted and periodic partition function; the SU(N) extension holds for the stated one-plaquette actions. The inequality has no small-coupling hypothesis, but the twist ratio is an input, not estimated by the inequality. **Bearing:** it makes twist sensitivity useful if already controlled; it does not bound the twisted free energy, the centre-even seed sum, or the difference of ground-state plaquette means. **4D Wilson SU(2):** yes, the SU(2) theorem applies on finite periodic lattices, but does not supply the needed twist ratio at target coupling. **Read status:** the generalized theorem and hypotheses were read in full in the existing `library/prior/gap-lit-flux.md` (reused); the original TY proof was not independently read in this task.

**Mack, Petkova, and collaborators (1982), “Inequalities for magnetic-flux free energies and confinement in lattice gauge theories,” [DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(82)90470-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(82)90470-9).** The abstract states a conditional size-dilation implication: if the magnetic-flux free energy vanishes under sufficiently large uniform dilation, it decreases exponentially in cross-section area, a sufficient condition for permanent static-quark confinement. **Bearing:** this is the closest rigorous size comparison for a twisted free energy, but assumes the large-dilation premise and controls magnetic flux/confinement, not the electric/magnetic mixed flux sector or row 767’s pair of plaquette means. **4D Wilson SU(2):** the cited work concerns lattice gauge theories, but the exact hypotheses and group-specific 4D implication were not reached from the abstract; no stronger applicability is claimed. **Read status:** abstract only, as recorded in `gap-lit-flux.md`; body not read.

**Kanazawa (2009), “Confinement in SU(N) lattice gauge theory from a duality transformation,” [arXiv:0805.2742](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2742), DOI [10.1016/j.physletb.2008.11.017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physletb.2008.11.017).** For positive character coefficients and arbitrary positive weights on a subgroup of the centre, the paper gives an exact blocking identity for a weighted magnetic-twist partition-function sum, relating its ratio to a coarse-lattice ratio with one denominator coupling shifted by (O(1/|Λ|)). This is an equality, not a monotonicity or upper bound. **Bearing:** potentially informs magnetic twist sectors if the weights match, but leaves the coarse ratio/prefactor to estimate and does not directly control winding Wilson moments, the electric transform, or plaquette shifts. **4D Wilson SU(2):** the group/action class includes SU(2) where the character positivity hypotheses hold, but the identity alone does not evaluate the target ratio at order-one effective coupling. **Read status:** full HTML theorem and proof were read in `gap-lit-flux.md` (reused).

**’t Hooft (1979), “A property of electric and magnetic flux in non-Abelian gauge theories,” [DOI 10.1016/0550-3213(79)90595-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(79)90595-9).** Electric and magnetic flux sectors are related by a finite-centre Fourier transform/duality relation in a periodic box, with Euclidean rotation relating the symmetric geometry.
