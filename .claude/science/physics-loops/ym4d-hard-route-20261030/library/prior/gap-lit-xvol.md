## Findings

### Positivity bootstrap: bounded loop systems

**Anderson–Kruczenski (2017), arXiv:1612.08140, DOI 10.1016/j.nuclphysb.2017.06.009.** Their method combines large-N loop equations and positive semidefinite matrices of open-path/Wilson-loop expectations, truncating the maximal loop length. In four dimensions it reports numerical bootstrap bounds/estimates for the plaquette; the paper's finite-lattice loop comparison is a simulation check. The derivation does not impose a joint feasible moment system for two box sizes and gives no theorem for loops whose length scales with the box. It bears on P-small only as a possible source of extra single-volume positivity inequalities; its reported values are **GUIDE**, not a hand proof of either integral bound. 4D is studied, but at large N, not Wilson SU(2). Read status: method/results read in the existing `gap-lit-loops.md`; no new PDF pass.

**Kazakov–Zheng (2023), arXiv:2203.11360, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.107.L051501.** For SU(∞) in dimensions 2, 3 and 4, the numerical bootstrap combines loop equations with positive matrices and a maximal loop-length cutoff, and reports upper/lower plaquette bounds at selected couplings. The 4D computation uses a fixed truncation in each run; the cutoff is not tied to a torus side, and there is no joint cross-volume constraint. The bounds can inform single-volume `U_s`/`L_s` choices but do not establish P-small. Numbers and solver outputs are **GUIDE**. Not SU(2). Read status: existing `gap-lit-loops.md` records that the paper and supplement's solver discussion were read.

**Kazakov–Zheng (2025), arXiv:2404.16925, DOI 10.1007/JHEP03(2025)099.** Their finite-N SU(2) loop equations close linearly on single-trace Wilson loops; the paper reports convex-bootstrap upper/lower margins for free energy per plaquette and string-tension estimates in dimensions 2–4, using loops up to length 24. The published abstract reports sub-percent agreement in 3D/4D; the numerical precision, couplings, and truncation values are **GUIDE**. The constraints are a truncated system for the theory, not a paired `(L^4,(2L)^4)` certificate. The method is directly relevant to SU(2) in four dimensions, but its optimization outputs do not by themselves hand-prove a moment relation or either P-small tolerance. Read status: methods, 4D results, and solver discussion were read in the existing `gap-lit-loops.md`; publisher abstract independently checked here.

**Li–Zhou (2024), arXiv:2404.17071, DOI 10.1007/JHEP08(2024)154; Guo–Li–Yang–Zhu (2025), arXiv:2502.14421, DOI 10.1007/JHEP12(2025)033.** The former applies loop equations and positivity to Z₂/U(1), reporting solver-derived Wilson-loop bounds (including a 2D U(1) plaquette estimate at `Lmax=60`); the latter extends the computed positivity bootstrap to SU(3), including multiple-trace operators and a 2D-exact twist-reflection positivity condition. These papers show that larger finite loop bases and more positivity constraints can sharpen *within-model* bounds. Neither supplies a cross-volume inequality for 4D SU(2), nor a loop cutoff that grows with the torus side as a theorem. Reported numerical precision and cutoffs are **GUIDE**. Neither group is SU(2). Read status: existing `gap-lit-loops.md` reports the abstracts, 4D comparisons, and solver passages read.

**Liu–Yang (2026), arXiv:2601.04316, DOI 10.1007/JHEP04(2026)107.** The paper derives a geometric procedure for direct loop equations and “indirect” equations obtained by eliminating higher-length intermediate loops. It applies the bookkeeping to SU(2) in 2D/3D/4D and reports counts through finite loop-length cutoffs; e.g. its 4D equation counts are tabulated at lengths 8–16. Those are counts of algebraic constraints and a method for generating longer-loop relations, not bounds on their expectation values. The paper does not couple loop length to a finite box size or compare two tori. It is 4D SU(2) structural guidance for enlarging a bootstrap basis, but supplies no P-small bound. The tabulated growth fits and counts are **GUIDE** for this task. Read status: arXiv HTML abstract, 4D tables, and indirect-equation construction read; the HTML exposes no theorem asserting cross-volume estimates.

### Rigorous results with volume or loop-size dependence

**Cao–Nissim–Sheffield (2025), arXiv:2505.16585.** Theorem 4.1 proves a rectangular Wilson-loop area-law upper bound uniformly over every finite lattice \(\Lambda\) for U(N), under \(N\ge10^{10}d^{10}\) and \(\beta\le10^{-10}/d\) in that paper's strong-coupling convention (**GUIDE** for the quoted threshold). Theorem 1.2 extends the conclusion to every fixed N at a dimension-only sufficiently small-\(\beta\) threshold, using Osterwalder–Seiler for the finitely many remaining N. Thus, for 4D Wilson SU(2), the stated extension gives a rigorous finite-lattice loop bound in its strong-coupling regime, for rectangles contained in the finite lattice. The bound allows loop rectangles of different sizes, including sizes chosen as the box grows, but only bounds each loop expectation; it does not compare plaquette means between `L^4` and `(2L)^4`. Its coupling range is far from the order-one P-small target. The argument itself is analytic. Read status: Theorems 1.2/4.1 and their assumptions were read in the existing `gap-lit-loops.md`.

**Large-N volume independence (Eguchi–Kawai; Kovtun–Ünsal–Yaffe 2007, arXiv:hep-th/0702021; Ünsal–Yaffe 2010, arXiv:1006.2101).** For an appropriate center-neutral observable \(O\), the large-N equivalence states that \(\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle O\rangle_{L}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\langle O\rangle_{2L}\) when the required symmetry conditions (including unbroken center symmetry, and translation symmetry as appropriate) hold. A fixed contractible plaquette is neutral, so this is a genuine conditional large-N two-volume identity for that observable. Wilson loops with nonzero winding are outside the neutral sector. It is not a finite-N error bound uniform down to N=2. No theorem in these sources makes the loop length grow proportionally to L while retaining the equivalence. It therefore does not apply to 4D Wilson SU(2), and cannot bound P-small. Read status: abstract/metadata pages read; the complete proofs were not read. The fixed-observable scope is the equivalence statement; no claim is made about a stronger loop-uniform theorem.

**Lüscher (1986), “Volume Dependence of the Energy Spectrum in Massive Quantum Field Theories. I. Stable Particle States,” DOI 10.1007/BF01211589.** The abstract states that for a stable particle in a large periodic spatial box, its finite-volume mass shift decays exponentially with L at a rate controlled by the light spectrum, and gives an asymptotic formula in terms of infinite-volume forward elastic scattering amplitudes, proved to all orders of perturbation theory for arbitrary massive QFTs. This is a relation between particle energy levels and scattering data, not a theorem on the expectation of the local plaquette/action density on 4D lattice tori. It assumes a massive theory and does not establish the mass gap needed to apply it to this target. It cannot supply P-small. Read status: abstract/metadata only; an attempt to open the publisher paper returned HTTP 403, so I stopped there.

### Closest two-volume statement in the supplied outline

This is not published literature and is not a checked result. Sussman and Alpoge's outline at lines 26934–27058 constructs a fixed reference torus `T_*` and, from common companion families, a vacuum-density comparison. For repeated supported sources, finite chessboard/reflection positivity yields

\[
 0\le Z_\ell(W_z)\le Z_*(W_z)^{(\ell/\ell_*)^4}.
\]

The stated argument cuts through source-free faces; the reflected slab product is positive trace class, and `Tr(Y^q) <= (Tr Y)^q`. With the outline's stability, partition-floor, nonwrapping-compatibility and face-energy inputs, it then obtains the same source normalizer bound for the reference torus and larger admissible tori (lines 27003–27058). The index classifies the vacuum-density comparison as conditional and lists the actual formation/coupling compatibility and face-crossing/partition-floor inputs as named open (chunk 24, lines 26920–26932).

This is a candidate method for relating **source partition functions** across volumes, and its exact Wilson transfer-positivity step is for the lattice gauge measure. It is not an inequality for the plaquette mean difference `delta`, nor for the finite list of staple moments `M_A,N_A,M_B,N_B,M_E,N_E` in P-small. The missing conditional inputs prevent using it as an unconditional 4D SU(2) finite-size theorem here. ## Answer for P-small

The reviewed literature offers three kinds of nearby structure: solver-generated single-theory bootstrap bounds; rigorous finite-volume loop bounds in strong coupling; and conditional/large-N volume-equivalence or source-partition-function comparisons. I found no published, hand-checkable inequality for the 4D Wilson SU(2) plaquette means on periodic `L^4` and `(2L)^4` tori at an order-one effective coupling, and no bootstrap paper imposing a joint two-volume moment matrix that yields the P-small tolerances. This is a search result, not a theorem that none exists.

The loop-size option has a limited precedent: the finite-volume strong-coupling area-law theorem controls rectangular loops of all sizes in its range, while bootstrap papers increase fixed length cutoffs to improve numerical estimates. Neither reaches the needed coupling or turns those constraints into the two signed-integral upper bounds. The next precise missing item remains one hand proof of either P-small test at a specified `a, beta`, or a proved joint-volume relation strong enough to imply it.
