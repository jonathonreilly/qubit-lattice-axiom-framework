## B. Strong coupling on the lattice

**Hao Shen, Rongchan Zhu and Xiangchan Zhu (2022), “A stochastic analysis approach to lattice Yang–Mills at strong coupling,”** arXiv:2204.12737, abstract page: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12737>. Exact scope quoted: “at strong coupling in any dimension $d>1$.” For $SO(N)$ it assumes $|\beta|<(N-2)/(32(d-1)N)$; for $SU(N)$, $|\beta|<1/(16(d-1))$. The abstract says the infinite-volume measure has Log-Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities and “correlations of a large class of observables decay exponentially,” concluding that it “has a strictly positive mass gap.” Method: Langevin dynamics, ergodicity, and functional inequalities. The abstract reports finite-volume convergence to a unique infinite-volume invariant measure. It does not state an open problem in the fetched abstract. Its result is a lattice strong-coupling theorem; it is not a continuum-limit theorem.

**Ron Nissim (2025), “$\mathrm{U}(N)$ lattice Yang-Mills in the ’t Hooft regime,”** arXiv:2510.22788: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22788>. The abstract says it establishes “a mass gap” and “a unique infinite volume limit” for $U(N)$ lattice Yang–Mills in the ’t Hooft regime. It says earlier $SU(N)$ and $SO(N)$ results came from mixing of Langevin dynamics verified by Bakry–Émery. For $U(N)$, it combines cluster expansion and Langevin dynamics by representing it as a random-environment $SU(N)$ model with a $U(1)$ field. The abstract gives no dimension or coupling threshold; the arXiv HTML URL returned 404, so no fuller statement was available. It names no open problem. This extends the lattice picture to $U(N)$, not continuum YM.

## C. Weak coupling at fixed lattice spacing

**Sky Cao, Minjae Park and Scott Sheffield (2023), “Random surfaces and lattice Yang-Mills,”** arXiv:2307.06790, abstract page: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06790>. The abstract treats Wilson-loop expectations for compact Lie group $G$, and states an embedded-planar-map expression for “any matrix dimension $N\geq1$, any inverse temperature $\beta>0$, and any lattice dimension $d\geq2$.” Method: gauge-string duality and sums over embedded maps. The abstract gives no finite/infinite-volume qualification, asserts no exponential decay or mass gap, and states no open problem about SU(2).

**Riddhipratim Basu and Shirshendu Ganguly (arXiv 2016; journal publication 2017), “$SO(N)$ Lattice Gauge Theory, planar and beyond,”** arXiv:1608.04379, abstract page: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04379>. The abstract continues Chatterjee’s Wilson-loop program in the strong-coupling regime, using decorated trees/non-crossing partitions and free probability; it gives an example showing a Wilson-loop area-law lower bound “does not hold in full generality” in any dimension. It gives no finite/infinite-volume qualification and makes no mass-gap or correlation-decay claim.

**Malin Palö Forsström, Jonatan Lenells and Fredrik Viklund (first posted 2020; revised 2021), “Wilson loops in finite Abelian lattice gauge theories,”** arXiv:2001.07453: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07453>. The abstract states: “lattice gauge theories on $\mathbb Z^4$ with Wilson action and structure group $\mathbb Z_n$”; it computes Wilson-loop expectations “to leading order in the weak coupling regime,” extending/refining Chatterjee, with proofs using neither duality nor cluster expansion. It states no exponential correlation decay or mass gap and names no open question.

**Malin P. Forsström and Fredrik Viklund (2025; revised 2025), “Current expansion and couplings for Ising lattice gauge theory,”** arXiv:2502.19942: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19942>. The abstract says a random-current expansion and switching lemma lead to “a proof of exponential decay of correlations for small and large $\beta$,” and an area-law estimate for sufficiently small $\beta$. Method: random currents, switching, surface sums, and comparison with cluster/high-temperature expansions. The abstract gives no dimension, finite/infinite-volume qualification, or threshold values; it states no open problem. The title names the Ising lattice-gauge model but does not explicitly name its group; identifying it as $\mathbb Z_2$ uses the standard convention, not wording from this abstract.

The requested Adhikari–Cao source was not identified on a fetched primary abstract page; its exact statement remains **not established by this reading**. Chatterjee’s Wilson-loop results identified here concern Wilson expectations/area laws; they do not by themselves establish SU(2) exponential clustering or a continuum mass gap. No SU(2) fixed-spacing weak-coupling decay theorem was found in the fetched pages.
