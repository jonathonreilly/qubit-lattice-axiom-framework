- Stefan Knabe (1988), “Energy gaps and elementary excitations for certain VBS-quantum antiferromagnets,” *Journal of Statistical Physics* 52, 627–638, DOI [10.1007/BF01019721](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01019721). Springer abstract read. Exact abstract statement: “It is shown for a class of antiferromagnetic Hamiltonians how one can get lower bounds for the energy gap above the ground state by diagonalizing a finite system.” Its page says the method is applied to certain spin chains, including a spin-1 chain. In the readable primary follow-up below, Knabe’s finite-size inequality is explicitly quoted: for `n>2`, `m>n`, the periodic-chain gap obeys `ε°_m ≥ ((n−1)/(n−2))(ε_n−1/(n−1))`; hence one open-chain gap `ε_n>1/(n−1)` yields a positive lower bound uniform in `m`. Conditions are a frustration-free, nearest-neighbour, translation-invariant 1D local Hamiltonian in the follow-up’s formulation. This result supplies no use for the gauge seed: it needs a quantum Hamiltonian local-gap input and does not bound a thermal trace sum on a 4D torus. The exact full hypotheses in Knabe’s original paper: NOT READ (abstract only).

- David Gosset and Evgeny Mozgunov (2016), “Local gap threshold for frustration-free spin systems,” *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 57, 091901, DOI [10.1063/1.4962337](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.00088); arXiv:1512.00088. Abstract and theorem statements read. The exact abstract says the 1D local threshold improves from `1/(n−1)` to `6/[n(n+1)]`, for a frustration-free translation-invariant local Hamiltonian, and is asymptotically optimal. Theorem 3 states, for `n>2`, `m>2n`, `ε°_m ≥ (5/6)((n²+n)/(n²−4))(ε_n−6/[n(n+1)])`; the 1D assumptions are nearest-neighbour and frustration-free, with periodic global chain and open local subchain. Thus `ε_n>6/[n(n+1)]` gives a positive size-uniform gap. The same paper’s 2D square-lattice result has a local patch threshold greater than `8/n²`, yielding a global periodic gap bounded below independently of system size; the patch has two rough and two smooth boundaries, and the authors say they do not know a result for general lattices. No compact Lie group or gauge-theory application is stated. Contribution to the crux: **no use** without a representation of the Wilson transfer problem as a frustration-free local quantum Hamiltonian plus the required local gap certificate.

## 7. Gap stability, propagation, and Euclidean gauge models

- **Elliott H. Lieb and Derek W. Robinson (1972), “The finite group velocity of quantum spin systems,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 28, 251–257, DOI 10.1007/BF01645779.** The Springer abstract page was read at [article page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01645779). Its exact abstract statement is a light-cone bound for finite-range quantum-spin interactions: for local observables, the commutator tends to zero exponentially outside a cone when the speed exceeds the interaction-dependent threshold. It is a dynamical locality bound, not a spectral-gap theorem. No use for the seed by itself.
- **Matthew B. Hastings and Tohru Koma (2006), “Spectral Gap and Exponential Decay of Correlations,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 265, 781–804, arXiv:math-ph/0507008, DOI 10.1007/s00220-006-0030-4.** Read arXiv HTML abstract and main statement. The abstract states that for quantum spin/fermion systems with short-range interactions, a nonvanishing spectral gap implies exponential decay of the relevant correlation; when observables commute at large distance, it is the connected correlation that decays exponentially. Thus it starts from a Hamiltonian gap and propagates it to correlations, the reverse direction from the missing finite-volume seed. No use to prove the seed.
- **Sergey Bravyi, Matthew B. Hastings and Spyridon Michalakis (2010), “Topological quantum order: stability under local perturbations,” *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 51, 093512, arXiv:1001.0344, DOI 10.1063/1.3490195.** Read full arXiv HTML abstract and Theorem 1. Under TQO-1,2 for a geometrically local commuting-projector quantum-spin Hamiltonian on a D-dimensional lattice, there is a threshold `J_0>0` (depending on the stated locality/decay and dimension parameters) such that a short-range bounded-norm perturbation of strength `J≤J_0` leaves separated spectral bands with constant gaps; the ground band has exponentially small width in lattice size. This is a stability theorem for an already gapped, topologically ordered reference Hamiltonian under a *small perturbation*. It does not derive the unperturbed Wilson SU(2) gap or a thermal torus seed. No use for this crux.
- **Dorit Aharonov, Itai Arad, Zeph Landau and Umesh Vazirani (2008 preprint), “The Detectability Lemma and Quantum Gap Amplification,” arXiv:0811.3412, [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/0811.3412).** Read arXiv HTML. The paper states that for a local Hamiltonian with bounded participation degree and a finite set of possible local terms, the terms can be partitioned into a constant number of mutually commuting layers; its detectability lemma controls the probability of detecting local constraint violations by these layers. The paper’s abstract/intro describe it as a tool for analyzing local frustration-free spin Hamiltonians and quantum gap amplification, not as an independent proof that a gauge Gibbs measure has a gap. Its input is a frustration-free local-projector structure. No use for the Wilson torus seed. (The separate Aharonov–Arad–Eldar–Kuwahara–Landau 2016 simplified proof, arXiv:1602.01210, says the ground-space projector is approximated by a product of local projectors and that coarse-graining a local frustration-free Hamiltonian with gap `γ>0` to scale `O(γ^(−1/2))` gives gap amplification; this assumes the local gap and is not a way to produce the Wilson seed.)

**Euclidean gauge analogue.** None of the source pages read gives an analogue of these propagation/stability theorems that proves the periodic Euclidean SU(2) Wilson seed. This is a bounded literature finding, not a nonexistence theorem. The structural mismatch is that LR/Hastings/BHM/detectability concern zero-temperature local Hamiltonians or their dynamics; the seed is a finite-temperature transfer trace ratio on a periodic Euclidean lattice. Converting between these settings and verifying the required locality, frustration-free/topological-order hypotheses, and uniform constants would be additional theorems. That last sentence is an inference from the statements and the local crux definition, not a claim made by the cited papers.

**Verdict for topic 7: no use** for the order-one seed. LR controls propagation once a local Hamiltonian is defined; Hastings–Koma turns a known gap into clustering; BHM preserves a known topological gap under a weak perturbation; the detectability lemma is a projector estimate for frustration-free Hamiltonians. None supplies the missing four-dimensional Wilson SU(2) finite-volume premise.

## Overall conclusion

The closest published analogues are conditional finite-size criteria in quantum spin chains, compact-continuous-spin mixing/LSI equivalences, and the 2D SU(N) all-coupling LSI abstract. The spin criteria need frustration-free Hamiltonian local gaps; the Gibbs criteria need mixing, curvature or Hessian bounds. Recent 4D lattice Yang–Mills results read here concern strong coupling, finite groups, Abelian `U(1)`, Wilson loops, or conditional confinement implications. Positive-operator and phase-transition frameworks add no verified order-one Wilson SU(2) estimate. No published source read states or proves the requested finite-volume seed for 4D SU(2) Wilson theory at a coupling of order one. This is the result of the pages read, not a literature-wide nonexistence claim.
