#### O(4) sharp-mass paper: new reading, Sections 4–8 and 10

Read the unread sections in the posted PDF. Exact source locations below use the printed section/page and the PDF text line numbers in the linked 102-page copy.

- **§4, pp. 34–37 (PDF lines 2381–2823):** deterministic Gaussian/block-map identities, not averaging over spin backgrounds. The smooth block observation and free precision yield uniformly localized conditional means and covariances. Lemma 4.2 completes the block map as a positive isometry and preserves exact quadratic energy. This part supplies machinery for the next exact integration; it is not the selective gap mechanism.
- **§5, pp. 38–55 (PDF lines 2824–4256):** defines an exact block observation and a retained density class, then integrates the original finite-cutoff law exactly. The class tracks seven canonical terms, regular errors, and a mandatory covering sum for rough bonds. The smooth sector uses Gaussian completion in relative quaternion coordinates; rough components are kept with explicit positive energy reserves, rather than discarded. Lemma 5.2's reserve is a pointwise bound on the admitted sector tests, not an average-over-background estimate. Lemma 5.3 controls joint prepared-factor products under one Gaussian. The canonical coefficient map is triangular and contracts its diagonal; the regular error contracts after its affine Hessian is removed; periods/winding discrepancies are retained as errors. The theorem gives an exact next density and quantitative comparison of two inputs. Thus this is a full-law RG step with bounds uniform over the retained class, not a claim that only typical inputs are controlled.
- **§6, pp. 56–60 (PDF lines 4257–4841):** computes the asymptotic kinetic drift in the paper's lattice normalization from the canonical coefficients of the exact map. The first two terms are α_h = −(log L)/π + decaying error and κ_h = −(log L)/(2π²) + decaying error; it also obtains the half-log correction needed for the √β prefactor. This is a local expansion of the map at the reference identity, backed by global error bounds from §5; it is not an independent typical-field stiffness estimate.
- **§7, pp. 61–64 (PDF lines 4841–5083):** shoots the bare coupling/depth so the exact trajectory stays admitted and hits a prescribed terminal-coupling interval, with (N\log L=\pi b-\frac12\log b+O(1)). For endpoint matching it compares complete positive laws, including errors and covering sums; it does not compare signed pieces alone.
- **§8, pp. 65–76 (PDF lines 5084–6066):** this is where *typical-field* control appears. Theorem 8.2 assumes two positive, reflection-positive laws and quantitative hypotheses on their full retained densities, then constructs a common measurable set (G) whose complement under either law is at most (C_H M^2e^{-a_HK}), with a small relative density error on (G). Lemma 8.3 proves joint rarity of any prescribed collection of rough bonds by reflection positivity/chessboard dissemination. Lemma 8.4 fills a rough cluster in (S^3) while fixing its outer boundary; the change has controlled energy/Jacobian cost. Lemmas 8.6–8.8 use exact inclusion–exclusion and a tree expansion for the signed covering sum, but only complete positive densities are used as denominators. The comparison of partition functions then follows by normalizing on (G). This is typical under the two specified Gibbs laws, not a supremum over every configuration. The complementary rough configurations remain explicitly controlled; they are not assumed absent.
- **§10, pp. 79–80 (PDF lines 6067–6190):** for the upper mass bound, uses the one-law rough-bond rarity result to show neighboring retained spins align in expectation; pulls a nonzero correlation at fixed coarse separation back to bounded local observables of the original spins. This is an expectation statement under the actual retained law, not a worst-case background bound.

**Where the O(4) proof averages and where it takes a supremum.** The mechanism's central §3 stiffness induction (already read in row 1009) is a supremum: each step assumes (H_c\le hI) for every pinned cell, every boundary-spin pin, every allowed signed bond assignment and every fixed right twist, and proves the enlarged-cell bound uniformly. The 2D quaternion commutator supplies the decrease. It is therefore not an example where the *stiffness gain itself* is proved only for typical pins. Typicality enters later: §8's full-density comparison uses high-probability good configurations under two laws, and §10 uses an expected rough-bond estimate under one law. §5 also integrates over all microscopic fields exactly and retains bad/rough sectors with a reserve. Thus the paper combines a worst-case pinned stiffness mechanism with probabilistic typical-field RG/cutoff comparison; it does not replace the former by the latter.

**Selectivity and gauge transfer.** The special algebra is global (O(4)\simeq S^3\) spin rotation: three imaginary quaternion directions, with noncommuting axes and a current-rotation bracket. The O(2) plane-rotator has one commuting axis and the low-temperature massless/BKT regime; the commutator gain vanishes there. This is a sharp selective spin-model precedent. It is not a gauge-field theorem: local gauge rotations of links are gauge redundancy, while the physical centre symmetry is discrete; the paper has no gauge-covariant analogue of its global (S^3) twist/current family, no 4D Wilson measure, and no gauge claim. The loop-soup mechanism in Ding–Huang–Yang below is a separate route.

#### Ding–Huang–Yang loop-soup proof

**Reference.** Jian Ding, Fenglin Huang, and Peng Yang, “Exponential decay for the two-dimensional Heisenberg model,” arXiv:2611.04908v1 [math.PR], submitted 3 Nov 2026. [Official arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04908); [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04908). Read the abstract, §§1–5 and the proof of Theorem 5.3 (the technical core), including the cited proof outline and the conditional annulus argument.

**Result and hypotheses.** Theorem 1.1 proves exponential spin-correlation decay for square-lattice O(N), every fixed integer N≥3 and every β>0, in finite boxes when each boundary spin-component is constant over boundary sites; it implies the stated free-boundary interior two-point bound. The authors say extension to arbitrary boundary conditions is natural but not immediate (Remark 1.3). Theorem 1.4 is the loop statement for real N>2: for a bounded nonnegative local-time profile (max β_v≤β₀), x-copy connection probabilities decay exponentially, with constants depending on β₀ and N. The proof develops 2<N≤3 and says N>3 follows verbatim.

**Mechanism in brief.** Generalize the metric-graph loop-soup representation to real loop intensity N/2, split loops into copies, and condition on total vertex local times. For N>2 there is a positive “extra” z-copy beyond the two critical intensity-1/2 x,y copies. Given its local-time field, the other copies form an XY model with reduced, random local times. With a fixed positive probability at each scale the z environment produces a good box where x/y crossings are unlikely; a multiscale construction shows good boxes occupy most of the relevant volume. Carefully chosen S¹ spin waves in the conditioned XY model make crossing unlikely uniformly across annuli. Conditional annulus bounds iterate to exponential connection decay, and the loop-spin coupling transfers this to spin correlations. The authors describe their result as a loop-soup perturbation of the XY model that yields exponential decay (Remark 1.5).

**Typical versus supremum.** The proof genuinely averages over the z-copy local-time environment: good boxes arise with positive probability and their abundance is controlled probabilistically across scales. It does not require every z background to be good. At the same time, several conditional box estimates are uniform in the specified xy boundary data and bounded local-time profiles. So it is typical in the auxiliary environment, with uniform conditional estimates, rather than an all-background supremum mechanism. It is not a YM background-field argument.

**Where selectivity enters; dimension and gauge.** The sharp change is the positive extra loop-copy intensity ε=N−2: at N=2 no z-copy exists, and the projected XY model retains its two-dimensional BKT/power-law regime; any ε>0 supplies a random local-time perturbation that improves the spin-wave bound. This is not a non-abelian Lie-algebra commutator mechanism: the authors explicitly say their approach does not require S² rotation symmetry and instead uses XY/S¹ spin waves. The proof is built on 2D metric-graph loop soups, two-dimensional XY/random-current estimates and annular crossing geometry; it gives no higher-dimensional extension. No gauge version is claimed or identified here, and the argument does not itself rule one out.
