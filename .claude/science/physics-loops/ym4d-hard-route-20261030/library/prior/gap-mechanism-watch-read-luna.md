## New or updated results

### 1. Yan Ru Pei, O(n) anisotropy, arXiv:2611.01532v2 (11 November)

The v2 theorem says: for every finite inverse temperature beta, some delta(beta)>0 makes the two-dimensional S²-valued model with nearest-neighbor couplings `sum_i J^i sigma_x^i sigma_y^i` exponentially decaying in spin two-point functions, uniformly over finite free-boundary subgraphs and edge strengths up to beta, if every `|J^i-1| <= delta(beta)`. It also covers aligned single-ion anisotropy and nearly isotropic O(n), n>=3 models. For n>=4 it gives, under its stated small-anisotropy condition and beta <= (1-epsilon)|log delta|/(2 pi), exponential decay of odd one-site observables away from arbitrary boundary pins and two-point decay at scale `exp(pi beta + O(sqrt(beta log beta)))`. That latter assertion is explicitly conditional on estimates of the recent O(4) manuscript and transfers odd observables only.

This is a new version after November 1, and row 1117 already read and recorded v2. It is a real two-dimensional non-Abelian-symmetry spin-correlation result at any fixed finite beta, with a small anisotropy window depending on beta. It is not a full singlet mixing theorem, a periodic thermal sum, or a gauge-theory bound. The proof uses a finite annulus input from Ding–Huang–Yang and Ginibre/positive-association sign-cluster arguments. No computer-assisted proof step is identified in the abstract or the portions read. A one-scale stability argument for an O(n) sheet is a possible model-specific idea; it does not transfer the gauge action, periodic sectors, or the lane's group-selective inequality. Credit Yan Ru Pei, Ding, Huang and Yang, and OpenAI for the O(4) input.

### 2. Bringmann, Cao, Hairer and Zhao, 2D non-Abelian Yang–Mills–Higgs, arXiv:2611.03640v1 (2 November)

For a connected compact Lie group G, a finite-dimensional orthogonal representation on V, a G-invariant quadratic Higgs term M, and the paper's two-dimensional torus setup, the authors construct the non-Abelian Yang–Mills–Higgs measure by stochastic quantisation. The stochastic flow is globally well-posed modulo gauge on gauge orbits, has uniform-in-time estimates, and is topologically irreducible and strong Feller, hence has a unique invariant probability measure. The result is non-perturbative and is stated to work for any fixed coupling constants. Pure Yang–Mills is included as V={0}.

This proves construction and uniqueness of the stochastic-dynamics invariant measure, not a quantitative mixing rate, spectral gap, slab bound, or excited-state thermal-sum bound. Thus it supplies no order-one gap mechanism despite reading a non-Abelian group. Its stochastic quantisation and global energy/noise estimates are a possible conceptual input for a 2D continuum model; the paper's 2D SPDE bounds do not furnish a 4D lattice block estimate. No computer-assisted proof step is identified in the text inspected. Credit Bjoern Bringmann, Sky Cao, Martin Hairer and Wenhao Zhao.

### 3. Updated Bhattacharjee et al. 4D lattice paper, arXiv:2610.00341v6 (dated 2 November)

The new v6 advertises explicit fixed-spacing 4D Wilson-theory gaps in forest gauge. Its exact useful ranges include:

- **SU(2), Theorem 1:** `0 <= beta_W < 1/3`; free-boundary box expectations converge along every exhausting sequence to a unique infinite-volume gauge-invariant state. Lipschitz truncated correlations decay at every rate below `-log(3 beta_W)`, and its transfer matrix has `m >= -log(3 beta_W) > 0`. This is the hand single-link estimate already substantially present in row 1124.
- **SU(2), Theorem 3:** `0 <= beta_W <= 0.351`; the stated gap is `m >= -1/2 log alpha(beta_W) > 0`, with `alpha(1/3)<=0.9428`, `alpha(0.345)<=0.9805`, `alpha(0.351)<=0.99995`. But the 0.351 endpoint relies on ball-arithmetic evaluations of the constants. Under this lane's no-computer-assisted rule, that improvement is not a discharged proof step.
- **Every compact simple G, Theorem 5:** in the paper's normalization `N|beta| < 2 kappa_G/29`, with semisimple Lie algebra and `kappa_G` the least Ricci curvature in the chosen faithful representation, the same uniqueness, decay and transfer-gap conclusion follows with the displayed explicit `m_G(beta)`. For SU(N), `beta_W < N^2/29`; this is a small strong-coupling interval, not the order-one continuum window.
- **SU(3):** the paper advertises a plaquette-block extension to `beta_W=0.77` (Theorem 7). Its bounding constants are evaluated on hundreds of boxes with rigorous numerical error bounds. That computer-assisted evaluation cannot discharge the theorem for this lane. The paper also gives an analytic curvature theorem with a smaller range.

The block idea is the closest 4D local analogue: forest gauge reduces the link influence, then a three-link plaquette block or curvature estimate reads representation/geometry-dependent data. The only new numeric stretch beyond row 1124's `beta_W<1/3` is disallowed by the task's rule. The paper's continuum theorem is conditional: it says a Dobrushin–Shlosman estimate for one fixed-physical-size cube along `beta(a)->infinity`, plus existence of the renormalized limit, would imply a continuum gap. Its Section XI states that the cube estimate and limit are both unproved. It therefore does not prove a continuum mass gap or furnish the missing cube estimate. Source also identifies the conventional SU(2) `beta_W=4 beta` normalization. Credit Deep Bhattacharjee, Ushashi Bhattacharya, James Montgomery Lockwood and Tristan Romanov.

### 4. Kevin Remondière, periodic-box criterion, 12 November

The note “A scalar finite-volume criterion for a continuum mass gap in SU(2) lattice gauge theory” states the following conditional 4D Wilson-torus implication. Under hypercubic symmetry and a positive trace-class transfer representation, define `Delta_beta(n)=16 log Z(n^4)-log Z((2n)^4)`. If `Delta_beta(n)<=2^-9`, then `Delta_beta(2n)<=256 Delta_beta(n)^2`, and the excited eigenvalues obey `(lambda_i/lambda_0)^n <= 256 Delta_beta(n) <= 1/2` on the specified spatial tori `(2jn)^3`. Along a continuum sequence `a_k=ell/n_k`, if this one-scale test holds with `Delta_beta_k(n_k)<=eta<=2^-9` and the lattice OS forms converge, then the limit has the stated q-gap with `q=256 eta`; with OS reconstruction it has mass at least `-log(256 eta)/ell >= log(2)/ell` and a unique vacuum. Dimension three has the analogous `2^-7`/64 constants.

The transfer representation, continuum convergence and reconstruction are hypotheses. No test value is proved at order-one Wilson coupling. A claimed strong-coupling example at `beta<=0.005` depends on a finite enumeration and program-reported rational comparison, disallowed here. The criterion's implication is Lean-formalized, but formalization does not establish its missing hypotheses under the lane's rule. This is the closest new periodic-box mechanism and could be adapted as a format for a 4D thermal seed; it is group-blind once its transfer hypotheses are assumed and gives no SU(2)-specific inequality for Delta. Row 1125 already found that a periodic even-trace tree returns to blocked EV-STEP/Z0 inputs. Credit Kevin Remondière; the note itself says it transposes OpenAI's O(4) doubling inequality and makes no priority claim.
