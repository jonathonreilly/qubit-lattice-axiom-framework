## A — a full thermal bound on a window of shapes

Use A's normalization \(\beta_W=N_c^2\beta\), where \(N_c\) is the defining representation dimension; its cutoff parameter is separately denoted \(N\), with \(a_N=1/N\). For a periodic spatial torus \(s=(s_1,s_2,s_3)\), let
\[
V(s)=s_1s_2s_3,\quad R_s=T_s/\lambda_0(T_s),\quad
\mathcal Z_s(T)=\operatorname{Tr}R_s^T=Z(s,T)/\lambda_0(T_s)^T.
\]
Theorem F, pp. 59–60, assumes its torus-construction hypothesis **(W)** and
\[
\begin{gathered}
\exists t_0>0,\ 0<y_0\le1/20,\ p_0,\ N_0(p)\quad
\forall p\ge p_0\ \forall N\ge N_0(p)\ \exists T_0\in\mathbb Z,
\quad3\le T_0\le Nt_0,\\
\log\mathcal Z_s(T_0)\le\gamma V(s)\quad
\text{for every integer }s_i\in[2T_0,48T_0),\qquad
\gamma=\frac{y_0}{2(96T_0)^3}.                                           \tag{A-B*}
\end{gathered}
\]
All estimates are at the actual \(\beta_{N,p}\). Thus “one bound” is a family over shapes, cutoff and volume, at one physical time scale. It is on the **full gauge-invariant slice space**, without the lane's centre-neutral projection.

Proposition 7.57 replaces it by finite unnormalized partition functions:
\[
Z(s,T_0)^2\le e^{\gamma V(s)}Z(s,2T_0)
\quad(2T_0\le s_i<48T_0).                                               \tag{A-B**}
\]
A-B** implies A-B* with the same \(\gamma\); A-B* implies A-B** with \(2\gamma\). Indeed, its doubling defect obeys
\(\log\mathcal Z_s(T)\le2\log Z(s,T)-\log Z(s,2T)\le2\log\mathcal Z_s(T)\).
This is the same thermal-purity mechanism already present in the lane's older full periodic seed; the precise window and ascent theorem here are credited to A's six authors.

\[

\quad\sigma(H)\subset\{0\}\cup[\Delta,\infty),\quad\dim\ker H=1,
\]
and makes all (W)-phases coincide. It does not additionally assume the cube/slab estimate or exchange of limits (U).

## B — the cube dynamics and its local spectral form

Use \(\beta_W=N_c^2\beta\). For the Wilson measure \(\mu\) on an even four-torus \(\Lambda_R\), let \(V_x\) contain links based in \(x+[0,n)^4\), and
\(\mathcal P_xf=\mathbb E_\mu[f\mid U_{E\setminus V_x}]\). Let \(\mathcal A\) be its gauge-invariant functions. Definition 62 is
\[
\mathrm{BP}(n,\varpi):\quad
\varpi\operatorname{Var}_\mu f\le\mathcal E_n(f):=
\frac1{n^4}\sum_x\mu(|f-\mathcal P_xf|^2)
\quad(\text{all real }f\in\mathcal A).                                  \tag{BP}
\]
This is the gap of an **auxiliary cube heat-bath dynamics**, normalized so each link updates at rate one. It is not the physical time transfer gap or a thermal trace. A's Theorem B uses this same inequality; its Theorem E uses it on cylinders as (D\(_\ell\)), together with (W) and (U), or the thermal replacement (Z).

B's Theorem 66 assumes
\[
\exists\varpi,\ell>0\ \forall k\ \exists n_k\ge2:\ n_ka_k\le\ell,
\quad\mathrm{BP}(n_k,\varpi)\text{ at }\beta_k
\text{ on an infinite set of even }R\ge2n_k+4.                           \tag{B-BP}
\]
At each cutoff it takes a torus limit along that set, **before** the continuum limit. It concludes
\[

m_*=16\varpi/(625e^2\ell),\qquad\dim\ker H=1,

for every continuum limit satisfying its observable/Hilbert-space interface. Existence and nontriviality of the desired Yang–Mills limit remain separate.

Definition 83 and Theorem 84 weaken BP to the following cyclic spectral estimate. Set
\(\mathcal K=R^{-4}\sum_x\mathcal P_x\), \(\varepsilon=\varpi n^4/R^4\). Then
\[
\mathrm{BP}_{\rm loc}(n,\varpi):\quad
\|\mathcal K^t(f-\mu f)\|_2\le(1-\varepsilon)^t\|f-\mu f\|_2
\quad\forall t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},                                      \tag{B-local}
\]
for every real gauge-invariant \(f\) supported in a box of side at most \(R/2\). B-local, with the same uniform tuning quantifiers as B-BP, suffices. For SU(\(N_c\)), BP on the four-dimensional centre-neutral sector, denoted \(\mathrm{BP}_0\), also suffices:
\(\mathrm{BP}\Rightarrow\mathrm{BP}_0\Rightarrow\mathrm{BP}_{\rm loc}\).
It is the cube dynamics' neutral sector on a **four-torus**, not N's neutral physical transfer sector on a **spatial three-torus**. A one-time Rayleigh bound for each local function does not replace the all-\(t\) cyclic estimate.

 \frac{W_1^{d_V}(\gamma_V(\cdot\mid Q),\gamma_V(\cdot\mid Q'))}

Here \(V\) consists of the active links in an even side-\(n\) cube of the forest gauge. ## C — Zeng's common rate, and the later tunneling package

Both papers define the principal missing rate on the fixed-volume **continuum** correlations \(S_p\):
\[
\exists\epsilon_0>0\quad\forall\text{ finite label words }A\quad
\exists C_A<\infty:\quad
\sup_p\left|S_p(A^*A_s)-|S_p(A)|^2\right|
 \le C_Ae^{-\epsilon_0s}\quad\forall s\ge0.                              \tag{C-MG}
\]
The constant may depend on the word; the rate may not depend on word or volume. It is Proposition 10.34 of C492 (p. 45) and Proposition 10.26 of C543 (p. 43). Their finite-cutoff transfer proposal is
\[
\exists\epsilon_0>0:\quad
\|\widehat T_{N,p}|_{\Omega_{N,p}^{\perp}}\|
 \le e^{-a_N\epsilon_0}\quad\text{for all }N,p,                           \tag{C-MG2''}
\]
where \(\widehat T\) has top eigenvalue one. On one fixed continuum vacuum representation, the corresponding all-word exponential clustering statement is equivalent to the positive spectral gap by the Laplace spectral argument (C492 Proposition 10.22; C543 Proposition 10.19). On one channel it gives only that channel's threshold.

**What these papers prove, and the changed bridge.** They give conditional constructions from separately registered RG/analytic inputs, spectral/Laplace lemmas, the implication from a uniform spectral exclusion to the subsequential continuum gap, and examples separating clustering without a common rate from a gap. The finite-kernel positivity argument gives only a pointwise finite-volume gap at finite parameters, with a bound degenerating as the volume grows (C543 Lemma 10.11 and Proposition 10.14). The conditional construction follows its ultraviolet schedule; no volume- and cutoff-uniform Wilson weak-coupling interval is established for C-MG or C-MG2''. C492 asserts C-MG implies C-MG2'' in Proposition 10.34(1). **C543 explicitly registers that finite-cutoff bridge as open** in Proposition 10.26(1), while retaining the direct C-MG-to-continuum-gap implication. Recovering a uniform finite-cutoff gap from limiting correlations is extra input. Both leave the converse from a selected continuum gap to the volume-uniform C-MG open. The existence of a continuum gap cannot be cited as their proof of that converse.

C543's Remark 1.3 also limits its gapless comparison model to the axiomatic/phase/identification inputs: it does **not** instantiate the Wilson construction inputs (RG), (UV1), (C1).
