## 1. Adjoint SO(3) field and Z2 sector; random couplings

**Exact split (literature statement).** In the normalization printed by Kovács–Tomboulis, the finite-lattice Wilson SU(2) partition function is rewritten using SO(3) link variables `Û_b` and plaquette signs `σ_p∈{±1}` as an integral/sum with weight `exp[β Σ_p |tr U_p| σ_p]` and constraints `δ[η_c(Û) σ_c]` on every cube; on a torus additional global sheet/twist data matter. (The factor multiplying β changes with trace/action convention.) Thus, conditional on an SO(3) field, the plaquette factors look like a Z2 gauge weight with nonnegative, field-dependent couplings proportional to `β |tr U_p|`; the σ variables are constrained and then annealed together with the SO(3) field. This is an exact finite-lattice identity, not a decoupled independent random-bond model. It is the closest structural match to row 1155's adjoint formula and the candidate in row 1158(A). Credit E. T. Tomboulis and T. G. Kovács. Source: [Kovács–Tomboulis, hep-lat/9607068](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9607068), [hep-lat/9711009](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9711009); also the exact-representation discussion in [de Forcrand–Jahn, hep-lat/0211004](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0211004).

**Mack–Petkova comparison.** The 1979 article abstract reports an inequality comparing SU(2) and restricted Z2 Wilson-loop expectations and the implication that Z2 confinement gives confinement in their SU(2) model. Mack's later open exposition states the comparison `⟨tr U(C)⟩_{SU(2)} ≤ 2⟨σ(C)⟩_{Z2}` for the same β convention and also describes a vortex-container criterion: sufficiently fast decrease of the vortex free energy with container thickness is a sufficient condition for confinement. The latter bounds through a Fourier coefficient of the container-ratio law. These are one-way comparisons/conditional criteria, not an upper bound on the row-1155 rectangle affinity or on a finite-box low-energy count. They do not turn the dependent, constrained couplings `β|tr U_p|` above into independent disorder. No order-one coupling interval is asserted by the sources read; hypotheses include the matched actions and stated lattice/container setup. The argument is analytic; no computer-assisted step. Credit Gerhard Mack and V. B. Petkova. [1979 article record and abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0003491679903464); [Mack's open exposition, DESY 80/03, §5](https://www-library.desy.de/preparch/desy/scan/1980/desy80-003.kek.pdf); the related container criterion is summarized in the previously read `gap-lit-transport.md` §B.

**Other named vortex papers.** Kovács–Tomboulis (1996) give a *conditional* confinement mechanism in 3D/4D SU(2): two stated Z2-monopole correlation inequalities imply a nonzero effective field for the dual Ising/Z2 system; the needed bounded winding-monopole-pair free energy is presented as an assumption supported by semiclassical reasoning and Monte Carlo, not proved. Their 1997 weak-coupling work gives simulation evidence that thick vortices linked with a Wilson loop reproduce the string tension and that excluding them removes it; this is numerical, with no theorem coupling interval. Alexandrou, de Forcrand and D'Elia's Laplacian-center-gauge study likewise examines an SU(2) configuration sample at zero/finite temperature and reports numerical center-dominance tests, not a decomposition theorem or rigorous disorder estimate. de Forcrand–Jahn's Villain SO(3) study discusses twist sectors and an analytic weak-coupling path relating the SO(3) theory to SU(2) summed over twist sectors, but its free-energy measurements and scale conclusion are Monte Carlo; it does not prove a bound on the adjoint-collar affinity. These works give motivation and exact representations/conditional implications; the numerical evidence cannot discharge rows 1153–1157. Sources: [hep-lat/9607068](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9607068), [hep-lat/9711009](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9711009), [Alexandrou–de Forcrand–D'Elia, hep-lat/9909005](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9909005), [de Forcrand–Jahn, hep-lat/0211004](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/0211004).

**Random-plaquette Z2 results.** Wang, Harrington and Preskill define the 3D random-plaquette gauge model with independent quenched signs `η_p=±1` of wrong-sign concentration `p`, coupling `J`, and weight `exp(J Σ_p η_p U_p)`. Flux tubes end at monopoles where the quenched signs violate the cube Bianchi condition. They identify a Nishimori line and use Monte Carlo to estimate the zero-temperature critical concentration `p_{c0}=0.0293±0.0002`, a lower bound on the Nishimori-line threshold. This is numerical phase-boundary evidence, not a rigorous uniform spectral or affinity bound. Ohno–Arakawa–Ichinose–Matsui's 3D/4D RPGM threshold and phase-boundary studies are also Monte Carlo. Their disorder is independent quenched ±J, unlike the correlated, nonnegative, SO(3)-dependent annealed couplings in the exact SU(2) split. I found no rigorous theorem for that dependent constrained random-coupling ensemble that yields row 1155's strict affinity inequality. Credit Chenyang Wang, Jim Harrington and John Preskill; Takuya Ohno, Gaku Arakawa, Ikuo Ichinose and Tetsuo Matsui. [Wang–Harrington–Preskill](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0207088), [Ohno et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0401101).

## 2. Low-eigenvalue counting on a finite box

**Exact nearby counting theorem (different operator).** Molchanov and Vainberg's heat-kernel bound applies to `H=H0+V(x)`, where `H0` is self-adjoint and nonnegative, `V=V_+−V_-` is a real multiplication potential, and their semigroup/heat-kernel assumptions (a)–(c) hold. It bounds the number `N_0(V)` of nonpositive eigenvalues by `g(1)^{-1} ∫_0^∞ π(t)t^{-1} ∫_X G(tW(x))dμ(x)dt`, with `W=V_-` and `π(t)` a uniform diagonal heat-kernel majorant (their Theorem 2.1). For symmetric graph Laplacians with `a(x,x)>0`, `a(x,y)=a(y,x)≤0`, and row sums zero, their Theorem 3.1 gives `N_0(H0+V)≤C(h)[#X_h^+ + Σ_{x∈X_h^-}W(x)^{d/2}]` if the diagonal heat kernel has `π(t)≤Ct^{-d/2}` at large t; `X_h^+={W>h^{-1}}`, `X_h^-={W≤h^{-1}}`. This is a rigorous potential-counting bound for scalar graph Schrödinger operators (including discrete Laplacian examples), with no gauge coupling parameter and no numerical step. It needs an exact heat-kernel majorant and a scalar multiplication potential. It does not apply as stated to the nonlinear compact-group transfer operator or its magnetic energy on the gauge-invariant configuration space. Source: S. Molchanov and B. Vainberg, [arXiv:0812.2968](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.2968).

The searched results do not yield a Weyl/CLR bound for the number of low-lying eigenvalues of the compact-SU(2) gauge transfer operator or Hamiltonian with magnetic energy as a confining potential. Finite-group gauge-Hilbert-space dimension formulas count basis states, not levels below a threshold, and do not count levels in a continuous-group Wilson transfer operator. The familiar finite-box compactness/positivity of a transfer kernel implies discrete eigenvalues but no uniform count or useful energy-weighted estimate.

The strongest close target result remains the outline PL15 estimate just recorded: an energy-weighted momentum density under translation invariance and reflection positivity, not an unweighted level count and not a magnetic-potential CLR inequality. Row 1153 (24) needs a count with a thermal energy weight; row 1155's (1)–(2) isolate the residual states and adjoint representation; a generic counting theorem would only help after proving a comparison of its kinetic operator/potential, gauge constraint, heat kernel, and box-volume constants with that specific transfer spectrum. These statements are analytic/combinatorial; the spectral literature's numerical density-of-states studies are not rigorous bounds. No computer-assisted calculation was used here.

## 3. Three-dimensional slice models and SO(3) monopoles

The exact SU(2) → SO(3)×Z2 rewriting and the monopole constraints above are the only close theorem-level results located for the one-slice idea. Kovács–Tomboulis's 3D and 4D statements are conditional in their monopole-correlation hypothesis; their empirical support is numerical. de Forcrand–Jahn's SO(3) Villain work is a finite-lattice topological/twist-sector analysis with numerical free-energy measurements, not a rigorous 3D effective-slice inequality. The SO(3) monopole papers define gauge-invariant Z2 cube defects and study their suppression/correlation, often with modified actions or simulation; they do not give a coupling-uniform affinity/spectral estimate for the 3D law induced by a 4D transfer.

The positive nearby mathematical results have mismatched scope: (i) strong-coupling transfer/spectral-gap results for Wilson SU(N) or SO(N) require their explicit small-β convergent range (see the prior digest `gap-lit-recent.md`; no continuum or order-one weak-coupling estimate); (ii) Adhikari–Cao's rigorous exponential covariance decay is for finite, possibly non-Abelian gauge groups in four dimensions under a large-β weak-coupling threshold, not continuous SO(3)/SU(2); (iii) three-dimensional Yang–Mills–Higgs scaling limits concern a Higgs model and fixed-length matter, not a pure three-dimensional slice action inherited from a 4D Wilson transfer; and (iv) finite-group Z2 RPGM results above are mostly phase-diagram simulations.
