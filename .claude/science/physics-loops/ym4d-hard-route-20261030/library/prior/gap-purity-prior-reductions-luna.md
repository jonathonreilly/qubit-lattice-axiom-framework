## Closest direct comparison: one-cube continuum criterion

**Deep Bhattacharjee, Ushashi Bhattacharya, James Montgomery Lockwood, and Tristan Romanov, “Yang–Mills Mass Gap on the Lattice and One Cube from the Continuum,” Hexagon preprint 2610.00341, v6, dated 2 November 2026.** The paper is available [here](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00341v6). It states a direct, conditional continuum-gap theorem for four-dimensional lattice Yang–Mills with compact simple gauge group.

Definition 42 defines `D(n, alpha)` from conditional laws in one finite cube `V` of active links. For each outside link `j`, `C_{V,j}` is the worst-case Wasserstein response of the cube law to changing only `j`. Sum these responses over each of at most 64 period-two classes. The condition is `K_c <= alpha (n/2)^4` for every class (paper lines 2052–2111). Theorem 43 says this condition implies a transfer-matrix gap at least `log(1/alpha)/(2n)` (2113–2119).

Theorem 45 assumes one fixed `alpha < 1`, one physical bound `ell`, and even box sizes `n_k` with `n_k a_k <= ell`, such that `D(n_k, alpha)` holds at `beta_k` for **every** cutoff `k`. Every continuum limit then has simple vacuum and spectrum contained in `{0} union [log(1/alpha)/(2 ell), infinity)`. If the limit is nontrivial, it has a physical mass gap of that size (2172–2180). The paper says `beta_k` typically tends to infinity. Thus the premise is at weak bare coupling along the continuum tuning, at a fixed physical box size. The cube size in lattice units grows as the spacing shrinks.

**Comparison with `(PUR-io)`: different in kind.** `D(n, alpha)` is a uniform boundary-response criterion for all outside links and classes. It must hold at every cutoff and implies a continuum gap in every limit. `(PUR-io)` asks for one neutral-sector trace ratio at one aspect and only infinitely many cutoffs. In cutoff quantification, the cube criterion is stronger. The observables and boundary quantifiers differ, so I found no implication between their hypotheses. The consumer of `D(n, alpha)` is proved in the preprint's Theorems 43 and 45. The paper does not prove `D(n, alpha)` along the tuning. It also does not prove that a nontrivial continuum limit exists. The authors report Lean certificates for selected combinatorics and finite arithmetic, not for Theorems 43 or 45, nor for the cube estimate. No whole-paper formal verification is reported. One short source phrase: “the two statements that remain unproved” (lines 2048–2049).

The same paper's Theorem 46 gives a second consumer. Assume that each test in a spanning family of renormalized observables has a correlation bound `C_f exp(-m_0 tau)` for all sufficiently fine cutoffs and all sufficiently large dyadic physical times. Then every continuum limit has simple vacuum and spectrum outside zero at or above `m_0` (lines 2213–2245). This resembles a finite observable-family route, but the paper does not state that the spanning family is finite. It is different from the one scalar purity test. The premise is along the continuum sequence and in physical units; the paper proves this implication, but not the assumed correlation decay.

The paper explicitly tests its `D` criterion against weak 4D compact `U(1)`: Proposition 80 says `D` fails at sufficiently large beta. That gives the desired model selectivity for this criterion, as stated in that paper (lines 4254 onward). It is evidence about this comparator, not a proof of `(PUR-io)` or its selectivity.

## Other direct Yang–Mills or gauge-theory reductions

**Sourav Chatterjee, “A Probabilistic Mechanism for Quark Confinement,” Communications in Mathematical Physics 385 (2021), 1007–1039, arXiv:2006.16229.** [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16229), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-021-04086-y). For a closed connected matrix Lie group on a lattice in dimension at least two, Definition 2.3 defines exponential decay under arbitrary boundary conditions: constants `K1,K2>0` bound the connected covariance of any two bounded local edge observables by `K1 exp(-K2 dist)` in every finite cube and for every boundary condition. Theorem 2.4 says this hypothesis implies unbroken slab center symmetry and an area law for Wilson loops in any finite-dimensional unitary representation on which the center acts nontrivially. Theorem 2.2 gives the area-law conclusion from unbroken slab center symmetry alone. The coupling is fixed; there is no continuum limit or weak-coupling hypothesis. The hypothesis is uniform over all cubes, boundary conditions, and local observables, rather than one finite box or one finite observable. The conclusion is confinement by area law, not a transfer-matrix mass gap. It is therefore different in kind from `(PUR-io)`; its broad uniformity makes the assumption much stronger in spatial and observable scope, but no implication between it and the purity estimate is stated. The theorem's consumer is proved in the paper. I found no report of formal verification.

**Lluis Eriksson, “Morse–Bott Spectral Reduction and the Yang–Mills Mass Gap on the Lattice,” viXra 2602.0035v1 (February 2026).** [Preprint PDF](https://www.ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0035v1.pdf). This is a viXra preprint. Its Theorem 6.3 is explicitly conditional on Balaban's RG input (Theorem 6.1) and two terminal-scale hypotheses. H1 assumes a transfer-matrix gap at the final RG cutoff of at least `c0>0`, uniformly in box size. H2 assumes RG preserves exponential decay of connected gauge-invariant correlations at rate `m_nmax/a_nmax`. Under them the conclusion is a lattice mass gap at least `c0 exp(-C/g^2)`, with `C=24 pi^2 ln(2)/(11 Nc)`, uniformly in lattice size at weak bare coupling. The proof also uses the stated Balaban flow and polymer error bounds. This is two inputs plus the RG premises, not one finite observable estimate. The cutoff is terminal and finite; the stated result is a weak-coupling lattice bound, not a fixed-physical-volume continuum theorem. The terminal gap and decay transport are premises, not established there. The consumer is a conditional argument in the preprint; no formal verification is reported. Relative to `(PUR-io)`, the hypothesis is different in kind: it assumes a terminal gap and its RG stability, while `(PUR-io)` seeks a neutral-sector purity bound along infinitely many cutoffs. Neither implies the other as stated.

**Lluis Eriksson, “Exponential Clustering and Mass Gap for Four-Dimensional SU(N) Lattice Yang–Mills Theory Via Balaban’s Renormalization Group and Multiscale Correlator Decoupling,” viXra 2602.0088v1 (February 2026).** [Preprint PDF](https://ai.vixra.org/pdf/2602.0088v1.pdf). Hypothesis 5.2 assumes constants `gamma0,rho0>0` such that, for every weak coupling `gbar <= gamma0`, every lattice spacing `eta>0`, and every physical volume `Lphys>0`, the pure Wilson measure has log-Sobolev constant at least `rho0`, uniformly in spacing and volume. Theorem 1.1 then states exponential decay of connected correlations for bounded gauge-invariant local observables with positive mass `m`, uniformly in `eta` and `Lphys` for sufficiently small `eta`. Corollary 1.2 gives a physical mass gap along sequences with `eta -> 0` and `Lphys -> infinity`. These are weak-coupling, continuum and infinite-volume statements. The log-Sobolev premise is not proved in the preprint. Theorem 1.3's reconstruction discussion does not establish the full O(4) OS1 condition, which the text identifies as missing for the full Clay result. No formal verification is reported. The log-Sobolev hypothesis ranges over every physical volume and all the stated weak couplings, and is a functional inequality, not a single neutral-sector purity observable. It is different in kind and much broader in quantification than `(PUR-io)`; no implication between hypotheses is established. The consumer is conditional, while the hypothesis and the full continuum reconstruction remain open in that account.

These two Eriksson texts do not establish their assumed inputs. Their conclusions therefore provide conditional reductions, not evidence that the Yang–Mills gap has been proved. I did not find a finite-box criterion in the Sussman–Alpöge construction that supplies either input.

## Spin-system and quantum-chain context

These results concern classical Gibbs uniqueness/mixing or quantum spin Hamiltonians. They illustrate the finite-size-criterion pattern but do not directly address Yang–Mills or imply `(PUR-io)`.

**R. L. Dobrushin and S. B. Shlosman, “Constructive Criterion for the Uniqueness of Gibbs Field,” in *Statistical Physics and Dynamical Systems*, Birkhäuser (1985), pp. 347–370, DOI [10.1007/978-1-4899-6653-7_20](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-6653-7_20).** Their finite-volume `C_V` criterion is a condition on the Gibbs specification in a finite set `V`; when it holds for some finite volume, it yields uniqueness of the infinite-volume Gibbs field. The chapter's criterion is not numbered as a theorem. The hypothesis is at fixed interaction parameters; the finite set `V` is a lattice volume, not a fixed physical-size box in a continuum sequence, and the criterion does not invoke weak bare coupling. This is a local finite-volume-to-infinite-volume uniqueness criterion, but its conclusion is Gibbs uniqueness, not a Yang–Mills mass gap. The hypothesis and conclusion are different in kind from `(PUR-io)`. No formal verification was reported in the source.

**Fabio Martinelli, Enzo Olivieri, and Roberto H. Schonmann, “For 2-D Lattice Spin Systems Weak Mixing Implies Strong Mixing,” Communications in Mathematical Physics 165 (1994), 33–47, DOI [10.1007/BF02099735](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099735).** Theorem 1.1 assumes weak spatial mixing for a finite-range spin system on `Z^2`. It concludes that on every square, changing boundary conditions changes the distribution in an interior subset by at most `C exp(-gamma ell)`, where `ell` is the distance to the boundary sites on which the conditions differ. The interaction parameters are fixed. The square size is in lattice units; there is no weak-coupling premise, continuum sequence, or fixed physical-size box. This is a finite-volume mixing conclusion from a global weak-mixing premise, not a one-box gap criterion. It differs in kind from `(PUR-io)` and has no mass-gap consumer. I found no formal verification report.

**Stefan Knabe, “Energy Gaps and Elementary Excitations for Certain VBS-Quantum Antiferromagnets,” Journal of Statistical Physics 52 (1988), 627–638, DOI [10.1007/BF01019721](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01019721); David Gosset and Evgeny Mozgunov, “Local Gap Threshold for Frustration-Free Spin Systems,” Journal of Mathematical Physics 57 (2016), 091901, arXiv:1512.00088; and Marius Lemm, “Finite-Size Criteria for Spectral Gaps in D-Dimensional Quantum Spin Systems,” Communications in Mathematical Physics 372 (2019), 853–875, arXiv:1902.07141.** Knabe's periodic-chain criterion is the starting point: for a translation-invariant, frustration-free, finite-range chain, a sufficiently large gap on a finite open subchain implies a positive thermodynamic periodic-chain gap. Gosset–Mozgunov improve the threshold: Theorem 1 states for `n>3` that if the open-chain local gap exceeds `6/[n(n+1)]`, periodic-chain gaps are bounded below by a positive constant (the older Knabe threshold is `1/(n-1)`). The assumptions are fixed Hamiltonian couplings and finite chain sizes; there is no weak-coupling or continuum limit, and no fixed physical-size scaling parameter. This is a finite local Hamiltonian gap criterion, different in kind from Wilson-measure purity. The implication is proved in the cited paper; no formal verification is reported.

Lemm's Theorem 3.1 extends the pattern to `D`-dimensional nearest-neighbor, translation-invariant frustration-free Hamiltonians with periodic boundary conditions. If the open subsystem of linear size `n` has gap `gamma_n` and the periodic full system has linear size `N >= 2n+1`, it gives `gamma_N >= gamma_n - 1/n - 2/n^2`. In particular, a local gap above the explicit threshold `3/n` gives a positive system-size-independent lower bound. These are fixed finite-dimensional spin interactions; the finite box is measured in lattice sites, with no continuum, weak-coupling, or fixed physical-size parameter. Again the assumption is a local Hamiltonian spectral gap, not `(PUR-io)`'s gauge-theory trace ratio. The consumer is proved; no formal verification is reported.

A boundary version is due to **Marius Lemm and Evgeny Mozgunov, “Spectral Gaps of Frustration-Free Spin Systems with Boundary,” Journal of Mathematical Physics 60 (2019), 051901, arXiv:1801.08915.** Theorem 2.5 gives for a one-dimensional chain a lower bound on the full-chain gap from both the bulk gap `gamma_n^B` and the minimum edge gap `gamma_{n-1}^E`: for `m >= 8` and `4 <= n <= m/2`,

`gamma_m >= [min{gamma_n^B, gamma_{n-1}^E} - 2 sqrt(6) n^(-3/2)] / [2^8 sqrt(6n)]`.

Thus the finite-volume condition must control both bulk and edge modes. It is a fixed-coupling frustration-free spin-chain criterion; its length is in lattice sites, with no weak-coupling, continuum, or fixed physical-size scaling. It is distinct in kind from the gauge-theory purity estimate. Its consumer is proved in the paper. No formal verification is reported.

## Answer to the owner

The open target is the same broad physical problem: a mass gap for four-dimensional Yang–Mills. It is a finer reduction than the general gap question because it isolates one explicit finite-physical-size, neutral-sector purity inequality, required only at infinitely many cutoffs, as a sufficient input to the lane's fixed-observable gap consumer. Published and preprint work already contains close finite-size and conditional reductions. In particular, the one-cube continuum criterion is a close precedent in the form of its question, but it asks for a different and more uniform boundary-response estimate at every cutoff. I found no evidence that its hypothesis is equivalent to, implied by, or implies `(PUR-io)`. So the target is neither simply “the same problem everyone works on” nor a wholly uncharted reduction: it is a more specific sufficient condition inside that common mass-gap problem, with established neighboring reduction patterns and no identified prior result for this exact purity condition.
