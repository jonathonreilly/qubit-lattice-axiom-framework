## 7. Dense subsets of pseudorandom sets

**Citation verified.** Omer Reingold, Luca Trevisan, Madhur Tulsiani and Salil Vadhan, “Dense Subsets of Pseudorandom Sets,” *Proceedings of the 49th Annual IEEE Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science* (FOCS 2008), pp. 76–85, DOI 10.1109/FOCS.2008.38. Citation is correct. I read the full 10-page FOCS extended abstract, including §§1–4 and its references. The authors point to a full ECCC version; the FOCS text is the cited item. Author-hosted copy: `https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/pseudodense-focs.pdf`.

**Answer to §4.4 question.** The paper gives a dense model theorem for its specified class: all distributions that are `δ`-dense in the uniform law on a finite set. That class is convex: mixtures preserve the pointwise density bound. The proof uses minimax/LP duality in Claim 2.3 to turn “each model has some distinguisher” into one convex combination of distinguishers that separates every model (pp. 4–5); Theorem 2.2 then uses a threshold of that mixture and approximates it by a low-complexity threshold or product (pp. 5–6). This answers the method question: **the paper’s general separation step needs convexity of the model class used in the minimax argument**. It gives no theorem for an arbitrary nonconvex candidate class. It also does not prove that *every* dense-model theorem needs convexity: a particular nonconvex class could have extra structure or a separate approximation theorem. So Lemma NC (gap-purity-strategy-mine-fable.md §4.3) closes the direct convex-mixture/minimax route for a class of clustering laws; it does not, by itself, rule out every possible theorem for that class.

**Purity relevance: NO.** The paper transfers pseudorandomness and density into indistinguishability from a dense model. It supplies no estimate on Wilson transfer eigenvalues, thermal purity, or a fixed-physical-size SU(2) law. Its finite-domain convexity argument has no gauge-group or dimension input. The same statement and proof apply to compact U(1), so it is not selective for the crux card.

## 8. Interlacing Families II

**Citation verified.** Adam W. Marcus, Daniel A. Spielman and Nikhil Srivastava, “Interlacing Families II: Mixed Characteristic Polynomials and the Kadison–Singer Problem,” *Annals of Mathematics* 182 (2015), 327–350; arXiv:1306.3969. Citation is correct. I read all 24 pages, including the proofs and references. Source: `https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/PAPERS/Marcus_Spielman_Srivastava_IFII.pdf`.

**Answer to §4.4 question.** Yes, with a useful qualification. The paper defines the mixed characteristic polynomial for positive semidefinite matrices `A_i` of any rank by
`μ[A_1,…,A_m](x) = (∏_i(1−∂_{z_i})) det(xI+Σ_i z_i A_i)|_{z=0}`.
Theorem 4.1 (pp. 335–336) identifies it with the expected characteristic polynomial of a sum of independent random rank-one PSD matrices with covariances `A_i`. Corollary 4.4 (p. 337) proves real-rootedness. Theorem 5.1 (pp. 337–344) bounds its largest root by `(1+√ε)^2` when `Σ_i A_i=I` and `Tr(A_i)≤ε` for every `i`. The `A_i` in this formula and bound can be high rank; rank one is the random summand in the expectation representation, not a rank restriction on `A_i`. A fixed positive sandwich can be absorbed into each summand and its covariance, but it does not by itself give `Σ_i A_i=I` or the trace budget. The theorem is an upper bound on the largest root; it is not a generic bound for arbitrary `e^{-V/2}(·)e^{-V/2}` without those hypotheses.

**Purity relevance: UNLIKELY.** Interlacing turns an averaged polynomial into a favorable deterministic choice and controls a top eigenvalue. PUR-io is a lower bound on a ratio of neutral-sector trace powers. The paper supplies no such trace-ratio estimate, Wilson measure, or uniform carry across cutoffs. The abstract polynomial theorem can also hold for compact U(1) matrices under the same PSD and trace hypotheses; it has no group or dimension test and so is not selective.

## 9. A new approach to strong convergence

**Citation verified and updated.** Chi-Fang Chen, Jorge Garza-Vargas, Joel A. Tropp and Ramon van Handel, “A New Approach to Strong Convergence,” arXiv:2405.16026 (first posted 2024; v3, 11 February 2025). It is now published in *Annals of Mathematics* 203 (2026), no. 2, pp. 1–48. The title, authors and arXiv number in §4.4 are right; the journal citation is now available. I read all 37 pages of arXiv v3, including Appendix A and references. Sources: `https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16026`; published copy: `https://tropp.caltech.edu/papers/CGV%2B26-New-Approach.pdf`.

**Answer to §4.4 question.** The method needs a rational parameterization with degree control. For each polynomial test function `h` of degree at most `q`, it requires the expected normalized trace to have form `Φ_h(1/N)=f_h(1/N)/g_q(1/N)`, with polynomial numerator and denominator, degree bounds proportional to `q`, and denominator bounded away from zero near `1/N=0` (§2.2, eq. (2.3), Lemmas 5.1 and 6.3). It also needs controlled low-order asymptotics, starting with `ν₀(h)+ν₁(h)/N` (eq. (2.4)). Markov inequalities on the rational numerator and denominator give a nonasymptotic polynomial-test bound; Chebyshev/Fourier estimates extend it to smooth tests, and moment growth then bounds the support of the correction distribution (§2.2; Theorems 6.1 and 7.1).

A fixed-lattice SU(2) character expansion in `c_j(β)` does not visibly have this structure. There are infinitely many spin labels `j`; the finite set of lattice plaquettes does not truncate the character sum. A partition function or trace ratio is therefore an infinite character series or a ratio of such series, not the finite-degree rational function of `1/N` used here. The paper proves no degree-controlled rationality or low-order correction-support bound for that expansion. This is a mismatch identified from the displayed hypotheses and the Wilson character expansion, not a theorem that no other reparameterization could work.

**Purity relevance: NO.** The paper proves strong spectral-norm convergence for random permutation models as matrix size tends to infinity; it does not control neutral-sector transfer trace powers along the Wilson asymptotically-free trajectory. Its rationality input is proved from word counts in symmetric groups, which has no supplied Wilson analogue. Compact U(1) also has an infinite Fourier-charge expansion, and the method itself is not SU(2)- or four-dimensional-specific. It gives no selective test for the crux card.

## 10. Sphere packing and quantum gravity

**Citation verified.** Thomas Hartman, Dalimil Mazáč and Leonardo Rastelli, “Sphere Packing and Quantum Gravity,” *Journal of High Energy Physics* 12 (2019), article 048; arXiv:1905.01319 (v2, 17 September 2019). The citation in §4.4 is correct. I read the full 72-page arXiv version, including appendices and references. Sources: `https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.01319`; published record: `https://doi.org/10.1007/JHEP12(2019)048`.

**Answer to §4.4 question.** The linear-functional method in this paper does not yield a lower bound on the spectral gap. Its sign test gives an **upper** bound on the first non-vacuum primary dimension. In the modular bootstrap, eqs. (2.12)–(2.13), §2.3, a functional nonnegative on all states above a proposed threshold and positive on the vacuum rules out a gap above that threshold. The exact extremal functionals have zero vacuum action at the special central charges; away from them, a positive vacuum action again yields an upper-gap bound (e.g. §7, eqs. (7.1)–(7.4)). The large-(c) improvement in §7.3 is also an upper bound.

The sphere-packing side is a linear-programming **upper bound on packing density**, not a lower bound on a CFT gap. Its Cohn–Elkies conditions, §3, eqs. (3.10)–(3.11), use a positive Fourier transform and a nonpositive tail, with the value at zero controlling the diagonal/self term in the density estimate. The paper's map identifies this with the modular bootstrap problem for a (U(1)^c) chiral algebra: vacuum/identity data enter the crossing equation. It does not give an upper bound on an arbitrary density of states after removing the crossed-channel vacuum contribution. In the 1D crossing formulation, the explicit sign criterion in §5.4, eq. (5.37), compares the two channel-vacuum actions and imposes signs on the non-vacuum spectrum. Thus the crossed-channel vacuum contribution is part of the certificate, not dispensable data. This answers “ever?” for the techniques and claims in this paper; it does not assert that every conceivable functional method in another setup needs a protected vacuum.

**Purity relevance: NO.** The estimates concern modular-invariant CFT spectra and Euclidean sphere packing, not neutral-sector Wilson transfer trace powers or a cutoff-uniform SU(2) law. The paper's (U(1)^c) denotes a 2D chiral algebra, not the weak four-dimensional compact gauge theory in the crux card. The linear-programming setup also applies without an SU(2)-selective ingredient. The methods do not supply PUR-io or its required compact-U(1) discriminator.

## 11. Göpfert–Mack: 3D compact U(1) confinement

**Citation verified.** Markus Göpfert and Gerhard Mack, “Proof of Confinement of Static Quarks in 3-Dimensional U(1) Lattice Gauge Theory for All Values of the Coupling Constant,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 82, no. 4 (1981), 545–606, DOI 10.1007/BF01961240; preprint DESY 81/036 (July 1981). The strategy citation has the year wrong; the DESY record gives 1981 for the journal volume, and the pages match. I read the complete 51-page DESY preprint, including Appendices A–E. Source: `https://bib-pubdb1.desy.de/record/397063/files/8108189.pdf` and the journal DOI above.

**Answer to §4.4 question.** The explicitly abelian step is the exact transformation from compact (U(1)) link variables with Villain action to an integer-valued spin field, followed by writing its jumps as integer-multiplicity chains. The authors state the duality and Wilson-loop identity in §1, eqs. (1.3)–(1.6); Appendix A derives it with boundary conditions. In §5, eqs. (5.2)–(5.7), a spin configuration is equivalently a chain (T=2\pi\beta^{-1/2}\sum_b N(b)b) with prescribed coboundary, and the partition function becomes a positive sum of weights (e^{-\|T\|^2/2}). The crucial “defect” variable is therefore (N(b)\in\mathbb Z), or the corresponding chain/domain wall; its exact conservation/coboundary constraint and positive quadratic weight come from the abelian dual representation. The proof then uses a low-temperature expansion of connected domain walls (§5, eqs. (5.8)–(5.12)), and later block/cluster expansions with unit blocks on the Debye scale (§§6–8; in particular §8, eqs. (8.3)–(8.6)). The screening length is (m_D^{-1}), introduced in §1, eq. (1.8), and the small-activity estimates are built into the effective action and defect expansion, not a generic theorem that any gauge group has many defects in a screening volume.

There is no SU(2) analogue established here. A large-clock-(s) plaquette could be proposed as a defect observable, but this paper supplies none of the needed SU(2) facts: an exact integer-chain representation with a boundary identity, nonnegative weights, a defect activity with a uniform small bound, or the same block-scale counting. Those properties would have to be proved for the proposed variable. The paper is 3D and proves a Wilson-loop area law, not the four-dimensional PUR-io trace-ratio bound.

**Purity relevance: NO.** The U(1) duality supplies a concrete variable and counting architecture, but both the exact defect representation and its screening estimates are tied to the abelian model and 3D setting. In compact U(1), this method works *because* the integer Fourier/dual variables exist; that makes it a useful U(1) counter-test, not an SU(2)-selective route. It does not imply weak 4D U(1) has a gap or help establish the required SU(2) purity lower bound.

## 12. Proof of the fundamental gap conjecture

**Citation verified.** Ben Andrews and Julie Clutterbuck, “Proof of the Fundamental Gap Conjecture,” *Journal of the American Mathematical Society* 24, no. 3 (2011), 899–916, DOI 10.1090/S0894-0347-2011-00699-1. Citation is correct. I read all 18 pages, including the proof and references. Source: `https://www.ams.org/journals/jams/2011-24-03/S0894-0347-2011-00699-1/`.

**Answer to §4.4 question.** The two-point maximum principle is independent of the dimension (n) in the displayed gap estimate, but it is **not** independent of the diameter. For a convex domain Ω of diameter (D), Dirichlet conditions, and convex potential (V), Corollary 1.4 (p. 4) gives \(\lambda_1-\lambda_0\ge 3\pi^2/D^2\). More generally Theorem 1.3 (p. 4) compares the gap to the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator on ([-D/2,D/2]) with a chosen modulus of convexity; Theorem 1.5 gives the matching two-point log-concavity estimate. The maximum-principle proof uses a two-point function on Ω×Ω and its full (2n\times2n) Hessian (§§2–4; see the second-derivative test around eqs. (15)–(16), pp. 12–13). Thus it handles every finite (n) without an (n)-dependent loss, but its comparison scale is geometric and proportional to (D^{-2}). It cannot give a diameter-free lower bound as (D\to\infty).

For a sequence with more modes on a *fixed* bounded continuum domain, the theorem's lower bound does not deteriorate merely because the discretization has more coordinates, provided the same continuum hypotheses and diameter remain valid. But that is not an automatic theorem for the growing Wilson configuration spaces or neutral transfer operators. Neither a fixed common convex domain nor the required convex potential and two-point diffusion equation has been derived for PUR-io. So the argument does not by itself settle the infinite-mode limit in the crux card.

**Purity relevance: NO.** This is a geometric Schrödinger gap theorem; it does not control the neutral Wilson transfer spectrum, powers, or trace ratio. Its lower bound is useful only with a domain diameter and convexity structure that the target has not been shown to possess. The same theorem applies to a compact-U(1) model whenever those analytic hypotheses hold; it has no group-specific term that could separate SU(2) from U(1).
