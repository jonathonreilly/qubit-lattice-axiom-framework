## Bandeira–Singer–Spielman (full read)

**Citation verified/corrected.** Afonso S. Bandeira, Amit Singer and Daniel A. Spielman, “A Cheeger Inequality for the Graph Connection Laplacian,” *SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications* 34(4) (2013), 1611–1630, DOI 10.1137/120875338. The first author is Afonso (not A. S. alone); the row's title and journal/year/pages are correct. I read all 20 PDF pages, including the appendix and references, in the SIAM published text.

**Question and result. MAY APPLY (as a conditional finite-graph conversion only).** Theorem 2.6 (paper pp. 1619–20) states for a weighted graph with edge transports in O(d), normalized graph Laplacian gap λ₂(L₀), and minimum O(d)-synchronization frustration ν_G:
`(1/d) Σ_{i=1}^d λ_i(L₁) ≤ ν_G ≤ (1026 d³/λ₂(L₀)) Σ_{i=1}^d λ_i(L₁)`.
The lower bound is the Rayleigh relaxation; the upper bound rounds the first d eigenvectors by the polar factor, with a penalty for vertices whose local matrix is near singular. The hypotheses are a finite undirected weighted graph, orthogonal edge data and a usable scalar graph spectral gap. It does not prove that the connection eigenvalues are positive, random-Wilson estimates, a trace bound, or control after removing a scalar contact line. It treats O(d) group potentials, not an arbitrary prescribed tensor subgroup or a centered two-time observable. Thus it could translate an already proved low-eigenvalue bound into a frustration statement for a finite adjoint graph, but it does not supply the missing target estimate. For compact U(1), the adjoint transports are identity on every edge; constant vector sections give d zero modes, hence zero frustration and no positive selective bound. A tensor product of two adjoints also has an invariant scalar section under diagonal U(1), so the same obstruction remains.

## Lüscher (2010), Wilson flow (full read)

**Citation verified.** Martin Lüscher, “Properties and uses of the Wilson flow in lattice QCD,” *JHEP* 2010(08), article 071 (published 13 Aug. 2010), DOI 10.1007/JHEP08(2010)071, arXiv:1006.4518. I read all 21 pages of arXiv v3 and checked the journal metadata. The requested citation is correct.

**Question and result. UNLIKELY for the target estimate.** At finite lattice volume, the Wilson flow is an ODE on a finite product of compact Lie groups; the paper states existence, uniqueness, smoothness at positive/negative time and monotone decrease of the Wilson action (p. 2, eq. (1.4), citing Arnold). At leading order, the continuum flow is the heat kernel convolution (pp. 4–5, eqs. (2.7)–(2.13)); its smoothing radius is `√(8t)` in four dimensions. This is the configuration-by-configuration portion. The claimed/observed continuum smoothing and small-plaquette rarity are not proved configuration-wise: §3 relies on perturbation theory and SU(3) simulations; §4.2–4.3 gives empirical probabilities and a numerical suggestion (pp. 12–14, Figs. 3–4), and §4.4 says equality of topological moments remains to be established (p. 16). The change of variables through flow time is an invertible finite-lattice diffeomorphism, with transformed action/Jacobian (p. 14, eqs. (4.1)–(4.3)). None represents a centered curvature observable in row 1683's joint line lists or bounds its two-time insertion in the neutral trace. For compact U(1), the same finite-lattice flow and leading heat smoothing hold; in the continuum it damps short wavelengths while leaving the long-wavelength Maxwell photon massless. The flow is therefore not a selective mechanism. The separate local reading named as `F1` in the row-1683 citation trail is earlier lane context, but the exact 2010 paper was not fully read there; the earlier `gap-lit-transport.md` reads a different 2009 trivializing-map paper.

## Kenyon (2011), vector-bundle Laplacian (full read)

**Citation verified.** Richard Kenyon, “Spanning forests and the vector bundle Laplacian,” *The Annals of Probability* 39(5) (2011), 1983–2017, DOI 10.1214/10-AOP596, arXiv:1001.4028v3. The citation in row 1683 is correct. I read the complete 36-page author preprint reprint (including references); its reprint page numbers run 1–36.

**Question and result. NO for a rank-nine positive-trace estimate.** Theorem 1 (pp. 7–9) is Forman's line-bundle matrix-tree/CRSF formula, with cycle factor `2 − w − w⁻¹`; unitary line connections yield determinantal edge measures (Theorem 2, pp. 10–11). Theorem 9 (p. 17) extends the CRSF determinant formula to a **rank-two** `SL₂(C)` bundle via a Q-determinant, cycle factor `2 − tr(w)`, and a noncommutative Cauchy–Binet theorem (Theorem 10, pp. 17–18). These are not an ordinary positive rank-nine trace formula. The paper explicitly lists “any combinatorial meaning” for higher-rank vector-bundle Laplacians as an open question (p. 35, item 2). Thus it does not establish the desired positive expansion at coincident paths; its relevant premise is only available for line bundles and a special self-dual rank-two Q-determinant. For compact U(1) in the adjoint representation, every edge transport is identity, so the connection Laplacian is the direct sum of scalar graph Laplacians (pp. 5–6, §3.2); on a connected graph each has a constant zero mode. Removing a scalar contact direction does not create the missing nonabelian frustration. Row 1683 records that its paper-title search found no prior lane hit for Kenyon; no earlier full read of this paper is on file.

## Fröhlich–Spencer (1982), Abelian gauge theory and masslessness

**Citation verified.** Jürg Fröhlich and Thomas Spencer, “Massless phases and symmetry restoration in abelian gauge theories and spin systems,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 83(3) (1982), 411–454, DOI [10.1007/BF01213610](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01213610). The full 1981 IHES/P/81/40 author preprint is a typeset 85-page text of the article; I inspected that text. The 1982 journal metadata matches. The lane already has a full reading in `library/prior/gap-lit-u1.md`, which is the source of the scope summary below.

**Question and result. MAY APPLY as a rigorous Abelian control, not as the requested relative-trace estimate.** Section 2.11, eqs. (2.89)–(2.93), pp. 44–48, identifies the gauge-invariant plaquette field strength as the photon observable. Section 2.12, eqs. (2.94)–(2.107), pp. 47–52, proves bounds by massless Gaussian covariances for large β; the Fourier-space field-strength covariance is discontinuous at zero momentum (2.91), so its position-space correlation is not summable (pp. 45–46). Earlier, (2.88), p. 43, gives a perimeter lower bound for a Wilson loop at large β. The detailed proof is for the four-dimensional Villain measure. The authors say the result extends to Wilson cosine by a direct argument, with details omitted (pp. 44 and 83).

This is a precise gauge-invariant signal of the massless photon. It does not bound a product of two open Abelian transports or the row's joint-list law. For compact U(1), the adjoint link representation is exactly trivial while the photon remains massless; hence this paper is a direct counterexample to treating trivial adjoint transport as evidence of a mass gap. It supports the *need* for a selective SU(2)-versus-U(1) test, but does not prove the test's SU(2) side.

## Gu–Eisenstat (1996), strong rank-revealing QR

**Citation verified.** Ming Gu and Stanley C. Eisenstat, “Efficient algorithms for computing a strong rank-revealing QR factorization,” *SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing* 17(4) (1996), 848–869, DOI [10.1137/0917055](https://doi.org/10.1137/0917055). The complete Yale technical-report predecessor, TR-967 (1994), was read (24 manuscript pages). I could not obtain the journal PDF, so theorem locators below refer to the Yale report's numbering, not a checked journal pagination.

**Question and result. UNLIKELY for the target's independence requirement.** Theorem 2, report §3, pp. 7–8, shows that if a QR partition satisfies ρ(R,k)≤f, then the selected block singular values obey (3.1)–(3.2), with factor `sqrt(1+f^2 k(n-k))`. Algorithm 5 in §4 (pp. 9–10) constructs such a factorization by data-dependent column swaps; the report's Theorem 4 and §6 concern interchange counts and numerical performance. Applying the method to a transpose can select rows and control a deterministic rank loss in the matrix of observations. It provides no probability bound, no independence statement for unselected random blocks, and no rule that makes a data-dependent selection independent of those blocks. For compact U(1), the factorization is group-blind: it cannot distinguish identical adjoint data from SU(2), and selection based on the observed random matrix can create dependence. Thus it might be a numerical conditioning tool after an independent probabilistic theorem, but it does not supply that theorem.

## Lüscher (1977), positive transfer matrix

**Citation verified.** Martin Lüscher, “Construction of a selfadjoint, strictly positive transfer matrix for Euclidean lattice gauge theories,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 54(3) (1977), 283–292, DOI [10.1007/BF01614090](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01614090). I read the full DESY 76/54 preprint text through the DESY-hosted scan's complete text extraction; the scan is the preprint version, and the journal metadata agrees.

**Question and result. MAY APPLY as transfer-matrix structure only.** Proposition 1, manuscript pp. 9–11, proves the constructed transfer matrix self-adjoint, bounded and strictly positive. Its proof reduces positivity to nonnegative character coefficients of the one-link kernel, eqs. (17), (22)–(23), then uses that SU(N) irreducibles occur in products of fundamental and antifundamental representations. Proposition 2, pp. 11–13, reconstructs Euclidean expectations as operator matrix elements; in (26)–(27), the infinite-time limit inserts the projection onto the lowest-transfer-energy eigenspace (the vacuum if nondegenerate). This gives the physical-slice framework in which one can place an insertion. It does not identify the row's displaced two-color path operator, cancel a contact term, or bound a vacuum-subtracted two-time insertion.

The proof is written for SU(N), with fermions included in the construction. Its character-kernel positivity argument has an Abelian counterpart for compact U(1), but strict positivity only legitimizes the transfer Hamiltonian; it does not create adjoint selectivity. In U(1), the adjoint is neutral, so the operator-level formalism alone cannot distinguish the massless photon.
