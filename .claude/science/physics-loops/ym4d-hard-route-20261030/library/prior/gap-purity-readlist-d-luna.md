## 1. Kopper–Magnen–Rivasseau: Gross–Neveu mass generation

**CITED.** C. Kopper, J. Magnen, and V. Rivasseau, “Mass generation in the large N Gross-Neveu model,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 169(1) (1995), 121–180, DOI [10.1007/BF02101599](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02101599). The DOI and pages match the journal record. I read the 1993 CERN preprint, which states the same title, authors, setup, estimates, and theorem. Its printed page numbers run 2–75. The author and publisher metadata are at [the publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02101599); the full author PDF is [here](https://cds.cern.ch/record/257886/files/P00020270.pdf).

**CITED — result.** The paper studies the two-dimensional Euclidean Gross–Neveu fermion model with a UV cutoff set to one. The main theorem states exponential decay of the infinite-volume fermion two-point function: (208)–(209), pp. 72–73, with decay rate `m' = m(1 + o(N^{-1/10}))`. The abstract and introduction also state existence of at least two pure phases, obtained by boundary conditions (pp. 1–3). The authors say the model is massive for fixed coupling λ of order one. They give the weak-λ mass scale as approximately `e^{-π/λ}` and say their cluster expansion needs `N >> m^{-1}` (p. 2). Thus small λ forces N very large.

**CITED — order-one estimate.** There is no single convexity or positivity estimate for an auxiliary-field effective potential that alone proves the crossing. The auxiliary scalar σ and the large-N effective-potential picture motivate the proof (p. 4, equations (3)–(4), and Fig. 1); `rg` search of the full PDF found no convexity claim. The proved bridge is a chain of block estimates. Proposition 2, (155), pp. 47–48, gives small-field factors including `exp(N^{-1/10}|S|)` and large-field suppression `exp(-b|Γ|N^{1/5})`. Proposition 3, (185), p. 58, is the volume-uniform polymer summability criterion. Its proof ends in (207), p. 71, with a sum bounded by `1/2` for sufficiently large N; each small-field block contributes `m^{-3/2}N^{-1/10}`, while large-field terms carry `exp(-b|T|N^{1/5}/2)`. The threshold depends on m. This is a cluster-expansion estimate after the saddle-centred σ-field representation, not a finite-N positivity shortcut.

The paper has no hierarchical lattice and uses no abelian duality. It uses a vector large-N expansion, an auxiliary bosonic field and a cluster/Mayer expansion. The `1/N` corrections control fluctuations around the nonzero σ phases. They are essential. This is a fixed-cutoff, two-dimensional fermion model. It does not give a uniform fixed-N SU(2) gauge estimate.

**ARGUED — fit. UNLIKELY.** It proves that an asymptotically free model can acquire a nonperturbative mass after choosing a nonzero σ phase. But the mechanism needs N sufficiently large relative to the generated mass scale. The target is fixed SU(2), with no demonstrated positive local σ measure or large parameter. The result is a model analogy, not a transfer lemma for (PUR-io).

**ARGUED — 4D compact U(1).** This fermion theorem has no direct compact-U(1) consequence. Weak four-dimensional compact U(1) is in a massless Coulomb regime. The GN mass mechanism cannot be read as a photon mass proof; U(1) has no non-Abelian antiscreening step.

## 2. Gawędzki–Kupiainen: hierarchical O(N) sigma model

**CITED, citation confirmed.** K. Gawędzki and A. Kupiainen, “Continuum limit of the hierarchical O(N) nonlinear σ-model,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 106 (1986), 533–550. The accessible author copy has the title page and journal pagination. Its abstract states a rigorous 2D continuum limit for a hierarchical kinetic term and establishes asymptotic freedom and a weak-coupling Wilson flow. (Some later bibliographies misprint the first page as 523.) [Author full-text copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38329931_Continuum_limit_of_the_hierarchical_rm_ON_nonlinear_sigma-model).

**CITED — invariant family and crossing.** The main theorem, printed p. 536, chooses the bare coupling as a function of scale and the renormalized parameter, then shows the rescaled characteristic function converges locally uniformly to an entire O(N)-invariant function with a stability bound. The iteration uses radial single-site potentials. Near their minimum, the effective potential is decomposed into a radial shell sum plus a controlled remainder; see (44), p. 540, and its iterated form (48), pp. 540–541. The key crossover control is the recursion estimate in §4: the higher coefficients stabilize rapidly, while the running inverse temperature satisfies `β_a=β(a)+O(1)` uniformly over scales (pp. 544–545, especially (77)). The third-order term `b3` is needed for boundedness; asymptotic freedom enters through `b2<0`. The same stability and small-field form are propagated by the one-site hierarchical recursion. The constants are computable and independent of RG scale, but can depend on N. The proof is for fixed N and does not require N to be large.

The O(2) comparison is explicit on printed p. 545. The hierarchical model is asymptotically free also for N=2, because it has no independent wave-function renormalization. The full O(2) model has an extra logarithmic term in its beta coefficient from that renormalization; the paper says asymptotic freedom there occurs only in the nonabelian case. This means the hierarchy does not retain the full model's KT mechanism. The article constructs a continuum limit and RG trajectory; it does not state a separate spectral mass-gap or massless-phase theorem for hierarchical O(2). No large-N-only inequality or abelian duality is used; the decisive simplification is the exact hierarchy.

**UNLIKELY for the target.** The result shows one-site hierarchical RG can track a radial interaction over many scales. It does not bound the nonhierarchical SU(2) neutral transfer form or its scale-dependent coupling. The O(2) discussion warns against carrying the hierarchical trajectory over to a full compact abelian model.

**4D compact U(1).** No direct estimate. The massless weak-coupling phase cited above is not the hierarchical sigma-model flow.

## 4. AKLT finite-size methods: Lemm–Sandvik–Wang; Pomata–Wei

**CITED, citations confirmed.** M. Lemm, A. W. Sandvik, and L. Wang, “Existence of a Spectral Gap in the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki Model on the Hexagonal Lattice,” *Physical Review Letters* 124 (2020), 177204, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.177204](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.177204), [arXiv:1910.11810](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11810). N. Pomata and T.-C. Wei, “Demonstrating the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki Spectral Gap on 2D Degree-3 Lattices,” *Physical Review Letters* 124 (2020), 177203, DOI [10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.177203](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.177203), [arXiv:1911.01410](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01410).

Lemm–Sandvik–Wang, main text Theorem 1 and finite-size criterion (1)–(7), with supplement proof (S1) onward, builds a periodic-lattice gap bound from a **36-site cut-out with open boundary conditions**. The edge terms at the patch boundary are weighted by a parameter `a`; the test is a threshold on the gap of that weighted open-patch Hamiltonian. The infinite-volume bound is then `Δ > 0.006`. The algebraic criterion uses frustration freeness and an operator inequality equivalent to `H² ≥ γH` on the orthogonal complement of the common ground space, but also requires the specific finite-patch gap estimate and weighted boundary construction. The latter is verified in the paper by DMRG, not by an exact proof. With the owner's computation rule here, an unchecked numerical DMRG value is not a proof step. This does not make the authors' stated publication theorem vanish; it means this source does not supply an accepted exact/Lean-checked input under this task's rule.

Pomata–Wei (1)–(9) first enlarges the local terms to parent projectors on regions. It proves a global gap from a local anticommutator bound `{E,F} ≥ −η(E+F)` for overlapping projectors, yielding a sum-of-projectors inequality and a nonzero gap when `η z < 1`; see Proposition 1. It then reduces the finite local eigenvalue problem and applies Lanczos on the honeycomb patch, and exact diagonalization for other degree-3 tilings. Thus it uses more than the generic common-null positivity statement: the region ground-space projectors and a quantitative overlap/anticommutator estimate are essential. Its final gap value also depends on numerical eigenvalue work. Neither paper establishes that a free-boundary or averaged-boundary patch is valid for the target's Fokker–Planck operator. The Lemm patch condition is specifically weighted open boundary; Pomata–Wei is a different parent-projector criterion.

**NO for the target.** These are frustration-free quantum spin Hamiltonians with finite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces and explicit ground-space projectors. They do not use large N, hierarchical structure, or abelian duality. The target is a neutral-sector gauge transfer operator. No map preserving the required boundary form, local null spaces, and exact patch gap is supplied. 4D compact U(1) remains massless at weak coupling; these AKLT gap results do not imply otherwise.

## 5. Fannes–Nachtergaele–Werner: finitely correlated states

**CITED, citation confirmed.** M. Fannes, B. Nachtergaele, and R. F. Werner, “Finitely correlated states on quantum spin chains,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 144 (1992), 443–490, DOI [10.1007/BF02099178](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02099178). [Author-uploaded full text](https://www.academia.edu/21897636/Finitely_correlated_states_on_quantum_spin_chains).

The article's transfer map `E` acts on a finite-dimensional auxiliary C*-algebra. Proposition 3.1, pp. 456–457, characterizes ergodicity by a one-dimensional fixed eigenspace at eigenvalue 1 in the minimal representation; when 1 is the only peripheral eigenvalue, the correlations cluster exponentially. A sufficient finite-dimensional test is strict positivity of all component maps `E_k`, which gives trivial peripheral spectrum (p. 481). But the spectral-gap conclusion is a separate Hamiltonian theorem: Theorem 6.4, pp. 478–479, assumes a finitely correlated pure ground state and the intersection property, plus a finite-volume gap; it controls overlapping local ground-space projections. It is not merely “primitivity implies a gap.”

**NO for the target.** The actual Wilson transfer operator's neutral-sector state space is not a finite-dimensional auxiliary algebra. A matrix primitivity test or a finite matrix eigenvalue computation therefore has no evident domain. One could seek an infinite-dimensional positive-operator analogue, but the paper gives no checkable sufficient condition for that Doob transform. There is no large N, hierarchy, or Abelian duality here. FCS spin-chain gap/clustering results do not alter the massless weak-coupling 4D compact-U(1) Coulomb phase.

**Repo prior.** `gap-purity-mp-strategy-fable.md` §5.4 is the only exact first-author/title hit in the local gap-purity search. The rest of the lane cites general transfer-map and AKLT results, but I found no earlier read of this paper. This pass read the full public text's abstract, Sections 2–7, Propositions 3.1 and 6.4, and the classical-process discussion; the publisher metadata verifies the citation.

## 6. Dell'Antonio–Zwanziger and Zwanziger: Gribov-region estimates

Zwanziger, “Vanishing of zero-momentum lattice gluon propagator and color confinement,” *Nuclear Physics B* 364 (1991), 127–161, DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(91)90581-H](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(91)90581-H).

Dell'Antonio–Zwanziger prove, in the L2 topology on continuum gauge connections and gauge transformations, that each orbit is closed and its connection norm attains an absolute minimum in the Gribov region. This is a gauge-fixing/orbit-geometry theorem, not a positive lower bound for a physical transfer-operator quadratic form. Zwanziger's lattice paper defines minimal Landau and Coulomb gauges through minima of the lattice action. Its bound on the color magnetization in the Gribov region implies that the zero-momentum **gauge-fixed gluon** propagator vanishes in infinite volume; see eqs. (4.9)–(4.10) and the opening of §5, pp. 142–143. The conclusion concerns gauge-dependent colored correlations. The paper itself proposes a confinement mechanism for gauge-invariant singlets; it does not prove their positive mass gap. It gives no neutral-sector kinetic-form inequality of the requested type at every β. There is no hierarchical structure, useful abelian-duality step, or large-N parameter. For U(1) the nonabelian Gribov-region color-spin estimate is absent, but that absence does not yield an SU(2) gauge-invariant inequality.

**NO for the target.** The requested quadratic-form implication is not in either result. The 4D compact-U(1) weak-coupling Coulomb phase is massless, consistently with the failure of any such nonabelian gauge-fixed inference to imply a physical photon mass.

## 7. Fröhlich–Morchio–Strocchi and Kennedy–King: Higgs models

**CITED, citations checked and corrected status.** J. Fröhlich, G. Morchio, and F. Strocchi, “Higgs phenomenon without symmetry breaking order parameter,” *Nuclear Physics B* 190(3) (1981), 553–582, DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(81)90448-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90448-X). T. Kennedy and C. King, “Spontaneous symmetry breakdown in the abelian Higgs model,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 104(2) (1986), 327–347, DOI [10.1007/BF01211599](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01211599). The second paper's page range in the reading list is correct.

FMS construct gauge-invariant composite observables and reformulate Higgs perturbation theory without a nonzero gauge-dependent scalar expectation. The paper's abstract and §§2–4 discuss gauge-invariant fields, particle masses, and defect configurations; it is not a theorem about an SU(2) theory with adjoint matter having a massless phase. Kennedy–King introduce a gauge-invariant order parameter for the lattice **abelian Higgs** model and prove spontaneous breaking of its global gauge symmetry in a suitable parameter range in d≥3. The 1985 companion PRL (55, 776–778) states the distinction explicitly: the order parameter is nonzero in a Higgs region and zero in a QED region. Neither paper proves the claimed SU(2)-factor massless phase. Kennedy–King use neither large N nor hierarchy nor Abelian duality as a bridge to nonabelian SU(2); the gauge group studied is itself abelian.

**UNLIKELY for the target.** FMS is a gauge-invariant interpretation of the Higgs phenomenon, not the desired gauge-only gap result. Kennedy–King distinguish phases in a scalar-coupled abelian model. For 4D compact U(1) without that Higgs matter, the weak-coupling Coulomb phase is massless; the abelian Higgs order-parameter theorem does not replace it by a mass-gap result.

## 8. Falco; Dimock–Hurd: KT RG

**CITED, citations confirmed.** P. Falco, “Kosterlitz-Thouless transition line for the two dimensional Coulomb gas,” *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 312 (2012), 559–609, DOI [10.1007/s00220-012-1432-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-012-1432-4), [arXiv:1104.1974](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.1974). J. Dimock and T. R. Hurd, “Sine-Gordon revisited,” *Annales Henri Poincaré* 1(3) (2000), 499–541, DOI [10.1007/s000230050005](https://doi.org/10.1007/s000230050005), [arXiv:math-ph/9907017](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/9907017). (One author bibliography gives 491 as the first page for Dimock–Hurd; the journal record and article pagination give 499.)

Falco's Theorem 2.1 proves existence of the pressure on a small piece of the KT transition curve `β=Σ(z)` near `(z,β)=(0,8π)`. The proof follows the marginal RG flow along the separatrix, Theorem 3.6 and estimates in §§4–7. This is not a theorem for the plasma side and does not prove a correlation length or mass with `exp(c/√t)` scaling. Dimock–Hurd Theorem 1, pp. 502–503, and its precise restatement as Theorem 18, §4, prove infrared stability for sine-Gordon at β>8π and small fugacity: the perturbation activities contract as `δ^j |ζ|^{1−ε}`. For β<8π, their theorem controls the UV construction at fixed volume. Neither result follows the relevant flow on the plasma side all the way to strong coupling or establishes the proposed mass formula. These arguments use neither large N nor a hierarchical lattice, but the model is two-dimensional and uses its Coulomb/sine-Gordon representation. That is not a duality argument for the 4D SU(2) target.

**NO for the target.** The specific proposed KT mass is not proved by these two cited papers. Their rigorous outputs are pressure on the critical line and stability/construction in the two sine-Gordon domains. In 4D compact U(1), the weak-coupling Coulomb phase has massless photons; this 2D KT analysis does not change that fact.
