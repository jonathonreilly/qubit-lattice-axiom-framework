## Question and disposition

I found **no published criterion in the literature checked** with all of the requested features: (i) exponential correlation decay or a spectral gap; (ii) a hypothesis consisting of finitely many expectations in the infinite-volume state itself; (iii) proof from reflection positivity or DLR alone; and (iv) applicability to compact continuous single-site spaces such as 4D Wilson SU(2). In the closest candidates, the actual input is a supremum/uniform family of boundary responses, a finite-volume partition-function or disseminated-event quantity, a functional inequality, or a contour/cluster-expansion assumption. None turns a finite list of state moments or loop averages into the needed conclusion by RP or DLR alone.

This is a literature-search disposition, not a theorem that no such result can exist. It leaves the row-1501 conclusion intact and closes R8 on the literature searched: no criterion found to reopen it. The result is not an audit or a scientific status grant.

| **Dobrushin–Shlosman complete analyticity (CA).** The 1987 paper gives twelve equivalent CA conditions and constructive finite-size versions: for each condition, checking its corresponding bound only on volumes with diameter at most a constant determined by that condition’s parameters characterizes the CA class. One explicit condition, IIIc, bounds total-variation distance between marginals on every tested set `A` in every finite `V` when two exterior boundary conditions differ at one site, by `K exp(-gamma dist(t,A))`; the constants are uniform over `V,A,t` and the boundary pair. The authors state their setup is finite-range with a **finite spin set**. | Requires a family of boundary-conditioned finite-volume bounds (and the uniformity across all such tests); the finite-size reduction restricts volume size, not the quantifier over boundary conditions. CA implies uniqueness/mixing; this is not a criterion that starts from expectations in a selected infinite-volume state. | Not directly: finite spin is outside compact continuous SU(2) link variables. More fundamentally, the hypothesis is exactly the kind of worst-case boundary influence excluded by the question. The result can be adapted only with a separate continuous-space theorem and its influence assumptions, not by finite loop moments. |
| **Stroock–Zegarlinski continuous-spin LSI.** Theorem 1.6 of their 1992 paper gives a sufficient condition on a local specification for its Gibbs state to satisfy a logarithmic Sobolev inequality; Theorem 3.10 relates that condition to Dobrushin–Shlosman mixing. | The condition is a local-specification/functional-inequality control, not a finite expectation test. LSI yields strong dynamical control; the available abstract does not establish a finite-moment input or state-only implication. | The continuous-spin setting makes this closer in state space, but it does not apply from compactness and DLR alone. It needs stronger quantitative mixing/functional-inequality hypotheses. No SU(2) Wilson application from finite loop expectations is stated. |
| **Martinelli–Olivieri finite-size mixing conditions.** Part I reviews and compares finite-volume mixing conditions and their consequences for Gibbs measures and Glauber dynamics in arbitrarily large volumes; it explains that DS complete analyticity and its dynamical counterpart require mixing for regions of arbitrary shape, and develops a different strong-mixing-in-a-finite-cube route for the attractive case. | The criteria are finite-volume mixing estimates, not finitely many expectations of an infinite-volume state. The general/non-attractive treatment also uses a strong-mixing assumption on a finite cube. The cited implications operate in a one-phase regime and entail the corresponding uniqueness/mixing behavior; they do not dispense with the mixing input. | No direct application to continuous SU(2) gauge links from loop moments. Attractive-spin or cube-mixing hypotheses are additional conditions, not consequences of bare DLR/RP alone. |
| **Kotecký–Shlosman chessboard / Biskup–Kotecký forbidden-gap theorem.** Biskup and Kotecký’s Theorem 2.4 is the RP chessboard inequality: the probability of a finite set of reflected block events is bounded by a product of their fully disseminated torus-event probabilities to the inverse-volume powers. Theorem 2.5 assumes a small bad-block chessboard quantity `p_beta(B)` and incompatible good block events, then concludes that every block-shift-ergodic Gibbs state has small bad-block density and one good type has density near one. The paper notes its conclusion can relax compact single-spin and bounded-interaction assumptions to finite average energy. | `p_beta(B)` is defined by a limit of finite-torus disseminated event probabilities, not an expectation in a chosen infinite-volume law. The conclusion is stated for each ergodic Gibbs state and gives phase selection among good-block types; the theorem does not assume uniqueness, nor does it give generic exponential decay of correlations or a transfer spectral gap. | RP itself can apply to Wilson-type actions where the required reflections hold, but this theorem’s input remains a torus chessboard/large-deviation quantity and its output is phase-density control. It is not the proposed state-expectation criterion for SU(2) in 4D. |
| **Borgs–Imbrie polymer/contour methods.** Their 1989 unified phase-diagram construction applies to systems admitting a low-temperature polymer/cluster expansion, converting the system to a contour model with small activities; their applications concern low-temperature scalar-field models and periodic-volume partition-function asymptotics. | Requires a convergent contour/polymer representation and small-activity estimates. The input is structural expansion control, not a finite set of infinite-volume expectations. Phase selection is explicit; uniqueness is not a universal output across coexistence. | No known direct reduction of 4D Wilson SU(2) at the relevant coupling to the required contour expansion. Even if available, it would not meet the finite-state-expectation hypothesis. |
| **Fröhlich–Israel–Lieb–Simon / Fröhlich–Spencer.** FILS systematizes reflection positivity, infrared bounds, and chessboard estimates; Fröhlich–Spencer study massless phases and symmetry restoration in Abelian gauge theories and spin systems. These are method- and model-specific results, not a general finite-size theorem whose input is a finite list of expectations in a limiting state. | The relevant inputs are reflection-positive torus quantities or model-specific estimates, rather than finite state moments. These works do not state a criterion of the requested form; RP alone is not the claimed finite-moment criterion. | The cited Fröhlich–Spencer work is Abelian and concerns massless phases; it is not a gap criterion. Neither it nor the general RP method furnishes the stated criterion for compact continuous SU(2) Wilson theory in 4D at the target coupling. |
| **Kazakov–Zheng loop bootstrap (2024).** For finite `N`, their SU(2) study includes dimensions 2, 3, and 4 and combines the lattice Makeenko–Migdal loop equations with positivity constraints on bounded-length single-trace Wilson loops; it reports upper/lower free-energy-per-plaquette bounds and string-tension estimates. | This is indeed finite loop-average data in a bootstrap problem, but its output is bounds on selected loop/free-energy quantities. It does not prove exponential clustering or a spectral gap from a finite expectation window; the loop equations and positivity constraints are joint hypotheses of the bootstrap, not a theorem from DLR/RP alone. | Group and dimension match, but the requested conclusion does not. The finite-N paper provides no correlation-decay/spectral-gap criterion of the specified type. |
| **General measurable-space block-comparison theorem (Rebeschini–van Handel).** Theorem 2.4 bounds differences of local-function expectations from a block influence matrix, provided the update kernels/couplings exist and the matrix series contracts. | Requires explicit block kernels, pairwise discrepancy couplings, a perturbation vector, and a contracting influence matrix. It is not a finite-volume decay criterion based on a finite set of moments in one state; the repository’s 2026 scan already documents this distinction. | General measurable spaces can include compact link spaces, but application requires proving its full influence-matrix hypotheses for the actual specification. It does not apply to SU(2) from RP/DLR or loop expectations alone. |
