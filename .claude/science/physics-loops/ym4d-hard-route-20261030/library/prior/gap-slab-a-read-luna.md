# Literature

## 1. Commutator noise, compact groups and SU(2)

For a compact group `G`, let `A,B` be independent Haar elements and let `μ` be the law of `[A,B]=ABA^{-1}B^{-1}`. Frobenius's formula, extended to compact groups by Michael Magee and Doron Puder, says for every irreducible unitary representation `ρ` of dimension `d`,

`E ρ([A,B]) = d^(-2) I_d`.

Equivalently, for every irreducible character `χ`, `E χ([A,B])=1/d`; the commutator law is central, and its character expansion has coefficient `1/d` (with normalized Haar conventions). For `n` independent commutator steps the Fourier multiplier is `d^(-2n)`. Thus on SU(2), where `d=2j+1`, the largest nontrivial multiplier is `1/4` (the fundamental representation); this is an exact operator fact, not a model-dependent estimate. The law of a product of `g` independent commutators is the orientable genus-`g` surface-word measure; Magee–Puder also establish the corresponding expected-character identity. This describes random walks whose steps are commutators. It supplies smoothing/convergence of the group convolution, not a lower bound on a Wilson transfer kernel. Generic compact-group random-walk/upper-bound methods (including Diaconis–Shahshahani-style Fourier bounds) can convert representation coefficients to convergence estimates under their own summability/generation hypotheses; there is no additional commutator-specific Diaconis theorem needed for the `d^-2` multiplier. No lattice-volume or gauge-coupling range occurs in this group identity, and its derivation is analytic, not computer-assisted. [Magee–Puder, *Surface Words are Determined by Word Measures on Groups* (arXiv version; published 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04873).

There are two different positivity statements in the already indexed sources. In the Sussman–Alpoge outline, the one-link SU(2) Wilson convolution has Peter–Weyl eigenvalues `c_l=2e^{-beta} I_(2l+1)(beta)/beta > 0`, with factorial decay (outline lines 2195–2247); its block product is used in their transfer operator (lines 2313–2345). This proves positivity of that convolution's eigenvalues, not positivity of its inverse/deconvolution after applying `1-Delta`. Their stated reflection-positivity/plateau argument explicitly says no spectral gap above invariants has been proved (lines 3717–3720). In the lane's row 1132, the hand calculation for the normalized single temporal-link density `h_beta(g) ∝ exp(beta tr(g)/2)` gives `(1-Delta)h_beta >= 0` exactly for `0 <= beta <= 1/3`; beyond that range it is negative near `g=-I`. Twelve electric steps in the spatial-weight-free model have a strictly positive deconvolution for `beta <= 13/5`, yielding `p=1` there. These are model-specific positive-deconvolution calculations, already present in the lane; they do not include the interacting spatial Wilson weights, and neither yields the fixed-time uniform-in-volume bound in (A). No computer-assisted step was used in the present reading; the cited lane arguments are explicitly hand derivations and have not been independently checked here. Credit Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their outline text.

The commutator mechanism relevant to (A) is exact and simple: `W_E` acts independently on each output link and has `q_G=1/4` on SU(2) linkwise center-odd modes. But (A) asks for the actual stationary two-slice density to dominate a positive fraction `p` of a Haar-preserving entrance kernel followed by this commutator noise. Group Fourier decay alone says nothing about whether that signed, interacting conditional density has such a positive submeasure.

## 2. Doeblin, Dobrushin and volume-uniform transfer/mixing bounds

The standard Doeblin condition for a Markov kernel `P` is that for some fixed integer `m`, `epsilon>0`, and probability measure `nu`,

`P^m(x, A) >= epsilon nu(A)` for every state `x` and measurable set `A`.

It yields uniform total-variation contraction at block time `m` and geometric convergence. To obtain a volume-uniform result, `m`, `epsilon`, and the minorizing measure/control must be uniform over volumes. This is an abstract sufficient condition, not an automatic consequence of a smooth strictly positive finite-volume kernel: its minimum can deteriorate with the number of links. In (A), the lower measure is more structured than an arbitrary `nu`: `rW_E` preserves Haar at the entrance and then applies a specified smoothing convolution; the required coupling range is every admitted dyadic spatial size along the given cutoff trajectory, with the same fixed physical time `t` and `p>0`.

For continuous spins, Li-Ming Wu's 2006 theorem is more quantitative than a bare uniqueness criterion. For a finite site set `T`, a Polish (in particular compact) one-site space with metric `d`, and Gibbs single-site conditional kernels `mu_i`, define `c_ij` as the supremum Wasserstein-1 change in `mu_i` when only site `j` is changed, divided by `d(x_j,y_j)`. If the Dobrushin matrix has spectral radius `rho(C)<1` (with the theorem's finite second-moment assumption), then

`(1-rho(C)) Var_mu(f) <= E_mu sum_i Var_{mu_i}(f)`.

This is a spectral-gap/Poincare bound for the single-site heat-bath Glauber generator. Uniformity in volume follows only if the influence spectral-radius bound and conditional-kernel hypotheses hold uniformly for the volumes and boundary conditions in question. It does not itself minorize a gauge transfer operator or imply the special kernel inequality (A). Dobrushin–Shlosman complete analyticity is a stronger block-mixing framework; in finite-range systems it can give uniform mixing/functional inequalities under its block criteria, but the criterion must be established for the model and does not furnish a universal coupling threshold. [Wu, *Poincaré and Transportation Inequalities for Gibbs Measures under the Dobrushin Uniqueness Condition*, Annals of Probability 34 (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0611635).

For the 4D SU(2) Wilson transfer, the accessible 2026 Hexagon manuscript by Deep Bhattacharjee, Ushashi Bhattacharya, James Montgomery Lockwood and Tristan Romanov claims a single-link Wasserstein-Dobrushin route giving uniqueness and a transfer-matrix gap `m >= -log(9 beta_W/2)` for `beta_W < 2/9`; its strong-coupling result for compact simple groups states a lattice gap at `|beta| < 1/(8d)` (thus `1/32` at `d=4`). It also describes a gauge-invariant Dobrushin–Shlosman finite-box criterion: an averaged estimate in one fixed-physical-size cube, if held along the full tuning, implies a gap for every continuum limit. The source page says the SU(2) coarsest-scale estimate is shown only on tori with side at most `exp(2^k/(17k))` after `k` blocking steps, while the estimate along the whole tuning remains open. These are claims in a version-1 mixed human/AI manuscript, not a joint minorization of a time slab; the full paper body was not read in this pass, so I rely only on its abstract/provenance page. The page reports Lean formalization of finite/algebraic steps and Python checks of analytic identities; those computer-assisted checks are not used here as proof evidence. It states that the analytic arguments are by hand and that it is a fixed-lattice-spacing result, not a proof of the continuum Clay problem. The page gives no arXiv identifier; a previous lane note's attribution of this manuscript to arXiv:2610.00341 is incorrect (`2610.00341` is not this manuscript). [Hexagon version 1, “One Cube Estimate Implies the Yang–Mills Mass Gap”](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00341?version=1).

Jack Piazza and Hao Shen's 2026 preprint proves a log-Sobolev inequality for the two-dimensional SU(N) Wilson lattice gauge measure at every fixed `beta`, with constant depending on `beta,N` but uniform in lattice volume, by two-scale disintegration. It is continuous compact-spin gauge theory, but dimension two and an edge-law LSI; it is not a four-dimensional transfer-kernel minorization or a theorem about a slab. No computer-assisted step is claimed in its abstract. [Piazza–Shen, arXiv:2610.35286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35286).

The nearest older lattice hit in the repository is PR 10114/10133's result above: for SU(2), a one-step loop observable comparison yields a physical transfer gap at `beta <= 1/32` uniformly in finite volume. It assumes the specified Wilson transfer and uses a positive convolution-operator quadratic-form estimate; it does not give a Doeblin lower bound for all pairs of slices, nor a commutator-noise branch. The cited external criteria and the Hexagon/2D results therefore do not presently meet the task's requested uniform-volume hypothesis for the actual four-dimensional Wilson time-slab law, except in their distinct stated regimes/objects.

## 3. Has a lattice-gauge transfer gap been proved from joint slab minorization?

I found no published proof of a spectral gap for a lattice-gauge transfer operator that starts from the joint two-slice minorization in (A), or its specific commutator-noise form. This is a bounded literature-search finding, not a claim of nonexistence. The nearest analogues are (i) the abstract Doeblin implication, (ii) Wu's Dobrushin spectral gap for a Glauber generator, (iii) the Hexagon single-link Dobrushin/finite-box route, and (iv) the PR 10114 one-step transfer comparison. Their kernels, observables, hypotheses, or time scales differ from (A).

The lane has already worked out the proposed consumer: if (A) holds with the same `t,p` on all boxes and `q_G=1/4`, then the contraction margin is at least `p(1-q_G)=3p/4>0`, which is the sought uniform transfer contraction. The remaining estimate is precisely positivity and domination for the actual stationary joint slab law, uniformly over the admitted boxes and cutoffs. Existing fixed-box positivity/smoothing arguments have constants depending on box/cutoff; electric-only deconvolution omits spatial plaquette weights; the one-step Wilson sign can fail. None of the literature above removes that gap. The coupling range sought by (A) is fixed physical time while `a -> 0`, all sufficiently large admitted dyadic `L`, and a positive `p` independent of both `L` and cutoff; the published results reviewed here do not establish it.
