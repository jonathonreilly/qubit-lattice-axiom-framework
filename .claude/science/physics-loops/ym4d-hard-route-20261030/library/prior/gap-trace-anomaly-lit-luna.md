## Source-level findings on the uncovered point

- **Michael (1987), “Lattice action sum rules.” ON FILE: FINISH row 711; NEW in this pass: CERN full text retrieved, relevant equations and context read.** For 4D SU(N) Wilson theory, his transfer-matrix differentiation gives, for an isolated zero-momentum glueball level in a sufficiently large spatial box, equation (2.8): `-∂β(aE_1)=⟨1|Σ|1⟩−⟨0|Σ|0⟩`, where `Σ` is the sum of plaquettes on one time slice, including both spatial (magnetic) and time-directed (electric) plaquettes. This is a Feynman–Hellmann identity, with the derivation using a discrete lowest state and large-time dominance. The continuum fixed-mass relation (2.10) then uses the scaling assumption and asymptotic-freedom `∂β ln a`; it is not a uniform finite-cutoff lower bound at order-one β. The identity has no asserted sign at that coupling. The magnetic part is not isolated by the isotropic identity; the anisotropic sum rules resolve electric and magnetic combinations but require their coupling derivatives. No U(1)-failing estimate is stated. It is the closest published structural match, but it does not give the requested band bound. [CERN primary PDF](https://cds.cern.ch/record/169894/files/198608015.pdf), pp. 3–5, equations (2.4)–(2.13).

- **Rothe (1995), “Lattice energy sum rules and the trace anomaly.” NEW at this specific-paper level; related Rothe sum-rule correction ON FILE: row 711.** In pure lattice gauge theory, Rothe identifies the extra term in Michael’s static-potential energy rule with the trace-anomaly contribution and displays the analogous anomalous field-energy contribution in Michael’s glueball sum rule. This is an identity/interpretation of the sum rule, not a positivity theorem or a lower bound on state-minus-vacuum action at finite coupling. Its glueball relation inherits the regime and assumptions of the underlying sum rule; no U(1)-selective estimate follows. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9508005); its stated journal reference is *Phys. Lett. B* 364 (1995), 227–230.

- **Meyer (2007), “Sum Rules and Cutoff Effects in Wilson Lattice QCD.” NEW.** The transfer-matrix identity (arXiv eqs. (9)–(10)) expresses the β-derivative of a finite-volume glueball transfer gap as magnetic plaquette excess plus electric plaquette matrix elements with the transfer normalization factors. For external-size-dependent levels, eq. (20) gives `∂β Ĕ(n,β)=□^n_Ω`; eq. (23) is a Michael–Rothe relation with explicit `O(a²)` remainder. The model is 4D SU(N) Wilson gauge theory and Wilson-fermion QCD; spatial volume is assumed large enough to neglect finite-volume gap effects, and continuum scaling / a monotone reference scale is then used for extrapolation. These are nonperturbative sum-rule identities with cutoff errors, not inequalities: the RHS has no sign or order-one lower estimate in the paper. It is not shown to fail in U(1), and has no U(1)-selective step. [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-lat/0609007).

- **Giusti–Pepe (2015), “Energy-momentum tensor on the lattice: non-perturbative renormalization in Yang–Mills theory.” NEW.** In 4D SU(3) Wilson pure-gauge theory with shifted temporal boundary conditions, they define/renormalize the lattice EMT by Ward identities and show it has the correct continuum trace anomaly; the renormalization constants are determined numerically for bare `g₀²∈[0,1]` using simulations. Their identities hold to all perturbative orders, while the nonperturbative normalization is simulation-based with vanishing continuum discretization errors. This addresses the operator normalization and thermal EMT expectation, not an excited-transfer-state minus vacuum magnetic-action lower bound. No order-one lower bound or U(1)-failing mechanism. [arXiv primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07042), especially abstract and §§II–IV.

- **Faria da Veiga–O’Carroll (2024 version 2), “On Yang–Mills Stability Bounds and Plaquette Field Generating Function.” NEW.** The stated theorem treats finite-lattice Wilson models in d=2,3,4 for U(N), SU(N), free or periodic boundary conditions, `g²∈(0,g₀²]`, all lattice spacings `a∈(0,1]`, and even side length L. Theorem 4 bounds the generating function of any r scaled plaquette fields uniformly in L, a, g and source locations/orientations, with the paper deriving absolute correlation bounds by source analyticity. This is a claimed analytic theorem (not independently checked here); it is a uniform upper/control bound on scaled plaquette correlations, not a lower bound on the magnetic action excess in a selected excited level. The gauge family includes U(1) through U(N≥1), so it supplies no selective U(1) failure. [arXiv primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.07376), abstract and Theorem 4.

- **OpenAI Yang–Mills submission (released 2026-10-28; committee assessment 2026-11-10). ON FILE: existence/mass-gap scope in FINISH row 1446; NEW here: the source-specific trace statement.** The assessed Lean result defines a centered continuum trace of the stress tensor and proves a nonzero short-distance two-point law `r⁸ log²(1/r) S_{ΘΘ}(…) → 3 dim(g)/π⁴` (assessment Theorem 7.1). This is a trace-correlator/nonfree result for its constructed continuum theory, not a lattice plaquette expectation or an excited-level action deficit. The assessment explicitly says the submission does not prove uniqueness or a mass gap. The result's simple compact gauge groups are not the weak compact-U(1) comparison class, and it gives no bound whose failure there could select the band. [YM results repository and assessment](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/YM/tree/main/submissions/openai-20261028).

- **Positivity of the gluon condensate/action density.** The microscopic Wilson plaquette action `1−Re tr(U_p)/N` is pointwise nonnegative, but positivity of its expectation does not imply positivity of the difference of expectations in an excited level and vacuum. The continuum composite `F²`/trace operator also requires renormalization and an identity subtraction. The checked Michael/Rothe identities furnish a difference only tied to a mass derivative or scale response; they do not prove a positive finite-band condensate. Phenomenological SVZ condensate values and numerical plaquette subtractions are not rigorous lower bounds on this state difference. No selective U(1) result located.
