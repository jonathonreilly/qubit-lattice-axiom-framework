## 2005 journal article (read in full)

Source: A. C. D. van Enter and S. B. Shlosman, *Commun. Math. Phys.* 255 (2005), 21–32, DOI 10.1007/s00220-004-1286-1; author-institution repository version of record, downloaded from Groningen Pure. The PDF includes an institutional cover page followed by the 12 journal pages (journal pp. 21–32); all pages and references were read. Extracted text is beside the PDF at `~/ym4d-papers/dl-2026-11-11/van-enter-shlosman-cmp-255-2005/article.txt`.

The theorem’s stated scope is broader than the worked example. The introduction (journal pp. 22–23) says the gauge groups may be abelian or non-abelian and identifies compact continuous groups such as (U(1)) and (SU(n)) (p. 23). The explicit gauge construction begins on p. 27 for a (U(1))-invariant interaction in three dimensions, with (5), (L(U_P)=((1+\cos\phi_P)/2)^p). The proof uses a tree gauge and ordered/disordered plaquettes; the ordered partition-function lower bound is (7), p. 29. Theorem 5, p. 29, states a first-order transition for lattice gauge plaquette actions (((1+L(U_P))/2)^p), with (L(U_P)=\operatorname{Tr}(U_P+U_P^*)), in dimensions (d\ge3), for “(p) high enough.” The conclusion is nondifferentiability of free energy at some temperature and coexistence of Gibbs measures with different energy densities. It gives no numerical threshold or definition of the least admissible (p); the preceding discussion indicates only that the required largeness may depend on the model parameters (p. 27 for liquid crystals; p. 28 says the gauge well width is of order (p^{-1/2})).

The non-abelian and four-dimensional cases are asserted in the theorem and motivation, not proved in detail: the authors say the argument is presented for (U(1)) in (d=3) (p. 27), and the summary says the lattice-gauge arguments adapt from the Potts gauge proof (p. 24). The displayed derivation on pp. 27–29 is not an (SU(2)), (d=4) proof. The paper invokes reflection positivity for contour estimates (pp. 27–29), but the gauge section does not state the required plane locations or characterize the admissible weights by positive character coefficients. The journal article and preprint do not specify those details. The theorem’s printed (L)-normalization is potentially consequential: its displayed definition is (\operatorname{Tr}(U+U^*)), whereas the worked (U(1)) interaction in (5) has base ((1+\cos\phi_P)/2). I have not identified an explicit convention equating these, so I will not silently replace the theorem’s (L) by the normalized (SU(2)) character.

## Proposition V comparison

`library/gap-crux-fp.md` Section 4.1 (lines 315–368) takes x = (1/2)tr U and proposes w_p(U) = exp(beta ((1+x)/2)^p). It says the inner polynomial has nonnegative fundamental-character coefficients for integer p, and its exponential retains nonnegative coefficients for beta >= 0. It then claims the cited theorem gives a transition for every qualifying p and beta_t, with seed >= 1/5041 for a >= 3, conditional on its TM-gen input.

That is not yet the theorem in the form actually printed. To identify the proposed base (1+x)/2 with the article's theorem one must add a trace/normalization convention that makes its L equal x. The paper does not supply that convention; its worked U(1) example also displays a different-looking normalization. Even after adding that identification, the article only states the general d >= 3, compact-group result for p “high enough.” It does not give a threshold, and it writes no SU(2), d=4 proof. Thus it does not establish the proposition for a specified p or discharge the SU(2), four-dimensional application; it gives only a qualitative conditional result if the missing applicability assumptions hold.

The article's gauge section invokes reflection positivity but does not give plane locations or state positive character coefficients as its theorem hypothesis. Proposition V's coefficient positivity is an argument about its candidate weight, not evidence that this is the exact reflection-positivity condition used in the cited gauge proof. Finally, TM-gen (including the continuity input acknowledged in Section 4.1) is an additional premise not furnished by van Enter–Shlosman. The defensible status of Proposition V from this reading is conditional on (i) an explicit matching of normalizations and admissible weights, (ii) applicability of the qualitative theorem to the desired group/dimension/p, and (iii) TM-gen. The article alone does not verify those conditions.
