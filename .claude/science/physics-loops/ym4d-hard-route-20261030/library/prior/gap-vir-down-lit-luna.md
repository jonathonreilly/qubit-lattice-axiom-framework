### General convexity and coupling monotonicity

- **Finite-volume Wilson partition function (identity; background, not a new paper).** For any compact link group and bounded Wilson action `S`, `Z(beta)=int exp(-beta S)dHaar` has `d log Z/d beta=-E_beta S` and `d^2 log Z/d beta^2=Var_beta(S)>=0`. Thus log partition function is convex and its action mean decreases with beta. This is exact at every finite volume and applies equally to U(1), SU(2), finite groups, and spin systems. It is not a lower bound on a thermal excess: `log Tr(P_0 T^t)-t log lambda_0` is a difference of convex functions, and convexity alone fixes neither its derivative's sign nor a positive size. No order-one selective estimate.

- **Griffiths (1967); Ginibre (1970), “General formulation of Griffiths’ inequalities,” CMP 16, 310–328, DOI [10.1007/BF01646537](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01646537). ON FILE: FINISH row 740 (`gap-lit-ineq.md`); scope cross-read against row 1073.** Griffiths’ inequalities give nonnegative spin-product expectations and covariances in ferromagnetic Ising systems; differentiating a spin expectation in a ferromagnetic coupling gives a covariance and hence monotonicity. Ginibre extends the correlation inequality to suitable positive-definite-function cones on commutative compact groups. The gauge applications recorded in row 740 are finite Abelian gauge groups (Wilson action) and the real positive cone for U(1): they yield monotonicity/positivity for the cone observables in their hypotheses. The second U(1) inequality does not hold for arbitrary Wilson loops (a 2D example reverses it). This is not an SU(2) thermal-trace inequality and supplies no positive lower bound for `R`; the statements either do not cover SU(2), or include U(1) and so cannot be the requested selector. Primary Ginibre text was not independently reopened in this row; the exact gauge theorem and caveats are from the already-read Li–Zhou primary text recorded at row 740.

- **Messager–Miracle-Solé–Pfister (1978), “Correlation inequalities and uniqueness of the equilibrium state for the plane rotator ferromagnetic model,” CMP 58, 19–29, DOI [10.1007/BF01624786](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01624786). ON FILE: row 740 and its cited spin-system literature.** Their inequalities concern plane-rotator/O(2) ferromagnetic spin correlations and imply uniqueness of the translation-invariant Gibbs state under the paper’s isotropy/differentiability hypotheses (and the stated anisotropic variants). Their configuration variables are commuting spins, not non-Abelian gauge links; no transfer to the Wilson SU(2) plaquette law or to a thermal trace derivative is stated. The spin inequalities do not furnish an order-one rate and are group-blind only at the level of their spin models, not a gauge result.

### Loop equations, Ward identities, and work after 2024

- **Finite-lattice Schwinger–Dyson / Makeenko–Migdal identities. ON FILE: rows 755 and 915.** Chatterjee’s SO(N) and Jafarov’s SU(N) master loop equations are exact Haar-integration-by-parts identities at finite lattice and arbitrary real Wilson beta (Jafarov’s theorem is for finite N and loops whose one-neighbourhood lies inside the domain). Their split/merge/deformation terms are identities among loop expectations, not sign inequalities or volume-uniform thermal derivative bounds. Michael/Karsch sum rules likewise give energy identities rather than the required size lower bound.

- **Liu–Yang (2026), “Direct and indirect loop equations in lattice Yang–Mills theory,” JHEP 04 (2026) 107, [arXiv:2601.04316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04316). ON FILE: rows 755 and 915.** For SU(2), d=2,3,4, the paper gives a geometric procedure for enumerating local direct Schwinger–Dyson/trace-reduction equations and indirect equations obtained by eliminating longer loops; it reports finite-cutoff equation counts and growth fits. - **Chatterjee–Frasca–Ghoshal–Groote (2026), “Exact Lattice Identities and Continuum-Limit Dyson–Schwinger Equations for Yang–Mills Theory,” [arXiv:2608.05415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05415).
