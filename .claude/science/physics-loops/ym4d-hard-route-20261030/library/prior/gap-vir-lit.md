## 2. Fundamental–adjoint SU(2), endpoint evidence, and (NOCRIT)

**Bhanot and Creutz (1981), “Variant Actions and Phase Structure in Lattice Gauge Theory,”** *Physical Review D* 24 (1981), 3212, DOI 10.1103/PhysRevD.24.3212. [INSPIRE abstract/API record](https://inspirehep.net/api/literature?q=doi%3A10.1103%2FPhysRevD.24.3212). The abstract says they study a generalization of Wilson SU(2) that reduces in limits to SU(2), SO(3), or Z2 models. It says their **Monte Carlo** study on a four-dimensional lattice follows the SO(3) and Z2 first-order transitions into the phase diagram: they merge at a triple point and continue to a critical endpoint; the SU(2) specific-heat peak is a “shadow” of that nearby singularity. This is numerical phase-diagram evidence, not a rigorous transition theorem. The abstract does not expose the endpoint coordinates or a theorem on the Wilson axis; those details are NOT READ. It supports K7 as an action-dependence test for (NOCRIT), but proves nothing about (NOCRIT) for the Wilson action.

**Enrico Rinaldi, Giuseppe Lacagnina, Biagio Lucini, Agostino Patella, and Antonio Rago (2012), “Scaling properties of SU(2) gauge theory with mixed fundamental-adjoint action,”** *PoS LATTICE2012* (2012), 252, arXiv:1212.0635, DOI 10.22323/1.164.0252. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.0635), [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1212.0635v1). The article writes the plaquette action as a sum of fundamental and adjoint terms. Its §2 says the Wilson axis is `β_adj=0` and has a crossover region around `β_fund≈2.30`. It says turning on the adjoint coupling produces a first-order bulk transition, stronger as `β_adj` grows. From hot/cold hysteresis and volume scaling, the authors estimate that the separation of the two branches vanishes near `β_adj≲1.25`; they say the `β_adj=1.25` run on `40^4` has poorly controlled systematic and statistical errors. They explicitly say they do not aim to pin the endpoint precisely and call its nature controversial. This is a Monte Carlo study, not a rigorous theorem. It corrects the queued recollection only partially: the later page supports a nearby endpoint and a Wilson-axis crossover, but does not verify the remembered coordinate `(β_f,β_adj)=(1.5,0.9)`.

**Rigorous status on the Wilson axis.** No source page read here proves a thermodynamic phase transition on the four-dimensional pure SU(2) Wilson axis, nor proves its absence at all `β>0`. The numerical “crossover near 2.30” in Rinaldi et al. is not such a theorem. The mixed-action first-order line is a useful warning against deriving (NOCRIT) from group-level or generic positivity properties alone. It does not refute (NOCRIT), whose target is the Wilson weight. No rigorous Wilson-axis phase-transition result was found in this bounded search; this is not a literature-wide nonexistence claim.


## 3. Which Wilson-weight properties survive an adjoint term?

**Finding.** I found no published theorem in the inspected sources that is explicitly stated to hold for the pure Wilson action and fail for the mixed fundamental-adjoint SU(2) action. The premise needs qualification: loop equations from Haar integration by parts are not unique to the Wilson action, although their action-dependent terms change; positivity can also survive.

- **Exact loop equations.** The prior digest `gap-crux-lit-nonab.md:164–167` records Kazakov’s finite-N SU(2) identity and loop equations, with arXiv HTML source `https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16925`, §2. These equations use Haar integration and the SU(2) trace identity; they are not a proof of a gap. A smooth mixed plaquette density still permits Haar integration by parts, producing a different insertion involving the derivative of the mixed action. That last sentence is the direct group-calculus observation, not a theorem quoted from Kazakov. The associated numerical truncation is excluded as proof evidence (see topic 4).
- **Character positivity.** A direct SU(2) algebra check: for positive fundamental and adjoint couplings, the one-plaquette weight is proportional to `exp(c_f χ_1/2) exp(c_a χ_1)`, with `χ_1=χ_1/2^2−1`. Expanding both exponentials in nonnegative powers and decomposing products of SU(2) characters gives nonnegative character coefficients. This is an elementary representation-ring derivation, not a published source claim; it means that “an adjoint term destroys character positivity” is not valid in this positive-coupling regime without a more specific positivity notion. No source page read here states a contrary theorem.
- **Reflection positivity and twist bounds.** Takuya Kanazawa (2008 preprint; 2009 *Annals of Physics* 324, 1634–1665), “Generalizing the Tomboulis-Yaffe Inequality to SU(N) Lattice Gauge Theories and General Classical Spin Systems,” [arXiv HTML §2.1, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/0808.3442). As summarized from the primary page at `gap-crux-lit-2.md:261`, Theorem 1 states a Tomboulis–Yaffe type SU(N) inequality for **any one-plaquette action**, under site-reflection positivity. It is therefore not specifically a Wilson-weight property. For the mixed action, the exact applicability still requires checking its coefficients satisfy the theorem’s reflection-positivity hypotheses; the theorem is not itself a mixed-action phase result. It gives an area-law criterion from twist sensitivity, not MIX or a gap estimate.

**Result for the target.** No verified example was found of a proof that depends on a property destroyed by the adjoint plaquette term. The exact finite-N loop equation survives in changed form; SU(2) character positivity has a simple positive-coupling argument; the general reflection-positive one-plaquette inequality is not Wilson-specific. No use for NOCRIT or VIR-log. The exact hypothesis check for the mixed-action measure in Kanazawa’s theorem is **NOT READ** at proof level beyond the statement “any one-plaquette action.”

## 6. Step scaling and the Schrödinger functional

**Martin Lüscher, Rajamani Narayanan, Peter Weisz and Ulli Wolff (1992), “The Schrödinger Functional — a Renormalizable Probe for Non-Abelian Gauge Theories,”** arXiv:hep-lat/9207009. [arXiv abstract page read](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9207009). The abstract says: “Following Symanzik we argue that the Schrödinger functional in lattice gauge theories without matter fields has a well-defined continuum limit”; it adds that gauge invariance requires no extra counterterms, says the observable is accessible to numerical simulations, and proposes using it to study scale dependence of the renormalized coupling, with the perturbative analysis carried to one loop. This is a theoretical renormalizability argument and a perturbative calculation, not a rigorous nonperturbative inequality between boxes at coupling of order one.

**Martin Lüscher, Peter Weisz, Rainer Sommer and Ulli Wolff (1992), “Computation of the Running Coupling in the SU(2) Yang-Mills Theory,”** arXiv:hep-lat/9207010. [arXiv abstract page read](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-lat/9207010). The page states: “A finite-size scaling technique is applied to the SU(2) gauge theory (without matter fields) to compute a non-perturbatively defined running coupling α(q) for a range of momenta q given in units of the string tension K”; it reports that the evolution is “well described by the 2-loop approximation” already at rather low q, and gives a measured value at the highest momentum with two quoted uncertainties. This is numerical finite-size-scaling evidence, not a rigorous inequality. Its abstract does not establish that this computation uses the Schrödinger functional; the link between these two papers is therefore **NOT READ** here.

**Finding for the requested claim.** The Schrödinger-functional paper argues for a well-defined continuum observable and proposes its numerical use; the SU(2) running-coupling paper reports a measured scale evolution. Neither page states a proved inequality comparing two box sizes at a coupling of order one. Across the inspected pages and prior digests, I found no rigorous step-scaling inequality of the requested kind. No use for (VIR-log), (NOCRIT), (BOOT_eps), or (MIX); numerical guide only. This is a bounded literature finding, not a claim that no such result exists.
