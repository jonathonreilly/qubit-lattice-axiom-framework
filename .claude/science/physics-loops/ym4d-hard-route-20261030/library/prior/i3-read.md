## 1. What the five Y passages say

**(a) Y 65408–65412, label `hi:prepared-real-endpoint`.** Exact text: “At a stage of the depth-\(m\) problem set \(q=j+1\) and put \(e_l=\check R_l^2\varepsilon_l\).” The final line says the tests “with sharper thresholds keep those thresholds.” This range defines the scale-weighted error; it does **not**, by itself, state the claimed `C max e_q` bound. In the same labeled paragraph, Y 65417–65424 specifies the entering data and Y 65476–65520 derives a bound `C e_l` for each tested plaquette from retained tests and a profile estimate. Thus the cited paragraph contains an argument, not a separate named theorem.

**(b) Y 78322–78338, hyperlink `hi:prepared-real-endpoint`.** Exact text: “On its admitted incoming fibre the old lower holonomy bounds and the exact top averaging relation give the prepared datum bounds \(C e_q\) of the endpoint comparison.” Y then says to “Carry these bounds as a support condition on the prepared datum,” and that the indicator is one before the old averaging deltas are integrated, “on the admitted entering fibre.” This is an application of the endpoint-comparison paragraph, while the lower-holonomy and averaging facts are conditions of the admitted fibre/support being used. Following the hyperlink one step, Y 65417–65424 says the data include the still-carried operation fields, actual determining links, full-double-block averages, and retained averaging constraints; Y 65425–65491 explains which carried ordinary/stage tests supply each footprint. Y 65485 says, “Thus each test gives a bound \(C e_l\) directly.”

Y does not give a separate formal definition headed “admitted incoming fibre.” Its operative definition/constraint, quoted from `hi:prepared-real-endpoint` (65417–65424), is: “The input at a point of a history includes the fields of the operations still carried by it. A datum plaquette uses the actual determining links and, for a completed bond, the average of the lower field on its full double block. These values agree with the fields in the tests by the retained averaging constraints.” The endpoint-comparison admission and retained tests are detailed at 65459–65491. Y 69492–69515 separately defines an “admitted labelled fibre of a stage” by fixing outgoing and retained data, with active old-strip fields `a` and free collar variables `B`; this describes a stage integration fibre, not a replacement definition of the particular “admitted incoming fibre.” The explicit compatibility definition `hi:compatible-fibre` at 74072–74100 is for a drop-out evaluation set and is also not interchangeable with this incoming fibre.

**(c) Y 134198–134216, prose in the marked-test admission discussion (no theorem/lemma label at this passage).** Exact text: “For that guard and the later prospective guards, use the promoted datum bounds on the entering support. These bounds, \(C e_q\) in each layer \(q\), use the actual promoted averages before their delta functions are integrated.” A joined tested holonomy “either agrees with a tested holonomy on the global datum, is one in the lifted collar, or equals a tested top holonomy of the datum being lifted.” Y concludes regularity for `bar epsilon > C max_q e_q`, then invokes weak-coupling ceilings to get the regular orbit before the guard. This is a clause asserted on the entering support, not a universal statement for all configurations. The nearby carried-datum discussion Y 76628–76650 and 76654–76689 describes how passed tests, their full footprints, and retained/promoted averages provide layer bounds; it also names extra support/read conditions.

**(d) Y 71383–71392, uses `mb:datum-profile` and `mb:weighted-maximum`.** Exact screening statement: “Here \(\check R_m\) is formed from the running minimum of the frame, which is at least \(\theta_m-c_5\), and the exponent of its logarithm is at least 2. Thus \(\varepsilon_\star e^{-c\delta_{\rm c}M\check R_m}=o(\varepsilon_m)\).” In this passage it is the contribution of remote datum holonomies to the profile at a crossing plaquette. Following the cited labels one step, `mb:datum-profile` defines the profile with weight \(e^{-\delta d_{\mathbf B}(x,\tau)}\) (Y 12471–12481), and `mb:weighted-maximum` proves the resulting local-radius bound (Y 12483–12503). The rate used in the displayed screening term is the local regularity rate \(\delta_{\rm c}\), with weighted metric \(d_{\mathbf B}\); it is not an unweighted Euclidean-distance estimate.

**(e) Y 77940–77949, collar/floor geometry in the same weighted metric.** Exact text: the band from `G_{i_flat}` to the first prepared face “has thickness of order \(a_{i_flat+1}\) in layers at most \(i_flat\), which gives weighted distance at least \(cM\check R_i\) even at a later compressed stage. The artificial tower collar at \(\partial X\) is also this far in the weighted metric.” The preceding lines (77924–77938) say the complete order-`q` averaging blocks stay in the unchanged interior of the join and the old `Omega_i` wall is separated from the outer tower collar. This is a geometric assertion in Y's prescribed nested/quiet-box setup, not a bound on arbitrary sets.

## 2. Configuration scope

The `C e_q` claims are **not** for every configuration. They apply on Y's admitted incoming/entering support: the relevant old lower holonomy tests have their recorded values; the actual promoted averages are retained before their delta constraints are integrated; completed bonds use their full double-block averages; the carried operations and their fields are included; and the history/stage has the stated separated geometry and prescribed collars. Y 65417–65520 details the test-by-test endpoint comparison. Y 78328–78338 explicitly calls the prepared bounds a support condition. Y 134198–134216 applies the promoted bounds on that support.

The profile statement is conditional on a real constrained-critical configuration in the local regularity radius and its datum tests. `hi:real-datum-profile` states the local inequality on that admitted real geometry (Y 15317–15338); the global weighted maximum then gives the weighted profile. The screening is for the crossing plaquette/tests under the stated separated shell, frame and floor geometry (Y 71331–71347), not every field or every collar point.

## 3. Constant, levels and uniformity

Y does **not** state the I3 constant at Y 134212 as `C(L)`; it writes unqualified `C`. It does explicitly use `C(L)` for adjacent-scale conversion in the endpoint argument (Y 65553–65573), while other constants depend on the profile/geometry choices (for example `K_loc`, `R=20L sigma`, and the fixed geometry floors in `hi:real-datum-profile`, Y 15317–15338). So the cited I3 constant cannot be read as a proved function of `L` alone. Its full dependence is not specified there.

The maximum is over the tested levels `q` of the joined datum at the depth-`m` stage, not over arbitrary levels. Y sets `q=j+1` in the stage discussion (65408–65412) and discusses tests on actual levels/layers through the depth-`m` operation (65436–65455, 65536–65563). Y 134212 does not print an explicit closed interval under `max_q`; reading it as all test levels present, up to the top depth `m`, is my reading.

Uniformity is mixed. The local weighted-maximum proof says it works on infinite volume when the input radius is bounded (Y 12492–12495); the endpoint argument says the profile is not to be iterated with a growing comparison constant and records uniformity in the waiting window (Y 65586–65591). But the bare `C max_q e_q` clause at Y 134212 does not itself specify uniformity in `m`, volume and every history, nor give all dependencies of `C`. The background-existence theorem has its own depth/volume-uniform constants (Y 35632–35646), but that does not by itself make every premise of I3 uniform. My reading: the argument intends geometry-controlled bounds for admitted histories; the exact all-three uniformity requested is not expressly stated for I3 as a whole.

## 4. Screening distance versus the outer cell layer `E`

Y gets the `c M check R_m` suppression by separating a test/read from a crossing collar through prepared shells. For the passage at 71331–71347, the shell between `Z''_{h+1}(X)` and `Z''_{h+2}(X)` has width at least `7 a_{h+1}` and level spacing `L^{h+1}`; after the footprint allowance every path to a crossing plaquette at `partial Y` has weighted length at least `cM check R_m`. The local estimate therefore weights the remote datum by `e^{-c delta_c M check R_m}`. For the artificial tower collar, Y 77943–77949 uses the band from `G_{i_flat}` to the first prepared face; its layer thickness is order `a_{i_flat+1}` and yields the same lower bound, including at a later compressed stage.

For comparison, `library/rim-scale.md` §6.1 defines `X_in` by removing one level-`m` cell layer from `X`, and `E` as bare plaquettes of `X` with a corner outside `X_in`; thus `E` is the outer cell layer of plaquettes. Y's `eq:elab-history-quiet-boxes` defines `O_m := Omega_m^c cap X = H_m^[8a_m]`, `G_m=H_m^[10a_m]`, and `G_m=X` (Y 63748–63769); `hi:preceding-guard-support` says the earlier guards read only `X cap Omega_m^c`, two level-`m` cells inside `X`, and the artificial bare rim occurs only in the final guard (Y 64221–64232). Hence, on this quiet-box geometry, `X cap Omega_m^c` and `E` are separated by at least one level-`m` cell after accounting for the one-cell plaquette layer in `E`. Since a level-`m` cell has side `a_m=M check R_m L^m` and no intervening layer has spacing greater than `L^m`, the same order-`cM check R_m` weighted-distance lower bound follows. This last comparison is my reading of Y's box and layer definitions; Y 77947–77949 states the distance to its artificial collar/floor, not in the notation `E` used by the later RIM-SCALE restatement.

**Answer to the row's question.** Y contains a conditional argument for layerwise datum bounds and weighted screening, but I3 is not an unconditional theorem about every field or every history. The incoming lower-holonomy facts and retained averaging relation are properties of the admitted incoming support; the Y text uses them there and carries the resulting bounds forward. The collar screening follows from its weighted profile and prepared-shell separation. I3 remains a conditional Y input unless the admission/support clauses and the precise constant/uniformity are separately established for the histories consumed by RIM-REG.
