## 3. Thickness, fingers and the three different hulls

**Precisely what Proposition 3.1 gives.** `out-pi.md:325–329,1148–1168`
requires **every** Omega_i to be a union of cells on a grid aligned with the
level-i big-block grid, of spacing s_i, a multiple of M_b ell_i and at least
`12 M_b ell_i`. Its conclusion is restricted to x with
`N(x) subset Omega_0`. Even Omega_0=T does not remove the condition on the
other members. Increasing the gap alone proves neither the cell condition
nor the boundary conclusion.

For the compatible prepared M-grids at j>=1, the size requirement is available
**after matching M_b with that section's M_1**: I 52679–52711 imposes
`M >= 13 L^2 M_1^sharp` and `M_1^sharp >= 3 M_1`, much more than the factor
12. The step grids are also large compatible grids, with powers of L and
running block sizes; their unit conversion must be stated (`W_t/ell_t =
M check R_t` in level-k units). SA 10262 explicitly describes label regions
at n>=1 as unions of LM-cubes. These are useful interior inputs, not reasons
to ignore the outer hull or newly eroded/localized members.

I 12712–12724 and 16321–16380 give only M_b-scale unions for general or
maximal eroded towers. Their wording does not establish s_i>=12 M_b ell_i.
Likewise “cube-wise meet” and “admissible” alone do not establish it. A proof
lane must port the actual larger-cell property where available, and leave it
unproved where it is not supplied; it must not assert that every constructed
family contains a thin finger either.

**Balaban's restriction is on disk, and sometimes used.**
`lit-balaban.md:127–148` points to CMP 99b §3/p.396.
`print-inverse-r2.md:140–147` quotes the outer member as the union of big
blocks whose distances to Omega_1 are **“<= RM”**. I 52469–52519 quotes it
again, calls it **Omega_0^cl, the class hull**, and explicitly says it is
“to be distinguished from the hull Omega_0 of the nest”. It also says
membership in the account's C_0 itself “imposes no condition relative to a
hull” (I 52504ff). This rules out the claim that the account never mentions
or imposes such a restriction.

There are three distinct objects:

1. The **nest hull** Omega_0, arbitrary in §4.5, but a Dirichlet hull in
   prepared-data A1 (I 45584–45595), and a one-cell enlargement of Omega_1
   for labels (SA 17317; N 61973).
2. The **class hull** Omega_0^cl of the distance quotation (I 52474–52490).
3. The **data region** Omega_0^dat, containing `Omega_0^(+2 eta)` under
   `(col)` (I 52371–52385). This last inclusion furnishes coefficients;
   it does not by itself bound them.

I 52755–52761 explains a possible identification: at level zero a class
cube loses nothing on intersection with the data region if
`Omega_0^cl subset Omega_0^dat`, as when the latter is taken to be the
Balaban hull. It does not identify all three objects for every invocation.
The one-cell label enlargement is a genuine outer-neighbourhood restriction
of the same kind; matching its cell size and distance constants with the
quoted RM-hull is still necessary. A lattice “corridor” elsewhere is an
averaging/transport carrier or history bridge, not an extra general
no-thin clause.

**What the existing counterexamples do and do not establish.**

* `out-pi.md:331–345,1170–1208` already refutes the unrestricted class using
  a finger at most nine big blocks wide, remote from Omega_1, and a real
  one-bond field with zero twist. The tested cubes miss it. The written
  spectral-crossing argument makes Delta_a singular. The CMP 99b distance
  restriction excludes this particular remote finger. Large M-grid step
  regions also cannot have that particular width relative to M_b once the
  above floors are matched.
* `out-pi.md:339–347,1210–1225` is stronger at the boundary: for **any**
  nonempty proper Omega_0 with the stated room, a crossing field bond is
  untested by internal cube bonds. Its six whole-action plaquettes remain
  in the Hessian. A real field supported on that bond gives a singular
  Delta_a while (S1), (S2), (S2+) hold. No geometric restriction retaining
  a nonempty proper Omega_0 removes that freedom by itself.

These are counterexamples to the **operator input class** as stated. They
are not proofs that a positive-weight history of the actual RG produces
that singular reference. Passed bare tests, minimizer restrictions, or the
stronger box domain can exclude that field. Establishing their exclusion on
every actual complete read is precisely the admission question. The new
bare test at N 61974–61991 is relevant supply, not a proof for every later
localized/reference-changing call.

SA itself retains extra boundary premises: 9799 requires small curvature on
plaquettes which “protrude from Omega_0”; 10287 says the G_0 real-background
bound “includes the plaquettes that stick out”. SA 9801 instead trims the
outer member before using a gauge proposition on interior enlargements.
Neither passage proves the full boundary-patch hypothesis for the original
untrimmed PI setting.

## 6. Verdict and the next proof lane

**Minimal useful PI-ADMIT statement.** For each operator setting reached by
the construction and each parameter in its **prescribed** complex domain,
identify one real unitary reference and the actual twist, on the complete
background domain `Omega_0^+`, such that:

1. The actual tower, literal rows, corner-block convention, spaces,
   transposes, mass, metric and **whole-action** compressed Hessian match
   the PI setting. Where the consumer instead has the inner-action Hessian
   or symmetric/plain/straightened rows, supply a separate identification
   or the appropriate other theorem. It is not admitted by renaming it.
2. Every PI patch N(x), including patches crossing the outer boundary,
   carries the required unitary gauge with the scaled value and ordinary
   first-difference bounds (S1-loc). Use Proposition 3.1 only for the
