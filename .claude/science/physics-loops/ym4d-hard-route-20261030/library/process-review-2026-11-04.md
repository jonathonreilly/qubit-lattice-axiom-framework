# Process review: why we deliver less than Sussman-Alpoge with the same tools

Lead (Claude Opus 5.5), 2026-11-04, at the owner's request: "we should do deep review of our process and approach if its not delivering what they are able to do with the same tools". This review compares the two processes and proposes a new method. It does not grade the mathematics in either set of texts.

## 1. How they work (their own words)

Sources: their [README](https://github.com/ethanwsussman/Yang--Mills-LLM-experiment/blob/31a47c543775/README.md), the repository tree at `31a47c543775`, `YM3/Revisions/CHANGES-2026-10-26.md`, and the NL1 submanuscript README. I read all of these myself.

- **Claude generates at volume.** "The manuscripts cited above are the result of cutting material from literally thousands of pages of slop generated by Claude. That slop is highly redundant and over-elaborated."
- **Codex distills.** "It is then fed to Codex to extract a minimal manuscript. This seems to work reasonably well." The README names one known defect: some definitions are explicit in the slop but must be inferred in the abridged version.
- **The volume is large.** YM4: "Total slop, ~61k pages. Closed slop, ~20k pages." YM3: about 30k pages, all closed. QED3: 4,007 pages, cut to a 242-page manuscript.
- **They decompose the proof.** Each part is a codenamed submanuscript (NL1, RR1, VT1, FB1, ...). Each has an outline (`*O1`), an AI manuscript (`*M1`) and a README that states its main theorem with its imported hypotheses. An "assembly overview" shows how the main result is put together from the parts.
- **Simpler models come first.** QED3, then YM3, then YM4. "The assembly overviews for the three models have analogous structure." Hard YM4 arguments are shown first in the easier three-dimensional setting.
- **They referee, then freeze.** "Closed slop is the slop that has been refereed and frozen." A revision gets a change log that restores the hypotheses from the slop (YM3 CHANGES file).
- **A human guides the ideas.** "Novel mathematical ideas should be attributed to Claude, under the guidance of one of us [LA]."

## 3. Root causes

1. **Our roles are inverted.** They use Claude to generate long arguments and Codex to cut them down. We use Codex to generate short notes, and Claude to orchestrate, check and keep books. Claude writes almost no mathematics here.
2. **We own no architecture.** They wrote their whole argument and then cut it. We read their outline and patch it one gap at a time. That is the "grunt work" the owner named. It makes every lane depend on their text and their choices, such as the (R5) chart that stopped ten lanes.
3. **We stop early by design.** Our rules ask for short notes (about 80% ASD-STE100), one question per lane, and a named first gap. Those rules fit outward posts and checks. They are wrong for generating a proof. They produce gap lists, which the owner's hard-core standard forbids.
4. **We check fragments, not wholes.** "One check per proof" applies to each 150-line note. They referee a whole submanuscript once and then freeze it.
5. **Bookkeeping costs too much.** Rows, outline-check lines, provenance lines, relays between machines and pacing take most lead turns. They keep status inside the manuscript ("Proof outstanding").
6. **We went hardest first with no easier analogue in hand.** "Hardest first" is right for choosing what to attack. It is wrong for how to build: they made each YM4 mechanism work in QED3/YM3 first. Their YM3_I (857 pages, claimed complete OS for tr F^2) has the same structure as YM4, and we have not used it as a template.

## 4. The new method (proposed)

1. **Our own skeleton first.** We write an assembly overview of T0 ourselves. It lists codenamed submanuscripts, each with its main statement, imported hypotheses and dependencies, in the style of their READMEs. Published work is an input, credited: their YM4 sketch and complete account, YM3_I/II, QED3, Balaban, Dimock. We choose our own architecture where theirs stops us, for example intrinsic domains in place of (R5) charts (row 259).
2. **Claude generates each submanuscript at length.** Long sessions, no brevity rule, no stop-at-gap rule. Each session aims for a complete argument, and redundancy is fine. The output is the raw draft.
3. **Codex distills the draft** into a minimal manuscript with numbered statements and proofs. Codex lists every definition it had to infer, which fixes the defect their README names.
4. **One referee pass per submanuscript.** The other model reads the whole distilled manuscript line by line. Fixes go in with a change log. Then we freeze it, as their "closed" status does.
5. **Use the easier analogue.** For each submanuscript, first find the matching YM3 or QED3 submanuscript and port its argument.
6. **Status lives in the manuscripts.** FINISH keeps one row per submanuscript, not per lane.
7. **The rules that stay:** be right about others' work (read before claiming), no computer-assisted proofs (everything must be Lean-formalizable), credit, owner approval of outward text, and pacing. Spend Claude capacity when it is there.
8. **The same method applies to the TOE** once T0 is skeleton-complete: write a skeleton from the four axioms, then submanuscripts, generate, distill, referee, freeze.
