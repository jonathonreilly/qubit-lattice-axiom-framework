## 2. The signed inverse difference: exact exclusion perturbation

**Discharged — the current that must be estimated.** For a first-level bond `c=(y,y')`, let `I_c` be its `N=L^4` starting points, `D_c` its *actual* excluded subset, `R_c=I_c\D_c`, and `q_c=|D_c|/N`. These are starting lines, not complete face columns. Write

    J_cx = Gamma_yx + [x,x+L e_mu] - Gamma_y',x+L e_mu.

Here a path means its signed fine-bond current, or its prescribed probability average if the kernel averages paths. Use the actual routed contour whenever its endpoint is used by a surviving line. The source requires the same contour at a given block endpoint in every direction (`relative-density-bound.md:327`). Extend this family to unused endpoints by arbitrary paths inside their blocks. This extension is an algebraic device and does not alter any actual retained row. Every `J_cx` has boundary `y'-y`. The derivative at the identity of the relative-log formula is its mean current: the derivative of the reference path subtracts inside the logarithm and adds back outside it.

Let `bar Q_h` average these currents over *all* `I_c`. Then `bar Q_h-Q_0` is the gradient of the difference of the two block contour averages, by telescoping at the endpoints. Define the closed row current

    R(c) = q_c [ avg_(x in R_c) J_cx - avg_(z in D_c) J_cz ],
    R(c) = 0 if D_c is empty.                                  (2.1)

Directly subtracting `(1/N)sum_(I_c) J_cx` from `(1/|R_c|)sum_(R_c) J_cx` proves `(Q_h-bar Q_h)(c)=R(c)`. Thus `R` kills every gradient and every constant fine cochain. A second all-lines map sends gradients to gradients, so, with `B_1=d_t Q_path^(1)`,

    C_h = C_0 + D,             D=B_1 R.                         (2.2)

The only nonzero rows of `R` are those with excluded lines. This expression includes the changed normalization and the direction-dependent retained averages of the routed contours. Omitting either would change the question. The choice of unused contour extensions changes `R` only by something annihilated by `B_1`.

**Discharged — Gram perturbation, including its mixed terms.** All adjoints in the following formula use `X`:

    G_h = G_0 + C_0 D^* + D C_0^* + D D^*.                     (2.3)

This is the precise sense in which the perturbation is generated by excluded lines. The Gram perturbation itself is not a diagonal local deletion: the energy adjoint contains the fine inverse, and the two mixed terms must be retained. Put

    A=G_0^(-1/2) C_0,       M=G_0^(-1/2) D,
    u=G_0^(-1/2)y,          eps=||M||.

Positive square roots here require only finite-dimensional diagonalization: maximizing the quadratic form of a real symmetric matrix on the unit sphere gives an eigenvector by the Lagrange multiplier equation; its orthogonal complement is invariant; induction gives an orthonormal eigenbasis. Positive definiteness makes every eigenvalue positive, so taking its positive square root defines the displayed matrices. Then `AA^*=I` and `G_0^(-1/2)G_h G_0^(-1/2)=(A+M)(A+M)^*=:B`. For every `z`, `||A^*z||=||z||`; the triangle inequality and its reverse give

    (1-eps)^2 I <= B <= (1+eps)^2 I,       if eps<1.             (2.4)

No sign of the mixed terms was assumed. In particular inversion of the upper inequality, obtained by diagonalizing the positive finite matrix `B`, gives the *signed* estimate

    <y,(G_0^(-1)-G_h^(-1))y>
       = <u,(I-B^(-1))u>
       <= [1-(1+eps)^(-2)] ||u||^2
       <= 2 eps ||u||^2.                                    (2.5)

The last inequality follows from `(2+eps)/(1+eps)^2<=2`. It is valid whether the left side is positive or negative.

**Discharged — simultaneous control of the changed minimizer.** In these coordinates `H_h y=(A+M)^*B^(-1)u` and `H_0y=A^*u`. Also `||B-I||<=2eps+eps^2`. Subtraction and (2.4) therefore give

    ||(H_h-H_0)y||_X
      <= (3eps+eps^2)/(1-eps)^2 ||u||
      <= 6 eps ||u||,                  0<=eps<=1/4.           (2.6)

For the last step, `3+eps<=13/4` and `(1-eps)^2>=9/16`, whose ratio is `52/9<6`. Equations (2.5) and (2.6) reduce the difficult signed inverse term and the perturbation of its row to one energy-norm estimate on the actual deletion currents. They are finite-dimensional identities and inequalities, not numerical evidence. The unperturbed fine-plaquette row and all constants needed to use them are proved next.

## 3. Two all-lines steps: explicit bounds, uniform in the torus

**Discharged — square weights and norms.** Put `R=L^2` in this section only; the row operator `R(c)` of (2.1) will always carry its argument. The two all-lines path averages compose to the single scale-`R` path average. Indeed the two nested uniform starting-point sums list each of the `R^4` starting points once, and the two nested longitudinal sums concatenate into a length-`R` line. All contour terms are gradients and disappear after taking the top curl. Thus `C_0 x=B_R d_f x`, where the row of `B_R` for an oriented top `(mu,nu)` plaquette is the average of the `R` by `R` fine squares with those starting points.

For one coordinate, let `b_R` be the indicator of the `R` consecutive block sites and let `t_R` be its convolution with the indicator of `R` consecutive line positions. Up to translations, a row is

    R^(-4) t_R(n_mu)t_R(n_nu)b_R(n_k)b_R(n_l).              (3.1)

Here `sum b_R=R`, `sum t_R=R^2`, `max t_R=R`, and `sum |Delta b_R|=2`, `sum |Delta t_R|=2R`. The translates of `b_R` by `R` partition the integers; the corresponding translates of `t_R` sum to `R`, by summing its defining convolution. These identities remain true periodically. Consequently each row of `B_R` sums to `R^2` and each column sums to `R^(-2)`. For `Delta_j B_R^*`, the absolute row sums are at most `2/R^2` (the sum of two columns of `B_R`), and the absolute column sums are at most `2R` (use the displayed total variations in the differentiated factor of (3.1)).

For a matrix `K` with absolute row sums at most `a` and column sums at most `b`, Cauchy–Schwarz in each row gives `||Kz||^2<=ab||z||^2`. Apply this to the six separate plaquette orientations, and then sum the four differences. It proves

    ||B_R||<=1,
    sum_(j=1)^4 ||Delta_j B_R^*lambda||^2
                                      <=(16/R)||lambda||^2. (3.2)

**Discharged — an all-lines right inverse of norm at most 64.** The following is a finite Fourier argument, not a numerical Fourier check. On a periodic lattice, summing a finite geometric progression proves orthogonality of the characters; expanding in these characters proves Parseval. At a top frequency `p`, choose the representative `-pi<p_j<=pi` and write `a_j=exp(i p_j)-1`, `sigma(p)=sum |a_j|^2`. A top exact two-form has zero coefficient at `p=0`. At any other frequency write it as `a wedge v`, and remove the component of `v` parallel to `a`. Expanding the squares gives

    |a wedge v|^2=|a|^2|v|^2-|sum conjugate(a_j)v_j|^2.

Thus the remaining `v` has `d_t v=y` and `sigma(p)|v(p)|^2=|y(p)|^2`. This constructs the required top potential frequency by frequency.

Lift its frequency to `p/R` on the fine torus. The scale-`R` path row in component `mu` has multiplier of magnitude

    R s_R(p_mu)^2 product_(j!=mu) s_R(p_j),
    s_R(t)=|sin(t/2)|/[R |sin(t/(2R))|],  s_R(0)=1.         (3.3)

This follows by multiplying the finite geometric sums for the block average and for the line. Concavity of sine on `[0,pi/2]` gives `sin u>=2u/pi`; integrating `cos u<=1` gives `sin u<=u`. Hence `s_R(t)>=2/pi>1/2`, so the multiplier is at least `R/32`. Here `pi<4` follows, for example, from `pi/4=integral_0^1(1+t^2)^(-1)dt<1`. Divide each component of `v` by its full complex multiplier, including the phase. Its path average is then exactly `v`.

The fine frequency obeys `sigma(p/R)<4 sigma(p)/R^2`, by the same sine inequalities. The fine squared norm has `R^4` times the top Fourier normalization. The squared curl of any potential at a frequency is at most `sigma` times its squared norm, by the displayed wedge identity. Therefore the constructed fine cochain `x` satisfies

    C_0 x=y,
    ||x||_X^2 <= R^4 (4/R^2)(32/R)^2 ||y||^2
                =4096||y||^2.                              (3.4)

If the chosen Nyquist representatives produce a complex lift of real data, take its real part; the constraint is real and the norm cannot increase. Minimality of `H_0y`, proved by orthogonality in `sp-decide.md:101–115`, now yields

    ||G_0^(-1/2)||<=64,       ||G_0^(-1)||<=4096.             (3.5)

These constants do not depend on `Q` or `L`.

**Discharged — a point evaluation inequality with no remembered Green bound.** Set `n=QR`, even as in the question. For a scalar mean-zero field `f` on this fine torus, Fourier expansion and Cauchy–Schwarz give

    |f(z)|^2 <= [n^(-4) sum_(k!=0) 1/sigma(2pi k/n)]
                           sum_(j=1)^4 ||Delta_j f||^2.     (3.6)

Choose integer representatives with `|k_j|<=n/2`. The sine bound above gives `sigma(2pi k/n)>=16|k|^2/n^2`. The number of integer points with `max|k_j|=r` is at most

    (2r+1)^4-(2r-1)^4=64r^3+16r<=80r^3.

Overcounting the boundary representatives is harmless. With `m=n/2`, the bracket in (3.6) is at most

    (1/(16n^2)) sum_(r=1)^m 80r
       = (5/(2n^2))m(m+1) <= 5/4,                         (3.7)

because `m+1<=n`. This is a displayed shell estimate, not an enumeration by computer.

**Discharged — the unperturbed minimizer spreads.** Identify `X` isometrically with the subspace `E=image d_f` of fine two-forms, and let `P_E` be its orthogonal projection. Then

    d_f H_0y=P_E B_R^*lambda,          lambda=G_0^(-1)y.

Translations preserve `E` and its orthogonal complement, so `P_E` commutes with each difference; its norm is at most one. Each component of an exact two-form has mean zero by telescoping. Apply (3.6)–(3.7) to its component at `p`, and (3.2) to the sum of its four differences. This gives, for *every* fine plaquette `p`,

    |ell_p H_0y| <= (2 sqrt(5)/sqrt(R)) ||lambda||
                 <= (5/L)||lambda||
                 <= (20480/L)||y||.                        (3.8)

The decay is deliberately weak but proved: `R=L^2`, so the two averages give the factor `1/L`. The projection onto exact curls has been included; treating `B_R^*lambda` itself as the minimizer would omit it.

**Discharged — the resulting explicit analytic test.** Let `delta=||D||_(X->Y)` for the literal perturbation (2.2), and set `e=64 delta`. If `e<=1/4`, (2.5), (2.6), (3.5) and `||ell_p||<=1` imply

    sqrt(max(0,<y,(G_0^(-1)-G_h^(-1))y>))
                                            <=64 sqrt(2e)||y||,
    |ell_p H_hy| <= [20480/L+384e]||y||.                    (3.9)

Every constant in these two bounds is explicit. The first bounds the signed lowering of the minimum, including relaxation of every excluded-line variable; the second bounds the actual changed minimizer, rather than the prescribed face field.

## 4. A local, purely geometric estimate for the perturbation

**Discharged — filling the actual row currents without an exterior theorem.** For each `c`, use the rectangular fine patch consisting of its two adjacent blocks and their crossing bonds. Its coordinate sides have length at most `2L`; all actual internal contours stay in their respective blocks. No patch wraps, by the choice of `Q`. Fix its lower corner `o` and the coordinate order `1,2,3,4`. Let `pi_z` be the axis-ordered path from `o` to `z`. For a positive bond `b=(z,z+e_j)`, commute the final `j`-step in `pi_z+b` left past the later coordinate steps to obtain `pi_(z+e_j)`. Each interchange contributes one signed plaquette. This constructs a two-chain `F_b` with

    boundary F_b = pi_z+b-pi_(z+e_j),       ||F_b||_1<=8L.

Use the opposite chain on a negative bond. The bound follows because at most four coordinate runs, each of length at most `2L`, are crossed. Everything stays inside the rectangle. For the *actual* closed current (2.1), put

    K_c=sum_b R(c)(b) F_b.                                 (4.1)

The `pi_z` terms cancel since the current is closed. Therefore `boundary K_c=R(c)`, and finite summation by parts gives

    R(c)x=<K_c,d_f x>,          ||K_c||_2<=8L||R(c)||_1.     (4.2)

The fill may cross the hull. This is legitimate here because `X` charges all fine plaquettes, including hull plaquettes; none has been deleted from the quadratic energy. Thus the deformation theorem left open at `relative-density-bound.md:365` is not an input to (4.2).

**Discharged — constants from the excluded fraction and the row structure.** Let `ell_c` be an upper bound for the length of every full current path `J_cx`, including the unused-endpoint extensions in Section 2. For a path average use its expected absolute length. Equation (2.1) gives

    ||R(c)||_1<=2q_c ell_c,
    ||K_c||_2<=16L q_c ell_c.                              (4.3)

No factor `1/(1-q_c)` was suppressed: it is already accounted for by writing a difference of two *normalized* means multiplied by `q_c`. This requires nonempty survivors, as the question does.

For an explicit overlap bound, enlarge each rectangle to `P_c=y+[-2L,2L]^4`, including its plaquettes, and define the single geometric number

    k_h := max_(fine plaquettes p)
                    [sum_(c:p in P_c) ||K_c||_2^2]^(1/2).  (4.4)

Cauchy–Schwarz in (4.2), followed by exchanging the two finite sums, proves `||R x||_2<=k_h||x||_X`. A fixed fine plaquette can belong to at most `4*6^4=5184` of these enlarged patches: for each of four bond directions, each coordinate of the block centre has at most six possible multiples of `L` (the interval has length at most `4L+2`, and `L>=3`). Thus, if `q=max q_c` and `ell=max ell_c`,

    k_h <=72*(16Lq ell)=1152Lq ell.                         (4.5)

The first-step row count, however numerous the holes, introduces no volume factor.

**Discharged — the second average supplies another inverse power of L.** A row of the positive path map `Q_path^(1)` has sum `L`. A first-level fine bond has total coefficient `L^(-3)` over all rows: fixing its direction, its longitudinal line positions number `L`, and its transverse starting block is unique. The row/column Cauchy–Schwarz argument used in Section 3 gives `||Q_path^(1)||<=1/L`. Each plaquette curl has four terms, and each bond is in six plaquettes, so `||d_t||<=sqrt(24)<5`. Together with (2.2) this proves

    delta=||D||<=5k_h/L,          e=64delta<=320k_h/L.       (4.6)

This is an energy-norm perturbation bound for the actual excluded-line maps. It is not an estimate on a frozen face ansatz.

**Implication — what the advertised short-path sparse geometry would give.** Suppose, for the literal hull, each row's excluded set is caused by a cover of at most `m` cubes of side at most `S`, and the actual full paths and the unused-endpoint extensions have length at most `5L`. A cube hits at most `2L(S+2)^3` starting lines: there are at most `(S+2)^3` transverse positions and at most `2L` longitudinal starts. The union bound, which does not replace the actual excluded subsets by complete columns, gives

    a=max(1,2m(S+2)^3),       q<=a/L^3,
    k_h<=5760a/L,
    delta<=28800a/L^2,        e<=1843200a/L^2.               (4.7)

This implication holds for every placement and shape covered by those hypotheses, with the very same constants. It does not prove those hypotheses for the literal singleton hull or for every sparse core. In particular a bound on the number of core cells is not a proof of a cube-cover bound for its filled hull and collar, and the existence of a short detour does not prove that the actual routing uses that detour.

## 5. An explicit reserve from one geometric inequality

**Discharged — size of the endpoint vector.** For `L>=9`, `v/T<9/8`, `rho>=2`, the second-average estimate in Section 4 and `||d_t e_*||=sqrt(6)<3` give

    ||y_*|| <=45/(8L)+3rho/4 <=rho+1=:M.                  (5.1)

The count six is the six unoriented plaquettes incident to one bond on this nondegenerate torus. The same bound holds at every centre `W(w)`, `0<=w<=T_2/4`, because its coefficient of `e_*` lies between zero and `rho/4`.

**Implication — a sufficient numerical budget, with no unspecified constant.** For `M>=1`, the following choices imply (QR) for every `y` with `||y||<=M` and every fine `p`:

    L>=2^18 M,
    k_h <= L/[5*2^23 M^2].                                (DC)

Indeed (4.6) gives `e<=2^(-17)M^(-2)<1/4`. The first row term in (3.9) is at most `5/64`; its perturbation term is at most `3/(1024M)<=3/1024`; and the square-root term is at most `1/4`. Hence

    |ell_p H_hy|+sqrt(max(0,<y,(G_0^(-1)-G_h^(-1))y>))
                         <=339/1024<1/2.                   (5.2)

All three contributions have been paid. With the geometric hypotheses of (4.7), one explicit sufficient scale is `L>=2^19 M sqrt(a)`: its square is at least `2^38 M^2 a`, and `1843200<2^21`, proving the required bound on `e`. This is an additional, displayed scale condition, not an assertion that an already fixed admitted `L` meets it.

**Implication — extent of the quadratic window/core result.** In commuting dimensionless bond coordinates, a point of a window differs from its centre by a field `eta` with `|eta_b|<1/100`. There are `4Q^4` positive top bonds, so `||eta||<Q^2/50` and `||d_t eta||<Q^2/10`. Thus replace `M` in (DC) by

    M_win=rho+1+Q^2/10.                                    (5.3)

Equations (3.9) and (5.2) then cover the *entire linear window*, all `w` in the stated interval, every fine plaquette, and every non-Haar sparse pattern whose actual currents satisfy the same (DC). The estimates also apply componentwise to the three-colour tangent problem with the Euclidean colour norm. Under (4.7), `L>=2^19 M_win sqrt(a)` is sufficient for all those patterns. The constants in (3.9)–(4.7) are free of `L` and `Q`; (5.3) retains a global `Q^2` cost. Consequently this argument alone does not supply a volume-uniform local S1 estimate. It also does not turn a nonlinear group-distance window into a linear window without a chart comparison.

## 6. What a Wilson passage would have to prove

**Discharged — the scalar action remainder, at its actual scope.** For commuting plaquette angles `t_p` with `|t_p|<=r<=1`, twice integrating the elementary inequalities for sine used in `sp-decide.md:30–34` gives

    0 <= (1/2)sum_p t_p^2 - sum_p(1-cos t_p)
                                      <= (r^2/24)sum_p t_p^2. (6.1)

If `t_p=T(d_f x)_p` and `E(x)=||x||_X^2/2<=E_max`, the error divided by `T^2` is at most `r^2 E_max/12`. For example `r^2<=3/(16E_max)` makes it at most `1/64`. This is an aggregate bound, not an error per plaquette that can be summed without paying volume. In SU(2), (6.1) still concerns the *actual* plaquette angles, but their identification with `T d_f x` is a separate assertion and is not made here.

**Implication — a sufficient chart comparison with a specified reserve.** Suppose a low-action numerator history `U` in an actual window has a cochain `x`, a top curl `y` covered by (QR), and a marked fine plaquette `p`, such that

    C_h x=y,       |ell_p x|>=15/16,
    A(U)/T^2 >= E(x)-1/64.                                  (6.2)

Suppose also there is an all-zero-label reference history `U_0`, in the *same window*, with fine and intermediate flux strictly below `T`, top strictly inside the window, and

    A(U_0)/T^2 <= (1/2)<y,G_0^(-1)y>+1/64.                  (6.3)

Write `alpha=|ell_p H_h y|` and `beta^2=max(0,<y,(G_0^(-1)-G_h^(-1))y>)`. The orthogonal constrained-minimum calculation of `sp-decide.md:115–134` now gives

    E(x)-(1/2)<y,G_0^(-1)y>
       >= [(15/16-alpha)^2-beta^2]/2
       >= 161/512-7alpha/16
       >=49/512>=3/32,                                     (6.4)

using `alpha+beta<=1/2` and `r_p<=1`. The case `r_p=0` is either impossible here or obeys the same lower bound. Subtract (6.3) from (6.2): the two errors total `1/32`, leaving

    A(U)-A(U_0)>=T^2/16.                                   (6.5)

Strictly subthreshold reference flux and an interior top give positive base-measure neighbourhoods of `U_0` with arbitrarily close action, by continuity and the finite-product Haar argument in `sp-decide.md`, Section 2. Thus `a_all(w)<=A(U_0)`, even though `U_0` can depend on `U`. Applying (6.5) to every numerator history in the low-action range would give the same essential action separation there. Remaining histories need a separate absolute-action lower bound above this comparison; they cannot simply be discarded.

**Gap — these are the nonlinear obligations, not a Wilson proof.** One must construct a common gauge/logarithm chart covering the relevant low-action histories and their corrections, with the literal pattern map, compatible torus periods and actual window. One must transfer the nonlinear constraint to `C_hx=y` with the *total* energy error `1/64` in (6.2); prove the hole-to-curl error at most `1/16`; control all commutator and relative-log errors in summed norms; and construct the positively supported all-lines comparison (6.3). A stationary solution in a previously supplied class ball does not provide these assertions for every numerator. Arbitrarily rough branches outside that chart, positive support, the non-quiet predicate and the joint retained-hole comparison remain separate. No part of (6.1) supplies the missing chart or allows an absolute action error proportional to the number of fine plaquettes to be ignored.

## 7. The decision and one smaller remaining inequality

**Gap — literal geometric input not established.** The required passages give two descriptions whose identification cannot be assumed: mapped `606–614` specifies pocket filling, spanning 3-chains and a collar; `relative-density-bound.md:327` describes merged bounding cubes and contours routed around them. `rarity-repair.md:35–59` explicitly makes its complete-column singleton construction conditional on the *actual* hull. Neither that conditional face construction nor a convenient cubical detour fixes the actual excluded starting points and routed currents used in (2.1). The sentence about paths of length `5L` at `relative-density-bound.md:365` is not accompanied there by a construction verifying that length for the actual routing, including its stems and collar. The deformation argument in that passage does not verify the length. The cited class-contour estimates in `c0-loc.md:1245–1251` expressly exclude rough rerouted contours. I have not established (4.7), or (DC), for the literal singleton. Treating their intended geometry as an extra hypothesis gives (5.2), not a decision of the literal (QR).

**Gap — the single residual current inequality, with all inverse analysis removed.** Fix the singleton and its actual current family of (2.1), use axis-ordered paths only for unused endpoints, and take `M=rho+1`, `L>=2^18 M`. The one next inequality is

    max_(c:D_c nonempty)
      || q_c [avg_(R_c) J_cx - avg_(D_c) J_cx] ||_1
                       <= 1/[45*2^29 M^2].                 (RC)

The norm is the sum of absolute coefficients of a fine-bond current after cancellation; it is not a supremum over fields. An empty maximum is zero.

**Discharged — sufficiency and reduction.** Equations (4.2), (4.4) and the overlap count give `k_h<=576 L` times this maximum. Hence `e<=184320` times this maximum; since `184320=45*2^12`, (RC) gives `e<=2^(-17)M^(-2)`. The reserve (5.2) follows with no further analytic input.

This is strictly smaller *as a proof obligation*: it concerns only finitely many first-step currents meeting one literal hull, with no fine field, Green inverse, constrained minimization, fine-plaquette evaluation, second-step geometry, top-window optimization or Wilson action. Its only quantities are exact excluded-line fractions and finite path-current coefficients. All the inverse and spreading estimates needed to convert it to (QR) have now been proved in Sections 2–5. It is a stronger sufficient geometric condition, not a necessary consequence of (QR); failing this deliberately conservative norm bound would not refute (QR). For scales below the displayed lower bound the estimates do not decide (QR), and I do not change an already fixed scale without saying so.

**Unchecked — expectation and the specific reason.** I expect (RC) to hold after sufficiently large admitted scale choices if the literal routing has the intended bounded detours.
