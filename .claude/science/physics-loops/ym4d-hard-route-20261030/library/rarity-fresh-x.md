## 2. One complete proposed route: positive component deletion

Components are bookkeeping for the actual predicates, not regions in which raw fine action is capped. All finer fluctuations are integrated before comparing their weights. This is preferable here to a new chart-tail estimate because it addresses the missing full numerator; preferable to the rejected reflection argument because it uses only positivity; and preferable to uniform one-link repair because it can pay for the surrounding rough component. No cluster-expansion theorem from memory is invoked.

**Discharged — exact finite partition, conditional only on the consumer's predicates being defined.** Fix a compatible finite torus, depth and one of these ensembles: a class-top history, its admitted quiet-boundary version, or the full mu hierarchy. Include the actual auxiliary labels in the sample space. At each level below the prescribed top put a type-I vertex for each brick, active when that brick is non-quiet, and a type-II vertex for each cell, active when the source's R test holds (labelled or above 2T, with its stated Haar charge). For an active vertex set D write

    W_x(D)=integral 1_{active set exactly D} WW,
    Z_x=sum_D W_x(D).

This is a finite positive partition. It leaves rough intermediate parents, all unmarked regions, all label branches and all fine coordinates integrated. Conditional densities at an exact top are used only when defined with their full fibre measure; finite-window measures give a version not requiring that extra density assertion. **Unchecked — geometry to be supplied on the admitted domain.** Connect tests whose full read patches interact at the same or adjacent levels, including the parent patch in relative quiet and the one-level Haar charges. With the fixed finite-factor/read ranges, a graph containing these interactions can have degree bounded by D_0(Theta), independently of depth and volume: a fixed patch meets finitely many cells at its level and at most a fixed multiple of L^4 cells one level down. Long-range dependence *between* components is allowed in their masses; no factorization or independence of those masses is asserted. In particular quiet columns are integrated, not added as paid vertices at every level.

**Gap — the hardest and first proposed estimate (CD).** Let gamma be a connected component of D. For every residual pattern D\gamma, in every one of the ensembles just specified, require

This uniform comparison is a deliberately stronger sufficient producer than RAR; a failure to prove it is not a refutation of RAR. It is an integrated analogue of local activity control, and does not assert an injective configuration repair.

For the chosen proof method I seek this comparison uniformly for all sufficiently large bare couplings at fixed finite geometry, with the bounded-step effective couplings. This additional producer uniformity makes the zero-temperature test below applicable. ## 3. Attack on the full marginal comparison

**Discharged — integrate the fine fields without changing the reference measure.** The actual kernel in mapped Text 5.2 is

    K(V,dV')=sum_labels P(labels|V)
                 product_{parents} [delta_{M_labels(V)} or Haar].

Its thresholds, label probabilities and maps have no bare-coupling dependence. At fixed finite depth let nu be the augmented history law with fine Haar input and these Markov kernels. It is a probability measure. The Wilson history law is exactly proportional to `exp(-t A(U)) dnu`, t=x_0. This statement does not identify the parent marginal with Haar. For a conditional history, the same statement holds on a supplied disintegration nu_W with its *own* parent marginal: weighting the defining disintegration identity by exp(-tA) proves it. Such an identity is only almost everywhere until a pointwise fibre version is supplied. The boundary version needs a finite nonzero base fibre measure with the same data. Neither the Markov property nor this calculation proves that version for every datum or proves FILL.

**Discharged — exact zero-temperature test for a component comparison.** Take any such fixed finite, x-independent base measure and two sectors D,D_0 of positive measure. Define essential infima, with respect to their restricted base measures,

    m(D)=ess inf_{active set D} A(U),
    W_t(D)=integral_{active set D} exp(-tA(U)) dnu.

For every epsilon>0 the set `A<m(D)+epsilon` in that sector has a positive measure b_epsilon, by the definition of essential infimum. Therefore

    b_epsilon exp[-t(m(D)+epsilon)]
       <= W_t(D) <= nu(D) exp[-t m(D)].

Taking logarithms, dividing by -t, and then letting t tend to infinity and epsilon to zero proves

    lim_{t->infinity} -log W_t(D)/t = m(D).             (3.1)

No saddle expansion, attainment, dimension count or continuity of a coarea density was used. Apply (3.1) to the proposed arbitrarily-large-coupling version of (CD), with D_0=D\gamma. If the bounded-step flow gives `x_l(t)=t+O_(K,Theta)(1)` at this *fixed* depth, its necessary consequence is

    m(D)-m(D\gamma)
      >= a_I eps_q^2 T^2 |gamma_I|+a_II T^2 |gamma_II|. (SEP)

Without that flow hypothesis the right side is instead the liminf of `sum_gamma h(v)/t`. A factor `C(geometry) t^p` in (CD) does not alter (SEP), since its logarithm divided by t vanishes. If the deleted sector has zero measure and the original has positive measure, (CD) already fails before this limit. The argument concerns essential support, not isolated configurations of zero fibre measure.

**Discharged — the exact two-sided comparison still owed at finite coupling.** Define `J_t(D)=integral_D exp[-t(A-m(D))] dnu`. Factoring out exp[-t m(D)] from both sector integrals gives

    -log[W_t(D)/W_t(D\gamma)]
      =t[m(D)-m(D\gamma)]-log[J_t(D)/J_t(D\gamma)].   (3.1a)

The proposed route must bound this entire right side below by `sum_gamma h(v)`. A gap at fixed depth does not bound the second term uniformly as depth and volume grow. In particular the conversion from the bare t to the own-level x_l, and the cancellation of all remote fine fluctuation factors, still have to occur in this relative comparison. Equation (3.1) is a necessary test of the proposal, not a substitute for (3.1a).

**Implication — the same test for a full-law marked estimate, without the stronger deletion premise.** If such an estimate is available for arbitrarily large t on the same fixed fibre, replacing D by the event that all prescribed marks hold and the denominator by the entire fibre gives

    m(all prescribed marks)-m(all histories)
                              >= limiting claimed mark rate. (3.2)

Thus a proof with this large-coupling uniformity cannot bypass *all* variational comparisons merely by integrating fine fluctuations first. Equation (SEP) is stronger than (3.2), because it fixes the residual defect pattern. Neither assertion is the printed all-chain AE-R: that uses a specified class reference, every consumed R cell and pointwise chains, rather than essential minima of sectors. **Discharged — where the proposed marginal-convexity shortcut breaks algebraically.** On a fixed coordinate domain on which differentiation under the integral is justified, write the full finer integral as

    r_t(v)=integral exp[-S_t(u,v)] du,  F_t(v)=-log r_t(v),
    S_t=t A-log J.

The quotient rule gives `d E[f]/dv=E[f_v]-Cov(f,S_v)`: differentiate numerator and denominator, retaining their common denominator. Applying this first to log r and then to f=S_v gives

    F_t''(v)=E[S_vv]-Var(S_v).                         (3.3)

With several branch domains the formula applies to their sum when they have common fixed coordinates; moving domains require additional boundary terms, not their omission. The negative variance term is part of integrating the finer fields. A bound on the inverse Hessian around each admitted stationary fill does not bound this full-mixture variance.

**Discharged — a hand counterexample to that inference, not to SU(2) rarity.** For two branches with positive unit reference weights, take

    r_t(v)=exp[-t(v-a)^2/2]+exp[-t(v+a)^2/2],  a>0.

    F_t(v)=t(v^2+a^2)/2-log[2 cosh(tav)],

So even globally positive Hessians on *each* of two branches do not imply convexity of their full marginal. At a point where one branch is taken as the reference, declaring the other exponentially small is precisely an additional mass comparison. This refutes the generic branchwise-Hessian inference; the standing estimates would need an additional Wilson-specific argument to justify the shortcut. ## 4. The entire probability conclusion if the comparison were proved

**Implication — a finite positive component lemma.** Let G be any finite graph of degree at most d>=1. Let W(D)>=0 be weights on all vertex subsets with `0<Z=sum_D W(D)<infinity`. Suppose (CD) holds for every component gamma of D, with `h(v)>=H` and

    H >= 2 log(d^2+1).                                 (4.1)

Then for every prescribed vertex set M,

    Z^(-1) sum_(D containing M) W(D)
                         <= exp[-(1/2) sum_(v in M) h(v)]. (4.2)

**Discharged — proof of the implication, cancellation first.** If M is empty both sides are one. Otherwise, for a D containing M, let Gamma be the union of its components that meet M. Delete those components successively. Deleting one does not join or split the others, so (CD) gives

    W(D)<=exp[-sum_Gamma h] W(D\Gamma).

For a fixed Gamma, the map `D -> D\Gamma` is injective. Its image is a subfamily of all vertex subsets, hence the sum of the image weights is at most Z. Summing over Gamma and cancelling Z gives

    P(M subset D) <= sum_Gamma exp[-sum_Gamma h],        (4.3)

where Gamma contains M and every component of Gamma meets M. All remote components stayed in this denominator. No estimate of their number or total mass was made. Since h>=H,

    sum_Gamma h >= (1/2)sum_M h+(H/2)|Gamma|.

It remains to bound the sum of `exp[-H|Gamma|/2]` by one.

**Discharged — the count, including several marks in one component.** Fix an order of M and of every neighbour list. For each Gamma choose a forest rooted at *every* vertex of M: each other vertex takes the first neighbour one unit closer to M in the induced graph on Gamma. Distances strictly decrease along parent edges, so this is a forest spanning Gamma with exactly |M| rooted trees. Order its children by the fixed lists and traverse each tree depth first. A tree with n_j vertices gives a walk of length `2(n_j-1)` starting at its specified root, with at most `d^(2(n_j-1))` possible neighbour strings. These walks recover their visited vertices and hence Gamma, so they give an injective encoding after the tree sizes are recorded. For `a=exp(-H/2)` the weighted count is consequently bounded by

    sum_(n_1,...,n_|M| >=1) product_j a^(n_j) d^(2(n_j-1))
           = [a/(1-d^2 a)]^|M| <=1.                    (4.4)

The equality is the product of geometric series, obtained by writing n_j=1+k_j and summing `(d^2a)^k_j`; (4.1) gives convergence and the last inequality. This permits a single component to cover many prescribed marks, arbitrary nesting in level, and an entire connected torus. It counts only active vertices; there is no entropy for a mark's quiet ancestral column. Equations (4.3)–(4.4) prove (4.2).

**Implication — all requested probability scopes, without a smaller endpoint.** Apply this lemma to the Section 2 partition with d=D_0(Theta). It produces the mixed marked boundary product bound with rates `a_I x_l eps_q^2 T^2/2` and `a_II x_l T^2/2`, uniformly at any depth, provided (CD) holds in that ensemble. Restrict to the separated type-I marks at one level for RAR^1. Restrict to type-II marks at arbitrary prescribed levels for RAR^ch; since a nonregular cell belongs to R, its event is a subset of this marked event. Nothing in (4.2) assumes that intermediate parents are class or that marks form a tree. The same lemma for the *mu* weights includes type-II marks at K, rather than reducing them to an assumed regular top. The extra several-level type-I conclusion under mu would follow from this stronger premise but is not required by the target.

**Implication — constants and flow are not hidden in the count.** Under the consumer's supplied bound `x_l>=X/2+b'(K-l)`, choose, for example, positive slopes strictly below

    alpha <= min{a_I eps_q^2 T^2/(32w_0), a_II T^2/8},
    l_1   <= min{a_I eps_q^2 T^2 b'/(16w_0), a_II T^2 b'/4}.

Then the two rates in (4.2) are at least `8w_0 lambda_l` and `2lambda_l`. Increase X after these choices so

    (X/2) min{a_I eps_q^2 T^2,a_II T^2}
                                      >=2log(D_0^2+1).

This verifies the promised product form with coefficient one, not an accumulating factor `1+epsilon` per level. The constants can be translated into smaller producer c_S,c_T if necessary; this does not prove the source's energy constants. FLOW, positivity of b', the finite-degree domain, and (CD) are explicit premises, not additional standing results obtained here. Mixtures preserve the resulting common right side by integrating the inequalities.

**Gap — completion boundary.** Section 4 is a complete implication to the full probability contract, not a proof of its antecedent. It does not establish FILL, field-dependent derivatives of activities, the MD* boundary cross-ratio, the classical minimum normalization, or the regular package. The failure identified in Section 3 occurs before this counting argument can be applied to the Wilson hierarchy.
