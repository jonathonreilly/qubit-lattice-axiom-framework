## 2. Counterexample first (revised, row 143)

Pass/fail criteria fixed before the proof: a counterexample is an admitted datum of the reference problem (exterior data $\varepsilon$-regular with $\varepsilon\le C\varepsilon_k$, box data in the mild class) at which either (a) the axial-gauge Hessian of $f(V)=A(U_{k,X}(V))$ in the box variables has a direction with $h(w)\le o(1)\lVert w\rVert^2$ as the depth $k$, the exterior volume or the coupling vary with $M$ fixed, or (b) the minimum of $f$ over the class is not attained in its interior, or is attained twice. A failed attempt is not a proof; the proof is Theorem H and Theorem 2.4. Row 140's gate found that attempts 3 and 4 of the first version used the Landau response as the raw response; they are replaced below.

1. *A soft mode at flat data from the fine scales.* The freed Hessian at $\mathbf V=\mathbf 1$ is $\lVert dY\rVert^2$ on the Landau tangent, whose level-$k$ rows are the datum plus an exact coboundary (Lemma V$'$ at $U=\mathbf 1$). A soft mode would be a $w$ whose fine extensions can all be nearly curl-free. Excluded exactly: the curl of the linearised average is a positive average of the fine curl with $\lVert\cdot\rVert_{2\to2}\le1$ at every step (Lemma E), and the coboundary has zero curl, so $\lVert dY\rVert^2\ge\lVert d_kw^0\rVert^2$ at every depth, with no constant.
2. *A soft mode from gauge directions.* Gauge transformations at box sites are exact symmetries of $f$; the tree of $\delta_{\rm ax}$ with its external bond fixes them completely, with no residual constant gauge (Lemma G). A curl-free level-$k$ field vanishing on the tree and outside the box is zero (Lemma P). The gate's block-axial pure gauge (a coarse gauge at one site) is a gauge direction, so it is not a slice direction.
3. *Curved: the node gauge (the gate's objection, tried as a counterexample).* A datum direction concentrated on one variable bond has a Landau tangent whose node jet $\sigma$ is of order one, not small (T4, top-gauge member); its raw tangent is of order one at block crossings. Could the node gauge give a negative contribution of order one? In (5.1$'$) it enters in three ways only. (i) Its second jet $\tau$ enters through $\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_c(\delta_V\tau)_c)$, which is zero (Lemma WI). (ii) Its first jet enters the rows as the coboundary $\delta_V\sigma$ (5.5); in the local axial gauge the flat part $d\sigma'$ has zero curl, and the rest is $(1-\operatorname{Ad}V^g)\sigma'=O(\bar a)\lVert w\rVert_\infty$. (iii) It enters $\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cQ_c)$, quadratic in $(\sigma,w)$ and weighted by multipliers of size $C_{\rm M}r$. A counterexample therefore needs one of: a node jet growing with the depth (excluded by the depth-uniform top-gauge bound of 7.5, gate row 89); multipliers not $O(r)$ (excluded by Lemma M, which reads Lemma WI); or a level-$k$ row of the box whose datum value is far from $\mathbf 1$ in the axial gauge (excluded by Lemma N, $V^g=\mathbf 1+O(\bar a)$). The attempt fails at each of the three.
4. *A long-range mode through the exterior.* Freeing the box couples it to the whole of $X$ through the background. The exterior averages stay fixed, so both the Landau response and its node jet decay exponentially in the joined metric (T4, both members), including down the collar tower; the far exterior enters the Hessian only through sums that Lemma BC bounds without depth or volume.
5. *A minimiser on the boundary of the class.* This needs a gradient at the flat-cycle centre of the class of size comparable to $c(M)a_\Lambda$. The gradient there is the box multiplier, at most $C(M)\varepsilon$ (Lemma M, repaired by Lemma WI), while the class has fixed radius $a_\Lambda$. Excluded for $\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_*(M)$.

None fires. The obstruction would need curvature of the order of $a_\Lambda$, outside the admitted regime once $a_\Lambda$ is chosen after $M$, or a failure of one of the gated inputs T4 (both members), Lemma M or Lemma N.

## 3. The reference problem, the slice and the class

Level-$k$ units throughout: $\mathbb T^{(k)}$ has unit spacing, the bare lattice has spacing $\eta=L^{-k}$, a top $y$ has scale $\xi_y\le1$. On $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ (traceless Hermitian $2\times2$ matrices, also their complexification) $\lvert\cdot\rvert$ is the operator norm; for $Z=z\cdot\sigma$, $\lvert Z\rvert=\lvert z\rvert$ and $\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}Z^2=\lvert Z\rvert^2$. $a_p(U)=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}U_p$, $A=\sum_pa_p$.

**The problem.** $X$ is a component of the class at level $k$, $\Lambda^{\rm pr}$ its box (side $\le C_\Lambda M$), $\Lambda:=\Lambda^{\rm pr}\cap\mathbb T^{(k)}$ its sites. The localised problem of $X$ (SA 9786, Theorem 6(c)) has determining bonds: the level-$k$ bonds of $X$ off its collar, and the collar tower, whose data are the lift of the level-$k$ field (the value $\mathbf 1$ inside a block, $V(c)$ on a bond from the block of $c_-$ into that of $c_+$). Its datum is therefore a function of the level-$k$ field $V$ on $X$ alone. A lifted test loop has the holonomy of a level-$k$ plaquette or of a closed loop inside one block (value $\mathbf 1$), so its error is at most the largest level-$k$ plaquette error. Write $U_{k,X}(V)=\Phi(V)$ for its background (P81) and
\[
 f(V):=A(U_{k,X}(V)).
\]

**Variables, class.** $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$ is the set of level-$k$ bonds with an end point in $\Lambda$; $n_b:=\lvert\mathsf B_{\rm var}\rvert\le8(C_\Lambda M+1)^4$. $\Lambda^+$ is the set of level-$k$ sites within $\ell^\infty$-distance $1$ of $\Lambda$, and $S$ the set of level-$k$ plaquettes with all four corners in $\Lambda^+$. A plaquette containing a variable bond has a corner $x\in\Lambda$ and all its corners within $\ell^\infty$-distance $1$ of $x$, hence lies in $S$; every other test loop is a function of the exterior data alone. (SA 17656's "within distance $1$" is measured from the geometric box. *Row 143, after gate row 140, Section 3:* the first version claimed that a Euclidean reading leaves edge plaquettes unconstrained; that was wrong. The box $\Lambda^{\rm pr}$ is a union of centred half-open cubes (SA 5585-5596, enlarged in that partition, SA 17585; `geo-4.md` Section 1), so its faces lie halfway between adjacent level-$k$ sites. In each coordinate the sites of the box run from $a$ to $b$ and the carrier from $a-\frac12$ to $b+\frac12$: every site of $\Lambda^+$ is within $\frac12$ of the carrier in each coordinate, hence within Euclidean distance $\sqrt4/2=1$ of it, and every other site is at least $\frac32$ beyond a face. So the Euclidean site neighbourhood of the carrier is $\Lambda^+$, and the outline's class is the cubical class used here. If "within" is strict, the only sites lost are corners at distance exactly $1$, and plaquettes touching only such corners have no variable bond, so they are controlled by (H$_\varepsilon$) with $\varepsilon\le a$.) Hypothesis (H$_\varepsilon$): every level-$k$ plaquette with no variable bond has $\lvert V_p-1\rvert<\varepsilon$. The class $\mathcal K(a)$ is the set of $V$ with exterior data fixed and $\lvert V_p-1\rvert<a$ for $p\in S$. For a datum write $\alpha(V)$ for its datum norm (largest test-loop error); on $\mathcal K(a)$, $\alpha(V)<\max(a,\varepsilon)$ by the remark on lifts. Gauge transformations $u$ at the sites of $\Lambda$ act only on variable bonds, and $f(V^u)=f(V)$ by covariance of the average and gauge invariance of $A$.

**Trees.** $T$ is the tree of $\delta_{\rm ax}$ on $\Lambda$ and $e_0=(x_0,z_0)$ the external bond of $T_0$, $x_0\in\Lambda$, $z_0\in\Lambda^+\setminus\Lambda$. The shell $\Lambda^+\setminus\Lambda$ is the set of sites of the boundary of a lattice cube of side $\ge3$ in dimension four; it is connected through its own bonds, so it has a spanning tree $T_{\rm ext}$ of exterior bonds. $T^*:=T\cup\{e_0\}\cup T_{\rm ext}$ is a spanning tree of $\Lambda^+$ (two trees glued at the single site $z_0$). For a bond $b$ of $\Lambda^+$ not in $T^*$, $\gamma_b$ is its fundamental cycle in $T^*$: $b$ followed by the tree path back.

**Lemma G (slice).** Each gauge orbit of variable data meets $\Sigma:=\{V:V=\mathbf 1\text{ on }T\cup\{e_0\}\}$ in exactly one point, and a point of $\Sigma$ is critical for $f$ if and only if it is critical for $f|_\Sigma$.

*Proof.* $V^u(e_0)=u(x_0)V(e_0)$ ($z_0\notin\Lambda$ is not transformed), so $V^u(e_0)=\mathbf 1$ forces $u(x_0)=V(e_0)^{-1}$; along $T$ outward from $x_0$, $u(x)V(x,x')u(x')^{-1}=\mathbf 1$ forces $u(x')=u(x)V(x,x')$. Every site of $\Lambda$ is reached once, so $u$ exists and is unique. Linearising, the map from $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued functions on $\Lambda$ to variations on $T\cup\{e_0\}$ is triangular with invertible diagonal, so every tangent vector at $V\in\Sigma$ is a gauge direction plus a tangent vector of $\Sigma$. $f$ has zero derivative along gauge directions. $\square$

**Coordinates.** For $b\in\mathsf B_{\rm var}\setminus(T\cup\{e_0\})$ write $\gamma_b=b\cdot\pi_b$ and let $R_b$ be the transport of $V$ along $\pi_b$: it uses only bonds of $T\cup\{e_0\}$ (value $\mathbf 1$ on $\Sigma$) and of $T_{\rm ext}$ (exterior data), so it does not depend on the variables. The *centre* $V^{\rm c}\in\Sigma$ is $V^{\rm c}(b):=R_b^{-1}$, and every $V\in\Sigma$ is $V=e^{iu}V^{\rm c}$ with $u(b)=\tfrac1i\log(V(b)R_b)=\tfrac1i\log\operatorname{hol}_{\gamma_b}(V)$. Put $\varphi(u):=f(e^{iu}V^{\rm c})$.

**Surfaces.** Fix a corner $o$ of $\Lambda^+$ and, for a site $x$, the axial path $P(x)$ from $o$ (directions $1,2,3,4$ in turn); it stays in the cube $\Lambda^+$. For a bond $e=(v,v+e_\mu)$ the closed path $P(v)\,e\,P(v+e_\mu)^{-1}$ bounds a staircase strip of at most $3(C_\Lambda M+3)$ plaquettes of $\Lambda^+$, all in $S$: the two axial paths agree in the directions before $\mu$ and run at distance one in the directions after $\mu$. A closed path $\gamma$ of $\Lambda^+$ is the product of these triangles over its bonds, so it bounds a surface $\Sigma_\gamma$ of at most $\lvert\gamma\rvert\cdot3(C_\Lambda M+3)$ plaquettes of $S$, and its holonomy is an ordered product of conjugated plaquette holonomies of those plaquettes. Fundamental cycles have length $\le\lvert\Lambda^+\rvert+1$, so
\[
 \lvert\Sigma_{\gamma_b}\rvert\le A_M:=3(C_\Lambda M+3)\big((C_\Lambda M+3)^4+1\big).
\]
For unitary matrices $\lvert\prod_iW_i-1\rvert\le\sum_i\lvert W_i-1\rvert$, and conjugation preserves $\lvert W-1\rvert$.

**Lemma S (class and ball).** Let $\varepsilon\le a\le\tfrac1{8A_M}$. (i) If $V\in\Sigma\cap\mathcal K(a)$ then $\lvert u\rvert_\infty\le2A_Ma=:\rho_1(a)$. (ii) $\lvert V^{\rm c}_p-1\rvert\le C_{\rm c}(M)\varepsilon$ for $p\in S$, with $C_{\rm c}(M)=4C_{\rm sh}(M)$ and $C_{\rm sh}(M)$ the area constant of loops in the shell, defined in the proof. (iii) If $\lvert u\rvert_\infty\le\rho\le\tfrac14$ then $\alpha(e^{iu}V^{\rm c})\le C_{\rm c}(M)\varepsilon+8\rho$.

*Proof.* (i) $\lvert\operatorname{hol}_{\gamma_b}(V)-1\rvert\le\sum_{p\in\Sigma_{\gamma_b}}\lvert V_p-1\rvert<A_Ma\le\tfrac18$, and $\lvert\log W\rvert\le2\lvert W-1\rvert$ on that ball. (ii) Write $\partial p$ as a product of conjugated fundamental cycles of its bonds not in $T^*$, times a closed path in the tree $T^*$, which is trivial. Variable non-tree bonds contribute $\operatorname{hol}_{\gamma_b}(V^{\rm c})=\mathbf 1$. An exterior non-tree bond $e$ of the shell has $\gamma_e$ inside the shell (the tree path between two shell sites is in $T_{\rm ext}$), of length $\le\lvert\partial Q\rvert+1\le8(n+1)^3+1$, where $Q=\Lambda^+$ is a cube of side $n:=C_\Lambda M+3$ and $\partial Q=\Lambda^+\setminus\Lambda$. *Shell filling:* a closed path $\gamma$ of $\partial Q$ is a product of at most $40n(n+1)^2\lvert\gamma\rvert$ conjugated boundaries of plaquettes of $\partial Q$. Indeed: in a three-dimensional face the axial triangles give at most $2n$ plaquettes per bond. Let $R$ be $\partial Q$ minus the open top face $\{x_4=n,\ 0<x_1,x_2,x_3<n\}$; for $v\in R$ let $\delta(v)$ drop straight down to $x_4=0$ inside its side face. For a bond $e=(v,v')$ of $R$, $\delta(v)^{-1}e\,\delta(v')\,\bar e^{-1}$ ($\bar e$ the bond below at height $0$) is trivial if $e$ is vertical and bounds a vertical strip of $\le n$ plaquettes of one side face if $e$ is horizontal; so a loop in $R$ is filled by $\le n$ plaquettes per bond plus a filling of its projection in the bottom face, in all $\le3n\lvert\gamma\rvert$. A general loop is first moved into $R$: each excursion $\sigma$ through the open top face, between rim points $r,r'$, is replaced by a path $\tau$ in the rim (the boundary of a three-cube, $\le6(n+1)^2$ sites), at the cost of filling $\sigma\tau^{-1}$ in the closed top face; there are at most $\lvert\gamma\rvert/2$ excursions. Adding up gives the bound. All these plaquettes have no variable bond, so $\lvert\operatorname{hol}_{\gamma_e}-1\rvert\le C_{\rm sh}(M)\varepsilon$ with $C_{\rm sh}(M):=40n(n+1)^2(8(n+1)^3+1)$. A plaquette has at most four non-tree bonds. (iii) $\lvert e^{iu}-1\rvert\le2\lvert u\rvert$; each plaquette of $S$ has at most four variable bonds; the other test loops are exterior or lifts. $\square$

Thus $\Sigma\cap\mathcal K(a)\subset\{\lvert u\rvert_\infty<\rho_1(a)\}$, a convex set, and $\{\lvert u\rvert_\infty\le2\rho_1(a)\}$ consists of data with $\alpha\le C_{\rm c}(M)\varepsilon+32A_Ma$.

## 4. Two exact linear facts

**Lemma E (energy of the linearised averages, every depth).** Let $K$ be the derivative at $U=\mathbf 1$ of one step of the actual block map (QC3), on the unscaled bond logarithms of the finer lattice, and $K^{(k)}=K\circ\cdots\circ K$ ($k$ factors), the derivative of $\mathcal M^k$ at $\mathbf 1$. For a set $S'$ of level-$k$ plaquettes let $\mathcal N_0(S')$ be the bare plaquettes in its ancestor rectangles (the $L\times L$ squares of (4.0) below, iterated). Then for every $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued bare $1$-form $Y$,
\[
 \sum_{p'\in S'}\lvert d_k(K^{(k)}Y)(p')\rvert^2\le\sum_{p\in\mathcal N_0(S')}\lvert dY(p)\rvert^2 ,
 \tag{4.1}
\]
where $d$ is the oriented lattice curl $Y(b_1)+Y(b_2)-Y(b_3)-Y(b_4)$ on each lattice. No constant depends on $k$.

*Proof.* One step, at a positive coarse plaquette $p'=(y;\mu\nu)$ with bonds $c_1=(y,y+Le_\mu)$, $c_2=(y+Le_\mu,y+Le_\mu+Le_\nu)$, $c_3=(y+Le_\nu,y+Le_\nu+Le_\mu)$, $c_4=(y,y+Le_\nu)$. By (QC15) (SA 8177-8189: the derivative of the contour mean at the identity is the arithmetic mean, and the derivative of $U[c]$ cancels in each closed logarithm), $(Ka)(y,y')=\sigma_a(y)+L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(y)}a([x,x+Le_\mu])-\sigma_a(y')$. In $(Ka)(c_1)+(Ka)(c_2)-(Ka)(c_3)-(Ka)(c_4)$ the eight $\sigma$ terms cancel in pairs. Since $B(y+Le_\mu)=B(y)+Le_\mu$, the rest is $L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(y)}$ of the oriented sum of $a$ around the $L\times L$ square with corner $x$ in the $\mu\nu$ plane, which by the abelian Stokes identity is the sum of $da$ over its $L^2$ plaquettes, all of orientation $\mu\nu$. So
\[
 d(Ka)(p')=(P\,da)(p'),\qquad (Pf)(y;\mu\nu)=L^{-4}\sum_{x\in B(y)}\sum_{r,s=0}^{L-1}f(x+re_\mu+se_\nu;\mu\nu),
 \tag{4.0}
\]
the matrix of `sa-2.13-2.md` (6.2), now as an exact linear identity with nonnegative entries. Row sums are $L^2$. A fine plaquette $(z;\mu\nu)$ occurs for $L^2$ pairs $(r,s)$, each fixing $x$ and hence the unique block $B(y)\ni x$, with weight $L^{-4}$: column sums are $L^{-2}$. Hence $\lVert P\rVert_{2\to2}\le1$: by Cauchy-Schwarz in each row, $\lvert(Pf)_i\rvert^2\le(\sum_jP_{ij})\sum_jP_{ij}\lvert f_j\rvert^2\le L^2\sum_jP_{ij}\lvert f_j\rvert^2$, and summing over $i$ with column sums $L^{-2}$ gives $\lVert Pf\rVert^2\le\lVert f\rVert^2$; this holds for $\mathbb R^3$-valued $f$ since $\lvert Pf\rvert\le P\lvert f\rvert$ entrywise. Iterate $k$ times (the chain rule at the identity at every level) and restrict the rows to $S'$ and the columns to the entries those rows read. $\square$

This is the step `sa-2.13-2.md` Section 7 found missing between a tree curl inequality and the freed Hessian: the freed Hessian at flat data is a minimum of $\lVert dY\rVert^2$ over fine extensions of the box datum, and (4.1) bounds it below by the level-$k$ curl energy of that datum, with constant one at every depth.

**Lemma P (tree Poincaré inequality at level $k$).** Let $w$ be $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued on $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$ with $w=0$ on $T\cup\{e_0\}$, and $w^0$ the level-$k$ $1$-form on the bonds of $\Lambda^+$ equal to $w$ on variable bonds and $0$ on the others. Then
\[
 \lVert w\rVert_2^2\le C_{\rm P}(M)\sum_{p\in S}\lvert dw^0(p)\rvert^2,\qquad C_{\rm P}(M):=n_bA_M^2 .
 \tag{4.2}
\]

*Proof.* For a variable bond $b\notin T\cup\{e_0\}$, $w^0$ vanishes on every bond of $\gamma_b$ but $b$ (bonds of $T\cup\{e_0\}$ by hypothesis, bonds of $T_{\rm ext}$ because they are exterior). So $\pm w(b)$ is the oriented sum of $w^0$ around $\gamma_b$, which is the oriented sum of $dw^0$ over the $2$-chain $\Sigma_{\gamma_b}$ of Section 3 (its boundary is $\gamma_b$, by the telescoping of the axial triangles). With multiplicities, $\Sigma_{\gamma_b}$ has $N_b\le A_M$ plaquettes, so $\lvert w(b)\rvert^2\le A_M\sum_{p}m_b(p)\lvert dw^0(p)\rvert^2$, and $\sum_bm_b(p)\le n_bA_M$. $\square$

Balaban's constant for the axial tree is $CM^5$ (outline, SA 17656); (4.2) is cruder and suffices, since only its dependence on $M$ alone is used.

## 5. The freed problem: envelope, second variation, multipliers

Fix a real datum $V$ of the reference problem with $\alpha(V)<\bar\varepsilon$ and let $U=\Phi(V)$, real, regular, $r(U)\le B\alpha(V)$ (P81, (8.2)). For every determining bond $c$ (level-$k$ rows and tower rows) put
\[
 \tilde q_c(U'):=\tfrac1i\log\big[(M_{\mathbf B}U')(c)\,V(c)^{-1}\big],
\]
defined for $U'$ near $U$ (raw rows, left logarithms). Fine variations are left variations $e^{iY}U'$, $Y$ an unscaled bare $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued $1$-form; $Y=\eta h$ with $h$ the continuum potential. $\operatorname{Hess}A(U)[Y,Y]:=\frac{d^2}{ds^2}A(e^{isY}U)|_0$, and likewise $D^2\tilde q_c(U)[Y,Y]$.

**Lemma V (envelope and second variation).** (a) There is a unique family $\lambda=(\lambda_c)$, $\lambda_c\in\mathfrak{su}(2)$, with $DA(U)[Y]=\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_c\,D\tilde q_c(U)[Y])$ for every $Y$. (b) For $w$ on $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$, $\frac d{dt}f(e^{itw}V)|_0=\sum_{c\in\mathsf B_{\rm var}}\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cw_c)$. (c) With $Y_1:=\frac d{dt}\big|_0\tfrac1i\log\big(\Phi_V(tw)U^{-1}\big)$ (bond by bond),
\[
 h_V(w):=\frac{d^2}{dt^2}f(e^{itw}V)\Big|_0=\operatorname{Hess}A(U)[Y_1,Y_1]-\sum_c\operatorname{tr}\big(\lambda_c\,D^2\tilde q_c(U)[Y_1,Y_1]\big).
 \tag{5.1}
\]

*Proof.* P81 gives the holomorphic chart $\Phi_V$ on $\lVert B\rVert_\infty<r_{\rm B}$ with $M_{\mathbf B}\Phi_V(B)=e^{iB}V$, so $\tilde q\circ\Phi_V(B)=B$ for small $B$ and $D\tilde q(U)\circ D\Phi_V(0)=\mathrm{id}$: $D\tilde q(U)$ is onto, the fibre $\{\tilde q=0\}$ is a submanifold near $U$ with tangent space $\ker D\tilde q(U)$, and $U$ is critical on it (P81: the background is a critical configuration of the constrained problem). A linear form vanishing on the kernel of a surjection factors through it, uniquely: (a). For (b) and (c) let $U(t)=\Phi_V(tw)$ (data $e^{itw}V$ on $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$, $V$ elsewhere); by gauge invariance $f(e^{itw}V)=A(U(t))$. Write $U(t)=e^{iY(t)}U$, $Y$ holomorphic, $Y(0)=0$, $Y'(0)=Y_1$. Exactly, $\tilde q(U(t))=tw$ on $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$ and $0$ on the other rows. Differentiating $A(e^{iY(t)}U)$ once gives $DA(U)[Y_1]=\sum\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cD\tilde q_c[Y_1])=\sum_{\mathsf B_{\rm var}}\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cw_c)$. Differentiating twice gives $\operatorname{Hess}A(U)[Y_1,Y_1]+DA(U)[Y''(0)]$, and differentiating $\tilde q(e^{iY(t)}U)=tw$ twice gives $D^2\tilde q[Y_1,Y_1]+D\tilde q[Y''(0)]=0$; insert (a). $\square$

So the gradient of $f$ in the box variables is the box part of the multiplier, and the freed-average Hessian is the Lagrangian Hessian, with the full multiplier, evaluated on the **raw** tangent $Y_1$. At a critical point of $f$ the box multipliers vanish and only the exterior ones remain.

T4 bounds the Landau potential of `a3-derivative.md` Proposition 6.4, and $\Phi_V(B)=(e^{i\eta a(B)}U)^{u_B}$ carries a node gauge, so $Y_1=\eta h+X_\chi$ with $X_\chi(b)=\chi(b_-)-\operatorname{Ad}(U(b))\chi(b_+)$ and $\chi$ the bare-site derivative of $u_{tw}$, which is of order one at block crossings (the gate's block-axial example). Lemma V(c) is true for $Y_1$, but nothing bounds $Y_1$. From here on one tangent is used, the Landau tangent $\eta h$; the node gauge enters only through its first and second jets at the nodes.

**Setting for the Landau tangent.** Apply `a3-derivative.md` Sections 6-7 with reference $U_0:=U=\Phi(V)$ and datum $\mathbf V_0:=V$ ($U\in{\rm Reg}$ by `a3-close.md` (1)).  \tag{5.4}
\]
For a datum direction $b$ (a function on the rows) put $h_b:=\partial_ta(tb)|_0$ and $v_b(t):=v[a(tb)]$ at the tops. Then $v_b(0)=\mathbf 1$, and $v_b(t)\in{\rm SU}(2)$ for real $t$, so $v_b(t)(y)=\exp\big(i(t\sigma_b(y)+\tfrac{t^2}2\tau_b(y)+O(t^3))\big)$ with $\sigma_b,\tau_b$ $\mathfrak{su}(2)$-valued; since $u_{tb}=v_b(t)^{-1}$ at the tops, $\lvert\sigma_b(y)\rvert=\lvert\partial_tu_{tb}(y)|_0\rvert$.  \tag{T4}

The first is (34); the second is the top-gauge member of 7.5, $\lvert\dot u(y)\rvert\le Ce^{-\theta d_{\mathbf B}(y,y_c)}\lvert e\rvert$ (gate row 89: ADVANCE, with its product-rule check that the bound is uniform in depth, $\sum_{n<j}L^n\eta\le\xi_j/(L-1)$). $C_4$ is the larger of the two constants.

**Lemma WI (Ward identity).** For every $\mathfrak{su}(2)^{\mathbb C}$-valued function $\chi$ on the tops, $\sum_c\operatorname{tr}\big(\lambda_c(\delta_V\chi)_c\big)=0$, where $(\delta_V\chi)_c:=\chi(c_-)-\operatorname{Ad}(V(c))\chi(c_+)$.

*Proof.* Both sides are complex-linear in $\chi$, so take $\chi$ real, and extend it to the other bare sites arbitrarily. With $U^g(b)=g(b_-)U(b)g(b_+)^{-1}$, the curve $U^{e^{it\chi}}$ has left tangent $X_\chi(b)=\chi(b_-)-\operatorname{Ad}(U(b))\chi(b_+)$ at $t=0$. $A$ is a sum of plaquette functions, so $A(U^{e^{it\chi}})=A(U)$ and $DA(U)[X_\chi]=0$. The contour means are left and right equivariant (proof of `a3-derivative.md` Lemma 6.1), so $(M_{\mathbf B}U^g)(c)=g(c_-)(M_{\mathbf B}U)(c)g(c_+)^{-1}$; with $M_{\mathbf B}U=V$, $\tilde q_c(U^{e^{it\chi}})=\frac1i\log\big(e^{it\chi(c_-)}V(c)e^{-it\chi(c_+)}V(c)^{-1}\big)$, whose derivative at $0$ is $(\delta_V\chi)_c$. Apply Lemma V(a) with $Y=X_\chi$. $\square$

**Lemma V$'$ (second variation along the Landau curve).** For every datum direction $b$ and every row $c$,
\[
 D\tilde q_c(U)[\eta h_b]=b_c+(\delta_V\sigma_b)_c .
 \tag{5.5}
\]
For $w$ on $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$ (extended by $0$ to the other rows), with $h:=h_w=\sum_{c\in\mathsf B_{\rm var}}h_{c,w_c}$ and $\sigma:=\sigma_w=\sum_{c\in\mathsf B_{\rm var}}\sigma_{c,w_c}$,
\[
 h_V(w)=\operatorname{Hess}A(U)[\eta h,\eta h]-\sum_c\operatorname{tr}\big(\lambda_c\,D^2\tilde q_c(U)[\eta h,\eta h]\big)+\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cQ_c),
 \tag{5.1$'$}
\]
\[
 Q_c:=i\Big([\sigma(c_-),w_c]-[\sigma(c_-),\operatorname{Ad}(V(c))\sigma(c_+)]-[w_c,\operatorname{Ad}(V(c))\sigma(c_+)]\Big).
\]

*Proof.* $u_{tw}$ is a gauge transformation of the bare lattice, so $f(e^{itw}V)=A(\Phi_V(tw))=A(U_L(t))$ with $U_L(t):=e^{i\eta a(tw)}U$. Put $Z(t):=\eta a(tw)$, so $Z(0)=0$, $Z'(0)=\eta h$, $Z''(0)=:\eta k$. The maps $Z\mapsto A(e^{iZ}U)$ and $Z\mapsto\tilde q_c(e^{iZ}U)$ are holomorphic near $0$ in the bare $1$-form $Z$, with second differentials $\operatorname{Hess}A(U)$ and $D^2\tilde q_c(U)$ as defined above. So $h_V(w)=\operatorname{Hess}A(U)[\eta h,\eta h]+DA(U)[\eta k]$, and $DA(U)[\eta k]=\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cD\tilde q_c(U)[\eta k])$ by Lemma V(a). By (5.4) with $B=tb$, $\tilde q_c(e^{i\eta a(tb)}U)=q^{\rm curve}_c(t):=\frac1i\log\big(v_b(t)(c_-)\,e^{itb_c}\,V(c)v_b(t)(c_+)^{-1}V(c)^{-1}\big)$. Write $v_b(t)(c_-)=e^{i\mathsf A(t)}$ and $V(c)v_b(t)(c_+)^{-1}V(c)^{-1}=e^{-i\mathsf B(t)}$, so that $\mathsf A=t\sigma_b(c_-)+\frac{t^2}2\tau_b(c_-)+O(t^3)$ and $\mathsf B=\operatorname{Ad}(V(c))\big(t\sigma_b(c_+)+\frac{t^2}2\tau_b(c_+)\big)+O(t^3)$. The Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula to second order, $\log(e^Xe^Ye^Z)=X+Y+Z+\frac12([X,Y]+[X,Z]+[Y,Z])+O(3)$, gives
\[
 q^{\rm curve}_c(t)=\mathsf A+tb_c-\mathsf B+\tfrac i2\big([\mathsf A,tb_c]-[\mathsf A,\mathsf B]-[tb_c,\mathsf B]\big)+O(t^3).
\]
Its first derivative at $0$ is (5.5). For $b=w$ its second derivative is $(\delta_V\tau_w)_c+Q_c$, and differentiating $\tilde q_c(e^{iZ(t)}U)=q^{\rm curve}_c(t)$ twice gives $D^2\tilde q_c(U)[\eta h,\eta h]+D\tilde q_c(U)[\eta k]=(\delta_V\tau_w)_c+Q_c$. Insert this; Lemma WI removes $\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_c(\delta_V\tau_w)_c)$. $\square$

This carries out the gate's "keep the node jets" (row 140, Section 6). The second jet $\tau$ of the node factors drops out exactly, by the Ward identity. What remains of the node gauge is quadratic in the first jet $\sigma$ and in $w$, and it is weighted by the multipliers, which are $O(r)$. Lemma V(c) stays a true identity for the raw tangent; nothing below reads it.

**Lemma BC$'$ (single-top count).** Under GEO4 ($\operatorname{dist}(\Omega_j^c,\Omega_{j+1})\ge c_g\xi_j$, $64\log L\le\theta\min(M,\rho)\le\theta c_g$), for every top $y_c$ of the reference problem,
\[
 \sum_{\text{tops }y}e^{-\theta d_{\mathbf B}(y,y_c)}\le C_{\rm BC'}:=\sum_{R\ge0}\Big(\tfrac{2(R+1)}{c_g}+3\Big)\Big(2(R+1)L^{2(R+1)/c_g+2}+3\Big)^4e^{-\theta R}<\infty .
\]

*Proof.* A path of $d_{\mathbf B}$-length $<R+1$ from $y_c$ (level $q$) reaches only levels $j$ with $\lvert j-q\rvert\le(R+1)/c_g+1$: between levels it crosses each intermediate layer, at cost $\ge c_g$ (step 1 of the proof of Lemma BC). Its Euclidean length is at most $R+1$ times the largest scale met, $\le\xi_qL^{(R+1)/c_g+1}$. Level-$j$ tops form a lattice of spacing $\xi_j\ge\xi_qL^{-\lvert j-q\rvert}$, so at most $(2(R+1)L^{2(R+1)/c_g+2}+3)^4$ of them lie in that Euclidean ball, for each of at most $2(R+1)/c_g+3$ levels. Sum over $R$: the $R$-th term is at most a polynomial in $R$ times $L^{8(R+1)/c_g}e^{-\theta R}$, and $8\log L/c_g\le\theta/8$ by $64\log L\le\theta c_g$, so the series converges. $\square$

For a top in $\Omega_k$ this is also Lemma BC with $E=\{y_c\}$.

**Lemma M (multipliers).** For every determining bond $c$, $\lvert\lambda_c\rvert\le C_{\rm M}\,r(U)$ with $C_{\rm M}:=4L^3C_4C_{\rm BC'}$.

*Proof (row 143: Ward repair, as in gate row 140, Section 5).* For $\lvert e\rvert=1$ at $c$, Lemma V(a) with $Y=\eta h_{c,e}$, the Landau response to the datum direction $e$ at $c$, and (5.5) give $\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_ce)+\sum_{c'}\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_{c'}(\delta_V\sigma_{c,e})_{c'})=DA(U)[\eta h_{c,e}]$, and the sum vanishes by Lemma WI. The cancellation that makes this volume- and depth-free is in $J$: the force on a bare bond is $\eta^3J$, of order $\eta^3r(U)$, not the $\eta^2r(U)$ of a single plaquette. **Lemma R (rows).** Let $c$ be a determining bond of scale $s$ with carrier $S_c$, and $U'$ a real reference with $r(U')\le\bar\varepsilon$ (or $U'=\mathbf 1$). The map $a\mapsto\tilde q_c(e^{i\eta a}U')-\tilde q_c(U')$ (complex $a$) is holomorphic on $\{s\sup_{S_c}\lvert a\rvert<r_*\}$ and bounded there by a constant $C_{\rm R}(L)$.  \tag{5.2}
\]
with $C'_{\rm R}=C'_{\rm R}(L)$; both bounds are homogeneous in $h$ (of degree $2$ and $1$), so they hold for every $h$.

*Proof.* The row is the $k$-step recursion (6)-(8) of `a3-derivative.md` 4.1 with its accumulated endpoint factors; under $s\sup\lvert a\rvert\le r_*$ the recursion stays in its fixed analytic ball ($\lvert a_r\rvert\le2r_*t_r$) and the endpoint factors have condition number at most $2$, so the group-valued row stays in a fixed ball about its reference value and its left logarithm is holomorphic and bounded by a constant. For the second, let $F(a):=\tfrac1i\log(\mathcal M^k(e^{i\eta a})(c))$ at the flat reference; $DF(0)=K^{(k)}_c\eta$. A left variation $e^{is\eta h}$ at the base $e^{i\eta a}$ is the additive variation $h+O(\eta\lvert a\rvert\lvert h\rvert)$ of $a$ (Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff on each bare bond), and the left logarithm of the row at $F(a)$ differs from $F$ by a factor $1+O(\lvert F(a)\rvert)$ with $\lvert F(a)\rvert\le C s\sup\lvert a\rvert$ (Schwarz's lemma on the bounded holomorphic $F$). Finally $\lvert DF(a)[h]-DF(0)[h]\rvert\le\sup_{t\le1}\lvert D^2F(ta)[a,h]\rvert$, bounded by the two-variable Cauchy estimate on the same ball by $C(s\sup\lvert a\rvert)(s\sup\lvert h\rvert)$. $\square$

No gradient of $a$ is needed: the radius condition of the recursion is on $s\sup\lvert a\rvert$ only.

**Lemma N (local axial gauge).** Let $N$ be the union of the level-$k$ blocks of the sites of $\Lambda^+$: a bare cube of side $\ell_N:=C_\Lambda M+4$ (level-$k$ units), inside the level-$k$ part of the localised problem. **Plaquette Hessian.** For a plaquette $p=b_1b_2b_3^{-1}b_4^{-1}$ and left variations $Y_i$ of its bonds, moving all factors of $U_p(t)$ to the left gives $U_p(t)=e^{itZ_1}e^{itZ_2}e^{itZ_3}e^{itZ_4}U_p$ with $Z_1=Y_1$, $Z_2=\operatorname{Ad}(U_1)Y_2$, $Z_3=-\operatorname{Ad}(U_1U_2U_3^{-1})Y_3$, $Z_4=-\operatorname{Ad}(U_p)Y_4$.  \tag{5.3}

for real $U$ (then $\lvert Z_i\rvert=\lvert Y_i\rvert$). At $U=\mathbf 1$, $d_UY=dY$. ## 6. Theorem H: the freed-average Hessian has a uniform gap (repaired, row 143)

**Theorem H.** There are $\alpha_*(M)>0$ and $c_*(M):=1/(8C_{\rm P}(M))$, depending on $M$, $L$ and the constants of P81, T4 (both members), RD, BC and GEO4 only, such that for every real datum $V$ of the reference problem with $\alpha(V)\le\alpha_*(M)$ and every $w$ on $\mathsf B_{\rm var}$ vanishing on $T\cup\{e_0\}$,
\[
 h_V(w)\ \ge\ c_*(M)\,\lVert w\rVert_2^2 .
\]
No constant depends on the depth $k$, on $X$ or its volume, on the datum or on the frame. At $V=\mathbf 1$ the bound holds with $C_{\rm P}(M)^{-1}$ in place of $c_*$.

*Proof.* One tangent throughout: $Y:=\eta h$, with $h=h_w$ the Landau response of Section 5 and $\sigma=\sigma_w$ its node jet. Let $U=\Phi(V)$, $\alpha=\alpha(V)$, $r=r(U)\le B\alpha$, and let $E:=\Lambda^+$, a set of level-$k$ tops of diameter $\le C_\Lambda M+2$ containing the tops of all variable rows. By (T4) and linearity, for a bare bond $b$ assigned to the top $y_b$, and for every top $y$,
\[
 \lvert h(b)\rvert\le C_4n_b\lVert w\rVert_\infty\,\xi_{y_b}^{-1}e^{-\theta d_{\mathbf B}(y_b,E)},\qquad
 \lvert\sigma(y)\rvert\le C_4n_b\lVert w\rVert_\infty\,e^{-\theta d_{\mathbf B}(y,E)} .
 \tag{6.1}
\]
Bonds of one plaquette, and the bonds and end nodes of one row carrier, have tops within $d_{\mathbf B}$-distance $D_L$ of each other and scales within a factor $L$; $D_L$ depends on $L$ only. Each bare plaquette is assigned to its lower corner and through it to a top, so a top $y$ carries at most $6(\xi_y/\eta)^4$ plaquettes; each row is assigned to its top $y_c$, at most $8$ rows per top. By (5.1$'$),
\[
 h_V(w)=\Sigma_{\rm near}+\Sigma_{\rm far}-\Sigma_{\rm mult}+\Sigma_{\rm node},
\]
where $\Sigma_{\rm near}+\Sigma_{\rm far}=\operatorname{Hess}A(U)[Y,Y]$ is split over $p\in\mathcal N_0(S)$ and $p\notin\mathcal N_0(S)$, $\Sigma_{\rm mult}:=\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cD^2\tilde q_c(U)[Y,Y])$ and $\Sigma_{\rm node}:=\sum_c\operatorname{tr}(\lambda_cQ_c)$.

*Far plaquettes.* By (5.3), $H_p\ge-8\lvert U_p-1\rvert\sum_{\partial p}\lvert Y\rvert^2$. For $p$ assigned to the top $y$: $\lvert U_p-1\rvert\le r\eta^2L^2\xi_y^{-2}$ (P81) and $\lvert Y(b)\rvert\le\eta LC_4n_b\lVert w\rVert_\infty e^{\theta D_L}\xi_y^{-1}e^{-\theta d_{\mathbf B}(y,E)}$. With at most $6(\xi_y/\eta)^4$ plaquettes per top, the contribution of $y$ is at most $192L^4e^{2\theta D_L}C_4^2n_b^2\,r\lVert w\rVert_\infty^2e^{-2\theta d_{\mathbf B}(y,E)}$: the powers of $\eta$ and $\xi_y$ cancel exactly. Lemma BC ($\theta'=2\theta$) gives $\Sigma_{\rm far}\ge-E_{\rm far}$ with
\[
