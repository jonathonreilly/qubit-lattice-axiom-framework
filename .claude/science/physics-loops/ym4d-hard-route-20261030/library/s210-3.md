*Distance.* For $X$ Hermitian and traceless, $\|X\|$ is a multiple of an Ad-invariant inner-product norm on su(2). So $r(W)=\min\{\|X\|:e^{iX}=W\}$ is the geodesic distance of a bi-invariant metric. It has the triangle inequality, and $r(We^{iX})\le r(W)+\|X\|$. The cut-off $\theta=\tilde\theta\circ r$ is smooth. The function $r$ fails to be smooth only at $\pm1$, and $\tilde\theta$ is constant near $0$ and near $\pi>5\delta/3$.

*Fixed point.* $F_H(W)=U$ holds if and only if $W=Ue^{isH}$ with $s=\theta(Ue^{isH})$. The map $g(s)=\theta(Ue^{isH})$ sends $[0,1]$ into itself. Also $|g(s)-g(s')|\le{\rm Lip}(\theta)\,r(e^{i(s'-s)H})\le{\rm Lip}(\theta)h|s-s'|$, and ${\rm Lip}(\theta)h<1/2$. So Banach's theorem gives one fixed point. Also $\partial_s(s-g(s))\ge1/2$, so the implicit function theorem gives a smooth inverse.

*Jacobian.* Put $\theta_0=\theta(W)$. Then
$F_H(W)^{-1}F_H(We^{i\epsilon X})=\exp\{i\epsilon[{\rm Ad}_{e^{i\theta_0H}}X-d\theta_W(X)H]+O(\epsilon^2)\}$.
So the left-trivialised differential is ${\rm Ad}_g-H\otimes d\theta_W$, with $g=e^{i\theta_0H}$. The determinant lemma gives $\det({\rm Ad}_g)\,(1-d\theta_W({\rm Ad}_g^{-1}H))=1-X_H\theta(W)$. Here $\det{\rm Ad}_g=1$ and ${\rm Ad}_g^{-1}H=H$. The determinant is positive, so it is the Haar Jacobian. Right translation by $B$ keeps Haar measure, so $\int f(V)\,dV=\int f(T_H(W))J_H(W)\,dW$.

*(8).* If $r(W)\le4\delta/3$, then $F_H(W)=W$. Otherwise $r(F_H(W))\ge r(W)-h>7\delta/6>a$. For the second identity, use $\|XY-1\|\le\|X-1\|+\|Y-1\|$ for unitaries and $\|e^{iA}-1\|\le\|A\|$. ## 2. What (10) asks, and in which variables

**Records and erasure.** A history $h$ is its record of outcomes (SA L17407-17413). Its clusters are as at SA L17449. Fix a cluster $\mathfrak c$ of $h$ whose root $X$ heals at level $k$. As in SA L17451, $h$ carries no cluster that heals below $k$. Every other cluster is *retained*. The erased record $h^-$ keeps every outcome of $h$ outside $\mathfrak c$. ## 3. The erasure/replay map

**Definition 3.1 (formula coordinates).** At step $t=k'+1$ of either history, the variables are the following.

- (F1) Raw stored fields: $V_q$ on $\Omega_{q+1}^c$, for each $q<t$, with surviving delta functions read as definitions (SA L18038). The measure is Haar.
- (F2) Fluctuation coordinates: $A_{k'}(b)=\tfrac1i\log(V_{k'}(b)(V^{\rm bg}_{k'}(b))^{-1})$ at the nonpivot bonds of $\Omega_t$ where no strip substitution is made. Pivot values are not coordinates; they solve the averaging constraint (SA L7481, L8278-8285). The measure is Lebesgue times the fixed Haar density $\sigma_{\rm H}$.
- (F3) Strip coordinates: $W'(b)$ at the cut-off and fully shifted strip bonds, with $V_{k'}(b)=T_{H(b)}(W'(b))$ as in `s210.md` (6). The measure is Haar.
- (F4) The new field $V_t$, until step $t+1$ splits it on $\Omega_{t+1}$ by (F2).

**Definition 3.2 (reconstruction maps).** $\Phi_h$ sends formula coordinates to raw fields: $V_{k'}=e^{iA_{k'}}V^{\rm bg}_{k',h}$ on (F2) bonds, $V_{k'}=T_{H_h}(W')$ on (F3) bonds, pivots from the constraint, and (F1) unchanged. Here $V^{\rm bg}_{k',h}$ and $H_h=\log(V^{\rm bg}_{k',h}(V^{\rm loc})^{-1})$ are formed on the label of $h$. The map $\Phi_{h^-}$ is the same with $h^-$ in place of $h$. The two maps differ only through $V^{\rm bg}$, that is, through $H$ and the (F2) translation.

**Supplied rules.** SA states these only in words. They are adopted here as definitions. Each one agrees with the cited text, and none changes a measure on its support.

- (L1) *Localised strip background.* $V^{\rm loc}_{k'}$ is the averaged background $U^\circ_W$ of SA Theorem 6(c) (L9783). Each component $W$ of $\Lambda_t^c\cap\Lambda_{k'}$ has its own problem: the joined sequence and joined datum of $W$ (stored data on old bonds, the lift of $V_{j_a}$ on collar bonds). No plaquette and no averaging constraint meets two components, so a problem posed on their union splits exactly.
- (L2) *Zones.* Each strip bond is in exactly one zone. K: the bond is read by a passed test of threshold at most $\delta_{k'}$; it is left unsubstituted, so it carries (F2). C: the bond is not in K, and a failed test of threshold at most $\delta_{k'}$ reads it; it gets the cut-off substitution. F: every other strip bond gets the full shift, $\theta\equiv1$. The zone is read from the record (SA L17453). With this rule, no bond of zone F is read by a test of threshold at most $\delta_{k'}$.
- (L3) *Pivots in old tests.* An old test $\chi_{k'}(\square)$ with $\square\subset\Omega_t^\sim\setminus\Omega_t$ is either carried with SA's pivot value or omitted. On the support, the two choices give the same measure, by Lemma 2.2(a) (SA L17369).
- (L4) *Fill.* At the places of $\mathfrak c$, $h^-$ records a pass for every test that the never-large record makes there. These tests are activated by the replay.

## 4. Theorem E: (10) holds on the supports

Let $G^h$ be the set where (EX) and (M) hold for the label of $h$ at every level. By (EX) and (M), $G^h$ contains the support of the raw plaquette tests $\chi_q(\Omega_q)$, $q\le k$, of $h$ (SA L17369, Lemma 2.2(c)). These tests are factors of the indicator product of $h$. Define $G^{h^-}$ in the same way. There, the fill's plaquette tests are factors.

**Theorem E.** Assume (SUP), (L2.2), (EX), (M) and (CAR), with rules (L1)-(L4). Let $m$ be a move of a retained cluster $\mathfrak r$ at step $t$, and let $o$ be an outcome of $m$. Then there is one set $D_{m,o}$ of formula coordinates with these properties.

- (a) $\mathbf 1_{D^h_{m,o}}(\Phi_h(e,\xi))=\mathbf 1_{D_{m,o}}(\xi)$ on $G^h$, and $\mathbf 1_{D^{h^-}_{m,o}}(\Phi_{h^-}(e,\xi))=\mathbf 1_{D_{m,o}}(\xi)$ on $G^{h^-}$.
- (b) $D_{m,o}$ reads formula coordinates only within one level-$t$ cell of the level-$(t-1)$ region of $\mathfrak r$. The guard of $m$ is the same in $h$ and $h^-$. The guard is whether $m$ occurs, together with its cubes, zones, rounds, class and localised problems.
- (c) Each test that the replay activates (L4) reads only formula coordinates within one cell of the places of $\mathfrak c$. None of these is a coordinate of $\mathfrak r$.

Hence (10) holds for every retained move and outcome. The indicator products factor as

$$
\prod I^h=\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak r}(\xi_{\mathfrak r})\,\mathbf 1_{E}(\xi_E)\,\mathbf 1^h_{\mathfrak c}(\xi^h_{\mathfrak c};\xi_\partial),\qquad
\prod I^{h^-}=\mathbf 1_{\mathfrak r}(\xi_{\mathfrak r})\,\mathbf 1_{E}(\xi_E)\,\mathbf 1_{\rm fill}(\xi^-_{\mathfrak c};\xi_\partial).
\tag{E}
$$

Here $\xi_\partial\subset\xi_E$ are the exterior coordinates within one cell of the places of $\mathfrak c$. They are disjoint from $\xi_{\mathfrak r}$.

**Proof of (a).** The proof goes through the indicators by type. Each step names the set $D_{m,o}$.

1. *Tests on raw stored fields.* These are $\chi_t(\square)$ and $\chi'_{k'}(\square)$ for $\square\subset\Omega_t^c$, and the old tests on $\Omega_t^c\setminus\Omega_t^\sim$. They read raw fields within $O(1)$ test cubes. The localised backgrounds $U_{t,\square}(V_t)$ read $V_t$ near $\square$ only (SA L17317). These fields lie in $\Lambda_t^c\subset\Omega_{t+1}^c$, so they stay (F1) at every later step. $\Phi_h$ and $\Phi_{h^-}$ are the identity on (F1) and (F4). So $D_{m,o}$ is the same raw set. This case does not use $G$.
2. *Old tests and gauge indicators on $\Omega_t^\sim$.* By (L2.2)(a) they equal $1$ on the support of the step-$t$ tests and constraints. This holds in both histories. Put $D_{m,o}$ equal to the whole space, as (L3) allows. On $\Omega_t^{\sim-1}\setminus R_t$, $\chi'_{k'}$ equals $1$ on the support of $\chi^{(k')}$ by (L2.2)(b). It is treated in the same way.
3. *Tests in fluctuation coordinates.* These are the third test on cubes in $\Omega_t^{\sim-1}$, and the collar and rim tests of every round. Each one reads $A_{k'}$ at nonpivot bonds, and its only global object is $V^{\rm bg}$ (SA L17317, L17336). By totality (SA L17346), the test is the indicator that $V^{\rm bg}$ and the logarithm exist and that $|A_{k'}(b)|<a_b$ at the bonds read. Here $a_b\in\{\delta_{k'},\delta_{k'}/(4\Theta_1),\theta_c\delta_{k'}\}$. On $G^h$, $V^{\rm bg}_{k',h}$ exists. The logarithm exists off a Haar-null set, and the coordinate is $A_{k'}(b)$ itself. So $D_{m,o}=\{|A_{k'}(b)|<a_b\ \forall b\}$, or its complement for a failed outcome. The same holds in $h^-$ on $G^{h^-}$. If such a test reads a strip bond, case 4 applies. A passed test puts the bond in zone K, and a failed test puts it in zone K or C (L2). Every threshold $a_b$ is at most $\delta_{k'}$, so `s210.md` (8) covers zone C.
4. *Strip tests.* Take a strip bond $b$. In zone K, $b$ carries (F2), and its low-threshold tests are as in 3. Its $2\delta_{k'}$ test equals $1$ on its kept condition: $\|e^{iA}V^{\rm bg}Q^{-1}-1\|<a_b+\|H\|+\|V^{\rm loc}Q^{-1}-1\|<2\delta_{k'}$ by (M). This is the unshifted-bond remark of `s210.md` 5.1. In zone C, by `s210.md` (8) and (M), a test of threshold $a\le\delta_{k'}$ is $\mathbf 1\{r(W'(b))<a\}$, and the $2\delta_{k'}$ test is $\mathbf 1\{\|W'(b)V^{\rm loc}(b)Q(b)^{-1}-1\|<2\delta_{k'}\}$. In zone F, $T_H(W')=W'V^{\rm loc}$ exactly, and no low-threshold test reads $b$ (L2). Products and complements keep these identities. So failed-cube indicators of $R_t$ are covered. $V^{\rm loc}$ is a function of the (F1) coordinates on its component $W$ (L1, R2). Also $W\subset\Lambda_t^c$ is connected, so it lies in the component of $\mathfrak r$. Its sequence and datum are therefore the same in $h$ and $h^-$ (SUP). $Q$ reads $V_t$ near $\square$ only. So $D_{m,o}$ is the same set in both histories.
5. *Operations $\mathbf R$ of retained components.* These are the stages with tests at $\delta'_j$, the frame sum, the trees, the test on $B$, the last-shell test, the fates and the inner histories. Their tests use backgrounds localised in the component (SA L17344, L17585). By (CAR), each support $S_\omega$ is a set of the component's own variables. The class conditions read the component's own boxes in its window (SA L17566). By the window lemma (`s210.md` (1)), no creation read there has healed. So each $D_{m,o}$ is the same.
6. *Undefined objects.* In cases 1, 4 and 5, every object is localised. Its existence is the same local event in both histories. In case 3, the only global object exists on $G^h$ and on $G^{h^-}$.
7. *Pivots.* No test of cases 3-4 reads a pivot value (SA L17317). A pivot inside $\Omega_t$ enters only through $\Phi$, that is, through densities. A surviving delta function on $\Omega_t^c$ fixes an (F1) pivot from (F1) data and $V_t$ in its own double block (SA L8283-8285). That is a local map, the same in both histories.

**Proof of (b).** The cubes tested, the regions $\Omega,\Lambda,S$, the rounds, the collar and rim sets, the cut-off zone, the class and the fates are all functions of the record. They read it within one cell of $\mathfrak r$ (SUP; SA L17453 records the three bond sets in the trace). The record of $h^-$ equals that of $h$ off the places of $\mathfrak c$, and these places are at least one cell from $\mathfrak r$ at every level (SUP). The reads of cases 1-5 have reach less than one cell (SUP).

**Proof of (c).** By (L4), the activated tests sit at the places of $\mathfrak c$. They are tests of cases 1 and 3 of the never-large record, so their reach is less than one cell. Their coordinates are $\xi^-_{\mathfrak c}$ and exterior coordinates. Coordinates in the gap between $\mathfrak c$ and $\mathfrak r$ may be read by both, but no move of either writes them. That is allowed: shared, unchanged exterior parameters need no edge in `s210.md` Section 3.

**Proof of (E).** In both histories, multiply the indicators over all moves. By the definition of $G^h$, $\prod I^h=\mathbf 1_{G^h}\prod I^h$. Replace each retained factor by $\mathbf 1_{D_{m,o}}$ using (a). The raw plaquette tests that imply $G^h$ are unchanged factors (case 1), so the factor $\mathbf 1_{G^h}$ can be dropped again. The exterior tests away from $\mathfrak c$ are cases 1 and 3 of the never-large record, the same in both histories. Collect the rest by (b) and (c). $\square$

**What Theorem E does not show.** It is an identity of indicators and guards. It says nothing about the densities: Jacobians, actions, Gaussian weights, normalisers. Those are treated in Section 5.

## 5. (5): the supports agree, the densities do not

**Corollary K ((5) for supports and reference measures).** Assume the hypotheses of Theorem E. Use formula coordinates, and keep every density out of the kernel. Then each retained move has the same kernel in $h$ and $h^-$: the same support, reference measure and draw normalisers.

*Proof.* The supports agree by Theorem E. The reference measures are fixed products: Haar on (F1) and (F3), and Lebesgue times $\sigma_{\rm H}$ on the nonpivot (F2) coordinates. The draws are normalised in a way that reads no label. The gauge factor has $\int\mathfrak G_k(V^u)du=1$ for every $V$ (SA L17346). Each frame has weight $|W_4|^{-1}$. The reference law of a retained $\mathbf R$ and its denominator are formed from the component's own data (SA L17585, case 5). $\square$

So the Lemma of `s210.md` Section 3 applies to the support-and-reference layer. Deleting $\mathfrak c$ keeps every retained support, guard and normaliser. The densities do not split, and the next statement shows that they cannot be asked to.

**Proposition D (the literal (5) is false for densities).** Let $b$ be a zone-C strip bond of $\mathfrak r$, with exterior data in $G^h\cap G^{h^-}$. Suppose $H_h(b)\ne H_{h^-}(b)$. Then the strip Jacobians differ on a set of $W'(b)$ of positive Haar measure. So the kernel of the retained strip move, density included, is not the same in $h$ and $h^-$.

*Proof.* Write $W=e^{i\varphi\,n\cdot\sigma}$ with $|n|=1$ and $0<\varphi<\pi$, and write $H=\mathbf h\cdot\sigma$. Then $r(W)=\varphi$ and $\|H\|=|\mathbf h|$. Differentiate $\operatorname{tr}(We^{isH})=2\cos\varphi-2s\sin\varphi\,(n\cdot\mathbf h)+O(s^2)$. This gives $\frac{d}{ds}r(We^{isH})|_{s=0}=n\cdot\mathbf h$. By (7),
$$J_H(W)=1-\tilde\theta'(\varphi)\,n\cdot\mathbf h .$$
Also $\int_{4\delta/3}^{5\delta/3}\tilde\theta'=1$, so $\tilde\theta'\ne0$ on an open interval $I$. If $\mathbf h_1\ne\mathbf h_2$, then $n\cdot(\mathbf h_1-\mathbf h_2)\ne0$ on an open set $N$ of directions. The Haar measure is $\propto\sin^2\varphi\,d\varphi\,dn$, so $I\times N$ has positive measure, and there $J_{H_1}\ne J_{H_2}$. $\square$

No cited statement gives $H_h(b)=H_{h^-}(b)$. That equality would make the true background at $b$ independent of the label at the places of $\mathfrak c$. SA Theorem 6(c) bounds the difference by $2C_\mu\varepsilon e^{-\delta_Wd}$; it does not make it vanish. So `s210.md` (5), read with densities, is not the right target. SA does not use it either: it keeps the strip Jacobians in the action (L17541) and the differences in $V_{\mathfrak X}(Y)$ (L17959-17980). The right target is Corollary K together with exact locality of the density terms. That locality is the input (LOC) below. Exact locality does not need equality. It can be built from bounds by telescoping, as follows.

**Lemma N (exact local pieces from nested bounds).** Fix a strip bond $b$ of level $k'$ at step $t$, with exterior data on the support. Let $W_0\subset W_1\subset\dots\subset W_N$ be collared regions of SA Theorem 6(c). Take $W_0$ to be the component of rule (L1), and $W_N$ the torus, so that $U^\circ_{W_N}$ is the true background. Assume:

- (N1) each $W_n$ is fixed by the label inside $W_{n+1}$, and its localised problem exists;
- (N2) the nested form of Theorem 6(c): $\|\log(V^{(n+1)}(b)V^{(n)}(b)^{-1})\|\le C_N\varepsilon e^{-\delta_Nn}$, where $V^{(n)}(b)$ is the level-$k'$ average of $U^\circ_{W_n}$ at $b$ in block axial gauge, and $2C_N\varepsilon/(1-e^{-\delta_N})<\delta/6$.

Put $H^{(n)}=\log(V^{(n)}(b)V^{\rm loc}(b)^{-1})$. Then $H^{(0)}=0$ and $H^{(N)}=H$. Let $F$ be a function of $H(b)$ alone, $C^1$ on the ball $\|H\|<\delta/6$; take, for example, $F=\log J_{H(b)}(W'(b))$ at fixed $W'(b)$. Then
$$
F(H)=F(0)+\sum_{n<N}t_n,\qquad t_n=F(H^{(n+1)})-F(H^{(n)}),
\qquad |t_n|\le2\|F'\|_\infty C_N\varepsilon e^{-\delta_Nn}.
$$
Each $t_n$ is a function of the label and the data inside $W_{n+1}$ only. For $F=\log J$ one has $\|F'\|_\infty\le2\,{\rm Lip}(\theta)$, and $F(0)=0$.

*Proof.* The sum telescopes. Exponential coordinates on SU(2) are normal coordinates of the unit 3-sphere. So for $\|A_i\|\le\rho\le1$, $\|A_1-A_2\|\le d(e^{iA_1},e^{iA_2})/(1-\rho^2/6)\le2\,d(e^{iA_1},e^{iA_2})$. Bi-invariance gives $d(V^{(n+1)}V^{\rm loc\,-1},V^{(n)}V^{\rm loc\,-1})=\|\log(V^{(n+1)}V^{(n)\,-1})\|$. So $\|H^{(n+1)}-H^{(n)}\|\le2C_N\varepsilon e^{-\delta_Nn}$, and every $H^{(n)}$ lies in the ball. The bound follows from the mean value theorem. Locality follows from (N1): $U^\circ_{W}$ reads only the joined sequence and joined datum of $W$ (SA L9783). For $\log J$, use $J=1-\tilde\theta'(\varphi)n\cdot\mathbf h\ge1/2$ and $|\tilde\theta'|\le{\rm Lip}(\theta)$. $\square$

*Consequence.* Suppose $W_{n+1}$ meets no place of $\mathfrak c$. Then $t_n^h=t_n^{h^-}$ exactly. The other pieces have $n\ge d(b,\mathfrak c)/\mu_W-1$, where $\mu_W$ is the step of the nest. They go into the interaction $V_{\mathfrak X}(W_{n+1})$. Each one depends on $\mathfrak X$ only through the components that meet $W_{n+1}$, as SA L17964 requires, and each is bounded by $8{\rm Lip}(\theta)C_N\varepsilon e^{-\delta_Nn}$. The same argument applies to any density of the step that is a $C^1$ function of the true background at finitely many bonds near $b$. That covers the elimination factors $z(c)$ and the gauge-factor density on $\Omega_t$. An exponentially small estimate alone cannot give an equality, as `s210.md` 5.1 says. It does give an exact local decomposition, and that is all the gas needs.

Status of the inputs: (N1) is a choice of nests; it is not specified here, but the repo's Definition A localisation is a candidate. (N2) is SA Theorem 6(c) applied to nested regions. It is cited, not rebuilt. At complex data, (N2) would need the complex comparison of `wf-c.md`.

## 6. The paid read closure (3), for the support layer

Take a cluster $\mathfrak c$, rooted at $X$ at level $k$. Let $A(\mathfrak c)$ be the set of pairs $(j,c)$ where $c$ is a level-$j$ cell of its lineage region $\mathsf Z_j(X)$ (SA L17479), $j\le k$. Let $\widehat A(\mathfrak c)$ add every $(j,c')$ with $c'$ touching a cell of $A_j(\mathfrak c)$. Weight each cell by $\ell_j(MR_j)^4$, as in `s210.md` Section 4. Write $V$ and $W$ for the weighted volumes of $A$ and $\widehat A$.

**Proposition P.** Assume the hypotheses of Theorem E. Then every support read of a move of $\mathfrak c$, and every read of its fill in $h^-$, lies in $\widehat A(\mathfrak c)$. Moreover:

*Proof.* The reads: Theorem E (b), (c) bound the reach by one cell. $V^{\rm loc}$ reads its component $W$, and $W$ lies in the cluster's own cells by case 4. (a) Each component of $\mathsf Z_j(X)$ lies in $\mathsf Z_{j+1}(X)$ (SA L17479). Partitions are nested (SA L17334), so the parent cell of every cell of $A_j$ lies in $A_{j+1}$. The three bond sets of the trace are functions of these bits. Inner islands are recorded in their own cells, which belong to $A$. Multiply over the cells and use $\ell_j\ge1$. $\square$
