## 3. Exact read-graph repair: a conditional lemma

Fix exterior parameters $e$. Distinguish mutable stored slots from these parameters.
For each move record its read set and its written or deleted slots.
Reads include the branch guard, integration domain, determining rows, full localization boxes, gauges, and normalization integrands.
Include geometry and outcome records as slots. A guard includes the information needed to decide whether a move occurs.
Connect two moves if either changes something the other reads or changes.
Also connect coordinates coupled by a constraint, support condition, or reference measure.
Shared, unchanged exterior parameters alone require no edge.

**Lemma.** Suppose these sets remain valid under restriction and replay.
Suppose the conditional measures and supports split over the resulting components.
Then the operations factor over those components, with their original normalizers.
Deleting a whole component preserves every retained move. This last assertion concerns the retained operations, not the deleted factor's mass.

**Proof.** For two components write their slots as $x,y$.
A move in each has the form

\[
 (K_xK_yF)(e,x,y)
 =\int k_x(e,x,dx')\int k_y(e,y,dy')F(e,x',y').
 \tag{2}
\]

Neither kernel reads the variable changed by the other.
Tonelli interchanges these integrals for nonnegative integrands.
For complex integrands the same equality requires an integrable absolute majorant.
Their support indicators are included in the kernels, so this also preserves supports.
A normalizer $D_x(e,x)=\int q_x(e,x,u)\,du$ is unchanged because its integrand and domain are unchanged.
Thus adjacent moves from different components commute.
Repeated adjacent swaps group every finite sequence by component, without changing its internal orders.
On replay, each retained guard and input is unchanged by hypothesis.
Induction through that order gives the same retained kernels, domains, and normalizers. This proves the lemma.

This repairs the graph criterion, not its application to SA.
The graph of only the executed moves is insufficient if deletion activates previously absent moves.
To identify a legal erased history one must also check its empty-history fill and all newly required tests.
Nor may a mixed action factor be discarded: it remains an interaction for the later expansion.

## 4. How the repaired graph would be paid

Let $A(h)$ contain the paid genealogy cells, with

\[
 V(h)=\sum_{(j,C)\in A(h)}\ell_j(MR_j)^4,
 \qquad \ell_j\ge1.
\]

Let $\widehat A(h)$ contain the complete read closure, including connectors in the space-time address graph.
It must include reads activated by replay, not just those used before deletion.
Write $W(h)$ for its volume with the same cell weights.
An adequate repair would prove, uniformly in depth and volume,

\[
 \widehat A(h)\text{ connected},\qquad
 W(h)\le J V(h),\qquad
 \text{weighted number of codes on }\widehat A\le e^{bW}.
 \tag{3}
\]

The codes must decode injectively to histories. Component schedules are fixed, not summed over.
Frame sums retain their normalized weights. These are hypotheses for the following count.

The address graph has degree at most $D=L^4+3^4+2$, under SA's nested-grid rule.
Indeed, it has at most $3^4-1$ lateral neighbors, one parent, and $L^4$ children.
For a rooted connected set of $N$ vertices, traverse a canonical spanning tree twice.
Its $2N-2$ neighbor labels recover the visited set, so there are at most $D^{2N-2}$ such sets.
Since $W\ge N$ and $V\ge W/J$, (3) gives

\[
 \sum_{h\text{ rooted}}w_{\rm frame}(h)e^{-cV(h)}
 \le\sum_{N\ge1}D^{2N-2}e^{-(c/J-b)N}
 \le D^{-2}\sum_{N\ge1}2^{-N}\le1
 \tag{4}
\]

when $c\ge J(b+2\log D+\log2)$.
This proves the entropy implication by hand, with no depth factor.
It does not prove (3) for the actual histories.

A bound on one collar does not bound a transitive read closure.
For example, let a move at site $r$ set $x_r=x_{r-1}$, for $1\le r\le n$.
Changing $x_0$ changes every $x_r$, by induction.
All moves have range one, but the closure of site zero has $n+1$ sites.
If only site zero is paid, no uniform $J$ in (3) exists.
This example refutes that inference only. It is not a Yang–Mills history or a counterexample to 2.10.

### 5.1. Continuation at `f7973506f`: the fixed-branch strip map

The following rebuilds the substitution in Balaban, CMP 119, (1.21)–(1.24), (3.21)–(3.22).
It does not assume that deletion preserves its inputs.

Fix one nonpivot bond, its exterior data, and its branch.
Write $B=V^{\rm bg}$, $L=V^{\rm loc}$, $BL^{-1}=e^{iH}$ and

\[
 F_H(W)=We^{-i\theta(W)H},\qquad
 T_H(W)=F_H(W)B=W e^{i(1-\theta(W))H}L.
 \tag{6}
\]

Use the operator norm and the bi-invariant distance $r(W)=\|\log W\|$ near $1$ in $SU(2)$.
Extend $r$ as geodesic distance. Choose $0<\delta<\pi/8$.
Choose a smooth radial $0\le\theta\le1$, zero for $r\le4\delta/3$ and one for $r\ge5\delta/3$.
Put $h=\|H\|$ and require $h\operatorname{Lip}(\theta)<1/2$.
These margins specify one allowed cut-off; they are not estimates for an actual history.

For any $U$, solving $F_H(W)=U$ reduces to
$s=\theta(Ue^{isH})$, $W=Ue^{isH}$.
Iteration on $[0,1]$ contracts by at most $h\operatorname{Lip}(\theta)$.
Its successive differences form a geometric bound, so it converges to a unique fixed point.
The right-hand side has scalar derivative below $1/2$ in absolute value; implicit differentiation gives a smooth inverse.
Thus $F_H$ is a diffeomorphism.
With $X_H\theta(W)=\left.\frac{d}{ds}\theta(We^{isH})\right|_{s=0}$, its Haar Jacobian is

\[
 J_H(W)=1-X_H\theta(W)>1/2.                 \tag{7}
\]

Indeed, translate the differential back to the Lie algebra.
It is $\operatorname{Ad}_{e^{i\theta H}}-H\otimes d\theta$.
The adjoint map has determinant one and fixes $H$.
Multilinearity of the determinant leaves only the constant and single rank-one terms, giving (7).
In particular $J_H=1$ on each constant-cut-off region.

Let $Q$ be the approximate-test background at this bond.
Assume $h+\|LQ^{-1}-1\|<\delta/6$.
For every threshold $0<a\le\delta$,

\[
 \mathbf1_{\{r(F_H(W))<a\}}=\mathbf1_{\{r(W)<a\}},\qquad
 \mathbf1_{\{\|T_H(W)Q^{-1}-1\|<2\delta\}}
 =\mathbf1_{\{\|WLQ^{-1}-1\|<2\delta\}}.       \tag{8}
\]

For the first identity, $F_H(W)=W$ when $r(W)\le4\delta/3$.
Otherwise $r(F_H(W))\ge r(W)-h>\delta$, so both indicators vanish.
For the second, $\theta=1$ gives equality of the tested matrices.
If $\theta<1$, both norms are below $5\delta/3+h+\|LQ^{-1}-1\|<2\delta$.
Here $\|e^{iA}-1\|\le\|A\|$ follows by integrating $ie^{itA}A$.
Complements and finite products preserve these identities, including failed-cube indicators.

At an unshifted bond with a kept condition $r(W)<a\le\delta$, the original approximate test equals one:
$\|WBQ^{-1}-1\|<\delta+h+\|LQ^{-1}-1\|<2\delta$.
At a fully shifted bond, $T_H(W)=WL$ exactly.
A low-threshold indicator there cannot be discarded without a separate support implication.
Thus the three zone rules in SA 17459 work algebraically under these explicit conditions.
This does not establish their domains, pivot values, or replacement supports after erasure.

### 5.2. The retained error and the first failed application

The substitution preserves an integral, not its density without a correction.
For one bond and any chosen comparison exponent $A_0$ it gives exactly

\[
 \chi(T_H(W))e^{A(T_H(W))}J_H(W)\,dW
 =\chi(T_H(W))e^{A_0(W)+B_H(W)}\,dW,
 \quad B_H=A\circ T_H-A_0+\log J_H.          \tag{9}
\]

This follows by substitution and multiplication of exponentials.
It keeps the positive sign of $\log J_H$.
The Jacobian generally depends on $H$ in the transition region, by (7).
Moving it into $B_H$ is exact; deleting it is not.
For a differentiable interpolation $A_s$, the action difference is
$A_1-A_0=\int_0^1\partial_s A_s\,ds$, by the fundamental theorem of calculus.
This rebuilds the algebra behind Balaban, CMP 109, (3.21)–(3.23).
It supplies no decay estimate. Sharp indicators must first be transported by (8).
