Fix supplied cutoff data \(a_j=L^{-K_j}\downarrow0,\beta_j\ge0\) for which S3 has common constants. Existence of such first-passage data is upstream E1. It is not a conclusion of compactness. Read S1g as the Result of [am1-port.md](am1-port.md): every periodic Wilson DLR state has lattice translations, \(W_4\), and coordinate and diagonal reflection positivity.

Use \(\mathcal O_j(f)=\sum_p f(x_p)A_p\), with \(A_p=1-\tfrac12\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{Tr}U_p\), and one plaquette per coordinate plane and lattice site. For periodic states \(\rho_j,\nu_j\), put
\[
 Z_j(f)=\mathcal O_j(f)-\nu_j(\mathcal O_j(f)),\qquad
 m_{j,n}(\mathbf f)=\rho_j\!\left(\prod_{i=1}^n Z_j(f_i)\right),\quad m_{j,0}=1.
 \tag{1}
\]
S3 means D4 and G4, [d2-d4.md](d2-d4.md), (8)–(10). It bounds the singleton difference by \(C_1(R)\|f\|_\sharp\), and each higher cumulant by \(C_n(\delta;R)\prod_i\|f_i\|_\sharp\). Supports have radius at most \(R\) and mutual distance at least \(\delta>0\). Constants are independent of their centers, both states and \(j\). Here \(\|f\|_\sharp=\max(\|f\|_\infty,c_M\|\nabla f\|_\infty)\).

The finite moment–cumulant identity gives
\[
 m_{j,n}(\mathbf f)=\sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Part}[n]}
       \prod_{B\in\pi}\kappa^{\rho_j}_{|B|}(Z_j(f_i):i\in B),
 \qquad |m_{j,n}(\mathbf f)|\le M_n(\delta,R)\prod_i\|f_i\|_\sharp.
 \tag{2}
\]
Indeed, work with commuting symbols \(z_i^2=0\). Expanding the finite exponential of the cumulant polynomial gives partitions; the factorial cancels the block orders. Constants only alter singleton cumulants. Complexification costs at most \(2^n\). Thus (2) includes complex tests and reflected tuples. No bound on an uncentered mean is used.

## 2. Volume first

At fixed \(a,\beta\), choose one orientation of each bond. The configuration space \(X=\mathrm{SU}(2)^E\) is compact metric: enumerate bonds and use \(\sum_r2^{-r}\min(1,d(U_r,V_r))\). Diagonal extraction in the compact factors proves sequential compactness. Continuous cylinder functions are uniformly dense, since freezing coordinates after \(N\) changes this metric by at most \(2^{-N}\).

Lift Wilson measures from growing compatible cubic tori periodically to \(X\). Their densities are positive continuous functions on finite compact products, so they define probability measures. Here \(C(X)\) is separable: take a countable dense set of centers, finite rational-radius ball covers, and weights \((r-d(x,x_i))_+\) divided by their sum. Rational linear combinations of these weights approximate every continuous function, by uniform continuity. Diagonal extraction of their integrals and uniform approximation give a positive functional of norm one. Its Riesz measure \(\nu\) is a weak subsequential limit.

For a finite bond set \(W\), its conditional kernel integrates Haar variables in \(W\) with density
\[
 \frac{\exp[-\beta\sum_{p:p\cap W\ne\varnothing}A_p]}{
       \int\exp[-\beta\sum_{p:p\cap W\ne\varnothing}A_p]\,dU_W},
 \qquad 0\le A_p\le2.
 \tag{3}
\]
The denominator is at least \(e^{-2\beta|\mathcal P(W)|}\). Hence this kernel sends continuous local functions to continuous local functions. For a local \(F\), its support and the interaction neighborhood of \(W\) embed in every sufficiently large torus. Integration over \(W\) then gives \(\mu_N(\gamma_WF)=\mu_N(F)\). Passing to the weak limit gives this identity for \(\nu\). Cylinder approximation extends it to continuous \(F\); equality of Borel measures extends it to bounded Borel \(F\). For an outside-measurable \(g\), properness gives \(\gamma_W(gF)=g\gamma_WF\), proving the conditional DLR equation.

Translations and \(W_4\) preserve the finite-volume measures and pass to the limit. Thus \(\nu\) is a periodic Gibbs state. Perform this construction separately at every supplied \((a_j,\beta_j)\), before taking any cutoff limit. S1g supplies all its admitted mirrors. In (1), choose \(\rho_j=\nu_j\) for the centered volume-first limit; arbitrary periodic pairs give the stronger two-state conclusion. No interchange of limits or uniqueness is asserted.

## 3. One subsequence and moving tests

Take finite unions \(Q\) of rational closed boxes. The functions supported in \(Q\), with the \(C^1\) norm, embed into \(C(Q)^5\) with an equivalent norm, by their values and four derivatives. This is a separable metric space by the continuous-function approximation above. A subspace of a second-countable space is second-countable; choosing a point in each nonempty basic open set proves its separability. Choose a countable dense subset for every \(Q\), and complexify.

List all tuples drawn from these subsets whose supporting box unions are separated, at every order. Apply successive subsequence extraction to their bounded values in (2), then take increasing diagonal indices \(j_r\). Any additional countable collection of bounded scalar sequences can be included.

For supports of \(f_i,g_i\) in the same separated containers, multilinearity gives
\[
 |m_{j,n}(\mathbf f)-m_{j,n}(\mathbf g)|
 \le M_n(\delta,R)\sum_i\|f_i-g_i\|_\sharp
                  \prod_{h\ne i}\max(\|f_h\|_\sharp,\|g_h\|_\sharp).
 \tag{4}
\]
Every mixed tuple remains separated. Enclose any separated compact tuple in rational box unions with positive mutual distance. Approximate its entries in those supported spaces. Equation (4) makes the error uniformly small in \(j\); comparison through a fixed approximant proves that the original values are Cauchy. Write their limits as \(\sigma_n\), with \(\sigma_0=1\). This uses the same \(j_r\) for every tuple and every order.

Admissible multilinearity, permutation symmetry, conjugation, and (2) pass through their finite identities. The original constants pass at each tuple. The metric
\[
 d(\sigma,\sigma')=\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k}
       \min(1,|\sigma(X^k)-\sigma'(X^k)|)
 \tag{5}
\]
on the determining list separates such bounded forms by (4). Coordinate convergence implies convergence at every tuple by (4). Conversely, split (5) into a finite head and a uniformly small tail. Thus (5) induces pointwise convergence. Extraction proves compactness of the closed class with these common bounds.

If \(f_{i,j_r}\to f_i\) in \(C^1\), and their supports stay in fixed separated compact containers, (4) gives
\[
 m_{j_r,n}(f_{1,j_r},\ldots,f_{n,j_r})\longrightarrow
                    \sigma_n(f_1,\ldots,f_n).
 \tag{6}
\]
The same argument gives uniform convergence on each compact subset of a fixed product of supported \(C^1\) spaces, using a finite net.

## 4. Mirrors, the one-point constant, and cumulants

Write \(\tau_v f(x)=f(x-v)\). Compact \(C^1\) tests satisfy
\[
 \|\tau_vf-\tau_wf\|_\sharp
 \le\max\{\|\nabla f\|_\infty|v-w|,
                   c_M\omega_{\nabla f}(|v-w|)\}\longrightarrow0.
 \tag{7}
\]
Uniform continuity of the gradient proves this; no second derivative is needed. Round \(v\) to \(v_j\in a_j\mathbb Z^4\), apply lattice invariance of both states, then (6). Thus \(\sigma\) is translation invariant. The \(W_4\) action is exact at every cutoff and passes directly.

For \(x_\mu=c\), choose bond mirrors \(c_j\in a_j(\mathbb Z+\tfrac12)\) tending to \(c\). For \(x_\mu\pm x_\lambda=c\), choose \(c_j\in a_j\mathbb Z\). Their reflections differ from the target reflection by translations \(2(c_j-c)e_\mu\) and \((c_j-c)(e_\mu\pm e_\lambda)\), respectively. In unit-normal coordinates the diagonal offset grid is \(a_j\mathbb Z/\sqrt2\).

Let \(F=\sum_\alpha b_\alpha X_\alpha\) be a finite combination of separated tuples strictly in one open half-space. Compactness gives a positive mirror gap. Eventually every plaquette sampled by \(F\) lies in the corresponding rounded closed half-space, since its diameter is at most \(\sqrt2a_j\). Its centered lattice polynomial \(F_j\) is bounded. S1g and centering covariance give
\[
 0\le\rho_j(\overline{\theta_jF_j}F_j)
       \longrightarrow
 q_\theta(F,F):=\sum_{\alpha,\gamma}\bar b_\alpha b_\gamma
                  \sigma(\theta\bar X_\alpha\cdot X_\gamma).
 \tag{8}
\]
Each concatenated tuple has separated reflected and unreflected halves; each half retains its internal separation. Equation (6) therefore applies to every entry. It also passes reflection invariance. This proves positivity at all affine coordinate and diagonal mirrors, from either side.

S1g makes \(\delta_j=\rho_j(A_p)-\nu_j(A_p)\) independent of the plaquette. There are six unoriented coordinate planes. Hence exactly
\[
 m_{j,1}(f)=c_jR_j(f),\qquad
 c_j=6a_j^{-4}\delta_j,\qquad
 R_j(f)=\frac{a_j^4}{6}\sum_p f(x_p).
 \tag{9}
\]
Each orientation gives a shifted cubic Riemann sum. Comparing a sample with its side-\(a_j\) cube costs at most \(2a_j\|\nabla f\|_\infty\) pointwise. At most \(C(R+1)^4a_j^{-4}\) cubes contribute. Thus
\[
 |R_j(f)-\textstyle\int f|\le C(R+1)^4a_j\|\nabla f\|_\infty.
\]
Choose a fixed real smooth \(f_*\) of integral one. Along the existing subsequence,
\[
 c_j=\frac{m_{j,1}(f_*)}{R_j(f_*)}\longrightarrow
 c=\sigma_1(f_*),\qquad \sigma_1(f)=c\int f.
 \tag{10}
\]
D4 gives \(|c|\le C_1(R_*)\|f_*\|_\sharp\), uniformly over both states. Reality gives \(c\in\mathbb R\). For \(\rho_j=\nu_j\), \(c=0\).

The inverse partition formula is
\[
 K_n(\mathbf f)=
 \sum_{\pi\in\operatorname{Part}[n]}(-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
                        \prod_{B\in\pi}\sigma_{|B|}(\mathbf f_B).
 \tag{11}
\]
To obtain its coefficient, expand the finite logarithm in the squarefree variables used for (2). The term with \(r\) blocks counts \(r!\) orders and divides by \(r\). Every subtuple converges, so the lattice cumulants converge to (11), with the S3 bounds. Centering by \(c\int f\) removes singleton blocks and preserves every higher cumulant. No common analytic disc or uncentered one-point limit is required.

## 5. The compact Hilbert bridge

Choose \(x_4\) as time and let \(\theta\) reflect it. Let \(V_+\) be the complex span of separated tuples strictly above time zero, including the empty tuple. Define
\[
 q(F,G)=\sigma(\theta\bar F\cdot G),\qquad
 \mathcal H=\overline{V_+/\{F:q(F,F)=0\}},\qquad
 \Omega=[\varnothing].
 \tag{12}
\]
Equation (8) gives positivity. Expanding \(q(F+zG,F+zG)\) gives Hermiticity and Cauchy–Schwarz. Thus null vectors are orthogonal to all vectors, the quotient is well-defined, and \(\|\Omega\|=1\). No unrestricted multiplication of separated tuples is assumed.

On representatives, time translation \(T_t^0=\tau_{te_4}\), \(t\ge0\), is symmetric for \(q\). This follows from translation invariance and \(\theta\tau_{te_4}=\tau_{-te_4}\theta\). Spatial translation \(U_x^0=\tau_{(x,0)}\), \(x\in\mathbb R^3\), preserves \(q\) and has inverse \(U_{-x}^0\).

For a fixed finite \(F\), let \(\epsilon>0\) be its mirror gap and \(d>0\) its smallest within-tuple separation. Every entry of \(q(\tau_{se_4}F,\tau_{se_4}F)\), \(s\ge0\), has separation at least \(\min(d,2\epsilon)\). Individual support radii and norms do not change. Position independence in (2) gives
\[
 a(s):=\|\tau_{se_4}F\|_q\le C_F\quad(s\ge0),\qquad
 a(t)^2=q(F,\tau_{2te_4}F)\le a(0)a(2t).
 \tag{13}
\]
For constants or singletons the irrelevant internal separation can be any positive number. If \(a(0)=0\), (13) gives \(a(t)=0\). Otherwise iteration gives
\[
 a(t)\le a(0)^{1-2^{-r}}C_F^{2^{-r}}\longrightarrow a(0).
 \tag{14}
\]
Thus time maps preserve null vectors and extend to self-adjoint contractions \(T_t\). Spatial maps extend to unitaries \(U_x\). Translation laws and commutation pass from the dense domain. Also \(T_t=T_{t/2}^2\), so \(T_t\) is positive.

Equations (4) and (7), applied in fixed separated neighborhoods, give
\[
 \|(T_t-I)[F]\|^2
 =q(F,\tau_{2te_4}F)-2\operatorname{Re}q(F,\tau_{te_4}F)+q(F,F)
 \longrightarrow0,
 \tag{15}
\]
and \(\|(U_x-I)[F]\|^2=2q(F,F)-2\operatorname{Re}q(F,\tau_{(x,0)}F)\to0\). For a general vector, approximation by \([F]\) costs at most twice its norm error, uniformly in \(t,x\). Hence both families are strongly continuous at zero, and their laws give continuity everywhere.

If \(T_1v=0\), then \(\|T_{1/2}v\|^2=\langle v,T_1v\rangle=0\). Repeated halving and continuity imply \(v=0\). The spectral measure of \(T_1\) therefore has no atom at zero. Functional calculus defines
\[
 H=-\log T_1\ge0,\qquad T_t=e^{-tH},\qquad H\Omega=0.
 \tag{16}
\]
The domain of \(H\) is dense: its spectral subspaces for \(T_1\in[1/N,1]\) increase to the whole space. Positive roots give (16) first for dyadic \(t\), and strong continuity gives every \(t\ge0\). This also proves uniqueness of \(H\) and injectivity of \(T_t\), \(t>0\). The empty tuple is fixed, so \(\Omega\) lies in the zero eigenspace.

The commuting spatial unitaries have self-adjoint generators \(P_1,P_2,P_3\). Commutation of \(U_x\) with \(T_1\) passes to its spectral projections, hence to \(H\). Also \(U_x\Omega=\Omega\) and \(P_i\Omega=0\). The \(W_3\) action on spatial coordinates preserves \(q\), fixes \(\Omega\), and gives unitaries \(V_w\) with
\[
 V_wT_t=T_tV_w,\qquad V_wU_xV_w^{-1}=U_{wx},\qquad
 \sigma(\theta\bar F\cdot\tau_{(x,t)}G)
       =\langle[F],e^{-tH}U_x[G]\rangle.
 \tag{17}
\]
These identities hold on representatives and then by density. Rational boxes strictly above zero, the countable families of Section 3, and rational complex coefficients give a countable total set, by expanding the squared norm of an approximation error. Thus \(\mathcal H\) is separable.

## 6. The scalar extended domain

**The explicit S3 bound is sufficient here; abstract per-order boundedness alone would not supply this step.** In [d2-d4.md](d2-d4.md), (6)–(10), the cell size, margin and \(Q\) depend on \(n,R\), not on separation. The only separation loss in (10) is \(\max(1,\delta^{-4})\). Equation (2) therefore gives, for unit-radius supports,
\[
 |m_{j,n}(\mathbf f)|,\ |\sigma_n(\mathbf f)|
 \le A_n\max(1,\delta^{-5n})\prod_i\|f_i\|_1,
 \qquad \|f\|_1=\max(\|f\|_\infty,\|\nabla f\|_\infty).
 \tag{18}
\]
Indeed, each nonsingleton block costs at most \(\max(1,\delta^{-4})\); their product is bounded by \(\max(1,\delta^{-4n})\). We weakened the exponent to five to reuse the following construction. \(A_n<\infty\) is uniform in \(j\) and positions. No factorial control of \(A_n\) is assumed.

Let \(\Delta_n=\bigcup_{i<k}\{x_i=x_k\}\), and let \(\mathcal S_{\Delta,n}\) be Schwartz functions flat to all orders on \(\Delta_n\). Write
\[
 p_s(\Phi)=\max_{|\alpha|\le s}\sup_x
             (1+|x|^2)^{s/2}|\partial^\alpha\Phi(x)|.
\]
Here is the per-order version of the construction in [growth-qf3.md](growth-qf3.md), Sections 3–5. Within fixed separated slot spaces, multilinearity defines a functional on their algebraic tensor product. This tensor product injects into product functions: finitely many point evaluations distinguish each finite-dimensional slot span, and their tensor matrices remain injective.

Take slot cubes of scale \(\ell=h/64\), with expanded cubes separated by \(h/2\), \(0<h\le1/16\). Expand a smooth test \(u\) supported in the inner product box in its period-\(8\ell\) Fourier series. Integration by parts with four powers of \(1-\pi^{-2}\Delta\) in each rescaled four-dimensional slot gives
\[
 |\widehat u(\mathbf v)|\le B_nJ_{8n,\ell}(u)
                   \prod_i(1+|v_i|^2)^{-4},\qquad
 J_{r,\ell}(u)=\max_{|\alpha|\le r}\ell^{|\alpha|}
                                       \|\partial^\alpha u\|_\infty.
\]
Multiply each Fourier mode by a smooth cutoff supported in its expanded slot cube and equal to one on the inner cube. Its \(C^1\) norm is at most \(C\ell^{-1}(1+|v_i|)\). The single-slot sum converges: the shell \(|v|_\infty=r\) costs \(O(r^3)\), and the summand is \(O(r^{-7})\). Thus (18) defines a local functional with
\[
 |T(u)|\le B_n h^{-6n}J_{8n,\ell}(u).
 \tag{19}
\]
Extra integrations by parts give Fourier convergence in every derivative. To identify the sum, use the period-two Fejér kernel \(F_N(s)=N^{-1}|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{i\pi ks}|^2\). Orthogonality gives normalized integral one. The geometric sum bounds it by \([N\sin^2(\pi\epsilon/2)]^{-1}\) for \(\epsilon\le|s|\le1\). Thus products concentrate at zero, and uniform continuity proves that their convolutions tend to \(u\). On tensors, slotwise \(C^1\) convergence and (4) recover the prescribed value. On overlapping boxes, refine into smaller product boxes inside the overlap. Their Fourier tensors have the same values in both constructions; (19) passes equality to their limits. A finite partition glues \(T\) on compact tests off \(\Delta_n\). Partitioning each slot of a larger separated tensor recovers its prescribed value by multilinearity.

Choose a smooth \(\zeta\) equal to zero for \(|x|_\infty\le1\) and one for \(|x|_\infty\ge2\). Put \(P_h=\prod_{i<k}\zeta((x_i-x_k)/h)\), \(h_r=2^{-r}/16\), \(D_0=P_{h_0}\), and \(D_r=P_{h_r}-P_{h_{r-1}}\). For \(n=1\), \(P_h=1\). On every fine layer the closest pair has distance between \(h_r\) and \(4h_r\) in the sup norm.

Use a smooth product partition into cubes of scale \(\ell_r=h_r/64\), denoted \(\chi_{r,z}\). Such a partition comes from dividing translates of a nonnegative compact bump by their strictly positive periodic sum. Every active expanded product box has separated slots. Taylor expansion from a nearest diagonal, where all jets of \(\Phi\) vanish, gives
\[
 J_{8n,\ell_r}(\chi_{r,z}D_r\Phi)
 \le B_n h_r^{10n+2}p_{20n}(\Phi)
       \sup_{x\in\operatorname{supp}\chi_{r,z}}(1+|x|^2)^{-10n}.
 \tag{20}
\]
To check the orders, Taylor uses \(10n+2\) derivatives beyond at most \(8n\); \(18n+2\le20n\). Its segment has length at most \(8h_r\). Weights along that segment have a bounded ratio. Scaled derivatives in \(J\) cancel all cutoff powers of \(h_r^{-1}\). Leibniz contributes a finite constant depending only on \(n\).

The position sum of the weight in (20) is at most \(B_nh_r^{-4n}\). Indeed,
\[
 (1+\textstyle\sum_i|x_i|^2)^{-10n}
       \le\prod_i(1+|x_i|^2)^{-10},
\]
and comparison with side-\(\ell_r\) cube integrals gives a single-slot sum \(O(\ell_r^{-4})\). The integral is finite since its radial tail is \(O(\int_1^\infty r^{-17}dr)\). Combining (19)–(20) gives
\[
 \sum_z|T(\chi_{r,z}D_r\Phi)|
       \le B_n h_r^{10n+2-6n-4n}p_{20n}(\Phi)
       =B_nh_r^2p_{20n}(\Phi).
 \tag{21}
\]
The coarse layer has a finite bound without \(h_r^2\). Summing defines \(\Sigma_n\) on \(\mathcal S_{\Delta,n}\), with
\[
 |\Sigma_n(\Phi)|\le B'_n p_{20n}(\Phi).
 \tag{22}
\]
On compact off-diagonal tests the partitions sum to one, so this extends \(T\). The same construction gives extensions \(\Sigma_{j,n}\) with the same bound.

For completeness, separated smooth tensors are dense in this flat Schwartz space. First cut off infinity; Leibniz and an extra Schwartz weight make the error tend to zero in every fixed seminorm. Then multiply by \(P_h\). Near a diagonal, Taylor to arbitrary order \(M\) bounds the error in \(p_s\) by \(C h^{M-s}p_{s+M}\); choose \(M>s\). The resulting compact off-diagonal test has a finite product-box partition. Its Fourier partial sums converge in every derivative, with fixed compact support. These are finite sums of separated smooth tensors. A diagonal choice of approximation errors gives Schwartz convergence. This proves uniqueness of (22).

Approximate a fixed \(\Phi\) by one such finite sum. Bound both approximation errors by (22), and use already proved convergence on that finite sum. Therefore, along the original subsequence,
\[
 \Sigma_{j_r,n}(\Phi)\longrightarrow\Sigma_n(\Phi).
 \tag{23}
\]
The bound also allows \(\Phi_{j_r}\to\Phi\) in Schwartz topology. If D2 is supplied, [growth-qf3.md](growth-qf3.md), Result, improves \(B'_n\) to \(C^n(n!)^{k+32}\). Uniqueness identifies that H1 extension with this one.

## 7. H2 on finite positive-time Schwartz sequences

Let \(\mathcal E_{+,n}\subset\mathcal S_{\Delta,n}\) consist of tests supported where every time coordinate is nonnegative; set \(\mathcal E_{+,0}=\mathbb C\). Smoothness forces every jet to vanish whenever a time is zero. These spaces include tests touching the time boundary.

The density argument above works with all approximating tensors strictly above time zero. To see this, multiply also by \(\prod_i b(x_{i,4}/h)\), where \(b=0\) on \((-\infty,1]\) and \(b=1\) on \([2,\infty)\). Taylor to the zero-time hyperplane bounds its error in \(p_s\) by \(C h^{M-s}p_{s+M}\), as before. The compact cutoff then has a positive time gap; choose all Fourier boxes within it.

For finite sequences \(\Phi=(\Phi_p)\), \(\Psi=(\Psi_q)\), define
\[
 Q(\Phi,\Psi)=\sum_{p,q}
       \Sigma_{p+q}(\Theta\bar\Phi_p\otimes\Psi_q),
 \tag{24}
\]
where \(\Theta\) reflects every time argument. Each tensor belongs to \(\mathcal S_{\Delta,p+q}\). Internal diagonals are already flat. On a cross diagonal at positive time the reflected factor vanishes with every jet; at negative time the positive factor does. At time zero both are flat. Also
\[
 p_s(f\otimes g)\le p_s(f)p_s(g),
 \tag{25}
\]
by Leibniz in disjoint variable groups and \(1+|x|^2+|y|^2\le(1+|x|^2)(1+|y|^2)\).

Approximate each component by the strictly positive separated tensors just constructed. Equations (22) and (25) make their finite quadratic forms converge to (24). Each approximating form is nonnegative by (8), so \(Q(\Phi,\Phi)\ge0\).

The completed quotient for \(Q\) is canonically \(\mathcal H\). Both have the same scalar products on smooth compact separated tensors. These tensors are dense for \(Q\) by the preceding approximation. They are dense in (12) because small common mollifications converge in \(C^1\), preserve positive gaps, and (4) controls their norm errors. Hence the identity on this common dense subspace extends to a unitary.

This also removes tensor-presentation ambiguities. Smooth zero tensor relations have zero form by (24). Mollifying each slot at one common scale preserves any \(C^1\) zero tensor relation. Its smooth classes are null, and their convergence in (12) makes the original class null.

For each fixed \(t\ge0,x\in\mathbb R^3\), translations are continuous on these Schwartz spaces and preserve their positive-time domains. Apply them to the same approximating tensors. The unitary bridge therefore intertwines their quotient actions with \(T_t,U_x\) of Section 5. Thus (15)–(17), including strong continuity, \(H\ge0\) and \(H\Omega=0\), hold on the extended domain. The \(W_3\) action intertwines in the same way. There is no need to bound growing translated Schwartz seminorms uniformly in time; contraction was proved on the compact dense domain first.

## 8. Dimension and the first input

The compactness and contraction arguments are dimension independent. In four dimensions, the supported \(C^1\) embedding has five components, and (9) uses six plaquette orientations and \(a_j^4\). Time is \(x_4\), with three spatial generators and \(W_3\). The mirror-offset factors do not change.

The extension does change. A literal use of the three-dimensional Fourier order \(4n+1\) leaves the majorant \(|v|^{-(3n+1)}\) after tensor norms. In \(4n\) dimensions its shell sum is \(\sum_{r\ge1}r^{n-2}\), which diverges. This summability estimate is the first failing step of that literal port, not a counterexample to extension. The eight derivatives per slot in (19) repair it. Position counting costs \(h^{-4n}\); the integrable weight and Taylor order in (20) leave the summable \(h^2\) in (21).

No new input fails with S1g and the **explicit** S3 supplied here. If S3 is shortened to arbitrary constants \(C_n(\delta;R)\), (18) is the first unsupported step; Sections 2–5 still hold. Factorial growth remains the separate D2 result. The Result of [cluster-port.md](cluster-port.md) uses the same compact Hilbert form; Section 7 identifies its completion with the extended one, without assuming clustering to obtain contraction.

For an unconditional T0 statement, the first unproved source input remains **D1**, whose cited derivation starts at **C1/OH1**, as recorded in [d2-d4.md](d2-d4.md), Result. E1 must also provide the first-passage cutoff sequence. This note neither proves nor refutes those inputs.

## Result

**Conditional on the supplied cutoff sequence, S3 and S1g, S4 and S6 follow.** Fixed-cutoff periodic Gibbs states exist by a volume-first construction. One cutoff subsequence converges on every separated \(C^1\) tuple, its moving tests, one-point differences and cumulants. It preserves translations, \(W_4\), reality, symmetry, and coordinate and diagonal reflection positivity.

The compact and positive-time diagonal-flat domains give the same separable Hilbert space. It has strongly continuous \(e^{-tH}\), \(H\ge0\), \(H\Omega=0\), commuting spatial translations and \(W_3\). Thus the stated inputs supply O1 and O4; D2 additionally supplies H1's factorial bound through row 188.

Proved here by Codex as a research implication, not yet checked by the other model. No computer-assisted step or audit status is used. This does not establish T0 or discharge its source inputs.
