## 4. R-A first: cases, matching, and the missing invariant

### 4.1 What locality actually bounds

Fix a top row of level `q` and stop bare backward reads at `u=q-d`.
The source's invariant is

\[
 \mathcal R_{q-r}\subset
 B^{(r)}(c_-)\cup B^{(r)}(c_+),\qquad 0\le r\le d.
 \tag{3}
\]

Here `B^{(r)}` is an order-`r` block, with side `L^r` on that finer lattice.
This is Y 76308–76322, including nonpivots.
The proof takes unions of child blocks inside their parent blocks at each descent.
It stops at a restored or collar chart and includes that chart's actual stock.
Test and weight carriers are added separately.

For a nonwrapping bare carrier, the two blocks have `2L^{4r}` sites at depth `r`.
There are at most `8L^{4r}` positively oriented bonds with both endpoints in them.
Charge each bond to its initial site and one of four directions to obtain this bound.
Thus the number of geometric bond slots through depth `d` is at most

\[
 N_d=8\sum_{r=0}^{d}L^{4r}
     =8\frac{L^{4(d+1)}-1}{L^4-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]

For a fixed finite palette of slot types, this bounds the number of coloured masks by `a^{N_d}`.
Version labels and chart stocks must also be specified; they are finite for a fixed record.
This is a hand count, not an enumeration.

Crucially, (4) depends on `d`, not just `L`.
The source says two **order-`d`** blocks. It does not say two fixed-size fine blocks.
It proves finiteness for a fixed record and depth.
It does not prove a uniform finite list of complete prescribed-slot patterns at fixed `L,M`.
An induction with finitely many interface types could still work.
It needs an invariant on prescriptions crossing the interface, in addition to (3).

### 4.2 Written case split

Classify a slot by its role in the retained equations, before choosing a pivot.
An equation already consumed by a chart is not inserted again.
Old and fresh values at the same bond remain different slots.
These conventions are Y 75766–75816 and 75934–76014.

Substitute its original inverse graph in that factor.
There is no extra prescribed target and no extra Haar volume.
This is exactly Y 76263–76296. It is the test-only case.

**Case 2: the prescribed slots are distinct targets in one first outgoing stock.**

Two different double blocks do not read each other's selected middle links.
At fixed common nonpivots, the inverse kernels therefore multiply.
The price is `B_L^n` for `n` such rows.
For a restored box use its entire first-stock chart instead.

It then matches the other rows to their private face links.

These are Y 75438–75589 and 134982–134993.

**Case 3: a prescribed first-stock target is the next row's middle link.**

Read the old inverse at the prescribed middle value.

This is the two-level port, Y 134995–135019. Its containment and freedom clauses are essential.

**Case 4: a generated middle link is integrated, without a separate prescription.**

The source's composition formula is Y 76160–76189 and 76488–76494.

**Case 5: the proposed extra equation is redundant.**

This is Y 76249–76261.

## 5. R-B: endpoint gauges

### 5.1 The available result is a forest result

On an unsliced Haar stock, separate rooted tree links and nonroot site gauges.
The Haar Jacobian is one, SA 7660–7681.
The averaging map is covariant on every branch, Y 75339–75346.
In the source's convention an outgoing row is

\[
 W(c)=g_{c_-}^{-1}F_c(\widehat V)g_{c_+}.
 \tag{8}
\]

For a forest of prescribed rows, solve each child gauge after its parent.
Haar translation gives Jacobian one per row.
This exact argument is already Y 135592–135637.
It requires each child site's full link star in the free stock.
All other prescribed data must survive the change, and all weights keep their reconstructed arguments.
It is stronger than choosing an isolated extra gauge without checking the other reads.

The check proposes a pseudoforest as a sufficient count.
That count is insufficient for gauge variables alone.
Here is the source's cycle obstruction, Y 135638–135645, written for four rows.
Let `c_1,...,c_4` form a square, oriented cyclically.
At fixed quotient field write `F_j=F_{c_j}(Vhat)`.
Multiplying (8) cancels the three intermediate gauges and gives

\[
 W_1W_2W_3W_4
     =g_0^{-1}(F_1F_2F_3F_4)g_0.
 \tag{9}
\]

There are four rows and four sites.
Assigning each row to its terminal site uses each site once.
Nevertheless, after three solves, (9) remains.
At `F_j=1`, it is the exact relation `W_1W_2W_3W_4=1`.
Its product-Haar measure is zero: at each first three targets, it fixes the fourth to one value.
Thus the four gauge variables alone cannot provide four independent Haar target densities.

This is not an SJ counterexample.
SJ permits the relation in (9).
When the quotient fields also vary, however, (9) imposes a constraint on their holonomy.
The reference integral must still bound that constrained quotient measure at the prescribed targets.
Calling it a graph or choosing a nonproduct reference measure does not prove this bound.

The source supplies a repair for some cycles, Y 135648–135687.
Give each nonforest row a free private middle link in the quotient stock.
The forest paths miss those pivots, so the remaining row solves are diagonal.
For the conflict under study, those middle links are precisely the coordinates that may be prescribed.
The port therefore retains its freedom condition. It does not resolve that conflict.

There are two meanings of “at most one conflicted row per site.”
As an incident-row statement, it is not a consequence of four-dimensional lattice geometry.
For example, an interior site of the restored comb has two adjacent direction-one rows with tree-fixed middle links.
This is the actual comb described at Y 75475–75522.
As an assignment statement, a cycle can satisfy it and still leave (9).
The necessary all-history assertion is a forest solve, or an additional bound for every remaining quotient constraint.
No such assertion for the unresolved prescribed-spectator stock has been established here.

### 5.2 Fixed-centre weights do not have the needed invariance

Bare Wilson weights and plaquette norms are invariant under a site gauge.
That does not establish invariance of every retained weight with the background and inner data fixed.
The source explicitly says its shell and last-shell tests are not gauge invariant, SA 17585.
Its sharp collar guard has the fixed-centre form

\[
 V_j=e^{ia}S,\qquad \chi(a)=\mathbf1_{\{\max_b|a_b|<\delta'_j\}},
 \tag{10}
\]

at Y 65084–65110.
The failed sharp guard is retained separately, Y 65436–65450.

The invariance failure can be checked within the small logarithmic chart.
Set `S=1` and use a commuting one-parameter subgroup at a site `x`.
Take the incident link values from a pure gauge of angle `delta'_j/2` at `x`.
Every incident logarithm has magnitude `delta'_j/2`, and all plaquettes equal one.
An additional site gauge of angle `delta'_j` changes those magnitudes to `3delta'_j/2`.
The sharp guard changes from one to zero.
Both configurations remain below the approximate threshold `2delta'_j` on these links.
The centre was held fixed throughout.

This refutes invariance of the fixed-centre factor, not SJ on a completed history.
Transporting `S` as well would restore covariance.
It would change the fixed data unless the permitted joint gauge action also transports those data.
The source's lifted gauge argument does check such actions, Y 75352–75436.
It fixes coarse centres for the ordinary draw and is the identity near retained slices and drop-outs.
A gauge fixing the row's coarse endpoint cannot supply the proposed endpoint pivot there.

One could bound some noninvariant test factors by one before the change.
That is a different majorization argument and must retain the admission factors for its charts.
It still creates neither a missing gauge variable nor a bound for (9).

### 5.3 A retained tree cannot supply a fresh endpoint gauge

The “no tree slice at that site” condition is essential and is not true for all stopped stocks.
On a restored slice, an interior direction-one link can be fixed by the box comb.
An independent gauge at one of its endpoints moves it off that slice.
The source gives an explicit law comparison at Y 75466–75473.
With identity exterior and identity minimum, the cutoff keeps an interior raw average within `C_L epsilon_i` of identity.
An independent old endpoint Haar gauge instead makes that average Haar distributed.
These measures differ whenever this small ball is proper.
The old gauge cannot therefore be adjoined as a free pivot on that fibre.

CA-I 157076, (h9), does not justify this addition.
It preserves integrals of gauge-invariant functions on first-class components.
A prescribed raw output is not such a function.
CA-I 157069–157075 also explicitly retains the singular slice on second-class components.
The proper first outgoing treatment remains the restored-box chart of §4, not an extra Haar gauge.

**Failure of R-B.** The forest port works on its stated stocks.
On the full SJ class, site freedom and fixed-data weight invariance are not automatic.
Even granting them, a one-row-per-site matching can leave the quotient constraint (9).
The proposed gauge route supplies no bound for that constraint when its private pivots are prescribed.
These obstructions concern gauge orbits and the retained measure, rather than the row Gram estimate of H-E Route 2.
