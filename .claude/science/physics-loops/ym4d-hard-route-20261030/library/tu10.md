## 1. Check of Claude's Lemma N

The moment estimate is correct. Checked line by line by Codex; first failing step in that estimate: none. Its application is conditional on the ultraviolet lower law.

Credit: Ethan Sussman and Levent Alpoge, with Claude and Codex as authors of their text. `SA` denotes their `YM4_sketch_500.tex`; `Y3` denotes `YM3/YM3_I.tex`, at the commit above. Their `RP O(d) trick (RP1)/RPM1.tex`, lines 722–729, states exactly the exponential hypothesis used here. Claude supplied Lemma N in `t0-skeleton.md`, Section 2.

Write \(v_4=\pi^2/2\), \(Y_\pm=F(f\pm h)\), and \(u=d^{-2}\). Initially take smooth real tests, \(0\le f,h\le1\).

1. The pointwise inequality \(e^y+e^{-y}-2\ge y^2\) gives
   \[
   \mathbb E Y_\pm^2\le u^{-2}
       (\mathbb E e^{uY_\pm}+\mathbb E e^{-uY_\pm}-2).
   \]
2. Both signs satisfy \(\|f\pm h\|_1\le2v_4d^4\) and \(\|f\pm h\|_2^2\le4v_4d^4\). Thus each exponential moment is at most \(e^{6Cv_4}\), since \(d\le1\).
3. Hence \(\mathbb E Y_\pm^2\le B d^4\), where \(B=2(e^{6Cv_4}-1)\). Linearity and polarization give
   \[
   4\mathbb E F(f)F(h)=\mathbb E Y_+^2-\mathbb E Y_-^2,
   \qquad |\mathbb E F(f)F(h)|\le Bd^4/4.
   \]
4. Jensen, applied at \(\pm t f\), gives
   \( |\mathbb E F(f)|\le C(\|f\|_1+t\|f\|_2^2)\).
   Let \(t\downarrow0\). Each mean is \(O(d^4)\); their product is \(O(d^8)\). The connected value is therefore \(O(d^4)\).

RPM1 initially defines \(F\) on Schwartz tests. Smooth shapes suffice for the contradiction. For \(C_c^1\) tests, apply step 1 with \(u=(\|v\|_1+\|v\|_2)^{-1}\) when \(v\ne0\). Each exponential moment is at most \(e^{2C}\), giving
\[
\mathbb E|F(v)|^2\le 2(e^{2C}-1)(\|v\|_1+\|v\|_2)^2.
\]
Smooth approximation therefore defines the required extension in \(L^2(\mu)\).

For unit-height shapes in TU9, all cross-distances are at most \(10d\). Thus
\[
\iint f(x)h(y)|x-y|^{-8}\,dx\,dy\ge10^{-8}\vartheta_*^2.
\]
The **assumed** positive law SA 6953–6971 then gives a lower bound \(c[\log(1/d)]^{-2}\). This contradicts the preceding \(O(d^4)\) bound. Indeed, with \(t=\log(1/d)\), \(t^2e^{-4t}\to0\), directly from the exponential series.

This confirms Lemma N's exclusion of RPM1 **for a field satisfying that lower law**. It does not establish the lower law or exclude RPM1 for every proposed limit. The sentence about NT2 needs NT2 to imply this lower law for the same scalar observable; GOAL's general NT2 wording alone does not supply that identification. Read S17 with this conditional scope. Neither this check nor the failure of RPM1 supplies TU10.

## 3. A counterexample to the source/flow implication

The following bounded source model satisfies the displayed D1 estimates and E1 recursion, but its short-distance covariance does not vanish. It is **not Wilson theory**. It tests inference from those analytic clauses alone, not a Wilson-specific estimate or the mirror theorems.

Use D1's tori \(\ell=2L^m\), \(M=L^{m_0}\), and cubes \(Q\) of side \(\rho_s=ML^{-s}\), \(0\le s\le K\). Put independent fair signs \(\xi_{s,Q}\in\{-1,1\}\) in these cubes. With \(a=L^{-K}\), set
\[
q_{s,Q}(W)=\left(\frac a{\rho_s}\right)^4
       \sum_{y\in Q\cap a\mathbb Z^4}W(y),\qquad
F_K(W)=\sum_{s,Q}\xi_{s,Q}q_{s,Q}(W).
\tag{2}
\]
Half-open cubes contain exactly \((\rho_s/a)^4\) points. Hence \( |q_{s,Q}(W)|\le\|W\|_\infty\). Independence gives
\[
Z_K(W)=\mathbb E e^{F_K(W)}
 =\prod_{s,Q}\cosh q_{s,Q}(W)
 =\exp\sum_{s,Q}e_{s,Q}(W),\quad e_{s,Q}=\log\cosh q_{s,Q}.
\tag{3}
\]
Take \(r=1/4\). On the ordinary \(2r\) ball, \(|q|<1/2\). The power series gives \(|\cosh q-1|\le\sum_{j\ge1}4^{-j}=1/3\). The logarithm series therefore gives \(|e_{s,Q}|\le1/2\), with \(e_{s,Q}(0)=0\).

Choose \(U=V=0\). Equation (3) proves ST5, with any positive \(C_\pm\), and \(Z_K(0)=1\). Each domain is one cube, so \(m(Q)=0\), \(Q^+=Q\), and \(\operatorname{diam}Q=2Ma_s\). A radius-\(u\) ball meets at most \(C(1+u/\rho_s)^4\) cubes. There are exactly \((\ell/\rho_s)^4\) cubes globally. Counts with \(m\ge t>0\) vanish. Thus both ST6 and the stronger rooted counts hold, with a strict entropy gap and no extra domain types. All source terms are local and representation independent. The bounds hold even for arbitrary bounded sources, so they include the mirror profiles. The infinite product state is periodic under \(M\mathbb Z^4\).

Independently take \(b_j=b>0\), \(B_\sharp=\lambda=b\), and \(\beta_K=g^{-2}+b(K+1)\). The entire recursion \(x_j=\beta_K-bj\) meets the first-passage threshold at \(K\). It satisfies every displayed E1 flow bound, with \(C=0\), independently of torus and unused cutoff. In particular,
\[
g_K(d)^4=[g^{-2}+b(s(d)+1)]^{-2}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
\]

Now let \(d_n=\rho_n/16\). Inside one level-\(n\) cube, place two unit-height bumps at centers separated by \(3d_n\). Use

\[
f(x)=\phi((x-c_f)/d_n),\quad h(x)=\phi((x-c_h)/d_n),
\qquad \phi(u)=(1-|u|^2)_+^2.
\]
They belong to TU9: their support distance is \(d_n\); \(\operatorname{Lip}\phi\le4\); and \(\int\phi\ge9v_4/256>1/12000\). Both balls fit inside the cube.

For \(a\le d_n/4\), each cube of side \(d_n/2\) centered at \(c_f,c_h\) contains at least \((d_n/(4a))^4\) lattice points. On those cubes the corresponding bump is at least \(9/16\). Consequently
\[
q_{n,Q}(f),q_{n,Q}(h)\ge c_*:=\frac9{16}\,64^{-4}>0,
\qquad
\operatorname{Cov}(F_K(f),F_K(h))
 =\sum_{s,Q}q_{s,Q}(f)q_{s,Q}(h)\ge c_*^2.
\tag{5}
\]
Choose \(K\ge\max\{n,s(d_n)+n_0\}\) also large enough for this lattice count. As \(n\to\infty\), (4) and (5) contradict any uniform \(C g_K(d_n)^4\) bound.

The sign is also absent from the source clauses. Keep only scale zero, and insert a factor \(+1\) on each cube's left half and \(-1\) on its right half in (2). All bounds above persist. Bumps on opposite sides of a cube's middle plane have strictly negative covariance at arbitrarily small separation. No sign claim about Wilson theory follows from this example.

Thus a Wilson-specific relation between source response and the running coupling is indispensable to this proposed proof. Naming an E1 flow beside a D1 bound cannot replace it.
