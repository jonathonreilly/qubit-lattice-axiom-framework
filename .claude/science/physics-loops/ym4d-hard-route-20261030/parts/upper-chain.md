### 0. Model and notation

Dyadic lattices. a = 2^-n, L = 2^M, Lambda = (aZ/LZ)^4, |Lambda| = (L/a)^4. Links carry U_b in SU(2), Haar probability measure. For a plaquette p, s_p = 1 - (1/2) Tr U_p, which lies in [0,2] and is unchanged by orientation or base point. W = sum over the 6|Lambda| plaquettes of s_p. The law is d mu_beta = Z(beta)^-1 exp(-beta W) prod dU_b.

Field. e_a(x) = (1/4) sum of s_p over the 24 plaquettes with corner x, so sum_x e_a(x) = W. It is invariant under the hypercubic group fixing x. Field normalization: O_a(x) = a^-4 (e_a(x) - <e_a>). Smeared: O_a(f) = a^4 sum_x f(x) O_a(x) = sum_x f(x)(e_a(x) - <e_a>), for f continuous with compact support.

Sources. For a complex plaquette function sigma, G(sigma) = < exp(sum_p sigma(p) s_p) >. A test function f gives the source sigma_f(p) = (1/4) sum over the corners x of p of f(x), so |sigma_f| <= ||f||_inf, and G(sigma_f) = <exp(sum_x f(x) e_a(x))>. G is entire on any finite lattice.

Block weights. For a closed cube B of side ell = 2^j a with corners in ell Z^4, and a plaquette p in B, w_B(p) = 2^-c(p), where c(p) counts the coordinates mu in which p lies in a hyperplane x_mu in ell Z. W_B = sum over p in B of w_B(p) s_p. Each p lies in exactly 2^c(p) closed blocks, so sum_B W_B = W. For a dyadic box the same holds with side lengths ell_mu.

### 1. Reflections and the chessboard estimate

**Lemma 1.1 (site-plane RP; Osterwalder and Seiler for the Wilson action, proof written here).** Let c in aZ. The map r: x_mu -> 2c - x_mu fixes the two planes x_mu = c and x_mu = c + L/2. Proof. Every plaquette lies in one closed half, because a link never crosses a site plane. Let Y be the links on the two planes, X_+ and X_- the remaining links of the two halves. F) = int g(Y) | int F(X_+,Y) h(X_+,Y) dX_+ |^2 dY >= 0. No sign of beta(p) is used. []

**Lemma 1.2 (chessboard; method of Froehlich, Israel, Lieb and Simon, proof written here).** Let ell = 2^j a and L = 2^m ell in each direction, and let B_k, k in (Z/2^m)^4, be the closed blocks. For k, k' let r_{k',k}: B_{k'} -> B_k be the composition of block-face reflections, translation in coordinates where k'-k is even and reflection where it is odd. Proof. One direction. Write the product as G_+ G_- over the two arcs cut by the planes 0 and L/2. Cut it by the planes L/4, 3L/4. The half [L/4, 3L/4] contains the slabs of the outer quarter of G_+ and their mirror images, the other half the inner quarter and mirror images. Cauchy-Schwarz again gives two patterns, each built from a single quarter and its reflections. After m steps each pattern is one slab function and all its mirror images, with total exponent 2^-m on each of the 2^m patterns. For the four directions apply this in turn: in direction 1 take the slab function to be the product over the other three block indices. Each resulting pattern is again a product over block indices in direction 2. []

**Lemma 1.2' (even quotient, positive functions).** [Derived; the method is that of Froehlich, Israel, Lieb and Simon.] If L = 2 n ell in each direction with n >= 1 any integer, the bound of Lemma 1.2 holds for F_k > 0. Proof. One direction, slab functions. Let A be a finite set of positive functions on slab 1, moved to slab i by F^[i]. ### 14. Attack on the hardest inequality: can local response come from constant coupling?

Notation. V_k = M^k(U) is the level-k blocked field of any gauge-covariant hard-constraint blocking (for example hard-core--independent Section 1), G_k its sigma-field, a_k = 2^k a in the dyadic count. For a bounded weight f on sites, y_k(f; W) = E[ e(f) | V_k = W ] - <e(f)>. For a closed block B, y_k(B; W) uses W_B in place of e(f). T_k(W) = E[W | V_k = W] - <W> = -d/dbeta log rho_k(W; beta) + const: this is the only local-response object that constant coupling provides.

**The inequality (TR_p), named.** For f with |f| <= 1 and Lip(f) <= 1/l, supported in a cube of side l >= a_k: || y_k(f; .) ||_{L^p(mu)} <= A_p (l/a_k)^4. In words: the conditional mean of a smeared energy, given the coarse field at a scale finer than the smearing, has no contact term. The contact term a^-4 and the gradient term a^-2 sit entirely in the conditional fluctuation, which is removed by the conditional mean. By Theorem 14.1 below, (TR_p) with a decay statement is a fourth producer of (SM). It is real, first order in the source, and on the torus.

**Theorem 14.1 (torus producer of the separated bound).** [Derived.] Fix f_1..f_n with supports at mutual distance at least delta, and k with n_0 a_k <= delta/5. Let B_i be disjoint non-touching cubes around the supports at distance at least n_0 a_k from them. Suppose

- (TR_n) above, and
- (DEC_n) || E[ O_a(f_i) | G_k v sigma(U on dB_i) ] - y_k(f_i) ||_{L^n} <= D_n.

Then | E prod_i O_a(f_i) | <= prod_i ( ||y_k(f_i)||_n + D_n ), uniformly in a, L and positions.

Proof. Theorem 6, smeared form: E prod_i O_a(f_i) = E prod_i psi_i with psi_i = E[O_a(f_i) | U on dB_i]. By the tower property psi_i = E[ Pi_i | U on dB_i ], with Pi_i the conditional mean in (DEC_n). Conditional expectation is a contraction on L^n, so ||psi_i||_n <= ||Pi_i||_n <= ||y_k(f_i)||_n + D_n. Hoelder finishes. []

So no Dirichlet RG is needed. With a_k ~ delta/(10 n_0) this gives (SM) with M_n ~ (A_n v delta^-4)^n, sup norm and Lipschitz norm of the test, and it delivers the linear field, not only exponentials. No complex source, no exponential source, one insertion at a time. What (DEC_n) asks is conditional decay given the coarse field, which a massive fluctuation measure should supply.

**Theorem 14.1b (assembly by total cumulance; no boundary, no decay of the coarse measure).** [Derived.] Fix f_1, ..., f_n with supports at mutual distance at least delta, and a level k. kappa_I( e(f_i), i in I ) = sum over partitions pi of I of kappa^mu( kappa_B(V_k) : B in pi ),

Proof. Write the source as sum t_i sigma_i with t_i^2 = 0 and X = log rho_k^w(V_k), so that log G = log E exp(X(t) - X(0)) in that ring, and X(t) - X(0) = sum_B t^B Y'_B with Y'_B = kappa_B(V). **20.2 Three exact lemmas.** CR(Z) denotes Z(a, b) Z(a', b') / (Z(a, b') Z(a', b)), and sup |log CR(Z)| runs over all quadruples. [Derived; exact. `checks/upper-chain_crossratio.py`, C1 to C3.]

**Lemma 20.1 (positive averaging, constant one).** Let Z > 0 on X x Y, let P, Q be positive kernels, and Z-bar(x-bar, y-bar) := int P(x-bar, dx) Q(y-bar, dy) Z(x, y). Then sup |log CR(Z-bar)| <= sup |log CR(Z)|. Consequently: (a) if Z(a, b) = sum_(i,k) w_i(a) w'_k(b) Z_ik(a, b) with w, w' >= 0, then |log CR(Z)| is at most the supremum of the cross-ratio of Z_ik over the enlarged variables (a, i), (b, k); (b) if 0 <= Z_x <= tau Z_0, then |log CR(Z_0 + Z_x)| <= sup |log CR(Z_0)| + 2 log(1 + tau).

Proof. The numerator of CR(Z-bar) at (x-bar, x-bar', y-bar, y-bar') is the integral of Z(x_1, y_1) Z(x_2, y_2) against P(x-bar, dx_1) P(x-bar', dx_2) Q(y-bar, dy_1) Q(y-bar', dy_2). The denominator is the integral of Z(x_1, y_2) Z(x_2, y_1) against the same positive measure. The ratio of integrands is a cross-ratio of Z. (b): each of the four factors moves by at most 1 + tau, upward. []

This is why positivity is worth keeping: F20.1 is what happens without it.

**Lemma 20.2 (two slabs).** Let Z(a, b) := int rho(dx, dy) K_1(a, x) K_2(y, b), with rho any positive measure on the middle pair, and e_i := sup |log CR(K_i)|. Then |log CR(Z)| <= 4 artanh( sinh e_1 sinh e_2 exp(e_1 + e_2) ), which is at most 7 e_1 e_2 for e_i <= 1/4.

Proof. Fix base points: K_1 = u(a) v(x) f_a(x), f_a := exp(E_1(a, .)), with |E_1| <= e_1, and K_2 = u'(b) v'(y) g_b(y) likewise. By Grüss's inequality, |Cov(f, g)| <= (1/4) osc f osc g, so |c| <= sinh e_1 sinh e_2 exp(e_1 + e_2). In CR the factors u, u', E f, E g cancel exactly, and what remains is a sum of four terms +-log(1 + c). []

This is Birkhoff's projective contraction (credit Birkhoff and Hopf) in the form useful here. **20.6 Routes tried at this stop, and why they stopped.**

1. *Birkhoff contraction directly on (MD_j).* The cross-ratio is the projective quantity. But a slab kernel has diameter Delta of order x |face| at small thickness, and tanh(Delta/4) is about 1 - 2 exp(-Delta/2), so there is no useful contraction per slab at large surface. It becomes useful only once eps is already small: Lemma 20.2.

2. *A positive induction on relative densities, uniform in the exterior.* Since r_(j+1)(w, z) = int lambda_z(d eta) Phi(w; z, eta) with a measure not depending on w, the ratio r_(j+1)(w_1, z) / r_(j+1)(w_2, z) lies between the infimum and the supremum over eta of Phi(w_1)/Phi(w_2). With Lemma 20.1 this gives the cross-ratio of r_(j+1) across a regular shell, at most that of (MD_j), exactly, and it reduces a rough exterior to a regular one wherever a regular shell separates them. **It stalls at sets attached to a frozen rough field:** a comparison of two fields with a rough cross-section in common needs a lower bound for r_j at a rough field, and a trial ball around a rough field needs a Lipschitz bound on the block energy uniform in the level. The only bound I can prove, exact rotation of the crossing links, since Mean(R g_i) = R Mean(g_i), grows like L^3 per level, so the entropy is k log L per link against x_k eps_0^2, which loses at coarse levels. I believe a uniform bound holds, by regularity of the minimiser below a rough cell, over about log(1/eps_0) / (2 log L) levels. Not written. No longer needed.

3. *Classical no wetting, by hole filling.* At level 0 it is a fact, and the argument is short. Let W be regular on a ball of radius rho about c, the exterior arbitrary, and beta_t the energy of V relative to V^* on layer t, S_t := sum_(s <= t) beta_s. Replacing V by V^* inside radius t, with a glue on layer t, gains S_t up to terms in beta_t, and costs at most C_G beta_t, where C_G is of order L^3 / eps_0^2, since a rough plaquette on the layer costs at most 2 and has energy at least eps_0^2 / 2. If no t in [rho/2, rho] gains, S grows geometrically, against S_rho <= C rho^4. So for rho >= C_G log(rho/eps_0), a field with a rough plaquette at c can be lowered by a fixed delta, and since A is Lipschitz, P(c rough | exterior) <= exp(-x_0 delta/2) (C |T| / delta)^(n |T|). 4. *Dobrushin-Shlosman, a finite-size seed.* If sup P(c rough | any exterior at distance rho) <= 1/(2 rho^4), then sequential conditioning gives absolute rarity and no crossing, and complete analyticity (credit Dobrushin and Shlosman) gives ratio mixing for every boundary.
