Conclusion: the campaign has narrowed the C3 algebraic routes to a new
same-surface physical orientation/basepoint/orbit-member theorem selecting a
nontrivial physical line with accepted backend/projectors and matrix elements,
or accepted strict pole rows. It does not close the coefficient row.
The existing reflection axis cannot serve as that theorem; it selects the
singlet member, and rotated axes are new basepoint imports.
The current branch also does not contain a hidden accepted strict pole-row
packet under another response/backend/projector artifact name, so the strict
route requires new pole-row data or a new accepted backend/projector theorem.
An explicit scalar orientation-bias term does not provide the missing physical
section either; it only shifts the selected C3 orbit. The non-scalar
source-response extremal route also fails: the maximum readouts choose the
singlet and the minimum readouts import the missing selector. A positive C3
route now needs a physical basepoint/readout theorem beyond source-response
extrema, or the campaign must move through accepted strict pole-row
data/backend-projector matrix elements. The strict W/Z plus C3 splice route
does not bypass that: it remains a contract check until same-surface splice,
physical top-line/projector, and pole-row controls are accepted.
The new block-support result weakens the C3 coefficient requirement from
"choose `P_omega` or `P_omega2`" to "exclude `P_0`." That is a real
simplification, but it remains an open physical top-block membership law, not
positive retained closure.
