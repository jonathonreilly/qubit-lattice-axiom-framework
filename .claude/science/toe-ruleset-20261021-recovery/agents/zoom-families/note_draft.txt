## Question

Zoom first: read a 2x2x2 block of CL-1 cells as one coarse site. Does the coarse slab kernel get coarse zone corners
with Wilson-type structure, and does the coarse 'Wilson mass', fixed by the fine eps and copy mass Mc rather than chosen,
fall in a window with a three-node turn triplet? If so, is that window forced?

Repo first. Builds on wall-families (cell slab: D, B, eps algebra, at most two opposite-handed copies; the Wilson triplet
window (2,4) is unforced), generation-rungs (fine corners fold into a coarse cell in orbits 1, 3, 3, 1 with triplets

## Results

- A1 the coarse Floquet fibre equals the union of the eight fine fibres (2e-15). Zoom is a relabelling: it cannot create
  a kinetic zero, a node or a light state that the fine walk lacks.
- A2 |D(K)|^2 = (2/3) sum(1 - cos K_i) on all eight cell modes (1e-15). The cell kinetic is already doubler-free: it
  vanishes only at K = 0. Under zoom by any b, D_c(Q) vanishes only at Q = 0, since fine K = 0 has only that image. The
  coarse X, M, R floors are 0.8165, 1.1547, 1.4142, in the ratio 1 : sqrt2 : sqrt3, and never zero. A Wilson corner has
  a kinetic zero with a mass ladder 1 : 2 : 3. The coarse corners have neither.
- A3 the cell slab rebuilt on the 64-site block (Ls = 12, Mc = 1) has no zero mode at any coarse X, M or R for m in
  {0.5, 1.5, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 5, 7}. At Q = 0 the (s+, s-) counts are (1,0), (1,1), (0,1), then 0, exactly the fine windows.
  There is no triplet window at any layer mass. A3b: the lone copies are s+ +1 and s- -1, with the far wall opposite.
- A4 the Floquet gap at the coarse corners is 0.53 to 0.82 eps over the scan. The coarse corners are step-heavy.

## Answer

Zoom forces no family window. (1) Coarse zone corners: exact blocking cannot make a kinetic zero, and the cell kinetic
has none off K = 0. So the coarse X, M, R have no Wilson structure, and the coarse slab holds at most the two fine copies
at any m. (2) The Wilson-type structure is real but sits one rung down, at the fine zone corners, which zoom folds into
the coarse site as 1 + 3 + 3 + 1 internal Dirac multiplets. The tick fixes their masses as an affine ladder in n, with
the R mass exactly |2 eps - Mc|. (3) A triplet alone changes its mass sign only for a copy mass in an interval of width
about mu: here (2 eps + mu, 2 eps + 3 mu), bounded below by the M crossing near 2 eps + mu/2 and containing the X
crossing near 2 eps + 2 mu. That interval is outside CL-1's gapped regime (Mc < 2 eps, B2), its width is set by the
species mass angle (relative width ~ mu/eps, about 1e-20 or less for physical species, an estimate), and Mc is supplied. Inside
CL-1's regime no corner inverts, and the X triplet sits between the light copy and the M triplet, at mass
mu + (delta - mu)/3. Its splitting from the light copy is of order the step unless Mc is tuned to 2 eps. Not forced.

Cleanest remaining family source: the fine-corner 1 + 3 + 3 + 1 ladder inside one coarse site. It is forced in form,
equal in speed and turn-labelled, so a RULE that fixes Mc relative to 2 eps (for example, a copy mass set by the step
itself) would turn it into a framework-native family candidate. But the affine ladder always gives 1 + 3 (Gamma + X)
as the lightest four, not 3, and the turn-CP lemma still gives J = 0 for turn-invariant masses. The other open routes
are unchanged: the two-tiling parent-cube triplet (two-tiling-family-space B) and stacked copies (families-mod-3).

## Gate (anti-smuggling)

No rule proposed. Choosing Mc in (2 eps + mu/2, 2 eps + 2 mu) to isolate the triplet would be a fitted choice and
FAILS the gate. Prediction not built for (UNTESTED): a copy-mass domain wall that crosses Mc = 2 eps binds the R corner
first, so any wall content from the copy mass begins as a singlet, never a triplet.
