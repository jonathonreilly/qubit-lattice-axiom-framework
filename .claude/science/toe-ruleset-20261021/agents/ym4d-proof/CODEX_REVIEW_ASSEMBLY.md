### R13 — Why the common OS algebra is the highest-risk bridge

**Verdict: CONFIRMED** for the non-implication from a flowed one-point statistic to an invariant of the reconstructed theory; **NOT-VERIFIED** for the exact HINT2 witness beyond the quoted contract. **Claim state: GAP.** Attachments: `ASSEMBLY55.md:390–400` (F1); consumer `PANEL_P1.md:63` (`survives the physical OS quotient`).

Here is an independent contact/noise model of the failure mechanism. Add independent Gaussian white noise η of covariance cδ, c>0, to a Gaussian Euclidean field. At strictly positive and reflected negative times the added cross-covariance vanishes. For the noise alone, positive-half polynomials have OS form ⟨F,G⟩=conj(EF)·EG, because opposite open half-spaces are independent; its physical Hilbert space is one-dimensional. In the Gaussian field, Wick-ordered reflection forms are sums of products of cross-covariances, so the added contact covariance contributes no new physical one-particle space or Fock excitations.

Nevertheless a four-dimensional heat-smoothed quadratic one-point statistic changes by c‖K_t‖², where K_t(x)=(4πt)^{-2}exp(−|x|²/(4t)). Direct integration gives ‖K_t‖²=(4πt)^{-4}(2πt)²=1/(64π²t²). Multiplying that statistic by t² leaves a nonzero constant shift c/(64π²) in theories with the same reconstructed Gaussian dynamics. Fixed positive flow time also mixes both sides of the reflection plane. This model is not asserted to be Yang–Mills; it proves why an explicit OS-compatible observable identification is indispensable. A calibrated Euclidean statistic cannot replace that theorem by its name.
