## 2. A weaker large-field input that the consumer can use

### 2.1 An exact split and an open, testable insertion lemma

The normalized soft chain is the positive probability law

    dP = Z^(-1) exp(-S_0(U_0)) prod_(i<K) K_i(U_(i+1),U_i) dU,
    integral K_i(V,U) dV = 1.                                 (1)

Thus a bare observable has exactly the same expectation in this chain.
No hard-block/soft-block identification is used. For each target k choose
a fixed defect classification through level k, and a nonnegative partition

    chi_reg + sum_(j=0)^k sum_x chi_(j,x) = 1.                 (2)

The rule must be specified from the actual RG histories, independently of
the numerical value of B. For example, assigning each bad history to its
first failed cut in a fixed ordering gives a measurable disjoint partition
on a finite torus. This example proves only existence of a partition,
not locality or the estimates below. More useful local allocations are
allowed. Returned failures, frozen failures, and failed returns must all
be included; renaming a returned defect does not remove its insertion.

Define positive **observable-weighted** quantities

    S_(K,R,k) = E[B(t_(K-k)) chi_reg],
    M_(K,R,k,r) = sum_x E[B(t_(K-k)) chi_(k-r,x)],  0<=r<=k.

Then, exactly,

    f_(K,R)(t_(K-k)) = S_(K,R,k) + sum_(r=0)^k M_(K,R,k,r).    (3)

All spatial counts and distances are inside the sum over x. There is no
unpaid volume factor or implicit independence assumption in (3).

**F17-SF-B — OPEN regular-sector input.** For an explicit partition (2),
`S_(K,R,k) <= C_sf/u_k`, with C_sf independent of K,R,k on the required
trajectory and domain. This is bare-to-RG observable transport; a bound
on the auxiliary coarse plaquette alone does not prove it.

**F17-WINS-B — OPEN weighted insertion lemma (preferred sufficient input).**
For that same partition and actual normalized chain there are nonnegative
numbers w_r with `W=sum_(r>=0) w_r<infinity` and a nonincreasing function
`H(v)->0` as v->infinity such that

    M_(K,R,k,r) <= H(u_k) w_r,                                (4)

uniformly in admissible K,R,k,r. The constants may depend on N,L,kappa
and the fixed reference trajectory, but not on cutoff or volume. One
concrete target is `w_r=(1+r)^(-1-delta)`, delta>0, and, for large v,

    H(v) = C exp[-c (log(v)/2)^(2p)],   c>0, p>0.             (5)

Equation (4) is a source estimate, not an ordinary defect-probability
estimate. It permits weak per-step probability tails if the actual
response of the observable is correspondingly small. Proving it requires
the spatial sum, all histories and their normalizers; assuming that an
RG Markov kernel contracts these insertion weights is not a proof.
For signed polymer expansions an absolute majorant with these properties
would suffice, but the signed activities cannot be called probabilities.

The **minimal two-scale version**, F17-WINS-B(2), needs (2), F17-SF-B and
only the following aggregate bound at one fixed m_1>m_0:

    limsup_(n->infinity) sum_r M_(K_n,R_n,K_n-m_1,r)
          <= A/2 - C_sf/U_-(m_1),                            (6)

where the right side is nonnegative and (K_n,R_n) is the same sequence
as SEQ. A bound uniform in all sufficiently fine K and all R>=R_0 is
a stronger sufficient version. Condition (4) is a scale-resolved way to
obtain (6); (6) alone makes no all-grid UV claim. This explicitly states
the irreducible LF expectation budget after regular-sector control,
rather than relabeling the full TS-up bound as a probability estimate.

### 2.2 Library lemma F17-2S — PROVEN conditional reduction

Assume F17-SF-B and F17-WINS-B, the trajectory and lower bound of §1,
and W_T0's common-system, moment and sharp-time hypotheses. Then

    f_(K,R)(t_m) <= C_sf/U_-(m) + W H(U_-(m)).                (7)

The right side tends to zero. Choose a fixed m_1>m_0 making it <=A/2.
The coarse mean is at least A. Uniform integrability at these two
fixed labels, supplied by MOM2-B, and their joint convergence on SEQ
pass both inequalities to the same limiting state. ALG-B and SHARP-B
identify those limiting means with the specified vacuum-vector pairings.
Consequently

    W_B = [f(t_0)-f(t_1)]_+ >= A/2,
    W_B(mu)=0 for every mu in F_B,
    max_(i=0,1) |f(t_i)-f_mu(t_i)| >= A/4.                   (8)

Indeed, the two mean differences have sum of absolute values at least
`(f(t_0)-f(t_1))-(f_mu(t_0)-f_mu(t_1)) >= A/2`.
This proves the finite witness conclusion by the same OS identification
as W_T0. If only (6) is assumed, the same argument works using the
limsup at that fixed m_1, but does not give the all-grid UV conclusion.
With (4) at every label, (7) also gives that conclusion. No moving flow
time is passed through the cutoff limit. QED.

This is weaker than requiring any prescribed power TS-up. For example,
`H(v)=1/log(2+v)` suffices for (7), yet is not O(v^(-alpha)) for any
alpha>0. For the particular logarithmic tail (5), every p>0 suffices
for two-scale separation. Full power TS-up follows from (7) if p>1/2;
at p=1/2 it follows with any `0<alpha<=min(1,c/2)` (after enlarging
constants on bounded v ranges). For 0<p<1/2 this envelope alone does
not yield a positive power. The often-quoted condition c>2 at p=1/2
concerns the stronger `H=o(g_k^2)` requirement, not an arbitrary
vartheta<1 or the two-scale witness.

### 2.3 Why a probability exponent is not necessary — PROVEN logical examples

There is no implication from small `E[B 1_E]` to a particular rate for
P(E) without a positive lower bound on B on E. To avoid relying on the
degenerate example B=0, fix v>=e and b=beta log L>0, put u_(k-r)=v+br,
and take independent Bernoulli variables I_r of probabilities

    P(I_r=1) = exp[-(log(v+br))^2].

Set `Y_r=2^(-r-1) v^(-alpha) I_r`, alpha>0, and B=sum_r Y_r,
truncating the sum at each finite cutoff. Its LF mean is at most
v^(-alpha), and B<=v^(-alpha) pointwise, so all its moments are bounded.
Each nonzero contribution is positive on its event, but these probabilities
violate every bound `C exp(-a u_(k-r))`, a>0, uniformly in r. The ratio
has logarithm `a(v+br)-(log(v+br))^2-log C -> infinity`.
Even `Y_r<=L^(4r) I_r` holds. Thus a worst-case amplification upper bound
and a weak probability tail do not force an instanton rate. The family
can equally be viewed as an additive weighted defect decomposition;
a disjoint assignment only reallocates its total weighted mass.

These are countermodels to an implication between estimates, **not
Yang–Mills configurations or claims about the actual survival events**.
In particular no theorem here says that YM violates LF-inst. Necessity
for YM would require additional geometric or dynamical information.
If one instead proved `B>=b_0 L^(4r) 1_(E_(j,k))`, then TS-up would imply
`P(E_(j,k))<=C b_0^(-1) u_k^(-alpha) L^(-4r)` for each r.
That lower-amplitude premise has not been supplied, and even this
individual estimate does not imply a strict `c_LF beta_->4` law in u_j.

## 3. Even the amplification model admits a weaker exponential input

Assume, explicitly, that the LF contribution has the upper bound

    sum_(j<=k) E Y_(j,k) <= C_0 sum_(j<=k) L^(4(k-j)) P_(j,k), (9)

and that (F) holds. The events, spatial multiplicities, and domination
(9) are independent missing inputs for the flowed observable. They are
not inferred from HINT2's remembered instanton survival/shrinking claims.
In particular, (9) is not an established 3D magnetic-flow theorem.

With `P_(j,k)<=C exp(-a u_j)` and `a beta_->4`, summing the geometric
series gives

    LF <= C_0 C exp(a R_F) exp(-a u_k)
                        / (1-L^(4-a beta_-)).               (10)

The additive-flow factor is retained. Thus LF-inst is sufficient for
this first-moment step **conditional on (9) and regular transport**.

**Library lemma F17-CRIT — PROVEN.** The following strictly weaker
exponential-rate condition also suffices:

    a=4/beta_-,   s>1,
    P_(j,k) <= C exp(-a u_j) (1+u_j)^(-s).                  (11)

Proof. Put v=u_k, b=beta_- log L>0, r=k-j and
`r_0=ceil(2R_F/b)`. By (F), after multiplying the exponential in (11)
by L^(4r), its value is at most `exp(aR_F) exp(-av)`.
For r>=r_0, `u_(k-r)>=v+br/2`; for the finitely many r<r_0,
`(1+u_(k-r))^(-s)<=1`. Monotone integral comparison gives

    sum_(r=0)^k (1+u_(k-r))^(-s)
      <= r_0 + (1+v)^(-s) + 2(1+v)^(1-s)/(b(s-1)).           (12)

Equations (9), (11) and (12) bound LF by a constant times exp(-av),
uniformly in depth and volume. This is O(v^(-alpha)) for every fixed
alpha>0. QED. With a regular bound C_sf/v it supplies TS-up for
0<alpha<=1.

Strictness is explicit: on the exact linear trajectory u_(k-r)=v+br,
probabilities equal to the right side of (11), with C=1, obey (11)
but no `C' exp(-a' u_j)` with a'>4/beta_-. The ratio is
`exp((a'-a)u_j)/(1+u_j)^s -> infinity`. Independent Bernoulli events
and `Y_(j,k)=L^(4r)1_(E_(j,k))` realize (9) with equality and finite
first moments. This proves failure of strict-exponent necessity even
within that amplification model. It does **not** furnish MOM2-B.

The number 4 is specific to the postulated amplification. If a proved
transport estimate replaces it by sigma, the corresponding strict
geometric threshold is `a beta_->sigma`, with the analogous critical
prefactor. Merely replacing 4 by 3 because B lives on a spatial slice
is unjustified: neither its event count nor its response was derived.

## 4. The obstruction that the available raw prices really establish

**Library lemma F17-COUNT — PROVEN obstruction to a specified method.**
Consider the raw estimate

    Q_k = sum_(0<=r<=floor(k/2)) L^(4r) exp[-q(u_(k-r))].     (13)

Keep m fixed and k=K-m->infinity. The tuned endpoint u_k is bounded
above by U_+(m), even if it is not exactly constant. Assume q>=0 is
nondecreasing and q(v)=o(v). At r=floor(k/2), (F) gives

    log(term) >= 4r log L
                - q(U_+(m)+R_F+beta_+ r log L) -> infinity.  (14)

In particular `q(v)=c(log(v)/2)^(2p)`, any fixed p>0, fails by this
method. A fixed number n of paid defects per term replaces q by nq,
still o(v). Polynomial prefactors do not repair this exponential growth.
For the fixed-cut form q(v)=a_eff v, directly

    Q_k >= exp[-a_eff(U_+(m)+R_F)]
           sum_(r<=floor(k/2)) L^((4-a_eff beta_+)r).         (15)

This diverges when `a_eff beta_+<=4` (linearly in the equality case).
Hence choosing only one short physical time **does not fix** the raw
counting problem: the range of finer scales is still unbounded as K grows.

A divergent upper estimate is not a lower estimate for YM. Its exact
logical force can nevertheless be proved: on independent Bernoulli
variables with probabilities exp[-q(v+beta r log L)], the observable
`sum_r L^(4r) I_r` has mean exactly the failed counting expression.
Independence even gives product Peierls bounds for arbitrary finite
sets. Therefore these probability bounds plus amplification upper
control, by themselves, cannot imply the desired uniform expectation.
The bounded weighted construction in §2.3 on the same probability law
gives the opposite outcome. A missing observable-response estimate,
not an algebraic manipulation of those probabilities, must distinguish
the two. These countermodels prove an information obstruction within
this estimate class; they do not model the complete gauge RG.

The scope matters. A larger paid cluster size with
`n a_eff beta_->4+c_shadow` can beat a fixed-cut history entropy.
For a sublinear price q(u), a level-dependent n(u) of order u/q(u)
can also change the comparison. Neither possibility is excluded here.
It remains necessary to prove that the relevant surviving insertion is
actually charged that many defects, including every small failed return,
and to control all remaining returned insertions. RLF's large-cluster
partition-function result alone makes no such statement about B.

## 6. Instanton action and slope conventions — PROVEN normalization

Take components normalized by `-tr(T^a T^b)=delta_ab/2` and set

    S = (1/(4 g_std^2)) integral F^a_(mu nu) F^a_(mu nu),
    Q = (1/(32 pi^2)) integral F^a_(mu nu) tilde F^a_(mu nu).

The elementary identity `integral(F∓tilde F)^2>=0` gives
`S>=8pi^2 |Q|/g_std^2`; a self-dual or anti-self-dual field with
|Q|=1 saturates it. Thus the charge-one instanton action **8pi^2/g^2**
uses the standard continuum g=g_std. In this lane it is

    S_inst = 8pi^2/g_std^2 = (4pi^2/N) u,
    u=beta_Wilson=g_lane^(-2)=2N/g_std^2.                    (16)

The coefficients beta_± in (F) are slopes of u per log scale; they are
not beta_Wilson. If the stated universal one-loop slope is supplied,

    beta_1=11N^2/(12pi^2),
    (4pi^2/N) beta_1=11N/3>4  for N>=2.                    (17)

This is conditional arithmetic, not a derivation of the beta function.
For the actual lower bracket, (10) with the instanton coefficient needs
`beta_->N/pi^2`; an arbitrary positive slope is insufficient. Equations
(16)–(17) neither prove an integer-charge decomposition of rough lattice
fields nor turn a classical action into a normalized probability tail.
Entropic factors, determinants, boundary costs, and flow survival still
need bounds. No instanton shrinking or persistence assertion is used.
