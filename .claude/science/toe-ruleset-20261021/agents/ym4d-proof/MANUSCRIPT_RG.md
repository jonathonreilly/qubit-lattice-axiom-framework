## 4. The proofs

### 4.1 Exact normalization and the common backward measure — PROVEN

For every input, int K_j(V,U)dV=1. Positivity and Fubini therefore imply
int rho_j(V;J)dV=Z_0(J). The zero-source composite density is positive
and smooth in every terminal link. The latter assertion follows by
differentiating the last smooth kernel on a finite compact group, before
any parameter-dependent hard selection is made.

For an integrable path function F, put

    B_j F(V)=E_P[F(U_0,...,U_j) | U_(j+1)=V].

Explicitly its numerator is int F exp(-u_0 W) product_(l<=j)K_l and
its denominator is rho_(j+1)(V;0). This is the same denominator, not a
product of bare kernels or Z_A. For 1<=p<infinity, conditional Jensen gives

    ||B_j F||_(L^p(r_(j+1))) <= ||F||_(L^p(P_j)),             (B1)

and the corresponding L^infinity statement follows from conditional
averaging. For nonnegative complete past-history masses, sum_h B_j I_h=1;
more generally sum_h |B_j(I_h F)|<=B_j|F|. No mass is lost by splitting
histories provided it is not divided out and subsequently spent as a
rare-event price. This establishes normalization, not locality of B_j F.

### 4.2 The SU(2) Gamma domination — PROVEN

Let t>=0 and let M be any actual SU(2) arithmetic mean. At tr=0, X=0.
Otherwise quaternion rotation makes m/r the first coordinate. With
s=tr, Haar's first-coordinate density is (2/pi)sqrt(1-v_0^2) on [-1,1].
Under the kernel, X=s(1-v_0) has density proportional to

    x^(1/2) exp(-x) (1-x/(2s))_+^(1/2),  x>=0.             (G1)

Let Y have Gamma shape 3/2 and rate 1. Formula G1 is its law tilted by
the nonincreasing nonnegative function w_s(x)=(1-x/(2s))_+^(1/2).
For every nondecreasing nonnegative f,

    E[f(Y)w_s(Y)] <= E[f(Y)] E[w_s(Y)].                    (G2)

Indeed, for independent Y,Y', the product
(f(Y)-f(Y'))(w_s(Y)-w_s(Y')) is nonpositive; integrate and divide by two.
Truncation justifies unbounded f. The denominator E[w_s(Y)] is positive.
Consequently X is stochastically dominated by Y. In particular, for
0<=b<1 and q>=0,

    E X^a <= Gamma(a+3/2)/Gamma(3/2), a>=0,
    E exp(bX) <= (1-b)^(-3/2),
    E[exp(bX) 1_(X>=q)]
       <= Gamma(3/2,(1-b)q)/(Gamma(3/2)(1-b)^(3/2)).        (G3)

Gamma(a,z) is the upper incomplete Gamma function. The endpoint a=0
uses X^0=1, including X=0. For a>0, X=0 contributes zero. The formula
also covers q=0. None of G1--G3 contains delta as if it were a gap.

A separate integral derivation checks the moment bound. Put

    A(s)=int exp[-s(1-v_0)]dHaar=exp(-s) 2 I_1(s)/s,

with its value 1 at s=0. In the x=1-v_0 integral, substitute
y=(1-b)x in A((1-b)s). Since
sqrt(2-y/(1-b))<=sqrt(2-y) on the shorter integration interval,

    A((1-b)s)/A(s) <= (1-b)^(-3/2).                       (G4)

This uses the same exact kernel but a different integral comparison from
G2. The Bessel identity follows either by integrating the S^3 Haar
coordinate or differentiating its elementary class-angle integral.

For any deterministic finite spacetime set C of distinct kernel links,
any input probability law, and any nondecreasing f_c>=0,

    E_P product_(c in C) f_c(X_c)
          <= product_(c in C) E f_c(Y_c),                 (G5)

where Y_c are independent Gamma(3/2,1). Integrate the last selected
layer first: conditional on the earlier fields its output links are
independent and G2 is uniform in those fields. Remove that layer and
repeat. Truncation handles infinite moments. All unselected output
kernels integrate to one. This proves G5 at every finite volume and
depth, including arbitrarily rough inputs. An alternative check couples
successive gap CDFs to independent uniform random numbers and uses the
quantile order in G2.

G5 applies to a disjoint history sum with a common envelope, because
sum_h I_h=1 before integration. It does not assert G5 under every
individually normalized history or under a fixed terminal output.

The output-independent normalizer has zero output derivative. For an

    P_0(x)=1,

### 4.4 Mixed input/output jets, including the normalizer — PROVEN

First suppose r=|m|>=r_0>0. Freeze an evaluation point m_*,v and the
unit vector n=m_*/|m_*|. For nearby fine parameters y and output

    q(y,z;v)=t[m(y) dot v(z)-m_* dot v-(m(y)-m_*) dot n],

The exact identity is

    k_(t,m(y))(v(z))/k_(t,m_*)(v) = exp(q(y,z;v))/D(y).   (J3)

Thus the reference normalizer and all its fine derivatives are present.
The centering vector n is fixed during differentiation at the evaluation
point; it is not a differentiable polar field that must be transported.

(ell h_f)^a, by distributing the derivative positions among its factors.

    a_1(x)=2 sqrt(kappa) max(ell/sqrt(r_0),R) sqrt(x),
    a_r(x)=2 kappa [sqrt(2) max(ell,R)]^r, 2<=r<=6.      (J4)

    p_r=E P_r(Y),  c_0=1,

    Q_r(x)=sum_(l=0)^r binom(r,l) P_l(x)c_(r-l),
    Q(x)=sum_(r=0)^6 (2^r/r!) Q_r(x).                  (J5)

    T_6(k) <= k Q(X),  when |m|>=r_0.                  (J6)

This is uniform in u, volume and the fine input on that mean class.

Let G_x=1_(E_x>=a)+epsilon, epsilon>0. It is mapped to the corresponding
cube function by each site reflection. For a subset S define
R(S)=E product_(x in S)G_x /(E product_all G_x)^(|S|/n), n=|Lambda|.
Cauchy--Schwarz across any pair of antipodal planes gives
R(S)<=sqrt(R(S_+)R(S_-)), where each S_sign repeats one half by
reflection and |S_+|+|S_-|=2|S|. At a maximum of R over subsets both
reflections are maximizers. A maximal cyclic run of equal transverse
slab patterns of length l<2m can be extended to length at least
2 min(l,m)>l by reflecting at its endpoint and retaining that half.
Iterate in each direction; already constant directions stay constant.
A constant maximizer is either empty or full, and both have R=1.
Therefore R(S)<=1. Let epsilon decrease to zero. This proves the
chessboard inequality for these overlapping closed-cell events; it
does not assume link independence between cells.

On the disseminated event, W>=a n, so its unnormalized integral is at
most exp(-ua n). For the denominator, place every link in an operator
ball of radius u^-1/2 about I. The plaquette action is at most 8/u, so
its total exponent is at most 48n. A radius zeta Haar ball in SU(N)
has mass at least (1+4N/zeta)^(-2N^2): partition the real and imaginary
matrix entries into cubes of side at most zeta/(2N), pick a group point
in each occupied cube, and use their radius-zeta ball cover and Haar
invariance. There are at most (1+4N/zeta)^(2N^2) cubes. For N=2 and
4n links this gives Z_0>=exp[-d_2(u)n]. Take the nth root to prove W1.
This proof is valid on even compatible tori with the oriented arcs
retained at minimal side lengths.

Next, a ribbon with at most A_L plaquette occurrences compares each
paired transporter T_gamma to the straight transporter S_c. Telescoping
and Cauchy--Schwarz imply

    1-h_(M_c)(S_c) <= sum_p W_(c,p) f_p,
    W_(c,p)>=0,  sum_p W_(c,p)<=A_L^2.                 (W2)

Explicitly W_(c,p) is the average of n_gamma n_(gamma,p), where
n_gamma counts ribbon occurrences and n_(gamma,p) counts those of p.
All repeated occurrences are retained. Also
1-|m_c|<=1-h_(M_c)(S_c). Hence |m_c|<1/2 implies some ribbon plaquette
f_p>=1/(2A_L^2), and its lower-corner cube has E_x>=1/(8A_L^2).
There are at most 2L^4 possible lower corners, all in the two endpoint
blocks of c. A fixed lower corner belongs to at most eight such
two-block regions: the eight coarse links incident on its containing
block. These statements also count parallel periodic edges separately.

For m distinct coarse links, choose one witness cube for each short
mean. Every choice uses at least m/8 distinct cubes. By a union bound
and W1,

    mu_0(|m_c|<1/2 for all c in C)
       <= [(2L^4) q_u(1/(8A_L^2))^(1/8)]^m.           (W3)

The bound may exceed one; that is harmless. Let

    C_good=E Q(Y) from J5 at r_0=1/2,
    C_birth=2 L^4 exp(6) 9^4 [320 A_L^2/e]^5.

The last constant is a proved, deliberately conservative bound:

    sup_(u>=1) u^3 (2L^4) q_u(1/(8A_L^2))^(1/8)
      <= 2L^4 exp(6) 9^4 sup_(u>0) u^5 exp[-u/(64A_L^2)]
      = C_birth.                                      (W4)

Use (1+8 sqrt(u))^4<=9^4 u^2. For a selected output link write
J_c(U)=int T_6(k_c)dV_c. J6--J8 give
J_c<=C_good+C_rough u^3 1_(|m_c|<1/2). Expand the product over any
set C of distinct selected kernel links, integrate in the *same*
original Wilson law, and apply W3 to each chosen subset. The result is

    E_(mu_0) product_(c in C) J_c(U_0)
       <= (C_good+C_rough C_birth)^|C|.                 (W5)

Conditional independence of the output links shows that W5 is also the
joint integrated product of their full mixed jet densities. Fine readers
may overlap; no independence of fine fields was assumed. If multiple
insertions demand repeated powers at a link, J5/J8 and the tail count
must be redone at that total order. W5 does not spend that cost twice.
Original source insertions require their actual derivative bounds and
reader sets; W5 by itself is a kernel theorem, not the (6,4) RG theorem.

W5 covers the mean-degenerate branch for this normalized first-step
calculation. Extending W3 to the inherited marginal at arbitrary depth
would require U1/U2, not another application of bare Wilson chessboarding.

### 4.6 What normalization does not allow — PROVEN diagnostic

At the actual fine configuration U=I, every paired mean is M=I. At
the output V_c=-I in SU(2), X_c=2t. Thus exp(bX_c) grows exponentially
in t at this retained point, although its integrated expectation obeys
G3. By continuity, an open cap about -I still has X_c>=t; it has positive
kernel mass at every finite t. Normalizing the kernel on that cap makes
its exponential moment at least exp(bt). The cap's exponentially small
mass was divided out. The same configuration can be embedded in the
actual finite lattice; fine and output neighborhoods have positive
Wilson-chain mass. This is not a claimed counterexample to the target,
since the target is allowed to retain and charge that mass.

The mixed input proof has a second sharp scope boundary. At a zero mean
the law is Haar, and a nonzero fine variation of the mean can have a
weighted score of order sqrt(u). J8 retains this cost. G3 does not
bound fine jets merely because the gap is then identically zero.

### 4.7 Direct attack on the normalized backward covariance — PROVEN / GAP

For a bounded function F of the earlier fields only and a terminal
weighted link derivative D_c, differentiate the exact quotient B_j F
from section 4.1. Fixed compact domains justify the differentiation.
Writing s_c=D_c log k_j(V_c;M_c(U_j)), one obtains exactly

    D_c B_j F=E_pi(F s_c)-E_pi F E_pi s_c.             (C1)

If F also explicitly reads V, add E_pi(D_c F). Formula C1 includes
the full denominator derivative. In particular no small-tilt or
product-reference identity is needed for this algebra.

For SU(2), J2 and G3 give, under the complete joint chain,

    E s_c^2 <= 4 kappa R^2 E X_c <= 6 kappa R^2.       (C2)

Here sqrt(u_j/u_(j+1))<=R is the specified weight comparison, not a
derived flow theorem. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, then ordinary
Cauchy--Schwarz and total variance, give the uniform actual estimate

    int |D_c B_j F| r_(j+1)dV
       <= sqrt(6 kappa) R sqrt(Var_P(F)).              (C3)

Indeed E Var(F|V)<=Var(F) and E Var(s_c|V)<=E s_c^2.
For a disjoint complete past-history family F_h=I_h F, with I_h
measurable through U_j and not a new selector depending on V, a useful
absolute estimate is

    sum_h int |D_c B_j F_h| r_(j+1)dV
       <= 2 sqrt(6 kappa) R ||F||_(L^2(P_j)).          (C4)

To prove it, use |Cov(F_h,s_c|V)|<=E(|F_h||s_c||V)
+E(|F_h||V)E(|s_c||V), sum the identity sum_h |F_h|=|F|,
and apply Cauchy--Schwarz to each term. This is a sum of integrated
history costs under one reference, not a sum of separately normalized
history probabilities. It preserves original source insertions provided
their actual L^2 norm is used.

**GAP:** C3--C4 have no distance from the support of F to c. Summing
these bounds over terminal links introduces their number. They therefore
do not prove U1 or a rooted jet norm. For c!=d, forward conditional
independence and int D_c k_c=0 imply E_P(s_c s_d)=0, but C1 involves
posterior centering and products depending on V. This orthogonality
cannot be inserted into C1 as a spatial decay estimate.

The concrete next inequality is a complete-reader decomposition of C1
with an absolutely summable spatial tail, combined with a paid rough-core
alternative. Higher derivatives produce connected posterior cumulants
and differentiated scores; they require the same locality, not just more
applications of Cauchy--Schwarz. No such all-history bound has been
established here. The fixed nonlinear sector in section 4.11 has the
separately proved narrower covariance estimate.

### 4.8 A different large-field definition, and its remaining defect

For a fixed set C of current output links define the actual posterior
moment M_C(V)=E_pi exp(b sum_(c in C)X_c), 0<b<1. This is finite and
positive at every finite regulator. G5 and conditional expectation give

    int M_C(V) r_(j+1)(V)dV <= (1-b)^(-3|C|/2).

Thus for any a>0,

    nu_(j+1)(M_C>exp(a|C|))
       <= exp[-(a+(3/2)log(1-b))|C|].                 (P1)

This is **PROVEN**, uniform in the incoming law and depth. It suggests
using posterior moments rather than a pointwise small tilt to define a
good class. It is not a solution: M_C generally reads every retained V
through the backward normalization. P1 supplies neither a local selector
nor the joint rooted sum over different C nor the tensor extraction.
Declaring its reader set to be C would violate the support definition.
The localization of M_C reduces back to the covariance issue in C1.

### 4.9 The actual flat conditional Hessian and its Gaussian inverse — PROVEN

This section concerns the bare first step, N=2, output V_c=I and
t=kappa u. It is a calculation on the actual normalized action, not on
a kernel with its normalizer omitted. Write U_b(lambda)=exp(i lambda
A_b dot sigma), A_b in R^3. For a contour gamma let a_gamma be its
signed linear sum of A_b. Define

    (QA)_c=average_gamma a_gamma,
    ||RA||^2=sum_c average_gamma |a_gamma|^2.

R includes the averaging weights in its Euclidean norm. A product of
SU(2) exponentials gives, for each mean quaternion,

    m_0=1-lambda^2 average|a_gamma|^2/2+O(lambda^3),
    m_vec=lambda (QA)_c+O(lambda^2),
    |m|=1-lambda^2[average|a_gamma|^2-|(QA)_c|^2]/2
                                                        +O(lambda^3).

The scalar second term follows by expanding the product: paired cross
terms combine to the square of the signed sum, even when the A_b do
not commute. Let phi(t)=log Z_t(I) and alpha(t)=phi'(t)=E_(k_(t,I))v_0.
Expanding -t m_0+phi(t|m|), with the plus sign on the normalizer, gives

    D^2 S_cond(0)[A,A]
      =u ||dA||^2+t alpha(t)||QA||^2
                           +t(1-alpha(t))||RA||^2.       (F1)

This Hessian is for the action relative to Haar. The log Haar Jacobian
in exponential coordinates adds a positive diagonal 2/3 per real
coordinate at zero; it has not been silently included in F1.
Since 1-alpha(t)=E X/t<=3/(2t) by G3, for t>=3 one has alpha>=1/2.
Also 0<=alpha<=1: the upper bound is v_0<=1, and the lower follows by
pairing v_0 and -v_0 in the Haar integral when t>=0. Thus

    u^-1 D^2 S_cond(0) >= d^*d+(kappa/2)Q^*Q.             (F2)

Fine gauge transformations which equal I at the block roots leave every
coarse kernel unchanged. Fix a spanning tree inside each block, rooted
at its center, and set its links to I by these transformations. One exact
choice gives every nonroot site a parent by moving one step toward the
root in the first coordinate where it differs. Distance to the root
decreases, so this is a tree. Haar invariance gives independent Haar
variables on all remaining links and on the tree gauge transformations;
the latter integrate to one. There is no Faddeev--Popov determinant in
this tree parametrization and the retained V is unchanged.

At the linear level this sets A=0 on those tree links. Write the resulting
restriction of the right-hand operator in F2 as H_T^0. It has a uniform
positive lower bound, proved as follows. Fourier transform only in block
translations. A fiber has 4L^4-(L^4-1)=3L^4+1 free link coordinates per
colour, and Bloch momentum k in [-pi,pi]^4. Its matrix H_T^0(k) is a
continuous Hermitian matrix, since every factor has finite range.

The exact paired-path identity on gradients is

    Q(d psi)_mu(X)=psi(c_(X+e_mu))-psi(c_X).             (F3)

It follows by telescoping every contour, before averaging. A closed
Bloch one-form with k!=0 is a gradient: diagonalize the elementary
translations, whose symbols are exp(i p_mu)-1; if one is nonzero,
the vanishing curl makes the vector proportional to that gradient
symbol. At k=0 the only additional closed forms are constant link
forms a_mu. For a constant form Q a=L a.

If the quadratic form H_T^0(k) vanishes, dA=0 and QA=0. For k!=0,
write A=d psi. F3 and at least one nontrivial exp(i k_mu)-1 force the
root value psi(c_X)=0. Vanishing tree links then force every site
value in that block to be zero. For k=0 write A=a+d psi; QA=L a,
since the root values are periodic with one block. Thus a=0, and the
tree condition makes psi constant on the block, so A=0. Every fiber
is positive definite. Compactness proves that the *defined* constant

    g_T(L,kappa)=min_(k in [-pi,pi]^4) lambda_min(H_T^0(k)) > 0      (F4)

is independent of the number of blocks. No sampled eigenvalue is used
as its value. Real finite tori use subsets of these momenta, so inherit
the same bound. The three colours are identical independent quadratic
copies at the flat point.

For an explicit upper bound, a contour has at most ell occurrences and
a fine link can be read by only the eight coarse links incident to its
containing block. Cauchy--Schwarz along the contours gives
||R||^2<=8ell^2 and ||Q||^2<=8ell^2. Also d^*d<=16 in four dimensions.
Therefore the restriction H_T(t)=u^-1 D^2 S_cond(0) satisfies

    g_T I <= H_T(t) <= M_T I,
    M_T=16+8 kappa ell^2,    t>=3.                      (F5)

It connects blocks at graph distance at most two. Expand
H_T^-1=M_T^-1 sum_(n>=0)(I-H_T/M_T)^n. For sets of blocks A,B at
distance d, the terms with 2n<d vanish between their projections.
The remaining operator-norm geometric series gives

    ||1_A H_T^-1 1_B|| <= g_T^-1
                     (1-g_T/M_T)^(ceil(d/2)).           (F6)

The zero-ratio endpoint, if g_T=M_T, is interpreted as a diagonal
inverse. Thus the Gaussian with action u<A,H_T A>/2 has a
volume-uniform covariance bound u^-1 times F6. Adding the positive
Haar Hessian 2/3 I has the same argument, with upper constant
M_T+2/3 for u>=1. This establishes the spatial inverse estimate for
the correctly normalized *flat quadratic* reference. It does not
establish the covariance estimate for the nonlinear inherited law.

### 4.10 Nonlinear coercivity on an actual fixed bare sector — PROVEN

Keep the exact block-tree gauge just described. Every remaining fine
link has a unique coordinate U_b=exp(i A_b dot sigma) for |A_b|<a<pi.
Every tree link is I. Retained outputs are V_c=exp(i B_c dot sigma),
|B_c|<b. The integration domain is the fixed product of these fine
coordinate balls. It does not depend on B. This is a positive sector
of the actual gauge-fixed bare integral, with its full complement
still present in the full RG transfer.

First a uniform normalizer derivative bound is needed; a crude t^3
estimate would give the wrong perturbation scale. For r>0 set
F_t(m)=phi(t|m|). At s>0, radial cumulants of v_0=1-X/s and G3 give

    |phi'(s)|<=1,
    |phi''(s)|<=15/(4s^2),
    |phi'''(s)|<=147/(4s^3).                            (F7)

For the last bound, use
|cum_3(X)|<=E X^3+3(E X)(E X^2)+2(E X)^3,
with moments 3/2, 15/4 and 105/8. These are derivatives with respect
to s at the fixed Haar variable v_0, not derivatives treating X_s as
a parameter-independent variable. The cumulant identity justifies
the substitution at each s.

For the Euclidean norm r, ||Dr||<=1, ||D^2r||<=1/r and
||D^3r||<=6/r^2; the last bound follows from its three bilinear
terms and its term 3 n tensor n tensor n. The chain rule yields

    ||D F_t||<=t,
    ||D^2 F_t||<=t/r+15/(4r^2),
    ||D^3 F_t||<=6t/r^2+48/r^3.                        (F8)

On a fine coordinate cap with ell a<=1/2, telescoping gives
||M-I||<=ell a, hence r>=1/2 on every segment from 0 to A. Ordered
Frechet derivatives of a matrix exponential of an anti-Hermitian
matrix obey the norm-product bound by its simplex integral. Thus
||D^q M||<=ell^q for q<=3, in fine directions of maximum link norm
one. Applying F8 and the chain rule once more to the exact kernel
action -t h_M(V)+F_t(m) gives

    ||D_A^3(-log k_t)|| <= ell^3(429+32 kappa) u,
    ||D_B D_A^2(-log k_t)|| <= kappa u ell^2,           (F9)

for u>=1. The second derivative involves only the numerator since
the normalizer is output-independent. The constants in the first
bound are 48/(1/2)^3+45/[4(1/2)^2]=429 and
6/(1/2)^2+3/(1/2)+2=32; no fitted coefficient is used.

A fine link occurs in six Wilson plaquettes. Each plaquette reads at
most twelve real link coordinates and its third derivative is bounded
by 4^3 u in maximum-link-norm directions. Each fine link occurs in
at most eight coarse-kernel factors; a kernel reads at most 8L^4 fine
links, or 24L^4 real coordinates. Entrywise interpolation from the
flat point, followed by the row-sum bound for a symmetric matrix,
therefore proves the following volume-independent operator bound
for the action relative to Haar:

    ||Hess_A S(A;B)-Hess_A S(0;0)||
        <= u (D_T a+E_T b),
    D_T=4608+192 L^4 ell^3(429+32 kappa),
    E_T=192 L^4 kappa ell^2.                           (F10)

Here 4608=6 times 12 times 4^3. Restricting to the non-tree
coordinates cannot increase the bound. It is deliberately loose.

Choose, in this order, L,kappa, then g_T from F4, then

    0<a<=min(1/(2ell),g_T/(4D_T)),
    0<b<=min(1/2,g_T/(4E_T)),
    u>=max(1,3/kappa).                                (F11)

F2, F4 and F10 show Hess_A S(A;B)>=u g_T I/2 on the
whole fixed convex product domain. The Haar density in each free
coordinate is a constant times (sin|A_b|/|A_b|)^2. Its negative log
has tangential eigenvalue 2(1/r-cot r)/r and radial eigenvalue
2(csc^2 r-1/r^2), both positive for 0<r<pi, with limit 2/3
at zero. Hence including every Haar Jacobian preserves the bound:

    Hess_A [S(A;B)-sum_b log J_Haar(A_b)]
                       >= (u g_T/2) I.                (F12)

This proves strict convexity of the actual normalized bare-sector
potential (normalizing by its partition function changes no A
derivatives), uniformly in volume and u in F11. Constants and cap
sizes are fixed before taking u large. No shrinking cap at each
iteration has been introduced.

The limitations are decisive. This is the first-step Wilson density,
on a tiny identity sector in one exact gauge. It supplies no bound on
the sector's mass, its complement, a rough exterior, nontrivial flat
backgrounds, or the inherited interaction at later steps. A covariance
representation needs its boundary domain; the next section supplies
one on a fixed rectangular subsector. U1--U4 remain **GAP**.

### 4.11 A local normalized covariance on a fixed nonlinear subsector — PROVEN

Inside the cap of F11 use the fixed rectangular domain

    Omega_box=product_(free links b, colours a)
                            (-a_cap/sqrt(3),a_cap/sqrt(3)),

where a_cap is the a of F11, and denote its normalized actual
bare-sector measure by nu_B. Its potential relative to Lebesgue measure
is V=S(A;B)-sum_b log J_Haar(A_b). The boundary is independent of B.
All means, Wilson terms, kernel normalizers and Haar factors are those
of the original integral on this sector. Let g=g_T and set

    J_T=27+8 kappa ell^2+D_T a_cap+E_T b,
    eta=(1/2)log(1+g/(4J_T))>0.                         (L1)

Then for smooth real F,G with gradients supported in block sets X,Y,

    |Cov_(nu_B)(F,G)| <= [4/(u g)] exp(-eta d(X,Y))
                   ||grad F||_(L^2(nu_B)) ||grad G||_(L^2(nu_B)).  (L2)

The distance is the periodic nearest-neighbour block distance. The bound
is independent of volume and of B in its declared cap. Complex functions
follow by the same sesquilinear argument. The claim includes the true
normalization of this sector only; multiplying back by its unnormalized
mass is a separate step.

Here is a proof with the hard boundary specified. The Hessian H=Hess V
is at least u g/2 by F12. Its entries vanish between coordinates more
than two blocks apart. The flat Wilson Hessian has absolute row sum
at most 24; the two kernel Gram matrices together have row sum at most
8 kappa ell^2, by the same absolute incidence count used in F5.
The entrywise proof of F10 bounds the perturbed row sums as well.
The Haar Hessian has operator norm at most one for |A_b|<=1/2, so
each of its three-coordinate rows has absolute sum at most three.
For example sin r>=r-r^3/6 bounds its radial eigenvalue by
(2/3)(24/23)^2<1, and its tangential one by 16/23<1.
Consequently the absolute row sums of H are at most u J_T.

Let L=-Delta+grad V dot grad be the scalar nonnegative operator with
Neumann boundary condition on the rectangle. For G of zero mean solve
L psi=G with zero mean and Neumann condition. This finite-dimensional
weighted elliptic problem exists: the density is positive and smooth
on the compact rectangle, so its usual weighted Poincare constant is
finite; the mean-zero variational form is coercive. No volume-uniform
constant is needed merely for this existence step. Differentiation gives

    (L I+H)v=grad G,   v=grad psi.                      (L3)

On a face normal to coordinate i, v_i=0 and the other components have
zero normal derivative. These are, respectively, Dirichlet and Neumann
conditions for the vector equation. There is no outward flux term in
its quadratic form. The identity can first be proved on smooth functions
with these conditions and then in the closed forms. For the scalar
solution, H^2 regularity on a rectangle follows from the Neumann cosine
expansion for -Delta: the bounded smooth drift times grad psi is L^2,
so -Delta psi is L^2. Differentiating weakly gives the vector form and
the stated mixed conditions. Edges of faces introduce no extra flux;
the integration by parts is facewise, and the corresponding mixed forms
are their closures. This avoids rounding a corner into a boundary which
would fail to commute with the spatial weights.

Put w_i=exp(eta d(block(i),Y)) and let W be this constant diagonal
matrix on vector components. These are weights on the coordinate
indices, not functions of the integrated field A. They commute with
L and preserve every mixed boundary condition. If H_ij is nonzero,
|log(w_i/w_j)|<=2eta. Thus both absolute row and column sums of
W H W^-1-H are bounded by

    (exp(2eta)-1)u J_T=u g/4.

The column bound uses symmetry of H and the same ratio bound.
The Schur bound and H>=u g/2 imply that the real quadratic form of
W H W^-1 is at least u g/4. Multiply L3 by W^2 v and integrate.
The L term is nonnegative by the mixed conditions, giving

    ||W v||_(L^2(nu_B)) <= [4/(u g)]
                                      ||W grad G||_(L^2(nu_B)).  (L4)

Since grad G is supported in Y, its weight is one. Neumann integration
by parts gives Cov(F,G)=int grad F dot v dnu_B. On X the inverse
weight is at most exp(-eta d(X,Y)), and L2 follows by Cauchy--Schwarz.

There is a second operator check on the spatial estimate. On the same
mixed vector boundary domains, B=L I+u J_T I has an inverse diagonal
in the component index and norm at most 1/(u J_T). The multiplication
operator C=u J_T I-H has norm at most u J_T-u g/2 and range two in
that index. Thus

    (L I+H)^-1=sum_(n>=0)(B^-1 C)^n B^-1.

The series converges in norm. Between index sets at block distance d,
its first ceil(d/2) terms vanish, and its tail is bounded by
[2/(u g)](1-g/(2J_T))^(ceil(d/2)). This is even stronger than L2.
It uses no claim that L is local in the integrated field variables:
only its diagonality in the vector component index is used. It also
keeps the same mixed boundary domains instead of changing the measure.

This resolves the first covariance test (A) for this fixed nonlinear
bare sector. For a terminal link c let s_c=D_c log k_t with
h_c<=R sqrt(2/u). Its gradient reads only the two endpoint blocks
Y_c and satisfies the explicit bound

    ||grad_A s_c|| <= R kappa ell sqrt(48 L^4 u).        (L5)

Indeed there are at most 24L^4 real fine readers, each derivative is
at most h_c t ell, and the output-independent normalizer contributes
zero to s_c. Differentiating the sector quotient therefore gives

    |D_c E_(nu_B) F|
      <= [4R kappa ell sqrt(48 L^4)/(g sqrt(u))]
             exp(-eta d(X,Y_c)) ||grad F||_(L^2(nu_B))  (L6)

when F has no explicit retained-field dependence. This exponential
tail is summable over c in four dimensions, with a constant depending
on L,kappa and |X| but not on the surrounding volume.

**Scope check.** The source function F must be smooth in the stated
coordinates. An arbitrary hard history indicator is not. Higher
connected derivatives through (6,4), the exact logarithmic expansion,
coefficient extraction and regeneration have not been supplied by L6.
Nor has the mass of Omega_box or its complement been estimated. Thus
L6 supplies a real actual-model part of U1, not its all-history statement.

### 4.12 Hostile test: a negative direction of the full rough conditional action

**PROVEN diagnostic, not a counterexample to paid RG return.** Fix N=2,
any odd L>=3, 0<kappa<4L, and a periodic lattice whose fine side is an
even multiple of L. There are n_0 fine sites and n_1=n_0/L^4 coarse
sites. In one commuting colour direction put

    U_1^epsilon(x)=(-1)^(x_2) exp[i epsilon (-1)^(x_2) sigma_3],
    U_2^epsilon=U_3^epsilon=U_4^epsilon=I,
    V_1(X)=(-1)^(X_2) I,
    V_2(X)=(-1)^o I,  V_3=V_4=I,  o=(L-1)/2.            (R1)

At epsilon=0 this is the actual persistent-flux input and its maximizing
coarse output. All plaquettes in the (1,2) orientation are -I.
The Wilson action along this
path is exactly u n_0(1+cos(2epsilon)); its second derivative is
-4u n_0.

The means can be evaluated without a prescribed-mean replacement.
For direction 1 the translated axial legs cancel, and the middle L
links give the odd-parity sum. In the frame of V_1 the scalar part
and modulus are

    h_1(epsilon)=cos(L epsilon)/L,
    r_1(epsilon)=sqrt[cos^2(L epsilon)/L^2+sin^2(L epsilon)].    (R2)

For direction 2, write s=r_1_coordinate-o for the signed number of
direction-1 steps on the axial leg. Translating by L in direction 2
reverses their phases and changes their signs. Whether coordinate 1
precedes coordinate 2 in sigma changes the parity at which that leg
is evaluated; summing s and -s cancels its imaginary part in either
case. Consequently, in the V_2 frame,

    h_2(epsilon)=r_2(epsilon)
       =((-1)^o/L) sum_(s=-o)^o (-1)^s cos(2s epsilon),        (R3)

where equality to the modulus holds in a neighborhood of zero.
Directions 3 and 4 have identically unit means. These formulas use all
24 paired coordinate orders. The elementary identities
sum_(s=-o)^o(-1)^s=(-1)^o and
sum_(s=-o)^o(-1)^s s^2=(-1)^o o(o+1) give

    h_1''(0)=-L,    r_1''(0)=L(L^2-1),
    r_2''(0)=-(L^2-1)/L.                               (R4)

For each changed kernel the exact action is -t h_i+phi(t r_i),
t=kappa u. All first derivatives vanish at zero. With
alpha=phi'(t/L) in [0,1], the second derivatives of the two kernel
actions are respectively

    t L[1+(L^2-1)alpha],
    t(1-alpha)(L^2-1)/L.                               (R5)

Adding all factors of the *full normalized conditional action relative
to Haar*, and dividing by n_0, yields exactly

    S_cond''(0)/n_0
      =u[-4+kappa{2L^2-1+(L^2-1)^2 alpha}/L^5]
      <=u(-4+kappa/L)<0.                              (R6)

Thus the soft normalizer does not repair the Wilson negative mode in
this parameter range. This tests the full kernel, unlike a bare-action
Hessian alone. It says nothing from this calculation about kappa>=4L.

There is no gauge-coordinate evasion of this diagnostic for the action.
At the central background every first variation of a Wilson trace and

stationary, so a smooth exact root-fixed tree gauge preserves its

it cannot cancel R6 as u tends to infinity. An open neighborhood of

cannot be removed from the task by calling it an impossible history.
No lower bound uniform in volume or u on that neighborhood's mass is
being asserted.
