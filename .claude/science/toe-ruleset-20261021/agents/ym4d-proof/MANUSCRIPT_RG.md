## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Exact chain, sources, and normalization

Let the finite four-dimensional periodic lattices have even compatible side
lengths, with at least two sites in each terminal direction. A link lies in
SU(N), N>=2; Haar has mass one. Put

    f_p(U)=1-Re tr(U_p)/N=||I-U_p||_HS^2/(2N),
    W(U)=sum_p f_p(U),  d mu_0=Z_0^-1 exp(-u_0 W(U_0)) dU_0.

The actual paired-contour arithmetic mean is

    M_c(U)=(24 L^4)^-1 sum_(sigma,y in B(X)) T_y^sigma(U),
    h_M(V)=Re tr(V^* M)/N,
    k_t(V;M)=exp(t h_M(V))/Z_t(M),
    Z_t(M)=int exp(t h_M(W)) dW,
    t_j=kappa u_j,   K_j=product_c k_(t_j).

The same coordinate ordering sigma is used at both endpoints, and the
root is the center of the odd block. Every contour length is at most
ell=5L-4. Its operator norm is one; hence ||M||_op<=1. All normalizers
are retained. All results concern the soft kernel, not a deterministic
coarse observable or a hard block constraint.

Define the complete zero-source path probability

    dP_K=d mu_0 product_(j<K) K_j(U_(j+1),U_j)dU_(j+1).

At depth j let I_h be a disjoint complete partition of histories through
U_j, including absences and all complementary sectors, so sum_h I_h=1.
When a whole finite path is used, its complete partition may extend to
that path's terminal level. A zero child stays a zero summand.
Sources enter through the original fine observables O_i(U_0):
exp(sum_i J_i O_i), with every kernel and u_j frozen under source
differentiation. If a source has been centered, its actual scalar is kept.
Only four fixed labels are used here; this is not an all-order source result.

The exact source density and its history pieces are

    rho_(j,h)(V;J)=int I_h exp(-u_0 W+sum_i J_i O_i)
                         product_(l<j) K_l,  with U_j=V,
    rho_j=sum_h rho_(j,h),  r_j(V)=rho_j(V;0)/Z_0.

Thus r_j is the complete composite marginal. The separate bare comparator
Z_A(V)=int chi_A(U)exp(-u_j W(U))K_j(V,U)dU is not r_(j+1).
Any use of that comparator still has to prove the ratio to r_(j+1).

    T_6(k)=sum_(a+b<=6) sup_|directions|<=1 |D_f^a D_c^b k|/(a! b!).

This is an integrated-density estimate when integrated in V, not a bound

Complete supports contain all input, output, normalizer, source, selector,

### 2.3 The chosen leading reference, separate from the actual density

The proposed induction now fixes its leading action rather than leaving

Minimization over the compact group product preserves continuity, so

normalized-kernel tree exponent, as in FLOW's

Haar terms, fluctuation corrections, scalar recursion and all original

## 4. The proofs

### 4.1 Exact normalization and the common backward measure — PROVEN

For every input, int K_j(V,U)dV=1. Positivity and Fubini therefore imply
int rho_j(V;J)dV=Z_0(J). The zero-source composite density is positive
and smooth in every terminal link. The latter assertion follows by
differentiating the last smooth kernel on a finite compact group, before
any parameter-dependent hard selection is made.

For an integrable path function F, put

    B_j F(V)=E_P[F(U_0,...,U_j) | U_(j+1)=V].

Explicitly its numerator is int F exp(-u_0 W) product_(l<=j)K_l and
its denominator is rho_(j+1)(V;0). This is the same denominator, not a
product of bare kernels or Z_A. For 1<=p<infinity, conditional Jensen gives

    ||B_j F||_(L^p(r_(j+1))) <= ||F||_(L^p(P_j)),             (B1)

and the corresponding L^infinity statement follows from conditional
averaging. For nonnegative complete past-history masses, sum_h B_j I_h=1;
more generally sum_h |B_j(I_h F)|<=B_j|F|. No mass is lost by splitting
histories provided it is not divided out and subsequently spent as a
rare-event price. This establishes normalization, not locality of B_j F.

### 4.2 The SU(2) Gamma domination — PROVEN

Let t>=0 and let M be any actual SU(2) arithmetic mean. At tr=0, X=0.
Otherwise quaternion rotation makes m/r the first coordinate. With
s=tr, Haar's first-coordinate density is (2/pi)sqrt(1-v_0^2) on [-1,1].
Under the kernel, X=s(1-v_0) has density proportional to

    x^(1/2) exp(-x) (1-x/(2s))_+^(1/2),  x>=0.             (G1)

Let Y have Gamma shape 3/2 and rate 1. Formula G1 is its law tilted by
the nonincreasing nonnegative function w_s(x)=(1-x/(2s))_+^(1/2).
For every nondecreasing nonnegative f,

    E[f(Y)w_s(Y)] <= E[f(Y)] E[w_s(Y)].                    (G2)

Indeed, for independent Y,Y', the product
(f(Y)-f(Y'))(w_s(Y)-w_s(Y')) is nonpositive; integrate and divide by two.
Truncation justifies unbounded f. The denominator E[w_s(Y)] is positive.
Consequently X is stochastically dominated by Y. In particular, for
0<=b<1 and q>=0,

    E X^a <= Gamma(a+3/2)/Gamma(3/2), a>=0,
    E exp(bX) <= (1-b)^(-3/2),
    E[exp(bX) 1_(X>=q)]
       <= Gamma(3/2,(1-b)q)/(Gamma(3/2)(1-b)^(3/2)).        (G3)

Gamma(a,z) is the upper incomplete Gamma function. The endpoint a=0
uses X^0=1, including X=0. For a>0, X=0 contributes zero. The formula
also covers q=0. None of G1--G3 contains delta as if it were a gap.

A separate integral derivation checks the moment bound. Put

    A(s)=int exp[-s(1-v_0)]dHaar=exp(-s) 2 I_1(s)/s,

with its value 1 at s=0. In the x=1-v_0 integral, substitute
y=(1-b)x in A((1-b)s). Since
sqrt(2-y/(1-b))<=sqrt(2-y) on the shorter integration interval,

    A((1-b)s)/A(s) <= (1-b)^(-3/2).                       (G4)

This uses the same exact kernel but a different integral comparison from
G2. The Bessel identity follows either by integrating the S^3 Haar
coordinate or differentiating its elementary class-angle integral.

For any deterministic finite spacetime set C of distinct kernel links,
any input probability law, and any nondecreasing f_c>=0,

    E_P product_(c in C) f_c(X_c)
          <= product_(c in C) E f_c(Y_c),                 (G5)

where Y_c are independent Gamma(3/2,1). Integrate the last selected
layer first: conditional on the earlier fields its output links are
independent and G2 is uniform in those fields. Remove that layer and
repeat. Truncation handles infinite moments. All unselected output
kernels integrate to one. This proves G5 at every finite volume and
depth, including arbitrarily rough inputs. An alternative check couples
successive gap CDFs to independent uniform random numbers and uses the
quantile order in G2.

G5 applies to a disjoint history sum with a common envelope, because
sum_h I_h=1 before integration. It does not assert G5 under every
individually normalized history or under a fixed terminal output.

### 4.3 Integrated output-field jets — PROVEN

For any N, any mean of norm at most one, any unit generator H, and
g(z)=h_M(exp(zH)V), one has |g''(z)|<=1. Taylor's lower bound and the
definition of m(M) give

    m(M)>=g(0)+g'(0)z-z^2/2,
    |g'(0)|<=sqrt(2 psi_M(V)).                            (J1)

The choice z=g'(0) is allowed for every real z; no small chart is used.
Higher ordered derivatives of h_M are bounded in absolute value by one,
since they insert products of norm-one generators. For SU(2), t=kappa u,
u>=1 and h_c<=R sqrt(2/u), the logarithmic kernel scores obey

    |D_c log k| <= 2R sqrt(kappa) sqrt(X),
    |D_c^r log k| <= kappa 2^(r/2) R^r, 2<=r<=6.         (J2)

The output-independent normalizer has zero output derivative. For an
ordered r-word, scalar exponential differentiation gives the sum over
partitions of its positions, with order preserved inside each block.
Use b_1(x)=2R sqrt(kappa)sqrt(x) and
b_r(x)=kappa 2^(r/2)R^r for r>=2, and define

    P_0(x)=1,
    P_r(x)=sum_(partitions pi of {1,...,r})
                                product_(B in pi) b_|B|(x).

Then |D_c^r k|<=k P_r(X). All coefficients are nonnegative and all
powers are nonnegative half-integers. G3 therefore bounds
int exp(bX) 1_(X>=q)|D_c^r k|dV by the integral of P_r(Y) against the
same Gamma tail, uniformly in u and in **every** input mean. This is
a full normalized density estimate. It remains valid at M=0, where
the actual output derivatives vanish.

### 4.4 Mixed input/output jets, including the normalizer — PROVEN

First suppose r=|m|>=r_0>0. Freeze an evaluation point m_*,v and the
unit vector n=m_*/|m_*|. For nearby fine parameters y and output
translations z define

    q(y,z;v)=t[m(y) dot v(z)-m_* dot v-(m(y)-m_*) dot n],
    D(y)=E_(k_(t,m_*))[ exp(t(m(y)-m_*) dot (v-n)) ].

The exact identity is

    k_(t,m(y))(v(z))/k_(t,m_*)(v) = exp(q(y,z;v))/D(y).   (J3)

Thus the reference normalizer and all its fine derivatives are present.
The centering vector n is fixed during differentiation at the evaluation
point; it is not a differentiable polar field that must be transported.

The derivative of an ell-link contour in any ordered a-word is at most
(ell h_f)^a, by distributing the derivative positions among its factors.
The same bound holds for its mean. Since |v-n|=sqrt(2X/(tr)), the first
fine derivative of q is at most 2ell sqrt(kappa/r_0)sqrt(X). J1 bounds
its first output derivative. For total order r>=2, the pure fine word
has the extra factor |v-n|<=2; words containing an output derivative
have no larger bound. A common valid choice is

    a_1(x)=2 sqrt(kappa) max(ell/sqrt(r_0),R) sqrt(x),
    a_r(x)=2 kappa [sqrt(2) max(ell,R)]^r, 2<=r<=6.      (J4)

Let P_r be the partition polynomial formed with these a_r. Set

    p_r=E P_r(Y),  c_0=1,
    c_r=sum_(l=1)^r binom(r,l) p_l c_(r-l),
    Q_r(x)=sum_(l=0)^r binom(r,l) P_l(x)c_(r-l),
    Q(x)=sum_(r=0)^6 (2^r/r!) Q_r(x).                  (J5)

All are explicit finite positive sums, with moments evaluated by G3.
Differentiating D gives |D_word D|<=p_r at the basepoint D=1.
Differentiating D D^-1=1 gives the c_r recursion. Ordered Leibniz in
J3 gives k^-1|D_word k|<=Q_r(X). Derivatives of D containing an output
letter are actually zero; including them only enlarges this bound.
Finally sum_(a+b=r)1/(a!b!)=2^r/r!, proving

    T_6(k) <= k Q(X),  when |m|>=r_0.                  (J6)

For Q(x)=sum_alpha q_alpha x^alpha the integrated, regulated estimate is

    int exp(bX)1_(X>=q) T_6(k)dV
      <= sum_alpha q_alpha Gamma(alpha+3/2,(1-b)q)
                    /[Gamma(3/2)(1-b)^(alpha+3/2)].    (J7)

This is uniform in u, volume and the fine input on that mean class.
Kernels have no original-source derivatives. Multiplying by an insertion
requires its own full four-label jets before this inequality is used.

For all means, a cruder bound suffices on the remaining class. Without
centering, the logarithmic exponent in the numerator of J3 is replaced
by t[m(y) dot v(z)-m_* dot v], and the denominator by its exact fine
normalizer ratio. Its first weighted derivative is bounded by
sqrt(2) kappa max(ell,R) sqrt(u); every higher r-score is bounded by
kappa[sqrt(2)max(ell,R)]^r for u>=1. Form the same partition, expectation,
and reciprocal recursions, now replacing X by these constant bounds.
Every term of total order r has degree at most r in sqrt(u). Thus there
is an explicitly specified finite polynomial constant C_rough(L,kappa,R)
(evaluate that positive bound at u=1 and sum through order 6) for which

    int T_6(k)dV <= C_rough u^3,  all means, u>=1.      (J8)

This is not a uniform pointwise fine-jet claim at a zero mean. The u^3
factor is paid only where a genuine original-law roughness estimate is
available, as in the next section.

### 4.5 A joint first-step bound under the actual Wilson law — PROVEN

This section is only for the first step, with N=2. It uses no assumption
that a later inherited marginal is Wilson. Write A_L=3L(L-1)/2 and

    d_2(u)=48+32 log(1+8 sqrt(u)),  u>=1.

For a closed fine unit cube C_x put E_x=(1/4)sum_(p subset C_x) f_p.
Each plaquette is in four such cubes, hence sum_x E_x=W. The original
Wilson measure satisfies

    mu_0(E_x>=a for every x in S)
       <= q_u(a)^|S|,
    q_u(a)=min(1, exp[-u a+d_2(u)]),                  (W1)

for any set of distinct cubes S. Here is the full argument needed for
this use of the existing PROOF E8.1 mechanism.

For a site reflection, the two fixed planes carry boundary links W_b.
Every plaquette lies in one closed half or one boundary plane. The
action is S_b(W_b)+S_+(U_+,W_b)+S_+(theta U_-,W_b). Conditioning on W_b
turns E[F theta(conjugate F)] into the integral of an absolute square.
Both periodic planes are included. Thus the measure is reflection
positive on the closed-half algebra used by the cube indicators.

Let G_x=1_(E_x>=a)+epsilon, epsilon>0. It is mapped to the corresponding
cube function by each site reflection. For a subset S define
R(S)=E product_(x in S)G_x /(E product_all G_x)^(|S|/n), n=|Lambda|.
Cauchy--Schwarz across any pair of antipodal planes gives
R(S)<=sqrt(R(S_+)R(S_-)), where each S_sign repeats one half by
reflection and |S_+|+|S_-|=2|S|. At a maximum of R over subsets both
reflections are maximizers. A maximal cyclic run of equal transverse
slab patterns of length l<2m can be extended to length at least
2 min(l,m)>l by reflecting at its endpoint and retaining that half.
Iterate in each direction; already constant directions stay constant.
A constant maximizer is either empty or full, and both have R=1.
Therefore R(S)<=1. Let epsilon decrease to zero. This proves the
chessboard inequality for these overlapping closed-cell events; it
does not assume link independence between cells.

On the disseminated event, W>=a n, so its unnormalized integral is at
most exp(-ua n). For the denominator, place every link in an operator
ball of radius u^-1/2 about I. The plaquette action is at most 8/u, so
its total exponent is at most 48n. A radius zeta Haar ball in SU(N)
has mass at least (1+4N/zeta)^(-2N^2): partition the real and imaginary
matrix entries into cubes of side at most zeta/(2N), pick a group point
in each occupied cube, and use their radius-zeta ball cover and Haar
invariance. There are at most (1+4N/zeta)^(2N^2) cubes. For N=2 and
4n links this gives Z_0>=exp[-d_2(u)n]. Take the nth root to prove W1.
This proof is valid on even compatible tori with the oriented arcs
retained at minimal side lengths.

Next, a ribbon with at most A_L plaquette occurrences compares each
paired transporter T_gamma to the straight transporter S_c. Telescoping
and Cauchy--Schwarz imply

    1-h_(M_c)(S_c) <= sum_p W_(c,p) f_p,
    W_(c,p)>=0,  sum_p W_(c,p)<=A_L^2.                 (W2)

Explicitly W_(c,p) is the average of n_gamma n_(gamma,p), where
n_gamma counts ribbon occurrences and n_(gamma,p) counts those of p.
All repeated occurrences are retained. Also
1-|m_c|<=1-h_(M_c)(S_c). Hence |m_c|<1/2 implies some ribbon plaquette
f_p>=1/(2A_L^2), and its lower-corner cube has E_x>=1/(8A_L^2).
There are at most 2L^4 possible lower corners, all in the two endpoint
blocks of c. A fixed lower corner belongs to at most eight such
two-block regions: the eight coarse links incident on its containing
block. These statements also count parallel periodic edges separately.

For m distinct coarse links, choose one witness cube for each short
mean. Every choice uses at least m/8 distinct cubes. By a union bound
and W1,

    mu_0(|m_c|<1/2 for all c in C)
       <= [(2L^4) q_u(1/(8A_L^2))^(1/8)]^m.           (W3)

The bound may exceed one; that is harmless. Let

    C_good=E Q(Y) from J5 at r_0=1/2,
    C_birth=2 L^4 exp(6) 9^4 [320 A_L^2/e]^5.

The last constant is a proved, deliberately conservative bound:

    sup_(u>=1) u^3 (2L^4) q_u(1/(8A_L^2))^(1/8)
      <= 2L^4 exp(6) 9^4 sup_(u>0) u^5 exp[-u/(64A_L^2)]
      = C_birth.                                      (W4)

Use (1+8 sqrt(u))^4<=9^4 u^2. For a selected output link write
J_c(U)=int T_6(k_c)dV_c. J6--J8 give
J_c<=C_good+C_rough u^3 1_(|m_c|<1/2). Expand the product over any
set C of distinct selected kernel links, integrate in the *same*
original Wilson law, and apply W3 to each chosen subset. The result is

    E_(mu_0) product_(c in C) J_c(U_0)
       <= (C_good+C_rough C_birth)^|C|.                 (W5)

Conditional independence of the output links shows that W5 is also the
joint integrated product of their full mixed jet densities. Fine readers
may overlap; no independence of fine fields was assumed. If multiple
insertions demand repeated powers at a link, J5/J8 and the tail count
must be redone at that total order. W5 does not spend that cost twice.
Original source insertions require their actual derivative bounds and
reader sets; W5 by itself is a kernel theorem, not the (6,4) RG theorem.

W5 covers the mean-degenerate branch for this normalized first-step
calculation. Extending W3 to the inherited marginal at arbitrary depth
would require U1/U2, not another application of bare Wilson chessboarding.

### 4.6 What normalization does not allow — PROVEN diagnostic

At the actual fine configuration U=I, every paired mean is M=I. At
the output V_c=-I in SU(2), X_c=2t. Thus exp(bX_c) grows exponentially
in t at this retained point, although its integrated expectation obeys
G3. By continuity, an open cap about -I still has X_c>=t; it has positive
kernel mass at every finite t. Normalizing the kernel on that cap makes
its exponential moment at least exp(bt). The cap's exponentially small
mass was divided out. The same configuration can be embedded in the
actual finite lattice; fine and output neighborhoods have positive
Wilson-chain mass. This is not a claimed counterexample to the target,
since the target is allowed to retain and charge that mass.

The mixed input proof has a second sharp scope boundary. At a zero mean
the law is Haar, and a nonzero fine variation of the mean can have a
weighted score of order sqrt(u). J8 retains this cost. G3 does not
bound fine jets merely because the gap is then identically zero.

### 4.7 Direct attack on the normalized backward covariance — PROVEN / GAP

For a bounded function F of the earlier fields only and a terminal
weighted link derivative D_c, differentiate the exact quotient B_j F
from section 4.1. Fixed compact domains justify the differentiation.
Writing s_c=D_c log k_j(V_c;M_c(U_j)), one obtains exactly

    D_c B_j F=E_pi(F s_c)-E_pi F E_pi s_c.             (C1)

If F also explicitly reads V, add E_pi(D_c F). Formula C1 includes
the full denominator derivative. In particular no small-tilt or
product-reference identity is needed for this algebra.

For SU(2), J2 and G3 give, under the complete joint chain,

    E s_c^2 <= 4 kappa R^2 E X_c <= 6 kappa R^2.       (C2)

Here sqrt(u_j/u_(j+1))<=R is the specified weight comparison, not a
derived flow theorem. Conditional Cauchy--Schwarz, then ordinary
Cauchy--Schwarz and total variance, give the uniform actual estimate

    int |D_c B_j F| r_(j+1)dV
       <= sqrt(6 kappa) R sqrt(Var_P(F)).              (C3)

Indeed E Var(F|V)<=Var(F) and E Var(s_c|V)<=E s_c^2.
For a disjoint complete past-history family F_h=I_h F, with I_h
measurable through U_j and not a new selector depending on V, a useful
absolute estimate is

    sum_h int |D_c B_j F_h| r_(j+1)dV
       <= 2 sqrt(6 kappa) R ||F||_(L^2(P_j)).          (C4)

To prove it, use |Cov(F_h,s_c|V)|<=E(|F_h||s_c||V)
+E(|F_h||V)E(|s_c||V), sum the identity sum_h |F_h|=|F|,
and apply Cauchy--Schwarz to each term. This is a sum of integrated
history costs under one reference, not a sum of separately normalized
history probabilities. It preserves original source insertions provided
their actual L^2 norm is used.

**GAP:** C3--C4 have no distance from the support of F to c. Summing
these bounds over terminal links introduces their number. They therefore
do not prove U1 or a rooted jet norm. For c!=d, forward conditional
independence and int D_c k_c=0 imply E_P(s_c s_d)=0, but C1 involves
posterior centering and products depending on V. This orthogonality
cannot be inserted into C1 as a spatial decay estimate.

The concrete next inequality is a complete-reader decomposition of C1
with an absolutely summable spatial tail, combined with a paid rough-core
alternative. Higher derivatives produce connected posterior cumulants
and differentiated scores; they require the same locality, not just more
applications of Cauchy--Schwarz. No such all-history bound has been
established here. The fixed nonlinear sector in section 4.11 has the
separately proved narrower covariance estimate.

### 4.8 A different large-field definition, and its remaining defect

For a fixed set C of current output links define the actual posterior
moment M_C(V)=E_pi exp(b sum_(c in C)X_c), 0<b<1. This is finite and
positive at every finite regulator. G5 and conditional expectation give

    int M_C(V) r_(j+1)(V)dV <= (1-b)^(-3|C|/2).

Thus for any a>0,

    nu_(j+1)(M_C>exp(a|C|))
       <= exp[-(a+(3/2)log(1-b))|C|].                 (P1)

This is **PROVEN**, uniform in the incoming law and depth. It suggests

### 4.9 The actual flat conditional Hessian and its Gaussian inverse — PROVEN

This section concerns the bare first step, N=2, output V_c=I and
t=kappa u. It is a calculation on the actual normalized action, not on
a kernel with its normalizer omitted. Write U_b(lambda)=exp(i lambda
A_b dot sigma), A_b in R^3. For a contour gamma let a_gamma be its
signed linear sum of A_b. Define

    (QA)_c=average_gamma a_gamma,
    ||RA||^2=sum_c average_gamma |a_gamma|^2.

R includes the averaging weights in its Euclidean norm. A product of
SU(2) exponentials gives, for each mean quaternion,

    m_0=1-lambda^2 average|a_gamma|^2/2+O(lambda^3),
    m_vec=lambda (QA)_c+O(lambda^2),
    |m|=1-lambda^2[average|a_gamma|^2-|(QA)_c|^2]/2
                                                        +O(lambda^3).

The scalar second term follows by expanding the product: paired cross
terms combine to the square of the signed sum, even when the A_b do
not commute. Let phi(t)=log Z_t(I) and alpha(t)=phi'(t)=E_(k_(t,I))v_0.
Expanding -t m_0+phi(t|m|), with the plus sign on the normalizer, gives

    D^2 S_cond(0)[A,A]
      =u ||dA||^2+t alpha(t)||QA||^2
                           +t(1-alpha(t))||RA||^2.       (F1)

This Hessian is for the action relative to Haar. The log Haar Jacobian
in exponential coordinates adds a positive diagonal 2/3 per real
coordinate at zero; it has not been silently included in F1.
Since 1-alpha(t)=E X/t<=3/(2t) by G3, for t>=3 one has alpha>=1/2.
Also 0<=alpha<=1: the upper bound is v_0<=1, and the lower follows by
pairing v_0 and -v_0 in the Haar integral when t>=0. Thus

    u^-1 D^2 S_cond(0) >= d^*d+(kappa/2)Q^*Q.             (F2)

Fine gauge transformations which equal I at the block roots leave every
coarse kernel unchanged. Fix a spanning tree inside each block, rooted
at its center, and set its links to I by these transformations. One exact
choice gives every nonroot site a parent by moving one step toward the
root in the first coordinate where it differs. Distance to the root
decreases, so this is a tree. Haar invariance gives independent Haar
variables on all remaining links and on the tree gauge transformations;
the latter integrate to one. There is no Faddeev--Popov determinant in
this tree parametrization and the retained V is unchanged.

At the linear level this sets A=0 on those tree links. Write the resulting
restriction of the right-hand operator in F2 as H_T^0. It has a uniform
positive lower bound, proved as follows. Fourier transform only in block
translations. A fiber has 4L^4-(L^4-1)=3L^4+1 free link coordinates per
colour, and Bloch momentum k in [-pi,pi]^4. Its matrix H_T^0(k) is a
continuous Hermitian matrix, since every factor has finite range.

The exact paired-path identity on gradients is

    Q(d psi)_mu(X)=psi(c_(X+e_mu))-psi(c_X).             (F3)

It follows by telescoping every contour, before averaging. A closed
Bloch one-form with k!=0 is a gradient: diagonalize the elementary
translations, whose symbols are exp(i p_mu)-1; if one is nonzero,
the vanishing curl makes the vector proportional to that gradient
symbol. At k=0 the only additional closed forms are constant link
forms a_mu. For a constant form Q a=L a.

If the quadratic form H_T^0(k) vanishes, dA=0 and QA=0. For k!=0,
write A=d psi. F3 and at least one nontrivial exp(i k_mu)-1 force the
root value psi(c_X)=0. Vanishing tree links then force every site
value in that block to be zero. For k=0 write A=a+d psi; QA=L a,
since the root values are periodic with one block. Thus a=0, and the
tree condition makes psi constant on the block, so A=0. Every fiber
is positive definite. Compactness proves that the *defined* constant

    g_T(L,kappa)=min_(k in [-pi,pi]^4) lambda_min(H_T^0(k)) > 0      (F4)

is independent of the number of blocks. No sampled eigenvalue is used
as its value. Real finite tori use subsets of these momenta, so inherit
the same bound. The three colours are identical independent quadratic
copies at the flat point.

For an explicit upper bound, a contour has at most ell occurrences and
a fine link can be read by only the eight coarse links incident to its
containing block. Cauchy--Schwarz along the contours gives
||R||^2<=8ell^2 and ||Q||^2<=8ell^2. Also d^*d<=16 in four dimensions.
Therefore the restriction H_T(t)=u^-1 D^2 S_cond(0) satisfies

    g_T I <= H_T(t) <= M_T I,
    M_T=16+8 kappa ell^2,    t>=3.                      (F5)

It connects blocks at graph distance at most two. Expand
H_T^-1=M_T^-1 sum_(n>=0)(I-H_T/M_T)^n. For sets of blocks A,B at
distance d, the terms with 2n<d vanish between their projections.
The remaining operator-norm geometric series gives

    ||1_A H_T^-1 1_B|| <= g_T^-1
                     (1-g_T/M_T)^(ceil(d/2)).           (F6)

The zero-ratio endpoint, if g_T=M_T, is interpreted as a diagonal
inverse. Thus the Gaussian with action u<A,H_T A>/2 has a
volume-uniform covariance bound u^-1 times F6. Adding the positive
Haar Hessian 2/3 I has the same argument, with upper constant
M_T+2/3 for u>=1. This establishes the spatial inverse estimate for
the correctly normalized *flat quadratic* reference. It does not
establish the covariance estimate for the nonlinear inherited law.

### 4.10 Nonlinear coercivity on an actual fixed bare sector — PROVEN

Keep the exact block-tree gauge just described. Every remaining fine
link has a unique coordinate U_b=exp(i A_b dot sigma) for |A_b|<a<pi.
Every tree link is I. Retained outputs are V_c=exp(i B_c dot sigma),
|B_c|<b. The integration domain is the fixed product of these fine
coordinate balls. It does not depend on B. This is a positive sector
of the actual gauge-fixed bare integral, with its full complement
still present in the full RG transfer.

First a uniform normalizer derivative bound is needed; a crude t^3
estimate would give the wrong perturbation scale. For r>0 set
F_t(m)=phi(t|m|). At s>0, radial cumulants of v_0=1-X/s and G3 give

    |phi'(s)|<=1,
    |phi''(s)|<=15/(4s^2),
    |phi'''(s)|<=147/(4s^3).                            (F7)

For the last bound, use
|cum_3(X)|<=E X^3+3(E X)(E X^2)+2(E X)^3,
with moments 3/2, 15/4 and 105/8. These are derivatives with respect
to s at the fixed Haar variable v_0, not derivatives treating X_s as
a parameter-independent variable. The cumulant identity justifies
the substitution at each s.

For the Euclidean norm r, ||Dr||<=1, ||D^2r||<=1/r and
||D^3r||<=6/r^2; the last bound follows from its three bilinear
terms and its term 3 n tensor n tensor n. The chain rule yields

    ||D F_t||<=t,
    ||D^2 F_t||<=t/r+15/(4r^2),
    ||D^3 F_t||<=6t/r^2+48/r^3.                        (F8)

On a fine coordinate cap with ell a<=1/2, telescoping gives
||M-I||<=ell a, hence r>=1/2 on every segment from 0 to A. Ordered
Frechet derivatives of a matrix exponential of an anti-Hermitian
matrix obey the norm-product bound by its simplex integral. Thus
||D^q M||<=ell^q for q<=3, in fine directions of maximum link norm
one. Applying F8 and the chain rule once more to the exact kernel
action -t h_M(V)+F_t(m) gives

    ||D_A^3(-log k_t)|| <= ell^3(429+32 kappa) u,
    ||D_B D_A^2(-log k_t)|| <= kappa u ell^2,           (F9)

for u>=1. The second derivative involves only the numerator since
the normalizer is output-independent. The constants in the first
bound are 48/(1/2)^3+45/[4(1/2)^2]=429 and
6/(1/2)^2+3/(1/2)+2=32; no fitted coefficient is used.

A fine link occurs in six Wilson plaquettes. Each plaquette reads at
most twelve real link coordinates and its third derivative is bounded
by 4^3 u in maximum-link-norm directions. Each fine link occurs in
at most eight coarse-kernel factors; a kernel reads at most 8L^4 fine
links, or 24L^4 real coordinates. Entrywise interpolation from the
flat point, followed by the row-sum bound for a symmetric matrix,
therefore proves the following volume-independent operator bound
for the action relative to Haar:

    ||Hess_A S(A;B)-Hess_A S(0;0)||
        <= u (D_T a+E_T b),
    D_T=4608+192 L^4 ell^3(429+32 kappa),
    E_T=192 L^4 kappa ell^2.                           (F10)

Here 4608=6 times 12 times 4^3. Restricting to the non-tree
coordinates cannot increase the bound. It is deliberately loose.

Choose, in this order, L,kappa, then g_T from F4, then

    0<a<=min(1/(2ell),g_T/(4D_T)),
    0<b<=min(1/2,g_T/(4E_T)),
    u>=max(1,3/kappa).                                (F11)

F2, F4 and F10 show Hess_A S(A;B)>=u g_T I/2 on the
whole fixed convex product domain. The Haar density in each free
coordinate is a constant times (sin|A_b|/|A_b|)^2. Its negative log
has tangential eigenvalue 2(1/r-cot r)/r and radial eigenvalue
2(csc^2 r-1/r^2), both positive for 0<r<pi, with limit 2/3
at zero. Hence including every Haar Jacobian preserves the bound:

    Hess_A [S(A;B)-sum_b log J_Haar(A_b)]
                       >= (u g_T/2) I.                (F12)

This proves strict convexity of the actual normalized bare-sector
potential (normalizing by its partition function changes no A
derivatives), uniformly in volume and u in F11. Constants and cap
sizes are fixed before taking u large. No shrinking cap at each
iteration has been introduced.

The limitations are decisive. This is the first-step Wilson density,
on a tiny identity sector in one exact gauge. It supplies no bound on
the sector's mass, its complement, a rough exterior, nontrivial flat
backgrounds, or the inherited interaction at later steps. A covariance
representation needs its boundary domain; the next section supplies
one on a fixed rectangular subsector. U1--U4 remain **GAP**.

### 4.11 A local normalized covariance on a fixed nonlinear subsector — PROVEN

Inside the cap of F11 use the fixed rectangular domain

    Omega_box=product_(free links b, colours a)
                            (-a_cap/sqrt(3),a_cap/sqrt(3)),

where a_cap is the a of F11, and denote its normalized actual
bare-sector measure by nu_B. Its potential relative to Lebesgue measure
is V=S(A;B)-sum_b log J_Haar(A_b). The boundary is independent of B.
All means, Wilson terms, kernel normalizers and Haar factors are those
of the original integral on this sector. Let g=g_T and set

    J_T=27+8 kappa ell^2+D_T a_cap+E_T b,
    eta=(1/2)log(1+g/(4J_T))>0.                         (L1)

Then for smooth real F,G with gradients supported in block sets X,Y,

    |Cov_(nu_B)(F,G)| <= [4/(u g)] exp(-eta d(X,Y))
                   ||grad F||_(L^2(nu_B)) ||grad G||_(L^2(nu_B)).  (L2)

The distance is the periodic nearest-neighbour block distance. The bound
is independent of volume and of B in its declared cap. Complex functions
follow by the same sesquilinear argument. The claim includes the true
normalization of this sector only; multiplying back by its unnormalized
mass is a separate step.

Here is a proof with the hard boundary specified. The Hessian H=Hess V
is at least u g/2 by F12. Its entries vanish between coordinates more
than two blocks apart. The flat Wilson Hessian has absolute row sum
at most 24; the two kernel Gram matrices together have row sum at most
8 kappa ell^2, by the same absolute incidence count used in F5.
The entrywise proof of F10 bounds the perturbed row sums as well.
The Haar Hessian has operator norm at most one for |A_b|<=1/2, so
each of its three-coordinate rows has absolute sum at most three.
For example sin r>=r-r^3/6 bounds its radial eigenvalue by
(2/3)(24/23)^2<1, and its tangential one by 16/23<1.
Consequently the absolute row sums of H are at most u J_T.

Let L=-Delta+grad V dot grad be the scalar nonnegative operator with
Neumann boundary condition on the rectangle. For G of zero mean solve
L psi=G with zero mean and Neumann condition. This finite-dimensional
weighted elliptic problem exists: the density is positive and smooth
on the compact rectangle, so its usual weighted Poincare constant is
finite; the mean-zero variational form is coercive. No volume-uniform
constant is needed merely for this existence step. Differentiation gives

    (L I+H)v=grad G,   v=grad psi.                      (L3)

On a face normal to coordinate i, v_i=0 and the other components have
zero normal derivative. These are, respectively, Dirichlet and Neumann
conditions for the vector equation. There is no outward flux term in
its quadratic form. The identity can first be proved on smooth functions
with these conditions and then in the closed forms. For the scalar
solution, H^2 regularity on a rectangle follows from the Neumann cosine
expansion for -Delta: the bounded smooth drift times grad psi is L^2,
so -Delta psi is L^2. Differentiating weakly gives the vector form and
the stated mixed conditions. Edges of faces introduce no extra flux;
the integration by parts is facewise, and the corresponding mixed forms
are their closures. This avoids rounding a corner into a boundary which
would fail to commute with the spatial weights.

Put w_i=exp(eta d(block(i),Y)) and let W be this constant diagonal
matrix on vector components. These are weights on the coordinate
indices, not functions of the integrated field A. They commute with
L and preserve every mixed boundary condition. If H_ij is nonzero,
|log(w_i/w_j)|<=2eta. Thus both absolute row and column sums of
W H W^-1-H are bounded by

    (exp(2eta)-1)u J_T=u g/4.

The column bound uses symmetry of H and the same ratio bound.
The Schur bound and H>=u g/2 imply that the real quadratic form of
W H W^-1 is at least u g/4. Multiply L3 by W^2 v and integrate.
The L term is nonnegative by the mixed conditions, giving

    ||W v||_(L^2(nu_B)) <= [4/(u g)]
                                      ||W grad G||_(L^2(nu_B)).  (L4)

Since grad G is supported in Y, its weight is one. Neumann integration
by parts gives Cov(F,G)=int grad F dot v dnu_B. On X the inverse
weight is at most exp(-eta d(X,Y)), and L2 follows by Cauchy--Schwarz.

There is a second operator check on the spatial estimate. On the same
mixed vector boundary domains, B=L I+u J_T I has an inverse diagonal
in the component index and norm at most 1/(u J_T). The multiplication
operator C=u J_T I-H has norm at most u J_T-u g/2 and range two in
that index. Thus

    (L I+H)^-1=sum_(n>=0)(B^-1 C)^n B^-1.

The series converges in norm. Between index sets at block distance d,
its first ceil(d/2) terms vanish, and its tail is bounded by
[2/(u g)](1-g/(2J_T))^(ceil(d/2)). This is even stronger than L2.
It uses no claim that L is local in the integrated field variables:
only its diagonality in the vector component index is used. It also
keeps the same mixed boundary domains instead of changing the measure.

This resolves the first covariance test (A) for this fixed nonlinear
bare sector. For a terminal link c let s_c=D_c log k_t with
h_c<=R sqrt(2/u). Its gradient reads only the two endpoint blocks
Y_c and satisfies the explicit bound

    ||grad_A s_c|| <= R kappa ell sqrt(48 L^4 u).        (L5)

Indeed there are at most 24L^4 real fine readers, each derivative is
at most h_c t ell, and the output-independent normalizer contributes
zero to s_c. Differentiating the sector quotient therefore gives

    |D_c E_(nu_B) F|
      <= [4R kappa ell sqrt(48 L^4)/(g sqrt(u))]
             exp(-eta d(X,Y_c)) ||grad F||_(L^2(nu_B))  (L6)

when F has no explicit retained-field dependence. This exponential
tail is summable over c in four dimensions, with a constant depending
on L,kappa and |X| but not on the surrounding volume.

**Scope check.** The source function F must be smooth in the stated
coordinates. An arbitrary hard history indicator is not. Higher
connected derivatives through (6,4), the exact logarithmic expansion,
coefficient extraction and regeneration have not been supplied by L6.
Nor has the mass of Omega_box or its complement been estimated. Thus
L6 supplies a real actual-model part of U1, not its all-history statement.

### 4.12 Hostile test: a negative direction of the full rough conditional action

**PROVEN diagnostic, not a counterexample to paid RG return.** Fix N=2,
any odd L>=3, 0<kappa<4L, and a periodic lattice whose fine side is an
even multiple of L. There are n_0 fine sites and n_1=n_0/L^4 coarse

    U_1^epsilon(x)=(-1)^(x_2) exp[i epsilon (-1)^(x_2) sigma_3],
    U_2^epsilon=U_3^epsilon=U_4^epsilon=I,

    V_2(X)=(-1)^o I,  V_3=V_4=I,  o=(L-1)/2.            (R1)

At epsilon=0 this is the actual persistent-flux input and its maximizing
coarse output. All plaquettes in the (1,2) orientation are -I.
The Wilson action along this
path is exactly u n_0(1+cos(2epsilon)); its second derivative is
-4u n_0.

The means can be evaluated without a prescribed-mean replacement.
For direction 1 the translated axial legs cancel, and the middle L

    h_1(epsilon)=cos(L epsilon)/L,

For direction 2, write s=r_1_coordinate-o for the signed number of
direction-1 steps on the axial leg. Translating by L in direction 2
reverses their phases and changes their signs. Whether coordinate 1
precedes coordinate 2 in sigma changes the parity at which that leg
is evaluated; summing s and -s cancels its imaginary part in either

    h_2(epsilon)=r_2(epsilon)
       =((-1)^o/L) sum_(s=-o)^o (-1)^s cos(2s epsilon),        (R3)

where equality to the modulus holds in a neighborhood of zero.

sum_(s=-o)^o(-1)^s=(-1)^o and

    h_1''(0)=-L,    r_1''(0)=L(L^2-1),

For each changed kernel the exact action is -t h_i+phi(t r_i),

    t L[1+(L^2-1)alpha],

Thus the soft normalizer does not repair the Wilson negative mode in

### 4.15 Matched exterior comparison for a strong rough core — PROVEN

This section concerns only the bare first step, N=2, in the exact
root-fixed block-tree gauge of section 4.9. Fix odd L, kappa>0,
0<delta_exp<1/2, a threshold 0<s_0<2, and 0<theta<=1. Write

    epsilon(u)=u^(-1/2+delta_exp),  q=theta s_0.

Here theta denotes the remaining action fraction, independently of

For this theorem take four original source directions O_i=f_(p_i),

Let S be a nonempty nearest-neighbor connected set of m coarse blocks.

    n_A<=4 L^4 |A|,   |P_A|<=24 L^4 |A|,

The last count uses the actual paired contours: a kernel reads only

### 4.16 Six field derivatives, four source labels, and the rooted sum — PROVEN

### 4.17 Hostile composition and threshold tests of the newest bound

**PROVEN limited composition identity.** Take a finite collection of
cores with disjoint integrated link sets such that no original

the full integral of this particular sector divided by the full

The same identity holds with the specified numerator regulator

Here nu_good,y is the marginal of the *single full good-box measure*

upper bound by product_i sup z_(S_i) is valid. Replacing the right
side by product_i E z_(S_i), or by expectations under different
exterior normalizations, is not justified. Differentiating RC19
also differentiates its common exterior measure. RC13 does not
bound the resulting connected covariances or higher cumulants.

**PROVEN failure of the proposed partition identification.** Write
B_X for the original owned-plaquette bad event. Then

of section 4.12 has every B_X true, and this remains true on an open
neighborhood for s_0<2. The missing absence factors in a complete

the RG activities double-counts these sectors and supplies no
logarithmic connected expansion. Overlapping cores must be merged
or represented by a proved compatible construction; their full

**PROVEN failure of the current bound at a shrinking threshold.**
Test RC8 with s_0(u)=c epsilon(u)^2, with fixed 0<c<=8, while keeping
the same good-core comparison. Its worst-case |A|<=9m estimate is

    log z_S <= m[(9D_1-theta c)u^(2 delta_exp)
                                      +9D_2 log u+9D_3].     (RC20)

Since D_1>=192L^4 and theta c<=8, this upper bound is positive and
grows. RC20 cannot prove a small activity. This is not a claim that
the actual log z_S is positive. It identifies the precise wasted

proof does not extend to the full small/large-field boundary.

from these ratios, and no estimate here controls the connected

At j>0 the input density is rho_j, not exp(-u_j W): RC7 cannot be

### 4.18 New route: concentration about the actual convex-cap mean

**PROVEN at the specified bare-cap scope.** This step uses the normalized
measure on the *single full fixed rectangle* Omega_box of section 4.11.
It does not use a separate normalization for each rough core. Keep its
actual soft kernels, Haar Jacobians and four original plaquette sources
from section 4.15. Denote the resulting measure by nu_(B,J). All fine

max_c |B_c|<=epsilon(u)<=b_cap. Take u at least as large as required

    J_s=J_T+192R_J,
    eta_s=(1/2)log[1+g_s/(4J_s)].                     (CC1)

The source potential -sum J_i f_(p_i) has Hessian operator norm at

therefore at most 192r_i, and its block range is at most two.

row bound u J_s. The proof of L2 applies with g_s,J_s,eta_s, including
its rectangular boundary conditions. Adding any real linear tilt
in the fine coordinates changes none of these Hessian bounds.

**PROVEN mean estimate.** Write bar A_b=E_(nu_(B,J)) A_b for a free
fine link. At B=0 this vector is zero. Indeed simultaneous conjugation
by a pi rotation about a colour axis flips the other two components
of every A_b, preserves the coordinate rectangle, all tree links,
Haar density, Wilson terms, plaquette sources and kernels at V=I.
Each component changes sign under one such symmetry.

An unweighted output derivative of log k at c has fine gradient
norm at most kappa u ell sqrt(24L^4), supported on its endpoint
blocks Y_c. This follows by differentiating its numerator: its
normalizer is output-independent. L2 applied to A_(b,a) and this

    |partial_(B_c) E A_(b,a)|
       <=C_B exp[-eta_s d(b,Y_c)],
    C_B=4 kappa ell sqrt(24L^4)/g_s.                 (CC2)

The derivative bound is for a unit real direction at c. Summing
over coarse links costs at most 8S_eta, where

    S_eta=[(1+exp(-eta_s))/(1-exp(-eta_s))]^4,
    K_B=8 sqrt(3) C_B S_eta,
    max_b |bar A_b|<=K_B epsilon(u).                  (CC3)

For the counting, exp[-eta_s d(b,Y_c)] is at most the sum of the

on Z^4, which is S_eta. Integrate CC2 from the zero mean at B=0;
the factor sqrt(3) converts the three component bounds to a vector
bound. This uses a common cap measure throughout, at the same fixed J.

**PROVEN joint fluctuation estimate.** For any finite vector lambda
on the free real coordinates, apply L2 with distance zero to the
linear observable lambda dot A under each of its real linear tilts.
The second derivative of its log moment generating function is
its variance, bounded by 4|lambda|^2/(u g_s). Integrating that second
derivative from zero proves

    E exp[lambda dot (A-bar A)]
                       <=exp[2|lambda|^2/(u g_s)].    (CC4)

For any subset I of free links with d_I=3|I| real coordinates,
Gaussian integration of this linear bound gives, for 0<=b<g_s/8,

    E exp[b u sum_(b' in I)|A_(b')-bar A_(b')|^2]
                         <=(1-8b/g_s)^(-d_I/2).       (CC5)

Explicitly, introduce only for this calculation a standard real

exp[b u |Z|^2]=E_G exp[sqrt(2bu) G dot Z]. Positivity permits Fubini;
CC4 then leaves E_G exp[(4b/g_s)|G|^2]. Thus CC5 uses no independence
of the original fine links. At b=g_s/16 its right side is 2^(d_I/2).

**PROVEN shrinking-scale event price within this cap.** Fix d_0>=1 and

    c_w=8(K_B+d_0)^2,   a_w=g_s d_0^2/24,

    sqrt(f_p)<=2^(-1/2) sum_(b in p)|A_b|,

    sum_(b in p)|Z_b|^2>=4d_0^2 epsilon^2.            (CC7)

The middle inequality uses CC3 and the definition of c_w, and the

    sum_(b in I(S))|Z_b|^2
                           >=(2d_0^2/3)m epsilon^2,  (CC8)

where I(S) includes all free links of all plaquettes owned by S.

    nu_(B,J)(E_S(u))
                     <=exp[-(a_w u^(2delta_exp)-b_w)m].       (CC9)

If Z_cap(B,J) is the actual unnormalized integral over Omega_box,

    int_(Omega_box) 1_(E_S(u)) exp(-S_J) dA
       <=Z_cap(B,J) exp[-(a_w u^(2delta_exp)-b_w)m].    (CC10)

Here S_J includes the Haar Jacobian in coordinates and every original

**PROVEN first spatial response for smooth auxiliary insertions.**

    ||grad F_S|| <= [48L^4 m/(sqrt(c_w) epsilon)] 1_(E_S(u)),

    C_resp=192R kappa ell L^4 sqrt(48L^4)/(g_s sqrt(c_w)),

**Hostile check — what this new route still fails to prove.** CC9 is

**Failed localization attempt, with its precise cost.** Even CC13

    polynomial(r) exp[mu c r^4-eta_s r],  c>0, mu>0,          (CC14)

which are not summable uniformly in volume. This is a failure of

The new result replaces the failed

### 4.19 Failed absolute-walk localization in the actual flat matrix

**PROVEN diagnostic for this majorant, not for the signed inverse.**
The series after L4 converges as a signed operator series. A proposed
functional-support expansion would further expand its matrix products
into individual coordinate walks and take absolute values before summing
them. Already its flat matrix majorant need not converge.

Use L=3, kappa=1, one colour, the exact tree restriction and zero Bloch
momentum. There are n=244 free coordinates. Write

    H_infinity=d_T^*d_T+Q_T^*Q_T,
    Comp(H)_ii=H_ii,   Comp(H)_ij=-|H_ij|, i!=j.       (WP1)

At zero momentum Q has coefficient 1/27 on every free link of its
own direction and zero on other directions. This follows directly
from cancellation of the paired axial legs: each middle link is
read three times in the average over 81 fine sites. The numbers of
free links in the four directions are 27,63,75,79. In d_T^*d_T every
diagonal entry is 6. Its nonzero ordered off-diagonal incidences
number 1392 for equal directions and 2256 for unequal directions;
each has absolute value one. These counts can be reproduced with
the integer plaquette row

    e_(x,mu)+e_(x+mu,nu)-e_(x+nu,mu)-e_(x,nu),

with coordinates modulo three and the stated tree columns deleted.
For example the equal-direction counts are 162,342,426,462. In
729 H_infinity the trace is 1,067,500 and the absolute off-diagonal
sum is 2,672,928. Thus the all-ones vector gives exactly

    <1,Comp(H_infinity)1>/244
                     =-401357/44469 < -9.            (WP2)

This is compatible with H_infinity>0: taking entrywise absolute
values has removed essential cancellations. For any scalar
J>=max_i H_ii, entrywise |I-H/J|=I-Comp(H)/J. Its largest eigenvalue
is therefore greater than one whenever Comp(H) has this negative
Rayleigh quotient. Increasing J does not repair that condition.

This folded computation also tests actual periodic lattices with
at least two coarse sites per direction. Lift the positive cell
vector identically to every block. Triangle inequality for the
folded off-diagonal entries shows that its comparison Rayleigh
quotient on the full lattice is no larger than WP2. For entries
with the same within-cell index but a different block, the difference
has the sign -(|h|+h)<=0; hence folding their diagonal contributions
does not reverse the inequality.

The exact finite-u flat conditional Hessian, divided by u and
including the Haar Hessian, is

    H_u=d_T^*d_T+alpha(u)Q_T^*Q_T
                     +(1-alpha(u))R_T^*R_T+(2/(3u))I.

The absolute row sum of H_u-H_infinity is at most
(24ell^2+2/3)/u, by 1-alpha(u)<=3/(2u) and the separate row bounds
8ell^2 for Q_T^*Q_T and R_T^*R_T. Changing a comparison matrix
changes this Rayleigh quotient by at most that row bound. It is
therefore negative for every u>=644. The exact normalized kernel,
not an omitted normalizer, is used here.

**What failed.** The proposed pointwise coordinate-walk majorant
using |I-H_u/J| cannot bound the expansion by a convergent geometric
series. This does not disprove the original signed resolvent, a
block resummation, or the normalized return. In particular the
mixed-boundary scalar inverse in L4 has not been replaced by a
constant matrix operator. The failed step is the attempted absolute
matrix bound on that operator. The next route keeps the entire
Gaussian inverse as an edge instead of opening it into these walks.

### 4.20 The exact axial Gaussian step and an actual-density rate test

**PROVEN quadratic calculation.** At the trivial field the leading
u-stripped first-step action is

    (1/2)||dA||^2+(kappa/2)||QA-B||^2.

Let the coarse momentum be k=(p,0,0,0), 0<|p|<=pi, and the coarse field
have direction nu!=1 in one colour. Put s(p)=4sin^2(p/2) and

    a_L=(L^2-1)/(6L^2),   tau_1=kappa^-1-a_L.

The transverse coarse Schur complement is exactly

    Gamma_1(p)=s(p)/[1+tau_1 s(p)].                  (GS1)

Here is a derivation from the paired map. The endpoint axial-leg
term in Q_nu is proportional to 1-exp(i k_nu)=0. The remaining
straight map is L times the fine block average in direction nu.
Its transverse averages kill all aliases except
p_n=(p+2pi n)/L in direction 1. With

    S_L(p_n)=L^-1 sum_(r=0)^(L-1) exp(i p_n r),

the fine transverse Wilson covariance pushed through this map is

    G_push(p)=L^-2 sum_(n=0)^(L-1)
                       |S_L(p_n)|^2/[4sin^2(p_n/2)]
              =1/s(p)-a_L.                          (GS2)

The L^-2 factor is L^2 from the two block maps divided by the
four-dimensional Fourier cell volume L^4. For a direct evaluation,
|S_L(p_n)|^2=sin^2(p/2)/(L^2 sin^2(p_n/2)). The logarithmic
derivative of the finite product for sin(Lx) gives
sum_n csc^2(x+npi/L)=L^2 csc^2(Lx). Differentiating twice, using
(csc^2 x)''=6csc^4 x-4csc^2 x, gives

    sum_n csc^4(x+npi/L)
       =L^4 csc^4(Lx)+(2/3)(L^2-L^4)csc^2(Lx),

which proves GS2. The normalized soft Gaussian transition adds
kappa^-1 to this covariance; inversion proves GS1. Fine gradients
cannot change this transverse observable, since Qd=d_coarse at the
roots. Other output directions decouple by transverse reflection
symmetry. This is the same Schur mechanism already present in FLOW
F-tree, now evaluated in a closed axial form for the present check.

For L=3,kappa=1 the ratios Gamma_1(p)/s(p) at p=pi/2 and p=pi are
27/73 and 27/119. Thus no single Wilson coefficient matches this
quadratic action at both momenta. Tuning kappa=1/a_L makes this
particular axial correction vanish; the diagnostic is not a claim
for every possible kappa or for all momentum directions at that tune.

**PROVEN axial Gaussian recursion.** In the linear Gaussian chain,
an axial transverse covariance 1/s(p)+tau_j transforms to

    tau_(j+1)=kappa^-1-a_L+tau_j/L^2,
    tau_j=tau_*+(tau_0-tau_*)L^(-2j),
    tau_*=L^2/[kappa(L^2-1)]-1/6.                   (GS3)

Indeed sum_n |S_L(p_n)|^2=1 by finite Fourier orthogonality, so
the constant covariance term contributes tau_j/L^2 to GS2. This
proves stability of this axial sector of the actual linear block
map, not closure of a scalar ansatz for the full four-dimensional
matrix covariance. FLOW F-depth already identifies the general
linear contraction mechanism; GS3 is a checked special case.

**PROVEN actual finite-volume moderate-deviation test.** This
Gaussian coefficient also occurs in the actual first-step SU(2)
density, not merely in a replacement Gaussian model. Fix one finite
compatible torus and use the exact tree gauge. Define

    F_B(U)=W(U)+kappa sum_c psi_(M_c(U))(V_c),
    V_c=exp(i B_c dot sigma),   f(B)=min_U F_B(U).      (GS4)

At B=0, F_0=0 forces every plaquette to be I. A flat input has
identical contour transports in each paired mean, hence a unit
mean. Its zero output gap requires every root-to-root transport
to be I. Coarse root cycles generate the torus cycles, so all fine
holonomies are I. The flat field is pure gauge, and the root-fixed
tree gauge then makes every fine link I. This is the unique minimum
on the gauge-fixed compact product.

Near I the square root |m| is smooth and the A Hessian is
d_T^*d_T+kappa Q_T^*Q_T, positive by F4. The finite-dimensional
implicit function theorem and strict convexity give a unique smooth
local minimizer for B near zero. Compactness separates the rest of
this fixed torus from the zero minimum, so this local branch is the
global minimum for sufficiently small B on that torus. Consequently

    f(B)=(1/2)<B,Gamma_1 B>+O(|B|^3),                (GS5)

with the full Schur matrix in GS5 and its axial value GS1.

RC4 gives, for the actual unnormalized density rho_1,

    int exp(-u F_B)dU <= rho_1(V;0)
       <=H(kappa u)^(number of coarse links)
                                      int exp(-u F_B)dU.

The upper Laplace bound is exp(-u f(B)). For the lower bound,
integrate a coordinate ball of radius u^-1/2 about the interior
minimum; the Hessian is locally bounded, the Haar density bounded
below, and the volume is a fixed constant times u^(-d/2). These
bounds hold uniformly for B sufficiently small on this fixed torus.
Hence log rho_1(V;0)=-u f(B)+O(log u). For fixed b and
epsilon=u^(-1/2+delta_exp), 0<delta_exp<1/2,

    lim_(u->infinity) [log rho_1(exp(i epsilon b);0)
                       -log rho_1(I;0)]/(u epsilon^2)
                         =-(1/2)<b,Gamma_1 b>.       (GS6)

The errors are O(epsilon)+O(log u/u^(2delta_exp)) after division.
Constants in this Laplace argument may depend on the fixed torus;
GS6 supplies no volume-uniform prefactor or history sum.

**PROVEN failure of a particular reference conversion.** At L=3,
kappa=1, take a real transverse axial mode b with p=pi/2 or pi on
an even compatible coarse torus whose direction-1 length is divisible
by four. If one retains u times the bare
Wilson action and puts its difference from the tree action into
the residual multiplicative density, then GS6 gives

    lim (u epsilon^2)^-1 log[
       exp(u W(exp(i epsilon b)))
                   rho_1(exp(i epsilon b);0)/rho_1(I;0)]
                =(1/2)<b,(d^*d-Gamma_1)b> > 0.        (GS7)

This residual cannot have a volume-uniform bounded supremum on the
declared epsilon domains for all u>=u_*: it is unbounded already
on this one fixed torus. Matching the constant-curvature coefficient
does not remove the spatial quadratic difference. This tests the
conversion with its leading coefficient u; it is not a refutation
of LOCOP's projector or a conversion with a bounded loop coefficient.
LOCOP L6 explicitly retains a tree functional and leaves its scheme
conversion estimate open. The manuscript now makes that reference
choice explicit rather than assigning the leading difference to H3.

### 4.21 Connected supports for the bare nonlinear stationary background

**PROVEN local-branch construction, at zero source and N=2.** Use
the u-independent action F_B in GS4 near the trivial field. This is
the exact leading exponent of the normalized soft kernel. Its
normalizer's subleading terms and its fluctuation integral are not
part of the following stationary-background theorem.

Let n_A=3(3L^4+1) be the number of real free fine coordinates per
block. Define

    H=d_T^*d_T+kappa Q_T^*Q_T,
    M_G=17+8kappa ell^2,
    rho_G=1-g/M_G,   eta_G=-(1/2)log rho_G > 0,

where g=g_T(L,kappa)>0 is the proved lower constant in F4. Choose
0<sigma<eta_G before choosing the input field radius. For each pair
of coordinate blocks choose a shortest coordinate-ordered block
path and attach both endpoint blocks of the fine links i and j;
call this decorated connected set P_ij. F6 implies the absolute
weighted inverse bound

    sup_i sum_j |(H^-1)_ij| exp(sigma |P_ij|) <= K_H,
    K_H=(n_A exp(5sigma)/g)
       [(1+exp(-(eta_G-sigma)))/(1-exp(-(eta_G-sigma)))]^4.   (TB1)

This takes absolute values of the *full inverse entries*, after the
signed Gaussian inversion. It does not require the false walk
criterion of WP2. The periodic distance sum is bounded by the sum
on Z^4. The decorated path has at most d(i,j)+5 blocks. Fine-link
endpoint readers are therefore retained even for a boundary link.

The analyticity and coefficient bounds used below are elementary for
F_B. Complexify the exponential coordinates only as a calculation,
using the holomorphic trace formulas that agree with the real SU(2)
action. In particular inverse matrices, not complex conjugation of
the continued variables, appear in these formulas. Put

    r=1/(192ell),   E_0=18+32kappa,
    M_N=2E_0/r,
    D_loc=81(n_A+12),   C_N=2D_loc/r.                 (TB2)

If every complex coordinate has modulus at most r, then
||exp(i A dot sigma)|| and its inverse are at most exp(3r).
A contour therefore obeys ||M-I||<=exp(3ell r)-1<=1/63.
Its complex quaternion displacement from (1,0,0,0) has Euclidean
norm at most 2/63, so |m dot m-1|<1/2. The square root in
psi=sqrt(m dot m)-(1/2)tr(V^-1 M) has a single analytic branch
there, with |psi|<=4. Each Wilson factor has absolute value at
most three. An A_i derivative reads at most six Wilson factors
and eight kernels, whose local sum is bounded by E_0. Cauchy's
formula on the half-radius polydisc bounds this derivative by M_N.

Write the stationary equation exactly as

    A=kappa H^-1 Q_T^* B-H^-1 N(A,B),
    N_i=partial_(A_i)F_B-(HA)_i+kappa(Q_T^*B)_i.       (TB3)

N_i has no constant or linear term. Its readers lie in the
connected container T_i=N_(infinity,1)(block(i)), with at most
81 blocks and at most D_loc input coordinates. The Cauchy bound
gives each degree-n Taylor coefficient, n>=2, absolute value at
most M_N(2/r)^n. There are at most D_loc^n degree-n multiindices.
Thus the sum of their absolute contributions, when every argument
has size at most z, is bounded by

    M_N sum_(n>=2)(C_N z)^n
                         =M_N(C_N z)^2/(1-C_N z).    (TB4)

These constants are deliberately conservative but finite and do
not depend on the number of blocks.

Here is a supported version of the fixed point, not just an inverse
function theorem. Represent each component A_i as a sum of analytic
functions indexed by connected sets containing both endpoint blocks
of the fine link i, each using only B variables whose endpoints belong
to that set. The norm is
the sum of their suprema on |B_c^a|<=b, multiplied by exp(sigma|X|),
then the supremum over i. Index families may be kept as trees and
grouped by their union at the end; absolute convergence justifies
that grouping. Attach P_ij to every H^-1 edge, T_j to every local
nonlinear vertex, and the output link endpoints to each B leaf.
The union is connected and contains every functional reader.
For products, the union's weight is at most the product of these
weights. Thus the norm of TB3 is bounded by the scalar map

    a -> L_0 b+K_0 [C_N(a+b)]^2/[1-C_N(a+b)],
    L_0=8kappa ell exp(2sigma)K_H,
    K_0=K_H exp(81sigma)M_N,
    L_0bar=max(1,L_0).                               (TB5)

For L_0, the absolute row sum of Q_T^* is at most 8ell and its
output endpoint set has at most two blocks. For K_0, summation of
the inverse edge uses TB1, the local vertex costs at most
exp(81sigma), and TB4 includes all its coordinate and monomial
choices. No independence of fine fields or artificial factorization
of a scalar resolvent is used in these bounds.

Choose

    0<b<=b_*:=min{1/(6 C_N L_0bar),
                         1/(48 K_0 C_N^2 L_0bar)}.    (TB6)

On a<=2L_0bar b one has C_N(a+b)<=1/2. The nonlinear term in
TB5 is at most 18K_0 C_N^2 L_0bar^2 b^2, and its Lipschitz
constant with respect to a is at most
24K_0 C_N^2 L_0bar b<=1/2. The map preserves that ball and is
a contraction. Iteration from zero in the supported series norm
therefore converges absolutely to

    A_i(B)=sum_(X connected,block(i) in X) A_(i,X)(B),
    sup_i sum_X exp(sigma|X|)
                          sup_(|B|<=b)|A_(i,X)(B)|
                                  <=2L_0bar b.       (TB7)

Each Picard iterate is the actual analytic expression obtained
from TB3; composition only unions its declared supports. Its norm
limit solves that same equation and is its unique solution in the
ball. On real fields this is a strict local minimum: the absolute
row norm of partial_A N on this ball is at most
24M_N C_N^2 L_0bar b<=g/2, since K_0>=M_N/g. Symmetry and
H>=g then give Hess_A F_B>=g/2. This also verifies the local
branch selection without assuming a minimum from its name.

TB7 is functional locality with complete connected supports for
this stationary branch. Unlike ball telescoping, a long inverse
edge pays only for its attached path, and a nonlinear vertex pays
for the union of its attached child supports. On any smaller
output polydisc, Cauchy's formula gives finite coordinate-derivative
bounds through order six with the same supports. For example on
|B|<=b/2, r_field directions of coordinate maximum norm at most one
obey the factor (2 r_field/b)^(r_field) in the unweighted derivative
bound. This claim concerns coordinate derivatives of the background,
not the full group/source jet of an integrated activity.

**Hostile check of the newest step.** TB7 retains the entire signed
Gaussian inverse and only then takes absolute values of its entries;
WP2 therefore does not invalidate it. The local square root is used
only where its complex argument stays within distance 1/2 of one.
Every nonlinear vertex has a finite reader container, and no old
field has been normalized or discarded. The contraction counts all
trees and all overlaps before regrouping by their complete unions.

The limitations are decisive. The result constructs the bare local
stationary branch uniformly in volume, not its fluctuation integral.
GS4's compactness argument identifies it as the global minimum only
for a neighborhood that can depend on the fixed torus; uniform global
branch selection or a paid alternative has not been proved. TB7 uses
sigma<eta_G and does not certify H4's larger mu+2 support reserve.
Its component-rooted sum alone is not a rooted sum of action
activities over every possible anchor. GD4 below supplies an anchor
conversion with a rate loss and bounds the Gaussian determinant.
The four-label normalized source response, rough/hole interfaces
and higher connected fluctuation sum remain open. So do uniform-depth
bounds for the inherited nonlinear tree action.

**GAP.** Inserting this deterministic branch into a Gaussian or
nonlinear integral does not prove the remaining actual-posterior
covariance bound. The scalar resolvent's coefficients and the common
fluctuation normalization still read the whole measure. A supported
expansion of that fluctuation contribution, with its mixed jets and
history masses, remains U1. The tree-background result is not a
renaming of that unproved estimate and does not reduce the count of
U1--U4.

### 4.22 A connected expansion of the Gaussian normalization

**PROVEN, bare stationary branch, zero source, N=2.** This step sums
Gaussian loops, rather than the coordinate walks rejected in WP2.
Choose two rates 0<sigma_1<sigma_0<eta_G and use sigma=sigma_0 in
TB1--TB7. Write A_*(B) for that branch and put

    C=H^-1,  E(B)=partial_A N(A_*(B),B),
    H_B=H+E(B)=Hess_A F_B(A_*(B)),
    K_E=24 exp(81sigma_0) M_N C_N^2 L_0bar,
    q=K_H K_E b=24 K_0 C_N^2 L_0bar b <= 1/2.       (GD1)

All constants here are the explicit TB constants at sigma_0; b<=b_*.
No bound on a scalar diffusion resolvent is being used.

**PROVEN supported Hessian bound.** Expand each entry of E by
differentiating the local Taylor series for N_i before substituting
A_*. Summing over the differentiated coordinate counts the degree of
each monomial. With z=a+b<=3L_0bar b and C_N z<=1/2,

    M_N sum_(n>=2) n C_N^n z^(n-1)
                         <=8 M_N C_N^2 z.

Retain T_i at each vertex, including the differentiated coordinate's
fine-link endpoints. Substitute the supported TB7 series in every
remaining A argument. Then

    sup_i sum_(j,X) exp(sigma_0 |X|) ||E_(ij,X)||_b
                                                   <= K_E b.       (GD2)

Every X is connected, contains both fine-link endpoint sets i,j,
and contains every B reader. Here ||.||_b is the complex supremum
on |B_c^a|<=b. The factor exp(81sigma_0) pays for T_i. The
derivative majorant already sums the coordinate and multiplicity
choices; it is not multiplied by the volume.

**PROVEN determinant and inverse expansions.** The supported row
norm of CE is at most q. In this matrix algebra the support of a
product is the union of its factor supports. A C entry carries P_ij;
the adjoining E entry carries both of its indices, so this union is
connected. It follows that

    D(B):=(1/2)log[det H_B/det H]
       =(1/2) sum_(n>=1) (-1)^(n+1) Tr[(CE)^n]/n,
    C_B:=H_B^-1=sum_(n>=0)(-CE)^n C.                (GD3)

The logarithm is the analytic branch equal to zero at B=0.
The row norm q<1 proves convergence and nonvanishing of the
determinant along sE, 0<=s<=1. On real fields H_B>=g/2 by TB7,
so this branch is the real Gaussian normalization. A fixed trace
anchor i contributes at most q^n in the sigma_0 supported norm;
the inverse has supported row norm at most K_H/(1-q). This uses
absolute values after computing C, not before its signed inversion.

**PROVEN conversion to a rooted action norm.** This addresses the
anchor issue left open after TB7. Let Delta=sigma_0-sigma_1 and

    S_Delta=[(1+exp(-Delta))/(1-exp(-Delta))]^4.

For a connected X containing both block(i) and a specified block v,
|X|>=d(block(i),v). Thus lowering the rate from sigma_0 to sigma_1
costs at most exp[-Delta d(block(i),v)] for that anchor. Summing all
anchors gives at most n_A S_Delta on every compatible torus.
Keeping the cycles as indexed terms and then grouping by their
complete unions gives D(B)=sum_X D_X(B) and

    sup_v sum_(X containing v) exp(sigma_1 |X|)||D_X||_b
             <=(n_A S_Delta/2)[-log(1-q)].           (GD4)

There is no unrecorded extensive factor: the trace's free anchor was
summed using Delta. The same argument converts any uniformly bounded
family with one declared block anchor, with S_Delta in place of
n_A S_Delta. Translation invariance of that family is unnecessary.
On |B|<=b/2, r_field ordered coordinate directions of maximum norm
at most one cost at most (2 r_field/b)^r_field by multivariable
Cauchy; supports remain the same. These are coordinate derivatives,
not a claim about the full (6;[4]) RG jet.

**Scope.** GD3 is the exact normalization of a Gaussian with the
Hessian of the actual bare stationary action. It is not the
normalization of the nonlinear posterior. Its rooted rate sigma_1
is strictly below eta_G; no estimate at H4's mu+2 follows. No
inherited action or rough exterior has entered this proof.

### 4.23 The actual soft normalizers at the stationary field

**PROVEN zero-free local estimate, not a formal Bessel asymptotic.**
For complex s with Re s>0, the same Haar integral as in G2 gives

    A(s)=(2 sqrt(2)/pi) int_0^2 exp(-s x) x^(1/2)
                                         sqrt(1-x/2) dx.

The analytic continuation is fixed by this integral. Write

    A(t r)=sqrt(2/pi)(t r)^(-3/2)[1+e_t(r)],
    |r-1|<=1/4,   t>=12.

For 0<=x<=2, |sqrt(1-x/2)-1|<=x/2. The omitted leading-integral
tail x>=2 is bounded using x^(1/2)<=x^(3/2)/2. These two errors
together are at most Gamma(5/2)/(Re s)^(5/2) before the common
constant 2sqrt(2)/pi. Dividing by the leading term proves

    |e_t(r)| <= (3/2)|t r|^(3/2)/(Re(t r))^(5/2)
       <= [ (3/2)(5/4)^(3/2)/(3/4)^(5/2) ]/t
       <6/t <=1/2.                                 (GN1)

In particular A(t r) has no zero on this disk. Its logarithm,
normalized by its value at r=1, obeys

    -log A(t r)+log A(t)
      =(3/2)log r-log(1+e_t(r))+log(1+e_t(1)),
    |-log A(t r)+log A(t)|
               <=(3/2)log(4/3)+2log 2 <2.           (GN2)

This bound is uniform in t>=12. It is not an interchange of a
large-t asymptotic series with derivatives.

**PROVEN analytic amplitude bound.** On the full TB2 input polydisc,
the complex quaternion displacement obeys ||m-(1,0,0,0)||<=2/63.
Consequently |m dot m-1|<=4/63+4/63^2<1/4. The chosen square root
r_c=sqrt(m dot m) has positive real part and |r_c-1|<1/4. GN2
therefore applies to every actual kernel normalizer there, for
t=kappa u>=12. Its first derivative at A=0 vanishes: m_0 has no
linear term, and the other quaternion coordinates vanish at zero.

Remove only the constant 1/(2pi^2) from each SU(2) Haar coordinate
density and write

    j(A_b)=[sin(sqrt(A_b dot A_b))/sqrt(A_b dot A_b)]^2,
    G_u(A)= product_(free b) j(A_b)
                                product_c A(t r_c(A))^-1.       (GN3)

These are holomorphic formulas in the indicated neighborhood. If
z=A_b dot A_b, then |z|<=3r^2 and

    |sin(sqrt z)/sqrt z-1| <= |z| exp(sqrt(|z|))/6 <1/8.

Thus |log j(A_b)|<1 there, with the branch zero at A_b=0.
It too starts at degree two. This checks the Haar logarithm as
well as the soft logarithms, rather than assuming their zero-freeness.

Assign each free-link Haar term to its base block and each soft
normalizer to its coarse-link base. There are 3L^4+1 free links
and four coarse links per block. The local anchored sum

    g_x(A)=sum_(b based at x) log j(A_b)
             -sum_(c based at x)[log A(t r_c(A))-log A(t)]

is analytic, has no degree zero or one, and is bounded by
J_0=3L^4+9 on the TB2 polydisc. Its reader container lies in
N_(infinity,1)(x), with at most D_loc coordinates. Cauchy followed
by TB7 therefore bounds its supported composition by

    sum_X exp(sigma_0 |X|)||g_(x,X)(A_*)||_b
       <=exp(81sigma_0) J_0 (C_N a)^2/(1-C_N a)
       <=8 exp(81sigma_0) J_0 C_N^2 L_0bar^2 b^2,
    a=2L_0bar b.                                    (GN4)

The block x is retained in every term, and every branch reader is
retained in its complete union. Using the block-anchor conversion
proved in GD4 gives

    log[G_u(A_*(B))/G_u(0)]=sum_X G_(u,X)(B),
    sup_v sum_(X containing v) exp(sigma_1 |X|)||G_(u,X)||_b
       <=8 S_Delta exp(81sigma_0) J_0 C_N^2 L_0bar^2 b^2.       (GN5)

This estimate is uniform in volume and u>=12/kappa. Six coordinate
derivatives on the smaller output polydisc follow by the same
Cauchy argument as in GD4. In particular the complete leading
Gaussian prefactor

    log[G_u(A_*(B))/G_u(0)]-D(B)                     (GN6)

has the sum of the GD4 and GN5 rooted bounds. It contains the
actual Haar and soft normalizers at the stationary field, and the
Gaussian determinant. It omits their variation under fluctuations,
which is retained explicitly in the next identity.

### 4.24 The remaining integral and failure of the uncut Taylor route

**PROVEN exact identity for a fixed bare cap.** Choose a positive
coordinate half-width a_box<=min(a_cap/sqrt(3),r/4) and let
Omega=(-a_box,a_box)^d in the exact tree gauge. In addition to TB6,
take b<=a_box/(4L_0bar), so A_*(B) lies inside the half-width
a_box/2 box. Here d=n_A times the number of coarse blocks. Let
n_f=d/3 be the number of free fine links. With the original four
source functions O_i, define the actual unnormalized cap integral

    Z_Omega(B;J)=(2pi^2)^(-n_f) int_Omega
          exp[-u F_B(A)+sum_i J_i O_i(A)] G_u(A) dA.

No sources enter the kernel, the coupling, or the branch definition.
Define, only for a with A_*+a in Omega,

    R_B(a)=F_B(A_*+a)-F_B(A_*)-(1/2)<a,H_B a>,
    D_B=sqrt(u)(Omega-A_*),
    W_B,J(zeta)=exp[-u R_B(zeta/sqrt(u))]
       [G_u(A_*+zeta/sqrt(u))/G_u(A_*)]
       exp[sum_i J_i{O_i(A_*+zeta/sqrt(u))-O_i(A_*)}].

Let gamma_(C_B) be the normalized real Gaussian with covariance
C_B from GD3. A change of variables gives exactly

    Z_Omega(B;J)=(2pi^2)^(-n_f) (2pi/u)^(d/2)
       exp[-u F_B(A_*)+sum_i J_i O_i(A_*)]
       G_u(A_*) (det H_B)^(-1/2) Xi_Omega(B;J),
    Xi_Omega(B;J)=E_(gamma_(C_B))[1_(D_B) W_B,J].       (FI1)

The integrand is defined to be zero off D_B, so FI1 invokes no
extension of its local formulas to all real fields. The cap mass
is retained by FI1, not divided out. Xi_Omega retains the amplitude
fluctuations, four original source differences and moving boundary.
The same factor enters the common normalization. Derivatives in the
shifted variables must differentiate D_B as well. It is not
legitimate to erase that term because Omega was fixed before
changing variables. Nor is Z_Omega the full history sum.

**GAP, explicit remaining test.** The work in GD1--GN6 controls
the Gaussian determinant and the stationary amplitude, not
log Xi_Omega. For a Gaussian expansion to advance (A), it must
produce connected complete supports for the derivatives of

**PROVEN failure of an uncut fourth-order replacement in the actual

This proves smallness per local factor of the exact integrand on

**PROVEN skeptical test of dropping the cutoff.** This problem already

Here Phi is the standard normal distribution function; the interval

The retained claims survive those checks at their stated scopes:

They do not give the six-group/four-original-source jet of Xi_Omega,

### 4.25 Hard Gaussian cutoffs: a connected logarithm with its mass retained

**PROVEN at B=0 for the Gaussian reference in FI1.** This is a
different route from Taylor expanding the unbounded fluctuation.
It treats the cutoff boundary by Gaussian integration by parts.
The result concerns a normalized Gaussian cutoff probability, not
the weighted Xi_Omega or the actual nonlinear backward measure.

Use C=H^-1 at the zero-source, zero-output bare branch, with its
three colour components. Choose 0<sigma_1<sigma_0<eta_G and the
TB1 bound K_H at sigma_0. The real fine-coordinate index set is
denoted I_all, with n_A indices per coarse block. Put

    c=1/M_G,  Lambda=1/g,  c I<=C<=Lambda I,
    f_i(zeta_i)=1_(|zeta_i|>R),
    Z_R=E_(gamma_C) product_(i in I_all)(1-f_i)
       =P_(gamma_C)(|zeta_i|<=R for every i).          (CF1)

In FI7 the choice is R=u^delta. Every f_i refers to an actual free
fine coordinate, not a replica of a coordinate used at another
vertex. That restriction is essential below.

**PROVEN uniform covariance bounds during the forest interpolation.**
For a finite set I of n distinct coordinates and a labelled tree T
on I, give each edge e a parameter h_e in [0,1]. Let W_ii=1 and
W_ij be the minimum h along the T path from i to j. For a forest,
put W_ij=0 across components. The matrix W is positive semidefinite:
it is the integral from 0 to 1 of the matrices that are one within
each component of the edges with h_e>=s and zero between components.
Each such matrix is a direct sum of all-ones positive matrices.
For the principal covariance C_I define

    Sigma_(T,h)=C_I entrywise-multiplied by W(T,h).

The Schur product theorem, applied to C_I-cI and Lambda I-C_I,
gives at every h, including its boundary,

    c I<=Sigma_(T,h)<=Lambda I.                     (CF2)

No positivity of individual off-diagonal entries of C is assumed.
The Schur product assertion follows, for example, by writing both
positive matrices as Gram matrices and using tensor products of
their Gram vectors.

**PROVEN derivative estimate including hard boundaries.** Let
Sigma be any real covariance obeying CF2. Decompose a Gaussian
zeta=eta+xi, with eta_i independent of variance c and xi an
independent Gaussian of covariance Sigma-cI. For smooth bounded
one-coordinate functions F_i, integration by parts in eta_i gives

    E_Sigma product_i F_i^(d_i)(zeta_i)
       =c^(-sum_i d_i/2) E_(xi,eta)
            product_i [He_(d_i)(eta_i/sqrt(c)) F_i(xi_i+eta_i)].

Here He_d is the probabilists' Hermite polynomial, with
E[He_d(G)^2]=d! for G a standard normal. This identity and norm
follow by differentiating the Gaussian density, or from the
generating identity exp(tG-t^2/2). Conditional on xi, apply
Cauchy--Schwarz separately in each independent eta_i, then apply
Cauchy--Schwarz once in xi. This proves

    |E_Sigma product_i F_i^(d_i)|
       <= c^(-sum d_i/2) product_i sqrt(d_i!)
                            [E_Sigma product_i |F_i|^2]^(1/2).   (CF3)

The product of degree factorials is important. Replacing it by
sqrt((sum d_i)!) would lose the tree summation proved below.
For F_i=f_i, all n coordinates must have squared sum greater
than nR^2. Since Sigma<=Lambda I,

    P_Sigma(all |zeta_i|>R)
       <=exp[-nR^2/(4Lambda)]
                         E_Sigma exp(||zeta||^2/(4Lambda))
       <=2^(n/2) exp[-nR^2/(4Lambda)].
    epsilon_R=2^(1/4) exp[-R^2/(8Lambda)].             (CF4)

For hard f_i, interpret the derivatives in CF3 distributionally.
To justify this without dropping boundaries, convolve each f_i
with a positive compactly supported mollifier of width epsilon.
The smoothed function remains between zero and one and is supported
where |zeta_i|>R-epsilon. CF3--CF4 apply with that radius, uniformly
in the smoothing width. Its Gaussian moments converge to the hard
ones. The integration-by-parts expression on the right of the
identity also converges by Gaussian domination of the Hermite
polynomials. Taking epsilon down to zero proves the claimed hard
boundary formula and estimate. No analytic cutoff is assumed.

**PROVEN connected moments.** Apply the Brydges--Kennedy Taylor
forest formula to E_Sigma product_i F_i. Its covariance derivatives
are C_ij partial_i partial_j, with no factor of 1/2 for an
off-diagonal pair. The covariance becomes block diagonal across
forest components, so their expectations factor. Comparing with
the moment--cumulant partition identity gives, for distinct indices,

    kappa_I(f)=sum_(T tree on I) w_(I,T),
    w_(I,T)=product_({i,j} in T) C_ij
        int_[0,1]^(n-1) E_(Sigma_(T,h))
                            product_i f_i^(deg_T(i)) dh.     (CF5)

For n=1 the tree is empty and w_(i,empty)=E f_i. The exact forest
identity and its Gaussian application are the standard results in
[Abdesselam--Rivasseau, Theorem III.1 and section V.A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9409094).
Its hypotheses here are checked as follows: the dimension is finite,
CF2 keeps all evaluated covariances uniformly positive, and Gaussian
moments of the bounded smoothed functions are smooth in covariance
on that open positive cone. The compact set of forest covariances
lies inside that cone, so the function can be smoothly extended
off a neighborhood of that compact set if the formula is stated
on the full parameter space. Only values and derivatives inside
the cone occur. The preceding mollifier limit supplies the hard
functions. No derivative is taken through an unretained boundary.

Equations CF3--CF5 now prove

    |w_(I,T)|<=epsilon_R^n c^(-(n-1))
          product_({i,j} in T)|C_ij|
          product_(i in I) sqrt(deg_T(i)!).            (CF6)

This is an all-degree estimate with an explicit common Gaussian
tail price. There is no multiplication of separate conditional
rough probabilities and no discarded conditioning mass.

**PROVEN supported tree sum.** Give a singleton its fine-link
endpoint blocks. For n>=2 attach the decorated TB1 path P_ij to
each tree edge, retaining all vertex endpoint blocks as well.
Denote the resulting connected set by X(I,T). It has all declared
spatial addresses of this fixed Gaussian calculation. Introduce

    D=K_H/c,
    A_0=e sqrt(6),
    a_R=exp(1+2sigma_0) epsilon_R,
    t_R=A_0 D a_R,
    s_R=a_R[1-A_0 log(1-t_R)]       (t_R<1).          (CF7)

The factor exp(2sigma_0) pays each vertex's endpoint blocks, and
exp(1) pays exp(|I|) for the polymer criterion below. Both may
overcount; neither omits an address. For any fixed coordinate i,
the tree bound gives

    sum_(I containing i,T) exp(sigma_0 |X(I,T)|+|I|)
                                            |w_(I,T)| <= s_R.     (CF8)

Here is the combinatorial estimate in full. For a specified labelled
tree with its first coordinate fixed at i, summing the remaining
coordinate choices by deleting leaves bounds its edge product by
D^(n-1). Dropping the constraint that these coordinates be distinct
only increases this nonnegative majorant; the formula CF5 itself
still uses distinct coordinates. Divide by (n-1)! for unordered I.
The degree-weighted labelled-tree sum is, by the Pruefer bijection,

    S_n=sum_(T on {1,...,n}) product_i sqrt(deg_T(i)!)
       =(n-2)! [z^(n-2)]
                     [sum_(k>=0) sqrt((k+1)!)/k! z^k]^n.

The series at z=1 is bounded using Cauchy--Schwarz:

    sum_(k>=0) sqrt(k+1)/sqrt(k!)
       <= [sum_k 2^(-k)]^(1/2)
                      [sum_k (k+1)2^k/k!]^(1/2)=e sqrt(6)=A_0.

Therefore S_n/(n-1)!<=A_0^n/(n-1), n>=2. The size-n term in
CF8 is at most a_R A_0 t_R^(n-1)/(n-1); its sum is
-a_R A_0 log(1-t_R). The singleton contribution is at most a_R.
This proves CF8 without an uncomputed tree-count constant.

An explicit sufficient choice for the smallness used next is

    R^2 >=8Lambda log[exp(1+2sigma_0) 2^(1/4)
                            max{2A_0 D,4(1+A_0)}].           (CF9)

It gives t_R<=1/2 and s_R<=a_R(1+A_0)<=1/4. For R=u^delta,
raise the right side of CF9 to the power 1/(2delta) to obtain
a sufficient u. This is a Gaussian-cutoff threshold, not the
return threshold u_*; the g used here is the proved uniform
constant, not the minimum on a numerical momentum grid.

**PROVEN exact polymer identity and convergence of its logarithm.**
Inclusion--exclusion in CF1, followed by the moment--cumulant identity
and CF5, represents Z_R exactly as a finite abstract polymer gas.
Its polymers are P=(I,T), with activity z(P)=(-1)^|I| w_(I,T).
Two polymers are incompatible precisely when their coordinate
sets I intersect. Different trees on the same set are different
polymers and are incompatible. To check the identity, start with
each selected coordinate set S in inclusion--exclusion, partition
S into cumulant blocks, and choose one tree in each block. This
is a bijection with the compatible polymer collections. No
history probability has been divided out.

Set Q(P)=exp(sigma_0 |X(P)|)|z(P)| and r(P)=Q(P)exp(|I(P)|).
By CF8--CF9, r(P)<=s_R<=1/4 and

    sum_(Q incompatible with P) r(Q)<=|I(P)| s_R,
    product_(Q incompatible with P)(1-r(Q))
       >=exp[-(4/3)|I(P)|s_R]>=exp[-|I(P)|/3].

Thus Q(P)<=r(P) product_(Q incompatible with P)(1-r(Q)).
Including P itself in the last majorant can only strengthen the
sufficient estimate. This verifies the Dobrushin polymer criterion.
The cluster theorem in
[Kotecky, Cluster expansion, section 2, equations (24) and theorem (i)--(ii)](https://www.cts.cuni.cz/~kotecky/publ/K-CE-EMP06.pdf)
then gives an absolutely convergent logarithm. Its sum of absolute
terms containing a specified polymer P, at the weighted radii Q,
is at most -log(1-r(P))<=(4/3)r(P). All its nonzero clusters are
connected in the incompatibility graph. The polymer set is finite;
the infinite sum is over cluster multiplicities, which this bound
controls. These are exactly the hypotheses and conclusion used.

Consequently the sum of weighted logarithm terms whose coordinate
union contains a specified i is at most (4/3)s_R. Each such cluster
has a connected complete spatial support: its individual X(P) are
connected, and an incompatibility shares an actual coordinate and
therefore its endpoint blocks. Assign the cluster to the least
coordinate in its coordinate union. Applying the GD4 anchor argument
with Delta=sigma_0-sigma_1 yields

    log Z_R=sum_(X connected) L_(R,X),
    sup_v sum_(X containing v) exp(sigma_1 |X|)|L_(R,X)|
                       <=(4/3)n_A S_Delta s_R.         (CF10)

Here S_Delta is exactly the constant in GD4. Spatial supports of
compatible polymers may overlap along their decorated paths; the
proof has not changed incompatibility to path intersection. The
logarithm uses the original coordinate-intersection rule, whose
connected clusters have connected path unions as just proved.
This distinction preserves the exact identity.

Since activities are real, the nonvanishing theorem along their
straight scaling from zero fixes the real logarithm of the positive
probability Z_R. FI8's extensive cutoff mass is therefore retained
as a small rooted connected logarithm; it has not been set to one.

**Scope and GAP.** CF10 is a scalar statement at B=0 about the
Gaussian factor. It has no original source or output-field jets.
At nonzero B the unexposed covariance entries inside the expectation
in CF5 also depend on B; attaching paths only to the displayed
tree edges would not capture those functional readers. No such
extension is claimed. Neither is replacing W_B,J in FI7 by one.
The nonlinear local factors have overlapping fine-coordinate readers,
and their direct insertion into this proof fails the next test.

### 4.26 Hostile tests: smooth partitions and duplicate Gaussian readers

**PROVEN failure of the proposed analytic-cutoff estimate.** A local
smooth partition 1=chi_R+(1-chi_R), with chi_R equal to one inside
R and zero outside 2R, retains the complementary mass exactly.
The attempted forest estimate would require derivative bounds at
unbounded orders, since a tree vertex can have degree n-1. Bounds
through the six output derivatives are not bounds on that degree.
Moreover, no nonzero compactly supported smooth chi can satisfy

    ||chi^(m)||_infinity<=A B^m m!    for every m                 (SP1)

with finite A,B. Taylor's remainder on any interval shorter than
1/B would tend to zero under SP1. The function would be real
analytic, yet it vanishes on an open interval and equals one on
another, a contradiction. This rejects that proposed all-order
majorant, not smooth partitions themselves. Gevrey bounds, finite
order interpolation with retained remainders, and Gaussian boundary
integration remain possible; they have not been identified with SP1.
CF3 uses the Gaussian density's Hermite derivatives instead.

**PROVEN failure of a direct extension of CF2 to overlapping
interaction replicas.** Two actual FI6 vertices can share a fine
coordinate. For example the free direction-1 link at the origin
is read by the (0,1) plaquettes based at the origin and at minus
e_0. Those plaquettes are assigned to different coarse base blocks.
Both have nonconstant Taylor remainders along that link. Thus one
cannot treat the FI6 vertex labels as disjoint physical coordinate
sets in CF2.

If each vertex is nevertheless given a Gaussian replica of its
readers, a shared scalar coordinate of variance v=C_ii>0 has the
interpolated two-replica covariance

    Sigma(h)=v [[1,h],[h,1]],
    lambda_min(Sigma(h))=v(1-h).                       (SP2)

Its value at h=1 must identify the replicas. There is no fixed
positive independent-noise variance throughout this interpolation.
Using the best spectral lower bound in CF3 for this two-vertex
edge costs at least v/[v(1-h)]=1/(1-h). Its h integral diverges.
This proves failure of that majorant for this direct extension;
it does not prove divergence of the bounded covariance or of a
correctly organized forest expansion.

An exact check shows the distinction. For the bounded insertion
f(x)=1_(x>R) on this same scalar Gaussian, the retained boundary
formula is

    Cov(f(zeta),f(zeta))
       =int_0^1 exp[-R^2/(v(1+h))]/[2pi sqrt(1-h^2)] dh
       =p(1-p),  p=Phi(-R/sqrt(v)).                   (SP3)

Indeed f'=delta_R, so Gaussian covariance differentiation gives
v times the joint density at (R,R). At h=0 the fields are
independent, and at h=1 they coincide. The displayed singularity
is integrable; the extra loss in 1/(1-h) belongs to the estimate.
This example is a diagnostic for duplicate readers of the actual
Gaussian reference, not a new large-field sector of the chain.

**PROVEN initial repair: group the actual overlapping factors first.**
This statement returns to the weighted first term of FI7, at B=0,
with the four original plaquette sources and their FI5 polydisc.
Set R=u^delta and retain all the FI6 conditions.
Let R_x be the exact set of fine-coordinate readers of V_x, and
T_x=N_(infinity,1)(x) its complete block container. Then
|R_x|<=D_loc, |T_x|<=81. Define a bounded global function by

    F_x(zeta)=1_(|zeta_i|<=R for all i in R_x)
                                              [exp(V_x(zeta))-1].

Only evaluate V_x where the indicator is one; set F_x=0 elsewhere.
The FI6 conditions make this definition legitimate, and
||F_x||_infinity<=epsilon_V:=exp(lambda_u)-1. At B=0 the branch
is zero, so these fine-coordinate reader sets are local without
unexpanded output dependencies. Zero factors may be omitted; every
remaining R_x is nonempty since V_x(0)=0. The exact weighted good-sector
integral is

    Z_good(0;J)=E_gamma[1_(Gamma_u) W_(0,J)]
       =E_gamma product_i(1-f_i) product_x(1+F_x).     (RCF1)

Expand this finite product. Make a graph of the selected x vertices,
joining x,y exactly when R_x intersects R_y. For a connected
component S write

    Phi_S=product_(x in S) F_x,
    R(S)=union_(x in S) R_x,
    T(S)=union_(x in S) T_x.

A selected bad factor -f_i vanishes in the product if i belongs to
any selected R_x: its coordinate is outside the same cutoff that
F_x requires inside. All nonzero terms therefore consist of
reader-disjoint objects of just two types: a connected component S
with function Phi_S, or a singleton coordinate i with function -f_i.
Consequently

    Z_good(0;J)=sum_(collections of reader-disjoint objects beta)
                                     E_gamma product_beta Phi_beta.  (RCF2)

This is an exact regrouping, not a factorization of the expectations.
Each selected x set has a unique partition into overlap components;
conversely a reader-disjoint collection of connected components has
exactly that partition. This proves the counting in RCF2. All
complementary terms of FI7 remain outside Z_good with their mass.

The overlap graph has degree at most 5^4-1=624: intersecting readers
put x and y within coarse infinity distance two. Rooted connected
sets of size m in a graph of maximum degree 624 number at most
2496^(m-1). To see this, choose a rooted spanning tree and its
depth-first order. There are at most 4^(m-1) plane rooted tree
shapes, and at most 624 choices for each child edge. Counting
repeated or inadmissible embeddings only enlarges the bound.
Also ||Phi_S||_infinity<=epsilon_V^|S| and |T(S)|<=81|S|.
For any a>0 put beta_V=epsilon_V exp(81sigma_0+a). At most 81
possible vertex anchors x have i in R_x. Hence, if 2496 beta_V<1,

    sup_i sum_(S connected, i in R(S))
              exp(sigma_0 |T(S)|+a|S|)||Phi_S||_infinity
                         <=81 beta_V/(1-2496 beta_V).         (RCF3)

### 4.27 Full-posterior projective and variance routes; regeneration remains GAP

This attempt concerns the actual full posterior at every finite depth,
at zero sources and N=2. Histories are summed before differentiating.
No cap, stationary approximation, Gaussian law or fresh Wilson comparator
replaces it. The following identities test a proposed route; none closes
U1--U4 or counts as a return result.

**PP1 — PROVEN (exact likelihood geometry).** Write omega=(U_0,...,U_j)
and t=t_j. If V and V' differ only at the terminal link c, positivity gives

    d pi_j^(V')/d pi_j^V(omega)
      = exp(t [h_M(V'_c)-h_M(V_c)])
        / E_(pi_j^V) exp(t [h_M(V'_c)-h_M(V_c)]),           (PP1)

where M=M_c(U_j). All earlier densities and the input-dependent kernel
normalizers cancel in this quotient; the displayed posterior expectation
does not disappear. Define d_H(p,q)=ess osc log(dp/dq). Then

    d_H(pi_j^(V'),pi_j^V)=2t |v'_c-v_c|.                   (PP2)

For the upper bound use |m|<=1. For equality, every unit quaternion q
is an attainable mean at c: put the same commuting link q^(1/L) on
every fine link in c's direction, and identity in the other directions.
Each paired contour has signed displacement L in that direction, so
every contour product is q, independently of its anchor and ordering.
This connection is flat. Taking q=+/-(v'_c-v_c)/|v'_c-v_c| gives both
extrema. At finite couplings the complete past density is continuous
and strictly positive on the compact group product. Neighborhoods of
these configurations have positive posterior mass, so the extrema also
give the essential oscillation. This uses the sum of all histories;
it does not assert positivity inside each individual hard sector.
For V_c=I and V'_c=exp(i alpha sigma_3), PP2 is 4t|sin(alpha/2)|.

**PP2 — PROVEN (failure of a score-oscillation certificate).** For a
unit left-invariant output direction, let w be its unit tangent quaternion
at v and D_c=h_c partial_c. Since Z_t(M) is independent of V,

    s_c=D_c log k_t(V_c;M)=t h_c m dot w,
    D_c log pi_j^V=s_c-E_(pi_j^V)s_c,
    ess osc(s_c)=ess osc(D_c log pi_j^V)=2t h_c.            (PP3)

The last equality follows from the same attainable unit means. With
h_c=R_c sqrt(2/u_j), this equals 2 kappa R_c sqrt(2u_j).
For fixed positive R_c it is unbounded. Even terminal displacements
of order u_j^(-1/2) have growing projective distance; they fit within
the declared larger small-field threshold as u_j increases. Thus
posterior normalization alone does not give a uniform score-oscillation
constant. The bound |Cov(F,s_c)|<=osc(F)osc(s_c)/4 obtained this way
loses sqrt(u_j). This is a failed upper bound, not a lower bound on
the actual covariance. No estimate of the mass near these extrema
has been made.

**PP3 — PROVEN (diameter of the actual positive kernel).** Let n be
the number of coarse links. Its four-point diameter is

    Delta(K_j)=sup_(U,U',V,V')
      log[K_j(V,U)K_j(V',U')/(K_j(V,U')K_j(V',U))]
      =4t_j n.                                           (PP4)

All input normalizers cancel. For each link the remaining expression
is t_j(m-m') dot(v-v'), bounded by 4t_j. For equality take U+=I.
On an even coarse torus put g(X)=(-1)^(sum_a X_a) I at its roots,
extend g constantly over each fine block, and take the pure-gauge
fine connection U-_xy=g(x)g(y)^*. Every paired contour telescopes
between roots, giving M_c(U-)=-I for every c. Take V+=I and V-=-I
on all links. Both means have norm one, and each link contributes
4t_j to PP4. Both input Wilson actions vanish.

The positive integral operator on continuous functions of the full
past has the same projective diameter: multiplication by its strictly
positive past density cancels in cross ratios, and continuous inputs
concentrated near the two specified past configurations approach its
extremal columns. Division by the actual terminal density, to form
the Bayes operator, is a fixed positive output multiplier and also
preserves this diameter. Compactness and strict positivity give a
bounded linear map into the interior of the cone of positive continuous
functions. The Birkhoff contraction estimate consequently supplies
tanh(Delta/4)=tanh(t_j n), which approaches one. The theorem and its
cone hypotheses are stated in
[Gaubert--Qu, Dobrushin's ergodicity coefficient for Markov operators on cones, Theorem 5.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1307.4649).
This global estimate cannot serve as a fixed lambda<1 in H5.
It does not estimate the operator after tensor extraction or imply
that the local operator has no smaller bound.

**PP4 — PROVEN (gauge-inequivalent robustness check, not a local
counterexample).** PP3's extremizing pair is gauge equivalent. To test
whether that alone explains the global problem, take terminal fields
V^0=I and V^alpha with every direction-1 link exp(i alpha sigma_3)
and all other links identity. If that direction has n_1 sites and
0<alpha<pi/n_1, their holonomy traces are 2 and 2cos(n_1 alpha),
so they are not gauge equivalent. Both fields are flat. For any depth j,
choose the two full pasts omega^0 and omega^alpha with

    U^alpha_(l,1)=exp(i alpha/L^(j+1-l) sigma_3),
    U^alpha_(l,mu)=I for mu!=1,  0<=l<=j.                  (PP5)

Every paired mean at level l matches the next field. All original
plaquettes and every matched fresh gap vanish. All means have norm
one, so the earlier kernel factors, including their normalizers,
are identical between these two pasts. In the four posterior densities
the two posterior normalizers rho_(j+1)(V^0) and rho_(j+1)(V^alpha)
also cancel. With B the terminal number of sites, the exact cross ratio is

    log[pi_j^(V^0)(omega^0) pi_j^(V^alpha)(omega^alpha)
        /(pi_j^(V^0)(omega^alpha) pi_j^(V^alpha)(omega^0))]
      =2t_j B(1-cos alpha).                               (PP6)

Here pi(omega) denotes density with respect to the common product Haar
measure, not the probability of a point. Continuity and positivity
justify the corresponding essential-distance lower bound as in PP1.
Increasing transverse volume at fixed n_1, alpha and t_j makes PP6
unbounded. Alternatively alpha=u_j^(-1/2+delta)/2, with 0<delta<1/2,
gives growth of order B u_j^(2delta) at fixed finite depth and large
comparable couplings. The fields can be arbitrarily close to identity. But this
test changes B links, and holonomy is nonlocal. It supplies no local
covariance lower bound and does not show U1, U3 or U4 false.

**PP5 — PROVEN (posterior variance identity).** Let partial_(c,a),
a=1,2,3, be the unweighted left-invariant directions generated by
i sigma_a, and Delta_c=sum_a partial_(c,a)^2. For every M,
partial_(c,a)^2 h_M(V_c)=-h_M(V_c), because (i sigma_a)^2=-I.
Thus partial_(c,a)^2 log k_t=-t h_M. Differentiating the complete
finite compact integral for rho_(j+1) twice gives

    partial_(c,a)^2 log rho_(j+1)
      =-t E_pi h_M(V_c)+Var_pi(partial_(c,a) log k_t).

Equivalently, sum the three derivatives of the normalized score in
PP3; differentiating its posterior expectation supplies that same
positive variance. With s_(c,a)=h_c partial_(c,a) log k_t,

    sum_a Var_pi(s_(c,a))
      =h_c^2 Delta_c log rho_(j+1)(V)
        +3t h_c^2 E_pi h_M(V_c)
      <=h_c^2 Delta_c log rho_(j+1)(V)+6 kappa R^2.        (PP7)

All expectations are under pi=pi_j^V. For any real square-integrable
F of the past with no explicit terminal dependence, Cauchy--Schwarz gives

    sum_a |D_(c,a) E_pi F|^2
      <=Var_pi(F) sum_a Var_pi(s_(c,a)).                  (PP8)

There is no normalization loss hidden in PP7. There is also no upper
bound here for its unknown output curvature. A bound on that curvature
would control score variance, but PP8 would still lack spatial decay
and complete connected supports. Differentiating PP7 in an original
source J_i produces the cumulants Cum_pi(O_i,s_(c,a),s_(c,a)) and
Cov_pi(O_i,h_M), together with the corresponding derivative of the
unknown log rho_(j+1). It does not supply the (6;[4]) hierarchy.

**PP6 — GAP (the attempted regeneration).** No bound for the output
curvature in PP7 has been derived from H0--H4 at j. Estimating it by
asserting the successor reference, coefficient and residual bounds
uses H(j+1) to prove H(j+1). The global kernel route supplies no
uniform contraction constant, and the variance route supplies no
closed estimate even before support summation and extraction.
Neither accounts for the coherent branch's unused original credits
or proves that they can be reused at j+1. These failures leave U1--U4
open. They do not establish that any of U1--U4 is false as stated;
therefore no induction edit or counterexample status is proposed.
The count stays four and counted progress for this attempt is zero.

**Hostile check — PROVEN scope, GAP promotion.** PP1--PP4 are statements
about extrema of density ratios, not the actual distribution of their
mass. Their extremizers cannot be inserted as a two-point posterior
without changing the model. Gauge equivalence invalidates a physical
counterexample based on PP3; PP4 removes that equivalence but leaves
an extensive, nonlocal test. PP7's sign was checked by both quotient
differentiation and the centered-score calculation. Its missing upper
curvature bound is exactly where a proposed regeneration argument would
be circular. None of these calculations establishes the required local
bound (A), pays the bad-sector covariance, or justifies dropping tensor
extraction. The failure is confined to these two proposed estimates.

### 4.28 Conditioning on rough fields: a causal comparison and its failed return

The new route disintegrates the actual full posterior. Everything below
is at N=2 and zero sources.
It supplies a comparison for specified original conditional excitations at
every finite depth. It does not close U1--U4; counted progress is zero.

**PC1 — PROVEN (both covariance terms retained).** For any fixed sigma
field R of past readers, with pi=pi_j^V and s_c=D_c log k_j,

    Cov_pi(F,s_c)=E_pi[Cov_pi(F,s_c | R)]
                    +Cov_pi(E_pi[F | R],E_pi[s_c | R]).     (PC1)

In particular, for a past event E independent of the terminal variable,
put p=pi(E), G=E^c, f_E=E_pi[F|E], s_E=E_pi[s_c|E], and similarly on G.
Zero-mass terms are omitted. Expanding the same normalized expectations gives

    Cov_pi(F,s_c)=(1-p)Cov_G(F,s_c)+p Cov_E(F,s_c)
                     +p(1-p)(f_E-f_G)(s_E-s_G),
    D_c p=p(1-p)(s_E-s_G).                                (PC2)

Thus a convexity estimate within each sector would leave the last term.
The p here is a probability under the common full posterior; substituting
an original or forward probability for it is not an identity.

**PC2 — PROVEN (causal disintegration of the full path).** Fix a set D_0
of d>=1 original links. Recursively let D_(l+1) contain every output link
whose actual paired contours read a link in D_l. These sets depend on
the geometry, not on field values. Write z=U_0 restricted to D_0 and
w=U_0 outside D_0. Every kernel for a link outside D_(l+1) reads only
variables outside D_l. Consequently the entire outside path eta is an
autonomous Markov chain given w; its density is independent of z. Given
eta, all the inside kernels remain normalized transition densities.
This is a factorization of the original positive path density, with all
history selectors summed. It is not independence under the pinned law.

Let q_(eta,j)(z;V_D) be the density of the terminal variables in D_(j+1),
conditional on eta and z, after integrating the earlier inside variables.
For a quaternion mean of norm r<=1, write Z(s)=Z_s(I). Monotonicity of
e^s/Z(s) and e^(-s)/Z(s) follows by differentiating their logarithms:
the derivatives are 1-E_s cos(theta)>=0 and -1-E_s cos(theta)<=0.
Therefore every actual last kernel satisfies

    a_t=e^(-t)/Z(t) <= k_t(V;M) <= e^t/Z(t)=b_t,
    a_t^m <= q_(eta,j)(z;V_D) <= b_t^m,
    b_t^m/a_t^m=exp(2t m),  m=|D_(j+1)|, t=t_j.          (PC3)

The middle inequality integrates normalized earlier inside kernels.
No factor from an earlier level, or from the outside volume, is discarded.
If mu_0^w is the original Wilson conditional law of z, the conditional
law inside the actual pinned posterior is exactly

    pi_j^V(dz | eta)
      =mu_0^w(dz) q_(eta,j)(z;V_D)
        / int mu_0^w(dz') q_(eta,j)(z';V_D).              (PC4)

For any nonnegative original insertion A(z,w), PC3/PC4 imply

    E_(pi_j^V)[A | eta] <= exp(2t_j m) E_(mu_0^w) A.      (PC5)

The denominator is the actual conditional terminal integral. Averaging
PC5 over the actual posterior distribution of eta preserves this bound
whenever its right side is uniform in w.

The cone has a depth-independent cardinality bound for a local D_0.
Let q_0 be the number of original blocks meeting its endpoints. Every
paired contour stays within its two endpoint blocks, including their
connecting edges. Output endpoints therefore lie in N_1 of the block
projection of the input endpoints. The elementary inclusion
pi(N_2 S) subset N_1(pi S), valid for odd L>=3, gives by induction at
most q_0 translates of an N_2 ball at every subsequent scale. Hence

    |D_(l+1)| <= m_*=4(5^4)q_0=2500q_0.                 (PC6)

Periodic identification only decreases cardinality. This is a forward
reader cone, not the complete support of a posterior expectation.

**PC3 — PROVEN (an original excitation price with an excitation weight).**
Let P(D_0) be the original plaquettes reading D_0 and define, at fixed w,

    H_D(z;w)=sum_(p in P(D_0)) f_p(z,w),
    m_D(w)=min_z H_D(z;w),  e_D(z;w)=H_D(z;w)-m_D(w),
    E_D={e_D>=Delta},  0<Delta<=1.

The compact minimum exists. Fix 0<=b<1, gamma=1-b and
delta=gamma Delta/(12d). Changing each of the d links by operator distance
at most delta changes H_D by at most 6d delta: each link belongs to six
plaquettes and the unitary product telescoping bound gives one delta per
incidence. A product ball about a minimizer therefore has e_D<=gamma Delta/2.
The probability Haar mass of an SU(2) operator ball of radius delta<=1
is at least c_H delta^3, c_H=8/(3 pi^3). Indeed it contains the angle
ball 0<=theta<=delta, whose radial density is (2/pi)sin^2(theta), and
sin(theta)>=2theta/pi on this interval. It follows directly that

    E_(mu_0^w)[1_(E_D) exp(b u_0 e_D)]
      <=(c_H delta^3)^(-d) exp(-gamma u_0 Delta/2).        (PC7)

For completeness, the numerator before normalization is at most
exp(-u_0 m_D-gamma u_0 Delta), while the denominator is at least
(c_H delta^3)^d exp(-u_0 m_D-gamma u_0 Delta/2). Their common minimum
energy cancels. No claim that m_D(w)=0 or that a minimizer is smooth is made.
This estimates the excitation regulator exp(b u_0 e_D). Although e_D<=H_D,
a bound on this smaller regulator does not bound exp(b u_0 H_D).

**PC4 — PROVEN (bound under the actual full posterior).** Combining
PC5 and PC7, for every terminal V, finite depth and positive coupling prefix,

    E_(pi_j^V)[1_(E_D) exp(b u_0 e_D)] <= A_(D,j),
    A_(D,j)=(c_H delta^3)^(-d)
              exp(2 kappa u_j |D_(j+1)|-gamma u_0 Delta/2). (PC8)

To retain a literal fraction of the incident original action, keep
exp(b u_0 m_D(w)) as well. Since 0<=m_D<=H_D<=12d, the actual bound is

    E_(pi_j^V)[1_(E_D) exp(b u_0 H_D)]
       <=A_(D,j)^full:=exp(12d b u_0) A_(D,j).            (PC8r)

The extra cost cannot be deleted. Smallness of this full-regulator bound
requires 12d b+2 kappa (u_j/u_0)|D_(j+1)|<gamma Delta/2, with further
room for the prefactor. This is not a regenerated H2 certificate.

In particular p<=min(1,A_(D,j)) when b=0. As |s_c|<=t_j h_c,
for a bounded past F with no explicit terminal dependence,

    |D_c E_(pi_j^V)[F 1_(E_D) exp(b u_0 e_D)]|
       <=2t_j h_c ||F||_infinity A_(D,j).                (PC9)

The full-regulator insertion obeys the same bound with A_(D,j)^full.
This is an actual normalized response estimate for this insertion,
not for the unweighted full F in (A). The mixture term in PC2 is likewise
at most 2t_j h_c osc(F) A_(D,j) with b=0. Its good-sector covariance
remains to be bounded. These bounds are useful only when their displayed
exponent is sufficiently negative. For example, if u_j<=u_0 and
2 kappa m_*<=gamma Delta/4, PC8 has exponent at most -gamma u_0 Delta/4.
This implication is not a feasible choice of all the H5 constants.

There is also a joint version without separate posterior normalizations.
For original sets D_i whose incident plaquette sets are disjoint, their
original conditional laws factor after fixing the common exterior.
Repeat PC3 for each i and use the union of their causal cones in PC2.
Then

    E_pi product_i [1_(E_(D_i)) exp(b_i u_0 e_(D_i))]
      <= product_i (c_H delta_i^3)^(-d_i)
         exp(2t_j |union_i D_(i,j+1)|
                    -u_0 sum_i (1-b_i)Delta_i/2).        (PC10)

Overlapping terminal cones are integrated jointly; they do not produce
independent conditional probabilities. To replace every e_(D_i) by H_(D_i),
multiply the bound by exp(12u_0 sum_i d_i b_i), as in PC8r. Disjoint original
plaquette sets prevent repeated allocation to a plaquette in this one
comparison. PC10 is not asserted for overlapping incident sets without
an additional allocation argument, or for previously spent fractions.

**PC5 — PROVEN failure of the proposed good-sector classification.**
The excitation complement e_D<Delta need not have small plaquettes or
good-region convexity. Use the actual zero-staple core of RC2 at y=0:
D_0 is U_1(0)=q, the three positive parallel neighbors are I, the three
negative parallel neighbors are -I, and the transverse links are I.
Its six plaquette costs are three copies each of 1-q_0 and 1+q_0. Thus

    H_D(q;w)=m_D(w)=6,  e_D(q;w)=0 for every q,
    max_(p in P(D_0)) f_p(q,w)>=1 for every q.            (PC11)

This is not just an isolated exterior. If every incident exterior link
lies within operator distance epsilon of those values, each three-link
staple changes by at most 3epsilon. Their sum has quaternion norm at
most 18epsilon, so max_q e_D<=36epsilon. At least one plaquette cost
is >=1-3epsilon for every q. Taking epsilon=Delta/100 gives e_D<Delta
everywhere in this core, while a plaquette cost remains >=1/2. This
finite exterior cylinder has positive mass under every finite full
posterior, by strict positivity. No uniform lower bound on that mass
is claimed. The price in PC7 deliberately cancels m_D; it therefore
does not pay for these frustrated minima. The surrounding original
action may pay for them, but that joint comparison is still required.
The candidate rule "small conditional excitation implies good field"
is rejected. It was not a clause of H(j), so PC11 refutes none of U1--U4.

**PC6 — GAP (regeneration and complete supports).** PC8 is a bound for
one terminal level, already uniform in depth when its scalar exponent
condition holds. It can be reapplied to the same unweighted original
event without multiplying costs. This does not justify deleting the
majorants and allocated charges of an inherited history. If a previous
majorant is bounded by exp(a u_0 H_D), the full-regulator calculation uses
gamma=1-a-b and the cost 12d(a+b)u_0, not gamma=1-b and 12d b u_0.
Previously spent fractions do not reset.

Multiplying the comparison at each level is another possible attempt,
and fails a direct budget check. At L=3, the actual contour reader cone
of the root link is the eight links incident to the root after one step;
that star maps to itself. Repeated PC5 estimates therefore spend
16 kappa sum_(l<R) u_l, before the Haar prefactors. A fixed fraction
of this core's original action is at most 12d u_0. For a prescribed
constant positive coupling prefix the first quantity is unbounded in R
and the second is fixed. This rejects repeated payment by that finite

the actual connected operator requires such repeated payments.

Finally, the forward cone is not a posterior support theorem: changing
outside terminal fields changes the posterior law of eta in PC4/PC5.
That law includes the original exterior and its correlations. No bound
on this dependence in the complete connected norm has been proved.
Neither the conditional covariance on inherited good components nor
the covariance of conditional means on the excitation complement, which
contains PC11's fields, is controlled by PC8. The mixed source/field
hierarchy and successor domain/reserve
clauses are also unproved. U1--U4 remain GAP; none has been shown false,
and no edit of the current induction is justified by this attempt.

**Hostile check — PROVEN comparison, GAP promotion.** PC4 conditions

The bounds average a nonnegative insertion under one posterior and do

**CS2 — PROVEN (a center surgery with surface action cost).** In integer

### 4.30 Two replicas of the actual posterior: exact response and failed positive return

This route is new to this manuscript. It acts directly on (A), with every
history summed, at arbitrary finite depth. RX1/RX2 are exact at finite
coupling. RX3--RX5 disprove a proposed sufficient connection estimate under
the actual posterior. They do not disprove the connected response itself

**RX1 — PROVEN (a thin-region exchange identity for (A)).** At zero
sources write the actual, unnormalized past density at fixed V as

    p(omega)=exp[-u_0 W(U_0)] product_(l=0,...,j) K_l,
    pi(d omega)=p(omega)d omega/Z(V).

The past coordinate set I contains every link at levels 0 through j.
Factor p into its individual plaquette weights and normalized link
kernels w_a, with their full reader sets A_a subset I. A fixed terminal
output is a parameter of its last kernel, not an integrated coordinate.
Let omega,omega' be independent samples of this same pi. For a fixed
R subset I let tau_R exchange their coordinates in R; it preserves the
product Haar measure and is an involution. Define

    Delta_R=log[p(omega)p(omega') /
                         (p(tau_R omega)p(tau_R omega'))].       (RX1)

Only factors whose readers cross R contribute to Delta_R. A kernel's
normalizer belongs to that factor, including all its input readers.
Neither p nor its normalization is replaced by a separately pinned law.

For bounded real F supported inside R and s=s_c=D_c log k_j supported
outside R, put G=(F(omega)-F(omega'))(s(omega)-s(omega')). Then

    Cov_pi(F,s)=1/2 E_(pi tensor pi) G
               =1/2 E_(pi tensor pi)[G tanh(Delta_R/2)].         (RX2)

To prove the second identity, G changes sign under tau_R and Delta_R
changes sign, whereas G tanh(Delta_R/2) is invariant. On an exchange
orbit with pair densities q and q', tanh(Delta_R/2)=(q-q')/(q+q').
Thus the two sides have equal orbit integrals; integration proves RX2.
All densities are strictly positive at finite coupling. No conditional
mean term from PC1 is removed. This identity alone gives neither decay
nor an estimate of its mixed derivatives.

**RX2 — PROVEN (an exact positive auxiliary connection formula).** This
construction is defined for the actual finite factorization above, not
for a new Gibbs law. For each factor let

    g_a(omega,omega')=w_a(omega)w_a(omega'),
    m_a(omega,omega')=min_(R subset A_a) g_a(tau_R(omega,omega')),
    r_a=g_a-m_a>=0.

The minimum is over finitely many orientations of the same two values
at each reader; m_a is strictly positive and invariant under every such
orientation. Expand the exact product as

    product_a g_a = sum_B product_(a in B) r_a
                                product_(a not in B) m_a.       (RX3)

Together with product Haar measure and normalization Z(V)^2, this
defines a probability on (omega,omega',B) with marginal pi tensor pi.
Given the two replicas, factor occupations are independent with

    P(a in B | omega,omega')=1-m_a/g_a.                         (RX4)

An occupied factor joins all coordinates of A_a into a hyperedge.
If no occupied component meets both the support of F and the support
of s, exchange the union of components meeting F. Every occupied g_a
is either wholly swapped or untouched, and every m_a is orientation
invariant. The joint density and B therefore stay fixed while G changes
sign. With C(F,s) the event that some component meets both supports,

    Cov_pi(F,s)=1/2 E[G 1_C],
    |Cov_pi(F,s)|<=1/2 osc(F) osc(s) P(C).                      (RX5)

These are identities and an inequality under the single augmented
posterior. An unoccupied m_a still reads the values at all its readers:
RX3 does not give independent components of the field measure or the
polymer factorization demanded by H3. It gives only an orientation
cancellation. Also, the minimum need not be differentiable where its
minimizing orientation changes; no (6;[4]) bound is inferred from RX3.

**RX3 — PROVEN (the actual finite-depth zero-energy limiting law).**
Fix N=2, L=3, kappa>0, any finite depth j>=1 and any compatible finite
torus. Set all prescribed couplings u_l=u and the terminal field V=I.
These are actual model chains; their H(j) certificates are not assumed.
For a quaternion mean let r=|M| and H_ker(s)=exp(s)/Z(s). The exact density is

    p_u(omega)=exp[-u E(omega)] product_(l,c) H_ker(kappa u r_(l,c)),
    E=W(U_0)+kappa sum_(l,c) psi_(M_c(U_l))(U_(l+1,c)).        (RX6)

Section 4.14 and Z(s)<=exp(s) give, uniformly for 0<=r<=1,

    1<=H_ker(kappa u r)<=c_0^(-1)(1+kappa u)^(3/2).

There are finitely many kernels in this fixed system, so the product
is bounded by a polynomial P(u). E is continuous and nonnegative,
and E(I)=0. For any epsilon>0 the Haar measure b_epsilon of
{E<epsilon/2} is positive. The actual normalized posterior consequently
satisfies

    pi_u(E>=epsilon)<=P(u) b_epsilon^(-1) exp(-u epsilon/2)
                            -->0.                           (RX7)

All normalizers are included in P. This estimate is used at fixed
volume and depth, not as a volume-uniform concentration rate.

The set E=0 is precisely one gauge orbit with terminal roots fixed.
Indeed W=0 makes every original plaquette identity. The paired paths
are homotopic relative to their endpoints, so their transporters agree
and each mean is unitary. Zero gap then forces the next field to equal
that transporter. This propagates flatness through every level. Since
the terminal field is I, every original periodic holonomy is I as well.
A flat field with trivial periodic holonomies is pure gauge: transport
from a fixed root defines its site gauges independently of the chosen
path. Matching at every level forces these site gauges to have the same
value at all embedded terminal vertices. Fix that common value to I.

More explicitly, let Q be the original sites embedded from the terminal
lattice, and let G be the product of SU(2) over original sites outside Q,
with g(x)=I on Q. The orbit consists of

    U_l(x,mu)=g(L^l x)g(L^l(x+e_mu))^*.                       (RX8)

Conversely RX8 has E=0 and terminal I. Acting by these site gauges on
all levels together leaves every Wilson term, every soft kernel and
every Haar factor invariant; the last outputs are unchanged. Thus the
actual pi_u is G-invariant for every u, not just at E=0.

For completeness the limiting measure follows without selecting a
gauge chart. Average any continuous test function over G. Its average
is continuous and has one constant value on E=0, equal to its Haar
orbit average. Compactness and RX7 then force its pi_u integral to
converge to that constant. Invariance says the original test function
has the same integral as its group average. Therefore pi_u converges
weakly to the Haar pushforward of RX8; the two-replica law converges to
two independent such orbits. The free site gauges in each orbit are
independent Haar variables. This is a statement about the actual full
posterior at every fixed finite depth, not a replacement prior.

**RX4 — PROVEN (occupation saturation at every finite depth).** Consider
an original plaquette factor and any one of its links e. At a pair of
fields on RX8 its two plaquette actions are zero. Exchanging e alone
increases their sum by

    c_p=2[1-Re tr(U'_e U_e^*)/2]>0 almost surely.              (RX9)

To see the formula, cyclically start the plaquette at e; each remaining
transporter is the inverse of that copy's e. The two changed holonomies
are U'_e U_e^* and its inverse. Adjacent original sites cannot both lie
in Q because their separation in Q is at least L^(j+1)>=3. Hence at
least one endpoint is a free site, and U'_e U_e^* has Haar distribution.
It equals I with probability zero. The same exchange-energy difference
defines a continuous c_p away from the orbit as well. RX4 then gives

    P(p not in B | omega,omega')=m_p/g_p<=exp(-u c_p).

For an internal kernel at level l<j, exchange only its output link
v_1,v_2 between the replicas. Its two input normalizers remain exactly
unchanged. In quaternion notation the ratio of changed to original
pair factors is

    exp(-kappa u c_a),
    c_a=(m_1-m_2) dot (v_1-v_2).                            (RX10)

At RX8 one has m_i=v_i, so c_a=|v_1-v_2|^2>0 almost surely.
An output link at level l+1<=j again has at least one endpoint outside
Q when embedded in the original lattice. Its two values are independent
Haar variables. Since m_a is the minimum over all exchanges,
m_a/g_a<=exp(-kappa u c_a).

Weak convergence alone is not a justification for these u-dependent
probabilities. For either type of factor fix delta>0 first. Its empty
probability is at most

    pi_u tensor pi_u(c_a<=delta)+exp(-b u delta),           (RX11)

where b=1 for a plaquette and b=kappa for an internal kernel. Weak
convergence bounds the limsup of the first term by its limiting-orbit
probability on the closed set {c_a<=delta}. Let delta decrease to zero.
RX9/RX10 make that probability zero. Thus every original plaquette
and every internal kernel is occupied with probability tending to one.
There are finitely many such factors; a union bound proves that all of
them are occupied with probability tending to one. No independence
after averaging the replicas is assumed.

The hypergraph of all original plaquettes connects all original links:
plaquettes connect adjacent parallel links and different directions,
and the periodic lattice is connected. Each internal kernel connects
its output link to its nonempty input readers. Induction in l therefore
connects every past coordinate through U_j. It follows that for any
two nonempty past supports, including the readers of F_X and s_c,

    lim_(u-->infinity) P(C(F_X,s_c))=1.                     (RX12)

The last kernels remain in the actual posterior and in RX3 throughout.
Their occupation is unnecessary for this connectivity conclusion;
they are not deleted from the measure.

**RX6 — PROVEN algebraic normalizer check; finite contour realization.**
