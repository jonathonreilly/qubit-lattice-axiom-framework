## 2. A complete-reader mean-substitution proposition

Let a family f_Y(c;z,theta) have connected nonempty base reader Y,
fine slots I_Y with owners in Y, and q_Y=|I_Y|<=k|Y|. The symbol z
collects fixed fine variables, hard caps and contour parameters; theta

c_i, i in I_Y, on |c_i|<r_f, and has a common envelope

    sup_(|c_i|<r_f,z) J f_Y(c;z,theta)<=a_Y,
    Q=sup_x sum_(Y contains x) exp[(mu+2d)|Y|] a_Y<infinity. (ME4)

All these suprema include the admitted real external domain. For an
output atom family (F_lambda,S_lambda), ||F||_(mu;J) means
sup_x sum_(lambda:x in S_lambda) exp(mu|S_lambda|)sup_(theta,z) J F_lambda.
Real external derivatives in this assertion commute with fine Cauchy
integrals, for example by uniform continuity on smaller closed discs.
No assertion of fine analyticity follows from finite external jets alone.
Let b have complete scalar readers anchored at their outputs, with
V_mu(J b)<=t_J. Set theta_b=t_J/r_f<1 and require

    k[-log(1-theta_b)]<=d.                                (ME5)

Then f_Y(b)-f_Y(0) has an absolutely convergent connected complete-reader
expansion and the following MARKED finite-jet norm bound:

    ||f(b)-f(0)||_(mu;J)
       <=Q theta_b/(1-theta_b) [k B_1(d)+1],
    ||f(b)||_(mu;J)
       <=Q {1+theta_b/(1-theta_b)[k B_1(d)+1]}.             (ME6)

This is uniform in volume and in |Y|. The parameter d is an actual
support-rate reserve; its possibly large inverse is retained.

Proof. Write f_Y(c)=sum_nu f_(Y,nu)c^nu. Fine Cauchy and ME4 give
J f_(Y,nu)<=a_Y r_f^(-|nu|). Expand each b factor in its ACTUAL atoms.
A resulting support is Y union every chosen b reader, with repetitions
retained in the coefficient list. It is connected because each added
reader meets Y at its output slot. Cardinality subadditivity bounds its
exp(mu|support|) weight by the product of the original reader weights.
There is no geometric replacement by a ball containing that union.

For fixed Y the positive multiindex series is bounded by

    a_Y product_(i in I_Y)(1-theta_b)^-1,
    changed part <=a_Y[(1-theta_b)^(-q_Y)-1].              (ME7)

A marked cell x is either in Y or in one of the substituted readers.
The first case costs the changed part in ME7. Since
(1-theta)^(-q)-1<=q theta(1-theta)^(-q-1), ME5 and the remaining
d reserve bound its sum by Q k B_1(d)theta_b/(1-theta_b).

For the second case define

    d_x^b(i)=sum_(S contains X_i,x) exp(mu|S|) J b_(i;S).

Choose one occurrence containing x; repeated choices only overcount.
Differentiating the positive product of geometric series shows that
this case is at most

    sum_Y exp(mu|Y|) a_Y (1-theta_b)^(-q_Y-1)
                                  sum_(i in I_Y)d_x^b(i)/r_f. (ME8)

For fixed i the Y sum is rooted at X_i and is <=Q/(1-theta_b),
using ME5. The full marked-vector bound gives sum_i d_x^b(i)<=t_J.
This proves the second term in ME6. All sums are positive majorants,
so Tonelli and uniform convergence on smaller domains justify the
substitution, real termwise jets and grouping by complete support.
The unchanged f_Y(0) part costs <=Q. If I_Y is empty its changed part
is exactly zero; its original field-free scalar is not discarded.

The same argument works for any finite downward-closed external-jet
algebra satisfying the product inequality, with its actual input norm.
It is an extension of the analytic field-map substitution mechanism to
this reader/jet contract, not a new all-history integration theorem.

## 5. Real density translation on its actual marked coordinates

Here is a separate elementary lemma. Let g be a normalized real Gaussian
on a finite scalar set I, with arbitrary real mean, covariance C and
G=C^-1<=B I. For a multiindex nu,

    ||partial^nu g||_1 <=B^(|nu|/2) sqrt(product_i nu_i!). (ME19)

One proof expands exp(z.Z-z.Gz/2), with Z=G(X-mean), whose covariance
is G. Add independent Gaussian Y with covariance B I-G. Conditional
expectation maps the independent-coordinate B-Hermite polynomial at
Z+Y to the G-Hermite polynomial at Z. L2 contraction and the independent
norm B^|nu| product nu_i! prove ME19; Cauchy--Schwarz gives L1.
This is the identity also recorded in
[RJ](REAL_GAUSSIAN_JET_FOREST_ASTRA.md); it is rebuilt here, not supplied
by the pending review of that source. At nu=0 the exact integral is one.
An active marginal uses C[I], not an inverse principal precision.
For the actual RF real component-pair masks, keep the whole positive
core in each component and write C_I(s)=P[I]+E_I(s). The absolute
row bound e and P>=c0 I imply C_I(s)>=(c0-e)I, so B=(c0-e)^-1 works
uniformly for real |s_AB|<=1. Differentiate independent covariance pairs
before evaluating forest path minima; the normalized heat identity puts
all scalar derivatives on the density. Bounded measurable hard factors
are fixed in this artificial operation. This does not presume independent
PSD weakening of a new symmetrized core atom.

Shift only a subset H of I, with m_i=b_i/sigma there and zero elsewhere.
For fixed covariance and its fixed mean, density translation gives

    partial^nu g(x-m_H)
       =sum_(j supported in H) (-m)^j partial^(nu+j)g(x)/j!. (ME20)

Use sqrt((nu_i+j_i)!)<=2^((nu_i+j_i)/2)sqrt(nu_i!)sqrt(j_i!).
For L=sqrt(2B), s=t_J/sigma, z=L s, absolute expansion in the mean's
complete readers and their normalized external jet algebra gives the
following bound when ONLY the mean factors are differentiated:

    L^|nu| sqrt(product nu_i!) 2^(|H|/2) exp(L^2 s^2 |H|). (ME21)

The density itself is held fixed in this assertion. Each coordinate
series is S(z)=sum_(j>=0)z^j/sqrt(j!)<=sqrt(2)exp(z^2), by Cauchy--Schwarz
with weights 2^-j. Thus the density Taylor series converges absolutely
in L1 for every finite m. Its Fourier transform sums to that of the
translated Gaussian, proving equality in ME20. The reader expansion

or merely activated coordinates outside H have no such factor. H empty
gives ME19, and the exact zero-derivative translated normalization is one.
The displayed positive majorant need not preserve those cancellations.

For one marked mean-reader cell x, differentiating the positive series

sum_(j>=0)(j+1)(2z^2)^j/j!=(1+2z^2)exp(2z^2). The ME21 bound is multiplied

    K_tr^real sum_(i in H)d_x^b(i),
    K_tr^real=(L/sigma)sqrt[1+2L^2(t_J/sigma)^2].          (ME22)
