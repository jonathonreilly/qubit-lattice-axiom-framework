**PROVEN — Closure mechanism, not a construction.** Suppose for every finite tuple of continuum test observables the corresponding lattice moments converge, and the finite-cutoff observables obey the same reflection and permutation relations. For a positive-half polynomial F = sum_i c_i F_i, its reflection quadratic form is the finite sum sum_ij conjugate(c_i)c_j omega(theta(F_i)^* F_j). It is a limit of nonnegative numbers, hence nonnegative. Permutation identities pass by the same finite-limit argument. Uniform distributional seminorm bounds pass by taking limits of their inequalities. These observations prove preservation; they do not prove the hypotheses, uniformity, locality, or the required growth in insertion order.

**ARGUED — OS0 is more than bounded block variables.** Bounds at a fixed block resolution can grow without bound when that resolution is refined. Separated-support estimates alone leave a contact-term/extension obligation in formulations that require full Schwinger distributions. A strong exponential-source hypothesis is a possible sufficient input, not the definition of OS0 and not automatically appropriate for every composite field. Positive-flow-time observables have an additional localization problem: heat-flow smoothing in Euclidean time reaches across a reflection plane. One must specify a local field/net formulation and verify the version of reconstruction used, rather than infer it from an abstract compact block algebra.

## 2. Uniqueness is not necessary for OS1

**PROVEN — Exact counterexample to the logical implication.** Let the real scalar field be constant on R^4, phi(x) = c, with law nu = (delta_v + delta_{-v})/2, v nonzero. Define phi(f) = c integral f. All its Schwinger distributions are constant kernels E[c^n], are tempered, have bounds |E[c^n]| <= |v|^n, and are permutation symmetric. Every Euclidean motion fixes every configuration. Reflection fixes c, so for every polynomial F of positive-half smeared fields,

    E[ conjugate(F(theta phi)) F(phi) ] = E[ |F(c)|^2 ] >= 0.

Thus OS0–OS3 hold, while the two phases delta_v and delta_{-v} are distinct and their mixture has connected two-point function v^2 at every separation, hence fails clustering. This is even a lattice-limit example: on every finite lattice put the same two-point law on configurations with every site equal to c and embed them as constant functions; every limit is exactly the displayed field. It is not a Yang–Mills model or a witness of non-free dynamics. It disproves the purported implication from these OS axioms to phase uniqueness and any argument that relies on that implication alone.

**PROVEN — Convexity with a fixed reflection.** If omega_alpha are states on the same local algebra, each invariant under the same Euclidean action and reflection positive for the same theta, then omega = integral omega_alpha dnu(alpha) has both properties: integrate the invariance equality and each nonnegative reflection quadratic form. Uniform/integrable regularity bounds are needed to retain OS0. Multiple phases therefore need not obstruct an invariant reflection-positive state. A convex mixture need not be a limit of the specific fixed-boundary Wilson sequence: that separate realization requirement cannot be skipped.

**PROVEN — Why R9.1 is a warning, not a no-go.** For a rotation R, the reflected quadratic form of R_*omega at a fixed plane theta is that of omega at R^{-1}theta R, tested on the correspondingly rotated half-space. Positivity of omega only for coordinate planes says nothing about that general plane. Consequently Haar averaging rotated states has no positivity proof from coordinate-plane RP alone. Conversely, if omega is RP for every affine hyperplane, all its rotations are RP for the original theta and their average is RP by convexity. If omega was already rotation invariant, averaging changes nothing. Thus “averaging destroys RP” must mean “RP is not guaranteed by the available hypotheses,” not “every such average fails.” R9.1's final qualification recognizes part of this distinction; the categorical summary wording does not.

**ARGUED — A second defect in the uniqueness route.** Uniqueness only forces a symmetry when the symmetry maps the admissible solution class into itself. Uniqueness for one oriented hypercubic regulator does not prove that a rotated continuum field belongs to that same class. A universality/continuum-covariance input remains necessary. Conversely, a theorem that every subsequential limit of a chosen symmetric exhaustion obeys rotation identities would establish OS1 even if many other phases or continuum subsequences exist. UV cutoff uniqueness, thermodynamic uniqueness, uniqueness of an invariant vacuum in one representation, and uniqueness in a regulator-universality class must not share one unnamed E7/H-uniq hypothesis.

**ARGUED — No equivalence to a mass gap has been supplied.** The scale at which perturbation theory fails is a genuine obstacle to global estimates, but neither phase uniqueness nor clustering is synonymous with exponential clustering or a positive spectral gap. Even a uniqueness-first proof would need its own hypotheses and could conceivably use estimates much weaker than gap estimates. The lane has not established that all possible uniqueness proofs cost a mass-gap theorem.

**PROVEN — Unique vacuum does not imply a gap.** In the explicitly defined massless free boson Fock space over L^2(R^3, d^3p), let H act on the n-particle sector by multiplication by sum_i |p_i|, and on the vacuum sector by zero. On every n >= 1 sector this multiplier is positive almost everywhere, so ker H is exactly the one-dimensional vacuum sector. But normalized one-particle wave functions supported in 0 < |p| < epsilon have energies below epsilon for every epsilon > 0. Thus this model has a unique vacuum and no positive spectral gap. This proves the distinction for vacuum uniqueness, not uniqueness among all infinite-volume Gibbs states with arbitrary boundary conditions. It is deliberately not presented as the latter counterexample.

## 3. The strongest plausible route without thermodynamic uniqueness

**CONDITIONAL — A sufficient joint-limit contract.** Write a for lattice spacing, R for the physical box size, and omega_{a,R} for the normalized Wilson state with the chosen renormalization prescription. Suppose the following inputs hold on a fixed common local continuum algebra/test hierarchy:

1. **CONDITIONAL — H_reg.** Joint observable moments have cutoff- and volume-uniform continuity/growth bounds sufficient for OS0, and a subsequence (a_j, R_j) with a_j -> 0 and R_j -> infinity converges on a countable dense determining family. The observable maps respect the required renormalized identities and support in the limit.
2. **CONDITIONAL — H_RP.** The approximants to every positive-half polynomial obey reflection positivity, or have negative errors tending to zero, for one common time reflection. Localization/renormalization errors are controlled in the same moment topology.
3. **CONDITIONAL — H_Euc.** For each fixed compactly supported test polynomial F and each Euclidean motion g in a countable dense subgroup, the symmetry defect obeys

       |omega_{a_j,R_j}(F_g) - omega_{a_j,R_j}(F)| -> 0.

   The limiting action is continuous; the notation F_g includes the appropriate tensor transformations and lattice approximants. Sufficient, but stronger, is a bound C_{F,g} a^eta + b_{F,g}(R), with eta > 0 and b_{F,g}(R) -> 0. A power rate is useful, not logically required.
4. **CONDITIONAL — H_int.** A specified continuous finite-moment witness W vanishes on the named class of free physical theories and satisfies |W(omega_{a_j,R_j})| >= c > 0, with its sign or separation from zero controlled, at fixed nonzero renormalized coupling. The witness is local and survives the physical OS quotient; it does not count auxiliary soft noise.

**PROVEN — Assembly from this contract.** Use the same subsequence for all required joint moments. Normalization and ordinary polynomial positivity pass from the normalized lattice states by finite moment limits. H_reg gives continuous extension and OS0; H_RP gives OS2 by the finite quadratic-form limit in section 1; bosonic permutation identities give OS3. H_Euc gives invariance first on the dense subgroup and dense test family, then on the full Euclidean group by continuity. Continuity of W and H_int give |W(omega)| >= c, excluding the specified free class. This proves the implication from the four inputs, without taking two different orders of limits or proving any uniqueness/clustering statement. The substantive Yang–Mills estimates H_reg, H_Euc and H_int remain OPEN.

**PROVEN — Translation invariance has a simpler mechanism.** If the renormalized Schwinger distributions have a uniform test-function continuity bound and are invariant under translations by a Z^4, choose lattice translations h_a -> h for any fixed h. Their exact translation identities and the continuity bound imply S_n(tau_h f) - S_n(f) = lim_a [S_{n,a}(tau_h f) - S_{n,a}(tau_{h_a} f)] = 0. No equality of states from different boundaries is used. This argument requires common continuum test functions; it cannot be inferred from equality of separate one-grid marginals when independent soft noises are attached to different grids.

**ARGUED — Rotation control is the substantive extra estimate.** A shrinking mesh makes lattice translations dense, but the hypercubic rotation group stays finite. Therefore the previous proof has no rotation counterpart from mesh refinement alone. One can aim for rotational Ward identities with controlled cutoff and boundary terms, or direct comparisons of rotated local observables/regulators in the selected finite-volume state. This is weaker in conclusion than proving all thermodynamic states identical, but it is not supplied by compactness. If local continuum Ward identities admit symmetry-breaking states, their formal validity alone is insufficient: the selected state's symmetry, including the boundary term, must still be proved.

**PROVEN — A restricted favorable power-counting fact.** Consider a local term quadratic in curvature, sum c_{mu nu,rho sigma} tr(F_{mu nu} F_{rho sigma}), with the usual antisymmetry in each index pair and invariance under independent coordinate reflections and permutations. A reflection multiplies a component F_{mu nu} by a minus sign exactly when the reflected index belongs to that pair. For distinct unordered pairs there is an index belonging to only one pair, so their mixed term is forbidden. Permutations force the remaining coefficients to be equal. The term is therefore proportional to sum_{mu<nu} tr(F_{mu nu}^2). This proves isotropy only for this dimension-four quadratic local class. It gives no theorem about the law, nonlocal terms, higher-dimensional operators, or spontaneous breaking. Applying it to the soft scheme requires exact hypercubic covariance of that scheme and a proved local operator expansion.

**PROVEN — Why irrelevant coefficients alone do not finish OS1.** On any finite regulated configuration space, for a differentiable family of actions and observables with justified differentiation under the integral,

    d/ds <F_s>_s = <dF_s/ds>_s - Cov_s(F_s, dS_s/ds).

This follows by differentiating the numerator and normalization Z_s separately. If dS_s/ds = a^eta integral O_s(x) dx, the needed error involves a^eta integral Cov_s(F_s, O_s(x)) dx over the entire box. A small local action coefficient alone supplies no volume-uniform bound on that integral. A local conditional comparison, a symmetry identity with a vanishing boundary term, or another stability estimate must replace that missing bound. Phase selection can be sensitive to arbitrarily small extensive perturbations. Thus the tempting slogan “rotation breaking is UV irrelevant, so the IR is irrelevant” is not a proof.

**ARGUED — The two-family route is incomplete as currently phrased.** A rotation-covariant gradient-flow map obeys Flow_t(R A) = R Flow_t(A). This is an intertwining identity, not invariance of the input law or its pushforward. A covariant observable family cannot turn a noninvariant measure into an invariant one by notation. A valid auxiliary route must actually construct an invariant law, identify all needed joint observables with the lattice limit, and transfer RP to a local positive-time algebra. Fixed t > 0 flow smears across the time plane. The bound C exp(-c d^2/t) is not zero at fixed t; exact RP needs a controlled localization limit (such as t -> 0 at fixed support distance d), or a different exact argument. Equality merely of a few expectations is not an equality of OS structures.

**CONDITIONAL — A genuine averaging alternative.** Suppose the permitted continuum-limit states form a compact convex class closed under Euclidean transformations, have uniform regularity bounds, and are RP for every affine hyperplane. First average over O(4). Then average translations over expanding Euclidean balls: each such average remains rotation invariant, and translation defects are bounded by the relative volume of the symmetric difference between a ball and its fixed translate, which tends to zero. Compactness gives invariant limit points. These limits remain RP since translated states are RP at the original plane. To realize this strategy for Yang–Mills one must prove the all-plane RP, appropriate continuity/tightness for unbounded fields, and closure under these averages within the allowed lattice-limit construction. These are OPEN inputs. Averaging a state with only the known coordinate-plane RP is not this theorem; nor is rotating/averaging the original lattices automatically an admissible fixed-orientation Wilson sequence.

**OPEN — Interaction must also survive averaging.** The averaging argument establishes symmetry and positivity under its hypotheses, not preservation of H_int. A shared affine witness uniformly separating the entire orbit and its closed convex hull from the chosen free class would suffice. A nonlinear cumulant can change under mixing, so preservation of a particular non-free witness requires its own argument. This obligation also belongs to rank 3 below.

## 4. Non-free dynamics and the actual value of BFT

**PROVEN — Non-Gaussian composite observables do not certify an interacting theory.** Let X be a centered real Gaussian with variance sigma^2 and Y = X^2 - sigma^2. Gaussian integration by parts gives E[X^{2n}] = (2n-1)!! sigma^{2n}. Hence E[Y^2] = 2 sigma^4, E[Y^4] = 60 sigma^8, and the connected fourth moment is 60 sigma^8 - 3(2 sigma^4)^2 = 48 sigma^8, nonzero. Gauge-invariant curvature composites and path-ordered loops are nonlinear observables, so “connected fourth moment nonzero” without a calibrated free comparator can make exactly this mistake. Likewise a mixture of centered Gaussian laws with different variances has fourth cumulant 3 Var(sigma^2); absent OS4, a proposed witness should address mixtures of free sectors if the intended conclusion is interacting dynamics.

**PROVEN — Non-vacuum spectral weight is a weaker, useful test.** For an OS-reconstructed vector psi and H >= 0, set C(t) = <psi, exp(-tH) psi>. By the spectral theorem,

    C(0) - 2 C(h) + C(2h)
      = ||(1 - exp(-hH)) psi||^2
      = integral_[0,infinity) (1 - exp(-hE))^2 d rho_psi(E).

Strict positivity excludes support only at E = 0, including pure vacuum degeneracy. It does not put the spectrum above any positive threshold and does not exclude a free massless theory. A uniform lower bound on this expression concerns finitely separated local observations, not a large-time decay theorem. It is a sensible preliminary signal test, not the owner's full non-free conclusion.

**ARGUED — What the requested BFT actually targets.** REVIEW_S22's “Statement (BFT)” asks for a critical background tower, uniqueness in a specified small composite-slice ball, weighted regularity, complex analyticity, local derivatives through order four, a branch-action polymer norm, and a Hessian bound, uniformly in depth and the allowed volumes. Its uniqueness is a deterministic small-field variational statement. It is not uniqueness of the normalized Wilson state. Its exponential derivative locality concerns the conditional background construction at a blocked scale, not a physical mass gap. Its quantifiers over small-curvature backgrounds do not establish estimates for all probability-weighted large-field histories.

**PROVEN — Conditional locality is not full correlation decay.** For observables X,Y and a coarse field B, write X = (X - E[X|B]) + E[X|B] and similarly for Y. The centered cross terms have zero expectation conditional on B. Therefore

    Cov(X,Y) = E[ Cov(X,Y | B) ]
               + Cov( E[X|B], E[Y|B] ).

Even exponential decay of the first term says nothing by itself about the second. This is the exact reason an exponentially local background lift or a massive constrained fluctuation covariance cannot be relabelled a mass gap or global mixing theorem.

**ARGUED — BFT is valuable but only part of the required UV work.** BFT would support local integration and the large-field analysis; its volume-independent constants are particularly valuable for R^4. It still needs the full normalized large-field reconstruction, coupling tuning in the actual soft scheme for the specified SU(N), source/observable estimates (not only up to four derivatives if OS0 uses an all-order hierarchy), and the symmetry and non-free contracts above. The optional convergence of a classical branch action to a perfect action is not convergence of the quantum measure. This inspection does not validate any BFT clause.

## 6. Finite-torus first: useful, not logically necessary

**PROVEN — A fixed torus cannot supply the requested rotation group.** A linear Euclidean transformation R descends to R^4/(ell Z^4) only if R(ell Z^4) = ell Z^4. For orthogonal R the columns are integer unit vectors, hence a signed permutation. Generic SO(4) rotations therefore do not act as isometries of a fixed flat cubic torus. Torus covariance is a different statement from OS1 on R^4. This is a geometric limitation, not evidence that the infinite-volume limit cannot recover rotations.

**PROVEN — Full T_fin is not a necessary logical precursor.** The joint-limit implication in section 3 proves the desired existence conclusion without assuming convergence of the complete a -> 0 sequence at any fixed R. It only uses a suitable sequence (a_j, R_j). Thus a proof of T_OS by that contract does not require the separate, stronger finite-volume convergence theorem as an intermediate theorem. Conversely, fixed-R convergence with constants C(R) gives no bound on C(R) as R -> infinity; it does not imply the hypotheses of that contract.

**ARGUED — Recommended milestone.** Prove a finite-volume continuum construction or even the required finite-volume estimates as a coherent milestone, but formulate every local source, comparison, and interaction estimate with its volume dependence visible from the start. This is a useful way to validate BFT, coupling tuning, the soft-kernel dictionary, contact renormalization, and RP transfer. Carry the R^4 symmetry estimate as a coequal task while doing so. It becomes a detour if all effort is spent on full-sequence torus uniqueness, tiny-box weak-coupling formulae, or wrapping holonomy signals whose constants/supported observables cannot survive decompactification.

**ARGUED — The finite-volume trap is physical as well as formal.** Making the coupling small at the physical box size can keep every relevant scale perturbative only by restricting the box relative to the fixed dynamical scale. Sending the physical volume to infinity at fixed renormalized theory eventually leaves that regime. Retuning the physical coupling toward zero as the box grows changes the target and can approach a free limit. A torus non-free result needs a local, contractible, volume-uniform witness at one fixed physical renormalization condition, not just a winding/zero-mode effect or a bound positive separately for every R with a lower bound tending to zero.

The resulting field is phi(x) = cos(n dot x + Theta), with n uniform on the unit sphere S^3 and independent Theta; it is translation and rotation invariant. For the local observable F(phi) = partial_t phi(h,0), reflection gives F(theta phi) = -partial_t phi(-h,0), hence

    Q(F) = E[ F(theta phi) F(phi) ]
         = -(1/2) E_n[ n_0^2 cos(2 h n_0) ].

**PROVEN — Sign and legitimate smearing of that check.** Set h = 1/4. Since |n_0| <= 1 and cos u >= 1 - u^2/2, cos(n_0/2) >= 7/8. Symmetry of S^3 and sum_i n_i^2 = 1 give E[n_0^2] = 1/4. Therefore Q(F) <= -7/64 < 0, using exact rational bounds only. Point differentiation can be replaced by smearing partial_t phi with smooth unit-integral mollifiers supported near (h,0), wholly in t > 0. The fields and all their derivatives are uniformly bounded, so dominated convergence preserves the strict negative sign for sufficiently small supports. This provides an actual RP failure, while the all-plane condition in section 3 still gives an actual preservation theorem. Thus the lead's warning is justified; its categorical reading as a ban on all averaging is not.

**ARGUED — Final ranked conclusion.** (1) Prefer local RG with volume control, a joint/diagonal limit, and a dedicated quantitative symmetry proof. (2) Keep an invariant auxiliary construction with local lattice matching as the main alternative. (3) All-plane RP plus invariant averaging is a conditional fallback with substantial new obligations. (4) Full thermodynamic uniqueness plus universality is a stronger, lower-priority route. Finite-torus continuum work is useful, not necessary as a completed theorem and not a substitute for T_OS. No Yang–Mills construction is completed by this report.

**OPEN — Least sure of.** Whether the existing soft RG can control the normalized rotation-comparison/Ward remainder uniformly enough through volume growth, without needing information about phase selection beyond its present UV output. That is the decisive uncertainty behind rank 1; it should be tested rather than assumed away.
