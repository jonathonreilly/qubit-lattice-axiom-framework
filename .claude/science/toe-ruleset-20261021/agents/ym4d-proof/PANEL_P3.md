## 2. Rotation invariance: the genuine gap and two overstatements

**PROVEN — hypercubic symmetry and a unique cutoff limit do not imply O(4).** Consider a real centered Gaussian field with covariance C(x-y) = exp(-Σ_{μ=0}^3 |x_μ-y_μ|). Each one-dimensional factor is positive definite because its Fourier transform is 2/(1+p^2) ≥ 0; their product is positive definite. The Gaussian characteristic functional exp[-C(f,f)/2] therefore fixes all its finite-dimensional laws. C is translation invariant and invariant under signed coordinate permutations. Across t = 0 its reflected covariance form for f supported in t > 0 is

    ∫ d^3x d^3y [∫_0^∞ e^{-t} overline{f(t,x)} dt]
                    exp(-Σ_{i=1}^3 |x_i-y_i|)
                   [∫_0^∞ e^{-s} f(s,y) ds] ≥ 0.

**PROVEN — completion of that Gaussian example.** This covariance inequality implies Gaussian reflection positivity directly: for the Wick exponentials E_f = exp(φ(f) - C(f,f)/2), their reflection Gram matrix is exp[C(θf_i,f_j)]. Every entrywise power of the positive semidefinite matrix C(θf_i,f_j) is positive semidefinite (it is the Gram matrix of tensor powers), so its exponential power series is positive semidefinite. Differentiation, or L² approximation of Gaussian polynomials by these exponentials, gives polynomial RP. The argument works for every coordinate plane. Yet C(1,0,0,0) = e^{-1} whereas C(1/√2,1/√2,0,0) = e^{-√2}, so O(4) fails. Restrict this same continuous covariance to aZ^4; Riemann-sum smearings converge to C(f,g), and hence the Gaussian characteristic functions have this single continuum limit. This supplies even cutoff-limit uniqueness, coordinate RP and hypercubic symmetry without rotation invariance. It does not have the Yang–Mills ultraviolet action; that missing action information is exactly why a YM universality argument needs more than uniqueness.

**PROVEN — corrected reading of R9.1.** Let θ_n denote reflection through the plane normal to n and let ω_R(F) = ω(α_R F), where α_R rotates observables. Then ω_R((θ_{e_0}F)^*F) equals ω((θ_{R e_0}α_RF)^*α_RF), with the corresponding convention for the action on supports. Axis-plane RP of ω does not establish the latter inequality for a general R. Consequently the usual proof that positive averages preserve RP cannot be applied to the rotated family. This establishes a missing hypothesis, not the assertion that every rotational average destroys RP. An already rotation-invariant RP state is a direct counterexample to that universal assertion: its average is itself.

**PROVEN — a sufficient condition for safe rotation averaging.** Suppose ω is defined on a common rotation-covariant local algebra and is RP for every hyperplane through the origin. Suppose R ↦ ω(α_RF) is integrable. Then ω̄(F) = ∫_{SO(4)} ω(α_RF) dR is rotation invariant by Haar invariance, and is RP for θ_{e_0} by the preceding formula and integration of nonnegative numbers. If ω is also translation invariant, the average is translation invariant. This only proves a conditional averaging mechanism. Obtaining all-plane RP from a cubic regulator is itself OPEN here; the average need not remain a limit of the specified fixed-orientation lattices, and non-freeness needs a witness that survives the averaging. It is not a shortcut supplied by compactness.

**CONDITIONAL — the direct symmetry-restoration alternative.** For each fixed bounded-support polynomial F and each R ∈ SO(4), suppose there are renormalized lattice approximants F_a, (α_RF)_a and an error bound

    |ω_{a,V}((α_RF)_a) - ω_{a,V}(F_a)| ≤ η_F,R(a) + b_F,R(V),
    η_F,R(a) → 0,   b_F,R(V) → 0,

with the first bound uniform in the volume and along the chosen coupling trajectory. Assume the common-algebra and REG inputs above. Taking a joint subsequence with a → 0, V exhausting R^4 gives ω(α_RF) = ω(F). The proof is just passage to the displayed inequality; it compares two observables in the *same selected state sequence*. Uniqueness among all Gibbs states or all continuum limits is unnecessary. The OPEN input is this symmetry-defect estimate, not the elementary limiting argument.

**ARGUED — plausible mechanism, identified hole.** Gauge invariance and cubic symmetry strongly constrain local low-dimension counterterms; suppression of rotationally noninvariant higher-dimension terms, or renormalized rotational Ward identities, could yield that estimate. The hole is a nonperturbative bound with source insertions and constants uniform in the surrounding coarse-field law and volume. Formal power counting or an O(a^2) coefficient is insufficient if the inserted operator has a compensating divergence. A gradient-flow map that commutes with rotations is equally insufficient: covariance of a map says nothing about invariance of its input law. Its nonlocality in Euclidean time also prevents a direct inheritance of positive-time support at fixed flow time.

## 4. What the A/B/C inference does and does not establish; a route D

**ARGUED — B is credible but very broad.** One suitably controlled subsequential R^4 limit with local OS0–OS3 and a non-freeness witness meets the explicit target. The direct symmetry-defect argument in §2 is one mechanism within B; it does not require uniqueness. B is not justified by compactness alone, and calling it most plausible cannot fill its missing inputs.

**ARGUED — C is neither necessary nor excluded by “no mass gap”.** Uniqueness of the finite-volume UV limit, uniqueness of a UV extension given a coarse state, regulator independence of local observables in a chosen phase, uniqueness among Euclidean-invariant states, and uniqueness of all infinite-volume Gibbs states are different statements. The lane's paragraph conflates them. A gap can supply powerful clustering/uniqueness tools in suitable settings; its omission from a theorem does not imply uniqueness was not proved. Conversely, even cutoff-limit uniqueness does not prove rotations without regulator/action information, as §2 demonstrates. The hole in the lane's comparison to mass-gap difficulty is the absence of an implication or an applicable quantified uniqueness theorem.

**PROVEN — OS positivity and rotations do not force a mass gap.** For the massless scalar Gaussian field in four Euclidean dimensions, C(f,f) = ∫ |f̂(p)|²/|p|² d⁴p is finite on Schwartz tests: the singularity is locally integrable in four dimensions and the test decays at infinity. It is nonnegative and O(4)-invariant. After Fourier transforming in the three spatial directions, its reflected covariance form, up to a positive Fourier-normalization constant, is

    ∫_{R³} [1/(2|p|)] |∫_0^∞ e^{-|p|t} f̂(t,p) dt|² d³p ≥ 0.

**PROVEN — completion of the gapless example.** Gaussian RP follows by the Wick-exponential argument in §2. The corresponding one-particle time translation multiplies a spatial momentum wavefunction by e^{-s|p|}. Nonzero wavefunctions supported in 0 < |p| < δ exist for every δ > 0, so no positive lower bound on nonzero energies exists. This exhibits the compatibility of regularity, Euclidean invariance and RP with gaplessness. It is free and does not meet the YM non-freeness target; it only disproves a supposed general spectral necessity for these axioms. It does not assert uniqueness among every possible massless Gibbs boundary condition.

**ARGUED — D: construct a covariant continuum local theory first, then prove lattice identification.** One can seek continuum Schwinger functions or a Euclidean-covariant local specification by continuum RG, a covariant regulator, or a stationary stochastic construction; choose an invariant state without proving that every state coincides; and then prove that its gauge-invariant correlations are limits of the Wilson lattice theory. Lattice comparison can supply RP through §1. A more conservative version proves uniqueness only of the UV conditional construction above each fixed coarse state and proves symmetry of a selected continuum state. The missing obligations are source/field regularity, positivity-compatible lattice identification, symmetry of the state rather than just of the equations, and non-freeness. This is a legitimate alternative proof architecture, not an established solution in 4D.

**PROVEN — the claimed trichotomy is not a classification theorem.** If B means *any* existence proof on R^4 that avoids global uniqueness, then D is included in B by definition. If B means specifically “construct arbitrary block-law compactness limit points first and then repair their rotations”, D is a distinct route that the list omits. A and C also classify different attributes (domain versus uniqueness), rather than mutually exclusive mechanisms. Thus “must have done A, B or C” cannot be used as a deduction about the unseen claim. A fourth informal possibility is a theorem with different scope or additional hypotheses; that possibility says nothing about whether the claim is correct.

**ARGUED — two failed shortcuts for D.** Symmetric equations can admit symmetry-breaking states, so an invariant stochastic equation or rotation-covariant specification does not alone give an invariant stationary law. A global rotation Ward identity obtained from compactly supported transformations can leave an annular/boundary contribution as the cutoff recedes; that contribution must be controlled or avoided by a symmetry-preserving state construction. Neither “locality” nor formal Ward identities dispose of it. Naïve averaging remains unavailable without the all-plane positivity/common-algebra hypotheses in §2.

## 5. Non-freeness and the layer-1 theorem

**PROVEN — a nonzero connected four-point function of a composite observable does not establish an interacting theory.** Let X be a real standard Gaussian and Y = X² - 1. Gaussian integration by parts gives E[X^{2n}] = (2n-1)E[X^{2n-2}], hence the moments needed here are 1, 3, 15 and 105. It follows that E[Y²] = 3-2+1 = 2 and E[Y⁴] = 105-4·15+6·3-4·1+1 = 60. The fourth cumulant is 60-3·2² = 48 ≠ 0, although the underlying field is Gaussian. Gauge-invariant observables and path-ordered loops are nonlinear composites, so “connected four-point ≠ 0” needs a carefully specified free comparator.

**ARGUED — required witness.** A useful non-freeness proof should fix renormalized local gauge-invariant observables, their normalization and the comparator free gauge theory; establish a correlation identity or invariant running-coupling property incompatible with that comparator; and bound the nonperturbative error below the separating signal, uniformly in the volume. A nonzero OS vector, a nonflat block law, Polyakov-loop fluctuations on a tiny torus, or variance injected by a soft averaging kernel does not by itself meet this requirement. The missing work is a surviving, physically identified local witness, not merely a positive number in an auxiliary law.

**ARGUED — what REVIEW_S22's BFT would buy.** The statement headed `Statement (BFT)` asks for a scale-uniform critical background, weighted regularity, complex analyticity, locality and branch-action bounds, together with Hessian stability, for small-curvature coarse backgrounds. These are substantial potential ingredients of a UV induction. They are not themselves a probability-law or Schwinger-function theorem. Its constants are uniform over the stated tori, but its background-domain hypothesis does not cover arbitrary coarse fields. The further hole is summation of large-field histories and normalized source-inserted estimates in the actual measure. Exponential decay of a constrained fluctuation inverse or of background dependence in block units is not a mass gap for the unconstrained physical field.

**ARGUED — why BFT does not automatically settle OS1.** BFT's locality/analyticity controls one chosen blocking scheme on its background domain; it does not compare differently oriented lattices or construct a rotation action on local distributional fields. Its displayed locality derivatives stop at n ≤ 4 and concern backgrounds, not an asserted all-orders local-source OS0 bound. Even a classical perfect-action convergence statement requires a separate quantum-measure and observable comparison. Thus BFT is a defensible layer-1 milestone, while the full theorem retains distinct source, symmetry and non-freeness obligations.

## 6. Ranked recommendation recorded before reading PLAN_S26

**ARGUED — rank 1: retain Wilson-lattice positivity; extend the UV RG to local sources and symmetry defects; select an R^4 limit.** Keep a fixed physical renormalization condition and one tuning rule as the UV cutoff and volume vary, for every fixed N ≥ 2. First prove the actual soft-scheme BFT/large-field/coupling inputs with their stated quantifiers. Then prove volume-uniform bounds for normalized, local gauge-invariant source insertions, their coincident-point renormalization and translation modulus. Use these to construct a common local algebra and take a joint or diagonal UV/volume limit. Prove rotation invariance by a quantified defect estimate or an equivalent positivity-compatible regulator comparison. Carry a specified non-freeness witness through the same estimates. Global uniqueness, OS4 and the mass gap remain separate research questions. The route survives as a conditional strategy; no complete source/symmetry theorem is established in this report.

**OPEN — smallest discriminating next artifact for rank 1.** For one specified renormalized scalar gauge-invariant observable and two fixed, separated smooth test functions, write the lattice infinitesimal-rotation identity after UV integration to a fixed reference scale. Identify every source counterterm, the rotation-breaking insertion and the boundary contribution. Prove that the *normalized* defect tends to zero uniformly in the containing torus, using only explicitly listed background and local large-field estimates; or isolate the first term whose bound depends on the unknown coarse law. A calculation only at the zero background, a fixed torus, or a formal series is inconclusive. This is a narrower and more informative test than demanding uniqueness of every infinite-volume state; success for this pair alone would still not prove OS1 on the whole algebra.

**ARGUED — single hardest step for the full theorem.** My choice is **nonperturbative restoration of rotations on a renormalized local gauge-invariant algebra, with the source estimates uniform in volume after integration over the unrestricted coarse field**. This is the precise place where UV control must yield a genuinely Euclidean R^4 state while keeping lattice positivity. It may require more large-distance information than we currently see, but no argument here shows it requires a gap or global uniqueness. BFT may be the immediate analytic blocker; it is not evidence that this full-theorem blocker disappears once BFT is proved.
