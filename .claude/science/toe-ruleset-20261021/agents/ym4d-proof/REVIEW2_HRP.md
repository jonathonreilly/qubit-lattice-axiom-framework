**Findings, ranked by severity.**

1. **Low for HRP; a genuine endpoint-convention defect in the upstream statement.** References: OSLEAF55.md:292–299; HRP.md:63–68, 256–258, 554–562, 615, 774–781. OSLEAF55's RP-lat defines its half-algebra using links with both endpoints in `0 <= t <= R/2`, for any even `R/a`. At temporal extent two this includes both geometric temporal arcs, hence the whole link algebra. Its universal RP assertion is then false. Write `U_0` and `U_1` for the two distinct temporal edges at one spatial site and take the bounded real observable `F = Re Tr U_0 - Re Tr U_1`. Site reflection exchanges the edges with inversion, so `F(rho U) = -F(U)`. At zero coupling, independent Haar variables give `Q(F,F) = -E[F^2] < 0`. The same strict sign holds under any finite reflection-symmetric Wilson density, which has full support.

   **Fix:** define the consumer half-algebra by edges geometrically contained in the chosen closed arc, or restrict that endpoint formulation to temporal extent at least four. Qualify HRP's “re-prove … Lemma RP-lat” export accordingly. This is **not** a counterexample to HRP: its explicit geometric convention excludes the opposite arc, and its actual compact-test/continuum exports use eventual half-support (HRP.md:334–351, 485–509, 539–552). With `a_j -> 0` and radii bounded below, temporal extent two is eventually absent. No new estimate or change to HRP's proof is required. The earlier reviews did not identify this consumer wording caveat.

**What I independently re-derived.**

1. **Wilson positive kernel, integration and sign** (HRP.md:82–117, 156–173). Regard a unitary matrix as a vector `a` in the real Hilbert space with inner product `Re Tr(AB†)`. Its squared norm is `N`. In the complexified tensor Hilbert space set

   ```text
   Psi_c(A) = direct_sum_{n>=0} sqrt(c^n/n!) a^{tensor n},
   <Psi_c(B), Psi_c(A)> = exp(c Re Tr(BA†)),
   ||Psi_c(A)||^2 = exp(cN),                  c >= 0.
   ```

   This gives an integrated positive kernel directly: tensor the features over the finitely many crossing faces and integrate the bounded half-observable times its real half-weight. The reflected integral is the squared norm of that vector integral. The constant feature handles `c=0`; finite coefficients suffice, with no cutoff-uniform bound needed for this finite inequality. Independently expanding `(R + conjugate R)^{tensor n}` reproduces the nonnegative multiplicities and the majorant `exp(c dim R)`. Thus both the author's entrywise series and representation-ring argument have the correct sign `c=beta/N` and legitimate sum/integral interchanges. Positive Boltzmann weights alone were not substituted for positive kernels.

2. **Haar splitting and noncommutative order** (HRP.md:119–173). Product Haar under `C_v=h_{v,-}h_{v,+}^{-1}` pushes forward exactly to the original independent crossing-link law, even when crossing edges share vertices. Direct substitution gives

   ```text
   h_{v,-}^{-1}(U_- C_w U_+^{-1} C_v^{-1})h_{v,-}
     = (h_{v,-}^{-1}U_-h_{w,-})(h_{v,+}^{-1}U_+h_{w,+})^{-1}.
   ```

   Reflection exchanges the two independent auxiliary halves. No original half-observable acquires negative-half dependence. This proves the claimed all-bounded-observable form; gauge invariance is unnecessary. The L2 extension in HRP.md:70–72 follows from truncation and the reflection isometry in L2.

3. **Both periodic cuts, shared boundaries, and degenerate extent** (HRP.md:175–268). For `r_l(t)=1-t`, temporal bases `0,m` give the two crossing families; all others and all spatial layers pair. At extent two the parallel geometric arcs remain distinct, and splitting still works. The optional matching gauge argument is valid only at extent at least four. For `r_s(t)=-t`, condition on spatial links at **both** fixed slices: every remaining plaquette is contained in one closed half, so the integral is a positive boundary weight times an absolute square. No sign condition is needed for site RP; only crossing coefficients need be nonnegative for link/mixed RP. On an odd circle the single shared site boundary and the opposite crossing-link boundary combine the same two arguments. Complete temporal gauge would require trivial Polyakov holonomy; neither proof imposes it.

4. **The actual bare marginal and observable map** (HRP.md:272–356). The normalizer integrates exactly the outgoing-link numerator, so backward integration of every auxiliary field leaves precisely the original Wilson density. I checked this against PROOF.md:2277–2286, 2306–2313; RLARGE.md:49–57; ROTDEF.md:300–308; SPHERE55.md:223–245; and CODEX_REVIEW_HINT.md:229. The soft variables and S4'' deterministic block observables are different objects. S4' contour averaging changes neither the normalization identity nor the bare marginal. The clover definition at ROTDEF.md:117–121 stays within coordinate distance `a` and is permuted by reflection. Paired real centering, normalization and volume weights therefore give the displayed RP identity with exactly one conjugation. Source tilting really changes the bare coefficient by `-(1/2) sum_corners eta`, with fixed kernels (SRCAN55.md:129–140); conditioning or a terminal weight would also change the law. These are excluded rather than silently integrated away. Trajectory existence and the extension of its coupling rule to sphere spacings remain inputs (OSLEAF55.md:535–572).

5. **Actual sphere cells and weights** (HRP.md:360–479; SPHERE55.md:123–163, 196–245, 297–303, 867–908). The farthest-corner criterion is invariant under each coordinate sign change. A crossing chart square has its reflected coordinate interval centered at zero. Every other interaction square is a boundary edge times the collar interval. Collar edges preserve chart coordinates; the two chart edges of a crossing collar square are the crossing links. Its word satisfies, by conjugation,

   ```text
   B_s C_w B_n^{-1} C_v^{-1}
     ~ (h_{s,+}^{-1}C_w h_{n,+})(h_{s,-}^{-1}C_v h_{n,-})^{-1}.
   ```

   These exhaust the interaction faces, including repeated incidences. The actual coefficient is the incidence sum `(1/4) sum_{Q contains p} beta_Q`, not a constant-coupling shorthand. Radial cell-center weights pair exactly and are nonnegative under the stated assumptions on `G`. No digital-ball topology theorem, Moise assertion, seam-transparency estimate or empirical incidence bound enters this argument. For a great sphere through `S`, the ambient normal has zero fifth component; SO(4) fixing the poles acts transitively on those normals. Hence the rotation transport is valid **if the continuum state is invariant** on the specified cap domain. Translation transport in the flat limit likewise consumes state invariance, not merely covariance of observables.

6. **Quantifiers and closure in both domains** (HRP.md:483–577). For each fixed polynomial or finite sequence only finitely many pairings occur on the already selected common sequence. The eventual support index may depend on the test; no test-dependent subsequence is taken. Convergence of all required products is an explicit hypothesis. For the separated domain, reflected ordered negative times followed by ordered positive times avoid every coincidence. Smooth tests zero outside the ordering cone are flat on its boundary; after a compact cutoff, derivatives of gap cutoffs cost powers of `epsilon^{-1}` which boundary flatness absorbs to any fixed order. A diagonal choice of compact/gap cutoffs gives Schwartz convergence. Tensoring and reflection are continuous, so the supplied continuous limiting functional extends the inequality to the restricted positive space. This does not admit `e(f)^2` or supply its moments. At fixed radius the argument stays on the cap subspace; the radius limit and any common diagonal require the stated additional convergence/continuity hypotheses. I checked the source domains and corrections at OSLEAF55.md:149–194, 308–369, 767–795, 859–876.
