**Independent reconstruction of the decisive steps**

1. **Closure and diagonal selection.** For a fixed polynomial, its ordinary
   positivity or RP form is a finite linear combination of moments. If
   those actual moments converge on one algebra, the limit of its
   nonnegative forms stays nonnegative. Symmetry identities pass in the
   same way, with continuity needed to extend a dense group/test family.
   This argument does not apply to ordinary products absent from a flat
   test domain: a contact term changes an ordinary pairing while vanishing
   on all flat tests. For the radius/cutoff diagonal, choose R_j so the first
   j limiting pairings have outer error below 2^-j, then a_j<1/j so their
   fixed-R_j cutoff errors are below 2^-j. Their total error is below
   2^(1-j). No radius-uniform cutoff convergence rate is needed. These are
   precisely the scopes in `CODEX_REVIEW_ASSEMBLY.md:25–39,204–213`, and
   `AUDIT.md:47–54,155` preserves both the positive implication and its
   missing common-domain hypotheses.

2. **Full return versus a selected integral.** In `RD.md:371–397`, a
   nonempty defect support contributes
   `exp(beta*|S|) * ||d_S||_infinity` to q, with beta>=2 and q<=1/4.
   Setting d_S=Theta-1 for an indicator that vanishes somewhere in the
   supremum domain gives norm one and a contribution at least exp(2).
   An arbitrarily small probability of that zero set cannot repair this
   supremum estimate. This rules out that naive use of the displayed
   criterion, not an integrated or differently organized expansion.
   Moreover `RD.md:292–316` retains the complementary and contour-side
   integrals separately; controlling a selected-sector logarithm does not
   control their sum. The current source still explicitly leaves the full
   effective-history identity and induction open at `:543–556`.
   `AUDIT.md:83,135,188,263,299` reports the requested-theorem HOLD correctly.

3. **F17 is not a necessity proof.** The inequality
   `E[O 1_LF] <= ||O||_infinity P(LF)` is an upper-bound method. Its
   failure cannot establish a lower bound on the actual expectation:
   already O=0 on LF makes that contribution vanish for arbitrary P(LF).
   A necessity result would require additional matching hypotheses.
   `CODEX_REVIEW_ASSEMBLY.md:51–57` and `AUDIT.md:68,94,143–145` retain
   exactly this distinction. The hard-block witness's boundedness makes
   its moment/tightness requirements automatic under its matched law;
   it does not construct that law (`HINT2_55.md:415–418`).

4. **Sphere risks and the universality alternative.** The identity
   `Cov(F,G)=E[Cov(F,G|W)]+Cov(E[F|W],E[G|W])` leaves the second term
   uncontrolled by conditional decorrelation alone. For example, a common
   centered coarse variable W with F=G=W has zero conditional covariance
   and nonzero total covariance. This reconstructs the mechanism at
   `SPHERE55.md:659–663` without claiming a Wilson counterexample.
   The two-sided seam operator `sum_i tr(J_i F^Sigma_ni)` is gauge invariant,
   has engineering dimension three and is even when both factors reverse
   sign under the equatorial reflection. Taking both in a common nonzero
   Lie-algebra direction shows it need not vanish. This defeats an
   exhaustive symmetry-exclusion claim, not all transparency mechanisms
   (`CODEX_REVIEW_SPHERE2.md:43–53`). Finally, equality of conditional
   expectations as functions of exterior data does not imply equality
   after integration against different exterior laws. Thus the
   fixed-torus comparison cannot by itself close flat orientation
   independence (`UNIV55.md:129–133`). `AUDIT.md:89–90,123–149` keeps the
   distinct Ward/comparison suppliers and all these boundaries.

5. **Absolute witness versus comparator separation.** The positive
   tempered scalar spectral density
   `rho(ds)=s^2/[log(e+s)]^2 ds` supplies the GFF counterexample in
   `CODEX_REVIEW_NONFREE.md:104–114`. Substituting s=u^2/r^2 in the
   four-dimensional massive kernel gives the separated asymptotic
   `S(r) ~ 192/[pi^2 r^8 log^2(r^-2)]`; the integral
   `integral_0^infinity u^6 K_1(u) du=384` follows from the positive
   hyperbolic integral representation. It has the AF-e asymptotic yet
   remains Gaussian. Contact subtractions do not affect these separated
   pairings. Therefore exclusion of the specified finite canonical Wick

   identification. `AUDIT.md:93–102,136,187,247,260` correctly leaves the
   different witness identification obligations open, including WT0's

6. **One-loop advance and its boundary.** The actual UL4 statement is
   `|a_n-a_0|<=C_FP` and

   error bound is consequently at most 2 C_FP. This is stronger than an

7. **Physical spectral criterion.** For H>=0, b<m_* and a centered vector,
