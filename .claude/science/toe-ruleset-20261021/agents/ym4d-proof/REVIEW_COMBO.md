## 1. Common domain and reconstruction

Locators: COMBO 30–105; WITNESS 49–68, 447–469; CUMUL 20–69,
109–159; REC_CONT 43–64, 984–1004; REC_LIB 114–154, 388–449,
847–864, 1082–1103.

The coordinate half-width of each bump is epsilon=10^-4. Adjacent time
supports have gap at least 1-2epsilon>0, and the support of f4 is above
time one. Evenness gives theta f4=f1 and theta f3=f2. Each distinct-subset
tensor is a compact off-diagonal test and hence belongs to the stipulated
flat space. The two reflected pairings really are nonnegative centred OS
norms. Their distinct test pairs are not interchangeable producer inputs.

The partition formula uses products of numerical moments. It requires
one fourth and six second coordinates after centring, or all 15 nonempty
subset coordinates with means. It does not introduce an inadmissible
coincident fourth power. Deterministic mean subtraction changes only the
linear source term; the multiplicative normalization remains Z(a)=1.

For each partition block, successive subtuple gaps are sums of the full
tuple's future-cone gaps. Their cone margins are bounded below by the
corresponding sums of margins. Consequently the continued block functions
retain polynomial tube bounds on the full tube. Blocks use disjoint slots,
so the boundary of their product is a tensor product of distributions.
There is no multiplication of singular distributions in a shared slot.

Gaussianity on the Euclidean hierarchy makes this analytic cumulant zero
on an open totally real ordered slice, hence throughout the connected
tube. In the reverse direction, a Gaussian reconstructed field makes its
boundary cumulant zero; REC_LIB's boundary uniqueness forces the same
analytic function, and its matching Euclidean value, to vanish. Thus a
nonzero separated K4 survives reconstruction. Contact terms vanish on the
flat tests. Field- and vacuum-preserving equivalence preserves these data.

The exact common-triple E0' requirement is present. Neither a two-point
estimate nor the seven selected moments is substituted for all-order
growth and the actual E1/E2/E3/reality/S0 hypotheses. **Fix: none.**

## 2. Canonical exclusion and canonical mixtures

Integrating the four-dimensional Fourier Gaussian gives the heat factor
1/(16 pi^2). At zero mass the substitution v=r^2/(4t) gives
G0=1/(4 pi^2 r^2). For positive mass, substitution of
G=Y(mr)/(4 pi^2 r^2) into G''+3G'/r-m^2G=0 gives the printed equation.
On z^(2n)(a_n+b_n log z), its derivative part contributes

    z^(2n)[4n(n-1)(a_n+b_n log z)+(4n-2)b_n].

Matching the preceding coefficient from -z^2Y gives b1=a0/2 and both
recurrences in lines 137–139. In particular
b_n=a0/[2^(2n-1)(n-1)!n!]; the a recurrence adds at most harmonic factors.
The series and finite derivatives converge on punctured compact intervals.
The a0 and a1 solutions are independent, so the heat-integral solution
has this structure without selecting a Bessel solution from an incomplete
coefficient check.

Finite propagator derivatives and finite Wick contractions preserve
integer powers bounded below and a bounded nonnegative logarithmic degree.
Scalar covariance removes angular dependence. Cancellations either leave
a first power and highest log degree at that power or annihilate the
separated analytic kernel. Approximation of reflected points by centred
positive-time tests fixes the nonzero leading coefficient to be positive.

text correctly allows.

lambda_m^-1/2 V_m=O(lambda_m^(15/2-gamma)|log lambda_m|^j) tending to zero.

## 3. Gaussian exclusion and the requested edge cases

Locators: COMBO 162–174, 202–237; CUMUL 78–107, 151–165;
REC_LIB 824–864; REC_CONT 43–64.

In the ring with z_i^2=0, the Gaussian subset-moment polynomial is
exp(sum mu_i z_i+sum_(i<j) Cij z_i z_j). In its logarithm a partition
with k blocks occurs k! times and acquires coefficient
(-1)^(k-1)/k, giving the exact cumulant formula. The logarithm has no
degree-four coefficient. No nonsingular covariance, spectral cutoff,
positive finite covariance matrix or convergent moment-generating
function is required. This excludes every elementary F2/F3 member when
K4 is nonzero. Combining this fact with item 2 proves the literal union
theorem; it does not prove exclusion of its convex hull.

does not fall under this deterministic argument. A field Gaussian on e

belongs to F1, its AF clause fails independently.

Finite tensor products and scalar contractions of canonical finite
polynomials stay in F1. For a selected finite tensor test, smearing the

with nonnegative log degree or gives the identically zero pairing.
Reflection positivity fixes the sign of a nonzero leading term, giving

The text correctly withholds a general spinor reconstruction extension

## 5. Finite-moment obstruction and the replacement contract

Locators: COMBO 412–481; ASSEMBLY55 134–152, 205–217;
PANEL_P1 54–65; CODEX_REVIEW_NONFREE R16.

For any finite collection of separated moments, chi_ell's coordinates
converge to chi's. If a continuous function of those coordinates vanishes
on every F1 hierarchy, it vanishes at chi. But chi satisfies W. This is
an actual closure obstruction to a functional nonzero at every W-state,
not the failure of one attempted formula. It proves neither density of
F1 among all OS hierarchies nor impossibility of separating a particular
YM state using additional information. The manuscript retains that
quantifier and does not repeat NONFREE55's overextension of a two-point
obstruction to arbitrary moment order.

K4 alone is a continuous finite-moment polynomial null on F2/F3, but the
free canonical Maxwell composite has nonzero K4 at these tests. AF uses
infinitely many scale coordinates. Hence the logical conjunction is not
the existing N5 functional null on the entire union, and G-FIN rules out
a universal continuous finite-moment conversion. The N5 quotation and
the description of its N1F consumer match the cited source.

Under N5-COMBO, polynomial continuity passes the fixed nonzero K4 margin.
For each fixed m, take the cutoff/volume limit in l_m<=V_(m,j)<=h_m.
The resulting inequalities hold for all m in the same limiting hierarchy;
there is no need for a common tail index in j for all m. Only now send
m to infinity. The two limiting conditions on l_m,h_m give exactly AF-e.
The other finite OS forms and symmetry identities pass under the stated
regularity and common-sequence hypotheses. This proves the conditional
N1F-COMBO implication without interchanging limits or treating non-freeness
as a closed property.

The optional ratio is continuous on B>0 and passes a limit with a uniform
positive denominator margin. Scaling a moment tuple to the zero tuple
