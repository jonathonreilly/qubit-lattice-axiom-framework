3. **Gas formation, connected logarithm and nonvanishing.** Every selected
   label set has a unique overlap-component partition. A9 and the c_I
   factorization hypothesis turn its integral into the product of the
   w_J in the note. Conversely, compatible disjoint J's determine exactly
   one selection. Nonempty supports prevent compatible polymers from
   reusing an original label. A12 bounds each complete component weight
   by its product of alpha_i; separate marginal bounds would not do so.

   Put v_i=exp((mu+1)|H_i|)alpha_i and
   b_i=exp(|H_i|)v_i. The incompatible-child sum of b_j is at most
   Q|H_i|. Hence v_i exp(sum b_j)<=b_i for Q<=1, proving the rooted-tree
   supersolution by monotone iteration. Every connected selection has
   a spanning tree, and allowing repeated labels only enlarges this
   positive majorant. Rooting at any constituent through the output
   vertex bounds the integrated-polymer norm at mu+1 by Q.

   In the logarithm, fix an order on the incompatibility edges. A graph
   has Kruskal tree T precisely when it contains T and uses only additional
   edges later than every edge along their T-path. Its alternating sum is
   therefore (-1)^(|T|)(1-1)^(number of allowed extra edges), of modulus at
   most one. This proves the tree bound, including tuples with repeated
   polymer labels. Root marking supplies 1/(m-1)!, so the second tree
   recursion spends the remaining unit of support exponent and gives
   the claimed logarithm norm.

   There is also a separate nonvanishing check. For the integrated gas
   restricted to vertices A, delete x:

       Z_A=Z_(A\{x})+sum_(P:x in S_P, S_P subset A) w_P Z_(A\S_P).
