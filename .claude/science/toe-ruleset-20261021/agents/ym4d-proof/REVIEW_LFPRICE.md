The independent calculations and attacks were as follows.

1. **Threshold units and depth sums.** For unitary eigenvalues
   `exp(i theta_j)`,

       ||I-U||_HS^2 = sum_j (2-2 cos theta_j) = 2N s(U).

   The largest singular value is bounded by the Hilbert--Schmidt norm,
   proving (4). Substitution of `epsilon=g p` cancels `u=g^(-2)`
   exactly. Thus `p=c g^(-delta)` gives a price proportional to
   `u^delta`. A diverging raw group-distance threshold eventually exceeds
   its maximum 2; calling the resulting whole group small does not
   produce a chart. A curvature condition also does not bound flat link
   holonomies, so LF3 correctly introduces its own link-chart premise.

   For (5), the upper flow bracket gives a term logarithm bounded below
   by `d r log L - n c(v+beta_+ r log L+R)^delta`. For `delta<1`,
   division by r leaves the positive limit `d log L`. Fixed powers of u
   change this only by `O(log r)`. This proves failure of the displayed
   majorant uniformly over finite-depth truncations, not a lower bound
   on the actual history weight. For a linear price the lower bracket
   instead gives ratio `L^(d-n a_eff beta_-)`. At equality a factor
   `(1+u)^(-s)`, `s>1`, reduces the remaining tail to a convergent
   power sum. The distinction between sufficient and necessary
   conditions in lines 122-131 is therefore essential and correct.

2. **Taylor coefficient and continuation.** Expanding the ordered
   product through second order gives
   `tr P(g)=N+(g^2/2)tr[(sum_l Z_l)^2]+O(g^3)`: the first-order traces
   vanish and cyclicity combines the cross terms. The inverse product
   gives the same quadratic trace. This proves the sign and coefficient
   in (7) without commuting the matrices. The multinomial derivative
   estimate bounds each third derivative by `(4p)^3 exp(4gp)`;
   the two normalized traces and the Taylor factor `1/6` yield exactly
   `|u Rem| <= (64/6)g p^3 exp(4gp)`.
   Consequently `delta<1/3` is sufficient for this absolute remainder
   to vanish. Failure of that sufficient bound at larger delta is not
   a lower bound on the remainder or a coercivity obstruction.

   With `A=W^*M`, the real change of the holomorphic kernel exponent
   at `W exp X` is

       (t/N) Re tr[(cosh X-I)A].

   Diagonalize the positive matrix `cosh X-I`; contraction of A bounds
   its trace pairing by `N(cosh alpha-1)`. The unchanged real
   normalizer cancels, proving (8). Multiplying over `c_link n` readers
   and comparing to `theta c p^2 n` gives the exact arcosh in (9),
   not its larger quadratic approximation. The zero-reader case and
   `0<theta<1` are explicitly covered. For a signed superposition the
   right side is its absolute real integral, not its potentially
   cancelling real value. The note's raw-numerator scope is correct.

3. **Allocation and normalized seams.** Charging theta times the owned
   nonnegative plaquette terms leaves a nonnegative rest. Interior
   witnesses in distinct owner blocks are distinct. In overlapping
   windows a term occurs at most m times, so allocating `theta/m`
   preserves the nonnegative remainder and gives the stated price.
   Integrating (11) leaves `integral W_rest / Z_A` to estimate; there
   is no cancellation of that missing reference comparison.

   For a separate check of (13), interpolate between the means:

       d log Z_t(M+s DeltaM)/ds = t E_s h_DeltaM.

   Its modulus is at most `t||DeltaM||op`. The numerator contributes
   another such bound. Alternatively `|h|<=1` gives `|log k_t|<=2t`,
   so arbitrary simultaneous replacements of V and M cost at most
   `4t`, including the normalizer. A Wilson replacement costs `2u`;
   kernel deletion costs `2t`. These prove (14)-(15) on identical
   supports. With shared zero selectors they are domination statements,
   not literal divisions by zero. The two-point example in lines
   281-285 has denominator d and ratio `1+(exp(h)-1)/d`, confirming
   why positivity cannot be silently extended to complex references.

4. **Shadows and the boundary debit.** Traverse a spanning tree of Y
   in `2(n-1)` star steps. A segment of at most `L^ell` steps has
   coordinate span at most `L^ell`, so it intersects at most two
   contracted blocks in each coordinate. There are at most
   `1+2(n-1)/L^ell` segments. Enlarging by w multiplies this by at
   most `V_w=(2w+1)^4`; summing `L^(-ell)` over positive heights
   gives (16). Singletons, diagonal steps and lifted torus paths obey
   the same bound. The recursive inclusion propagates an existing halo
   because `s+ceil((2s+2)/L)<=2s+2` for `L>=3`; it needs the initial
   reader set inside that halo. This is a base condition, not a way to
   deduce finite readers for the actual model.

   Under LF7's coefficient-matching premise, all nonzero differences
   touch Bdy. Each sign costs at most `(q+b)|Bdy|`, hence
   `b_partial=2(q+b)`. Multiplication by `B_0=16V_w` and conversion
   of `exp(b_partial B_0 r)` to a power of L give exactly

       c_B = 32 V_w(q+b)/log L.

   The `B_1(n-1)` contribution is paid once in the size budget. Raw
   seam bounds instead contain a sum of u over the later levels, whose
   available envelope grows quadratically in r. That is failure of
   the crude envelope to supply a constant per-level bill; it is not
   an obstruction to matched cancellations.

   leave boundary functions, but cannot delete their readers or the
   absence-of-other-defect conditions. A normalized mandatory insertion

6. **Counts, integrated gas and logarithm.** The deterministic
   depth-first walk determines its visited connected n-set, proving
   the rooted count `Delta^(2(n-1))`. A support root has at most
   `V_w` possible nearby core anchors. Multiplication by `T^n`
   is bounded by (19). This is a static finite-label enumeration,
   not the summed multiplicity of integrated histories.

   For a connected selection I, its integrated weight is bounded by
   `product alpha_i` because the current statement includes `c_I`
   in the complete-term integral. Coefficient factorization and product
   integration then produce an exact hard-core gas of these selections.
   Nonempty supports prevent two polymers containing the same atom
   from being compatible. The unit empty coefficient fixes the
   constant term. None of those conditions can be dropped.

   Write `b_i=exp((mu+2)|S_i|)alpha_i` and
   `v_i=exp((mu+1)|S_i|)alpha_i`. Then

       sum_(j overlaps i) b_j <= Q |S_i|,
       v_i exp(sum_(j overlaps i) b_j) <= b_i.

   Iteration of the positive rooted-tree recursion below this
   supersolution bounds every connected selection. Marking an atom
   whose support contains the root and using
   `|union S_i|<=sum |S_i|` gives the integrated-polymer norm at
   exponent `mu+1` at most Q.

   In the logarithm, order the edges of an incompatibility graph.
   Connected subgraphs with a given Kruskal tree T form an interval
   `[T,R(T)]`; their alternating sum is
   `(-1)^|T|(1-1)^|R(T)\T|`. Its modulus is at most one.
   The resulting spanning-tree bound and a second rooted recursion
   spend the remaining unit of support exponent and give the log
   norm at mu at most Q. Repeated polymer labels are included in
   Mayer tuples, with the usual root-marked `1/(m-1)!` factor.

   Nonvanishing can also be checked without first assuming a logarithm.
   For this finite hard-core gas on a cell set A, delete one cell x:

       Z_A = Z_(A\{x}) + sum_(P: x in S_P) w_P Z_(A\S_P).

   Inductively bound each inverse one-cell ratio by e. The modulus of
   the second term divided by `Z_(A\{x})` is at most
   `sum_(P contains x)|w_P| exp(|S_P|-1) <= Q/e`.
   Since `1-Q/e > exp(-1)` for `Q<=1/4`, the induction closes
   and every denominator is nonzero. The same argument works after
   multiplying original atoms by an auxiliary z with `|z|<=2`,
   replacing Q by `2Q<=1/2`. This separately checks the continuation
   in that auxiliary parameter. The budget sums in (21) follow by
   grouping types by n; returned and frozen classes share the same
   total budget when their common geometric ratio is r.

   `d log u-(a_0-a_eff)u` gives `u=d/(a_0-a_eff)` for d positive,

       z^n L^((d_depth-n a_eff beta_-)r).

8. **All fixed ranks and the law boundary.** In `D=2N^2` real matrix
   coordinates, the stated grid has at most `(1+4N/rho)^D` occupied
   cells. Two entries in a cell differ by at most `rho/(2N)` in each
   real coordinate, so their Hilbert--Schmidt distance is at most
   `rho/sqrt(2)`, hence their operator distance is below rho.
   Representative group elements give a covering by equal-Haar-mass
   balls. Summing their masses proves (25) without an assumed sharp
   small-ball asymptotic.

   Trace duality gives `|h_M(V)-h_M(W)|<=||V-W||op`. Compactness
   supplies a maximizer. On its `1/t` ball, `t psi<=1`, proving
   the denominator lower bound in (26). On the tail, its numerator
   is at most `exp(-t a)` against Haar mass at most one. The product
   estimate follows from conditional independence of outputs; the
   multilevel estimate follows by integrating the latest events first
   and taking the bound uniformly over their past.

   If `M=W_0 H` as assumed in (27), expansion and cyclicity give
   `tr[(I-A)^*H(I-A)]=2tr H-2Re tr(A^*H)`. Positivity of H
   proves both maximality of W_0 and the lower bound. The SU(N)
   polar-factor and positive lower-eigenvalue requirements cannot
   be supplied for arbitrary means by notation. In particular the
   gap observable at M zero is identically zero although the output
   law is Haar. Finally multiplying `exp(-t psi)/N_t` by
   `exp(lambda t psi)` gives (28) with its M-dependent normalizer
   ratio. No tilted law or arbitrary coarse-bad event has thereby
   become a plain forward event.
