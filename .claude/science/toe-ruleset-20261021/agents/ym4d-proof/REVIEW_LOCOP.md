**Independent derivations and hostile controls.**

1. **Exact symmetry and the Ward test.** Every interior gauge matrix in a
   directed contour cancels against its inverse. Reversed links obey the
   same endpoint rule. Because all contours of a coarse link share their
   endpoints, their arithmetic mean transforms as M -> a M b^-1. Unitarity
   gives

       Re tr[(a V b^-1)^* (a M b^-1)] = Re tr(V^* M).

   Haar substitution W=a W' b^-1 proves the identical invariance of Z_t.
   No polar decomposition, logarithm, large-u expansion or normalizer
   approximation enters this argument. The finite positive real normalizer
   cannot introduce a gauge defect. The path family is closed under signed
   permutations: axial orders and offsets are relabelled; reversed coarse
   links replace both V and M by their adjoints, leaving the real trace and
   Haar integral unchanged. Translations changing the blocking origin are
   correctly excluded.

   Extend a prescribed coarse gauge arbitrarily to the other fine vertices.
   Haar substitution in the complete transferred density proves its
   single-field gauge invariance. An individual selected term requires its
   selector and every retained label to transform too. This is precisely
   why lines 110–117 cannot certify moving-sector regularity.

   At the identity an invariant linkwise linear functional annihilates
   commutators. Matrix commutators span su(N), so DK(1)=0. Differentiating
   DK(U)[gauge direction at U]=0 in a field direction leaves
   H(d omega,A)=0: the derivative of the gauge vector itself is multiplied
   by DK(1). A nonzero multiple of the identity link quadratic form fails
   this test.

   Subtracting the tree-path primitive makes a linear connection vanish
   on the tree. Every remaining edge is its fundamental-loop circulation,
   expressible by plaquettes inside the cubical box. Hence A-d lambda=RdA
   and H(A,B)=H(RdA,RdB). The construction uses the contractible box; no
   uniform norm for R follows. Nonlinearly, tree gauge leaves based
   holonomies invariant under simultaneous conjugation. For a fixed
   finite loop sampled from a smooth connection, its logarithm starts at
   epsilon^2 times the area-curvature contraction. The invariant linear
   functional vanishes, so the first possible nonconstant scalar starts
   at epsilon^4. This proves the stated engineering classification, not
   a small remainder for arbitrary rough fields. For the analytic bulk
   symbol, Pi(tv)d(tv)=0 and d(tv)/t -> i v give Pi(0)v=0 for every v.
   A nonanalytic transverse mass projector is expressly outside this
   argument.

2. **The mixed-field counterexample and chart measure.** At fine U=1,
   M=1 and the normalizer is independent of the coarse z. Since
   -tr(t_a^2)/N=1 and real traces of odd anti-Hermitian powers vanish,

       -log k_t(exp(z t_a);1)+log k_t(1;1)
           =t[1-Re tr exp(z t_a)/N]=t z^2/2+O(z^4).

   This is an exact counterexample to deleting every joint quadratic
   term by gauge symmetry. It is not a counterexample to joint covariance:
   QA-B is invariant under A -> A+d omega, B -> B+d ev omega because
   Qd=d ev. Finite-coupling fine-only normalizer terms remain in the
   complete action.

   The exponential differential is f(ad A), f(z)=(1-e^-z)/z. Expanding
   log f(z)=-z/2+z^2/24+O(z^4) and using
   tr_ad(ad A)=0, tr_ad((ad A)^2)=2N tr(A^2), gives
   log j(A)=(N/12)tr(A^2)+O(A^4). In SU(2), A=i z sigma_3 gives
   j=(sin z/z)^2 and log j=-z^2/3+O(z^4). Thus -log j is positive
   quadratic. Applying a single-field Ward identity to that chart term
   alone would discard the transformation of the measure and the other
   action terms. LOCOP does not do so.

3. **The tensor projector.** Direct forward differences of the displayed
   affine potential give dA^F=F. Changing its origin adds a constant
   one-form, which is a gradient on the box. Ward therefore makes all
   Hessian tests independent of that origin.

   Represent each colour bilinear by T using -tr(AB)/N. Ad-invariance
   says T commutes with all adjoint actions. On the complexified algebra,
   diagonal adjoint actions isolate each root line E_ij. The identities
   T[X,Y]=[TX,Y]=[X,TY] equate the root eigenvalues and determine T on
   the traceless diagonal subspace. In N=2, E_12, E_21 and their
   commutator already suffice. Therefore T is scalar, real on su(N);
   the average over D colours returns that scalar without a dimension
   factor. Hessian symmetry makes C_ab=C_ba.

   The plaquette angle condition gives a traceless principal logarithm:
   the sum of the N angles is a multiple of 2pi with magnitude less than
   pi. Each log is based at x and transforms by Ad(g_x). Inversion negates
   it on this domain. The signed four-loop average consequently transforms
   as a two-form under every signed axis permutation.

   Along a commuting one-colour affine test with parameter tau and
   orientation signs s,t, the loop log is exactly tau s t F_mu,nu
   near zero; the signed average is tau F_mu,nu.
   More generally its linear term is tau F, which is all the Hessian of
   the quadratic reconstruction uses. Thus reconstruction followed by
   testing returns C. Scalar reconstruction returns b I, whose trace/6
   is b. Values at identity return a_X. This proves both idempotents
   without needing any inverse coordinate map for general curvature.
   Gauge commutation is the stated property on invariant inputs, not
   a claim on arbitrary gauge-variant chart pieces.

   A reflection in an axis belonging to just one of two distinct planes
   reverses their cross coefficient. The full reflection average kills
   it; axis permutations equate all six diagonal coefficients. The
   orientation-odd dual-curvature term is excluded at this same bulk
   scope. Conversely, C=diag(1,-1,0,0,0,0) has b=0 and survives
   L^4 K(L^-2 F) unchanged. This exact witness rules out only the
   scalar-only contraction on nonsymmetric activities. Tensor extraction
   and complete orbit summation remain available.

4. **Bulk coefficient, scheme conversion and its norm loss.** Summing
   translates of a density whose interior weights total one gives one
   bulk density per site. Finite curvature factorization gives
   Pi=d^* h d. On a constant-curvature test the sum of h's entries is
   exactly C; after the full symmetry reduction it is b I. Therefore
   Pi_12(p)=-b p_1 p_2+o(|p|^2), whereas differentiation of the stated
   Fourier series gives

       partial_1 partial_2 Pi_12(0)
           =-sum_z z_1 z_2 Pi_12(z).

   Hence beta=b. The absolute moment/test summability in lines 291–303
   is needed to interchange the family sums and derivatives; it is not
   an estimate provided by the finite projector. Both Wilson and clover
   densities give (1/2)sum_a |F_a|^2 on these tests. Since
   |F|^2=-tr(F^2)/N, comparison with c_bar sum_a[-tr(F_a^2)] gives
   b=2N c_bar.

   FLOW's tree normalization also has the correct factor: in its
   transverse Schur recursion the singular zero-alias contribution
   scales as L^-4 times L^2 from the two Q factors times L^2 from
   the inverse curvature symbol. Their product is one; the soft noise
   and nonzero aliases are regular at zero momentum at each fixed
   depth. With the stated Hessian regularity this yields beta[W^tree]=1.
   The exact identity replacing bW^cl by bW^tree therefore leaves a
   beta-zero difference, not a norm-small difference. The SU(N) maximum,
   rather than the U(N) polar maximum, is essential to the normalized
   exponent; the cited degree-six correction cannot be ignored in C^6.

   At field order two the Taylor norm contains h^2|D^2 D_J^r K|/(2r!).
   Each affine test has direction norm at most A_X/h. Averaging the
   6D tests with weights 1/(6D), then summing the source derivatives
   at the common identity field, proves

       sum_(r<=4) |D_J^r b_X|/r! <= 2 A_X^2 h^-2 sup_D T(K_X).

   Mixed tensor entries obey the same bilinear bound. The coordinate
   radius bound uses ||t_c||op<=||t_c||HS=sqrt(N). Labelled source
   derivatives have the same argument without a source factorial.
   Since h^-2=u/N, the weighted-to-unweighted loss is real.

5. **Spatial localization and the full Taylor sum.** Subtracting the
   constant curvature test from a quadratic pairing gives exactly

       <F(p)-F(o),F(q)> + <F(o),F(q)-F(o)>.

   Telescoping a path of length r bounds its difference by
   r sup|nabla F|. This produces engineering dimension at least five,
   but only after spatial regularity is available. An alternating
   small-amplitude curvature has differences of its amplitude scale;
   amplitude smallness alone supplies no extra spatial gain. For the
   nonabelian problem the transports and their readers must be estimated,
   as LC2 says.

   For the independent finite-dimensional lemma, write G=P_2G+R.
   Its hypotheses leave only fz and zz in P_2G. Dilation multiplies
   these by L^-1 and L^-2, respectively. Reconstruction of a quadratic
   coefficient costs at most

       sum_(a<=i,b<=j) binom(i,a)binom(j,b)=2^(i+j)=4.

   All coefficients, including their source derivatives, are evaluated
   at the same (0,J). Their sum is bounded by T_(0,J)(G), so the
   polynomial contribution costs at most 4L^-1 sup T(G).

   Put s=L^-2 and S=diag(L^-2,L^-3). R has zero jets through degree two.
   Radial Taylor integration of a derivative of total field order
   r=0,1,2, evaluated at S(f,z), uses only third derivatives of G.
   Including the chain-rule factors, the bound at order r is
   binom(3,r)L^4 s^3. The split factorials are consistent because
   sum_(a+b=r) binom(i,a)binom(j,b)=binom(3,r) for i+j=3.
   The three low orders therefore cost (1+3+3)L^-2=7L^-2.
   At orders 3–6, R and G have identical derivatives, all evaluated
   at the same S(f,z); their entire sum costs at most L^-2 sup T(G).
   Source sums commute with the integrals, and the domain supremum
   stays outside the sums. Total cost: 4L^-1+8L^-2. With no surviving
   quadratic part the cost is 8L^-2. No seventh field derivative,
   fifth source derivative or bound on ||(1-P_2)G|| is used.

   The mixed factor is 20/9 at L=3, 28/25 at L=5 and 36/49 at L=7;
   the curvature-only factor is 8/9 at L=3. An upper bound exceeding
   one does not prove expansion or an obstruction. LOCOP states exactly
   the limited conclusion.

6. **Extraction and determinants.** Linearity of beta gives
   u'=u+beta(F_bulk)=u-b+r=u-b-rho. Subtracting (u'-u)W^tree and
   splitting G into Loc G and its complement is an exact action
   decomposition. A returned marginal action term -delta beta W
   contributes +delta beta to rho. Re-exponentiating a complete
   additive action is essential; subtraction from a multiplicative
   gas weight would not establish this identity.

   In standard generator units, a plaquette contributes
   a^4 sum_a(F^a)^2/(4N). Passing from unordered to ordered planes
   adds the factor 1/2, so u/(8N)=1/(4g_std^2). Consequently
   u=2N/g_std^2 and converting the stated b_0 gives
   2N(2b_0)=11N^2/(12pi^2). This does not compute b_0 or prove that
   the actual soft-kernel one-loop coefficients have this slope.

   For FP-exact let m_2=dim(range chi_2) and s=dim S. The integral
   with delta(hA) and the chi_2 Gaussian evaluates in orbit coordinates
   to

       det(Delta')^(1/2) |det(chi Gamma)|^-1
           (2pi)^((s+m_2)/2) det_S(Q)^-1/2.

   Linear coarea evaluates the same integral to

       det(hh*)^-1/2 (2pi)^(dim H/2) det_H(O)^-1/2.

   Since dim H=s+m_2, equating these expressions gives
   det_H O=det_S Q |det(chi Gamma)|^2/[det Delta' det(hh*)].
   Taking logarithms yields exactly LOCOP's FP identity. This independently
   fixes both determinant powers and the sign of the ghost term.

   For T-tel, covariance gives P_(k+1)T_k(1-P_k)=0, so compression
   closes the transverse covariance recursion. In orthonormal coordinates
   Ran D plus its orthogonal complement, the block inverse formula gives

       det(V*Y^-1 V)=det(Y_transverse)/det(Y).

   Writing D=VR then supplies the additional det(D*D) factor in the
   longitudinal Gaussian expression. At a critical background,
   det K_F=det H_transverse det Delta_U. The complete expression
   reduces to

       (1/2)log det H_transverse
         +(1/2)log det Ytilde_n+G_gh,n.

   Initial Ytilde_0=H_transverse^-1 now telescopes to the displayed
   sum of Phi_k^T. This uses the common subspaces, criticality and
   invertibility identified in FLOW §94(W3). Log absolute determinants
   are legitimate for this algebra on a nonsingular sector; they do
   not assert convergence of an indefinite Gaussian integral or
   continuation through a zero mode.

7. **Flow arithmetic, coefficient budgets and the actual gap.** Write
   E_n=B_n-nb_*. Then

       u_j-u_n=(n-j)b_*+(E_n-E_j)+sum_(k=j)^(n-1) rho_k.

   |E_n-E_j|<=2R0 and |rho_k|<=C/u_min give both sides of L15.
   Also b_j=b_*+E_(j+1)-E_j proves the per-step absolute bound.
   If U=u_n-2R0>0, the lower bracket gives
   u_k>=U+b_-(n-k). With m=n-j,

       sum_(r=1)^m 1/(U+b_-r)
           <=int_0^m dx/(U+b_-x)
           =(1/b_-)log(1+b_-m/U).

   This proves L16, including its slightly sharper integral estimate
   than the one printed in FLOW. There is no premise of stepwise
   monotonicity. Using u_n here proves a posteriori arithmetic; using
   a future terminal coupling to establish a step's hypotheses would
   violate the separate FI-1 condition.

   The vector-ball condition follows by the triangle inequality.
   A constant per fine site becomes L^4 times that constant per coarse
   site, giving the vacuum recursion. Source-dependent vacua cannot
   be dropped. Neither fact constructs the actual coefficient vector
   or its uniform estimates.
