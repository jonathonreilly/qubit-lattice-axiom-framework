**Independent re-derivation of the jet and ratio bounds.**
For a derivative word `I` of length `r`, repeated ordered Leibniz gives

    D_I exp H = exp H sum_(pi partition I) product_(B in pi) D_B H.

The word order inside each block is inherited from `I`; no two derivations
are commuted. Bounding each score by MB6 gives `M_r`. Integration against
the fixed nonnegative base weight and division by its positive integral
give `|D_I Z|/Z<=M_r`. Differentiating reciprocal and logarithm gives the
partition coefficients `(-1)^k k!` and `(-1)^(k-1)(k-1)!`; consequently

    Z |D_I Z^-1| <= I_r,
    |D_I log Z| <= L_r                         (r>=1).

Ordered Leibniz for `q_C/Z` then gives `C_r`. As a low-order analytic
cross-check, `C_1=2 b_1` and `C_2=2 b_2+6 b_1^2`. The factorial-weighted
sum over `a+b<=6,c<=4` is exactly the scalar-source RGA3 bound in MB7.
The labelled version sums distinct subsets with their own weights, without
an artificial `4!`. Its product inequality follows by distributing the
labels between factors. All these bounds differentiate the normalization;
none divides by a rare-sector mass after the relative estimate is taken.
At zeros of a fixed base weight its derivatives are zero. Pinning can only
restrict a score supremum already uniform over those pins.

**Independent re-derivation of the cumulant gas and its constant.**
In the commutative algebra with `t_i^2=0`, let
`M(t)=sum_(B subset I) m_B product_(i in B)t_i`. Its empty coefficient is
one. In `log M`, a partition into `k` nonempty blocks has `k!` ordered
occurrences and coefficient `(-1)^(k-1)/k`. The resulting coefficient is
exactly MB22. Exponentiation recovers the partition identity for each
`m_B`. Summing those identities over ordinary subsets `B` gives MB23:
each collection is a partition of its union. No substitution of one into
a nilpotent variable is used.

Splitting each cumulant into `c_(B,K)` produces a gas whose types are
`tau=(B,K)`. Two types can coexist exactly when their label sets are
disjoint. A shared label forces their supports to intersect because both
contain that label's nonempty `H_i`. Spatial overlap without a shared
label does not impose an exclusion. Consequently any connected
incompatibility cluster has a connected union of its complete supports.

Put `v_tau=exp(sigma|K|)a_tau` and `b_tau=exp((sigma+1)|K|)a_tau`.
The incompatibility sum is bounded by

    sum_(tau' incompatible with tau) b_tau'
      <= sum_(x in K) sum_(tau': x in K_tau') b_tau'
      <= Delta_sigma |K|.

Thus `b_tau` is a supersolution of the rooted-tree recursion when
`Delta_sigma<=1`; the stated `1/4` is more than sufficient for this step.
Iteration from zero bounds every rooted tree, including repeated types.
Identical types are incompatible with themselves because `B` is nonempty.
For the hard-core graph coefficient, grouping connected graphs by their
Kruskal spanning tree leaves precisely the optional edges later than all
edges on their tree path. Their alternating sum has modulus zero or one.
This gives the claimed tree bound without importing a cluster theorem.

Root marking bounds an unrooted cluster's indicator of containing a
physical root by the sum of its constituent indicators, replacing `1/n!`
by `1/(n-1)!`. Using `exp(sigma|union K|)<=product exp(sigma|K|)` then gives

    sum_(output supports containing v) exp(sigma|support|) sup T(log coefficient)
      <= sum_(tau: v in K_tau) T_tau
      <= Delta_sigma.

The finite jet norm is submultiplicative, so this controls the actual
derivatives of products, including those of the reference already in
`a_tau`. It proves MB24, not merely its order-zero version.

To identify the logarithm, multiply every type activity by a scalar `z`.
For `|z|<2`, the supersolution is `|z| b_tau`; its child sum is at most
`2 Delta_sigma |K|<=|K|/2`. The connected series therefore converges
absolutely there. The exponential formula near zero reconstructs the gas
by partition into connected components, and convergent power-series
regrouping extends it to `z=1`. The gas has no zero along that real path
and starts at one, so its value at one is positive and the log is real.
