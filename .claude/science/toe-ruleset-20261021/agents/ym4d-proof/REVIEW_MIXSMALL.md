1. **Ordered jets and positive normalization.** Applying an ordered word
   to exp(H) distributes its positions into blocks, retaining order within
   each block. As a separate check on its combinatorial coefficients, the
   uniform majorants obey

       M_0=1,
       M_(m+1)=sum_(r=0)^m binom(m,r) b_(r+1) M_(m-r),
       I_0=1,
       I_m=sum_(r=1)^m binom(m,r) M_r I_(m-r).

   The first recurrence follows by differentiating exp(H) DH and using
   ordered Leibniz; the second follows from Z Z^-1=1 and isolating the
   highest inverse derivative. Expanding the recurrences gives MS4's
   partition sums, including the r! reciprocal coefficient. The log
   coefficient is (-1)^(r-1)(r-1)!. Positive normalization bounds each
   integral derivative by its pointwise score majorant without an inverse
   sector-probability factor. Leibniz gives the convolution C_m for both
   a normalized density and a positive quotient. For example,
   M_2=b_2+b_1^2, I_2=b_2+3b_1^2 and C_2=2b_2+6b_1^2.

   Dividing the fine/coarse/source convolution by a! b! c! proves the
   product inequality on a+b<=p, c<=q. Labelled derivatives instead
   distribute subsets, so all fifteen nonempty subsets at q=4 remain.
   Differentiation through the fixed integral and the triangle inequality
   give MS7 even for signed F. Nothing in this argument differentiates a
   moving hard cut or obtains derivatives from a scalar value bound.
