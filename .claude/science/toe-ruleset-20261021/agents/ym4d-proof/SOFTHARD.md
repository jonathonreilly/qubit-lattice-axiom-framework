## 3. Exact comparisons that prevent a false cost verdict

### P1. Normalised Dirac blocking preserves the Wilson marginal

**PROVEN — finite-lattice measure identity, for compact G including SU(N).** Let μ(dU)=Z^{-1}e^{−S_W(U)}dU on a finite lattice and let B:G^E→G^{E′} be measurable. Define the probability kernel K_H(U,dV)=δ_{B(U)}(dV). For every integrable fine observable F,

    ∫ μ(dU) ∫ K_H(U,dV) F(U) = ∫ F(U) μ(dU).

This is simply δ_{B(U)}(G^{E′})=1; integrability justifies the iterated integral. In Haar-density notation ∫δ_G(B(U)V^{-1})dV=1, by Haar invariance. Iterating normalised hard, soft or mixed Markov kernels proves the same identity by integrating from the terminal level backwards. More strongly, inserting any bounded source e^{J O(U)} leaves its finite generating function unchanged; all existing finite derivatives agree. A measurable everywhere-defined B is sufficient; surjectivity is unnecessary. Its coarse pushforward need not have a smooth Haar density.

**PROVEN — boundary of P1.** If a small-field cut χ(U,V) is inserted without normalising its conditional mass, the inner integral becomes χ(U,B(U)), generally not one. Gauge fixing needs its exact orbit/FP identity; a local chart restriction alone is not that identity. Fixed V, V-dependent observables, backward conditionals and individual selected histories also do not enjoy the unconditioned marginal cancellation automatically. Thus P1 proves the marginal claim asked in the brief, but proves neither D4-obs nor a returned-denominator bound. No finite-group run is needed: the exact proof covers compact groups directly.

### P2. There are three different maps in this comparison

**QUOTED.** PROOF.md:2051–2055 uses the arithmetic contour mean M̄, with ‖M̄‖≤1, inside an exponential. PROOF.md:2080–2087 distinguishes these stochastic V variables from the deterministic S4″ observables. CODEX_REVIEW_LARGE.md:41 additionally distinguishes arithmetic means from the deterministic map defined through averaged logs.

**PROVEN — type restriction.** M̄ need not belong to SU(N); therefore δ_G(M̄V^{-1}) is not a group Dirac constraint in general. A hard replacement must specify an actual group-valued B(U): Balaban's own map, S4″ where defined, or a particular group projection/continuation. These are different proposals. At M̄=0, the current soft kernel is exactly Haar for every t=κ/g². It cannot approach a point mass as t→∞. On a chart with a unique nondegenerate maximiser B_*(M̄) of Re tr(V^{-1}M̄), concentration at B_* follows by comparing the strictly smaller maximum off any neighbourhood with a positive-Haar neighbourhood of the maximiser. This is a fixed-input weak-limit argument; it supplies no uniform result through degeneracies, no holomorphic delta density, and no equality B_*=S4″.

**PROVEN — additional map warning.** A single-valued, bi-equivariant projection depending only on M̄ cannot extend to M̄=0: equivariance would require B(0)=hB(0)h′^{-1} for all independent endpoint gauges. Taking h′=1 contradicts invertibility of B(0). A map depending on the full fine configuration can avoid this particular argument; no impossibility for all hard blocking maps follows.

### P3. Hardness removes a penalty, not all conditional normalisation

**PROVEN — on a regular finite-dimensional chart.** For a smooth submersion B between Riemannian configuration spaces, the coarea expression for a hard coarse density contains

    ρ_H(V) = ∫_{B^{-1}(V)} ρ(U) / J_B(U) dH(U),
    J_B = sqrt(det(DB DB*))

in compatible volume coordinates. To see the factor, use coordinates (V,z) supplied by the submersion chart, with z tangent to the fibre. The determinant of the full metric splits into the fibre metric determinant and the metric on its orthogonal complement. The volume dilation of DB on that complement is sqrt(det(DB DB*)); changing the transverse coordinate to V therefore divides the fibre volume by J_B. Integrating the delta in V gives the displayed identity. Thus no soft N_t appears, but the fibre integral and constraint Jacobian do. Gauge zero modes require a quotient/FP treatment before this chart formula applies. Its continuation in complex V requires a parametrised fibre/contour construction. The quotient of two history integrals can still have denominator zeros. The assertion here is a local change-of-variables identity, not global regularity of the actual hard map.

**PROVEN — loss of automatic smoothing.** On G=U(1), B(U)=U, hard pushforward preserves a discontinuous integrable density, whereas integration against exp(t cos(v−u))/N_t is analytic in v for every finite real t. Hence a uniform complex-envelope theorem for arbitrary bounded fine weights cannot be obtained merely from normalised hard blocking. This does not rule out analyticity for the actual Wilson pushforward after a separate proof.

### P4. Linear hard constraints clarify, but do not solve, the comparison

**PROVEN — finite-dimensional real linear algebra.** Let A be positive definite on a fixed gauge complement and Q have full row rank; stars denote transposes in orthonormal coordinates. The soft Hessian is A+κQ*Q. With C=A^{-1}, direct multiplication gives

    (A+κQ*Q)^{-1}
      = C − CQ*(κ^{-1}I+QCQ*)^{-1}QC.

Its κ→∞ limit is the covariance of the quadratic form restricted to ker Q, expressed in ambient coordinates. A source Q*ξ is constant on QA=b, hence contributes only ξ·b to a hard constrained integral. For the soft minimisation

    ½ x*Ax + κ/2 ‖Qx−b‖² − ξ*Qx,

completing the square changes b to b+ξ/κ and adds −ξ*b−‖ξ‖²/(2κ). Thus top-range sources and residual degrees of freedom really do simplify under a hard constraint. This argument assumes an invertible finite chart; it proves no uniform constrained Green-function, boundary, nonlinear gauge or source-norm estimate. In particular, the finite-κ calibration window is not a certificate at κ=∞.
