## 2. Actual all-order smallness at B!=0

### 2.1 Background norm and the common analytic domain

Use scalar matrix atoms, with the reviewed row/column norm M and support
incidence norm B at beta. Covariance atoms obey M(C)<=M_C and B(C)<=B_C
uniformly on a fixed original-B polydisc |B_j|<eta_0, for u>=u_*.
The reviewed vector atom norm is the maximum of component-root and support
incidence sums. Suppose ||b||_v<=tau on that polydisc. Since b(0,0)=0,
homogeneous projection in B gives, on |B_j|<=eta<=eta_0/2,

    ||b(B,0)||_v <= tau eta/(eta_0-eta) <= C_b eta,
    C_b=2tau/eta_0.                                         (C9)

Indeed apply the one-variable Cauchy formula to b(lambda B) on each smaller
circle inside |lambda|<eta_0/eta, sum degrees >=1, and take the limit of the
circle radii. Homogeneous projection acts on each existing atom and preserves
its original-B support. This proves a norm bound for the actual original-source
family, not just a sup-norm bound on its evaluated fields.

Set b_*=C_b eta, x=sigma p M_C and y=sigma p B_C. Both component and incidence
norms of b are bounded by b_*; those of delta a=sigma CW are bounded by x,y.
Fix a common scalar-coordinate Cauchy radius a>0 strictly inside the reviewed
normalizer, polar and Haar charts. Choose eta,p,u so

    eta<=eta_0/2,    eta<=c sigma p,
    b_*+x<=a/2,     p>=1.                                   (C10)

Here c is any fixed finite constant; estimates depend on it. The actual domain
is |B_j|<=eta and |epsilon_p|<=rho, not an unscaled field window. All constants
below count scalar Lie coordinates and all local vertices meeting an R-box.
The larger beta in (C1) is selected before R,eta_0 and the constants.

### 2.2 The baseline remainder, including normalizer and Haar density

Group the vacuum-subtracted F_0 into its actual local Wilson, soft-kernel,
normalizer and Haar vertices f_lambda(a|B_lambda;B|B_lambda). Choose common
bounds: at most q scalar a inputs, connected support size <=s, at most d such
vertex stencils meeting one R-box, and |f_lambda|<=H for |a_i|<a uniformly in
the original coarse fields on their chosen polydisc. These finite constants
follow from the explicit functions (C2) and the reviewed normalizer difference
bound. They are uniform in u>=u_*: F_0 scales the Wilson/soft terms by one and
the normalizer/Haar terms by sigma^2. The subtracted vacuum stays in F_0(b).

Let f(t)=H(1-t/a)^(-q). The nonnegative all-order majorant for the Taylor
remainder of a local vertex, expanded in delta a about b, is

    T_3(b_*,x)=f(b_*+x)-f(b_*)-x f'(b_*)-(x^2/2)f''(b_*).

The exact sum of these local remainders equals sigma^2 U_0. The sum of first
derivatives is zero by the actual critical equation, and the sum of second
derivatives is the actual K. All powers >=3 remain. Expanding at zero first
and then using a=b+delta a proves the coefficient bound without assuming that
Taylor coefficients at the nonlocal b are local.

The marked-support argument gives the explicit bound

    ||U_0||_beta <= sigma^(-2) d e^(beta s)
           [T_3+b_* partial_b T_3+y partial_x T_3].           (C11)

For a marked support vertex, it lies either in the local root stencil, in a
background atom, or in a fluctuation atom. Each attached atom meets the root
at its output coordinate. Hence unions are connected and retain every
original B variable. The two derivative terms count all choices of marked
factor; incidence sums over its starting coordinates are exactly the reviewed
vector/covariance incidence norms. The multiindex count
binom(n+q-1,q-1) is contained in f, so scalar coordinate choices are included.

Write (q)_r=q(q+1)...(q+r-1). Taylor's integral remainder and (C10) give

    T_3 <= H (q)_3 2^(q+3) x^3/(6a^3),
    b_* partial_b T_3 <= H (q)_4 2^(q+3) x^3/(6a^3),
    partial_x T_3 <= H (q)_3 2^(q+3) x^2/(2a^3).

Consequently (C11) is <= A_0 sigma p^3, with the concrete sufficient constant

    A_0=d e^(beta s) H 2^(q+3)/(6a^3)
          [((q)_3+(q)_4)M_C^3+3(q)_3 B_C M_C^2].             (C12)

Including the Haar Hessian in K is why no separate quadratic Haar remainder
is present here. This is an identity choice, not a dropped term.

    f_W(t)=H_W[(1-t/a)^(-q_W)-1-q_W t/a],

Thus the coefficient source in (C7) has the exact original-source atom bound

    h_src <= rho sigma^(-2) d_W e^(beta s_W)
                  [T_W+b_* partial_b T_W+y partial_x T_W].  (C14)

The original epsilon_p is included in the root plaquette label; it is not a

This is uniform in volume and u, with fixed c and the previously selected

## 3. Normalized fixed-chart insertions: cancellation before estimation

The following lemma uses the **same actual interaction** U_epsilon satisfying
(C17), not a replacement model. It also applies to any separately proved
CX-R input with the stated uniform majorants.

### 3.1 One common cluster majorant for all product measures

In CX-R's proof, replace each interaction factor e^(-U_S)-1 by its sup norm
before either connected-graph sum. This constructs nonnegative numbers a_Y,
independent of the normalized product measure, such that

    ||F_Y^lambda||<=a_Y,
    sup_v sum_(Y contains v)e^(nu |Y|)a_Y<=2h.               (C20)

Here lambda can be any product probability measure supported in the same
white cube, including point masses in selected scalar coordinates. The same
canonical connected-graph formulas define all F_Y^lambda. They only use the
measure on Y, so F_Y^lambda=F_Y^mu when the measures agree on Y. This equality
is essential; two unrelated logarithms with merely matching global bounds
would not justify the cancellation below.

Let A be a set of R-boxes containing all coordinates where lambda differs
from mu_p. Both partition functions are zero-free by CX-R, and the branches
from U=0 satisfy exactly

    L_A=log(Z_lambda/Z_mu)
       =sum_(Y intersects A)(F_Y^lambda-F_Y^mu),
    |L_A|<=4h |A|,     |Z_lambda/Z_mu|<=exp(4h |A|).         (C21)

No factor proportional to the volume outside A remains. Each difference
has connected original-source support Y intersecting A, dominated by 2a_Y.
The same conclusion holds when only part of one R-box's coordinates change:
grouping the scalar product measures by R-box preserves independence.

### 3.2 Actual face density and hard product events

Pin one scalar white coordinate W_i to either endpoint +/-p; denote the
resulting product measure by mu_(i,+/-). Its normalized face density for
the integrand in (C8) is exactly

    F_(i,+/-)=phi(p)/z_p * Z_(i,+/-)/Z_mu,
    phi(p)=exp(-p^2/2)/sqrt(2pi),
    |F_(i,+/-)|<=phi(p)/z_p * exp(4h).                       (C22)

This is a white-coordinate face density. An actual physical-coordinate
surface or contour flux also carries its Jacobian minor/velocity, treated
below; (C22) does not set that factor to one. For a face pinning k scalar
coordinates with box support A, the factor is [phi(p)/z_p]^k exp(4h|A|).

For product events E=intersection_(i in J){W_i in E_i}, E_i measurable and
of positive mu_p probability, condition those scalar measures. The exact
identity and bound are

    E_mu[1_E exp(-U)]/Z_mu=mu(E) Z_(mu|E)/Z_mu,
    absolute value <=mu(E) exp(4h|A|),                       (C23)

and the left side is nonzero on the source domain. Its logarithm is
log mu(E)+L_A. The event must stay fixed as the complex parameters vary.
Thus selected product histories have normalized, source-local logarithms
with the actual event probability retained. Zero-probability events give
zero and are explicitly excluded from the zero-free statement.

For example one may first prove (C17) on D_q with q>p, then take
E_i={p<|W_i|<=q}. Its per-coordinate probability under mu_q is at most
2 exp(-p^2/2)/(sqrt(2pi)p z_q). Formula (C23) supplies that price for each
specified bad coordinate, without paying the volume of the remaining
integrated coordinates. This treats a finite white shell only; fields
outside the larger analytic chart are not covered by the estimate.

### 3.3 Local insertions and what arbitrary histories do not inherit

Let Theta(W_A) be bounded and supported in the coordinates of A. Pin all
coordinates in A, apply (C21), and then integrate those coordinates:

    E_mu[Theta exp(-U)]/Z_mu
       =int dmu_A(w_A) Theta(w_A) exp(L_A(w_A)),
    absolute value <=exp(4h|A|) E_mu_A|Theta|.                (C24)

This is an exact normalized-insertion bound, valid also for a hard local
indicator. It does not generally prove its nonvanishing. If Theta>=0,
E Theta>0, and 4h|A|<pi/2, every exp L_A has positive real part and hence
the integral is nonzero. For a large or signed/correlated history that
criterion need not hold. Such a history remains inside (C8), not inside
a fictitious product probability measure.

The actual history after (C6) may depend nonlocally on W because C and b
have long support atoms. A hard cutoff of that field does not acquire an
analytic support expansion by Taylor-expanding b or C. Therefore (C23)
applies only when the actual history is this specified white-coordinate
product event, or its equality to one has independently been established.
(C24) applies only with an actually verified finite support A. Matching
general retained histories is still a producer obligation.
