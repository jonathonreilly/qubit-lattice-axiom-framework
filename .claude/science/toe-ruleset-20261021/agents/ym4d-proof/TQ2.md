## 2. Fixed-volume comparison and what averaging would have to prove

### 2.1 Proposition T rebuilt — PROVEN conditional implication

On a common coarse space let \(Z_1,Z_2>0\) be conditional partition
functions and \(h_1,h_2\) the conditional images of the same bounded
observable, with \(|h_2|\le M\). Suppose
\[
 \sup|h_1-h_2|\le\delta_c,\qquad
 \sup|\log Z_1-\log Z_2-c|\le\delta_d .
\]
Integrating the second inequality bounds the ratio of total partition
functions by \(e^{c\pm\delta_d}\). The normalized density ratio is
therefore in \([e^{-2\delta_d},e^{2\delta_d}]\). Splitting the difference
of expectations into an image difference and a density difference gives
\[
 |\omega_1(F)-\omega_2(F)|
 \le\delta_c+M(e^{2\delta_d}-1).                         \tag{2}
\]
If a per-block polymer difference is at most \(\eta_a\), summation gives
\(\delta_d\le N_b\eta_a\). At fixed volume this needs no susceptibility
or clustering bound for the coarse law. These are all-data hypotheses;
an averaged estimate cannot be substituted for their suprema.

For an unbounded approximant one possible extension requires (2) for
its actual truncations and uniform integrability of that same
approximant under both measures. A sufficient extra condition is
\(\sup\omega(|Y_a|^{1+\delta})<\infty\), giving a tail at most
\(M^{-\delta}\sup\omega(|Y_a|^{1+\delta})\). H_reg on a separated
distribution domain does not automatically give these absolute moments.
Direct comparison on the test domain is another option.

### 2.2 Averaging — PROVEN invariance, OPEN neutrality

Given a continuous state functional \(S\) on a rotation-stable domain
with integrable orbit moments, its group average
\[
 \overline S(F)=\int_{O(4)} S(F_U)\,dU
\]
is invariant: right translation of normalized group measure changes no
integral. Positivity on a common ordinary *-algebra and uniform absolute
moment bounds also pass through this integral, when that algebra exists.
But equality \(\overline S=S\) is equivalent to invariance of \(S\);
the averaging operation does not prove equality.

For finite regulators one can instead average finitely many transported
orientations and pass to suitable limits. To preserve the original
moments one must prove, for every determining \(F\),
\[
 \omega_j(F)-\overline\omega_j(F)\longrightarrow0.         \tag{AN}
\]
This is an **OPEN sufficient neutrality residual** when the averaged
limit is invariant. It is target-equivalent only after identifying that
averaged limit with the Haar average of the same original limiting
functional, for example using orbit equicontinuity and convergent
rotation quadratures. An independently invariant averaged limit need
not equal an invariant original limit. Bounds guarantee
subsequences/integrability, not AN or that identification.
Shape averaging has the same issue and also needs a nondegenerate
injectivity-radius exhaustion. A mixture of regulators is not
automatically a subsequential limit of the original periodic regulators.
Convexity preserves the RP form for one fixed time plane when every
component has RP for that same plane and domain. Rotated copies of a
state known to be RP only at coordinate planes do not supply that
sufficient premise. A different proof of RP for the mixture is not
excluded; see also the explicit averaging example in PANEL_P2 §2.

A bounded all-order example exposes the moment issue. Let the constant
vector field \(X(x)=X\) take values \(\pm2e_i\), \(1\le i\le4\), each
with probability \(1/8\). It is translation invariant and hypercubic
covariant; all moments are bounded. For any \(v\),
\[
 E(v\cdot X)^2=|v|^2,\qquad E(v\cdot X)^4=4\sum_i v_i^4.   \tag{3}
\]
Thus the covariance is isotropic while fourth moments are not.
For \(v=e_1\) the fourth moment is \(4\); for
\(v=(3e_1+4e_2)/5\) it is \(1348/625\).
The rotation average is uniform on the sphere of radius 2 and has
fourth moment 2 for every unit \(v\). To obtain this value directly,
write \(\mu=EX_1^4,\nu=EX_1^2X_2^2\); a 45-degree rotation gives
\(\mu=3\nu\), while \(|X|^4=16\) gives \(4\mu+12\nu=16\).
Insert the four factors at distinct spatial points to make a separated
four-point test; the constant field leaves the calculation unchanged.
This is a vector-valued probability example, not the scalar Wilson
clover field or a counterexample on the Wilson premise set.

## 3. Volume first: what boundary counting actually proves

### 3.1 Local-source pressure — PROVEN finite-cutoff construction

For this route require **LOC**, a specific additional domain condition:
each real \(F\in\mathcal D\) has a bounded finite-range, translation-
covariant approximant \(Y_a^G(F)\) at each fixed \(a>0\), independent
of \(R,P\) once its support embeds. Its bound \(M_{a,F}\) and lattice
range \(d_{a,F}\) may diverge as \(a\to0\). Finite products of compact
clover smears with state-independent subtractions have this property at
fixed cutoff. A flowed or nonlocal observable needs a separately
controlled localization limit. Complex \(F\) is split into real and
imaginary parts.

Translate the **entire product**, retaining all its relative separations:
\[
 A^G_{a,R,F}=a^4\sum_{x\in\Lambda^{G,P}_{a,R}}
                    \tau_xY_a^G(F),\qquad
 p^{G,P}_{a,R,F}(t)=\frac1V\log
                 \omega^{G,P}_{a,R}(e^{tA^G_{a,R,F}}).     \tag{4}
\]
For real \(t\) this is finite; no complex zero-free theorem is needed
for the definition. Since there are \(V/a^4\) anchors and the unperturbed
Wilson state is translation invariant,
\[
 p(0)=0,\qquad p'(0)=\omega^{G,P}_{a,R}(Y_a^G(F)),\qquad
 p''(t)=V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_t(A^G_{a,R,F})\ge0.       \tag{5}
\]
Thus each n-point moment becomes a **first** derivative of its own
pressure. One-point energy sources alone would not suffice.
The source range is allowed to depend on \(F,a\), not on volume.

### 3.2 Boundary independence of pressure — PROVEN

At fixed \(a,F\), for every compact real \(t\)-interval, the limit
\[
 p^G_{a,F}(t)=\lim_{R\to\infty}p^{G,P}_{a,R,F}(t)          \tag{6}
\]
exists on cofinal commensurate tori and is independent of \(P\).
This statement is about pressure, not its derivative.

Here is the proof for the bounded finite-range interactions in (4).
Set \(L=R/a\), and choose an integer block side \(b\gg d_{a,F}\).
Represent a torus by a half-open period parallelepiped. Tile its
interior by axis-aligned \(b\)-cubes in grid coordinates. Assign each
bond variable to its initial vertex, with its positive direction as a
finite internal label. Delete every plaquette or translated source
term whose variables do not all belong to one retained cube; also
delete terms involving the leftover boundary strip. The remaining
Haar integral factorizes into identical free-cube integrals and unused
Haar probability factors equal to 1.

The number of affected anchors is at most
\[
 C_P L^4\{d_{a,F}/b+(b+d_{a,F})/L\}.                     \tag{7}
\]
Indeed the internal cube-face layers occupy a fraction \(O(d/b)\);
the outside strip has volume \(O((b+d)L^3)\). A finite union of plane
faces has this bound also at rational tilted periods. Enlarge \(d\)
to include every plaquette's range. Each removed Wilson term is bounded
by \(2u_a\), and each source term by \(|t|a^4M_{a,F}\), with finitely
many interaction types per anchor. If two actions differ in sup norm
by \(B\), integrating \(e^{-B}\le e^{-\Delta S}\le e^B\) bounds their
log partition functions within \(B\).

Consequently the normalized pressure differs from the free-cube
pressure by at most
\[
 C_{a,F,T,P}\{d_{a,F}/b+b/L\},\qquad |t|\le T,             \tag{8}
\]
for \(b\ge d_{a,F}\); the same bound is applied at \(t=0\).
The missing-volume fraction contributes the same error, since the
free-cube log partition function per site is bounded at fixed \(a,T\).
Comparing any two large tori with this one cube pressure, taking
limsup and liminf as \(L\to\infty\), and then \(b\to\infty\), proves
existence and the same limit for each \(P\). Convergence is uniform
on compact \(t\)-intervals: (5) also gives the common Lipschitz
bound \(|p(t)-p(s)|\le M_{a,F}|t-s|\), so finite nets suffice.
Constants in (8) can grow arbitrarily with \(1/a\).
No Yang–Mills mass-gap or uniqueness theorem was used.

### 3.3 Why pressure is not enough — PROVEN logical counterexample

On \(V=R^4\) sites constrain all spins to one common sign \(\sigma\),
and give the two signs weights \(e^{\pm H_R\sigma}\), where \(H_R=R^3\).
The difference of these two energies is a surface-size term.
For \(F=\sigma_0\), every moment is bounded, but
\[
 \omega_\pm(F)=\pm\tanh H_R,\qquad
 p_{\pm,R}(t)=V^{-1}\{\log\cosh(tV\pm H_R)-\log\cosh H_R\}. \tag{9}
\]
Since the derivative of \(\log\cosh x\) is bounded by 1,
\(|p_{+,R}(t)-p_{-,R}(t)|\le2H_R/V=2/R\), uniformly in \(t\).
Both pressures tend to \(|t|\), while their derivatives at zero tend
to \(+1\) and \(-1\). The identity
\(|x|-\log2\le\log\cosh x\le|x|\) proves the pressure limit directly.
Thus a vanishing boundary/volume ratio does not imply boundary
independence of a local moment. This is a two-state comparison model,
not a finite-coupling Wilson or RP counterexample. It isolates a
large-volume phase-selection issue without claiming that YM has it.

## 4. A sufficient all-product pressure theorem

### 4.1 Named estimates — actual Yang–Mills status OPEN

Fix a cofinal tuned cutoff sequence \(a_j\to0\), and a fixed countable
sequence \(t_\ell>0\) tending to zero. All statements below cover
every real and imaginary part of every \(F\in\mathcal D\).

**NC (no cusp, with the required cutoff uniformity):**
\[
 \lim_{\ell\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}
 \frac{p^I_{a_j,F}(t_\ell)+p^I_{a_j,F}(-t_\ell)}{t_\ell}=0. \tag{NC}
\]
These pressures are centered at zero as in (4); a linear
state-independent subtraction cancels from the numerator.
Differentiability at zero separately for each fixed \(a\) is insufficient:
the modulus could deteriorate as \(a\to0\). NC is the needed common
modulus, and is not asserted to follow from local moment bounds.

**PC (thermodynamic source-pressure comparison):** for fixed
\(F,t\in\{\pm t_\ell\}\),
\[
 |p^I_{a_j,F}(t)-p^Q_{a_j,F}(t)|
       \le\varepsilon_{j,F}(t)\longrightarrow0.            \tag{PC}
\]
This compares the same physical observable on two grid orientations.
Section 5 gives a conditional UV producer. PC is not supplied by
Proposition T for a single local bounded observable.

**COMMON:** LOC, ALG/SEQ, compatible trajectories/subtractions, and
uniform local hierarchy bounds sufficient for extraction and extension
on the selected regulators. If assembly also consumes RP and H_int,
their estimates must hold on this selection. These are named application
inputs, not consequences of convexity.

### 4.2 Theorem — PROVEN conditional implication

Under COMMON and NC there is a common volume-first diagonal satisfying
VQ-all. If PC also holds, that same diagonal satisfies
\(\omega_j(F_Q)-\omega_j(F)\to0\) for every \(F\in\mathcal D\).
With the common continuity/extension hypotheses it gives H_Euc.
If U-all is supplied as well, it proves TQ §5's TQ-min on that sequence.

**Proof.** Enumerate the Cartesian product of observable labels, required
frames/periods, and source values \(0,\pm t_\ell\). At fixed \(a_j\),
(6) lets us choose a compatible \(R_j\ge j\), increasing if desired,
so all the first \(j\) pressure comparisons to their infinite-volume
values have error at most \(1/j\). Include \((I,I),(I,Q^{-1}),(Q,I)\)
and the transported tests. This is one sequence for all product orders.
There is no demand that one radius work for every order at finite \(j\).

For any differentiable convex \(f\) with \(f(0)=0\),
\[
 -f(-t)/t\le f'(0)\le f(t)/t,\qquad t>0.                 \tag{10}
\]
For the two period orientations, both limiting pressures are the same
by (6). Applying (10) to their finite-volume pressures gives, for each
fixed \(F,t_\ell\) once enumerated,
\[
 |m_j^{I,Q^{-1}}(F)-m_j^{I,I}(F)|
 \le \frac{p^I_{a_j,F}(t_\ell)+p^I_{a_j,F}(-t_\ell)
                      +2/j}{t_\ell}.                   \tag{11}
\]
Take limsup in \(j\), then \(\ell\to\infty\). NC proves VQ-all.

For rotation invariance, exact transport applies also to the extensive
source, including all its anchor points:
\[
 p^{Q,I}_{a,R,F_Q}(t)=p^{I,Q^{-1}}_{a,R,F}(t),\qquad
 p^Q_{a,F_Q}(t)=p^I_{a,F}(t).                            \tag{12}
\]
Apply PC to \(F_Q\). The two finite-volume derivatives on the original
torus then obey
\[
 \begin{split}
 |m_j^{I,I}(F_Q)-m_j^{I,I}(F)|
 \le{}&\frac{p^I_{a_j,F}(t_\ell)+p^I_{a_j,F}(-t_\ell)}{t_\ell}\\
 &+\frac{\max_{\pm}\varepsilon_{j,F_Q}(\pm t_\ell)+2/j}
                  {t_\ell}.                            \tag{13}
 \end{split}
\]
For example, bound the first derivative from above by its positive
secant, the second from below by its negative secant, and use
(12)/PC for the first pressure. Reverse the roles for the other sign.
Again take \(j\to\infty\) first, then \(\ell\to\infty\).
This proves the desired all-product endpoint equalities.

Extract a subsequence for moment convergence using COMMON; equalities
already proved on the full selected diagonal persist. The angle of
\(Q\) is an irrational multiple of \(\pi\): otherwise
\(z=(3+4i)/5\) is a root of unity and \(z+z^{-1}=6/5\) is a rational
algebraic integer, impossible by the monic-polynomial denominator test.
Its powers are dense in the first plane circle (pigeonholing powers
produces arbitrarily small steps). Conjugating by hypercubic symmetries
gives all coordinate-plane circles; plane elimination generates SO(4),
and a coordinate reflection gives O(4). Group stability of \(\mathcal D\)
and continuity extend invariance. Exact lattice translations and the
same equicontinuity yield translations. For compact test/motion
families, finite nets and a common continuity modulus give uniformity.
This does not construct a contact extension absent from COMMON.

Finally U-all gives
\[
 \int_0^\theta\tau_j^F(s,r_F)\,ds
 =m_j(F)-m_j(F_Q)+\int_0^\theta e_j^F(s,r_F)\,ds\to0.      \tag{14}
\]
No derivative convergence is needed. Without U-all, (13) is an
alternative symmetry proof and does not establish the existence or
matching of the proposed stress insertion. This concludes the implication.

### 4.3 What kind of input NC is

**PROVEN scope distinction.** NC concerns responses after infinite
volume, under sources translated throughout that volume. Example (9)
shows why volume counting and all local moments do not supply it.
It contains an **infrared thermodynamic input**, although it asks for no
pointwise decay rate, spectral threshold, or uniqueness of all Gibbs
states. Uniform admissibility of the real source also has a potential
UV/tail burden; §4.4 separates it from the phase-selection issue.
It is stronger than the selected moment-invariance target in
this architecture: an invariant mixture can still have a source cusp.
Indeed the unbiased version of (9) is invariant under permutations of
sites, yet its pressure is \(|t|\).

A concrete sufficient response estimate is
\[
 \sup_{a,R,|u|\le t_0}V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_{u}(A_{a,R,F})
       \le K_F<\infty.                                  \tag{SUS}
\]
Twice integrating (5) gives
\(p(t)+p(-t)=\int_{-t}^{t}(t-|u|)p''(u)\,du\le K_Ft^2\),
first at finite volume, then in (6); hence NC. SUS is an optional
stronger producer, not a necessary condition. For example the convex
function \(|t|^{1+\alpha}\), \(0<\alpha<1\), has vanishing secant
width but unbounded second derivative at zero; this is an analytic
example of the distinction, not a YM pressure calculation.

For the lattice source in (4), its variance density is exactly
\[
 V^{-1}\operatorname{Var}_u(A)
       =a^4\sum_y\operatorname{Cov}_u(Y_a(F),\tau_yY_a(F)). \tag{15}
\]
Absolute summability uniformly in the real tilt would imply SUS;
this is plainly a long-distance estimate. It includes nearby
translated copies with overlapping supports, so separated SRC bounds
alone cannot supply even its short-distance part. No such variance or
fine repeated-support moment bound is assumed elsewhere in this note.
Cancellation or a direct NC proof can avoid SUS. No claim that a
mass gap is necessary, or sufficient without additional amplitude and
tilt controls, is made.

### 4.4 Source admissibility and a shrinking-radius version

**PROVEN warning about the proposed hypotheses.** Even existence of all
ordinary polynomial moments does not imply an interval of finite real
exponential moments for every polynomial. For a standard real Gaussian
\(X\), every moment is finite by Gaussian tail integration, but
\(\int_{\mathbb R}\exp(t x^4-x^2/2)\,dx=\infty\) for every \(t>0\).
At sufficiently large \(|x|\), the exponent is at least \(t x^4/2\).
Thus the fixed-\(t_\ell\) form of NC/PC must not be sold as a consequence
of all-order moment bounds. The compact lattice makes (4) finite at
each cutoff; it gives no uniform continuum source interval. This is
another probability example, not a Wilson source-divergence theorem.

A more flexible version uses **moving source radii**. For each real
\(F\), prescribe \(t_{j,F}>0\), tending to zero, and require
\[
 w_{j,F}:=\frac{p^I_{a_j,F}(t_{j,F})+
                        p^I_{a_j,F}(-t_{j,F})}{t_{j,F}}\to0,\qquad
 e_{j,F}:=\frac{\max_\pm\varepsilon_{j,F_Q}(\pm t_{j,F})}
                       {t_{j,F}}\to0.                    \tag{MS}
\]
Here the PC bound must hold at these **actual** source values for
the transported product \(F_Q\), and both compared geometries use the
same source. No uniform fixed nonzero source radius is asserted.
Under COMMON and (MS), the conclusion of §4.2 is still **PROVEN**:
at cutoff \(a_j\), choose \(R_j\) so the finitely many pressures for
the first \(j\) products, at their respective source values, have
error at most \(t_{j,F}/j\). Equations (11) and (13) then give bounds
\(w_{j,F}+2/j\) and \(w_{j,F}+e_{j,F}+2/j\), respectively.
All these choices are finite at each stage, so one common diagonal
still suffices. To use (16) here its entire right side must be
\(o(t_{j,F})\), including the source-dependent scalar contribution.
Shrinking a source radius cannot be assumed to beat an unrelated UV
error, and it cannot remove a genuine pressure cusp.

**Optional bounded-source alternative, PROVEN conditional implication.**
Apply the same pressure theorem to the local clipping
\(T_M(Y)=\max(-M,\min(Y,M))\) of each real product. Its range and
translation covariance are unchanged and its bound is \(M\).
NC/PC or MS must be proved for every fixed \(M\), with their own
source comparisons. Recover the original moments only if, on the
same diagonal and required orientations,
\[
 \lim_{M\to\infty}\limsup_j
 |\omega_j(Y_{a_j}(F))-\omega_j(T_M(Y_{a_j}(F)))|=0.       \tag{TAIL}
\]
The triangle inequality proves the transfer. Uniform absolute
integrability is sufficient for TAIL, but stronger than the displayed
signed condition. Neither is provided by SRC-E′ or a separated
Schwinger-distribution bound. Clipping is an auxiliary lattice
observable here, not an asserted operation on a collision-flat
continuum algebra. This route therefore leaves TAIL as an explicit
additional OPEN interface.

## 5. Using Proposition T without its extensive total-variation loss

### 5.1 An extensive-source producer for PC — PROVEN implication; inputs OPEN

Write the **unnormalized**, real-source conditional functions as
\[
 Z^G_{a,R,F,t}(V_c)
 =\int k^G(V_c\mid U)\exp[-S^G(U)+tA^G_{a,R,F}(U)]\,dU .
\]
The coarse datum is \(V_c\), distinct from physical volume \(V\).
Forward kernel normalization gives the correct fine partition function
after integrating \(V_c\). On a common coarse space at length
\(\ell_0\), suppose for all compatible large \(R\), all coarse data
including large fields, and each fixed required \(F,t\),
\[
 \sup_{V_c}|\log Z^I_t-\log Z^Q_t-c_{a,R,F}(t)|
       \le N_b\eta_{a,F}(t),\qquad N_b=V/\ell_0^4,         \tag{SP}
\]
and separately
\[
 \frac{|c_{a,R,F}(t)-c_{a,R,F}(0)|}{V}
       \le v_{a,F}(t),\quad
 v_{a_j,F}(t)+\ell_0^{-4}
       [\eta_{a_j,F}(t)+\eta_{a_j,F}(0)]\to0.              \tag{VAC}
\]
A uniformly bounded ratio \(N_b/V\) is equally sufficient. These are
**extensive-source** and volume-uniform hypotheses. Source-dependent
vacuum terms cannot be discarded merely because they are constants
in \(V_c\).

Integrate the two exponential inequalities as in Proposition T, but
take logarithms, divide by volume, and subtract the identity at \(t=0\).
Exactly,
\[
 |p^{I,I}_{a,R,F}(t)-p^{Q,I}_{a,R,F}(t)|
 \le v_{a,F}(t)+\ell_0^{-4}[\eta_{a,F}(t)+\eta_{a,F}(0)]. \tag{16}
\]
Taking \(R\to\infty\) proves PC. There is no coarse covariance estimate
in this deduction and no factor \(e^{2N_b\eta}\). The volume factor
cancels for pressure. The price for recovering local moments is NC.

**OPEN producer contract.** Prove SP/VAC for translated sources of
every required product, with the same trajectory, normalizations,
source-dependent scalar terms, and all coarse fields. The sources in
(4) are local finite-range interactions at each fixed \(a\), but are
not just the pointwise linear e-source in SRCCD55. Their norm and
radius must be matched to an actual all-order source theory.
No claim is made that the fixed-source-radius coordinator result,

### 5.2 Why the old fixed-volume limits cannot just be interlaced

**PROVEN limitation of the limit argument.** A cutoff-first error
vanishing at each fixed \(R\), and a volume-first error vanishing at
each fixed \(a\), do not ensure a common good diagonal. For example
\[
 E_{\rm UV}(a,R)=aR,\qquad E_{\rm vol}(a,R)=1/(aR)
\]
have those separate limits but their product is 1. Neither can vanish
with the other. This is an array example, not a lower bound on Wilson
errors. Equations (SP)/(VAC) are a concrete stronger route around
this incompatibility, not a consequence of the old diagonal lemma.
Alternatively one could prove direct quantitative overlap of the
original UV and volume estimates on an admissible sequence.

No result here applies automatically to an already prescribed
\((a_j,R_j)\). For that sequence, supply its finite-volume pressure
errors divided by each fixed \(t_\ell\), or prove the direct VQ-all
estimate there. Existence of a freely selected diagonal is the scope
of §4's theorem.
