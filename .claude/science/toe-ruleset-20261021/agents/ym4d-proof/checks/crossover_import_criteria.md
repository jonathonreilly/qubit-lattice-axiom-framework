## Target and decision rule

Test whether the all-size Gaussian/core supplier used by the compact handoff
actually follows from its printed hypotheses. A failure of one asserted
implication prevents importing that implication. It does not disprove the
Yang–Mills theorem, rule out a repair, or establish that all other arguments in
the paper fail. No continuum target row will be closed by a finite diagnostic.

1. **Exact Gaussian normalization.** For two cores of dimension `d`, use
   `Qdiag = I`, `Qcross = [[0, t I], [t I, 0]]`, `0 < t < 1/8`.
   Derive the density ratio by both block determinant/Schur complement and
   orthogonal plus/minus modes. Keep the scalar normalizer. The operator row
   and positive spectral floor must remain independent of `d`. The
   log-normalizer must retain its factor `d`. Fixed weighted core distance is
   permitted and changes only the common row constant.
2. **Controls.** Check `t=0`, fixed rank one with growing ambient dimension,
   and `t=c/sqrt(d)` where Hilbert–Schmidt mass remains bounded. Repeated
   independent mode pairs must add their log-normalizers. These controls
   separate rank growth from dimension alone and from a sign/normalization
   error.
3. **Corrected bound.** For a general rectangular cross block `B`, derive
   `-log det(I-B*B)/2` and its two Hilbert–Schmidt bounds. Validate the formula
   against the determinant of the full positive block precision, including
   unequal dimensions. The proof, not finite numerical agreement, supplies
   the arbitrary-dimensional assertion.
4. **Source-sensitive control.** Let an external parameter multiply the
   cross block. Retain the determinant in the normalized source logarithm
   and check the first/second derivatives independently. A source-dependent
   normalizer cannot be discarded as a harmless vacuum constant.
5. **Pivot geometry.** Only if the reference-factor proof really uses
   `diam(A) >= c R (n-1)`, test separated grids on distinct parallel active
   paths, and a single-path control. Distinguish a false general geometric
   inference from an actual Wilson/Karcher factor violating the theorem.
6. **Valid bounded supplier.** Reconstruct the original Wilson single-link
   conditional density with every normalizer present. For pivots sharing no
   plaquette, test exact conditional factorization and the Haar minorization
   mechanism; do not identify a perimeter/carrier bound with dense local
   source clustering or a physical continuum mass gap.

Diagnostics will preserve failed runs, numerical tolerances, source hashes,
and explicit mutation predicates for omitted rank, omitted normalizer, wrong
Schur sign, and an invalid diameter-to-pivot-count conversion. Exact rational
or symbolic identities will be used where possible. Numerical controls are
finite checks only. No fitted constants will be used to make the candidate
estimate pass.

## Scope and possible outcomes

The Gaussian two-core example tests Lemma 3.26 as printed and its cited
normalization proof. A lattice placement with large separated cores tests
what exponential locality can imply; it is not an identification with the
actual constrained Karcher precision. A valid repair must establish the
needed local trace/size budget for that actual precision and re-prove its
downstream admission. Unproved source/denominator/domain identifications will
be reported separately from exact counterexamples.

No formal negative-route packet PASS, global literature exhaustion, audit
verdict, or claim of whole-paper invalidity is sought. Full existence and the
physical continuum mass gap remain open until their actual producers are
proved and independently checked.
