## Binding fixes

### B1 — Exterior factors cancel in gamma_C, not in the defined Z_C

At lines 94–96, `Z_C` is defined as the integral of the **full** weight
`w_h(y,x_C,z)`. Lines 116–119 correctly cancel factors not reading C in
the normalized conditional density. Lines 150–156 then prescribe an
array b_C obtained from incident smooth factors **after that cancellation**,
but lines 173–174 apply it to the originally defined full `Z_C` and
`log Z_C`. That inference is false when the canceled exterior factor
depends on a differentiated retained parameter.

A direct fixed-support counterexample needs no rare sector or moving cut.
Let the x_C base measure have mass one and let the full weight be
`w_h(y,x_C,z)=exp(A y)`, where A is any nonzero real constant and this
factor does not read x_C. The incident-factor product is 1, so the proposed
post-cancellation recipe gives b_C=0. The conditional density is indeed 1,
and all its positive-order y derivatives vanish. But the defined full
normalizer is `Z_C=exp(A y)`, so

    |partial_y Z_C|/Z_C = |A| > M_1(0)=0,
    |partial_y log Z_C| = |A| > L_1(0)=0.

The abstract Bell-polynomial theorem is correct when b_C actually bounds
the scores of the weight defining Z_C. The defect is the identification
of that weight with the incident-only score recipe.

One sufficient repair is to write, on a positive fiber,

    w_h = F_ext(y,z) q_C(y,x_C,z),
    Z_C^full = F_ext Z_C^loc,
    Z_C^loc = int q_C dx_C,
    gamma_C = q_C/Z_C^loc.

Apply the incident-factor versions of MB6–MB7 to `q_C,Z_C^loc`. If full
normalizer or full normalizer-ratio derivatives are claimed, retain
`log F_ext` and the corresponding exterior ratio with their own scores
and full readers. Alternatively, keep the original Z_C everywhere and
include all its y-dependent exterior factors in b_C; then do not describe
that b_C as an incident-only bound. Make MB8–MB9 and the budget references
in lines 553–560 use the chosen convention consistently. No change to the
positive conditional law or to the all-cut comparison identity is needed.

Lines 314–321 require H to contain S and the readers of F and n_S, but do
not require H to be connected. Line 366 nevertheless asserts that every
`Y=H union its attachments` is connected. Each attachment is connected and

comparison hypotheses. The zero-attachment term has `Y=H`, nonzero

the hypothesis used at `REFMATCH.md:184–185`; it cannot be removed while
retaining the connected-polymer conclusion. The scalar inequalities do
not fail merely because H is disconnected, but the advertised supported
