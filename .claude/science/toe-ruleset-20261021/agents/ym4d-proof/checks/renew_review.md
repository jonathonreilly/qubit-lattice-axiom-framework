### Linear map and its iterates

The initial and final axial legs contribute a scalar coboundary, so
`Q_root=Q_str-dh` and `dQ_root=dQ_str`. In the four coarse-link sum, each
straight long edge can be paired at the same within-block offset; discrete
Stokes then gives R4. Its nonnegative coefficient matrix has row sum L^2.
For a fixed fine plaquette and each pair of in-plane shifts s,t, the block
and within-block offset are unique. Hence its column sum is L^(-2).
Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz therefore gives the factor
`L^2 L^(-2)=1` in four dimensions, or `L^(4-d)` in dimension d.

A direct Fourier check independently tests sharpness on exact periodic
curvatures. Put `q=exp(2pi i/(LT))` and `A_2=q^x1/(q-1)`, with other
components zero. Then `F_12=q^x1`, and R4 has multiplier
`(1+q+...+q^(L-1))^2`. The coarse/fine number-of-sites ratio is L^(-4).
This gives R6, tending to one as T grows. For T>2 the sampled real and
imaginary modes each have half the complex squared norm at both levels,
so the same ratio holds for real fields. Scaling amplitude can make the
potential small; it does not change the ratio. This defeats a strict
volume-uniform constant for this operator, without invoking a forbidden
constant exact curvature on a periodic torus.

For two straight averaging steps, write the two block offsets as
`b=La+r` and the two path positions as `v=Lt+s`. These are bijections to
`0,...,L^2-1` in the respective coordinates. Induction yields the direct
side-n map, n=L^r. Root averaging maps a gradient to the gradient of its
root values, so all intermediate root corrections remain exact. For each
fixed fine/coarse plaquette pair, at most n^2 in-plane shifts contribute,
each with weight n^(-4), giving the matrix-entry bound n^(-2). Summation
against absolute fine curvature proves R7. The stated coarse-side and

remaining support exponent bounds the connected log norm at mu by Q.
