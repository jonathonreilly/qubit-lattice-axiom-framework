## Verdict in plain words
T12 is a counting wall. If each grid site holds one record ever, the record order has at most three dimensions whatever the rule: an interval whose longest chain has h steps sits inside a ball of radius h on the grid, so it holds about h^3 records, where a 3+1 spacetime needs h^4. The skewed 2D order is the best such a layer can do; the cube-symmetric growth orders I built do far worse. So 3+1 costs one thing: each site must host a growing number of events. I priced the wall; I did not pass it.

## The wall (checked)
- Plain: Records sit once on a 3D grid. If the grid is space and time together, only two directions are space and the law is one-way and lopsided. To get three space plus time, records must form again every tick, which the axioms exclude.
- Precise: derive a 3+1 event order from Lattice + Record. Supplied dynamics or re-recording do not count.
- Real? Yes, with two corrections. (1) "Z^3 as spacetime" is a comparator: the axiom says "physical sites" (`MINIMAL_AXIOMS:37`), and a 3+1 tick surface is already an approved primitive (`PRIMITIVE_REGISTRY_CHECK.md:58-96`), so the wall bites only the record layer. (2) The entry says order holds "only above" 3(I0+I2)/2; the note gives a sufficient condition. Its numbers reproduce. Not solved in `docs/`, `archive/` or 36 open PRs.
