## Verdict in plain words
The wall bundles three claims. A qubit cannot hold an SU(N) link: true, but a storage cost. No record pattern satisfies the Gauss law: true only for colour-resolved patterns in one fixed basis, which the source note flags. Nothing forms multi-site links: true, and this is the real residue. Rerunning the wall's own model with colour-blind local labels as records, the Gauss law becomes a local parity rule, each singlet is one label pattern (plus one local label at valence 4 or more), and the dynamics stays local. The wall is misframed; what remains is siblings T77 (forming star constraints) and T10 (readout basis).

## The wall (checked)
- Plain: Each site holds one qubit; only records are readable. An SU(2) or SU(3) link needs 4 or 6 states, more than a qubit. In the tested model no colour-labelled pattern is colour-neutral, so colour-neutral states look unreadable. No rule builds multi-qubit links from nearest-neighbour records.
- Precise: Target: a record-level account of SU(N), N>=2, gauge links. Supplied: link (N,1)+(1,N); the 16-qubit SU(2) four-corner cycle; occupation-basis readout. Proved: at least 2N states; 0 of 65,536 basis patterns in the Gauss kernel. Passing: a readout where Gauss-satisfying states are patterns, with local dynamics and a formation route.
- Real? Precise statements yes (reproduced). The plain wording drops "fixed occupation basis" (`docs/A_NON_ABELIAN_GAUSS_LAW_HAS_NO_RECORD_PATTERN_SOLUTIONS_BOUNDED_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-09-03.md`:4,21,113). Not solved elsewhere; no open PR touches it.

Hop: 144 label entries, all single-link, far-label spread 7e-16. Plaquette term local.
