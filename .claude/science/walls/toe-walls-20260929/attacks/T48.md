## Verdict in plain words
The wall is real but mislabelled. A circulant is not a mixing mechanism in any sector: two operators sharing it cannot mix, and leptons obey the same rule (circulant charged leptons plus circulant neutrinos give no PMNS; the repo gets large PMNS by putting charged leptons in the corner basis instead). Mixing is the relative orientation of two operators, and cubic covariance forces every law-level operator on the triplet to commute. One shared seed with sector-dependent masses fits CKM only if far from circulant, then misses the charged-lepton rotation and PMNS. Priced, not passed.

## The wall (checked)
- Plain: Each fermion type has three families. Lepton masses use a cyclic three-family pattern; if quark up and down types used it too, they would not mix at all. Quark mixing comes from another construction. Nothing says why they differ, or why quark mixing is small and neutrino mixing large.
- Precise: derive, on the hw=1 triplet, the (u,d,e,ν) spectra and the relative orientations V_CKM, U_PMNS from axioms plus registered primitives. Sector-specific fitted carriers do not pass.
- Real? Yes, misstated three ways. (i) The no-go tests P-Heavy-A, an unregistered candidate (`NEWPHYSICS_NP_CKM…npCKM.md:16-21,334-336`); the commuting-operator fact is general. (ii) The atlas (1+5, α_s/2) sits on the 6-state gauge block, not the triplet (L09-W3 iv); the triplet quark route is GST/NNI. (iii) The split also runs inside leptons: Koide circulant against `U_e = I`. No solution in `docs/`, `archive/` or open PRs.
