## Verdict in plain words
The wall is real and I did not pass it: ground-state methods only cap the wave's energy from above. Near the Rokhsar-Kivelson (RK) point, the quantity whose positivity makes the wave light-like (energy cost of uniform electric flux) is, to first order in the detuning, a classical counting number. I measured it to 32^3; it does not shrink (exponent p = 0.01 +/- 0.02). What remains is the original wall: is the lowest level that wave, and is the phase stable.

## The wall (checked)
- Plain: Qubits sit on grid links; arrows balance at every crossing; the only moves flip four arrows round a square. Evidence shows a wave costing almost nothing at long wavelength. Whether its energy grows linearly with wavenumber, like light, or as the square is undecided. Our methods give upper limits only, so a slower, faint wave is never excluded.
- Precise: for H = -K sum F_p + V sum F_p^2, V < K, ice-rule space, thermodynamic limit, Coulomb phase: the lowest excitation coupled to transverse E has omega >= c|k|, c > 0. Passing: proof or decisive lower bound. Not passing: moment upper bounds, the rotor photon, finite-size energies. V is supplied, so only "exists V" is needed; "all V < K" is probably false (ordering at V <= 0, probe 9 T7).
- Real? Yes, correctly stated, unsolved (docs, archive, open PRs searched).
