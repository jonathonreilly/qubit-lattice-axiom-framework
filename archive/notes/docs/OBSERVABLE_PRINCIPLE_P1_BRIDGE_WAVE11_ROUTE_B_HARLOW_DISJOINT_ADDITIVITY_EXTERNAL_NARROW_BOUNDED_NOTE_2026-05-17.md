## 0. Honest framing up front

The task posed the question: does HSSS disjoint additivity, applied to
the framework's staggered Grassmann substrate, close P1, or is the
framework one of HSSS's named counter-examples to full additivity?

The honest answer recorded here is **neither**: HSSS disjoint additivity
is about a **different additivity** than P1. Specifically:

- **HSSS additivity** is on the lattice of von Neumann algebras
  `A(R)` indexed by spacetime regions `R`. The "additivity" operation is
  the join `∨` in the lattice of von Neumann algebras: `A(R_1) ∨ A(R_2)
  := vN(A(R_1) ∪ A(R_2))`, the smallest von Neumann algebra containing
  both. Disjoint additivity is the assertion that for `R_1 ⊥ R_2`
  (disjoint), `A(R_1 ∪ R_2) = A(R_1) ∨ A(R_2)`.
- **P1 additivity** is on the abelian group `(R, +)` of real-valued
  scalar functionals `W: U_0 → R`. The "additivity" operation is real
  addition: for independent subsystems `J = J_A ⊕ J_B`, `W[J_A ⊕ J_B]
  = W[J_A] + W[J_B]`.

These are two structurally distinct additivities. The functorial bridge
"region algebras to scalar generator" requires choosing
`W = -log τ` (Connes-Stormer trace) or equivalently `W = log Z` or
`W = -beta · F`, which is the Cauchy log functional equation classifier.
The choice of `log` over `(.)^p` for `p != 0` IS the P1 selection step;
this is precisely Route A obstruction D5 ("Pattern L circularity") in
the [Route D consolidated no-go note](OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_P1_BRIDGE_ROUTE_D_SHARPENED_NO_GO_NOTE_2026-05-17.md).

The HSSS Route B attempt therefore lands as `bounded_theorem` with the
honest finding that the HSSS theorem is **orthogonal to P1 as posed**:
it constrains the algebra-of-regions side of the operator-algebraic
formalism, where the framework already satisfies it via tensor product

selection of "additive" over "multiplicative" (or `r^p`) on the scalar

## 1. The HSSS theorem statement (verbatim from abstract)

From the published arXiv abstract of D. Harlow, S.-H. Shao, J. Sorce,
M. Srivastava, *Disjoint additivity and local quantum physics*,
arXiv:2509.03589 (2025):

> Quantum systems of physical interest are often local, but there are
> at least three competing perspectives on how "locality" should be
> formalized: an algebraic framework, a path-integral framework, and a
> lattice framework. One puzzle in this competition is that systems
> with higher-form symmetries, which are perfectly local from the
> path-integral and lattice perspectives, can violate the algebraic
> principle of "additivity". In this paper, we propose a resolution to
> this puzzle by introducing a weaker locality principle, "disjoint
> additivity", which together with Haag duality should always be
> satisfied in local quantum systems. As evidence, we give examples in
> which disjoint additivity is preserved when ordinary additivity is
> violated; we show that Haag duality and disjoint additivity are
> satisfied in rather general lattice systems with local symmetry
> constraints; we give examples of nonlocal theories in which either
> disjoint additivity or Haag duality is violated; and finally we give
> examples of systems with nonlocal symmetry constraints in which
> disjoint additivity is violated, but can be restored by passing to a
> local "SymTFT" system in one higher dimension.

The key technical points relevant to P1:

- **(H1) Algebraic additivity** in HSSS: for a Haag-Kastler net
  `{A(R)}_R` indexed by spacetime regions, the additivity property
  `A(R) = ∨_α A(R_α)` for any cover `R = ⋃_α R_α`. This is the lattice
  join of von Neumann algebras, NOT scalar addition.
- **(H2) Disjoint additivity** (HSSS proposal): the **weaker** property
  `A(R_1 ∪ R_2) = A(R_1) ∨ A(R_2)` for `R_1, R_2` **disjoint** only.
- **(H3) Haag duality**: `A(R)' = A(R^c)`, where `R^c` is the causal
  complement and `A'` denotes the commutant.
- **(H4) The classes where ordinary additivity fails but disjoint
  additivity holds:** systems with higher-form symmetries (gauge theories
  with global subsystem constraints), as the HSSS abstract states "Haag
  duality and disjoint additivity are satisfied in rather general
  lattice systems with local symmetry constraints".

The four "named counter-examples" referenced in the task statement
(timelike hyperplane restriction, generalized free fields, global-
symmetry-invariant sectors, Virasoro identity multiplet) are the
standard published counter-examples to FULL Haag-Kastler additivity
prior to HSSS, surveyed in the algebraic QFT literature. The HSSS paper
documents that disjoint additivity continues to hold even when full
additivity fails. The runner verifies the framework satisfies disjoint
additivity in the relevant sense, and verifies that this finding is
about region algebras not scalar generators.

## 2. Structural finding — HSSS Route B does NOT close P1

### 2.1 The additivity-type mismatch (F1-Route-B)

**(F1-Route-B) Algebraic additivity vs scalar additivity are different
group-theoretic structures.** HSSS disjoint additivity is an identity
in the lattice `vN(H)` of von Neumann algebras of operators on the
physical Hilbert space `H`. The operation `∨` is the von Neumann join,
i.e., the smallest von Neumann algebra containing both arguments. The
operation has neither a scalar real-number incarnation nor a natural
abelian-group structure compatible with real addition. By contrast, P1
additivity `W[J_A ⊕ J_B] = W[J_A] + W[J_B]` is addition in the abelian
group `(R, +)` of real numbers, applied pointwise to a real-valued
scalar functional `W: U_0 → R`.

No natural functor `vN(H) → R` carries `∨` to `+`. The candidate maps
that do — `A ↦ -log τ(A)` for `A` positive in a trace state, or
`A ↦ log Z(A)` via the partition function — invoke `log` explicitly,
and the choice of `log` over `A ↦ τ(A)^p` for `p != 0` IS the Cauchy
log-functional-equation classifier, which IS P1 in different
vocabulary. This circularity is recorded as Pattern L (D5) in the
[Route D consolidated no-go note](OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_P1_BRIDGE_ROUTE_D_SHARPENED_NO_GO_NOTE_2026-05-17.md);
HSSS Route B reproduces it in disjoint-net vocabulary.

### 2.2 The framework satisfies disjoint additivity (F2-Route-B)

**(F2-Route-B) Disjoint Grassmann blocks satisfy disjoint additivity
trivially via tensor product factorization.** For the framework's
substrate (Cl(3) per-site Fock module + Z^3 sites + Wilson plaquette
+ compact SU(3)) restricted to a finite hierarchy block, the Hilbert
space factorizes on disjoint sub-blocks `Lambda = Lambda_A ⊔ Lambda_B`
(no shared sites, no hopping bonds across the partition):

```text
H_Lambda  ≅  H_{Lambda_A}  ⊗  H_{Lambda_B}                              (T1)
```

This is the Cl(3) per-site Fock tensor structure (Route A's (O1)) plus
the assumption that the partition is along the per-site product
direction. Local operators supported on `Lambda_A` act as `O_A ⊗ I_B`,
and the algebra of such operators is `A(Lambda_A) = B(H_{Lambda_A}) ⊗
I_B` (and analogously for `B`). The join is

```text
A(Lambda_A)  ∨  A(Lambda_B)  =  vN(B(H_{Lambda_A}) ⊗ I_B  ∪  I_A ⊗ B(H_{Lambda_B}))
                              =  B(H_{Lambda_A} ⊗ H_{Lambda_B})
                              =  A(Lambda_A ∪ Lambda_B).                (T2)
```

This is the operator-algebraic translation of Route A's (O1)+(O2). The
framework therefore satisfies HSSS disjoint additivity on disjoint
Grassmann blocks. (This is not "framework matches HSSS premises and
HSSS theorem closes P1" — it's "framework satisfies HSSS disjoint
additivity, which is the operator-algebraic restatement of Route A's
multiplicative factorization, which does not close P1".)

### 2.3 Higher-form symmetries on framework substrate (F3-Route-B)

**(F3-Route-B) Wilson plaquette gauge constraints are higher-form
symmetry candidates.** The framework's Wilson plaquette action enforces
SU(3) gauge constraints on closed paths around plaquettes. In the HSSS
classification, gauge theories with such local symmetry constraints fall
into the "higher-form symmetry / lattice systems with local symmetry
constraints" class where the HSSS abstract states "Haag duality and
disjoint additivity are satisfied in rather general lattice systems
with local symmetry constraints". The framework substrate is therefore
inside the HSSS "disjoint additivity holds" class, NOT inside any of
the four counter-example classes. The framework does **not** map to:

- HSSS counter-example 1 (timelike hyperplane restriction): framework
  is not restricted to a timelike hyperplane.
- HSSS counter-example 2 (generalized free fields): framework is
  Grassmann, not bosonic generalized free field.
- HSSS counter-example 3 (global-symmetry-invariant sectors only):
  framework is the full Grassmann algebra, not the restriction to
  global SU(3)-invariant sector.
- HSSS counter-example 4 (Virasoro identity multiplet): framework is
  not 1+1d CFT.

So the framework is not in any of the HSSS counter-example classes;
disjoint additivity holds. But — see (F1-Route-B) — this is about
region algebras, not scalar generators, so it does not close P1.

### 2.4 The four HSSS counter-examples do not map to P1 either (F4-Route-B)

**(F4-Route-B) Even if the framework matched one of the HSSS counter-
examples, that would not deliver a sharpened no_go on P1.** Suppose for
contradiction the framework were analogous to (e.g.) the Virasoro
identity multiplet HSSS counter-example. The HSSS-counter-example
content would be "full additivity of region algebras fails: there exist
covers `R = ⋃_α R_α` with `A(R) ⊋ ∨_α A(R_α)`". This is a statement
about operator algebras, NOT about scalar generators. It would not
imply P1 fails. Equivalently, the failure of full algebraic additivity
in (say) generalized free fields does NOT imply that the free energy
`W = -beta F = log Z` fails to be additive on independent subsystems;
the free energy is still additive on disjoint independent subsystems by
construction (it's `log` of a product). The two additivities are
independent.

So the HSSS Route B attempt cannot deliver a sharpened no_go on P1
either: it could only confirm or deny disjoint algebra additivity,
which is structurally orthogonal to scalar generator additivity.

### 2.5 The `F_p` counterexample family reappears (F5-Route-B)

**(F5-Route-B) The `F_p[J] = r(J)^p` counterexample family of
Route A reappears unchanged when viewed in HSSS disjoint-net
vocabulary.** For the disjoint Grassmann blocks of (F2-Route-B), let
`r(J) := |det(D + J)| / |det D| > 0` and consider the scalar
functionals `F_p[J] := r(J)^p` for `p in R \ {0}`. Each `F_p` is:

- continuous on `U_0`;
- CPT-even (depends only on `|Z|`);
- multiplicatively factorizing on disjoint blocks via (T2):
  `F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B] = (r_A(J_A) · r_B(J_B))^p = F_p[J_A] · F_p[J_B]`;
- **not** additive for `p != 0`:
  `F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B] != F_p[J_A] + F_p[J_B]` generically.

The HSSS disjoint additivity result is compatible with every `F_p` for
every `p`. It does not select the `p = 0` (i.e., `log r`) additive
representative over the multiplicative family. The selection of `log`
over `(.)^p` for `p != 0` IS the Cauchy classifier, which IS P1 in
different vocabulary. HSSS Route B therefore reproduces the same

### 6.2 Elon first-principles

From Haag-Kastler axioms (covariance, locality, additivity, vacuum):

- "Additivity" in Haag-Kastler is `A(R) = ∨_α A(R_α)` for any open
  cover. This is a property of the assignment `R ↦ A(R)`, not of any
  scalar quantity.
- The join `∨` is the von Neumann generation operator, defined as
  `A ∨ B := (A ∪ B)''` (double commutant). This is a non-numerical
  operation.
- Disjoint additivity is the weaker form `A(R_1 ∪ R_2) = A(R_1) ∨
  A(R_2)` for disjoint `R_1, R_2`, which follows from tensor product
  Hilbert factorization in the absence of long-range correlations or
  global constraints across the partition.
- The framework's Cl(3) per-site Fock substrate has explicit tensor
  product factorization on disjoint sub-blocks. Disjoint additivity
  therefore holds trivially.
- The bridge "region algebra to scalar generator" requires identifying
  a scalar functional of `A(R)`. Candidates: trace `τ(A)`, entropy
  `-tr(rho log rho)`, free energy `log Z(A) = -beta F`. Each candidate
  invokes `log` to get from multiplicative trace to additive scalar,
  which is the Cauchy classifier (= P1).

So even with HSSS disjoint additivity in hand, the bridge to P1
requires the Cauchy log classifier separately. HSSS does not provide
that classifier.
