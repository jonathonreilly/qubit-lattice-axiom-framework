# Proposal Record: How to Undo the Former Owner-Governed Premises

**Date:** 2026-07-11

**Type:** meta

**Proposal status:** This dated record lays out options that were put to the
owner. It chooses none of them and changes nothing. It supplies no premise.
For the current rule, use the
[Axiom Minimality Policy](audit/AXIOM_MINIMALITY_POLICY.md).

## The question that was put to the owner

On 2026-07-05, two unproved statements were placed in a new
"owner-governed" category:

1. **How to count the generation doublet.** A matched pair of generation
   modes counts once, as one pair, rather than once per real component. This
   says nothing about the value of the mass-ratio dial or about a weighting
   law.
2. **How to read the angle.** A particular registered angle may be read in its
   natural units, with no extra conversion factor.

At the time, the special category allowed later work to treat those two
statements as supplied even though they were not axioms, approved primitives,
or derived results.

On 2026-07-11, the owner objected that the framework could not keep such a
category. This record preserves the three options that were considered.

## Why the objection mattered

Moving the two statements into a new category did not derive either one.
Calling the admitted-input count zero could therefore suggest that the
science had done more than the bookkeeping had actually established.

## Options considered

### A. Remove the special category in full

- Return both statements to the list of explicit, unproved conditions.
- Restore the admitted-input count to two.
- Treat later results that use either statement as conditional on it.
- On the strong-CP lane, leave the gauge-side work unchanged but make the
  mass-side reading conditional on the doublet-counting statement.

### B. Remove the category but keep the exact boundary wording

Keep the two careful boundary descriptions, but present them only as ordinary
unproved conditions. This has the same scientific effect as option A. The
difference is documentary: the exact limits of each condition remain easy to
find.

### C. Wait for the running derivation work

Keep the category temporarily while the `r=1/2` work tries to derive the
doublet-counting and equal-weight laws. If both become theorems, there is no
unproved input left to remove. If the work stops short, use option A.

None of these options changes a numerical result by itself. The question is
whether later work may treat an unproved statement as supplied.

## What this record does not do

This record does not create or restore a premise category, adopt either
statement, change any claim, or choose among A, B, and C. It is an owner-facing
proposal record only.
