## Why This Note Exists

The sibling selection note proves that the clarified Admissibility clause
selects K1 on the licensed two-class surface at the trivial background. That
result uses a qubit-factor fact: K0 carries only the scalar availability
algebra `C * I`, while K1 carries the direction-tagged maximal abelian
algebras generated by the computed `Gamma_mu`.

The question here is whether the tested fixed gauge-link backgrounds change
that selector. On this bounded surface they do not, provided the gauge links
act where the gauged surface says they act: on the lattice/color factor of each
hop. A legal gauged hop has the form

```text
U_{x,mu} tensor C_mu,
```

where `U_{x,mu}` is a fixed link matrix on the lattice/color factor and `C_mu`
is the K0 or K1 qubit-factor direction coefficient. The runner does not assume
that factorization as its conclusion: it builds the gauged hop operator, cuts
out each directed link block, and recovers the tensor factors by
operator-Schmidt factor analysis before comparing the recovered qubit factor
with the sibling coefficient structure.

## Statement

Use the parent representatives exactly:

```text
K0: phi=+1, representative t == 1 (scalar tight-binding; extensive zero surface).
K1: phi=-1, representative eta0: eta0_1 = 1, eta0_2 = (-1)^{x1}, eta0_3 = (-1)^{x1+x2}
(Kawamoto-Smit class; 8 isolated Dirac zeros; = absorbed naive Dirac).
```

Let `C_mu(K0) = I` on the one-site qubit factor, and let
`C_mu(K1) = Gamma_mu`, where the `Gamma_mu` are recovered from the sibling
absorbing-frame construction. For a fixed background, replace each directed
nearest-neighbor hop by

```text
U_{x,mu} tensor C_mu(K).
```

**T1 - background blindness of the discriminators.** For fixed random `U(1)`
phases per link and a fixed random `SU(2)` color-link background, the runner
recovers each directed hop block as a rank-one tensor factorization. The
qubit factors have the same algebraic data as at `U = 1`: K0 has
per-direction algebra dimensions `[1, 1, 1]` and zero availability variation;
K1 has dimensions `[2, 2, 2]`, mutually anticommuting recovered
direction representatives, and `sqrt(2)` availability gaps per axis.

**T2 - selection persists on the bounded test surface.** The clarified
Admissibility variation rule still selects K1 on every tested fixed legal
background. Within the legal tensor-factor premise, the gauge background does
not create the nonvacuous variation on the K0 side and does not destroy it on
the K1 side, because the variation lives in the recovered qubit factor rather
than in the color-link factor. Composing with the sibling D1/D2 computations
and the parent absorbing-frame theorem gives the nonzero first-order
Dirac-square kinetic carrier with the mutually anticommuting
self-adjoint-unitary coefficient family.

The honest boundary exhibit is also computed: the parent `Z2` flux anchors are
the `U = 1` slice. Under a general fixed background the plaquette data are
background-dependent. In the `U(1)` tests the plaquette phases move away from
the `+1` and `-1` anchors; in the `SU(2)` test the normalized Wilson
plaquette traces vary across plaquettes.

**T3 - contrast outside the licensed gauge action.** If a background is allowed
to act on the qubit direction-tag factor itself, the discriminator is no longer
protected. The runner applies a non-diagonal `SU(2)` qubit rotation on a
sub-region. This illegal operation can create apparent K0 variation and makes
the K1 direction coefficients nonuniform across the blocked cell. This is a
contrast leg: T1 is a theorem about where legal gauge links act, not a
tautology.

**T4 - zero-background consistency.** At `U = 1`, the runner reproduces the
sibling values: K0 dimensions `[1, 1, 1]`, K1 dimensions `[2, 2, 2]`, K0
availability gaps zero, K1 gaps `sqrt(2)`, the K0/K1 `Z2` flux anchors
`+1/-1`, and the K1 Dirac-square and anticommutation checks.

## Proof Sketch

The runner first reconstructs the sibling K1 `Gamma_mu` family from the
Kawamoto-Smit `eta0` signs and the parent absorbing frame
`T(x) = sigma_1^x1 sigma_2^x2 sigma_3^x3`. This is the same construction used
by the three sibling realized-kinetic-branch runners.

For each legal background, it builds the finite one-particle gauged hopping
operator on an `L = 3` periodic lattice. Each directed link block is reshaped
as a bipartite color/qubit operator and passed through a rank-one
operator-Schmidt extraction. The rank-one residuals are at machine precision,
and the recovered color factor is unitary after normalization. The recovered
qubit factor is then tested for its star-algebra dimension, spectral
projectors, availability gap, and K1 anticommutation. These are computed from
the extracted blocks, then compared with the expected sibling structure.

The `U(1)` leg uses three deterministic random seeds. The `SU(2)` leg uses a
deterministic random color-link background. In all cases, K0 remains the
neighbor-constant `[1, 1, 1]` case and K1 remains the direction-tagged
varying `[2, 2, 2]` case. Therefore the same survivor function as the sibling
Admissibility-variation runner returns only K1 when variation is required.

The boundary exhibit is not hidden. The same random backgrounds change the
plaquette data: `U(1)` plaquette phases no longer sit at the bare `Z2` anchors,
and `SU(2)` Wilson plaquette traces vary. Thus this note does not claim that
the parent flux-class anchor itself is unchanged under arbitrary background
curvature. The invariant object is the qubit-factor availability selector on
the fixed-background gauged surface.

## Consequence

On the bounded fixed-background gauged surface tested here, the sibling
selection theorem is stable: legal gauge-link backgrounds acting on
lattice/color factors do not alter the qubit-factor discriminator that the
clarified Admissibility clause uses. The K1 branch remains the selected branch
under this enlarged premise set, and the selected branch carries the inherited
nonzero first-order Dirac-square kinetic carrier.
