## 2. The Minimum Directed-Ray Law

### 2.2 Ready front

Define the directed nearest-neighbor front

```text

```

Every readiness decision uses one nearest neighbor. For a general finite

### 2.3 Content kernel and one step


For each `x` in `F_e(C)`, use


ray there is only one ready site, so no same-layer factorization issue occurs.
For several lines the minimum candidate takes the joint kernel to be the

## 4. Logical Apparatus Identity

### 4.1 Three different identities

The construction separates three statements often hidden in “the same
apparatus moved.”

1. **Record identity:** the record at site `ne` remains that same
   `(site,content)` pair forever. It does not move.
2. **Logical state identity:** two pointed heads with the same current bit have
   the same conditional future law.
3. **Machine-type identity:** heads with either bit implement the same
   two-state transition architecture `K`; the bit is an internal state, not a
   different apparatus type.

Only the second and third move down the ray.

### 4.2 Translated role-patch theorem

Fix a radius `r`. For every `n>=r`, translate the pointed occupancy patch
around `h_n=ne` back by `-ne`. Its directed-ray part is

```text
offsets -r,...,0       recorded,
offsets  1,...,r       open.
```

The occupancy/port role is exactly independent of `n`. The remote left seed
is outside the patch.

This local equivalence is not equality of the full state. The full wake has
length `n+1` and continues to grow.

### 4.3 Forward-head future-operational bisimulation

Let two heads at arbitrary absolute positions have the same current content
`x`. For every future word `y_1,...,y_m`, the conditional probability is

```text
K_(x,y_1) K_(y_1,y_2) ... K_(y_(m-1),y_m).
```

It contains neither absolute position nor any record older than the current
head. Translating one pointed front to the other therefore preserves every
finite future head-word law in the isolated-ray sector. Equal logical head
states are bisimilar for that protocol.

This quotient does not identify arbitrary global futures. Extra heads,
off-line records, obstacles, or interaction rules can distinguish the two
surroundings. A final matter-identity claim would need the larger theorem that
every allowed record protocol relevant to that matter excitation factors
through the same quotient.

For the equal-row kernel

```text
K = [[1-q,q],
     [1-q,q]],
```

even the current bit drops out. Every future trial has the same one-step law
after every same-line archive in the isolated ray-head trial. This is strong
predictive reset inside that candidate protocol, not a reset theorem for every
laboratory continuation.

For a general Markov kernel, the current head bit is one bit of lawful visible
memory. The older permanent archive is predictively irrelevant once that bit
is given.

### 4.4 What the identity theorem licenses

Calling the successive heads “one logical apparatus” is not an added physical
identification of records. It abbreviates:

- one causal lineage `h_n -> h_(n+1)`;
- one translated local interface;

That is enough for automaton/process identity. It is not yet a theorem that a

## 5. Certified Trial Corpus

### 5.1 Minimum overlapping edge block

Define trial `n` by the directed causal edge

```text
B_n = (h_n, h_(n+1)).
```

The predecessor head record certifies the ready input/front. The new record
at `h_(n+1)` is the binary outcome and close certificate. Once formed, it also
becomes the input/front certificate for the next trial.

The blocks overlap at their boundary record, but their outcome records are
distinct. A corpus of `N` trials contains

```text
X_1,...,X_N
```

on `N` pairwise-disjoint outcome sites and costs exactly `N` new records plus
the initial seed.

For outcome indicator `X_n in {0,1}`,

```text
C_N = sum_(n=1)^N X_n,
F_N = C_N/N
```

are readable from the permanent wake. Finite scalar additivity applies to the
distinct outcome records.

### 5.2 Why one record per trial is minimum only here

With no new record, site-tethered permanence leaves the complete record state
unchanged. Record-content-only readout then cannot distinguish two new binary
outcomes. One fresh `M_2` record supplies two orthogonal contents and is
sufficient. Thus one is minimal for:

```text
binary perfect readout + one fixed setting + overlapping edge certificate.
```

This does not price:

- separate preparation, outcome, and close records;
- variable settings or adaptive choices;
- a self-contained direction/header decoder;
- collision reservations;
- coherent quantum work state; or
- timing-coded/global-history outcomes.

Pairwise-disjoint two-record input/outcome blocks cost asymptotically two
records per trial. Three separately recorded `preparation/outcome/close` roles
cost at least three. The minimum ray law does not locally generate those role
phases; a finite motif/compiler must.

### 5.3 Certificate labels do not derive statistics

The repeated edge shape defines trial boundaries. It does not imply IID,
stationarity, mixing, component-mean equality, or Born weights. Those remain
properties of `K` and the boundary/history distribution, exactly as Cycle 21

## 6. Storage, Density, And Relational Clock

### 6.1 Exact storage and spatial density

After `N` trials,


### 6.3 Relational clock

## 7. Stationary Statistics In The Moving Frame

### 7.1 Exact Markov witness

Take

```text

     [1/3,2/3]].
```

Its unique stationary row vector is

```text
pi=(4/7,3/7),
pi K=pi.
```

The second eigenvalue is `5/12`. If `p_n=P(X_n=1)`, then

```text
p_(n+1)-3/7 = (5/12)(p_n-3/7).
```

Starting the seed content with distribution `pi` makes every finite outcome
window `(X_(n-r),...,X_n)` stationary under advancing the head. The occupancy
role patch is already constant for `n>=r`. Thus every fixed finite co-moving
record window has an exact stationary distribution.

The stationary adjacent-pair table is

```text
P(00)=3/7,
P(01)=1/7,
P(10)=1/7,
P(11)=2/7.
```

Because the chain is finite, irreducible, and aperiodic, its shift is ergodic.
Cycle 21's theorem then gives

```text
(1/N) sum_(n=1)^N X_n -> 3/7
```

almost surely under this supplied process.

### 7.2 Exact controls

The same geometry admits:

1. **IID reset kernel:** equal rows `(1-q,q)` give predictive reset and IID
   outcomes.
2. **Frozen identity kernel:** `K=I` preserves the seed sector; stationary
   mixtures exist, but individual histories have frequency zero or one.
3. **Flip kernel:** `0<->1` with a uniform phase is stationary and has
   pointwise frequency one half, but is periodic and not mixing.
4. **Nonstationary boundary:** a time-inhomogeneous or nonstationary supplied
   outcome history gives no stationary co-moving statistics despite identical
   head geometry.

Therefore logical recurrence does not derive statistical recurrence.

### 7.3 Local stationarity versus full-state stationarity

For a finite seed, the full pointed state is not stationary: the permanent
wake length increases every trial. Only every fixed-radius co-moving window
can have a stationary distribution after the seed boundary lies outside it.

There is an exact global traveling-wave history:


It obeys

```text

```

With a bi-infinite stationary Markov content sequence, the entire pointed
history is invariant under the combined one-step time/space shift. It has an
infinite past wake and no finite seed. That is global-history/boundary data,
not a consequence of the finite-seed law.
