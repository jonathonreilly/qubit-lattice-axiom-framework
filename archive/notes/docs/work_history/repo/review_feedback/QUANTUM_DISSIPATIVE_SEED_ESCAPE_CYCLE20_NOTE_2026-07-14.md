## Why Direct Nearest-Neighbor Jumps Fail

Take two conflicting candidate sites whose direct creation-jump supports are
disjoint. For example, any two sites farther apart than twice the direct
radius but still within Chebyshev distance nine suffice. On the all-open
product state, suppose each local jump has nonzero matrix element to its `B0`
state. The two jumps commute on their disjoint supports, and

```text
J_y J_x |open> != 0.
```

A continuous-time trajectory therefore assigns positive order-`t^2` weight to
the history in which both jumps occur before either can causally disable the
other. That history contains a forbidden pair of permanent `B0`s. Permanence
then makes the error unrecoverable.

## Dissipation: What It Solves and What It Does Not

A time-homogeneous Lindbladian can implement an autonomous local stochastic
law. Its quantum trajectories can contain individual irreversible record
jumps at random finite times, and a blockade mediator can in principle prevent
commit until a conflict handshake completes. This is the strongest local
dissipative escape from Cycle 19.

But finite-rate relaxation does not create an exact deterministic terminal
instant. For the elementary open-to-record jump with rate `lambda`, the
no-jump survival is

```text
Pr(open at t) = exp(-lambda t) > 0
```

for every finite `t`. More generally, on a finite-dimensional finite system,
`exp(t L)` for a bounded generator `L` is an invertible linear map for every
finite `t`; it cannot be a nontrivial exact projection onto a terminal
subspace. Exact stationarity can hold after an individual absorbing jump, but
the ensemble reaches its absorbing limit only asymptotically. On an infinite
lattice, the probability that every independently pending finite-rate event
has completed by one finite global time is zero.

The correct target is therefore local causal finality, not a universal end-of-
epoch signal: once a `B0` record is written, its local exclusion and diamond
continuation must be final even though other regions continue evolving.


They give the same density-matrix channel but different outcome transcripts:
on input `|0>`, the first labels two equiprobable phase branches while the
second gives the `P0` outcome with certainty. Thus **a channel does not
uniquely select its record instrument**. A record theory must state or derive
the instrument/unraveling and which branch labels count as records. Saying “a
clock locks it” does not supply those facts unless “clock” names this full
physical instrument.

## Reversible QCA and the Garbage Bill

The static isolated-winner function is noninjective. Both an empty candidate
field and a field containing two adjacent candidates map to no winners. A
unitary QCA cannot send these orthogonal inputs to the same completely clean
output. It must retain enough information to keep the total map injective:

```text
candidate input -> winner copy + candidate/arbitration garbage.
```

This is not by itself a failure. A reversible compute-copy-uncompute scheme
could:

1. preserve the candidate field;
2. reversibly propagate conflict information;
3. copy the isolated-winner bit into a permanent `B0` record degree of
   freedom; and
4. uncompute transient messages while leaving the original candidate
   information encoded elsewhere.

The winning diamond does not consume the whole lattice. At the conservative
candidate activity `p=1/6859`, candidate entropy density is small, while even
the crude isolated-candidate winning fraction

```text

```

is below one percent. Entropy capacity therefore does not close the garbage-
export route. The difficulty is a local, covariant routing construction, not a
global shortage of cells.

Candidate garbage has a useful special geometry. An isolated winner has no
other candidate within Chebyshev radius nine; the Cycle 17 support lies within
radius four. The winning candidate itself can become `B0`, and no losing
candidate occupies any of the other 110 required sites. This makes preserved
candidate data locally compatible with the diamond.

Arbitration-message garbage is harder. Sending it down a single ray chosen by

them opposite forward directions. The first ray toward positive `x` and the
second toward negative `x` occupy the same intermediate sites. Hence one naive
frame rail is not collision safe. A viable QCA needs a spatial code,
collision rule, reversible scattering, or garbage content that itself becomes
lawful downstream structure.
