## Corrected conditional algebra

At fixed slack the delta generator is

```text
T_DELTA - T_Q = [[0,-2,0],[-2,1,-1],[0,-1,-1]].
```

Its characteristic polynomial is `(x+2)(x²-2x-2)` and its eigenvalues are
`-2, 1-sqrt(3), 1+sqrt(3)`. Thus its operator norm is `1+sqrt(3)`.
The other norms are `||T_M||=1` and `||T_Q||=2`. For exact real half-widths,
the triangle inequality gives the valid perturbation bound

```text
||H-H_center|| <= h_m + (1+sqrt(3)) h_delta + 2 h_slack.
```

The corresponding radii are approximately `.0261602540378444` and
`.0144667295593008`. Weyl's inequality can use this corrected bound for
Hermitian eigenvalues. The old radii `.0211602540378444` and
`.0119167295593007` used the wrong fixed-slack generator. Independently checked
corner perturbation norms `.0225` and `.0126` exceed those old norm bounds.
The tested corner eigenvalue shifts did **not** disprove the old eigenvalue
intervals: the counterexample invalidates their stated norm justification.

For a Hermitian matrix with simple spectrum, the diagonal projector weight
is the corresponding principal cofactor of `H-lambda_j I` divided by
`product_{k != j}(lambda_j-lambda_k)`. The three numerator formulas in the
runner satisfy this conditional algebraic identity. This identity is distinct
from a verified implementation of interval arithmetic.

## What the implementation computes

The runner constructs floating center eigenvalues and corrected radius
estimates, applies ordinary floating interval-style cofactor operations,
clamps the resulting row estimates to `[0,1]`, and evaluates the supplied
kernel at endpoints plus one stationary candidate obtained by bisection on
`[0,.05]`. It reports the resulting numerical estimates and the original
regression comparisons. Except for the justified norm correction, numerical
predicates and thresholds are preserved. A failed legacy comparison remains
FAIL and the command exits nonzero; no desired margin is enforced by changing
its threshold. PASS counts describe these calculations and documentary checks
only, not a certificate for every point in either box.

The historical `certify_box`, `psi_sup_on_interval`, and `*_ub` identifiers
are retained internal API names for uncertified estimates. They confer no
certification. Their `1e-15` additions have no proved error-enclosure meaning.

## Why whole-box certification remains open

Nearest-rounded arithmetic does not give outward enclosures: adding singleton
intervals `[1,1]` and `[2^-54,2^-54]` returns `[1,1]`, excluding the exact sum.
Irrational constants and center eigensolutions likewise lack certified error
bounds here. Clamping estimates to `[0,1]` cannot prove their enclosure property.

One bracketed stationary point does not prove a global kernel supremum or
uniqueness. A generic positive mixture `q(1000 exp(-1000q)+exp(-5q))` has
alternating derivative signs at `.0001,.005,.02,.3`; this refutes a generic
unimodality inference, not the actual supplied kernel's numerical maximum.
That kernel itself lacks a certified ODE/discretization error enclosure.

A positive box-wide claim would require outward-rounded operations, certified
center/constant enclosures, and a proof or exhaustive enclosure of all relevant
kernel extrema. Even that would not establish residual-carrier localization,
completeness outside the two boxes, or a physical selector. These are separate
open obligations.
