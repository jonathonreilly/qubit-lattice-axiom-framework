# Targeted literature comparison

2026-09-21. These are neighboring models and methods, not imported proofs for
the present generator. No novelty claim follows from this limited search.

Candia and Albano's [magnetic Eden study](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0511672)
adds irreversible spins at a cluster boundary, with orientation-sensitive
weights. Its simulations and finite-size analysis investigate ordering and
an equilibrium-Ising correspondence. The introduction and model definition,
along with the reported scope, were inspected; its full numerical evidence was
not independently reproduced. Our construction instead permits spontaneous
insertion at every vacant site, moves existing immutable contents through
vacancies, uses six contents, and fills a fixed graph. An observed ordered
pattern here cannot inherit that model's critical point or universality class.
The comparison makes correlated irreversible growth an established research
context rather than evidence, by itself, of a fundamental force.

Klymko, Garrahan and Whitelam's
[growth-trajectory study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06014)
separates population size from intensive composition in space-independent,
two-content growth models. Its fluctuation-free equations include the
population-growth dilution of composition, and its persistent-growth
attractors can resemble equilibrium mean-field equations. The main text,
model/rate assumptions and normalization equation were inspected; this
campaign does not import its mean-field closure. The present exact initial
derivative keeps dilution too, but the finite graph fills and stops, and its
correlations invalidate a product-law evolution in general. A matching
linear threshold in an independently postulated self-consistency map is thus
not enough to identify the actual stochastic growth law with that map.

The abstract of Mazilu et al.'s
[Cayley-tree cooperative-adsorption work](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2168)
identifies exact configuration/coverage calculations for specified attachment
rates via empty intervals. Its full proof has not been read here. It is a
possible source of methods for a separately scoped tree calculation, not a
current theorem dependency. This limited search did not establish prior art
or novelty for the particular weighted-deletion/Dirichlet startup identities.
