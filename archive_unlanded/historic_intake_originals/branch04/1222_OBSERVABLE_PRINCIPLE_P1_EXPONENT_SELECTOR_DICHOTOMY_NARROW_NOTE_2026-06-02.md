### Exercise 2 — Elon Musk first-principles

Strip to first principles. After #2456, the residual is: among the candidate
generators `{F_p = |det|^p}`, what can possibly single out one `p`?

The decisive first-principles fact is that **`{F_p}` is one orbit of a
one-parameter group**. Concretely, `F_{p'} = (F_p)^{p'/p}`: every member is a
strictly-monotone reparametrization `u ↦ u^{p'/p}` of every other (runner T2).
A strictly-monotone reparametrization is an isomorphism of the **single-sector**
data — it preserves the ordered value set and the multiplicative composition
structure (`(uv)^k = u^k v^k`). So **any condition that depends only on
single-sector data, even up to monotone reparametrization, sees the same object
for every `p`** and cannot distinguish them. This is the abstract reason the
Born/normalized gradient is exponent-blind (#2456 §3.3): it is a single-sector
readout of the expectation field, invariant under the orbit.

What can break the tie? Only a condition that is **not** invariant under the
orbit — one that references the **bare** generator value `W = Φ(r)`, not merely
its monotone class. The composite law `Φ(r_A r_B)` versus the part values
`Φ(r_A), Φ(r_B)` is the only place such a reference can live (independent
sectors are where `p` shows up: cross-block, not within-block). And the **unique
singling** value-referencing composite law is **additivity**
`Φ(r_A r_B) = Φ(r_A) + Φ(r_B)`: it holds for `p → 0` (`log`) and fails for every
`p ≠ 0` (runner T6, and #2456 T4 for the curvature face). By the Cauchy
classifier, additive + measurable `⇒ c · log` — the `p → 0` generator. That is
P1, by definition.

**First-principles bottom line.** The family is one reparametrization orbit;
orbit-invariant selectors are exponent-blind (Born is the instance);
orbit-breaking selectors reference the bare value and the only singling such
selector is additivity = P1. There is **no third option** that pins a finite
nonzero `p` via a non-additive principle. The two natural steelman escapes

## 2. The selector space and the precisely-defined class

A **selector** `σ` is a predicate on candidate generators
`Φ : (ℝ_+, ×) → (ℝ, +)`, `σ(Φ) ∈ {⊤, ⊥}`, with `σ(Φ) = ⊤` read as "this is THE
scalar generator." To **fix the exponent** `σ` must (a) be satisfiable by some
member of `{F_p}` and (b) single out a unique `p` (up to the overall scale
convention `c`).

Classify selectors by what they constrain:

- **Single-sector selectors** constrain only the readout `Φ` on one sector
  (pointwise data of `r ↦ Φ(r)`): positivity, monotonicity, convexity,
  analyticity, normalization `Φ(1) = 0`, the Born/normalized expectation field.
- **Sector-composition selectors** constrain how `Φ` on a composite `A ⊕ B`
  relates to `Φ` on the parts — i.e. a condition
  `C[ Φ(r_A r_B), Φ(r_A), Φ(r_B), r_A, r_B ] = 0` imposed identically. This is
  where the exponent can show up (independent sectors are the only place `p`
  acts nontrivially across blocks).

**The precisely-defined class (scope of this note's theorem).** The
**sector-composition selector class** `𝒞` is the set of sector-composition
selectors that are **statable without pre-encoding a privileged exponent** — i.e.
the defining relation `C` is not allowed to contain, in its own statement, a
factor of the form `r^{p₀}` or `p₀` for a fixed nonzero `p₀` (which would beg the
question by importing the answer). Equivalently: `C` is built from the bare
generator values and the bare sector moduli, with no privileged-power constants.

`𝒞` strictly **contains** #2456's four enumerated exponent-fixing selectors:

- (Add) `Φ(r_A r_B) = Φ(r_A) + Φ(r_B)` — value-referencing composite law;
- (Loc) `∂²W/∂j_a ∂j_b = 0` — the `K = 0` uniform cross-block condition;
- (Pot) `∂W/∂j_a` independent of `J_B` — equivalent to (Loc) by #2456 §3.1;
- (cumulant) `W = log Z` — the (Add) face (identification content is additivity);

and it **contains** the two steelman escapes #2456 §4.4 N7 flagged but did not
resolve:

- *extensivity at a nonlinear gauge* `h(W_comp) = h(W_A) + h(W_B)` — a
  value-referencing composite law with `h` a bare monotone gauge (no privileged
  power);
- *prescribing a uniform cross-block second derivative* `∂²W/∂j_a ∂j_b = K` —
  admitted into `𝒞` only when `K` is statable without a privileged power; §3.5
  shows the only such `K` is `0`.

**What `𝒞` deliberately excludes (the honest boundary).** Predicates that are
**not** sector-composition conditions in the above sense — arbitrary oracle
predicates, or conditions that smuggle a privileged power `r^{p₀}` into their own
statement — are **outside** `𝒞`. The latter are excluded because they are
circular (they assume the exponent to state the condition); the former are
excluded because "all predicates" is not a well-posed object. This is the
precise reason the verdict is `irreducible_against_precise_class`, not the
unscoped reading that would assert irreducibility against every conceivable
selector.

## 3. Load-bearing steps

### 3.0 Lemma (independent composition is the product on `r`)

On the real-`D` block, `det(D + J) > 0`, so `r = det`. For block-diagonal
`D = D_A ⊕ D_B`, `det(D_A ⊕ D_B + J) = det(D_A + J_A) det(D_B + J_B)`, hence
`r(A ⊕ B) = r(A) r(B)`. The value structure of independent composition is the
abelian group `(ℝ_+, ×)`. (Runner T1; #2456 T1.)

### 3.1 Lemma (`{F_p}` is a single reparametrization orbit)

For every `p, p' ≠ 0`, `Φ_{p'} = ψ ∘ Φ_p` with `ψ(u) = u^{p'/p}`:
`(r^p)^{p'/p} = r^{p'}`. For `p'/p > 0`, `ψ` is **strictly increasing**
(`ψ'(u) = (p'/p) u^{p'/p - 1} > 0`) and is an **automorphism of `(ℝ_+, ×)`**
(`(uv)^k = u^k v^k`). Hence `{F_p}` is a single orbit of the one-parameter group
`G = { r ↦ r^k : k > 0 }`, and any two members are related by a strictly-monotone
multiplicative reparametrization of the single-sector value. (Runner T2.)

**Consequence.** A condition that depends only on the single-sector value up to
the action of `G` is constant along the orbit — it cannot distinguish `Φ_p` from
`Φ_{p'}`.

### 3.2 Lemma (the induced self-similar composite law is exponent-blind)

Fix a readout coordinate. In the **power** readout `x = r^p`, independent
composition induces `x_A x_B` (multiplication) for **every** `p`:
`(r_A r_B)^p = r_A^p r_B^p`. In the **log** readout `w = p log r`, it induces
`w_A + w_B` (addition) for **every** `p`:
`p log(r_A r_B) = p log r_A + p log r_B`. Thus the self-similar composite law
(composite value as a fixed function of the part values **alone**) is **the same
for every `p`** — multiplication in the power coordinate, addition in the log
coordinate — and carries **no `p`**. (Runner T3.)

**Consequence.** A selector that sees only the self-similar composite law sees
the same object for every `p`; it is **cross-block-blind** and singles out
nothing among `{F_p}`. This is the abstract content of #2456's Born/normalized
result: the normalized gradient `(1/p) Z^{-p} ∂(Z^p)/∂j = ∂ log Z/∂j` for all
`p` (runner T4) is exactly an orbit-invariant single-sector readout.

### 3.3 Theorem (the dichotomy on `𝒞`)

**Statement.** Every selector `σ ∈ 𝒞` is exactly one of:

- **(BLIND)** invariant under the orbit action `G` (§3.1). By §3.2, `σ` returns
  the same object for every `p` and singles out **nothing** among `{F_p}`.
- **(ADD)** not invariant under `G`. Then `σ` references the bare generator value
  `W = Φ(r)` in its composite law (it distinguishes some `Φ_p` from some
  `Φ_{p'}`, which the orbit relates by a monotone gauge; the only way to break
  that gauge is to read the bare value). The **unique singling** bare-value
  composite law statable in `𝒞` (no privileged power) is bare additivity
  `Φ(r_A r_B) = Φ(r_A) + Φ(r_B)` (§3.5 eliminates the only other candidate, the
  prescribed-curvature law, as either `K = 0` = additivity-equivalent or
  out-of-class). By the Cauchy classifier (§3.4), its unique measurable solution
  is `c · log r` — the `p → 0` generator. So `σ` **is** P1.

**Proof.** A predicate on generators either commutes with the orbit action `G`
or it does not — these are exhaustive and mutually exclusive (a boolean
tautology, runner T9). If it commutes (orbit-invariant), §3.2 gives BLIND. If it
does not, it distinguishes at least two orbit members `Φ_p, Φ_{p'}`; since `G`
relates them by a strictly-monotone multiplicative gauge that preserves all
single-sector and self-similar-composite data (§3.1-§3.2), the only data that
distinguishes them is the **bare** value `Φ(r)` appearing in the composite law.
Within `𝒞` (no privileged power), the candidate bare-value composite laws are:
(α) bare additivity `Φ(r_A r_B) = Φ(r_A) + Φ(r_B)`, and (β) a prescribed uniform
cross-block second derivative. §3.5 shows (β) is either `K = 0` — which is (Loc),
equivalent to (Add) by #2456 §3.1 — or carries a privileged power and is hence

### 3.4 Cauchy/Shannon-Khinchin uniqueness instance (reproven from primitives)

**Statement (used for the ADD face).** An additive map `f : (ℝ_+, ×) → (ℝ, +)`,
`f(rs) = f(r) + f(s)`, that is **measurable** (equivalently monotone, or
continuous at one point) satisfies `f(r) = c · log r` with `c = f(e)`.

**Reproof (runner T7).** Put `g(t) = f(e^t)`; additivity becomes Cauchy on
`(ℝ, +)`: `g(t + u) = g(t) + g(u)`.

- *Integer skeleton (reproven by repeated addition).* `g(n) = n · g(1)` for
  integer `n ≥ 0`, built from `g(x + 1) = g(x) + g(1)`.
- *Rational skeleton (reproven from the additive recursion).* `q` copies of
  `g(p/q)` sum to `g(p) = p · g(1)`, hence `g(p/q) = (p/q) · g(1)`. So `g` is
  `ℚ`-linear in the log-coordinate.
- *Upgrade to `ℝ`-linear (one-point regularity).* A measurable / monotone `g`
  agreeing with the linear map on the **dense** rationals must equal it
  everywhere: the runner reproves the squeeze, bracketing `g` at an irrational
  point between rational bounds (Cauchy 1821 / Aczél 1966; standard). Hence
  `g(t) = g(1) · t`, i.e. `f(r) = g(log r) = c · log r`, `c = f(e)`. ∎

This is the `log`-universality the task names. **Comparator only:** the
literature supplies the uniqueness; the rational skeleton is reproven here. It
does **not** supply additivity — additivity is the hypothesis (the circularity
flag).
