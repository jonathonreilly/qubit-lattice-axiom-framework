# Independent mathematical record, PR8178 original

Domain: integer L>=1, beta>0, t a nonnegative integer, deterministic zero initial field; iid real two-component noises centered with covariance sigma² I, independent between sites and levels. These are supplied hypotheses, not nonlinear-kernel consequences.

Let N=L² and P=(I+T1+T2)/3 with (Tj f)(x)=f(x-ej). With U(k,x)=N^-1/2 exp(-ik.x), substitution y=x-ej gives U Tj f=exp(-ikj) U f. Therefore phi=(1+exp(-ik1)+exp(-ik2))/3. Expanding phi conjugate(phi) gives 9u=3+2cos k1+2cos k2+2cos(k1-k2), hence 1-u=(4/9)[sin²(k1/2)+sin²(k2/2)+sin²((k1-k2)/2)]. Only k=0 has u=1.

The noise has E[xihat(k)conj(xihat(l))]=sigma² delta_kl by finite root-of-unity sums. Real noises satisfy xihat(-k)=conj(xihat(k)); independent Fourier modes is false even for Gaussian noise unless one chooses an appropriate independent real-coordinate half-basis. Temporal independence suffices: unfolding theta(t)=sum_(j=0)^(t-1)P^j xi(t-1-j) gives each mode mean-square sigma² sum_j u^j. The zero mode is sigma²t; other modes sigma²(1-u^t)/(1-u). Inverse unitary transform and diagonal Hermitian covariance give site variance sigma²/N times [t+sum_(k!=0)(1-u^t)/(1-u)]. Direct averaging gives bartheta(t+1)=bartheta(t)+barxi(t), variance sigma²t/N, independently of Fourier reasoning.

Starting from zero the nonzero modes are not stationary: e.g. L2, k=(pi,0), sigma²=1 has variances 0,1,10/9,... . They converge in covariance. A stationary mean-zero field exists on the zero-mean subspace by the L²-convergent backward series sum_(j>=0)P^j Q xi(-1-j), Q removing the constant mode: its squared tail is bounded by sigma² sum_(k!=0)u_k^m/(1-u_k), which tends to zero. Do not assert Gaussian stationary law unless noises are Gaussian.

Define V_L(t)=sigma²/N sum_(k!=0)(1-u^t)/(1-u), V_L its finite limit. For L>=2 let rho=max_(k!=0)u_k<1. Then 0<=V_L-V_L(t)<=rho^t V_L. Thus exp(-v_t)=exp(-t/tau)exp(-V_L(t)) and exp(t/tau)exp(-v_t)->exp(-V_L), tau=N/sigma². This is an auxiliary scalar proxy, not nonlinear magnetization or a geometric angular hitting time. t is integer: tau is a real variance-one scale, with first integer crossing ceil(tau). A single component has RMS1 at tau; a two-component Euclidean norm has RMSsqrt2, not one radian.

For k=(2pi/L,0), u=1-(8/9)sin²(pi/L), so the variance relaxation e-fold is asymptotic 9L²/(8pi²); it is not of order V_L. The residual formula above is the exact replacement for the false transient statement. For L1, no nonzero modes and V_L=0; treat rho separately.

For symmetric representatives |ki|<=pi, |sin(x/2)|<=|x|/2 gives gap<=Q=(k1²+k2²+(k1-k2)²)/9<=|k|²/3. Concavity of sin on [0,pi/2] implies sin(|x|/2)>=|x|/pi; dropping the third squared sine gives gap>=4|k|²/(9pi²). Substitution |k|²=(2pi/L)²|n|² and summation proves 3sigma² S_L/(4pi²)<=V_L<=9sigma² S_L/16 for all finite L. For L1 both sides vanish. The constants are valid; finite sine grids merely corroborate these inequalities.

If logarithmic order is retained, a complete elementary comparison is possible: partition integer points into square shells max(|n1|,|n2|)=r. For full shell there are 8r points and r²<=|n|²<=2r², so shell sums are between4/r and8/r. Symmetric L-box contains shells through floor((L-1)/2) and is contained in shells through floor(L/2); hence S_L=Theta(log L). This proves order only, not coefficient 2pi or the sharper V_L coefficient. Finite L16/32/64 increments do not prove asymptotics; preserving the original sharper asymptotic as deferred is preferable to silently adding a new theorem. A 0.353 empirical offset is not an exact constant or an o(1) theorem.

Kernel control: direct sphere integration gives F(S)=A(beta|S|) S/|S|. If s_i=(epsilon a_i,sqrt(1-epsilon²|a_i|²)), S_perp=epsilon sum a_i and |S|=3+O(epsilon²), so dF_perp/depsilon=A(3beta) sum a_i/3. Normalizing F yields derivative sum a_i/3; this is a different observable. Exact aligned transverse variance A(3beta)/(3beta) is valid for raw Cartesian coordinates. Combining the normalized direction gain with raw one-step variance is a supplied auxiliary choice, not an exact stochastic linearization. Corrected current-main PR8170 explicitly states this distinction.

Nonlinear finite-lag estimator: D(l)=E|uhat(t+l)-uhat(t)|²/(2l) for unit vectors satisfies 0<=D(l)<=2/l. It cannot be a positive lag-independent asymptotic diffusion coefficient. A rotational Brownian model with coordinate correlation exp(-d l) would give D(l)=(1-exp(-d l))/l, approximating d only at short lag; this illustrative calculation is not a derived model for this chain. Stationarity of a distribution of |m| does not make individual |m_t| constant or eliminate radial-direction correlations. The number 1/(E|m|)² is not E[1/|m|²], so even the suggested noise-division explanation needs joint moment and state-dependent covariance control.

Existing data give beta6 lag25 ratios1.344±.014 (L16) and1.416±.005 (L32), difference .072 versus combined reported standard error .014866; not agreement within the quoted errors. Beta12 L32 lag1000 linear control is .960±.015, outside a universal ±3% calibration statement. No new simulations are needed to identify these contradictions. Their finite reported errors are not rigorous mixing or uncertainty certificates.
