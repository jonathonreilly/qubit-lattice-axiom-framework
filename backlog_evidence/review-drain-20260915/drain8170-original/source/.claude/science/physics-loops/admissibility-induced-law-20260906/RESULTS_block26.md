## Result, in one paragraph
The sphere kernel `K_β(·|S)` is the exponential-overlap law with concentration `β|S|`; its mean is `A(κ)û` and its second moment `(A/κ)I + (1 − 3A/κ)ûûᵀ`, `A(κ) = coth κ − 1/κ < κ/3` (T1). Its linearization at the aligned plane has gain one — the new record's transverse coordinate is the average of its three predecessors' — with exact one-step variance `A(3β)/(3β)` per component (T2, block 13's statement re-proved with the exact variance). The three-predecessor level walk's return sums satisfy `3√3/(4πk) − 1/k² − (5/(2k))e^{−k/4} ≤ P_k ≤ 3√3/(4πk) + 27/k² + e^{−√k/7}`, so `kP_k → 3√3/(4π)`, and `Σ_{k≤t}P_k ≥ (3√3/(4π))H_t − 1/4` (T3; the constant by a computer-assisted step, exact sums to `150` plus the tail bound). Every finite periodic plane has a unique, rotation-invariant invariant law, so it forgets its initial plane; on one site `m_t = A(3β)^t` (T4). The linearized field's variance is `σ²Σ_{k<t}P_k = γ(β) log t + O(1)` with `γ(β) = (3√3/(4π))A(3β)/(3β)` (`0.0408, 0.0217, 0.0112, 0.0057` at `β = 3, 6, 12, 24`), and it has no stationary law with finite variance (T5). Executed and not proved: the nonlinear law on periodic planes up to `512 × 512` for `20000` levels loses the memory of its initial plane at every `β` tried, with no plateau, at local exponents above `γ(β)` that approach it as `β` grows; the six-axis formation law at `(e^{β}, e^{−β}, 1)` keeps its plane at the same `β` (dissent `6·10⁻⁴` at `β = 3`, `3·10⁻⁵` at `4`, `0` at `6` over `3000` levels).
