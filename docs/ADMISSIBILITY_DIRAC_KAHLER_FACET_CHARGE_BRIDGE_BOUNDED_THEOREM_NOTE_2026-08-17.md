## 4. The Difference Lattice

Let \(L\) be the subgroup of \(\mathbb Z^2\) generated by every signed
difference among the three points. Equations (2)--(3) give

\[
 L=\mathbb Z\Delta_1+\mathbb Z\Delta_2,
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}5&1\\-7&-7\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \det=-28.                                   \tag{4}
\]

The two columns are independent, so \(L\) has full rank and index 28:

\[
 [\mathbb Z^2:L]=28.                         \tag{5}
\]

Now define the congruence kernel

\[
 K=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2:7x+y\equiv0\pmod {28}\}.       \tag{6}
\]

Both displayed generators lie in \(K\):

\[
 7(5)-7=28,
 \qquad
 7(1)-7=0.                                   \tag{7}
\]

Hence \(L\subseteq K\). Conversely, the homomorphism

\[
 \phi:\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow\mathbb Z/28\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 \phi(x,y)=7x+y\pmod {28}                    \tag{8}
\]

is surjective because \(\phi(0,1)=1\). Therefore its kernel \(K\) also has
index 28. The inclusion and equal finite indices force the both-ways
equality

\[
 \boxed{L=K
 =\{(x,y)\in\mathbb Z^2:7x+y\equiv0\pmod {28}\}}.       \tag{9}
\]

This is not merely a necessary congruence. Equations (4)--(9) prove that
every integer pair obeying the congruence is generated by the signed
differences, and every generated pair obeys the congruence.

The lattice is sign-indefinite in both components. A minimal paired witness
is

\[
 (1,-7)=\Delta_2\in L,
 \qquad
 (-1,7)=-\Delta_2\in L.                     \tag{10}
\]

Thus the first coordinate takes both signs and the second coordinate takes
both signs. Coordinate-isolated witnesses are also explicit:

\[
 (4,0)=\Delta_1-\Delta_2,
 \qquad
 (0,-28)=4\Delta_2-(\Delta_1-\Delta_2),      \tag{11}
\]

together with their negatives. These are lattice witnesses. Nothing in the
lattice construction says that every witness is a displayed directed move.

## 5. The No-Conserved-Total Theorem

Consider an affine facet total

\[
 F(TC,MC)=a\,TC+b\,MC+c.                     \tag{12}
\]

Conservation across the three points requires
\(F(P_1)=F(P_0)\) and \(F(P_2)=F(P_0)\). Subtracting the common offset gives

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}5&-7\\1&-7\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\end{pmatrix}.        \tag{13}
\]

The determinant is

\[
 5(-7)-1(-7)=-28\ne0.                        \tag{14}
\]

Consequently \(a=b=0\). The constant \(c\) remains, but it is a trivial
offset rather than a conserved charge combination. Therefore

\[
 \boxed{\text{no nontrivial conserved affine facet total exists on (1).}}
                                                        \tag{15}
\]

The same determinant proves noncollinearity: the two displacement vectors
from \(P_0\) span \(\mathbb R^2\). There is no affine line containing all
three points and no nonzero linear functional constant on them.

This is the exact parallel of the TT lane's Record-charge finding. In both
lanes a displayed noncollinear charge record defeats a proposed conserved
affine total by a two-by-two solve. The parallel is at the level of the
finite affine theorem. It does not identify the two charge pairs, construct
a shared differential, or show that cell-cutting charges source the gravity
constraint.

## 6. The Cone-Lattice Split

The displayed directed data generate the real cone

\[
 C=\mathbb R_{\ge0}\Delta_1+
   \mathbb R_{\ge0}\Delta_2.                 \tag{16}
\]

For \(\alpha,\beta\ge0\),

\[
 \alpha\Delta_1+\beta\Delta_2
 =(5\alpha+\beta,-7\alpha-7\beta).          \tag{17}
\]

If \((\alpha,\beta)\ne(0,0)\), the first component in (17) is strictly
positive and the second is strictly negative. Hence

\[
 C\setminus\{0\}\subset
 \{(x,y):x>0,\ y<0\},
 \qquad
 C\cap(-C)=\{0\}.                            \tag{18}
\]

The directed displayed move cone is therefore pointed and one-sided.

The signed difference lattice \(L\) answers a different question. Integer
span automatically supplies \(-v\) whenever it supplies \(v\). It is
negation-closed and sign-indefinite by (10), even though the displayed cone
contains neither \(-\Delta_1\) nor \(-\Delta_2\). Replacing \(C\) by \(L\)
as the achieved move set would import reversibility that the record does not
contain.

This cone-lattice distinction is the checker's correction and is credited
as such. The arithmetic lattice is exact; the physical reading of every
signed lattice vector as an available move is not.

## 7. The Dead Echo

There is a tempting numerical echo: the cell-cutting frame covering has an
order-four label, while the transfer lane uses a \(\mathbb Z_4\) momentum
structure. The displayed record excludes a naive identification for two
independent reasons.

The frame covering acts on eight points with cycle type

\[
 (2,2,2,2)=2^4,                              \tag{19}
\]

whereas the momentum generator acts on four labels with cycle type
\((4)=4^1\).

First, eight carrier points cannot be put in bijection with four momentum
labels. The action domains have different cardinalities. Second, a generator
of cycle type \(2^4\) has order two, whereas a generator of cycle type \(4^1\)
has order four. Conjugate actions must preserve generator order, so these
actions cannot be identified by relabeling.

The exclusions are independent: even ignoring generator order, cardinality
blocks a bijection; even after changing the carrier presentation, generator
order blocks conjugacy of the displayed actions. They kill only the naive
echo. A separately constructed quotient, many-to-one map, or nonconjugate
intertwiner is not ruled out; none is supplied by the displayed record.
