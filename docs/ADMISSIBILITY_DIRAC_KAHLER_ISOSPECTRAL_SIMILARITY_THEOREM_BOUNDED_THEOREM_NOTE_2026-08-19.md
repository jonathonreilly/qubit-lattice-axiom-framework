## 4. The Antiperiodic Half-Period Operator

On the eight-site time carrier, define the antiperiodic one-slice shift by

\[
 T_{\rm AP}e_t=

 \end{cases}                                          \tag{23}
\]

The minus sign on the wrap gives

\[
 T_{\rm AP}^{,8}=-I_8.                               \tag{24}
\]

Its half-period power acts by

\[
 Ue_t=
 \begin{cases}
 e_{t+4},&0\leq t<4,\\
 -e_{t-4},&4\leq t<8,
 \end{cases}                                          \tag{25}
\]

so, in the first-half/second-half decomposition of Z8 time,

\[
 \boxed{
 U=T_{\rm AP}^{,4}
 =\begin{pmatrix}
 0&-I_4\\
 I_4&0
 \end{pmatrix}.}                                      \tag{26}
\]

This form forces all of the operator identities used by the theorem:

\[
 U^2=
 \begin{pmatrix}-I_4&0\\0&-I_4\end{pmatrix}=-I_8,
 \qquad
 U^T=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_4\\-I_4&0\end{pmatrix}=-U,  \tag{27}
\]

and hence

\[
 \boxed{U^{-1}=U^T=-U},\qquad
 \boxed{U^TU=UU^T=I_8}.                              \tag{28}
\]

The checker verifies these statements entrywise. In particular, the
antiperiodic full-period sign is not discarded: it is exactly why the
half-period shift squares to \(-I_8\), and it is compatible with, rather

\[
 D_NS_ND_N=-S_N,

Thus \(D_N\) maps the \(k=0\) carrier to the \(k=N/2\) carrier. The

## 6. The Transfer-Monodromy Consequence

Similarity of the action blocks immediately proves (39), but the transfer
construction requires a separate argument. Its companion matrices consume
specific slice blocks of \(B_\epsilon\); arbitrary similarity can mix those
slices and need not induce a similarity of the resulting monodromy. Thus
the invalid shortcut

\[
 B_+\sim B_-
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \mathcal M_+\sim\mathcal M_-
 \quad\text{for an arbitrary intertwiner}              \tag{40}
\]

is not used.

The actual intertwiner has the extra structure needed. The eight time
slices are grouped by the committed companion construction into four
cyclic two-slice super-slices. Equation (25) sends super-slice \(n\) to
super-slice \(n+2\) modulo four. Consequently it carries the
block-tridiagonal slice pattern to itself with a shifted cyclic index.

If \(F_0,F_1,F_2,F_3\) denote the exact ordered companion factors for one
real-character block, the corresponding factors after conjugation are the
same four factors with their starting point shifted by two. Hence

\[
 \mathcal M_-
 =\operatorname{cyc}_2(\mathcal M_+),                 \tag{41}
\]

where `cyc` denotes a cyclic rotation of the same product, not a
reordering of its factors by commutativity. A cyclic rotation of an
invertible factor product is conjugate to the original product. Therefore
there is an invertible exact matrix \(G\) such that

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal M_-=G\mathcal M_+G^{-1}}.             \tag{42}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{tr}\mathcal M_-
  =\operatorname{tr}\mathcal M_+,
 \qquad
 \det\mathcal M_-=
  \det\mathcal M_+.}                                  \tag{43}
\]

These are exactly the transfer invariants entering Block 138's
real-character degeneracy rider. Thus, throughout the displayed family,

\[
 \boxed{\rho_0(c)=\rho_{N/2}(c)}
 \qquad(N\geq4\text{ even},\ c^2\ne1).                \tag{44}
\]

The route from (38) to (44) is therefore not “similar matrices have the
same characteristic polynomial, so every downstream construction agrees.”
It is the narrower exact statement that this similarity is a cyclic time
translation compatible with the companion slice structure, so the
monodromy factor product changes only by cyclic rotation.
