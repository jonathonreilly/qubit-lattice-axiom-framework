The quantum-gravity lens rode a question on the gate: do the connection residues
`K_e` close into a constraint algebra? If they did, the Dirac reading would be
available — the connection would be pure gauge and its invisibility on the live
sector would be the expected thing rather than a fact needing explanation.

Measured at both admissible witnesses, identically:

| quantity | cone witness (`tau = 118/9`) | TYPE B witness (`tau = -11`) |

| **distinct** residue matrices | 12 | 12 |
| `dim span(K_e)` | **3** | **3** |

The four commuting pairs are exactly `((t,0), (t,1))`, `t = 0..3`, and they are
**degenerate, not physics**: those two edges carry the *identical* residue,
because the committed healing weights repeat a leading zero. Zero pairs of
distinct residues commute.

**The consequence, stated carefully.** The residues are **neither first-class**
— nothing closes; the bracket leaves `span(K_e)` immediately and first-order
closure already doubles the generators — **nor inert**: the generated algebra is
an order of magnitude larger than the generating set. Both readings are
excluded, so **the Dirac pure-gauge reading of the connection's invisibility is
not supported**. The confinement of the connection to the dead-live sector
coupling stands as a **selection-rule fact with no constraint-algebra
interpretation behind it**.

Scope, and a defect: this fixture, these 16 residues, a three-dimensional
generating set, and a generated-algebra dimension that is itself
**carrier-dependent** — 30 against 56. A "generated constraint algebra" whose
dimension depends on which admissible point you sit at is not a structural
invariant, and that is why the finding is recorded as a selection rule.
