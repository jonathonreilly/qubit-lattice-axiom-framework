## 3. The Window Identity

Let \(Y=Y^\dagger\) be any three-time Hermitian corner, ordered by the
consecutive positive times \(0,1,2\). Write its two overlapping windows as

\[
 W_Y^{(0)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  Y_{00}&Y_{01}\\
  \overline{Y_{01}}&Y_{11}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 W_Y^{(1)}=
 \begin{pmatrix}
  Y_{11}&Y_{12}\\
  \overline{Y_{12}}&Y_{22}
 \end{pmatrix}.                                       \tag{4}
\]

This is exactly the Block 115 window convention, before its harmless
primitive-integer normalization. Define the symbolic pencil

\[
 f_Y(z):=\det\!\left(W_Y^{(1)}-zW_Y^{(0)}\right).
                                                               \tag{5}
\]

At \(z=1\), direct two-by-two expansion gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
 f_Y(1)
 &=\det
 \begin{pmatrix}
  Y_{11}-Y_{00}&Y_{12}-Y_{01}\\
  \overline{Y_{12}-Y_{01}}&Y_{22}-Y_{11}
 \end{pmatrix}\\
 &=(Y_{11}-Y_{00})(Y_{22}-Y_{11})
   -\lvert Y_{12}-Y_{01}\rvert^2\\
 &=(Y_{00}-Y_{11})(Y_{11}-Y_{22})
   -\lvert Y_{01}-Y_{12}\rvert^2.                     \tag{6}
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a symbolic lemma. It uses only Hermiticity and the overlap pattern;
it does not use positivity, an eigensolver, a branch sample, or a search.
It therefore applies before any restriction to a positive part of a self
chart.

Expanding (5) as

\[
 f_Y(z)=a_Yz^2+b_Yz+d_Y                              \tag{7}
\]

also identifies the endpoint coefficients exactly:

\[
 a_Y=\det W_Y^{(0)},
 \qquad d_Y=\det W_Y^{(1)}.                            \tag{8}
\]

The primitive convention in Block 115 clears rational denominators and
divides common integer content by a positive number. It therefore rescales
(5) by a positive constant only. The roots, the sign at one, and every
strict-straddling conclusion below are unchanged.

## 4. The Equal-Middle-Diagonal Collapse

Index a displayed self-block branch by \(\beta\), its real branch parameter
by \(u\), its self-conjugate momentum sector by \(k\), and its fixture by

\[
 c\in\left\{{5\over13},{3\over5}\right\}.
\]

Let \(Y_{k,c,\beta}(u)\) be the corresponding displayed three-time Gram
corner. Exact substitution of the involution branches gives the polynomial
identity

\[
 Y_{11;k,c,\beta}(u)=Y_{22;k,c,\beta}(u)
 \quad\hbox{identically in }u.                         \tag{9}
\]

This equality is not a coincidence at the Block 114 witness or at the
Block 116 plateau point. It holds on the entire displayed algebraic branch,
including parameter values for which the Gram is not positive.

Substituting (9) into the window identity (6) kills its diagonal product:

\[
 f_{k,c,\beta,u}(1)
 =-\left\lvert
   Y_{01;k,c,\beta}(u)-Y_{12;k,c,\beta}(u)
  \right\rvert^2.                                     \tag{10}
\]

The branch formulas expose the cancellation more directly. There are exact
functions \(L_{k,c,\beta}(u)\) and exact constants
\(r_{k,c,\beta},s_{k,c,\beta}\) such that

\[
\begin{aligned}
 Y_{01;k,c,\beta}(u)&=L_{k,c,\beta}(u)+r_{k,c,\beta},\\
 Y_{12;k,c,\beta}(u)&=L_{k,c,\beta}(u)+s_{k,c,\beta}.
                                                               \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
 Y_{01;k,c,\beta}(u)-Y_{12;k,c,\beta}(u)
 =q_{k,c,\beta}
 :=r_{k,c,\beta}-s_{k,c,\beta},                       \tag{12}
\]

where the displayed exact branch literals give
\(q_{k,c,\beta}\ne0\). The entire \(u\)-dependent part cancels exactly.
Equations (10)--(12) prove a parameter identity, not a finite list of
evaluations:

\[
 f_{k,c,\beta,u}(1)=-\lvert q_{k,c,\beta}\rvert^2<0
 \quad\hbox{for every displayed parameter }u.          \tag{13}
\]

In particular, (13) remains true at non-positive branch points. Positivity
is needed only to interpret the pencil as a positive generalized transfer
pair and to place its roots on the positive real axis; it is not needed for
the negative-square identity itself.

## 6. The Family Closures

The displayed involution solve also has two alternative algebraic families.
Neither supplies a positive point hidden from the negative-square wall.

**Opposite-sign family.** The opposite-sign Gram is the exact negative of
the main-branch Gram, so its leading principal minors obey the parity

\[
 \Delta_j\!\left(K^{\mathrm{opp}}_{k,c,\beta}(u)\right)
 =(-1)^j\,\Delta_j\!\left(K_{k,c,\beta}(u)\right),
 \qquad j=1,\dots,4,                                   \tag{21}
\]

and its displayed two-by-two windows satisfy
\(\det(-W)=\det W\), so the one-step pencil (5) is invariant under the
overall sign reversal. Two exact consequences close the family. First,
wherever the main branch is positive — including the displayed witness,
where the opposite profile is \((-,+,-,+)\) — equation (21) makes the
opposite first Sylvester minor negative, so the two families are never
positive at the same parameter point. Second, the pencil invariance
transports the negative square (13) verbatim: at any positive point of the
opposite family its window determinants are again the positive Sylvester
data of that point, so the strict straddling (19) holds there unchanged.
The opposite-sign family therefore contains no transfer-contractive
positive point, displayed or not.

**Reversed triangular family.** After exact branch substitution, clear the
declared nonzero rational denominators in the Gram determinant and write

\[
 \det K^{\mathrm{rev}}_{k,c,\beta}(u)
 ={P_{k,c,\beta}(u)\over D_{k,c,\beta}(u)},
 \qquad D_{k,c,\beta}(u)\ne0.                          \tag{22}
\]

The symbolic branch degrees give an exact finite bound

\[
 \deg P_{k,c,\beta}\le d_{k,c,\beta}.                  \tag{23}
\]

The exact certificate evaluates \(P\) to zero at
\(d_{k,c,\beta}+1\) distinct rational parameter values. A polynomial of
degree at most \(d_{k,c,\beta}\) with that many distinct roots is the zero
polynomial. Hence

\[
 P_{k,c,\beta}(u)\equiv0,
 \qquad
 \det K^{\mathrm{rev}}_{k,c,\beta}(u)\equiv0.           \tag{24}
\]

The reversed triangular family is Gram-singular identically, including at
parameters outside any proposed positive interval. Positive definiteness is
therefore impossible on that displayed family.

## 7. The Stationarity Diagnosis

For a stationary three-time pairing, the Gram kernel depends only on time
difference. On the displayed indices this is the Toeplitz condition

\[
 Y_{r+1,s+1}=Y_{rs}
 \quad(0\le r,s\le1),                                  \tag{25}
\]

which includes

\[
 Y_{00}=Y_{11}=Y_{22},
 \qquad Y_{01}=Y_{12}.                                 \tag{26}
\]

Equivalently, the two overlapping windows in (4) are exact translates and
have the same matrix entries. In particular, window stationarity kills the
obstruction term:

\[
 \lvert Y_{01}-Y_{12}\rvert^2=0,
 \qquad f_Y(1)=0.                                      \tag{27}
\]

On the displayed self branches, (9) is already present. The missing
Toeplitz equality is exactly \(Y_{01}=Y_{12}\), and equations (12)--(15)
prove that it fails by a fixed nonzero amount. Thus, within these displayed
sectors, contractivity requires the off-diagonal window-stationarity
condition. Indeed, if both positive generalized roots were at most one,
then

\[
 f_Y(1)=a_Y(1-\lambda^-)(1-\lambda^+)\ge0.             \tag{28}
\]

The negative-square collapse makes (28) possible only if
\(Y_{01}=Y_{12}\). Full Toeplitz stationarity supplies that necessary
equality together with the diagonal identities in (26). The obstruction is
the pairing's non-stationarity, not an optimization defect in the branch
parameter.

An action-derived stationary pairing supplies (25) by construction when
the action, state, reflection, and time-translation map are derived
compatibly: translating both arguments by one slice leaves the pairing
unchanged, so its two-point kernel is Toeplitz. A modular construction is
the live mechanism for making that statement intrinsic to the state
algebra, rather than imposing a relative window normalization after the
fact.

That explanation is a construction target, not a construction performed in
this block. In particular, (27) is a necessary escape from the displayed
negative square; it is not by itself a proof of OS positivity, transfer
contractivity for a newly built pairing, semigroup composition, or curved
reconstruction.

Block 118 must build and certify the object in the correct order:

1. derive the finite-carrier action pairing and its time-translation map;
2. formulate the stationary or modular state-algebra pairing without an
   inserted relative window normalization;
3. prove its Hermiticity and positivity on the declared carrier;
4. derive the Toeplitz window identities from the action and state; and
5. re-run the exact transfer pencil and composition tests on that pairing.

Only after those steps may the lane approach the gravity constraint
