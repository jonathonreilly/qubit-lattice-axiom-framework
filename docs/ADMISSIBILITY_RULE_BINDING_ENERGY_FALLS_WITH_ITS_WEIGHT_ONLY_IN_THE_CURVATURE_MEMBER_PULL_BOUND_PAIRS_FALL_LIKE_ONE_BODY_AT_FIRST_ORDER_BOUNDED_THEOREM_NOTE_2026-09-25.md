## Result up front

Within the expressly supplied continuum charge equations, self-field subtraction, trace-free field-momentum constraint and no-free-wave choice: the static ledger has the stated second-order expansion and the selected moving extension gives coefficient 1/2+1/p, for p nonzero. The resulting truncated three-body Hamiltonian gives passive mass M+2a<T>+2s2<U> in the far-field, weak-binding limit. For its p=1 coefficients this equals the inertial energy through that order. This conditional calculation does not derive a nonlinear dynamical completion from the landed static or quadratic source notes.


Block 144 (open) found that a pair bound by the member's pull carries its energy as inertia at first order: its weight `W = E₀ ∂²E/∂P²` is one along every axis. A bound pair has less energy than its parts. Does it also fall as if it weighed that much less? The calculation below supplies a point-source continuation of block 60's static charges and combines it with a selected momentum constraint. Compatibility as a full nonlinear dynamical theory is an additional open obligation.

- **T1: the static ledger to second order.** For bodies at rest the ledger is `M − Σ_{a<b} GM_aM_b/r_ab + (G²/2)Σ_a M_a(Σ_{b≠a} M_b/r_ab)²`, with `G = 1/(2πc)`. This holds for every bilinear member `cXΔX` of block 60's family, and `G = 1/(16πK)` for the curvature member, the value block 144 found.
- **T2: moving bodies.**
  - For the member with `X = ℓ^{p/2}`, the first-order ledger is `−(Gm₁m₂/r)[1 + (½ + 1/p)(k₁²/m₁² + k₂²/m₂²)]`. The ½ is the weight of the bodies' own energy of motion. The `1/p` is the stretching of the bonds they hop across.
  - For the curvature member (`p = 1`), the field momentum at the shift's constraint adds `(G/2r)[7k₁·k₂ + (n·k₁)(n·k₂)]`.
  - So the curvature member's first-order Hamiltonian is block 144's pull changed to momenta, plus T1's static term. The two routes, the exchange and the ledger with the field momentum, give the same velocity terms.
- **T3: the fall.**
  - A bound pair far from a third body couples to that body's potential with passive mass `M + 2a⟨T⟩ + 2s₂⟨U⟩`. Here `a` is the first-order kinetic coefficient and `s₂` the static second-order one.
  - This differs from the pair's energy `M + ⟨T⟩ + ⟨U⟩` by `(2s₂ − a − ½)⟨U⟩`.
  - For the curvature member, `a = 3/2` and `s₂ = 1`, so the passive mass is the pair's energy. Its inertia is also its energy (block 144, re-checked here). So the pair falls like one body, at first order in its binding.
  - For any other bilinear member the passive mass differs from the energy by `(1 − 1/p)⟨U⟩`.

In plain terms: a bound pair weighs a little less than its parts. For it to fall like a single body of that reduced energy, two corrections must cancel. The parts' energy of motion weighs extra, through the bonds the member stretches. The pull's own energy weighs through the member's second-order terms. The static second order is the same for every member in block 60's family; the stretching is not. Only for the curvature member, the one whose lengths are the square of its field, do the two cancel exactly. Then a pair bound by the member falls exactly as a single body with the pair's energy, to first order in how tightly it is bound.

## Theorem T1 — the static ledger to second order

*Statement.* For bodies at rest, with `G = 1/(2πc)`, the ledger is

`M − Σ_{a<b} GM_aM_b/r_ab + (G²/2)Σ_a M_a(Σ_{b≠a} M_b/r_ab)²`,

up to third order. This holds for every member of the supplied charge model with the same positive c and self-field prescription. For the curvature member, `G = 1/(16πK)`.

*Proof.* Solve `Q_i(1 + Σ_{j≠i}Q_j g_ij) = M_i/c` order by order and sum `cΣQ_i` (runner B1, three bodies). `c = 8K` gives `1/(16πK)` (runner B2). ∎

The same second-order term appears whatever `p` is, because the static constraint involves only `X`.

## Theorem T2 — moving bodies, and the curvature member's first-order Hamiltonian

*Statement.*
- (a) For the member with `X = ℓ^{p/2}`, the ledger of two moving bodies is `Σ_a(m_a + k_a²/(2m_a) − k_a⁴/(8m_a³)) − (Gm₁m₂/r)[1 + (½ + 1/p)(k₁²/m₁² + k₂²/m₂²)]` at first order.
- (b) For the curvature member, the shift's constraint with `tr Π = 0` and no free waves has the solution `Π̂ = i(pV + Vp − (2/3)δ(p·V))`, with `V = [P/2 − p(p·P)/(8p²)]/p²`. The field's energy between two bodies is `(G/2r)[7k₁·k₂ + (n·k₁)(n·k₂)]`.
- (c) Hence the curvature member's first-order Hamiltonian is `Σ(m + k²/2m − k⁴/8m³) − Σ_{a<b}(Gm_am_b/r)[1 + (3/2)(k_a²/m_a² + k_b²/m_b²) − (7/2)k_a·k_b/(m_am_b) − ½(n·k_a)(n·k_b)/(m_am_b)] + (G²/2)Σ_a m_a(Σ_b m_b/r_ab)²`. Its momentum terms are minus block 144's velocity terms at `v = k/m`.

*Proof.*
- (a) Solve the charges with `Q_i X(x_i) = √(m_i² + k_i²/ℓ_i²)/c` to first order, with `ℓ^{−2} = X^{−4/p} = 1 − (4/p)(X − 1)` at the order kept (runner C1).
- (b) The constraint, the trace and the pairing are exact rational identities (runner C2). `∂_i∂_j r = (δ_ij − n_in_j)/r`, so `p_ip_j/p⁴` is `(δ − nn)/(8πr)` (runner C3).
- (c) Add (a) at `p = 1`, (b) and T1. Compare with block 144's pull (runner C4). ∎

## Theorem T3 — the fall

*Statement.* Take a first-order Hamiltonian of the form in T2(c), with general coefficients `a`, `b`, `c` for the velocity terms and `s₂` for the static term. Take a bound pair (bodies 1 and 2) and a third body at rest far away, with potential `Φ = −Gm₃/R` per unit mass. Write `M = m₁ + m₂`.
- (a) The Hamiltonian's coupling to the third body is `Σ_{a=1,2} Φ(x_a)[m_a + 2aT_a + s₂U] + s₂(G²m₃/2)(m₁/r₁₃ + m₂/r₂₃)²`. For fixed positive masses, the last term is suppressed relative to the binding-dependent external coupling as `r₁₂/R` tends to zero; its ratio also contains mass ratios, so this is not uniform in an extreme mass ratio. So, for a small pair, `H ⊃ Φ(X)[M + 2aT + 2s₂U]`.
- (b) In a bound state, `2⟨T⟩ = −⟨U⟩`. So the passive mass `M + 2a⟨T⟩ + 2s₂⟨U⟩` differs from the pair's energy `M + ⟨T⟩ + ⟨U⟩` by `(2s₂ − a − ½)⟨U⟩`.
- (c) For the curvature member (`a = 3/2`, `s₂ = 1`) the passive mass is the pair's energy. Its inertia is also its energy: the same Hamiltonian's `P²` coefficient with the tensor virial gives `W = 1` (block 144 T4, re-checked). So the pair falls in the third body's pull like one body, at first order in its binding and in `Φ`.
- (d) For any other bilinear member (`a = ½ + 1/p`, `s₂ = 1`) the passive mass differs from the energy by `(1 − 1/p)⟨U⟩`, which is nonzero for `p ≠ 1`.

*Proof.*
- (a) Differentiate the three-body Hamiltonian in `m₃` at `m₃ = 0`, with the third body at rest (runner D1).
- (b) `{x·q, H₀} = 2T + U` (runner D2); its average vanishes in a bound state. Then the difference is algebra (runner D3).
- (c) Runner E1 and E2. The force on the pair's centre is `−∇Φ` times the passive mass, and its inertia is `E₀`.
- (d) T2(a) and T1, by substitution (runner D3). ∎

For `p ≠ 1` the pair's inertia is not treated, because no kinetic term for those members is landed. The statement there concerns the passive mass only.


## Finite-cluster extension under the same hypotheses

The pair identities extend to any fixed finite number of constituents with positive masses in the supplied pairwise Hamiltonian, with the same weak-binding, far-field, time-average/virial and differentiable energy-branch assumptions. This is not a uniform large-cluster limit. Put `M=sum m_a`, `U=sum_(a<b) U_ab`, `U_ab=-Gm_am_b/r_ab`, and `U_e=sum U_ab(n_ab.e)^2` for a unit direction e. The far-field coupling is `Phi[M+2aT+2s2U]`: differentiating the static second-order term attaches each internal pair to each of its two endpoints. External terms of order 1/R² remain excluded only at fixed mass ratios and cluster size.

For momenta `p_a=m_a P/M+q_a`, `sum q_a=0`, direct expansion of the kinetic fourth powers and pairwise velocity terms gives the quadratic correction `P²/(2M²)[-T-2T_e+2(2a+b)U+2cU_e]`. This follows term by term: each kinetic fourth power contributes its own q_a² and (e.q_a)², and each pair contributes the displayed U_ab coefficients. The scalar and tensor virial identities are `2<T>=-<U>` and `2<T_e>=-<U_e>`, obtained by differentiating the bounded averaged dilation observables; each pair has degree -1 and `(e.r) e.grad U_ab=-U_ab(n_ab.e)^2`. At `(a,b,c,s2)=(3/2,-7/2,-1/2,1)` the passive coefficient equals `M+<T>+<U>`, and the quadratic energy is `P²/(2E0)` through first binding order. Thus both response coefficients agree in this finite supplied model. Primary D4/E3 check a three-constituent cluster; the general finite statement uses the termwise proof just given, not extrapolation from that case.
