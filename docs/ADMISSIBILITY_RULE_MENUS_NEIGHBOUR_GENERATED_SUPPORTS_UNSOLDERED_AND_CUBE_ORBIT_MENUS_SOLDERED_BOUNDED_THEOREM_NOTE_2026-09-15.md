# Menus: under the unsoldered reading a finite record alphabet must be generated by the recorded neighbours (one neighbour gives the antipodal pair, a random seed gives the axis); under the soldered reading it is a union of the cube's orbits, of which the six axes are the smallest

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Type:** bounded_theorem
**Status:** bounded-support (exact; conditional on the named supplied readings; unaudited)

## Result up front

The Qubit axiom says no possibility is privileged, and the campaign reads
that sentence two ways: either the internal rotations of a site's
possibilities are tied to the lattice's rotations (soldered), or they act on
their own (unsoldered). This note asks what the alphabet of records can be
under each reading — which finite sets of directions a record may take,
given what its recorded neighbours say. Unsoldered, the answer is that no
fixed finite alphabet exists at all: a record with no recorded neighbour is
uniformly random on the sphere, a record with one recorded neighbour can
only copy it or oppose it, and a record with two non-collinear recorded
neighbours can sit anywhere in a frame built from them. So the alphabet is
made as the records form, from a random seed. Soldered, the alphabet is a
supplied choice, but among a short list: the cube's rotations partition the
sphere into orbits of size six, eight, twelve and twenty-four, and a covariant
finite alphabet is a union of such orbits; the six axes that blocks 01–17 use
are the smallest nontrivial choice. One consequence for the Born question:
on the smallest neighbour-generated alphabet the Born overlap is not a
probability at all but a deterministic copy, so Born statistics need a
richer alphabet.

Exactly: finite `SO(2)_q`-invariant sets lie in `{q, −q}` (M1);
`S(q_1, q_2) = F(q_1, q_2) S_0(q_1·q_2)` (M2); orbit sizes `6, 8, 12, 24` with
stabilizers of orders `4, 3, 2, 1` (M3); the alphabet `{s_0, −s_0}` on
one-neighbour windows (M4); `(1 + s·q)/2 ∈ {1, 0}` on `{q, −q}` (M5). Executed
with exact arithmetic: 15 checks, 8 mutations.

## Theorem M1 — one recorded neighbour: the antipodal pair

**Statement.** Under the unsoldered reading, let the recorded neighbours all
carry the values `±q`. The support of the conditional is invariant under
every rotation about the axis `q`. A finite set invariant under those
rotations is contained in `{q, −q}`. Hence a finite support with one recorded
neighbour (or with recorded values all `±q`) is `{q}`, `{−q}` or `{q, −q}`. With
no recorded neighbour the invariant law is uniform on `S²` (the landed note).

*Proof.* A rotation `g` about `q` fixes every recorded value, so covariance
gives `r(g s | ·) = r(s | ·)` and the support is `g`-invariant. A point `s` with
`s ≠ ±q` has nonzero component orthogonal to `q`, and its images under the
rotations about `q` by the angles `θ ∈ [0, 2π)` are pairwise distinct (the
orthogonal component rotates through a full circle), so any invariant set
containing `s` is infinite. ∎ Executed: the rational rotation about `z` with
cosine `3/5` and sine `4/5` moves the point `(3/5, 0, 4/5)` through twelve
distinct positions while fixing the pole (B1).

## Theorem M2 — two non-collinear values: frame-attached supports

**Statement.** Under the unsoldered reading, let two recorded values `q_1, q_2`
be non-collinear. The stabilizer of `(q_1, q_2)` in `SO(3)` is trivial, and the
covariant supports are exactly the assignments
`S(q_1, q_2) = F(q_1, q_2) · S_0(q_1·q_2)`, `S_0(t) ⊂ S²` an arbitrary subset for
each angle `t ∈ (−1, 1)` (and likewise for more recorded values in general
position, with the frame of any two of them). In particular no
site-independent finite menu is covariant under the unsoldered reading.

*Proof.* A rotation fixing two non-collinear vectors is the identity.
Covariance means `S(g q_1, g q_2) = g S(q_1, q_2)`; every pair with the same
inner product is `g`-related to a reference pair with the same frame up to
`g`, so `S` is determined by its value on one reference pair per angle, and
that value can be any subset; conversely every frame-attached assignment is
covariant since `F` is equivariant. A site-independent finite menu would have
to be invariant under all of `SO(3)` (take the empty neighbourhood), which
no finite nonempty subset of `S²` is. ∎ Executed: `F(g q_1, g q_2) = g F(q_1, q_2)`
for a rational rotation (B2).

## Theorem M3 — soldered: the cube's orbits

**Statement.** Under the soldered reading a site-independent finite menu is
covariant iff it is a union of orbits of `O` on `S²`. The stabilizer of a
point is nontrivial iff the point lies on a rotation axis of the cube; the
orbits are: the six axis points (stabilizer order `4`), the eight cube
diagonals (order `3`), the twelve edge directions (order `2`), and orbits of
`24` points (trivial stabilizer). The six-axis menu is the unique smallest
nontrivial covariant menu.

*Proof.* Covariance of a site-independent menu under the joint action
reduces, at the empty neighbourhood, to invariance under `O` acting on
values. A nontrivial rotation of `R³` fixes exactly the two unit vectors on
its axis; `O` has three four-fold axes (through the face centres, the `±e_i`),
four three-fold axes (through the vertices, the `(±1,±1,±1)/√3`) and six
two-fold axes (through the edge midpoints, the `(±1,±1,0)/√2` and
permutations); their unit vectors number `6 + 8 + 12 = 26`, every other point
has trivial stabilizer, and the orbit sizes are `24` divided by the
stabilizer orders. A union of orbits is invariant; a nontrivial menu contains
an orbit of size at least `6`, with equality only for the axes. ∎ Executed:
the 24 proper signed permutations, the orbits and stabilizers of the five
sample directions (C1); invariance of the six-axis, eight-diagonal and
twelve-edge sets and non-invariance of a mixed set (C2).

## Theorem M4 — the seed-generated alphabet

**Statement.** Under the unsoldered reading with finite supports, on any
window and order in which every record after the first sees exactly one
recorded neighbour (an isolated tree under seeded growth; the ends of a row
formed inward), every record lies in `{s_0, −s_0}`, `s_0` the first record's
value, which is uniform on `S²`. With frame-attached supports in general, the
alphabet after `n` records is contained in the orbit of the seeds under
compositions of the frame maps: the alphabet is generated by the formation,
not supplied.

*Proof.* Induction along the order with M1: a record seeing one recorded
neighbour of value `±s_0` takes a value in `{s_0, −s_0}`. The general case is
the same induction with M2's frame-attached supports. ∎ Executed: a path of
five sites with the antipodal support and a symbolic seed (D1).

## Theorem M5 — the Born overlap on the antipodal menu

**Statement.** On the support `{q, −q}` the overlap `(1 + s·q)/2` equals `1` at
`s = q` and `0` at `s = −q`: the Born form is deterministic copying there, and
any nontrivial Born statistic requires a support beyond the antipodal pair.
∎ (D2.)
