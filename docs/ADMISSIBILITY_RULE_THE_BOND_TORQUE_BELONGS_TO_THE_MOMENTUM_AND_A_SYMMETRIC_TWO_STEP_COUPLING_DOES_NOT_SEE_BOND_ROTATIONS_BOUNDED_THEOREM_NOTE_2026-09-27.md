# The bond torque belongs to the momentum, and a symmetric two-step coupling does not see bond rotations

**Date:** 2026-09-27

This note works within blocks 63, 64, 68, 69, 120 and 136 as landed on main (the walk's one-step and two-step momenta, the couplings they generate and the symmetric stress of the two-step content) and asks where the walk's twist on the bonds comes from; nothing is adopted and no gravitational claim is made.

## Result up front

Block 64 T5 found that the walk twists the bonds even in a uniform plane wave: the bond torque is `12/125` at sines `(3/5, 4/5, 0)`. Block 178 (pushed) showed two consequences under block 64's coupling. First, the coin's rotation cannot be a bond strain. Second, the member blind to bond rotations cannot balance the twist.

The landed programme had already moved on. Blocks 69 and 120 use the two-step momentum, and block 136 conserves a symmetric stress with a symmetric momentum. This note says why the one-step momentum twists and the two-step one need not. It also shows what a symmetric coupling does to block 178's conclusions.

- **T1: the twist is the momentum's.** Take any local coupling whose relabellings are generated by a local momentum density `P`. On a single plane wave its current is velocity times momentum, `v_a P_j`. So it is untwisted only if `P` points along `v` at every wave number.
  - The one-step momentum `sin k_j` does not. Its twist `(s_a s_j/E)(cos k_a − cos k_j)` is the same for every local current and every local density of it.
  - The two-step momentum `½ sin 2k_j = E v_j` does.
- **T2: a symmetric current without averaging.** The two-step momentum has an unaveraged local density `P^s` whose current `K^s` is exactly conserved and symmetric at every wave vector. Block 136's symmetric stress uses block 120's eight-neighbour average; `K^s` needs none. The coupling `H + Σ g_aj K^s_aj` sees only symmetric strains, keeps relabellings exact at first order, and for uniform strains is block 69 T4's coupling.
- **T3: the density matters.** At an exact pair of equal energies, block 63's current and block 69's own current both twist. `K^s` does not.
- **T4: a symmetric coupling does not see bond rotations.** Every pure bond rotation is unfelt through `K^s`, identically. So block 178's result, that the coin's rotation cannot be a bond strain, belongs to the one-step coupling. Whether bond rotations could carry the coin's rotation under a symmetric coupling is not constructed: that needs block 65's coupling attached to them.
- **T5: the member.** With `K^s` as the source, block 64's blind member balances every divergence-free source at leading order, and `β = 1`. This agrees with block 120's balance for block 62's symmetric member.

In plain terms: when the lattice is relabelled, the walk has to be moved, and the lattice offers two ways to move it.
- The one-step momentum twists the bonds, and nothing downstream undoes the twist.
- The two-step momentum can move the walk without twisting. Then the walk does not notice the bonds turning, and the field energy that ignores the turning balances it, with bending exponent one.

Block 73 (landed) had already found that the two-step momentum is the only one of reach two that every species reads as its own wave number.

## Theorem T1 — the twist is the momentum's

*Statement.*
- (a) For every local coupling with a momentum as declared, `i[H, P_j(x)] + Σ_a [K_aj(x) − K_aj(x − e_a)] = 0` for every state. That is, `K` is a current of `P`.
- (b) On a single plane wave of the positive band, with its coin normalised, `⟨K_aj⟩ = v_a ⟨P_j⟩`.
- (c) Hence `⟨K_aj⟩ = ⟨K_ja⟩` on every such wave only if `⟨P_j⟩` is parallel to `v` at every wave number.
- (d) For the one-step momentum, `⟨P_j⟩ = s_j`. So the uniform torque `⟨K_aj⟩ − ⟨K_ja⟩ = (s_a s_j/E)(c_a − c_j)` is the same for every local current and every local density of it; at block 64 T5's example it is `12/125`. By continuity in `q`, equal-energy pairs at small `q ≠ 0` also twist.
- (e) For the two-step momentum, `⟨P_j⟩ = s_jc_j = E v_j`, which is parallel to `v`.

*Proof.*
- (a) Sum by parts: `Σ_x (d_aξ_j) K_aj = −Σ_x ξ_j (div K)_j`. This equals `Σ_x ξ_j i[H, P_j(x)]` for every finitely supported `ξ`. Take `ξ = δ`.
- (b) In symbols, (a) reads `Σ_a (1 − e^{−iq_a}) K̂_aj = i(P̂_j h(k) − h(k') P̂_j)`. Sandwich with eigen-coins of one band and take `k' = k − εn`. The left side is `iε Σ_a n_a K̂_aj(k, k) + O(ε²)`. The right side is `i(E(k) − E(k')) P̂_j = iε (n·v) P̂_j(k, k) + O(ε²)`. The coins depend continuously on `k` where `E > 0`, so `Σ_a n_a K̂_aj(k, k) = (n·v) P̂_j(k, k)` for every `n`.
- (c) is immediate.
- (d) Every local density of `S_j` sums to `S_j`, so its single-wave value is `s_j`. Then `v_a s_j − v_j s_a = (s_as_j/E)(c_a − c_j)`.
- (e) `s_jc_j = E v_j`.

Runner B1 checks (a) for block 63's and block 69's currents as matrix symbols. B2 checks (b) on four rational waves for three currents, and B3 checks (d). ∎

Block 120 excluded every local realisation of the one-step current with its normalisation, through the divergence condition at non-zero `q`. T1(d) is the single-wave counterpart: every local current of every local density of the one-step momentum twists on single waves.

## Theorem T2 — a symmetric current without averaging

*Statement.* Let `Â_a = ¼(e^{ik_a} + e^{−ik'_a}) σ_a` and `f_a = ½(1 + e^{iq_a}) sin(k_a + k'_a)`. Define `K^s_aj = ½(Â_a f_j + Â_j f_a)` and `P^s_j = ½(½ f_j + h(k') Â_j + Â_j h(k))`.
- (a) `Σ_a (1 − e^{−iq_a}) K̂^s_aj = i(P̂^s_j h(k) − h(k') P̂^s_j)` as matrices, for all `k, k'`. So `K^s` is exactly conserved with density `P^s` for every state.
- (b) `K^s` and `P^s` are hermitian local forms, and `K^s_aj = K^s_ja`.
- (c) `Σ_x P^s_j(x)` is block 69's momentum `S_jC_j`, because `P̂^s_j(k, k) = s_jc_j · 1`.
- (d) The coupling `H^s[g] = H + Σ_x g_aj(x) K^s_aj(x)` to symmetric strains satisfies `H^s[sym dξ] = H + i[H, Σ_x ξ_j P^s_j(x)]` exactly. For uniform `g` it is `Σ_a σ_a[s_a + c_a Σ_j g_aj s_jc_j]`, block 69 T4's form, so all eight species see one geometry.

*Proof.*
- (a) Three identities give it:
  - `Σ_a (1 − e^{−iq_a}) Â_a = (i/2)(h(k) − h(k'))`, because `(1 − e^{−iq_a})(e^{ik_a} + e^{−ik'_a}) = 2i(s_a − s'_a)`;
  - `Σ_a (1 − e^{−iq_a}) f_a = i Σ_a sin q_a sin(k_a + k'_a) = i(E(k)² − E(k')²)`, with `h² = E²`;
  - `X_j h − h' X_j = (h² − h'²)Â_j` for `X_j = h'Â_j + Â_jh`.
  Runner C1 checks it.
- (b) is runner C2.
- (c) At `q = 0`, `f_j = 2s_jc_j` and `hÂ_j + Â_jh = c_js_j`.
- (d) follows from (a) by summing by parts. The uniform form is C3. ∎

`½ f_j` plays the part of block 136's carried two-step momentum `P″`, and `h'Â_j + Â_jh` the part of its coin-energy current `Q`. The placement `f` makes the unaveraged version conserved with its symmetric current.

## Theorem T3 — the density matters

*Statement.* Take the pair `k = (q₁/2, k₂, 0)`, `k' = (−q₁/2, k₂, 0)` with `e^{iq₁/2} = (4 + 3i)/5` and `e^{ik₂} = (3 + 4i)/5`. Both energies are one. At this pair:
- block 63's current has antisymmetric part `−72/125 − 96i/125` in the pair `(2, 3)`;
- block 69's own current has `−216/625 − 288i/625`;
- `K^s` has none, and is divergence-free.

On single plane waves of either band, `K^s` and block 69's current agree.

*Proof.* Exact evaluation (runner D1, D2). ∎

## Theorem T4 — a symmetric coupling does not see bond rotations

*Statement.* For every pure bond rotation `B_a^j = ω_aj(x)` (antisymmetric in `a, j`), `Σ_{a,j} ω_aj K^s_aj = 0` as an operator. Every tie of the coin's rotation into pure bond rotations therefore has `T†K^s = 0` identically. For example, block 178's forward-bond tie `B_a^j = ε_abj θ_b(x)` is unseen through `K^s` but seen through block 63's `J` at the pair of T3.

*Proof.* `K^s_aj = K^s_ja` (T2(b)). The comparison with `J` is runner D3. ∎

So block 178 T3, that no tie makes the coin's rotation a bond strain, is a property of the one-step coupling. T4 holds by construction: a coupling to symmetric strains cannot see antisymmetric ones. What it establishes is that the bond torque of block 178 T4(c) is absent. It does not establish that bond rotations carry the coin's rotation. That would need block 65's coupling attached to them, which is not constructed here.

## Theorem T5 — the member

*Statement* (block 64's family in continuum symbols, leading order). With `H^s`, the source of the strains is `⟨K^s⟩`: symmetric, and divergence-free on stationary states. At wave vector `κe₃`, the blind member's static form balances every symmetric divergence-free source (`K_3j = 0`, `K_12 = K_21`), and does not balance a bond torque. The blind member is `(c₁, c₂, c₃) = (K/4, K/2, −K)` with `c₄ = −2K`: block 64 T2's ratios with block 64's `c₄`. There `β = c₄/(2(2c₁ + c₂ + 2c₃)) = 1`.

*Proof.* The blind member's kernel is the three bond rotations (block 178 T4(c)), so its range is the symmetric transverse strains (runner E1). The exponent is E2. ∎

This agrees with block 120, which balances block 62's symmetric member with the two-step current.
