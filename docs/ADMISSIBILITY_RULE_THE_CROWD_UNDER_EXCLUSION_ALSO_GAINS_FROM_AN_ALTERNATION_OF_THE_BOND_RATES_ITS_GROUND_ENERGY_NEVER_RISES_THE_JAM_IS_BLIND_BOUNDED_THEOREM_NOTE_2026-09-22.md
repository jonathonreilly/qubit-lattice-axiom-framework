## Theorem T1 — nonincrease
The exclusion projector commutes with translations. Translation along every alternated axis conjugates H_n(delta) to H_n(-delta). The least eigenvalue is a minimum of affine expectations in delta, hence concave; being even, E_n(delta)<=E_n(0). This is non-strict: empty and jammed sectors provide equality.
On the four-ring the runner checks signed translation for n=1,2,3. In the n=2 sector it obtains E_2(0)=-sqrt(2) and E_2(3/10)=-sqrt(218)/10. No derivative or quadratic susceptibility follows from concavity, and a cusp is allowed.

## Theorem T2 — pure-hop jam and free-sea counting
With every site occupied and no on-site terms, any hop attempts an occupied target, so the compressed generator is zero for all coin configurations. The free one-particle model on a compatible even grid with equal nonzero |delta|<=1 has E^2>=d delta^2; chiral symmetry pairs every eigenvalue with its negative. Thus half of the 2N one-particle levels are negative. Filling that free sea with N particles does not implement site exclusion; the exclusion sector at N is the zero-hop jam instead.

## Theorem T3 — four-ring exception
On the four-ring the one-particle spectrum is +/-1 and +/-|delta|, each twice, so its ground energy is -max(1,|delta|). It is constant only when |delta|<=1. A six-ring already differs: the available pi/3 mode has absolute energy sqrt(3/4+delta^2/4), giving a strict gain for nonzero |delta|<1. The runner adds that exact counterexample.
The historical 3x3 and 4x3 simulations have odd alternated axes and are outside the periodic even-grid translation proof. Their fitted linear and quadratic coefficients cannot bound a three-dimensional continuum crowd threshold or establish a universal ordering against the free sea.
