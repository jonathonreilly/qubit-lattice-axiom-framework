and nu the law of H_0 in sigma. A7 gives the exact identity

    mu_c(B)=Tr(E_c rho_c E_c*)1_B(H_0), all Borel B.          (A21)

No high energy is discarded in this identity. It includes the entire
actual lattice one-particle band and every finite or infinite normal Fock
mixture through the maximal spectral measure. The actual and continuum
laws can have atoms, including the vacuum atom. Since the two densities
in A18 are normalized, spectral projection duality gives

    ||mu_c-nu||_TV<=delta_c^E/2,
    sup_x |F_mu_c(x)-F_nu(x)|<=delta_c^E/2,                 (A22)

where TV is the supremum over Borel events. There is no target-continuity
qualification needed for this total-variation result. A22 is stronger than
a conclusion from moment caps and a physical trace bound alone because
A18 supplies a different, exact energy-coordinate state comparison.

Free evolution conserves each of its own spectral measures. Consequently
A21--A22 hold after arbitrary actual and continuum free-hold durations,
even if those durations differ or grow without restriction. For the
smooth vacuum preparation they are O(c) bounds from A11/A16. No energy
moment is required for A22 itself: every normal density has a spectral
probability law. An unbounded response still requires additional tails.

Here are sufficient moment resources. For a chosen integer n assume literal
norm-flow commutators ad_(H_c)^j V_c(t), j=1,...,n, with integrable envelopes
kappa_j(t) uniform in c, together with the zeroth L1 norm bound. The fixed
smooth finite cell family supplies such envelopes by finite product Leibniz.
For a word, a sufficient j-th envelope is the sum over slot orders adding
to j of the multinomial coefficient times product_i ||E^(j_i)f_i||.
The actual discrete finite differences are contractions of the corresponding
continuous derivative averages, giving the same uniform sufficient norms.
Thus each separately chosen finite order is available for fixed smooth
compact inputs; no bound uniform in that order is claimed.

For clarity, the domain premise precedes the moment estimate. Norm-C^n free
orbits give graph-space boundedness of V in D((1+H)^n), with its finite
commutator expansion. Graph-space Dyson uniqueness identifies the domain
propagator with the Hilbert-space unitary. Removing the leading selfadjoint
interaction term leaves the triangular lower-graph-level inequality of P.
With b_j=int kappa_j and

    B_n=n! [z^n] exp(z+sum_(j=1)^n b_j z^j/j!),              (A23)

one has ||(1+H)^n U_prep(1+H)^(-n)||<=B_n. Explicitly, with A=1+H,
the norm levels y_l=||A^l U psi|| satisfy the integral majorant with
right side sum_(j=1)^l binomial(l,j) kappa_j y_(l-j). Initially each
y_l, l<=n, is at most ||A^n psi||. The exponential generating function
of this finite triangular system is exp(z+sum b_j z^j/j!); taking its
n-th coefficient proves the stated bound. The same argument applies
on the continuum. Interpolating the two-sided graph strip for any s>0,
n=ceil(s/2), and applying it to a closed moment column gives

    N_s(rho_c,H_c)<=B_n^(s/n) M_(in,s),
    N_s(sigma,H_0)<=B_(n,0)^(s/n) M_(in,s,0),               (A24)

where N_s=Tr rho(1+H)^s. In the vacuum case both initial moments are one.
Finite spectral moments mean closed (1+H)^(s/2)rho^(1/2) Hilbert--Schmidt
columns, not D(H^s) for every ensemble vector. Spectral cutoffs and finite
columns justify A24 for arbitrary normal mixed inputs. After the switch
to free dynamics all these moments are exactly constant for all holds.

Suppose a chosen s-th moment is bounded on both sides by the actual
resources in A24. For 1<=q<s the common-positive-part coupling of the laws
proves

    W_q(mu_c,nu)
       <=(delta_c^E/2)^(1/q-1/s)
             [(M_s^c)^(q/s)+(M_s^0)^(q/s)]^(1/q)
       <=(delta_c^E)^(1/q-1/s)(M_s^c+M_s^0)^(1/s),          (A25)

where M_s denotes the unshifted moment, bounded by N_s. Indeed the two
residual measures have equal mass t<=delta_c^E/2; product coupling and
|x-y|^q<=x^q+y^q cost at most the sum of their q-th moments. Holder on
each residual gives t^(1-q/s) times its s-moment to power q/s. The last
inequality is scalar concavity. No native-spacing error has to be added
in A25, because A21 compares the same H_0 spectral observable exactly.

A stronger scalar-response norm is also available. Define weighted measure
variation with its full, rather than half, mass convention. For 0<=k<s,
Holder with respect to |mu_c-nu| gives

    integral (1+x)^k |dmu_c-dnu|
        <=(delta_c^E)^(1-k/s)(N_s^c+N_s^0)^(k/s).            (A26)

The total variation mass is at most delta_c^E and the s-weighted mass is
at most the sum of the two s-moments. Thus A26 uniformly controls every
measurable response bounded in absolute value by (1+x)^k, including scalar
k-th moment differences. It is not obtained by using unbounded tests in
ordinary trace duality. With delta_c^E=O(c), A25 is O(c^(1/q-1/s)) and
A26 is O(c^(1-k/s)); the moment constants are fixed by A24. Every fixed
finite q or k is covered by choosing a sufficiently higher fixed s.
No W_infinity estimate, exact limiting exponent, uniform infinite-order
constant or finite moment-generating function is inferred.

## 7. Fixed normal targets versus arbitrary moving state families

There is a useful way to relate the auxiliary argument to the given
physical placement without redefining it. On coefficient carriers set

    T_c=J_c^E (J_c^cur)*:K direct-sum K ->K.

It is a contraction and a partial isometry between the two actual ranges.

    ||T_c(f,0)-f||
       <=||J_c^cur C_c f-(f,0)||+||J_c^E C_c f-f|| ->0.      (A28)

Also T_c* f->(f,0), since its squared distance is at most
2||f||^2-2 Re<f,T_c(f,0)>. This is not strong convergence of T_c on the

Restrict to positive spaces and second quantize: call the contraction
mathcal T_c:F_*->F_0. Finite wedges and the vacuum show

    mathcal T_c ell ->I strongly,
    mathcal T_c* ->ell strongly,                            (A29)

where ell psi=psi tensor Omega_H. Finite-particle truncations and norm at
most one extend the convergence to all vectors. The exact partial-isometry
identities are

    mathcal T_c I_c=E_c,  mathcal T_c* E_c=I_c.              (A30)

For a fixed normal sigma, finite-rank approximation and the rank-one
product estimate turn A29 into trace-norm convergence of the corresponding
state products. In particular, if an actual family obeys

    beta_c=||I_c rho_c I_c*-ell sigma ell*||_1 ->0,

then

    delta_c^E<=beta_c+
       ||mathcal T_c ell sigma ell* mathcal T_c*-sigma||_1 ->0. (A31)

This proves A20 and A22 for any such fixed-normal-target family, even
without a moment cap. It also applies at the input to a fixed finite
preparation, followed by A18. Normality and the actual trace identification
are used; a weak CAR limit is not substituted for them. Quantitative rates
in the second term of A31 require additional information about sigma;
there is no uniform modulus on an arbitrary energy-moment ball.

Conversely, for fixed sigma and delta_c^E->0,

    ||I_c rho_c I_c*-ell sigma ell*||_1
       <=delta_c^E+||mathcal T_c* sigma mathcal T_c-ell sigma ell*||_1 ->0. (A32)

Thus fixed finite vacuum preparation also has the expected physical full-

use sigma_t=exp(-itH_0)sigma exp(+itH_0). Its trace-norm orbit over a compact

A32 uniform on that interval. No such compactness over all times is supplied.
This is exactly where unrestricted physical-density control can fail,
despite the unrestricted fixed-word and energy-law conclusions already proved.

A31 is a sufficient state-selection criterion, not a theorem for arbitrary

## 8. Actual fixed preparation: failure of all-time physical trace convergence
