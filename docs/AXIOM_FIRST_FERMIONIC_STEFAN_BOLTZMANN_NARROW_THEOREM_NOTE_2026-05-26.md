```text

```

```text
∫_0^∞ x^(s-1) / (e^x + 1) dx  =  (1 - 2^(1-s)) · Γ(s) · ζ(s)
                              =  Γ(s) · η(s)                       (FSB2)
```

## Statement

Let `H_F` be the RP-reconstructed physical Hilbert space restricted
to a single massless Dirac fermion species (4 internal degrees of
freedom: 2 spin × 2 particle/antiparticle). Let `T > 0` be the
thermal temperature with Gibbs state `ρ_β = e^{-β H_F}/Z_F`. Then on
the cited upstreams:

**(FSB1) Fermi-Dirac distribution from APBC KMS.** For each fermion
mode of frequency `ω`, the mean occupation in the thermal state is

```text
n_F(ω, T)  =  1 / (e^{β ω} + 1)                                     (1)
```

The `+ 1` (rather than `- 1`) comes from anti-commutation: the
single-mode partition function for a fermionic oscillator with
occupation `n ∈ {0, 1}` (Pauli exclusion) is
`Z_F = 1 + e^{-βω}`, giving `n_F = e^{-βω}/(1 + e^{-βω}) = 1/(e^{βω}+1)`.

**(FSB2) Fermion energy density.** Integrating `ω · n_F(ω, T)` against
the 3D Dirac-fermion density of states:

```text
u_F(T)  =  ∫_0^∞ dω · ω · n_F(ω, T) · g_F(ω) / V
        =  (2 / π^2 c^3) · ∫_0^∞ ω^3 / (e^{β ω} + 1) dω             (2)
```

**(FSB3) Fermi-Dirac Stefan-Boltzmann.** Substituting `x = β ω`:

```text
u_F(T)  =  (2 T^4 / π^2 c^3) · ∫_0^∞ x^3 / (e^x + 1) dx
        =  (2 T^4 / π^2 c^3) · Γ(4) η(4)                            (3)
        =  (2 T^4 / π^2 c^3) · 7 π^4 / 120
        =  (7 π^2 / 60) · T^4 / c^3                                  (4)
```

In natural units `ℏ = c = k_B = 1`:

```text
u_F(T)  =  (7 π^2 / 60) · T^4   (per Dirac species)                  (5)
```

**(FSB4) Boson-fermion ratio at d=4.** Per single d.o.f. (i.e.
dividing out the spin/charge multiplicities), the boson:fermion
energy-density ratio at `d_spacetime = 4` is

```text
u_F^(per d.o.f.) / u_B^(per d.o.f.)  =  η(4) / ζ(4)
                                      =  1 - 2^(1-4)
                                      =  7/8                          (6)
```

This is **NOT a fit, not a coincidence:** it is forced by the
Fermi-Dirac vs Bose-Einstein occupation algebra plus the universal
3D-spacetime integration measure on `Z^3 × S^1_β`. The identity
`(1 - 2^(1-s))` enters through the Fermi-Dirac integral expansion
`Σ (-1)^(n-1)/n^s = (1 - 2^(1-s)) ζ(s)` (alternating Dirichlet eta).

## Proof

### Step 1 — Fermi-Dirac distribution from APBC KMS (proves FSB1)

Consider a single fermionic oscillator mode with creation/annihilation
operators `b^†, b` satisfying `{b, b^†} = 1`, `b^2 = (b^†)^2 = 0`,
`H = ω b^† b`. Number-basis states are `{|0⟩, |1⟩}` (Pauli exclusion;
only two states per mode). The thermal partition function is

```text
Z_F(β)  =  Tr e^{-β H}  =  1 + e^{-β ω}                              (7)
```

The mean occupation:

```text
n_F(ω, T)  =  <b^† b>_β  =  (1/Z_F) · 0 · 1 + 1 · e^{-β ω}
           =  e^{-β ω} / (1 + e^{-β ω})
           =  1 / (e^{β ω} + 1)                                       (8)
```

This is the Fermi-Dirac distribution. ∎

**Equivalent reading via Matsubara periodicities.** The KMS relation
forces fermion-bilinear correlators on `S^1_β` to satisfy
`G_F(τ + β) = - G_F(τ)` (antiperiodic) under the standard
spin-statistics theorem, while boson-bilinear correlators satisfy
`G_B(τ + β) = + G_B(τ)` (periodic). The APBC fermion modes are
`ω_n = (2n+1)π/β` (Matsubara odd integers); the PBC boson modes are
`ω_n = 2nπ/β` (Matsubara even integers). The frequency-sum identity
`Σ_{n odd} 1/n^s = (1 - 2^(1-s)) Σ_{n ≥ 1} 1/n^s` is then a direct
consequence of the odd-integer partial sum splitting, equivalent to
the alternating Dirichlet eta.

### Step 2 — 3D Dirac density of states (used in FSB2)

On `Z^3` with emergent Lorentz `ω = c|k|` at long wavelengths, the
density of states per Dirac species (2 spin × 2 particle/antiparticle):

```text
g_F(ω) dω / V  =  4 · (1/(2π)^3) · 4π k^2 dk
              =  (2 k^2 / π^2) dk
              =  (2 ω^2 / π^2 c^3) dω                                 (9)
```

This is twice the photon's `g_B`. ∎

### Step 3 — Energy density integral (proves FSB2, FSB3)

Combining (1) and (9):

```text
u_F(T)  =  ∫_0^∞ dω · ω · (1/(e^{βω}+1)) · (2 ω^2 / π^2 c^3)
        =  (2 / π^2 c^3) · ∫_0^∞ ω^3 / (e^{β ω} + 1) dω              (10)
```

Substitute `x = β ω`, `dω = dx/β`:

```text
u_F(T)  =  (2 / π^2 c^3) · (1/β^4) · ∫_0^∞ x^3 / (e^x + 1) dx
        =  (2 T^4 / π^2 c^3) · Γ(4) η(4)                              (11)
```

Using `Γ(4) = 6` and `η(4) = 7 π^4 / 720`:

```text
u_F(T)  =  (2 T^4 / π^2 c^3) · 6 · 7 π^4 / 720
        =  (7 π^2 / 60) · T^4 / c^3                                   (12)
```

This is (4)/(5). ∎

### Step 4 — Boson-fermion ratio at d=4 (proves FSB4)

Compare with the bosonic Stefan-Boltzmann (companion note's (18)):
`u_B^(photon)(T) = (π^2/15) T^4`. The photon has 2 polarizations, so
per d.o.f.: `u_B^(per d.o.f.)(T) = (π^2/30) T^4`. The Dirac fermion
has 4 internal d.o.f. (2 spin × 2 particle/antiparticle), so per
d.o.f.: `u_F^(per d.o.f.)(T) = (7 π^2 / 60) T^4 / 4 = (7 π^2 / 240) T^4`.

The ratio:

```text
u_F^(per d.o.f.) / u_B^(per d.o.f.)
  =  (7 π^2 / 240) / (π^2 / 30)
  =  (7 / 240) · 30
  =  7 / 8                                                            (13)
```

Equivalently `= η(4)/ζ(4) = 1 - 2^(1-4)`. ∎
