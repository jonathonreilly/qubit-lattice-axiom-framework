## Statement

On a finite block of the qubit-on-`Z^3` framework, suppose the physical local
action surface supplies:

1. a positive Hermitian temporal transfer matrix `T` on the finite physical
   Hilbert space;
2. finite-range local Hamiltonian support on the spatial graph;
3. the staggered reflection sign rule selecting the temporal axis and failing
   for spatial reflection axes.

Then:

- `H = -log(T / ||T||) / a_tau` is self-adjoint and non-negative on the support
  of `T`;
- `U(t) = exp(-i t H)` is a unique one-parameter unitary evolution generated by
  `H`;
- each equal-time slice `Sigma_t = {t} x Z^3` carries the mutually commuting
  tensor product of site algebras and is the codimension-1 Cauchy datum for the
  finite-range update;
- the staggered temporal-reflection sign rule excludes an independent spatial
  reflection-positive transfer matrix, so no second Hamiltonian clock is
  available on the same action surface.

Therefore the action surface has exactly one clock.

## Proof

### 1. Spectral calculus

For finite `T >= 0`, let `M = ||T||`.  On the support of `T`, the spectral
calculus defines

```text
H = -log(T / M) / a_tau.
```

The eigenvalues of `T/M` lie in `(0, 1]`, so the eigenvalues of `H` lie in
`[0, infinity)`.  Hence `H` is self-adjoint and non-negative.  The top
eigenvalue of `T` maps to zero energy.

### 2. One-parameter unitary group

Since `H` is finite-dimensional and self-adjoint,

```text
U(t) = exp(-i t H)
```

is unitary, satisfies `U(0)=I` and `U(s+t)=U(s)U(t)`, and is strongly
continuous.  The finite-dimensional Stone generator is unique: differentiating
at `t=0` recovers `H`.

### 3. Codimension-1 Cauchy data

A1 supplies one finite qubit algebra at every site and A2 supplies the `Z^3`
spatial adjacency graph.  Operators on distinct spatial sites commute at equal
time because they are tensor factors.  A finite-range Hamiltonian propagates
support only through a finite graph neighborhood per finite time step, with
the usual finite-block Lieb-Robinson exponential estimate.  Thus data on one
spatial slice is the local codimension-1 datum for the update.

### 4. No second clock

The staggered temporal-reflection sign rule used by the action surface is
orientation-selective.  The temporal reflection supplies the transfer
factorization; spatial reflections do not satisfy the same temporal-hop sign
condition and therefore do not produce an independent positive transfer matrix
on the same action surface.  With only one positive transfer axis, the
Hamiltonian generator above is the only clock.
