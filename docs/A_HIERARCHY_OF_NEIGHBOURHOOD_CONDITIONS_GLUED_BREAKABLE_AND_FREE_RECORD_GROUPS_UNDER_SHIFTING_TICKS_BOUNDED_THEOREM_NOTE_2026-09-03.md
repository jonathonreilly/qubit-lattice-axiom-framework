## The stipulated levels and tick models, declared in full

Three levels and four ticks, all declared here, none derived. A lane proposing different ones inherits the obligations of `T1`-`T5` and none of these choices.

1. **GLUED.** The condition is a **support condition**: every configuration violating it carries odds **exactly 0** and is never registered. Two forms, both from PR #7891: **`C_comp`**, every record has at least one adjacent
   record; **`C_rig`**, every pair adjacent now stays adjacent. The emergent instance is Gauss's law `G_v = (div E)_v - rho_v = 0` on the cube (`T4`).
2. **BREAKABLE.** The condition is an **energy cost**, `E(S) = -g A(S)` with `A(S)` the number of adjacent record pairs. With `B(S) = (n-1) - A(S)` the number of adjacencies the group has lost relative to its maximally
   bonded form -- exact for `n <= 3` on this bipartite lattice, where `A <= n-1` -- the odds of `S` are suppressed by `e^{-g}` **per broken adjacency**: `pi(S) ~ e^{-g B(S)}`. Low suppression under most conditions, raised
   under some, is exactly the dial `g`.
3. **FREE.** No condition: `g = 0`, every configuration admissible.
4. **`K1` the single-record tick.** Exactly one record shifts to a nearest-neighbour site, uniformly over the admissible shifts. At **reach `r`** the target is the record's site plus `r*d`. `D_1 = 1/6` for one record.
5. **`KB(r)` the neighbourhood tick.** The whole support set shifts by `r` units **as a block**, uniformly over the six unit directions.
6. **`KM(q)` the mixed tick.** With odds `q` a `KB(1)` block shift, with odds `1-q` a `K1` single shift.
7. **`BR(g, h, reach)` the breakable tick.** A record is chosen with odds `1/n`; a direction with odds `e^{h}/(5 + e^{h})` for `+x` and `1/(5 + e^{h})` for each of the other five; the target is the record's site plus
   `reach*d`. A target carrying a record makes the tick **null**. Otherwise the shift is registered with odds `min(1, e^{-g dB})`. At `h = 0` the proposal is symmetric, so `pi(S) ~ e^{-g B(S)}` is exactly stationary.

**Convention `K1`, declared so two conventions are not confused.** Under `K1` exactly one record shifts per tick, so a free `n`-record group has `D_free(n) = 1/(6 n^2)` and `D_free(2) = D_1/4`. PR #7889 and PR #7891 quote an
independent-record value `D_1/2`, which is the ratio under a tick where **both** records shift every tick. Both are reported and neither is preferred here.

## Definitions

A **group of records** is a finite set of occupied sites of the torus `Z_L^3`, one record each; `A(S)` counts its adjacent pairs, `B(S) = (n-1) - A(S)`, and `CoM(S)` is the arithmetic mean of the occupied sites. The
**translation class** of a group is its orbit under the torus's translations. A **cage certificate** is an exact `Fraction` potential `Phi` of the class with `CoM(s') - CoM(s) = Phi(c') - Phi(c)` at every admissible shift,
assigned by breadth-first sweep and then **re-checked on every edge**; a group carrying one has a lab centre of mass with finitely many values, hence `D_group = 0` identically. The **internal pattern** of a group is the
multiset of its pairwise minimal-image difference vectors.

The **ring** is one line of the torus, `R = 16` sites, four records, shifts restricted to `+-x`, `1820` configurations. The **cube** is the `2x2x2` cube graph: `8` corners, `12` edges, one **fermion edge record** `y_e` and
one **link record** `l_e` per edge, `2^24` joint record patterns, `star(v)` the three edges at `v`, `n_v(y) = |y intersect star(v)| mod 2`, `E_e = +-1/2` from `l_e`, `(div E)_v = sum_{e at v} s_{v,e} E_e`, and the sea
convention `rho_v = n_v - 1/2`. The whole cube construction is self-contained in the runner and reproduces PR #7893's census from its own incidence data.

## Theorem 1 -- the glued level: a cage under single ticks, exactly `D_1` under a neighbourhood tick

**Conclusion.** `[exact]` On the `L = 8` torus. **The cage.** The glued dimer admits `0` single-record shifts under either `C_comp` or `C_rig` -- `1` translation class, `0` coboundary inconsistencies, cage width `0` -- so it
is **frozen outright**. The glued bent trimer under `C_comp` admits `8` shifts over `4` classes with `0` inconsistencies and centre-of-mass spread exactly `(1/3, 1/3, 0)`: three corners of one **fixed** unit square, forever.
Under `C_rig` it admits `0`. In every case `D_group = 0` **exactly**. **The block tick.** Over all `512` base sites `x` `6` directions `= 3072` block shifts per group and per reach, the internal pattern is preserved
**exactly at every tick** -- `0` violations across all four censuses, and no two records ever coincide -- with `E|dCoM|^2 = 1` at reach `1` and `4` at reach `2`. Hence `D_block = D_1` **exactly** at reach `1` and `4 D_1` at
reach `2`, and the group's internal configuration is a constant of the motion. **The mixed tick.** `D(q) = q D_1` **exactly**: the Markov-additive slope of `Var|CoM|` matches `q` to `0.00e+00` in all ten cells (two groups,
five values of `q`). **Reach.** Under a reach-`2` **single** tick the same glue leaves the dimer `2` admissible shifts and `2` coboundary inconsistencies -- a self-loop carrying a nonzero centre-of-mass increment, so the
cage certificate **fails**; both shifts are **leapfrogs** over the partner, each moving the centre of mass a full unit along the pair axis, giving `E|dx|^2 = 1` and `D = D_1` exactly, confined to that one axis. The bent
trimer stays caged, at width `2/3`.

**Proof.** The class graphs are breadth-first closures of the admissible shift relation, taken modulo the torus's translations; the potential is built with exact `Fraction` increments `(y - x)/n` from one root per component
and then every edge is re-checked against it, so the certificate is a proof and not a fit. The block census enumerates every base site and every direction and compares the full internal pattern before and after. The
mixed-tick statement is an exact argument -- a block increment is a mean-zero unit vector independent of the shape process, the `K1` increment is the exact coboundary, so it telescopes to a bounded term and the cross terms
vanish -- and the recursion is a cross-check at `1e-12`.

**Reading, not theorem.** A rule with no exceptions holds a group absolutely, because the configuration that would part it carries odds `0` and is never registered; there is no strength parameter to turn down. But on this
lattice glue is not a vehicle. One step away from a site is one step away from all of that site's neighbours at once, so the very shift that would move a glued group is the shift its condition forbids: the dimer is frozen
solid and the trimer rattles in a box a third of a site wide. Let the **neighbourhood** shift instead -- the whole support set at once, which is what "its own shared condition" asks for -- and the group travels at exactly
the speed of a single record, no faster and no slower, with its internal pattern untouched. Mix the two and only the block part counts.

## Theorem 2 -- the breakable level: the cost is the odds, and what the odds buy

**Conclusion.** `[exact]` Under `BR(g, 0, 1)`. **Stationarity.** On the exact `511`-state relative chain of the dimer (`6` intact relative vectors, `505` broken) the odds `pi(S) ~ e^{-g B(S)}` are exactly stationary,
`|pi P - pi| <= 2.8e-17`. **Intact fractions.** `6/(6 + 505 e^{-g})` for the dimer, matched to the chain at `7.8e-16`; and `15/(15 + 1500 e^{-g} + 41920 e^{-2g})` for the trimer, from the exact translation-class census
`A = 2: 15`, `A = 1: 1500`, `A = 0: 41920`, total `43435 = C(512,3)/512`. **Lifetimes.** Every registered shift out of an intact dimer breaks it and `10` of its `12` proposals are unblocked, each with `dB = +1`, so
`P(break) = (5/6) e^{-g}` and the mean lifetime is `(6/5) e^{g}`; the bent trimer's `18` proposals split `4` null, `2` cage-preserving, `8` at `dB = +1` and `4` at `dB = +2`, giving `18/(8 e^{-g} + 4 e^{-2g})`. Both agree
with absorbing-chain first-passage solves to `1.7e-11`. **Transport.** `D_intact = 0` **exactly at every `g`**, including `g = 0`, for both groups; `[numerical, 1e-9]` the unconditional `6 D_group` runs
`0.2495, 0.2446, 0.2321, 0.1531, 0.006933, 2.391e-06` at `g = 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16`, i.e. from `0.998043` down to `9.6e-06` of `D_free(2) = D_1/4`. **Re-binding.** The relative chain is finite and irreducible, so
`P(re-bind) = 1` exactly at every finite `g`, and the mean recurrence to the intact set after a break is exactly `101 = 505/5` ticks **at every `g`** (maximum over broken starts `204.706`). **The two limits.** As
`g -> infinity` the admissible set contracts to exactly the `C_comp`/`C_rig` set of `T1`; at `g = 0` the pair is intact `1.17 %` of ticks and the trimer `0.035 %`.

**Proof.** The relative chain is exact because the tick is translation covariant on the torus, so the relative vector is itself Markov. Stationarity is checked entrywise against the closed-form odds. The lifetimes are closed
forms derived by counting proposals and their `dB`, then cross-checked by a first-passage solve whose matrix `I - A` is assembled with its diagonal as the **summed escape weight**, so the small quantity is never recovered by
cancellation. The variance recursion carries the martingale correction that makes it the true asymptotic constant. The re-binding statement's `g`-independence has an exact reason: once broken, every proposal has `dB <= 0`
and is registered with odds `1`, so the broken sector's chain does not depend on `g` at all.

**Reading, not theorem.** This is the owner's second question, answered on the declared model: the cost **is** the odds, and nothing else is added. What a suppression of `e^{-g}` per broken adjacency buys is a lifetime, and
the lifetime is exactly `(6/5) e^{g}` ticks -- steep in the cost, but always finite. What it does not buy is transport: a bound pair cannot move at all while it is intact, so at finite cost it gets anywhere **only** by
breaking, and its diffusion constant dies away exactly as fast as its lifetime grows. And the cost is one-sided. It slows the break and does nothing whatever to the re-binding: after a parting the pair finds itself again
with certainty, in `101` ticks on average, a number completely independent of `g`. Turn `g` up far enough and the whole admissible set is the glued set: the top level is the limit of the middle one.

## Theorem 3 -- what breaks a group, and what does not

`e^{g}(5 + e^{h})/(4 + e^{h})` transverse, matching the chain to `2.3e-16` over `30` `(g, h)` cells. So it stays between `5/6` and `5/3` of its zero-field value **at every `g` and every `h`**, and for an aligned pair it
**increases** with `h`. **The threshold runs backwards.** Under pure single ticks the intact group has no admissible shift, so its mean tick count to cross one site is infinite and `h*(g) = 0` identically -- it always tears
first. Under `KM(q)` with both components tilted, the exact threshold is `e^{h*} = [4(1-q) + q e^{g}]/[q e^{g} - (1-q)]`, defined when `q e^{g} > 1-q`, falling from `1.363508` at `g = 1` to `0.000001` at `g = 16` at

**Reading, not theorem.** A steady push does not pull a bound pair apart. It pushes both records the same way, so it carries the pair along; all it changes is which of the two is the likely one to move, and the whole effect

## Reading, not theorem -- the whole thing in plain words

Three kinds of togetherness. A group whose rule has no exceptions can only travel as one, and then it travels exactly as fast as a single record; if the rule is only expensive to break, the group holds for a while that grows
steeply with the cost, and it gets anywhere only by breaking and finding itself again; with no rule at all, records wander alone. A steady push does not pull a bound pair apart, it carries the pair along; what pulls it apart
is a push that differs across it, or a collision, and a strong enough rule turns collisions into mergers. In the emergent matter, a charge and its flux are of the first kind: no single record can change alone, and every
allowed change carries them together.
