# A site-count change by one carries the Spin(10) spinor, so under the odd-site rule every fermion is a mobile site defect; its cones stay vector-like

## Question

composite-sixteen (B) proved that on a FIXED set of five-mode sites the Spin(10) centre is (-1)^F (-i)^(#sites). So
every excitation is a vector-class representation, and a spinor 16 needs the site count to change by one. Its route 1
was left UNTESTED. This note tests that route, which ties matter to grid growth:
- Is a matter fermion a site defect, i.e. an extra site (interstitial) or a missing site (vacancy)?
- Can such a defect move coherently, covariantly and as a fermion, and what is its band?
- Is fermion-number conservation the same as site-count conservation?
- Is the split cascade, which adds sites, consistent with this?

## Setting (supplied)

- Five modes per site and the bilinear Spin(10) M_ab = (i/2) sum_s g_a^s g_b^s. Both are inherited from
  why-five-modes and are not rederived here.
- A lattice point holds a site or none.
- A site moves into an empty neighbouring point by transferring its whole mode content. This is the mode-level
  fermionic swap, i.e. the relabelling of mode operators with their Jordan-Wigner sign. The transfer adds no
  structure beyond the existing modes.
- The vacuum's exchange is the invariant pair operator B_xy = sum_ab M^x_ab M^y_ab.

## A. Centre algebra (exact)

- A1. The odd level of one site (N = 1, 3, 5; 16 states) is one irreducible 16. The generators keep it, its Casimir is
  45/4 and its commutant has dimension 1. Its weights carry an even number of -1/2. We call this class "16" (z = +i)
  and the conjugate "16bar" (z = -i). The naming is a convention, but the conjugate pairing below is not.
- A2. The element -1 of Spin(10), exp(2 pi i M_02), is the c-number (-1)^Ns (checked for n = 1, 2). So
  z = exp(i pi sum Cartans) = (-1)^F (-i)^Ns and z^2 = (-1)^Ns: **Spin(10)'s -1 is site-count parity.**
- A3. The relative charge is z_rel = (-i)^dn (-1)^dF. A defect is spinor class iff dn is odd, whatever its content
  parity dF. For example, dn = +1 with even content is a spinor-class BOSON. So "site defect" alone does NOT give the
  Spin x_Z2 tie between -1 and (-1)^F.
- A4. **Odd-site rule** (candidate): every present site holds an odd number of units. Then z = i^Ns and
  (-1)^F = (-1)^Ns hold as operator identities (checked over every basis state up to Ns = 4). Consequences:
  - -1 in Spin(10) equals (-1)^F on every state;
  - every fermion-odd excitation changes the site count by an odd number, and conversely;
  - an interstitial (dn = +1) is a 16 and a vacancy (dn = -1) is a 16bar, and both are fermions.
- A5. The even-site rule is the symmetric alternative. It gives z = (-i)^Ns with (-1)^F = +1, so every site defect is
  a bosonic spinor. It is rejected because it breaks the spin-statistics tie. The odd rule is therefore the one
  parity choice that makes the defect spinors fermions. This is a reason for the choice, not a fitted value.
- A6. Cost. The invariant one-mode hop K_st never stays in the odd-odd sector (it maps it wholly to even-even). Under
  the odd rule, CL-1-type single-mode hops between present sites are forbidden. At fixed site count only the
  per-site-even exchange B survives, and every fixed-Ns excitation is root class (bosonic).
- A7/C0. Covariant creation. 16 x 16 = 10 + 120 + 126 (Casimir 9, 21, 25) has no singlet, and 16^4 holds exactly 2
  singlets. A Spin(10)-equivariant map from Ns to Ns + dn sites must preserve z, so under the odd rule it needs
  dn = 0 mod 4:
  - a two-site creation must carry a 10, 120 or 126 partner (the 16.16 channels);
  - four sites can be created as a singlet.

## B. Transfer and statistics (exact)

- B1. Exchange two sites by three transfers through an empty point. The sign is (-1)^(N_A N_B), checked for all 1024
  content pairs. Odd sites are fermions: the site-content field f_alpha(x) is a hard-core 16-component fermion, and
  the transfer is f^dag_{x alpha} f_{y alpha}.
- B2. The transfer is local (one bond) and Spin(10)-covariant. The swap maps g^x to g^y, commutes with all 45 M_ab
  and is unitary.
- B3. A link phase u on the transfer acts as u^N on the content. This is covariant iff u = +-1 (deviation 1.00 for
  u = i, 0.87 for u = e^(i pi/3)). The register's -1 face phases pass. A complex link phase on a moving site breaks
  Spin(10).

## C. One vacancy on a four-slot ring (finite diagnostic)

- C1. The exchange vacuum H = J sum B, with J = 1, is a unique Spin(10) singlet (Casimir 8e-17, gap 6.0, E0 = -21).
  The weight-0 sector has 960 states and the vacancy sector 240.
- C2/C3. Removing a site in state alpha, a_alpha(x)|vac>, has weight 1/16 per slot. Every vacancy state has relative
  charge -i (16bar class) and is fermion-odd.
- C4. Model: H = -t sum transfers + J sum B.
  - At t = 0.5 the lowest state at every k is a 16bar (C = 45/4) and holds 0.98-1.00 of that k's removal weight.
    The band is E - E0 = +11.000, +10.378, +10.000, +10.378 at k = 0, pi/2, pi, 3pi/2.
  - At t = 2 the removal weight sits (0.83-1.00) in a 16bar band with minimum +8.500 at k = pi. At k = 0 a 144bar
    (C = 21.25) lies lower than the 16bar.
  - At both t, E_16bar(0) - E_16bar(pi) = 2t exactly. The vacancy moves at FIRST order, unsuppressed by J.
  - The formation energy is 8.5-11 J, i.e. cell scale.
- The interstitial band is not computed. It needs extra points (for example odd-cube centres) or double occupancy.

## D. 3D defect band (toy: polarized background, t >> J)

- D1. With the -1 face phases (which pass, B3) the defect hop is the pi-flux band
  E^2 = 4t^2 (cos^2 kx + cos^2 ky + cos^2 kz). It has 8 zero modes on 8^3 and isotropic cones of speed 2t at
  k_i = pi/2. This is the staggered fermion content: 2 four-component Dirac cones per internal state.
- D2. An invariant staggered on-point energy m(-1)^(x+y+z) gaps every cone exactly (min |E| = |m|). The cones pair up,
  so net chirality is 0. The 16 index is a spectator (B2), so each pair is 16_L + 16_R, which is vector-like. This is
  the Nielsen-Ninomiya theorem (cited), made explicit here.
- D3. Without face phases the band bottom is quadratic and there is no cone.
- The background is not the singlet vacuum. An interacting background is not tested.

## E. Split counting (exact)

- E1. The register's split takes each parent site to a child even cube, adding 7 sites per parent site, so one site's
  split multiplies z by i^7 = -i (spinor). But splits act on whole even cubes, adding 56 sites per cube, so
  z is multiplied by i^56 = 1. **The split is centre-neutral and creates no net matter.** This agrees with the
  register's "the glued split ... B, L and every fermion number kept" without being built for it.
- E2. Covariance needs dn = 0 mod 4 (A7, C0), and 56 = 4 x 14 complies. Every partial or collar pattern (hanging-nodes,
  PR #9941) must also add 0 mod 4 sites. That is unchecked.
- The odd level's mean occupation is exactly 5/2, so the register's "new modes start half filled" is the odd-rule
  average automatically.

## F. Conserved charges (exact)

- Fermion number is not a separate charge: N - 5Ns/2 is a Spin(10) Cartan generator.
- Under the odd rule, fermion parity equals site-count parity.
- The U(1) of site count is Q16 = (number of interstitials) - (number of vacancies), i.e. one unit per 16 quantum.
- F1. B and L are not in span{Y, B-L, Q16} on one 16 (residuals 1.0 and 1.0). Site conservation gives neither B nor L.
- F2. A chiral Dirac mass (l e^c) has Q16 = 2, so exact site conservation would forbid chiral masses. The vector-like
  defect mass of D2 is Q16-neutral. This point is moot while the cones are vector-like.

## Answers to the four tests

1. **Yes.** One extra site is spinor class, as is one missing site. Under the odd rule the interstitial is the 16 and
   the vacancy the 16bar, exactly. Without the rule the class depends on content parity, and spinor bosons occur.
   Vacancy against interstitial is particle against antiparticle: the conjugate class, the same statistics, and
   Frenkel-pair annihilation (dn = 0) is covariant. It is NOT matter against mirror. The internal centre does not fix
   spacetime chirality.
2. **Yes.** The hop is local, covariant (only Z2 link phases) and fermionic. On the ring it is a coherent first-order
   16bar band. In 3D with face phases it has cones, but they are vector-like and gappable by an invariant mass. No
   chirality.
3. **Fermion parity is site-count parity.** The conserved U(1) is Q16, not B or L.
4. **The Lattice axiom is bent** (points may be empty, and sites move). The split cascade is consistent and
   centre-neutral. Growth by whole-cube splits never creates net matter. In an infinite or growing grid, defects are
   defined locally against the vacuum pattern, so the reading survives.
