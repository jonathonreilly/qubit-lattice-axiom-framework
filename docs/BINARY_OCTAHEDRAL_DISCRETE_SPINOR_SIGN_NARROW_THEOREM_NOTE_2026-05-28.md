Precisely, write `2O` as the 48 unit quaternions in `S³ ≅ SU(2)` (the 24
unit Hurwitz quaternions of the binary tetrahedral group `2T`, together
with the 24 quaternions of the form `(1/√2)(±1, ±1, 0, 0)` over distinct
coordinate pairs). The covering homomorphism `SU(2) → SO(3)`,
`q ↦ (v ↦ q v q^{-1})` on the imaginary quaternions, has kernel `{±1}`
and image `O ≅ S₄` (order 24). The central element

```text
z = −1   (the quaternion −1, i.e. (−1, 0, 0, 0)),
```

is the unique nontrivial element of the centre `Z(2O) = {+1, −1}`, has
order 2, and is the lift of the identity rotation along the `2π`
loop in `SU(2)`. The irreducible representations of `2O` split by the

```text
spinorial (faithful) irreps,        z = −1,   dims {2, 2, 4},  Σ d² = 24;
non-spinorial irreps (factor thru O), z = +1, dims {1, 1, 2, 3, 3}, Σ d² = 24.
```

The total `Σ d² = 24 + 24 = 48 = |2O|` exhausts the regular

```text
ρ(z) = ρ(−1) = −I₂,        χ_defining(z) = trace ρ(−1) = −2 = −dim.
```

The `−1` arises from the **group structure of `2O`** — the central

## 1. Claim scope

Let `O ⊂ SO(3)` be the cubic (octahedral) rotation group, the 24 proper
rotations of the `Z³` spatial substrate's cubic point group, isomorphic
to the symmetric group `S₄`. Let `π : SU(2) → SO(3)` be the standard
two-to-one covering homomorphism with kernel `{±1}`. The **binary
octahedral group** is the preimage

```text
2O := π^{-1}(O) ⊂ SU(2),        |2O| = 2 · |O| = 48.
```

`2O` is a non-split central extension `1 → {±1} → 2O → O → 1` (it is
**not** isomorphic to `O × Z₂`; the extension is the one realized inside
`SU(2)`). The narrow theorem comprises the following statements, all of
which are exact finite-group / character-theory facts and are certified
by the companion runner.

**(C1) Group.** The 48 listed unit quaternions are closed under
quaternion (Hamilton) multiplication, contain the identity `+1` and
inverses, and hence form a group, the realization of `2O` inside `SU(2)`.

**(C2) Unique nontrivial central element.** The centre of `2O` is exactly
`{+1, −1}`. The element `z = −1` is central, has order 2 (`z² = +1`,
`z ≠ +1`), and is the **unique** nontrivial central element. It is the
lift of the `2π` rotation: traversing a full `2π` loop in `SU(2)` from
the identity returns to `−1`, not `+1`.

**(C3) Conjugacy structure.** `2O` has exactly 8 conjugacy classes, with
class-size partition `{1, 1, 6, 6, 6, 8, 8, 12}` (summing to 48). The
class `{z}` is one of the two singleton classes (`{+1}` and `{−1}`).

**(C4) Irreducible-representation dimensions.** `2O` has exactly 8
irreducible complex representations (one per conjugacy class), with
dimension multiset

```text
{1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4},        Σ d² = 1+1+4+4+4+9+9+16 = 48 = |2O|.
```

**(C5) Sign partition by the central element.** By Schur's lemma, the
central element `z` acts as a scalar `c(R) ∈ {+1, −1}` on each
irreducible representation `R` (it is central and `z² = e`, so the scalar
is a square root of unity, hence `±1`). The sign partitions the eight
irreps into

```text
spinorial (faithful) irreps,  c(R) = −1,  dims {2, 2, 4},  Σ d² = 24;
non-spinorial irreps,         c(R) = +1,  dims {1, 1, 2, 3, 3}, Σ d² = 24.
```

The non-spinorial irreps (`c(R) = +1`) are exactly those that factor
through the quotient `O ≅ S₄` (i.e. `z` is in their kernel), and they
realize the five complex irreps of `S₄` of dimensions `{1, 1, 2, 3, 3}`.
The spinorial irreps (`c(R) = −1`) are exactly the **faithful** irreps of
`2O` (those not factoring through `O`); on each, `χ(z) = c(R) · dim =
−dim`.

**(C6) Defining spin-½ representation.** The defining homomorphism
`ρ : 2O → SU(2)`, `q ↦ q`, is faithful (kernel `= {+1}`) of dimension 2,
and `ρ(z) = −I₂`, so `χ_defining(z) = −2 = −dim`. The defining
representation is one of the three spinorial (faithful) irreps of (C5).

Statements (C1)–(C6) are pure finite-group / quaternion / character-theory
facts. They make **no** claim that the framework's matter is fermionic,
that the on-site spinor sign controls a two-site exchange operator, that
anticommutation / canonical anticommutation relations (CAR) hold, or that
spin-statistics has been derived. The narrow records only that the
discrete spinor sign `−1` **exists** as a finite-group fact.

## 5. Proof

### 5.1 (C1) `2O` is a group of order 48

The 48 listed unit quaternions are the standard quaternionic realization
of `2O` (Conway–Smith, *On Quaternions and Octonions*, Ch. 8; Coxeter,
*Regular Complex Polytopes*). The 24 unit Hurwitz quaternions
(`(±1, 0, 0, 0)` and permutations, plus `(±½, ±½, ±½, ±½)`) form the
binary tetrahedral group `2T = SL(2, 3)`; adjoining the 24 quaternions
`(1/√2)(±1, ±1, 0, 0)` over distinct coordinate pairs yields a set closed
under Hamilton multiplication, containing `+1` and closed under
quaternion conjugation (`= inverse` for unit quaternions). Closure of all
`48 × 48` products is verified exactly by the runner. Hence the set is a
group of order 48. ∎

### 5.2 (C2) `z = −1` is the unique nontrivial central element

For unit quaternions, `q` is central iff it commutes with the imaginary
units `i, j, k`, which forces the imaginary part of `q` to vanish, i.e.
`q ∈ {+1, −1}`. Both are unit quaternions in `2O`, so `Z(2O) = {+1, −1}`.
The element `z = −1` satisfies `z² = +1` and `z ≠ +1`, hence has order 2,
and is the **unique** nontrivial central element. Under the covering
`π : SU(2) → SO(3)`, `π(−1) = π(+1) = identity`, and `−1` is the endpoint
of a `2π` loop based at the identity in `SU(2)`: it is the lift of the
`2π` rotation. ∎

### 5.3 (C3) Conjugacy structure

`2O` has 8 conjugacy classes with sizes `{1, 1, 6, 6, 6, 8, 8, 12}`
(verified exactly by the runner from the multiplication table). The two
singleton classes are precisely the central elements `{+1}` and `{−1}`;
in particular `{z}` is a class of size 1, consistent with `z` central.
The number of conjugacy classes equals the number of inequivalent
irreducible complex representations, namely 8. ∎

### 5.4 (C4) Irreducible-representation dimensions

The dimensions `d_R` of the 8 irreps satisfy the Burnside / regular-
representation identity `Σ_R d_R² = |2O| = 48`. The unique solution
consistent with `2O` having `O ≅ S₄` (whose irreps have dimensions
`{1, 1, 2, 3, 3}`, `Σ d² = 24`) as a quotient and three additional
faithful spinorial irreps is

```text
{1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4},     Σ d² = 1+1+4+4+4+9+9+16 = 48.
```

The runner checks this partition by decomposing the class algebra (the centre
of the group algebra `C[2O]`) into simultaneous eigenspaces and recovering the
multiset `{1, 1, 4, 4, 4, 9, 9, 16}`, whose square roots are
`{1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4}`. That class-algebra step uses numerical eigenspace
clustering as a consistency check against the standard character table; the
exact runner proofs are the quaternion group law, centre, conjugacy structure,
and defining spin-½ central sign. ∎

### 5.5 (C5) Sign partition by `z`

Since `z` is central, Schur's lemma forces `ρ_R(z)` to be a scalar
`c(R) · I` on every irrep `R`; since `z² = e`, `c(R)² = 1`, so
`c(R) ∈ {+1, −1}`. An irrep `R` has `c(R) = +1` iff `z ∈ ker ρ_R`, i.e.
iff `ρ_R` factors through the quotient `O = 2O / {±1} ≅ S₄`. The five
irreps factoring through `S₄` have dimensions `{1, 1, 2, 3, 3}`
(`Σ d² = 24`); these are the non-spinorial irreps with `c(R) = +1`. The
remaining three irreps are **faithful** (do not factor through `O`), have
`z ∉ ker`, and therefore `c(R) = −1` (the only nontrivial central square
root of unity); these are the spinorial irreps, of dimensions `{2, 2, 4}`
(`Σ d² = 24`). On a spinorial irrep, `χ_R(z) = c(R) · d_R = −d_R`; on a
non-spinorial irrep, `χ_R(z) = +d_R`. The runner reads `c(R)` for each
isotypic component by restricting the regular-representation matrix of `z` to
that component and confirming the constant diagonal `±1`, then checks the
dimension partition above. This is the same numerical class-algebra consistency
check described in §5.4, not a replacement for the standard character-table
fact. ∎

### 5.6 (C6) Defining spin-½ representation

The defining homomorphism `ρ : 2O → SU(2)`, `q ↦ q`, sends a unit
quaternion `q = (w, x, y, z)` to the `SU(2)` matrix
`ρ(q) = w I₂ − i (x σ₁ + y σ₂ + z σ₃)`, with `trace ρ(q) = 2w`. This is
injective (`ρ(q) = I₂` forces `w = 1`, hence `q = +1`), so `ρ` is
faithful of dimension 2; it is therefore one of the three spinorial
irreps of (C5). For the central element,

```text
ρ(z) = ρ(−1) = −I₂,      χ_defining(z) = trace(−I₂) = −2 = −dim.
```

The runner exhibits `ρ(−1) = −I₂` and `χ_defining(z) = −2` by exact
`2 × 2` sympy arithmetic, verifies faithfulness (trivial kernel), checks
the homomorphism property `ρ(ab) = ρ(a)ρ(b)` on a covering family of
products, and confirms `χ_defining(+1) = +2`. ∎

This completes the proof of (C1)–(C6).
