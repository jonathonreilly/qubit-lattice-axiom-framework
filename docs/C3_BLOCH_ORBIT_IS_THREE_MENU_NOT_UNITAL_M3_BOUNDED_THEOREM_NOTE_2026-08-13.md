## Result Up Front

Work in one-site `M_2(C)`. Let `ω` be a primitive cube root of unity:
`ω^3 = 1`, `ω ≠ 1`, `ω^2 + ω + 1 = 0`. Let `R = diag(1, ω)` and
`Ad_R(X) = R X R^{-1}` with `R^{-1} = diag(1, ω^2)`. This is an
order-3 automorphism of `M_2(C)`.

Start from the equatorial projector
`P0 = |+⟩⟨+| = ((1/2, 1/2), (1/2, 1/2))`. Set `P1 = Ad_R(P0)` and
`P2 = Ad_R(P1)`. Then `{P0, P1, P2}` is a 3-orbit of distinct
rank-1 projections, `Ad_R(P2) = P0`, and

```text
P0 + P1 + P2 = (3/2) I_2.
```

The unweighted projections are not a resolution of the identity. Scaling
them gives the standard mathematical trine normalization

```text
Ek = (2/3) Pk,              E0 + E1 + E2 = I_2.
```

The `Ek` are effects, not projections, since `Ek^2 = (2/3)Ek ≠ Ek`.
Thus the scaled family is a three-outcome POVM but still not a PVM. The
unital `*`-algebra generated by the `Pk` is all of `M_2(C)`. There is no
unital `*`-hom `M_3(C) → M_2(C)`.

This set is not `{|0⟩⟨0|, |1⟩⟨1|, |+⟩⟨+|}`. Here “menu” means only the
displayed finite candidate support; it does not assert that the framework's
fixed Admissibility rule selects that support. Conditional on the support at
a site being supplied as exactly `{P0, P1, P2}`, Record locks one member of
that supplied support when a record forms. The note derives no menu-selection
rule, probability weights, measurement implementation, `SU(3)`, or QCD
identification.

## Theorem 1 — a 3-orbit of rank-1 projections

Let `|ψ_k⟩ = (1, ω^k)^T/sqrt(2)`. Direct multiplication gives
`Pk = |ψ_k⟩⟨ψ_k|` for `k=0,1,2`. Hence each `Pk` is self-adjoint,
idempotent, and trace one. The off-diagonal entries are respectively
`1/2`, `ω^2/2`, and `ω/2`; primitivity of `ω` makes the three projections
distinct. Conjugation advances `k` modulo three, so `Ad_R(P2)=P0`.

## Theorem 2 — not the computational-basis-plus set

Both coordinate projectors are diagonal. The displayed formulas show that
`P1` and `P2` have nonzero off-diagonal entries, so neither is
`|0⟩⟨0|` or `|1⟩⟨1|`. Pairwise distinctness shows neither is `P0`.
The two unordered three-element sets therefore share only `P0=|+⟩⟨+|`
and are unequal.

## Theorem 3 — not a PVM

`P0 + P1 + P2 = (3/2) I_2 ≠ I_2`. Off-diagonal parts cancel because
`1 + ω + ω^2 = 0`. The unweighted projectors therefore are not a PVM.

There is a sharp partial closure: `Ek=(2/3)Pk` obeys `Σk Ek=I_2`, so the
scaled effects form a three-outcome POVM. They are not projections because
`Ek^2=(4/9)Pk=(2/3)Ek≠Ek`.

The corresponding Naimark construction does not change the algebra type.
Define `V:C^2→C^3` by

```text
(Vx)_k = sqrt(2/3) <ψ_k|x>.
```

Then `V*V=Σk Ek=I_2`, and compression of the coordinate projection `Qk`
gives `V*QkV=Ek`. Thus `Φ(A)=V*AV` is a unital completely positive map
from `M_3(C)` to `M_2(C)`. It is not a `*`-hom: `Qk^2=Qk`, while
`Φ(Qk)^2=Ek^2≠Ek=Φ(Qk^2)`.

## Theorem 4 — generated algebra is `M_2`, not `M_3`

Flatten `I_2`, `P0`, `P1`, and `P0P1` into four-entry columns. Exact
elimination over `Q(ω)` gives rank four; equivalently, after the standard
complex embedding their determinant is `3/16`. These four matrices belong
to the generated algebra and already span the four-dimensional ambient
algebra. Hence the generated unital `*`-algebra is all of `M_2(C)`, not an
algebra of complex dimension nine.

## Theorem 5 — still no unital `M_3 → M_2`

Suppose `φ:M_3(C)→M_2(C)` were a unital `*`-hom. Its kernel is an ideal
of the simple algebra `M_3(C)`. Unitality makes `φ` nonzero, so the kernel
must be zero and `φ` must be injective. No injective complex-linear map can
send a nine-dimensional vector space into a four-dimensional one. Therefore
no such `φ` exists. This is the `3 ∤ 2` instance of the usual finite-
dimensional representation divisibility criterion.
