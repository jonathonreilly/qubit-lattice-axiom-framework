# Candidate laws

Claim type: meta.

Candidate laws are the current working rules adopted to build on. They are not axioms. The [minimal axioms](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-06-29.md), [policy section 6](audit/AXIOM_MINIMALITY_POLICY.md#6-explicit-owner-approval-for-axioms-and-primitives) and `docs/audit/data/axiom_premise_nodes.json` alone control premise admission, so this register has zero premise weight.

The laws are judged against measured results, not against theoretical preference. The axioms are to be revisited once the laws account for the measured world.

Tags used below: [definite edges] derived with definite, continuous edge lengths (holds for the edge registers only in the coherent many-cell limit, up to (l_P/L)^2); [old window] computed on the window [L, 2L] before the floor was lowered to L/8 and the top raised to 2^(1+Delta) L; [pre-wave-shift] computed before CL-3's wave-law shift; [label-frame] computed under the shift law read in labels, before the normal-frame law (shift-charge-law); [cubic] and [site] link-register layouts (see Link-register carriers).

## CL-1: update

> "Sites are the points of Z^3, tiled in two ways by 2x2x2 cubes offset by (1,1,1); each site's qubit is one fermion mode, and modes at different sites anticommute. The odds, one amplitude per joint occupation of the modes, change only by half-ticks that alternate between the tilings. In each half-tick every cube applies a unitary read from its own edges: the inverse spatial metric gamma^ij, fixed by its edge lengths, and the lapse N, the proper time per tick along the normal to its slice. The lapse is not free: every cube ticks at the same fraction of its own light crossing, sqrt(3) N times the rms of sqrt(gamma^ii) being one constant, sin(eps0), in every cube and every half-tick. Let W_i pass each site's odds to its edge neighbour along axis i with a phase, the phases multiplying to -1 around every unit face. The cube's hop R is a sum of the W_i with positive weights, sqrt(gamma^ii) when gamma^ij is diagonal, scaled so that R^2 = 1 wherever none of the cube's registers is at an extreme value, and its step is eps0. A species of mass m applies exp(-i(eps - mu/2)R) on every even cube and exp(+i(eps + mu/2)R) on every odd cube, with its step eps and mass angle mu the angles at which its dressed light cone, link dressing included, is the null cone of the cube's edges and its dressed rest phase is mN per tick; without links these are eps0 and mu = mN. Let s = i R_1 R_2 R_3 R, with R_i = sqrt(3) W_i read from the even cube's undressed hops, be the cube's copy sign; each even cube applies exp(i (M N/4)(1 - s) R) before and after its half-tick, giving the copy with s = -1 an extra mass M. The shift and the shear are read one rung up: the even cubes, taken as sites, are tiled in two ways by 2x2x2 blocks offset by one cube; between the even and the odd half-tick every block of the first tiling rotates each pair of its cubes along axis i into each other by the angle theta_i with sin(2 theta_i) the shift per tick in cubes, plus a part proportional to the shear and to the odd half-tick's displacement along the other axes, and every block of the second tiling undoes the same rotation in the same site labels. Hop weights, angles and shear parts are those for which the cube's light cone is the null cone of its edges.

> "Links. Each factor's register is read at its own rung: its rishons per end are role-assigned on the axis 6-orbits and 24-orbits of a block of side B_f made of whole even cubes and of whole finer blocks; a hop whose sites lie in different B_f-blocks, as only odd-cube hops can, is dressed by V^q of the one register on their shared face, with V the register's raising operator normalised on its domain and q the site's charge in the factor's unit, and hops inside a B_f-block are undressed for that factor. Each half-tick also advances the odds by the links' plaquette phase of CL-3, and only odds obeying Gauss's law in every B_f-block occur. Matter modes sit on the finest role block and edge registers on the coarsest.

> "Edges. Each edge of the block triangulation carries a quantum edge register: the triangulation is that of the cubes of a coarser block lattice of b_e cells to an edge, by their axis edges and, on every square block face, the diagonal joining its two even-sum corners, so that every block cube is five tetrahedra alternating between neighbours (six registers per block site, one per metric component). A register is (4 + Delta)s fermion-mode sites at each end, role-assigned in a coarser block, each site a bond partner of one site at the other end; its raising operator is the sum over partner pairs of the hop from the second end to the first, normalised on its domain; its length is its flat length (b_e rung lengths on an axis edge, sqrt 2 b_e on a diagonal) times 2^(n/s - 3), with n the occupation at its first end, so lengths run from an eighth of the flat length to 2^(1+Delta) times it, and each step changes the edge's log-length by ln2/s. Only lengths at which every tetrahedron of the triangulation has positive volume occur. Each cell reads the flat metric of the block tetrahedron holding its centre, the block registers advance once per block half-tick, and each cube's half-tick is the unitary above controlled on its block's edge registers' values.

> "Split. A block splits all its cubes at once into eight child blocks. A split belongs to the even tiling of blocks: a split's children are the eight child even cubes that have its sites as least corners, on a child lattice that contains every parent site with each parent edge a union of child edges, and a split bisects exactly the edges inside them (so each block site owns its six forward edges). Its split image is the state its child blocks take from it by the passing below. At the middle of an even half-tick, the raise of an edge register that would bring the mean value of a block cube's edge registers to 4s (log-mean edge twice the flat length) carries the odds onto the split image of the raised state, and the matching lowering carries that image back, so the update is unitary on the sum over refinement patterns, a tensor product over parent cubes of each cube's patterns, and children's odds outside the split images have no lowering across the glue.
