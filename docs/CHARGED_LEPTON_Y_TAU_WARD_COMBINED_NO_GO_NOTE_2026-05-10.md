# Charged-lepton Ward construction: six attempted routes and open obligations

**Date:** 2026-05-10; scope correction 2026-10-21

**Claim type:** open_gate

The six historical attempts below did not supply a derivation of a physical
charged-lepton Yukawa coefficient on their stated source packet. This is a
report of attempted routes, not a partition of all admissible constructions.
The old assertion that no seventh class exists is withdrawn. No complete
proof that the current axioms forbid a Ward identity is supplied.

The hierarchy value v and the proposed top Ward normalization are conditional
source inputs, not facts established by this note. In particular, matching
fields, residues, couplings and the physical Higgs scale require their own
premises. The [top Ward source](YT_WARD_IDENTITY_DERIVATION_THEOREM.md)
and [direct gauge-selection discussion](CHARGED_LEPTON_DIRECT_WARD_FREE_YUKAWA_NO_GO_NOTE_2026-04-26.md)
are context for those proposals; their entire physics is not certified here.

The old blanket assertion that Fierz identities exist only for nonabelian
SU(N) is false. Spinor completeness/rearrangement and abelian normalizations
must be distinguished from the particular color-generator contraction.
For the supplied U1 charge vector, the squared norm is6; normalizing it
introduces sqrt6. This refutes the algebraic claim that abelian data cannot
produce that radical. It does **not** derive a Ward identity or physical
coupling. On a trivial color representation the Lie generators are zero,
not the identity matrix.

The finite gauge-neutrality witness uses Y1=I3 and Y2=diag(1,2,3).
Every entry multiplies the same neutral `bar L H e` monomial under the
supplied charge assignment. It shows that this charge test alone does not
fix Y. It does not prove underdetermination under every additional axiom,
dynamics or normalization condition.

Koide-based constructions, lepton-block scalar constructions and
cross-sector relations remain research routes. Neither missing retention
nor an unsuccessful search proves mathematical impossibility. New progress
might follow from existing premises; a new axiom is not shown necessary.
