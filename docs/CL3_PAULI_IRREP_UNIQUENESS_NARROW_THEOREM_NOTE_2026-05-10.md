# Cl(3) Pauli Irrep Uniqueness Narrow Theorem

**Date:** 2026-05-10
**Type:** positive_theorem
**Claim scope:** the finite-dimensional complex representation theory of the
real Clifford algebra `Cl(3,0)` defined by
`gamma_i gamma_j + gamma_j gamma_i = 2 delta_ij`. Its complexification is
proved directly to be the direct sum of exactly two simple ideals, each
isomorphic to `M_2(C)`. Consequently the real algebra has exactly two
irreducible complex representations up to complex-linear equivalence; both
have complex dimension two and are faithful as representations of the real
algebra. The two representations are distinguished by the ordered volume

The claim is only an abstract algebra/module classification. It does not

## Theorem

Let

```text
A_R := Cl(3,0)
```

be the unital real algebra on generators `gamma_1, gamma_2, gamma_3` with

```text
gamma_i gamma_j + gamma_j gamma_i = 2 delta_ij 1,                 (Cl3)
```

and let

```text
A := A_R tensor_R C
```

be its complexification. Put

```text
omega := gamma_1 gamma_2 gamma_3,
e_+   := (1 - i omega)/2,
e_-   := (1 + i omega)/2.                                       (1)
```

Then:

1. `A_R` has the exact real basis, and `A` has the corresponding exact
   complex basis,

   ```text
   B = (1, gamma_1, gamma_2, gamma_3,
        gamma_12, gamma_13, gamma_23, omega),                    (2)
   ```

   where `gamma_ij := gamma_i gamma_j` for `i < j`.

2. `omega^2 = -1`, `omega` is central, and the sign convention in (1)
   gives

   ```text
   omega e_+ = +i e_+,       omega e_- = -i e_-.
   ```

3. `e_+` and `e_-` are nonzero complementary central orthogonal
   idempotents. The ideals

   ```text
   A_+ := A e_+,             A_- := A e_-
   ```

   are complementary four-dimensional two-sided ideals.

4. The maps

   ```text
   Phi_+ : A_+ -> M_2(C),     e_+ -> I,   gamma_i e_+ -> +sigma_i,
   Phi_- : A_- -> M_2(C),     e_- -> I,   gamma_i e_- -> -sigma_i             (3)
   ```

   are complex-algebra isomorphisms. Hence

   ```text
   A = A_+ direct-sum A_-  ~=  M_2(C) direct-sum M_2(C).          (4)
   ```

5. Up to complex-linear equivalence, `A` has exactly two irreducible unital
   complex modules: the natural `C^2` module of `A_+` with `A_-` acting by
   zero, and the natural `C^2` module of `A_-` with `A_+` acting by zero.
   Equivalently, `A_R` has exactly two irreducible complex representations

   ```text
   rho_+(gamma_i) = +sigma_i,       rho_-(gamma_i) = -sigma_i.    (5)
   ```

   Both restrictions `rho_+|A_R` and `rho_-|A_R` are faithful real-algebra
   maps. Their complex-linear extensions to all of `A` are not faithful:
   their kernels are `A_-` and `A_+`, respectively.

6. Every finite-dimensional unital complex `A`-module has unique
   multiplicities `n_+, n_- >= 0` and is isomorphic to

   ```text
   (C^2_+)^(direct-sum n_+) direct-sum
   (C^2_-)^(direct-sum n_-).                                    (6)
   ```

7. If the generators act Hermitian in a compatible inner product, then
   complex-linear equivalence within a fixed sign in (5) upgrades to unitary
   equivalence. This is a conditional `*`-representation refinement, not part
   of the bare complex-module classification.

## Exact basis and multiplication

Every word in the generators can be reordered into increasing index order
using `gamma_j gamma_i = -gamma_i gamma_j` for `i != j`. Repeated indices
then cancel using `gamma_i^2 = 1`. Thus the eight monomials in (2) span the
universal algebra.

For subsets `S,T` of `{1,2,3}`, write `gamma_S` and `gamma_T` in increasing
order. The defining relations give the exact rule

```text
gamma_S gamma_T = (-1)^N(S,T) gamma_(S symmetric-difference T),
N(S,T) = #{(s,t) in S x T : t < s}.                              (7)
```

This produces the complete table below; rows multiply columns.

| `·` | `1` | `gamma_1` | `gamma_2` | `gamma_3` | `gamma_12` | `gamma_13` | `gamma_23` | `omega` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `1` | `1` | `gamma_1` | `gamma_2` | `gamma_3` | `gamma_12` | `gamma_13` | `gamma_23` | `omega` |
| `gamma_1` | `gamma_1` | `1` | `gamma_12` | `gamma_13` | `gamma_2` | `gamma_3` | `omega` | `gamma_23` |
| `gamma_2` | `gamma_2` | `-gamma_12` | `1` | `gamma_23` | `-gamma_1` | `-omega` | `gamma_3` | `-gamma_13` |
| `gamma_3` | `gamma_3` | `-gamma_13` | `-gamma_23` | `1` | `omega` | `-gamma_1` | `-gamma_2` | `gamma_12` |
| `gamma_12` | `gamma_12` | `-gamma_2` | `gamma_1` | `omega` | `-1` | `-gamma_23` | `gamma_13` | `-gamma_3` |

For completeness, construct an eight-dimensional real vector space with this

three `gamma_i`, so the universal Clifford algebra maps onto it. The normal
form above gives dimension at most eight, while this quotient has dimension

## Volume element and central idempotents

The table gives

```text
omega^2 = -1,
omega gamma_1 = gamma_23 = gamma_1 omega,
omega gamma_2 = -gamma_13 = gamma_2 omega,
omega gamma_3 = gamma_12 = gamma_3 omega.                         (8)
```

Since the generators generate `A`, (8) proves centrality. Using
`omega^2 = -1` in (1) gives

```text
e_+ + e_- = 1,       e_+ e_- = e_- e_+ = 0,
e_+^2 = e_+,         e_-^2 = e_-.
```

Centrality of `omega` makes both idempotents central. Their abstract labels
are fixed, not interchangeable under the stated ordered-volume convention:

```text
omega e_+ = +i e_+,            omega e_- = -i e_-.                (9)
```

There are no other central summands. Indeed, solving the commutators of a
generic element in the basis (2) with all three generators gives

```text
Z(A) = {a 1 + b omega : a,b in C}.
```

For `p=a 1+b omega`, the equation `p^2=p` is exactly

```text
a^2-b^2=a,                    2ab=b.
```

Its complete solution set is `0,1,e_+,e_-`. Thus (1) exhausts the nontrivial
central idempotents rather than merely displaying two idempotents visible in
chosen quotients.

## The two four-dimensional ideals

Because `e_+` and `e_-` are central, `A e_+` and `A e_-` are two-sided
ideals. Inside either ideal, the bivectors and volume element reduce as

```text
omega e_s    = s i e_s,
gamma_12 e_s = s i gamma_3 e_s,
gamma_13 e_s = -s i gamma_2 e_s,
gamma_23 e_s = s i gamma_1 e_s,                 s in {+1,-1}.     (10)
```

Thus each ideal is spanned by

```text
(e_s, gamma_1 e_s, gamma_2 e_s, gamma_3 e_s).                    (11)
```

Expanding (11) in the already proved basis (2) gives an exact rank-four
coefficient matrix, so the four vectors are independent without invoking a
Pauli map. Therefore each ideal has dimension four. Also

```text
a = a(e_+ + e_-) = a e_+ + a e_-
```

for every `a in A`, and the intersection is zero because multiplication by
the opposite idempotent kills it. Hence

```text
A = A e_+ direct-sum A e_-.
```

## Abstract matrix units and explicit isomorphisms

Before introducing Pauli matrices, construct four elements inside each
abstract ideal. For `s in {+1,-1}`, set

```text
E_11^(s) = e_s(1+s gamma_3)/2,
E_22^(s) = e_s(1-s gamma_3)/2,
E_12^(s) = s e_s(gamma_1+i gamma_2)/2,
E_21^(s) = s e_s(gamma_1-i gamma_2)/2.                            (12)
```

Direct multiplication with table (7) gives all sixteen identities

```text
E_ab^(s) E_cd^(s) = delta_bc E_ad^(s),
E_11^(s)+E_22^(s) = e_s.                                         (13)
```

The four elements in (12) have rank four in the canonical basis and span
`A_s`. Therefore sending `E_ab^(s)` to the standard matrix unit `E_ab`
defines a unital algebra isomorphism `A_s -> M_2(C)`: (13) proves
multiplication and the unit, rank four proves injectivity and surjectivity.
This is an algebra-level certificate in each ideal, not a conclusion inferred
from the dimensions of two desired quotient images.

It also proves simplicity internally. If a two-sided ideal of `A_s` contains
a nonzero element `X=sum x_ab E_ab^(s)`, choose `x_ab != 0`; then

```text
E_pa^(s) X E_bq^(s) = x_ab E_pq^(s)
```

for every `p,q`. Hence the ideal contains every matrix unit and equals `A_s`.
Together with the complete central-idempotent solve above and the
eight-dimensional direct sum, this proves that `A_+` and `A_-` are exactly
the two simple summands; no additional summand can occur.

Only now introduce the Pauli assignments. The maps
`gamma_i -> +sigma_i` and `gamma_i -> -sigma_i` satisfy all nine relations
(Cl3), so they extend to algebra homomorphisms. They give

```text
rho_+(e_+)=I,  rho_+(e_-)=0,
rho_-(e_+)=0,  rho_-(e_-)=I,                                    (14)
```

and send the four abstract units (12) in the matching ideal to the four
standard matrix units. Thus their restrictions to the matching ideals are
exactly the isomorphisms in (3). The runner checks all abstract products and
verifies

```text
Phi_s(xy) = Phi_s(x) Phi_s(y).                                   (15)
```

## Finite-dimensional module classification

Let `V` be a unital complex `A`-module. Centrality and completeness of the
idempotents give the canonical submodule decomposition

```text
V = e_+ V direct-sum e_- V.                                      (16)
```

It remains to classify modules of one `M_2(C)` summand without importing a
classification theorem. Let `E_ab` be its matrix units and put
`W := E_11 V`. The maps

```text
F : C^2 tensor W -> V,
F(e_1 tensor w) = w,               F(e_2 tensor w) = E_21 w,

G : V -> C^2 tensor W,
G(v) = e_1 tensor E_11 v + e_2 tensor E_12 v                     (17)
```

are `M_2(C)`-module maps. Their inverse identities are explicit. For
`w in W`, `E_11 w = w` and `E_12 w = 0`, so

```text
F G(v) = E_11 v + E_21 E_12 v = (E_11 + E_22)v = v,
G F(e_1 tensor w) = e_1 tensor w,
G F(e_2 tensor w) = e_2 tensor E_12 E_21 w = e_2 tensor w.
```

Equivariance is the matrix-unit identity

```text
F(E_ab e_j tensor w) = delta_bj F(e_a tensor w)
                      = E_ab F(e_j tensor w).
```

Thus `F` and `G` are inverse module maps and

```text
V ~= C^2 tensor W.                                                (18)
```

If `V` is nonzero and irreducible, `W` must be one-dimensional: any proper
nonzero subspace of `W` would give a proper nonzero submodule in (18).
Conversely the natural `C^2` module is irreducible because the matrix units
send either coordinate direction to either basis vector. This proves the
unique two-dimensional irreducible module of each simple summand and yields
(6), with the unique multiplicities
`n_s = dim_C(E_11 e_s V)`. In particular, (16) forces an irreducible
`A`-module to choose exactly one of the two summands, so the two modules in
(5) exhaust all irreducible complex representations. This argument uses no
Schur-lemma shortcut: its hypotheses are exactly a unital finite-dimensional
complex module and the displayed matrix-unit relations.

Every complex representation of the real algebra `A_R` extends uniquely to a
complex-linear representation of `A` by

```text
rho_C(a tensor z) := z rho(a).
```

Thus the preceding classification is exactly the classification of complex
representations of `Cl(3,0)`. The complexified `rho_+` kills `A_-` and the
complexified `rho_-` kills `A_+`. Each complex map has rank four, and its
opposite ideal already has dimension four, so these are the exact kernels:

```text
ker(rho_+ on A) = A_-,              ker(rho_- on A) = A_+.
```

On the real basis (2), however, either Pauli assignment gives, up to signs
and ordering,

```text
I, sigma_1, sigma_2, sigma_3, iI, i sigma_1, i sigma_2, i sigma_3.
```

These are a real basis of the real algebra `M_2(C)`. The exact real image
matrix therefore has rank eight, so both restrictions to `A_R` are
injective and faithful. This is the precise sense in which the two
irreducible complex representations of the real Clifford algebra are
faithful even though neither extension is faithful on the nonsimple
direct-sum complexification. There is no faithful irreducible `A`-module.

## Conditional unitary refinement

Fix one sign and let `rho,rho'` be irreducible `*`-representations for which
the three generator images are Hermitian. The complex classification supplies
an invertible intertwiner `T` with

```text
rho'(gamma_i) = T rho(gamma_i) T^(-1).
```

Taking adjoints and using Hermiticity shows that `T^dagger T` commutes with
the image `M_2(C)`. The matrix-unit calculation gives a scalar commutant, so
`T^dagger T = c I` with `c > 0`. Then `U := T/sqrt(c)` is unitary and gives
the same intertwining. This is a generic commutant/polar argument, not an
inspection of selected intertwiners.

The Hermitian hypothesis is decisive. Let `D=diag(2,1)` and replace a Pauli
triple by `D sigma_i D^(-1)`. The new triple still satisfies (Cl3), but it is
not Hermitian. If a unitary `U` intertwined it with the original triple,
then `D^(-1)U` would lie in the scalar commutant. Thus `U=lambda D`, which
cannot be unitary because `D^dagger D=diag(4,1)` is not scalar. Bare
complex-linear equivalence therefore does not imply unitary equivalence.

## Scope firewall

The theorem proves only the finite-dimensional algebra consequences above.
In particular:

- An irreducible `A`-module is an abstract module, not a physical lattice
  carrier or a per-site Hilbert space.
- The theorem defines no action of spatial translations or cubic rotations on
  a carrier.
- It supplies no Hamiltonian, transfer law, time evolution, admissibility
  dynamics, record-formation rule, probability, or observable.
- It does not select `rho_+` over `rho_-`, identify chirality with a physical
  handedness, or assert uniqueness of a representation realized in nature.
- A downstream use of `dim_C V = 2` for a physical site must separately prove
  that its physical carrier is one of these abstract irreducible modules.
- The conditional unitary statement uses an explicit compatible
  Hermitian/`*` structure; that structure is not inferred from the bare
  complex representation.
