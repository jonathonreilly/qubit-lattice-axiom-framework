- **Translation invariance of restricted Hamiltonian.** Required hypothesis on the restriction. Pure statement of translation symmetry.

## Statement

Let `Λ_2D ⊂ Z^3` be a 2-dimensional coordinate sublattice (plane) and
`Λ_1D ⊂ Z^3` be a 1-dimensional coordinate sublattice (line). Let `H_2D`
be a finite-temperature, translation-invariant, local/short-range Hermitian
Hamiltonian on `Λ_2D` carrying a continuous global symmetry and a
Ward-normalized order/charge pair satisfying W1-W4 of the cited bridge note.
Similarly for `Λ_1D`. Then:

**(S1) 2D no-order conclusion.** At fixed T>0, the W1-W4 order parameter satisfies

```text
lim_{L -> infinity} m_L = 0 on the 2D sublattice.                 (1)
```

Here m_L is the symmetry-breaking order parameter entering the Ward commutator bound, not the expectation of the conserved charge density q_x. If m_L is the uniform expectation of a specified local order operator, that order expectation vanishes in the corresponding thermodynamic limit. No assertion is made that every charge density vanishes.

**(S2) 1D no-order conclusion.** The same limit holds on the 1D sublattice under the same uniform W1-W4 hypotheses.                                       (2)

For example H=-h sum_x sigma_z(x), h!=0, is short-range and U(1)-invariant. Its product Gibbs state has <sigma_z>=tanh(beta h)!=0 while the transverse order <sigma_x>=<sigma_y>=0. Nonzero conserved charge density need not break this U(1). The runner checks this counterexample explicitly.

**(S3) Conditional continuous no-order conclusion.** No equilibrium phase satisfying the same uniform W1-W4 hypotheses can carry the specified nonzero order parameter for the continuous symmetry generated by Q at finite temperature.

**(S4) Universality across embeddings.** (S1)–(S3) hold independently of which specific 1D line or 2D plane is chosen, provided the same W1-W4 assumptions hold for the restricted order/charge pair. There are three coordinate orientation families of planes and three of lines, modulo translations. Proper cubic rotations relate the orientations; they are not inequivalent under the full lattice symmetry. Each family obeys (S1)–(S2) under the stated hypotheses.

(S1)–(S4) constitute the no-SSB-on-low-dimensional-sublattice theorem on the
explicit Ward-normalized CMW bridge surface.

## Proof

The proof applies the finite-volume Ward-normalized Bogoliubov bridge to the
sublattice setting, using the cited IR-sum threshold as the dimension input.

### Step 1 — Identify sublattice as Z^d for d ≤ 2

The 2D coordinate-aligned plane `Λ_2D ⊂ Z^3` is isomorphic as a graph to the 2D lattice `Z^2`. Similarly `Λ_1D ≅ Z^1`. The lattice site algebra at each point of the sublattice inherits from Cl(3) ⊗ Z^3 (or any restriction of it).

### Step 2 — Apply the Ward-normalized Bogoliubov bridge

By
[`CMW_WARD_NORMALIZED_BOGOLIUBOV_BRIDGE_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-06-04.md`](CMW_WARD_NORMALIZED_BOGOLIUBOV_BRIDGE_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-06-04.md),
the W1-W4 hypotheses imply

```text
|m_L|^2 <= (beta C_A C_H) / (2 c_W^2 I_d(L)).
```

By
[`AXIOM_FIRST_COLEMAN_MERMIN_WAGNER_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-04-29.md`](AXIOM_FIRST_COLEMAN_MERMIN_WAGNER_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-04-29.md),
`I_d(L)` diverges for `d = 1` and `d = 2` under the framework lattice
dispersion. Since `dim(Λ_2D) = 2` and `dim(Λ_1D) = 1`, the bridge forces
`m_L -> 0` in both cases, establishing (S1) and (S2).

For completeness, a direct discrete lower bound avoids treating a continuum
proxy or a finite-size fit as the limit proof. Since 2(1-cos x)<=x²,
the single n=1 mode in d=1 gives I_1(L)>=L/(4 pi²). In d=2 retain
the distinct modes (n_1,n_2)=(s,j), 1<=s<=floor(L/4), 0<=j<=s.
Each has E_k<=(2 pi/L)²(s²+j²)<=8 pi² s²/L². Their contribution is
therefore at least

```text
I_2(L) >= (1/(8 pi²)) sum_{s=1}^{floor(L/4)} (s+1)/s²
       >= (1/(8 pi²)) sum_{s=1}^{floor(L/4)} 1/s -> infinity.
```

These bounds suffice for the no-order implication; sharp asymptotic
coefficients and d=3 convergence are not needed for it. ∎

### Step 3 — Continuous no-order conclusion (S3)

If a finite-temperature phase on the sublattice carried a nonzero order
parameter for the continuous symmetry generated by Q, it would be a
continuous-symmetry-broken phase. By (S1)/(S2), that order parameter
vanishes. ∎

### Step 4 — Universality across embeddings (S4)

The IR-sum threshold depends only on the sublattice dimension `d`, not on the
specific coordinate embedding. The framework's Z^3 admits three coordinate orientation families of
planes and three of lines, with infinitely many translates in each family. Step 2's argument applies to each sublattice independently whenever
the restricted Hamiltonian/order pair satisfies W1-W4. ∎

## Corollaries

C1. **Ferromagnetic application is conditional.** For a short-range 2D Heisenberg model, application to its magnetization requires verifying W1-W4 with uniform constants for that order/charge pair. Under those hypotheses its finite-temperature order vanishes.

C2. **Antiferromagnetic application is conditional.** The same conclusion applies to a staggered order parameter only after establishing the appropriate Ward and susceptibility bounds; SU(2) symmetry alone does not supply those bounds here.

C3. **Coupled layers are outside this restriction.** A nonzero inter-layer interaction defines a different, three-dimensional Hamiltonian. This theorem neither proves nor excludes order in that coupled system.

C4. **Finite-temperature boundary.** Under W1-W4, continuous-symmetry order is excluded in d<=2. This does not exclude zero-temperature order or all gapless excitations, and finiteness of the d=3 IR sum does not itself prove SSB or a Goldstone spectrum.

C5. **No dimension selection.** This conditional restriction does not derive the choice of three spatial dimensions or a long-range force law.
