proves continuum ground clustering using the distance that permits passage
inside the bounded defect interval. The second proves distant ground response,
including a norm-unit-ball continuum local-algebra bound and the older
polynomial-resource native bound. The G and L equation labels refer to their
respective unchanged source parts.

including rough compact L2 fields and norm-closed local-algebra elements.

# Independent spatial clustering bound for a bounded local defect

Status: proposed ordinary conditional theorem for the supplied continuum
Dirac CAR construction. This core was derived before exposure to a new root
spatial-correlation or response candidate. The exact ground synthesis and
canonical Local/Current sources are shared inputs, not independently audited
premises or retained claims. No external clustering theorem is invoked: the
bounded-observable gap estimate used below is proved explicitly.

**Result.** Let the bounded selfadjoint even potential `V` belong to the
continuum even algebra of one fixed bounded interval `W`, and let
`||V|| <= K < m/2`. Its unique ground in the supplied full Fock representation
has exponentially decaying connected correlations of compact even observables.
One valid nonoptimal estimate is

\[
 |\omega_V(AB)-\omega_V(A)\omega_V(B)|
 \le 2\|A\|\|B\|\exp\!\left[-\frac{m-K}{v}\,d_W(X,Y)\right],       \tag{G1}
\]

for `A in A(X)`, `B in A(Y)`, where

\[
 d_W(X,Y)=\min\{\operatorname{dist}(X,Y),
                 \operatorname{dist}(X,W)+\operatorname{dist}(Y,W)\}.
                                                                    \tag{G2}
\]

This distance permits an instantaneous passage inside `W`. The original
Euclidean finite-speed commutation property is not generally preserved by
an interaction which is nonlocal within `W`; an explicit counterexample is
retained below. Since `W` is fixed and bounded, (G1) still gives exponential
decay in ordinary separation with only a fixed diameter penalty. It uses no
energy moments of vectors created by rough observables.

## 1. Supplied net, ground, parity and exact hypotheses

Use the time-zero continuum net in canonical Local section 7:
`A(I)` is the norm-closed unital algebra generated by even words of the bounded
Majorana fields `c(f)`, with real `L2(R;R^2)` coefficients supported in the
bounded interval `I`. In particular `||c(f)||=||f||`; every fixed finite
polynomial in these fields is bounded. Different even local algebras commute
at disjoint supports. The free dynamics is

\[
 \alpha_t(c(f))=c(e^{itD}f),\quad
 D(p)=-vp\sigma_x-m\sigma_y,
 \quad H_0=d\Gamma(E),\quad E(p)=\sqrt{m^2+v^2p^2},                 \tag{G3}
\]

implemented by `exp(itH_0)` on the full positive-polarization Fock space.
The physical coefficient support refers to `f`, not to the nonlocal
positive-energy projection of `f` used to realize its field on Fock space.

Local L27-L30 prove, including by `L2` approximation for nonsmooth inputs,

\[
 [\alpha_t(\mathcal A(I)),\alpha_s(\mathcal A(J))]=0
 \quad\text{if }\operatorname{dist}(I,J)>v|t-s|.                  \tag{G4}
\]

The proof uses the Dirac continuity equation with flux bounded by `v` times
density; it is not inferred just from a group-velocity formula. The free
CAR action is point-norm continuous. No norm derivative of an arbitrary
rough local observable is asserted.

Assume now, as a spatial hypothesis in addition to the bounded-potential
conditions in the ground synthesis,

\[
 V=V^*\in\mathcal A(W),\qquad \|V\|\le K<m/2.                    \tag{G5}
\]

This allows an arbitrary bounded element of the even algebra of `W`, including
finite polynomials coupling separated subregions of `W`, and their norm limits.
It is a uniformly bounded **total** interaction, not an extensive sum of bounded
densities. Being a bounded element of global low CAR alone would not imply (G5).

Put `G=H_0+V` on `D(H_0)`. The exact ground synthesis sections 3 and 8 give
selfadjointness on that domain, a simple ground `Omega_V` with energy
`E_g in [-K,K]`, and full-Fock gap at least `delta=m-2K>0`. These facts follow
there by bounded resolvent perturbation and a rank-one Riesz projection;
no compact resolvent or excited discrete spectrum is assumed.
The ground is even. Restricting the same perturbation argument to the even
sector, whose free excited threshold is `2m`, gives

\[
 \gamma=2m-2K>0
 \quad\text{as a lower gap on the even complement of the ground}.  \tag{G6}
\]

We use the full Fock representation throughout. The stronger number `gamma`
is legitimate here because `A,B,A^*,B^*` are even and `Omega_V` is even:
their ground-orthogonal vectors all lie in that sector. It is not a bound
on the entire full-Fock excited spectrum or on arbitrary normal states.
Using only the full gap `delta` instead would give the weaker, also valid,
exponent `delta/(2v)` in (G1).

Write `omega_V(C)=<Omega_V,C Omega_V>` and
`beta_t(C)=exp(itG) C exp(-itG)`. The ground is stationary. Uniqueness here
is in the specified representation, not a classification of ground states
in inequivalent representations. The potential, coupling, interval and
representation remain supplied hypotheses.

## 2. The bounded perturbation's exact causal window

For `t>=0` use the bounded cocycle from the ground synthesis,

\[
 Z(t)=e^{itG}e^{-itH_0},\qquad
 Z'(t)=iZ(t)V_t,\quad V_t=\alpha_t(V),\quad
 \beta_t(A)=Z(t)\alpha_t(A)Z(t)^*.                              \tag{G7}
\]

The derivative is the norm derivative of the cocycle, not of the two separate
unbounded-generator groups. The bounded point-norm continuous function `V_t`
has norm at most `K`. For fixed `C`,
`d_t[Z(t)C Z(t)^*]=i Z(t)[V_t,C]Z(t)^*`.
Iterating this integral equation, and then substituting `C=alpha_t(A)`, gives

\[
 \begin{split}
 \beta_t(A)=\alpha_t(A)+\sum_{n\ge1}i^n
 \int_{0\le s_n\le\cdots\le s_1\le t}
  \operatorname{ad}_{V_{s_n}}\cdots
  \operatorname{ad}_{V_{s_1}}(\alpha_t(A))\,d\mathbf s .
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{G8}
\]

Here `ad_C(D)=[C,D]`; the innermost commutator is at the largest time `s_1`.
Each term has norm at most `(2Kt)^n ||A||/n!`, so the series converges in norm.
This time ordering is important to the support argument.

Let

```text
r=dist(X,Y),  a=dist(X,W),  b=dist(Y,W).
```

Assume `vt<r` and `vt<a+b`. Then `B` commutes with `alpha_t(A)` by (G4).
For each integrand in (G8), there are two possibilities:

- If `v(t-s_1)<a`, then `[V_{s_1},alpha_t(A)]=0` by the free causal relation.
  The entire nested commutator vanishes.
- Otherwise `v s_1<=vt-a<b`. Every `s_j<=s_1` then satisfies `v s_j<b`,
  so `B` commutes with each `V_{s_j}` by (G4). It also commutes with
  `alpha_t(A)`, hence with the whole nested commutator.

In the second case `t>=a/v` is automatic; if `a>vt` the first case applies
to every integrand. Thus every term commutes with `B`, including cases where
`V` has arbitrary internal couplings inside `W`. Norm convergence gives

\[
 [\beta_t(A),B]=0\qquad\text{whenever }v t<d_W(X,Y),\quad t\ge0.    \tag{G9}
\]

For negative times repeat the same bounded-cocycle argument with free flow
`alpha_{-t}` and potential `-V`; positivity is not used in this locality step.
Interchanging `A,B` if needed yields the symmetric statement

\[
 [A,\beta_t(B)]=0\qquad\text{for }v|t|<d_W(X,Y).                  \tag{G10}
\]

This proof does not require `A`, `B` or `V` to lie in the domain of the free
CAR derivation. No commutator with the unbounded `H_0` was taken on a rough
field. Smallness is needed for the gap, not for the bounded Dyson support
calculation itself.

For bounded intervals put `ell=diam(W)`. Choosing points closest to `W` gives
`dist(X,Y)<=dist(X,W)+ell+dist(Y,W)`, and therefore

\[
 d_W(X,Y)\ge[\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)-\ell]_+.                    \tag{G11}
\]

The direct route is also present in (G2). If `X,Y` are on the same side of
`W`, no diameter loss is needed. No claim of optimality of this sufficient
causal window is made for a particular potential.

## 3. A spectral-gap clustering lemma for bounded observables

Here is the exact analytic step, including the regularization that avoids
unproved energy moments. Let `L>=0` be selfadjoint with a normalized ground
`Omega`, and let `P=|Omega><Omega|`, `Q=I-P`. Suppose the spectral measures
of `QA^*Omega`, `QB Omega`, `QB^*Omega`, and `QA Omega` have support in
`[gamma,infinity)`, where `gamma>0`. A full gap of that size suffices;
a sector gap for these four vectors suffices as well.
Let `tau_t(C)=exp(itL) C exp(-itL)`. Assume for some `T>0`

\[
 [A,\tau_t(B)]=0\quad (|t|<T).                                  \tag{G12}
\]

For arbitrary bounded, not necessarily selfadjoint `A,B`, the connected
quantity is

\[
 C=\langle\Omega,AQB\Omega\rangle.
\]

Put `h(t)=<Omega,[A,tau_t(B)]Omega>`. The spectral theorem gives finite
complex measures `mu,nu` supported in `[gamma,infinity)` such that

\[
 h(t)=\int e^{itE}\,d\mu(E)-\int e^{-itE}\,d\nu(E),\quad
 C=\int d\mu(E),\quad
 \|\mu\|_{\rm TV},\|\nu\|_{\rm TV}\le\|A\|\|B\|.                 \tag{G13}
\]

Explicitly `dmu=<QA^*Omega,1_{dE}(L)QB Omega>` and
`dnu=<QB^*Omega,1_{dE}(L)QA Omega>`.
For a partition into disjoint spectral sets, Cauchy-Schwarz on the projected
vector norms bounds the sum of the absolute matrix elements by the product
of their norms. This proves the total-variation bounds. No `E`, `E^2`, or
other moment of either measure is assumed finite.

For `eta>0`, `epsilon>0`, define the absolutely convergent integral

\[
 I_{\eta,\epsilon}=\frac1{2\pi i}
  \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{-\eta t^2}}{t-i\epsilon}h(t)\,dt.        \tag{G14}
\]

Its Fourier kernel can be computed without a singular interchange. Use

\[
 \frac1{t-i\epsilon}=i\int_0^\infty e^{-\epsilon u}e^{-itu}\,du.
\]

For `epsilon>0`, the double integral is absolutely convergent, and the
Gaussian Fourier transform yields

\[
 F_{\eta,\epsilon}(E)=\frac1{2\pi i}
  \int_{\mathbb R}\frac{e^{-\eta t^2}e^{itE}}{t-i\epsilon}\,dt
  =\frac1{2\sqrt{\pi\eta}}\int_0^\infty
       e^{-\epsilon u}e^{-(E-u)^2/(4\eta)}\,du.                 \tag{G15}
\]

Thus `0<=F_{eta,epsilon}(E)<=1` for every real `E`. For fixed `epsilon`,
Fubini with the finite measures in (G13) is legitimate. As `epsilon` tends
to zero, dominated convergence in those measures gives the filter

\[
 F_\eta(E)=\tfrac12\left(1+\operatorname{erf}\frac{E}{2\sqrt\eta}\right).
\]

On the time side, `h(t)=0` for `|t|<T`, so the same limit is the ordinary
absolutely convergent tail integral with denominator `t`:

\[
 I_\eta=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{|t|\ge T}\frac{e^{-\eta t^2}}t h(t)dt.
\]

Indeed `|t-i epsilon|>=|t|`, giving an integrable uniform majorant on the
tail. No principal-value estimate against a rough spectral vector or
unstated differentiability at `t=0` is needed.

For `x>=0`, shifting the Gaussian integration variable shows
`int_x^infinity exp(-u^2)du <= (sqrt(pi)/2) exp(-x^2)`.
Consequently, for `E>=gamma`,

\[
 1-F_\eta(E)\le\tfrac12e^{-\gamma^2/(4\eta)},\qquad
 F_\eta(-E)\le\tfrac12e^{-\gamma^2/(4\eta)}.
\]

Equations (G13)-(G15) give

\[
 |C-I_\eta|\le\|A\|\|B\|e^{-\gamma^2/(4\eta)}.                  \tag{G16}
\]

For the tail use `|h(t)|<=2||A||||B||` and
`int_T^infinity exp(-eta t^2)/t dt <= exp(-eta T^2)/(2 eta T^2)`:

\[
 |I_\eta|\le\frac{\|A\|\|B\|}{\pi\eta T^2}e^{-\eta T^2}.        \tag{G17}
\]

Take `eta=gamma/(2T)`. Combining these bounds with the trivial
`|C|<=||A||||B||` gives

\[
 |C|\le\|A\|\|B\|\min\left\{1,
       \left(1+\frac2{\pi\gamma T}\right)e^{-\gamma T/2}\right\}
 \le2\|A\|\|B\|e^{-\gamma T/2}.                                \tag{G18}
\]

For the last inequality use the trivial bound when `gamma T<=1`, and
`1+2/(pi gamma T)<2` when `gamma T>=1`. The first case works because
`2 exp(-1/2)>1`. For `T=0` the last bound remains valid by the trivial
estimate. This completes the lemma. Its exponent is a sufficient constant,
not a claim of the best possible clustering length.

## 4. Application and a necessary locality limitation

Apply the lemma to `L=G-E_g`, `Omega=Omega_V`, and the even-sector gap
`gamma=2m-2K` from (G6). All four spectral vectors in (G13) are even.
Use `T=d_W(X,Y)/v` from (G10), with the trivial estimate if `d_W=0`.
Equation (G18) proves (G1). By (G11), it also proves

\[
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_V}(A,B)|
 \le2\|A\|\|B\|e^{-(m-K)[\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)-\ell]_+/v}
 \le2e^{(m-K)\ell/v}\|A\|\|B\|e^{-(m-K)\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)/v}.
                                                                    \tag{G19}
\]

This is a continuum equal-time connected two-block bound. It applies to all
bounded elements of the stated even local algebras, and hence to all compact
even `L2` polynomials, with no derivatives or weighted Fourier seminorms.
It is not a lower bound or a universal sharp exponent, and it does not by
itself bound arbitrary higher connected cumulants.

The following explicit test prevents restoring the original Euclidean light
cone without extra structure on `V`. Take separated nonempty subintervals
`X,Y` inside `W`. Choose four orthonormal real smooth compact coefficient
fields, `f_1,f_2` supported in `X` and `f_3,f_4` supported in `Y`, and write
`c_j=c(f_j)`. Then `c_j^2=I` and distinct generators anticommute. For
`0<k<=K` set

\[
 V=i k c_2c_3,\qquad A=i c_1c_2,\qquad B=i c_3c_4.               \tag{G20}
\]

These are even selfadjoint elements of their indicated local algebras;
`||V||=k`, `||A||=||B||=1`. `V` is bilocal within `W`, as allowed by (G5).
Direct CAR multiplication gives

\[
 [V,A]=2k c_1c_3,\quad
 i[[V,A],B]=-4k c_1c_4\ne0.                                    \tag{G21}
\]

For these smooth inputs the free CAR norm derivative of `A` exists.
The bounded perturbation adds `i[V,A]` to that derivative. The free
commutator `[alpha_t(A),B]` vanishes for small `|t|` by (G4). Therefore

\[
 \left.\frac d{dt}[\beta_t(A),B]\right|_{t=0}=-4k c_1c_4,
 \qquad \|[\beta_t(A),B]\|=4k|t|+o(|t|).                        \tag{G22}
\]

The product `c_1c_4` has norm one. Thus the perturbed commutator is nonzero
at arbitrarily small nonzero times despite positive separation of `X,Y`.
Here `d_W(X,Y)=0`, so there is no conflict with (G10). This counterexample
refutes an unmodified strict Euclidean propagation claim under the stated
hypotheses. It does not prove that the diameter loss in every possible
static clustering estimate is optimal. No claim of relativistic microcausality
for an arbitrary internally nonlocal bounded potential is made.

## 5. Uniform translations in the continuum

Let `A_x`, `B_y` be free geometric spatial translates of fixed compact local
observables. Their norms equal `||A||,||B||`, and their supports are `X+x`,
`Y+y`. The interacting ground for a fixed `V in A(W)` need not be translation
invariant. Nevertheless (G1) and (G19) hold for every real `x,y`, with the
same constants `m,v,K,diam(W)` and the actual translated distances. Thus

\[
 \sup_{\operatorname{dist}(X+x,Y+y)\ge R}
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_V}(A_x,B_y)|
 \le2\|A\|\|B\|e^{-(m-K)[R-\ell]_+/v}.                        \tag{G23}
\]

No invariance of the state was used to obtain this uniform spatial bound.
The finite defect has a fixed diameter. Allowing its diameter to grow with
`R` would change the bound; a bound on `||V||` alone does not reinstate the
free Euclidean causal cone. The spatial statement does not assert boost
covariance, an interacting bulk limit, or spatial uniformity for unbounded
operator norms or increasing word degree.

## 6. What the actual finite-spacing comparison supplies

This section is conditional on an actual family in the exact ground synthesis:
`V_c` is a uniformly small bounded even physical-cell potential, its unital
current-carrier images converge to `V tensor I_H`, and the relevant ordinary
fixed compact-cell observables converge in that same norm. A general bounded
continuum element of `A(W)` is not assigned a particular microscopic sampling
map without this additional family specification.

At time zero Current C21 and CAR product continuity give, for the specified
fixed polynomial inputs,

\[
 a_c=\|\widehat A_c-A\otimes I_H\|\to0,\qquad
 b_c=\|\widehat B_c-B\otimes I_H\|\to0.                          \tag{G24}
\]

Assume both actual and target norms are bounded by `M_A,M_B` respectively.
Let `P_{g,c}` be the actual ground density, embedded isometrically in the
common Fock space, and let `P_{g,0}` be the target ground density tensored
with the high vacuum. The source's (39),(43),(44) give, for fixed `mu>K`,

\[
 \begin{split}
 \theta_c:=\|\widehat P_{g,c}-P_{g,0}\|_1
 &\le2\min\{1,\varepsilon_c/d_\mu\},\\
 \varepsilon_c&=\frac{\mu^2c/v+\nu_c}{(\mu-K)^2},\quad
 \nu_c=\|\widehat V_c-V\otimes I_H\|,\\
 d_\mu&=\frac{m-2K}{(\mu+K)(\mu+m-K)}>0.
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{G25}
\]

All these conditions concern the actual sufficiently small spacing range
where the common bound `K<m/2` holds. Convergence to a strictly small target
also permits choosing a slightly larger uniform bound below `m/2` eventually.
The state embedding here is isometric conjugation; observable embeddings in
(G24) are unital CAR maps, not zero extensions of observables.

Split the product expectation and then the two one-point expectations.
The elementary bounds are

```text
|omega_c(A_c B_c)-omega_V(AB)|
  <= theta_c M_A M_B + M_B a_c + M_A b_c,
|omega_c(A_c)-omega_V(A)| <= theta_c M_A + a_c,
|omega_c(B_c)-omega_V(B)| <= theta_c M_B + b_c.
```

It follows that

\[
 \begin{split}
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_c}(A_c,B_c)
       -\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_V}(A,B)|
 &\le3\theta_c M_A M_B+2(M_Ba_c+M_Ab_c),\\
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_c}(A_c,B_c)|
 &\le2M_A M_Be^{-(m-K)d_W(X,Y)/v}
          +3\theta_c M_A M_B+2(M_Ba_c+M_Ab_c).
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{G26}
\]

For every fixed pair of physical profiles this is an actual finite-`c`
comparison and its error tends to zero. It neither states exact finite-`c`
causal commutation nor identifies the band-limited current images with
exactly local continuum elements. Canonical Local L31 itself distinguishes
vanishing limiting commutators from exact microscopic commutation.
No separate microscopic Lieb-Robinson or finite-`c` clustering theorem has
been proved in this core.

For fixed translated profiles with separation tending to infinity only after
the continuum limit, (G23),(G26) yield the valid iterated statement

\[
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{c\downarrow0}
 |\operatorname{Cov}_{\omega_c}(A_c,B_{R,c})|=0                  \tag{G27}
\]

when `dist(X,Y+R)` tends to infinity. A joint choice `R=R(c)` needs the errors
in (G24) to be controlled for that moving family; pointwise C21 does not
provide that control.

On bounded translation parameter sets, there is a useful uniform positive
statement. The translated inputs of a fixed `L2` function form a norm-compact
set when the translation parameter ranges over a compact interval. Since
`J_c C_c` are contractions converging strongly to the low inclusion, convergence
is uniform on that compact set by a finite epsilon-net argument. Finite
polynomial telescoping gives uniform (G24) on bounded translation sets. The
same observation with Current C25 covers an additional fixed compact time
window in the source's dynamical comparisons. This does not cover unbounded
translation parameters or an arbitrary growing observation family.

## 7. An explicit obstruction to uniform current-carrier translations

For precision, the lack of an unbounded-translation coefficient premise is
not merely an absent estimate. Take any nonzero compactly supported real
`L2` field `f` and a fixed `s_0!=0`. Let

```text
N_c = nearest_integer(s_0/c^3),   ell_c=N_c c,
f_c(x)=f(x-ell_c).
```

Then `ell_c c^2 -> s_0`. Integer-cell translation commutes exactly with cell
averaging. Current C30 says that its low current-coordinate multiplier is
`exp(-i ell_c q_c,0(p))`, where `q_c,0(p)=arcsin(cp)/c`, whereas translating
the continuum coefficient has multiplier `exp(-i ell_c p)`.
For each fixed `p`,

\[
 \ell_c(q_{c,0}(p)-p)\longrightarrow s_0p^3/6.                  \tag{G28}
\]

By C21 the untranslated low component tends strongly to `fhat(p)` and the
high component tends to zero. Multiply the compared low components by the
unit modulus `exp(i ell_c p)`. Bounded multiplier convergence on fixed `L2`
vectors, first on compact momentum sets and then by a tail approximation,
gives the exact norm limit

\[
 \|J_c C_c f_c-(f_c,0)\|
 \longrightarrow\|(e^{-is_0p^3/6}-1)\widehat f(p)\|>0.           \tag{G29}
\]

The high component's norm is unaffected by the translation. The phase-one
set on the right is countable and has Lebesgue measure zero, so the limit is
strictly positive for every nonzero `f`. This uses the exact grid-shift
formula and C21; it discards no alias or high-branch contribution by fiat.
It can in particular use a smooth compact input.

Equation (G29) is a failure of a uniform **coefficient norm identification**
over arbitrary translations in this current carrier. It is not by itself a
counterexample to scalar ground-state clustering for the native chain;
scalar correlations can have cancellations or admit a separate microscopic
argument. It prevents using pointwise C21/C25 as an unjustified uniform
moving-observable comparison. No stronger negative conclusion is claimed.

## 2. Ground differentiation on the common unbounded domain

No compact resolvent or discrete excited spectrum is assumed.

Locally in s the resolvent Neumann series is norm analytic. It is also

    H(G_s-z)^-1=I+(z-sV)(G_s-z)^-1.

The contour projection has the same graph regularity. Thus normalized
local ground vectors can be differentiated in that graph space. Choose
their phases horizontally and join the local choices along [0,1].
Differentiating the eigenvalue equation gives

    E_s'=omega_s(V),
    Omega_s'=-R_s Q_s V Omega_s,
    R_s=Q_s(F_s restricted to ran Q_s)^-1 Q_s.     (L7)

V Omega_s, A Omega_s or A* Omega_s to belong to any positive energy domain.

    f'(s)=-<Q_s A* Omega_s,R_s Q_s V Omega_s>

In particular |f'(s)|<=2K||A||/gamma. Integrating on [0,1] gives the
first entries of L4 and L6. For selfadjoint A, L8 is minus twice the
real part of its first term. The minus sign corresponds to the path

We now express L8 through a bounded commutator filter. For fixed s define
finite complex measures on [gamma,infinity) by

    mu_s(B)=<Q_s A* Omega_s,1_B(F_s)Q_s V Omega_s>,

## 3. A no-moment reciprocal filter

For q>0 set w_q(t)=erfc(t/(2sqrt(q))) for t>=0, where
erfc(z)=(2/sqrt(pi))integral_z^infinity exp(-y^2)dy. This nonnegative
weight is integrable, w_q(0)=1, w_q(infinity)=0, and

    integral_0^infinity w_q(t)dt=2sqrt(q/pi),
    w_q(t)<=exp(-t^2/(4q)).

The integral follows by Tonelli; shifting y=z+x and using
exp(-(z+x)^2)<=exp(-z^2)exp(-x^2) for z,x>=0 gives the upper bound.

For omega>0 integration by parts, using
w_q'(t)=-(pi q)^(-1/2)exp(-t^2/(4q)), gives

    integral_0^infinity w_q(t)sin(omega t)dt
       =(1-exp(-q omega^2))/omega=:g_q(omega).       (L11)

The cosine Gaussian integral used here is sqrt(pi q)exp(-q omega^2).
Equivalently L11 follows by differentiating that Gaussian transform and
integrating once; all its time integrals are absolutely convergent.

Define the ordinary absolutely convergent bounded-readout integral

    I_s,q=(i/2) integral_R sign(t) w_q(|t|)h_s(t)dt.

There is no 1/t singularity in this reciprocal filter. Fubini against the
finite variation measures is justified by integrability of w_q. The odd
time kernel sends exp(-it omega) to -2i g_q(omega) and exp(it omega)
to +2i g_q(omega), so

    I_s,q=integral g_q(omega)(dmu_s+dnu_s),
    |f'(s)+I_s,q|
       <=(2K||A||/gamma)exp(-q gamma^2).             (L12)

This is a uniform inverse-energy approximation, not an assumption of a
first excitation moment. In particular the rough compact-L2 witness whose
ground excitation has infinite first moment does not invalidate it.

If ||[beta_s,t(A),V]||<=H_s(|t|), with a nonnegative even-time bound,
then

    |I_s,q|<=integral_0^infinity w_q(t)H_s(t)dt.     (L13)

For any T>0 the elementary Gaussian tail estimate is

    integral_T^infinity w_q(t)dt

It follows by using 1<=t/T in the Gaussian tail. These formulas give both

## 8. Limits of uniformity and negative controls

**Observable resources.** Native L4 is uniform when a and F_A are bounded.
At each fixed c a finite even spin element has a finite CAR expression,
but its F_A can grow with its support or with c^-1. The proof does not
give an expression-independent bound for the entire norm-one native local
algebra when the number of sites diverges. This is a missing stronger
estimate, not a counterexample or an impossibility claim. In contrast
continuum L27 has a genuine norm-only scope because its short-time
commutator is exactly zero for the entire even local algebra.

A useful qualified extension is available. Suppose a native probe family
can, for every eta>0, be approximated within eta in operator norm,
uniformly in c and translations, by even polynomials B_c with resources
a_eta,F_eta independent of those parameters, without enlarging the stated
support intervals. Then

    |(omega_c,1-omega_c,0)(A_c)|
       <=2eta+C_eta exp(-kappa r_c).

Taking the distance to infinity and then eta to zero proves qualitative
uniform local stability for that family. A uniform approximation of this
kind has not been proved for an arbitrary native local-algebra unit ball.
It is not needed for the fixed physical-cell polynomial question.

**Distance and support.** Neither estimate requires a uniform upper bound
on defect interval diameters across a family, provided each interval is
bounded and its entire support remains at distance r from the probe.
A growing interval can, however, approach or contain a probe
far from a chosen origin or center. Distance from that reference point
cannot replace distance from the whole interval. Boundedness of V alone
also cannot replace its spatial support condition.

Here is a concrete control for those last distinctions. Choose a real
smooth normalized compact f=f_1 e_1 and a disjoint spatial translate g, and let
A=i gamma(f)gamma(g). Then ||A||=1 and A is even local. Its excited
free-vacuum component xi is nonzero: the positive-energy vectors of f
and g differ by the nonconstant multiplier exp(-ip d) for a nonzero
translation d and cannot be complex collinear on a nonzero L2 vector.
Their two-particle wedge in A Omega is therefore nonzero. Positivity of
