## 2. Nonuniform-gap domination, with no quantum independence assumption

Choose a computably certified rational q_bar with q<q_bar<1/2 and a rational lambda_bar<1 with |lambda|<=lambda_bar. Choose an integer ell>=1 such that

    lambda_bar^ell <= q_bar-q                              (4)

using a certified positive lower bound on q_bar-q. These choices are effective for computable a,b with the stated strict gaps. They may be expensive near zero bias or slow mixing. They are not inferred from a one-site marginal. Boundary q=0 is permitted; the stationary diagonal is then already all-vacuum, although the same finite circuit remains valid.

For a chain started at x in {0,1}, its one-probability obeys p_(d+1)=a+(1-a-b)p_d. Since q=a+lambda q, induction gives

    P^d(x,1)=q+lambda^d(1_(x=1)-q).                       (5)

For d>=ell its deviation above q is at most |lambda|^d<=lambda_bar^ell, so (5)<=q_bar. Negative lambda and nonuniform gaps are covered; no sign-monotonicity of the original one-step chain is used.

Take any ordered subset t_1<...<t_Q of sites with successive gaps >=ell. Summing the complete Markov law over unobserved sites proves that its selected bits form a nonhomogeneous Markov chain with initial probability q and transitions P^(t_(k+1)-t_k). This summation is a statement about the B marginal diagonal, not about conditional states after measuring R or Z.

For an explicit coupling, let U_1,...,U_Q be independent uniform[0,1] variables. Generate Y_1=1_(U_1<=q), and then Y_k=1_(U_k<=P^gap(Y_(k-1),1)). Let T_k=1_(U_k<=q_bar). The Y vector has exactly the selected-bit law, the T_k are independent Bernoulli(q_bar), and Y_k<=T_k pointwise for every k, since every conditional threshold is at most q_bar. Consequently, for every increasing Boolean function f,

    Pr[f(Y)=1] <= Pr[f(T)=1].                              (6)

This proof does not assume that different selected subsets are independent. It establishes each subset's marginal upper coupling separately. There is also no requirement that the full quantum state be classical: the eventual tested event is the diagonal output of a reversible computational-basis permutation, so its probability depends only on (2).

## 1. Finite Gibbs map and endpoint normalization

Write pi_0=b/(a+b), pi_1=a/(a+b), with 0<a<b<1. Detailed balance is exact:

    pi_0 a = pi_1 b = ab/(a+b).

Consequently D_pi^(1/2) P D_pi^(-1/2) has off-diagonal entries sqrt(ab). Its diagonal entries are 1-a and 1-b. Rewriting one edge energy gives an independent telescoping check:

    -log Q_xy / beta
      = -log P_xy / beta -(log pi_x-log pi_y)/(2 beta).

Summing from i=1 to L-1 and adding the two endpoint energies

    -log pi_(x_1)/(2 beta) -log pi_(x_L)/(2 beta)

leaves precisely

    H_B(x) = [-log pi_(x_1)-sum_i log P_(x_i,x_(i+1))]/beta
           = -log p(x)/beta.

Both endpoints are needed. This proof applies to every one of the 2^L basis vectors; it does not restrict an excitation sector or presume a clean memory state. Since all pi_x and Q_xy are strictly between zero and one, the diagonal eigenvalues of every two-qubit h and every one-qubit g are strictly positive. Their sum is a positive NN Hamiltonian on the full register.

An additional normalization check uses v=(sqrt(pi_0),sqrt(pi_1))^T. Directly, Qv=v and v^T v=1. Thus the finite partition with the endpoint vectors is

    Z = v^T Q^(L-1) v = 1.

Equivalently, summing exp(-beta H_B(x)) sums the row-stochastic Markov law. No Perron limit, infinite-volume boundary prescription, covariance formula, or thermalization theorem is needed. An additive scalar shift kI changes this partition to exp(-beta k), although it does not change the normalized Gibbs distribution.

There is an important distinction between the two meanings of positivity. Q is symmetric and entrywise positive. Similarity to P shows spec(Q)={1,1-a-b}. For example a=2/5, b=4/5 gives q=1/3 and eigenvalue -1/5. Q is then not positive semidefinite. Neither the componentwise logarithm defining h nor the telescoping proof uses a matrix logarithm or assumes Q>=0 as an operator. The source's later positivity statement about h and g is correct. Replacing “positive transfer matrix” by “entrywise-positive transfer matrix” would make this distinction explicit without changing any theorem.

## 2. Every literal coefficient and its price

## 3. Sharp event-TV bound and extensive calibration
