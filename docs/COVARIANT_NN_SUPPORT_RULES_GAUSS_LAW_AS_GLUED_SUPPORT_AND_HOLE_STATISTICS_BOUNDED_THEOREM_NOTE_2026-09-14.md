# Covariant nearest-neighbour support rules: Gauss's law as glued support, and the hole statistics of the order-blind rule

**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Type:** bounded_theorem
**Campaign block:** support-rule (Gauss as glued support), wave A of the TOE
derivation campaign by underdetermination witnesses (design note 2026-09-13).
The block asks whether a covariant nearest-neighbour rule's zero-probability
set can implement a local constraint on finished records whose free part
carries a gauge structure, and which clause, if any, the axioms as written
would need for that to be forced rather than merely possible.

## Result up front

**The success criterion of the block is met, with a supplied role pattern.**
With roles assigned by coordinate parity on the 3x3x3 window (V at all-even
sites, L at one-odd, P at two-odd, C at three-odd), the rotation-covariant
nearest-neighbour support rule "a V site's value equals the sum mod 2 of its
L neighbours" has as zero set a linear code of dimension 19 on 27 sites. That
code is the graph of the divergence map of the superlattice cube Q_3: the 12
links, 6 plaquette sites and the centre are free, and the 8 vertex values are
their divergence. Its glued groups (the minimal codewords, the objects the
landed hierarchy note names glued) are exactly the 28 simple loops of Q_3 (6
of length 4, 16 of length 6, 6 of length 8), the 444 simple paths of Q_3 with
their two endpoint charges (dipoles: 12, 24, 48, 72, 120, 96, 72 by number of
links 1 to 7) and the 7 free P/C units, 479 in all. That is the glued-group
content of a Z_2 gauge theory with matter: Gauss's law appears as an instance
of gluing, exactly as the owner's hierarchy intuition (2026-09-03) put it.

**Without roles the same window carries no such structure.** Every
rotation-covariant linear nearest-neighbour rule on a pure Z_2 or Z_3 site
alphabet is a sum rule a r_v + b (sum of neighbours); on the 2x2x2 cube all
256 covariant binary rules were enumerated, the 41 group-closed ones fall
into 5 subgroups (orders 1, 2, 4, 16, 256), all generated by rotation-invariant
pattern classes, and their glued groups are rigid clusters (weights 1, 4 or 8)
with pair-derived edge field exact and flux dimension 0. The witness pair is
therefore Model P (site alphabet, rigid clusters, flux 0) against Model R
(role-carrying alphabet, 28 loops + 444 dipoles + 7 units, flux 5). Both
satisfy every axiom sentence and the primitives; they differ on the target.

**Order-blind hard support with holes makes the record law order-independent
except for a hole mass.** Read Admissibility's support literally: a site
forming last in a Gauss check takes the value the check forces; a link that is
last in two checks with conflicting demands has empty support and stays
unrecorded (the axioms allow a site to carry no record). Then for every
formation order the fully recorded patterns are exactly the Gauss set, uniform
on it, and all order dependence sits in the hole mass 1 - 2^(-d), where d is
the number of double-closing links. On the 3x3x1 plane the exact histogram of
d over all 9! orders is 0.3, 0.6, 0.1 (d = 0, 1, 2), so E[2^(-d)] = 5/8 and
the hole mass is 3/8. On the cube's 20 V/L sites the exact histogram over all
20! orders has d <= 4 (double closers form a matching of Q_3), E[d] = 12/7 and
E[2^(-d)] = 924167/2598960 = 0.355591, so the hole mass is about 0.644. An
inclusion-exclusion over the 108 matchings of Q_3 reproduces every value.

**Decision points recorded, none adopted.**

1. The roles are next-nearest-neighbour data (landed role-pattern note); the
   Gauss support is nearest-neighbour only once roles are supplied. Either the
   support window widens from nearest-neighbour to nearest-neighbour plus the
   second axial orbit (an axiom-change candidate against Admissibility's
   "nearest-neighbor" wording), or a sector-selection clause picks the
   role-carrying alphabet, or the roles are derived downstream. Wave B
   (possibility-covariance) inherits the third option.
2. For q = 3 the two exterior conventions differ in kernel dimension and the
   pair-derived edge field is not exact (circulation rank 5 and 22 against
   cycle dimensions 5 and 28): 180-degree rotations reverse edges, so covariant
   edge data is symmetric unless the alphabet action is soldered to the lattice
   rotation. Odd-order (U(1)-like) links need a soldered alphabet; this is a
   wave B clause.
3. The hole mass (3/8 on the plane, about 0.644 on the cube, with the exact
   rationals above) is a record statistic that distinguishes formation orders
   under the order-blind rule; it is an input to the clock-and-rate block.

## Premises and declared objects

**Axiom sentences used (canonical text, `docs/MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-06-29.md`).**
Admissibility: "There is one fixed nearest-neighbor admissibility rule,
covariant under lattice translations and proper cubic rotations. For each
site, the probability distribution over the possibilities is determined by,
and varies with, the nearest-neighbor conditions." Support wording: the local
possibility domain's "available"/"admissible" part "denotes its support";
"Record locks a supported realization." Record: "When present, a record locks
exactly one admissible local possibility. A site never carries more than one
record; records are permanent. Only records are readable. A readout value is
determined by record content alone. A site with no record cannot be read." A
site need not carry a record. Lattice: sites Z^3, six nearest neighbours,
translations and the 24 proper cubic rotations. Qubit: finite menus of
distinct pure states of M_2(C) are axiom-compatible alphabets of any finite
size; the alphabets Z_2 and Z_3 below are such menus, and a role-carrying
alphabet is a menu whose elements carry a role label as well as a value.

**Windows.** CUBE = the 2x2x2 window (8 sites, 12 edges, all degree 3); BOX =
the 3x3x3 window (27 sites, 54 edges, degrees 3 at 8 sites, 4 at 12, 5 at 6,
6 at 1); PLANE = the 3x3x1 window (9 sites). Rotations act on the box about
its centre; on the cube about the cube centre.

**Support rule, code, glued group.** A covariant nearest-neighbour support rule
is a rotation-invariant subset Z of the local configurations (own value,
six neighbour values); its zero set on a window is the set of record patterns
whose every in-window local configuration lies in Z. When Z is a subgroup of
Z_q^7 the zero set is a linear code over GF(q). A glued group is a minimal
codeword (a nonzero codeword whose support contains no other nonzero
codeword's support), the object the landed hierarchy note identifies with the
owner's glued neighbourhood conditions. The flux dimension of a code is the
dimension of the image of its edge (pair-derived) field in the cycle space.

**Exterior conventions (A4 Theorem 6 definitions, applied here).**
Exterior-empty: sites outside the window carry the empty value 0, so every
site sees a full 6-star and the divergence-type rule becomes A_W (adjacency)
plus a multiple of I. Exterior-unrecorded: exterior edges carry no field, so
the in-window star appears and the divergence rule becomes D_W + A_W (degree
plus adjacency). Plain sum rules a r_v + b (sum of neighbours) give aI + bA_W
under both conventions; only the divergence-type rule sees the degree.

**Roles (supplied).** On the box, a site's role is fixed by the parity of its
coordinates relative to the centre: V (all even, 8 sites), L (one odd, 12), P
(two odd, 6), C (three odd, 1). Each L site has exactly two V neighbours and
each V site exactly three L neighbours; the V and L sites with their adjacency
form the superlattice cube Q_3 (8 vertices, 12 links). The roles are an input
of this note, not a derived quantity; the landed role-pattern note shows that
such a pattern is a next-nearest-neighbour support rule over roles and that
roles are not record values.

**Model P and Model R.** Model P: pure site alphabet Z_q, support rule a
group-closed covariant nearest-neighbour rule (a sum rule). Model R:
role-carrying alphabet; the support rule is the Gauss check "at each V site,
own value = sum mod 2 of the L neighbours' values" (the divergence of the link
field), no constraint at L, P, C sites. Both are covariant nearest-neighbour
rules on their alphabets.

**Order-blind hard support rule with holes.** Fix a formation order of the V
and L sites (P and C sites are inert). A site's support at its formation time
is the set of values compatible with every Gauss check it completes (a check
is completed by the last of its member sites to form). A V site completing its
own check takes the forced parity; a link completing one check takes the
forced value; a link completing two checks with conflicting demands has empty
support and stays unrecorded (a hole); a site completing no check takes a free
coin value. Holes never feed later checks. d(order) is the number of links that
are last in both of their checks. The fully recorded fraction is 2^(-d).

## Theorems

All statements are exact (GF(2), GF(3) and rational arithmetic) and every
number below is printed and checked by the paired runner.

**Theorem 1 (rotation orbits and windows).** The 24 proper cubic rotations act
transitively on the six nearest-neighbour directions with stabiliser of order
4. The neighbour-pattern orbits number 10 for Z_2 and 57 for Z_3 (Burnside);
the (own value, neighbour orbit) classes number 20 and 171. On the cube every
site sees exactly 3 in-window neighbours, so 8 binary classes are visible
there; on the box the in-window degrees are 3, 4, 5, 6 at 8, 12, 6, 1 sites.

**Theorem 2 (covariant linear nearest-neighbour rules are sum rules).** The
rotation-invariant linear functionals on Z_q^7 (own value and six neighbours)
are exactly a r_v + b (sum of neighbours): 4 functionals on 3 lines for q = 2,
9 on 4 lines for q = 3. The rotation-invariant subgroups of Z_q^7, by
dimension 0 to 7, number 1, 3, 2, 3, 3, 2, 3, 1 (18 in all) for q = 2 and
1, 4, 2, 5, 5, 2, 4, 1 (24) for q = 3; each is closed under sums, and the
neighbour-slot-only summands number 7 and 8. On the cube the 256 covariant
binary rules (one per subset of the 8 visible classes) contain 41 whose
allowed set is closed under addition; these realise 5 distinct subgroups, of
orders 1, 2, 4, 16 and 256, each generated by rotation-invariant pattern
classes, and no group-closed rule lies outside that family.

**Theorem 3 (zero-set kernels, exterior conventions, pair fields).** Kernel
dimensions of the divergence-type rules (A = adjacency, D = degree, I =
identity), brute-forced on the cube and by rank on the box:

| window, q | A | D + A | I + A | I - A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cube, 2 | 0 | 4 | 4 | (= I + A) |
| cube, 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| box, 2 | 7 | 7 | 0 | (= I + A) |
| box, 3 | 9 | 4 | 0 | 0 |

On the cube (degree 3) D + A = I + A for q = 2 and D + A = A for q = 3; on
the box the two exterior conventions differ in kernel dimension only for
q = 3. Plain sum rules a r_v + b (sum of neighbours) equal aI + bA under both
conventions. The incidence matrix has rank 7 on the cube and 26 on the box
(|V| - 1), with kernel the constants for q = 2 and the staggered constants for
q = 3; the cycle spaces have dimension 5 and 28. For q = 2 the pair-derived
edge field of any site code is exact (C B = 0 for the cycle matrix C), so the
flux dimension is 0 against cycle dimensions 5 and 28. For q = 3 the
pair-field circulation has rank 5 and 22, not exact: 180-degree rotations
reverse edges, so covariant edge data on Z_3 is symmetric unless the alphabet
action is soldered to the rotation (decision point 2).

**Theorem 4 (glued groups of Model P are rigid clusters).** The cube cycle
code has dimension 5 (32 words); its minimal codewords are the 28 simple
cycles of the cube graph (6 of length 4, 16 of length 6, 6 of length 8); of
the 31 nonzero words the 3 non-minimal ones are unions of opposite faces. For
the cube site codes over Z_2, the glued groups by subgroup order are: order 1,
none; order 2, one group of weight 8; order 4, two of weight 4; order 16,
fourteen of weight 4; order 256, eight of weight 1. On the box over Z_2 the
kernel of A (dimension 7, 127 supports) has 21 glued groups of weights 6
(eleven) and 8 (ten); the kernel of D + A (dimension 7) has 75 glued groups
with weights 6 to 14 (4, 1, 3, 6, 6, 12, 15, 12, 16). Over Z_3 the kernel of A
(dimension 9, 4837 supports) has 106 glued groups with weights 6 to 10
(10, 8, 34, 36, 18) and the kernel of D + A (dimension 4, 36 supports) has 20
groups with weights 8, 11, 12, 17 (1, 2, 3, 14). None of these families
contains a loop of edges or a path with endpoint charges; every glued group is
a rigid cluster of site values, and the flux dimension is 0 for q = 2.

**Theorem 5 (Model R: the Gauss support is a Z_2 gauge theory with matter).**
With parity roles V = 8, L = 12, P = 6, C = 1, the Gauss check matrices under
the exterior-empty and exterior-unrecorded conventions coincide (every L site
has both of its V neighbours in the window). The Gauss set S_Gauss is the
GF(2) kernel of an 8 x 27 matrix of rank 8, of dimension 19: the 12 link
values, 6 plaquette values and the centre value are free and the 8 vertex
values are their divergence, so S_Gauss is the graph of the divergence map and
has 2^19 records. The V/L subcode has dimension 12. The link-only glued groups
are exactly the 28 simple loops of Q_3 (6, 16, 6 by length 4, 6, 8); the glued
groups with matter are exactly the 444 simple paths of Q_3 with their two
endpoint charges (12, 24, 48, 72, 120, 96, 72 by number of links 1 to 7); the
7 P/C singletons are codewords (free roles). Every codeword splits as a P/C
part plus a V/L codeword. The glued groups number 7 + 28 + 444 = 479. The
pair-derived link field of Model R has flux dimension 5 against 0 for Model P
on the same window.

## No-Go Discipline Gate

The negative content of this note is Theorem 4 together with the second half
of the Result up front: on the 2x2x2 and 3x3x3 windows with pure Z_2 or Z_3
site alphabets, no group-closed rotation-covariant nearest-neighbour support
rule has a Gauss-type zero set; the glued groups are rigid clusters and the
flux dimension is 0 for q = 2. The gate is applied to that content only.

- **N1 alternative routes.** Alphabets Z_q with q >= 4 or composite; covariant
  rules whose allowed set is not a subgroup (on the cube these are the other
  215 of the 256 binary rules, enumerated but not given a glued-group reading
  because minimal codewords are defined for codes); windows larger than 3x3x3
  or periodic; soldered alphabet actions; role-carrying alphabets derived from
  a rotation-covariant possibility domain rather than supplied. Each remains
  open and is named as a next action.
- **N2 wall independence.** The negative statement is a finite computation on
  the stated windows; it uses no Hamiltonian, transfer operator, weights or
  clock, and is independent of any landed no-go.
- **N3 hidden walls.** The parity role assignment is supplied; the two exterior
  conventions are two of the possible boundary treatments; the hard support
  rule with holes is one realisation of Admissibility's support. None of these
  enters the negative statement, which concerns pure site alphabets only.
- **N4 residual matching.** What a pure site alphabet lacks is a distinction
  between check sites and field sites; the residual is exactly the role
  pattern, which the landed role-pattern note places at next-nearest-neighbour
  range. The separating clauses of decision point 1 match this residual.
- **N5 rhetoric audit.** The prose says "on the stated windows and alphabets"
  and names the open routes; no closure or completeness of a search space is
  asserted beyond the enumerations actually performed.
- **N6 partial-closure paths.** A soldered Z_3 action may admit oriented link
  data (Theorem 3's circulation ranks 5 and 22 are the finite trace); periodic
  boundary on the box may change the D + A kernel; both are wave B work.
- **N7 steelman.** Could a non-group-closed covariant binary rule on the cube
  imitate Gauss's law? Its zero set is not a code, so the glued-group reading
  used here does not apply to it; the claim is restricted to group-closed
  rules for that reason and the 215 remaining rules are left as a boundary.
- **N8 cross-cycle echo.** Consistent with the role-pattern note (roles are
  not record values and are next-nearest-neighbour data) and with the
  fermions-U(1)-links note (Gauss's law lands as a support condition with a
  supplied kernel); no landed result is contradicted.

## Falsifiers

- A group-closed covariant nearest-neighbour rule on a pure Z_2 site alphabet
  on the cube whose glued groups include a 4-loop of edges, or whose
  pair-derived field has nonzero flux, falsifies Theorem 4.
- A rotation-invariant linear functional on Z_2^7 or Z_3^7 that is not of the
  form a r_v + b (sum of neighbours) falsifies Theorem 2.
- A Gauss check matrix on the parity-role box that differs between the two
  exterior conventions, or a GF(2) kernel of dimension other than 19,
  falsifies Theorem 5.
- A simple path or loop of Q_3 missing from the glued-group list, or a glued
  group that is neither a loop, a dipole nor a free unit, falsifies the count
  479.
- A formation order on the plane or cube under which the fully recorded
  patterns are not uniform on S_Gauss, or number other than 2^(n-d), falsifies
  Theorem 6; a disagreement between the subset dynamic programme and the
  inclusion-exclusion over matchings falsifies the histograms.
