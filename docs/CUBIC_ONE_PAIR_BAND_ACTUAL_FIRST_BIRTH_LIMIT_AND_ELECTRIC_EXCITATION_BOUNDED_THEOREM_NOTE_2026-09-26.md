## Invariant bounded fields and finite relative support

Every first birth output has |E|<=1. For each overlapping A pair, an outward
move on a vacant-B edge takes E in {0,qa} to {-qa,0}; each adjoint return
empties its occupied B source. Both outward moves precede both adjoint returns.
Edges at finally occupied B sites received no adjoint return. Edges at finally empty B sites
satisfy final D=0. Thus P0 H4 P0 preserves |E|<=1. This proves an invariant
property, not a chosen rotor truncation.

There are two cases, with all nonzero fields viewed as a graph. The star
classification below itself proves finite support, including on the torus.

1. Negative particle on B. All A have q=+. For every vacant B, D=0 makes its
incoming fields nonnegative, and Gauss makes their sum zero, so all vanish.
The only nonzero edges end at the two occupied B sites. Every incident A has
zero divergence and therefore must join both occupied B sites, with opposite
fields f,-f. Two distinct cubic B vertices have at most two common A neighbors.
At the negative B, the f values sum to 1. Since f in {-1,0,1}, the only
possibilities for at most two values are (1) or (1,0). Thus the field is exactly
one two-edge path between the two occupied B sites. There are 30 relative
possibilities: six choices of central A and five of the positive B.

2. Negative particle on A, at n. Both occupied B sites have q=+. At a vacant B,
only the edge from n can have a negative field. Every nonzero vacant-B star
therefore consists of E_(n,b)=-1 and exactly one E_(a,b)=+1 at another A.
In particular such B is adjacent to n. All other active edges end at the two
occupied B sites: there are at most 12 edges through the six active vacant-B
stars and at most 12 through the two occupied B stars. Every connected
component of this finite graph
has total Gauss charge zero. The three nonzero Gauss charges are -2 at n and
+1 at each occupied B, so all three must lie in the same component. Any
additional charge-free component would have neither n nor an occupied B and
hence no permitted nonzero edge. Thus the whole field graph is connected.
A shortest path from n first reaches an occupied B either directly or through
one vacant B and one positive A, in at most 3 edges. Reaching the other occupied
B adds at most 2 edges through a positive A; a route through another vacant B
would return toward n and cannot shorten this bound. Thus both occupied B
sites are within lattice l1 distance 5 of n. Every remaining active A is adjacent
to an occupied B or to a vacant B adjacent to n, so the entire support lies
within distance 6 of n.

## Dispersive-band proof

real-space hopping coefficients are nonpositive. The connected quotient at k=0
has a simple lowest eigenvalue lambda0 and strictly positive normalized vector v
by finite Perron-Frobenius (shift by a sufficiently large scalar first).
For each directed hopping edge (i,j,t), put c_ij(t)=(-H_ij(t))v_i v_j>0;
include i=j when t!=0, and omit the ordinary onsite diagonal. Reverse edges
have equal conductance. The eigenvector equation gives the exact form

 <v z,(H(k)-lambda0)v z>
   = (1/2) sum_(i,j,t) c_ij(t) |z_i-exp(i k.t) z_j|^2.

The norm is sum_i v_i^2 |z_i|^2. At k=0 the sole null direction is constant z.
Finite-dimensional analytic perturbation of a simple eigenvalue then gives

   = (1/2) min_chi sum_(i,j,t) c_ij(t) (k.t+chi_j-chi_i)^2 + O(|k|^4).

Here chi is real, modulo a constant; reality gives lambda(k)=lambda(-k).
The quadratic form could vanish only if k.t+chi_j-chi_i=0 on every hopping
edge. Summing along the explicit return-to-same-internal-state paths with net
translations (1,1,0),(1,0,1),(0,1,1) then forces k=0. Hence its Hessian is
positive definite. The component is invariant under cubic rotations, so the
quadratic term is alpha |k|^2 with alpha>0 in the original coordinate units.

### Projected second moment at long time

In the infinite projected Hilbert space put X_j=i partial_(k_j), so X is the
anchor observable. Let U(u,k)=exp(-iu H(k)). Since phi is constant,

 U(u)^* X_j U(u) phi /u
   = [1/u integral_0^u exp(isH) (partial_j H) exp(-isH) ds] phi.

For each finite Hermitian H(k), the time average converges in matrix norm to

 V_j(k) = sum_(distinct eigenvalues lambda of H(k))
             P_lambda(k) (partial_j H(k)) P_lambda(k).

This is just integration of exp(is(lambda-mu)); unequal eigenvalue terms
vanish and equal terms remain. It requires no uniform interband gap or global
eigenvalue labeling. Each integrand norm is at most ||partial_j H||, uniformly
finite because H is a finite trigonometric polynomial. Dominated convergence
therefore gives convergence in the Bloch L2 norm. In particular,

 C2 := lim_(u→infinity) <|X|^2>_u/u^2
      = integral sum_j ||V_j(k) phi||^2 dk < infinity.

This step is a vector/domain calculation on the localized first vector, not
trace-distance control of an unbounded observable. Its subsequent finite-law
use will involve only the bounded Q effect.

projector. Hellmann–Feynman gives

### Fourth moment and a bounded escape consequence

Set v_j=sup_k ||partial_j H(k)|| and a_ij=sup_k ||partial_i partial_j H(k)||.
These are finite. Differentiating the unitary Duhamel formula twice gives

 ||partial_i partial_j U(u,k)|| <= u*a_ij + u^2*v_i*v_j.

There are two ordered first-derivative insertions, each over a triangle of
area u^2/2; the single second-derivative insertion has interval length u.
The intervening propagators are unitary. The constant initial phi introduces
no derivative terms. Parseval and commuting coordinate multiplication yield

 <|X|^4>_u = sum_ij ||X_i X_j U(u)phi||^2
           <= sum_ij (u*a_ij+u^2*v_i*v_j)^2.

E[Z_u^2]<=D4 for Z_u=|X|^2/u^2. Since C2>0, D4>0. For sufficiently large u,

(1-theta)E[Z] <= E[Z 1_(Z>=theta E[Z])] <= sqrt(E[Z^2] Pr(Z>=theta E[Z])).
Set theta=1/2 and use theta E[Z_u]>=C2/4 to obtain

 Pr(Z_u >= C2/4) >= C2^2/(16 D4) =: q_* >0.

 liminf_(u→infinity) <1_(|X|>=v_* u)>_projected >= q_*.
