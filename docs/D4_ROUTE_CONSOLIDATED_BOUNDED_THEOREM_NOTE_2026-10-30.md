# The D4 event route: consolidated results

**Question.** Can the time-blind D4 event construction replace the cubic tick with a consistent theory of relativistic matter and gravity?

**Status authority.** Independent audit lane only. These are author statements for review.

## What this theme found

**Not yet. The D4 construction gives a partial answer, all in supplied free or linearised models, with time along a root.**

What the kept results support:
- In the Euclidean W(F4)-symmetric weight, the first Lorentz-violating term of free spin 0, 1/2 and 1 fields is at dimension eight; on the scalar kernel, root slicing is exponentially local and axis slicing is not (#10094). A unitary walk on the same events keeps only the 48-element stabiliser and has a dimension-six term (#10096, table only).
- The nearest-neighbour fermion family has no fully positive transfer: in a window of w (including w = 1) it has a positive real sector plus one complex-conjugate pair (#10097). The zoom fixed point, whose weight contains the free continuum Dirac propagator as a stated import, has only real positive poles, but site positivity needs a one-slice buffer, and no finite truncation or rung is pair-free (#10098). Its overlap index equals the abelian flux charge, and the light chiral slab mode has dimension six equal to zero; the slab is vector-like (#10099).
- With one mode per event, nearest-neighbour root hops and the stated cells, no spin-1/2 arises (#10104). The zoom runners use four supplied spinor components per event, a supply that departs from one qubit per site. Whether a one-mode route can avoid it is not decided.
- Hinge-sum gravity carries over at linear order with an extra frame doublet and graviton birefringence (#10100). Under a Lorentzian reading one doublet component is a ghost; a metric-only edge reading removes the pair, but the birefringence was checked only in the Euclidean reading and removal of the Lorentzian cell-scale ghost pair was not run (#10102).
- The matter dimension-eight coefficient is set by the kernel on the events and falls as 16^-n with rung count n (#10111).

Conflict: #10111 and #10112 report different finite-rung working rules (the section "Corrected or replaced claims", entry 1). In supplied U(1) backgrounds at n <= 3, the finite-n chirality error fell by x1.1 to x1.6 per step, which #10112 reads as about 2^-n.

This also leans on table-only results: the remaining one-mode routes (#10105 to #10107; the order-96 Dirac block allows dimension-four gauge anisotropy, and the 64-event cell is untested) and far-wall gap evidence on patches of at most 2x4 sites, with a naive hop and no thermodynamic claim (#10108, #10109, #10113).

Open: mirror gapping on the 3+1D wall; the supplied 16 and families; dynamical links, gauge loops and non-abelian fields; gauge-sector protection; nonlinear gravity and the doublet's graviton coupling; a rule fixing n or selecting the exact fixed point; the image tower versus one qubit per site; record-state Lorentz violation.

### #10097: Nearest-neighbour root-sliced D4 fermion: a positive real sector and one complex-conjugate pair

**Statement (copied from the note, numerical labels turned into plain words).**

Exact results.
- W(F4) has 1152 elements and exactly 24 reflections (12 long-root lines, 12 short lines). A pure time reflection exists only for two direction classes, roots and short directions (e1, (1,1,1,1)); it does not exist for the 24-cell edge directions (2,1,1,0) or face directions (1,1,1,0). Twisted reflections fail, as in #10096. The direction census is exhaustive for pure reflections.
- At root time, k = 0, m = 0, both g_t sectors have discriminant 27648 (144 w^4 - 213 w^2 + 2). The pole pair is complex exactly for w- < w < w+, where (w-)^2, (w+)^2 = (71 -+ 17 sqrt17)/96, i.e. w- = 0.09721 and w+ = 1.21232. The edge moves with m: w+(m) = 1.2139, 1.2330, 1.2660 at m = 0.02, 0.3, 1 (a scratch bisection, not in the runner).
- Exact bosonization (Jordan-Wigner, and BKSF in a fixed flux sector) is a unitary rewriting: on a 6-site chain with a non-normal hop and a complex single-particle pair (Im 0.17), the spin spectrum equals the fermion Fock sums for all 64 levels to 8e-15. So bosonizing a fermion keeps its complex energies, ghosts and dimension-six term. Conversely, a Z2-charged excitation of any W(F4)-invariant reflection-positive bosonic weight is positive by Osterwalder-Schrader reconstruction in that superselection sector (this step uses a supplied literature theorem); W(F4) has 1 quartic scalar, 1 cubic vector covariant (p^2 p) and 2 quintic covariants against the hypercubic group's 2, 2 and 4, so such an excitation has no on-shell dimension-six term. The note does not build a reflection-positive W(F4)-invariant bosonic weight with a fermionic Z2 charge; the string attachment may make W(F4) act projectively.

Finite diagnostics in the supplied family.
- Short time (e1): real negative-norm branches at every w tried (0.5, 1, 1.2, 2; wrong-sign fraction 1.00). Edge time (2,1,1,0): the poles are not closed under conjugation, so no reflection-compatible transfer exists.

Outside the window (w = 0.05 and 1.25) a real ghost appears (wrong-sign 0.49 and 0.67).
- The light branch at w = 0.5, 1, 1.2 has v = 1 and the k^2 coefficient of v - 1 is below 1.1e-9 in the axis, face, body and generic directions, so dimension six is zero in the positive sector.
- At w = 1 the pair never meets the real sector on real k (minimum distance 0.254), so the spectral projector onto the real sector is analytic on the real torus and the projected H = -ln T is exponentially local. Every branch has rank 2. The real sector consists of the light Dirac branch plus a positive staggered branch with lam < 0, which is why the transfer is T^2 rather than T.
- Coarse slices n -> b n: the indefinite weight of the Hankel form is 4.5e-4, 7.4e-7 and 1.3e-13 at b = 1, 2, 4. It falls exponentially with the rung factor and is never exactly zero.

What the note concludes in its scope. For w- < w < w+ the nearest-neighbour family along a root has a positive, transfer-invariant real sector that contains the light fermion; the free theory's only defect is one complex-conjugate (Lee-Wick) pair. The note states that the broad no-go "no lattice fermion with W(F4) symmetry and reflection positivity" is false as stated, on the basis of the positive real sector above (a finite diagnostic) and of the existence argument below; what survives is the narrow statement that no finite-range nearest-neighbour W(F4) fermion of this family has a fully positive transfer in any reflection-symmetric direction (scope: the family D(w, m), the second shell sampled in #10096, root and short directions). A third item, an H4 quasicrystal, is a feasibility remark only and was not tested.

The note's pre-registered criterion required all poles real; that is not met because of the pair, and the note says so.
