## 3. General Two-Atom Row And Epsilon Falsifier

For any row with two supported outcome possibilities write the most general
tested unnormalized atom-weight deformation as

\[
 \omega_+=\omega(1+\epsilon),\qquad
 \omega_-=\omega(1-\epsilon),
 \quad \omega>0,\quad |\epsilon|<1.                       \tag{2}
\]

The conditional write probabilities are

\[
 p_+={\omega_+\over\omega_++\omega_-}
     ={1+\epsilon\over2},\qquad
 p_-={1-\epsilon\over2}.                                 \tag{3}
\]

No numerical half is placed in the primitive row. If a represented
automorphism `Theta` fixes the complete input and covariance gives
`omega(Theta input,Theta output)=omega(input,output)`, while `Theta` exchanges
the two exhaustive outputs, then `omega_+=omega_-`. Equation (2) gives
`epsilon=0`; normalization then derives the two equal probabilities.

Conversely, spatial covariance or global complement covariance that maps the
input to a different input need not kill `epsilon`. Defining `Theta` only as
"swap the weights" is circular. The carrier action and its complete-input
fixed-point property must be established first.

These are conditional probability masses after normalization, not kinetic
exit rates. The common scale `omega` has no physical meaning here. A generator,
action, or clock law would be required before any waiting-time claim.

The classification here concerns a local Admissibility row. A global
microscopic action that produces this row is not derived. A globally
commuting involution of every later compiler stage would be a stronger
theorem and generally fails once asymmetric preparations are compiled.

Combined with (1), this proves the five positive rows in the headline table.
The executable checker constructs exactly those five witnesses and rejects an
oriented-axis mutation and an internally noninvariant identical-guard

There is also an exact all-axis classification. Write the preparation Bloch
vector as `s` and the unoriented menu axis as the unit vector `n`. Any
orthogonal internal action `R` that fixes the preparation and exchanges the

\[
 s\mathbin{\cdot}n=(Rs)\mathbin{\cdot}(Rn)
 =s\mathbin{\cdot}(-n)=-s\mathbin{\cdot}n.
\]

Hence `s dot n=0` is necessary. It is also sufficient within represented
proper internal rotations: for nonzero `s`, the pi rotation about `s` sends
every perpendicular `n` to `-n`; for `s=0`, a pi rotation about any axis
perpendicular to `n` does so. Thus `I/2` admits a sign swap for every binary
axis, while `P_z` admits one exactly on the equator `n_z=0`. The Pauli table
contains five positive rows because it samples three axes for each of those

## 5. Direct-Preparation Symmetry Obstruction

\[
 \operatorname{spec}(I/2)=\{1/2,1/2\},\qquad
 \operatorname{spec}(P_z)=\{1,0\},                        \tag{4}

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}[(I/2)^2]=1/2,

Every listed automorphism preserves these invariants. Therefore a direct

## 7. Why The Directional Cubic Survives

For pure preparation `P_z` and a binary effect direction `n`, covariance under
the stabilizer of `P_z` preserves `n_z`. Exchanging `P(n)` with its complement
requires `n -> -n`, which changes `n_z` to `-n_z`. An input-fixing exchange can
therefore exist in the interior only when `n_z=0`. At `n_z=1`, the support
rule can make the row deterministic. For

\[
 0<|n_z|<1,                                               \tag{10}
\]

neither mechanism selects a value.

In particular, at the current-main comparison direction

\[
 m=(\sqrt3/2,0,1/2),                                      \tag{11}
\]

the Born/affine row is `3/4` and the directional cubic is `9/16`. Both obey
binary complement normalization, `Z`-axis stabilizer covariance, endpoint
support, and the equatorial fair-row condition. In the atom-weight coordinates (2),
they are simply

```text
affine: epsilon = 1/2,
cubic:  epsilon = 1/8.
```

The Pauli axes sample only `n_z=0` and `n_z=1`, where the two laws agree.
Consequently the selected one-shot delayed `X/Y/Z` protocol does not
discriminate the live cubic.
This is not a claim that the cubic is physically correct. It is a proof that
the selected symmetry experiment cannot eliminate it.

More generally, every odd-power family

\[
 g_{2k+1}(n)={1+n_z^{2k+1}\over2}                         \tag{12}
\]

passes the same binary endpoint/equator conditions and disagrees at (11).
The existing scaled-projector theorem removes these families only after the
