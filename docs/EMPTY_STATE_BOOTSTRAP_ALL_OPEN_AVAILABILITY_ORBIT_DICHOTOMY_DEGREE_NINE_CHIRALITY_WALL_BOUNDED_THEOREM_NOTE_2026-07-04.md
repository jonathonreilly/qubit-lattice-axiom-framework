## Answer

1. **Bootstrap constraint (Theorem 1).** The all-open pattern is fixed by
   every direction permutation (all 48), so rule covariance under proper
   cubic rotations forces `A0` to be **proper-cubic-invariant as a set**
   of contents (contents transform as polar vectors under the coupled
   action). The first records — whose occurrence is now axiom content —

2. **Orbit dichotomy (Theorem 2).** Proper-cubic orbits on the content
   sphere split exactly: orbits through **improper-stabilized points**
   (the mirror loci — axes, corners, edge midpoints, general mirror-plane
   points; orbit sizes 6, 8, 12, 24) are inversion-invariant — achiral;
   **generic 24-point orbits are chiral**, their inversion images being
   disjoint twin orbits. `A0` is achiral exactly when its generic orbits
   appear twin-paired; the twins are connected by no supplied
   transformation (verified exhaustively on model sets).

3. **The degree-nine det-channel wall (Theorem 3).** The det-character multiplicity
   in the spherical harmonics of degree `l` under the full cubic group is
   **zero for every `l <= 8` and one at `l = 9`** (exact character sums,
   cross-checked by direct det-weighted group-averaging of generic
   polynomials). Hence no polynomial transforming in the full-cubic
   det-character channel exists at degree eight or less. The unique minimal
   harmonic in that channel is the `l = 9` chiral cubic harmonic

   ```text
   Psi9(v) = x y z (x^2 - y^2)(y^2 - z^2)(z^2 - x^2),
   ```

   which transforms by the det character under all 48 elements and whose
   zero locus is **exactly the mirror loci**: pointwise,
   `Psi9(v) != 0` if and only if `v`'s orbit is chiral. This is not a claim
   about arbitrary low-degree functions: for example, a coordinate function
   can distinguish some improper pairs, but it imports a direction and does
   not transform as a cubic pseudoscalar.

4. **The bit and the readout face (Theorem 4).** For a chiral model
   `A0`, the twin selector is `sign(Psi9)`: it flips under the twin swap,
   flips under the frame swap, and the paired object is invariant under
   the simultaneous flip — the selection is the same single orientation

   content triple product. For achiral (twin-paired or mirror-locus)
   model sets the selector vanishes identically. And any additive scalar

   global content flip, while the configuration's `Psi9` data flips. This
   blindness is conditional on the extra even-per-record premise; Record
   requires additivity and content dependence but does not require an even
   readout. This construction therefore proves no orientation-blindness of
   the named readout.

## Theorem statements and proofs

### Theorem 1 (the first availability set is proper-cubic-invariant)

*Proof.* The empty state is the record-free configuration (`I(empty)=0`;
a state is a configuration of records), so every site's neighbor
condition pattern is all-open — a single constant pattern fixed by every
direction permutation (A1, literal). Rule covariance under proper cubic
rotations then gives `g . A0 = A0` for every proper `g` acting on
contents. "Records form." makes the first step non-vacuous: records
occur, and each locks an element of an availability set; for the first
records that set is `A0`. The distribution supplies the lock-outcome odds;
the realized draw and the formation site/rate remain outside this theorem.

### Theorem 2 (orbit dichotomy and the twin pair)

*Proof.* Orbit sizes and the achirality criterion are computed: an orbit
is inversion-invariant exactly when its point is fixed by some improper
element (five representatives and thirty random points, two independent
code paths, A2-A3). Model availability sets witness the dichotomy: a
generic orbit added to an axis orbit is chiral; adding its twin, or using
corner orbits, is achiral (B1). The chiral model and its inversion image
are both proper-invariant, distinct, and no proper element maps one onto
the other (B2, exhaustive): fixing one twin is a selection no supplied
structure determines.

### Theorem 3 (the degree-nine wall and the unique detector)

*Proof.* Character path: the det-character multiplicity
`(1/48) sum det(g) chi_l(g)` vanishes for `l = 0..8` and equals one at
`l = 9` (C1). Projection path, independently: det-weighted group
averages of generic degree-7 and degree-8 polynomials vanish
identically, while a generic degree-9 polynomial projects onto a nonzero
multiple of `Psi9` (C3, ratio constant across evaluation points). `Psi9`
transforms by the det character (C2); its zero locus is exactly the
mirror loci, pointwise equivalent to the improper-stabilizer criterion
(C4-C5). Since polynomials of degree `<= 8` decompose into harmonics of
degree `<= 8`, no such polynomial has a det-character component. Arbitrary
polynomials need not transform in that channel and are not covered by this
conclusion.

### Theorem 4 (the selector is the bit; an even readout is blind to it)

*Proof.* `sign(Psi9)` supplies the twin selector: the paired
selector-times-frame-density object is invariant under the simultaneous
twin-and-frame flip and flips under either alone (D1); the orbit-sum
selector vanishes exactly on the achiral model sets and is nonzero on
the chiral one. Additive readouts built from even per-record functions
are invariant under the global content flip while the configuration's
`Psi9` data flips (D2): registered, not read by that conditional class. The
evenness premise is not supplied by Record, so no conclusion about the
framework's named readout follows without a bridge.
