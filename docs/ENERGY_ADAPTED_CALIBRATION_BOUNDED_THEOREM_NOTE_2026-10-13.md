## 1. Quantified result and precise map

**Conditional analytic theorem.** There are linear maps

## 2. The all-alias formula from the physical cell integral

For `q in B_a=(-pi/a,pi/a)`, define

    xi_l(q)=q+2pi l/a,
    s_a(xi)=exp(i a xi/2) sinc(a xi/2).

The exact identity, with all aliases retained, is

    (L_a C_a f)(q) = sum_(l in Z) s_a(xi_l(q)) g(xi_l(q)). (6)

A direct inverse-Fourier-series check supplies its normalization and
phase independently of a formal Poisson-delta manipulation. The
inverse lattice transform of the right side is

    sqrt(a/(2pi)) integral_R s_a(xi) g(xi) exp(i a j xi) dxi
      = a^(-1/2) integral_0^a f(aj+x) dx,

because `s_a(xi)=a^(-1) integral_0^a exp(i x xi) dx`.
The phase is positive, and the displayed prefactors cancel exactly.
For Schwartz `g`, periodization is absolutely convergent and the
interchanges above are justified by decay. Equality first holds as a
Fourier-series identity and hence in the lattice L2 space.

Use the source's definitions

    p_a(q)=2 sin(aq/2)/a,       q_a(p)=2 arcsin(ap/2)/a,
    w_a(p)=sqrt(1-a^2 p^2/4), I_a=(-2/a,2/a).

Then on `I_a` the actual Fourier output is

    (L T_a f)(p) = F(p)* w_a(p)^(-1/2) F_a(q_a(p))
                     sum_l s_a(xi_l(q_a(p))) g(xi_l(q_a(p))), (7)

and it is zero outside. No alias multiplicity factor is inserted.
Denote by `H_a g` the `l=0` term of (7), including extension by zero,
and by `R_a^alias g` the sum of all remaining terms.

### Global alias bound

For `l!=0` and `q in B_a`,

    |xi_l(q)| >= pi(2|l|-1)/a,    |s_a(xi_l(q))| <= 1.

For every integer `M>1`, the sum of the Schwartz upper bounds therefore
gives, uniformly in `q`,

    |sum_(l!=0) s_a(xi_l(q)) g(xi_l(q))|
        <= c_M p_(M,0)(f) a^M.                     (8)

The two frames in (7) are unitary. Crucially, `dp=w_a(p)dq` at
`p=p_a(q)`. Thus the square of `w_a^(-1/2)` cancels exactly in the
L2 change of variables, and

    ||R_a^alias g||_(L2(dp))
      = ||sum_(l!=0) s_a(xi_l(q))g(xi_l(q))||_(L2(B_a,dq))
      <= sqrt(2pi) c_M p_(M,0)(f) a^(M-1/2).        (9)

Equation (9) controls the full alias sum and both endpoints. It does not
bound `w_a^(-1/2)` in the supremum norm, which would be false. Since
`M` is arbitrary for Schwartz data, aliases are smaller than any chosen
finite power of `a` in L2. The data need not have compact Fourier support.

Here `J_a` on the left of (10) is the source's scalar coupling, not the

## 4. Expanding cutoff and a uniform Taylor estimate

Fix a small positive `a` and keep the Fourier interval

    D_a={p: |p|<=1/a}                               (15)

fixed throughout the following Taylor argument. For `0<=s<=a` and
`p in D_a`, `|sp|<=1`, so `w_s>=sqrt(3)/2`.
All the formulas in (13) extend smoothly to `s=0`, with
`q_0(p)=p`, `M_0(p)=I`. Define on this interval

    b(s,p)=M_s(p) g(q_s(p)).                        (16)

Only this interior formula is differentiated. The moving indicator of
`I_s` is never differentiated, and no assertion about the derivative of
the full output at a positive spacing is used.

Here are explicit polynomial bounds establishing the needed uniformity.
Write `Q(t)=2 arcsin(t/2)/t`, extended by `Q(0)=1`; then
`q_s(p)=p Q(sp)`. Bounded derivatives of `Q` on `[-1,1]` imply

    |partial_s^j q_s(p)| <= c_j <p>^(j+1),
    |q_s(p)| >= |p|.                               (17)

All `s` derivatives of `V_s` are uniformly bounded for `0<=s<=1`.
The derivatives of `w_s^(-1)`, `h_s`, and `r_s` have the corresponding
polynomial bounds in `p`, because they are smooth functions of `sp`
on this compact interval. In particular, if
`y_s=V_s p/(m w_s)`,

    |partial_s^j y_s| <= c_(j,m,v) <p>^(j+1).

Every derivative of `arctan` of positive order is bounded on the real
line. Applying the chain rule to `beta_s=arctan(y_s)` gives, for `j>=1`,

    |partial_s^j beta_s| <= c_(j,m,v) <p>^(2j).

Indeed each product in that derivative has total differentiation order
`j` and at most `j` factors. Product and chain rules applied to (13)
then yield

    ||partial_s^j M_s(p)|| <= c_(j,m,v) <p>^(2j),

including a uniform bound when `j=0`. Combining this with (17) and
the chain rule for `g(q_s(p))` proves

    |partial_s^k b(s,p)|
       <= c_(k,m,v) <p>^(2k)
                    sum_(r=0)^k |g^(r)(q_s(p))|.    (18)

For `k=N+1`, (17), (18), and (2) imply the integrable uniform estimate

    |partial_s^(N+1) b(s,p)|
       <= c_(N,m,v) p_(2N+4,N+1)(f) <p>^(-2).       (19)

Define the coefficients globally, for each fixed `p`, by the germ at zero:

    (L B_k f)(p) = (1/k!) partial_s^k b(s,p)|_(s=0).  (20)

These germs exist for every `p`, since a neighborhood of `s=0` always
satisfies `|sp|<1`. Taylor's integral remainder, Minkowski's inequality,
and (19) give

    ||1_(D_a)(H_a g - sum_(k=0)^N a^k L B_k f)||_2
       <= c_(N,m,v) p_(2N+4,N+1)(f) a^(N+1).        (21)

The constant does not grow with `1/a`. This uniform bound, on an
expanding interval, is the step that a fixed compact-frequency Taylor
estimate would not supply.

Each coefficient in (20) is a finite sum of derivatives of `g` with
polynomial coefficients in `p`. One way to see polynomiality, without
a large formal expansion, is to use (14): the Taylor coefficients of
`V_s`, `w_s`, and `(V_s+v w_s)^(-1)` are polynomials in `p` with
constants depending on `m,v`; the latter denominator equals `2v` at
zero. Composing the arcsine series preserves this property. The same
is true of `r_s`, `h_s`, and `q_s`; the finite chain rule in (20)
finishes the claim. Therefore `B_k` preserves Schwartz space. This
argument uses finite jets only, not convergence of an infinite series
after integration over all `p`.

finite form

    (S_n f)hat = sum_(d=0)^n P_(n,d)(p) g^(d)(p),
    deg P_(n,d)<=2n.                                  (W15)

Here P_(n,d) are constant-matrix polynomial coefficients. One way to
verify the degree bound is to combine the polynomial jets from W12 with
the chain-rule count in W13: a multiplier jet of order j has degree at
most 2j, and a product of coordinate jets of total order n-j has degree
at most 2(n-j). Both degree and derivative-order bounds are conservative,
not claims of minimal order. Consequently, for M>=0 and L>=0,

    P_(M,L)(S_n f) <= C_(n,L,M,m,v)
                              P_(M+2n,L+n)(f).        (W16)

Differentiating a polynomial coefficient does not increase its degree,
which proves this estimate directly by Leibniz's rule. Realness follows
from q_s(-p)=-q_s(p) and M_s(-p)=overline(M_s(p)). Fourier polynomial
differential expressions become spatial differential operators with
polynomial x coefficients; they preserve smooth compact support.

## 4. Weighted global remainder with aliases and endpoints retained

The strict inequality in W1 avoids a logarithmic borderline in this

For aliases, the full-source bound |xi_l(q)|>=pi(2|l|-1)/a for l!=0

Use W7 for this alias sum alone and the exact band bound

For the entire central edge region, exact change of variables gives

    ||1_(R\D_a) E^r H_a g||_2^2

because |p_a(q)|<=|q| and E_a(q)<=E(q). Thus

    ||1_(R\D_a) E^r H_a g||_2

This covers the whole remainder of I_a up to both endpoints, with its

## 5. The local inverse in the same graph norm

The recursion W3 is an ordered right formal inverse. Its coefficient
identity is

    sum_(j+k=n) S_j R_k = I if n=0, and 0 if n>=1.     (W22)

The order matters because polynomial multiplication and Fourier
differentiation do not commute. Induction using W15 shows

    (R_k f)hat=sum_(d=0)^k Q_(k,d)(p) g^(d)(p),
    deg Q_(k,d)<=2k,
    P_(M',L)(R_k f)<=C P_(M'+2k,L+k)(f), M'>=0.       (W23)

In a composition, derivatives falling on a polynomial coefficient only
lower its degree; the maximum degree adds and the maximum differential
order adds. This proves W23 without assuming commutativity or an inverse
on a Banach space. Every R_k is real and support preserving for smooth
compact inputs.

For each k<=N, apply W2 to the fixed input R_k f at order N-k with

    M_k=M-2k,  L_k=N-k+1.

W1 implies M_k>2(N-k)+r+5/2, so this is an allowed positive exponent.
Multiply the resulting error by a^k. By W23 its resource is bounded by

    P_(M-2k,N-k+1)(R_k f) <= C P_(M,N+1)(f).

Each of the finitely many errors is consequently O(a^(N+1)) in H_r
with the same displayed resource. All coefficients through order N
cancel by W22, proving W4. The argument never applies a merely
fixed-input theorem to an uncontrolled a-dependent family: it treats
the finitely many fixed R_k f separately and accounts for their exact
resource losses.

Realness and support are inherited term by term. The finite physical
cell inputs C_a f_a^[N] live on cells meeting supp(f); their spin hull
is contained in the same interval enlarged by at most a cell width.
The number of lattice sites may grow. The exact J_a image remains
band limited and generally nonlocal. Thus graph-norm calibration does
not create an exact spatial, current or boost intertwiner.

## 6. Separate energy and coefficient checks

Three checks help distinguish a true energy-graph estimate from an

      <=m^2||f||^2+v^2||f'||^2=||f||_1^2.             (W24)

## 7. Immediate finite-CAR free-commutator consequence

Use the canonical real CAR convention ||gamma(h)||=||h|| for real h.
The continuum free flow is
