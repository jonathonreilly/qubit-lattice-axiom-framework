## 2. A normalized positive kernel and a triangular packet

For integer m>=1, set `a_k=(m-|k|)_+`, on integer k, and

    Q_m=sum_k a_k^2 = m(2m^2+1)/3,
    g_m(x)=sum_k a_k exp(i k omega x)
          = |sum_(l=0)^(m-1) exp(i l omega x)|^2,
    J_m(x)=g_m(x)^2/(S Q_m),
    alpha_m(x)=g_m(x)/sqrt(S Q_m).                                  (5)

Thus J_m is nonnegative, has integral one and Fourier degree **2(m-1)**; alpha_m is a normalized wavefunction of Fourier radius **m-1** and `|alpha_m(x)|^2=J_m(x)`. Its momentum amplitudes are `a_k/sqrt(Q_m)`. These two bandwidths must not be confused. With the convention `F_(m-1)=g_m/(Sm)`, the normalization is

    J_m = (S m^2/Q_m) F_(m-1)^2.                                    (6)

Squaring a normalized density without this factor does not preserve its integral or a convex-average norm bound.

For all integer j define `q_j=sum_k a_k a_(k+j)` and `w_j=q_j/Q_m`. They are even and vanish for |j|>2m-2. For j>=0 their exact finite formula is

    q_j = Q_m - m j^2 + (j^3-j)/2,        0<=j<=m;
    q_j = [(2m-j)^3-(2m-j)]/6,           m<=j<=2m;
    q_j = 0,                             j>=2m.                      (7)

The two expressions agree at m, and the last two points of the second expression are zero. One proof uses the second difference of a: it is +1 at -m, -2 at zero and +1 at m. Hence the second difference of q is `a_(j+m)-2a_j+a_(j-m)`, equal to `-2m+3j` on 0<=j<=m and `2m-j` on m<=j<=2m. The cubic formulas have these differences and the zero boundary values; symmetry fixes the value at zero. Equivalently, expand the finite sums. In particular

    q_0=Q_m,   q_1=Q_m-m,
    integral J_m(x) cos(omega x) dx = 1-3/(2m^2+1).                  (8)

A distinct short control of (8) is the discrete gradient identity

    2(Q_m-q_1)=sum_k (a_k-a_(k-1))^2 = 2m.                           (9)

There are exactly 2m unit increments, including both zero-extension endpoints. This also shows why abruptly cutting a flat amplitude sequence has a different moment cost.

Use the representative |x|<=S/2 and circular distance |x|_S. Since `sin(pi|x|/S)>=2|x|/S`, (8) gives

    integral |x|_S^2 J_m(x) dx <= 3S^2/[8(2m^2+1)] =: C_m^2,
    integral |x|_S J_m(x) dx <= C_m,
    C_m <= sqrt(3)S/(4m).                                           (10)

The second line is Cauchy–Schwarz against the normalized positive density. This exact cosine-moment proof, not a numerically fitted decay, yields the new 1/m scale.
