## Inline argument

We establish the three sub-claims (4a-i), (4a-ii), (4a-iii) by direct
construction on finite blocks.

### Sub-claim (4a-i): signed-record incarnation matches additive shift form

The `YT_SOURCE_ACTION_SUPPORT_PACKET_NOTE_2026-05-22` proves the exact
finite-support identity on `epsilon_x ∈ {-1, +1}`:

```text
R_h(epsilon)  :=  exp(sum_x h_x epsilon_x) / Z(h)                       (RN)
```

with `Z(h) = sum_epsilon exp(sum_x h_x epsilon_x)`, and the equivalent
source-coupled site-diagonal local action

```text
S_h(epsilon)  :=  S_0(epsilon) - sum_x h_x epsilon_x                    (action)
```

generates the same RN family relative to `S_0`. The `retained_bounded`
identity is the structural step: the source enters `S_h` as an
**additive diagonal scalar shift** at each site (the term
`- h_x epsilon_x` couples site `x` to source `h_x` only, and is
diagonal in the site basis).

This is the Factor 4a additive-shift form `(4a-shape)` restricted to the
scalar signed-record surface. The "diagonal" structure on the signed-
record side is the site-by-site decomposition; on the operator side
of (4a-shape), it is `diag(j_1, ..., j_n)`. The two are the same algebraic
shape on the two surfaces: per-site source `j_x` (or `h_x`) couples
linearly to a site-local diagonal projector (here `P_x = e_x e_x^T` or
the equivalent indicator `epsilon_x ↔ \delta_{epsilon=epsilon_x}`).

### Sub-claim (4a-ii): supplied formal `A+B C` operator form matches additive shift form

The `CHARGED_LEPTON_TWO_HIGGS_CANONICAL_REDUCTION_NOTE` proves that every
**supplied** distinct-offset matrix of its stated class reduces under explicit
matrix relabelings and a specified diagonal phase action to the canonical form

```text
Y  =  A + B C,       A, B diagonal, C = displayed 3-cycle.             (Y-form)
```

After phase reduction, the generic normal form is

```text
Y_can = diag(x_1, x_2, x_3) + diag(y_1, y_2, y_3 e^{i delta}) C.
```

The diagonal part `A = diag(x_1, x_2, x_3)` is precisely the
site-diagonal block of the operator. Source couplings that take
`(A, B) ↦ (A + diag(j), B)` produce the additive shift
`Y ↦ Y + diag(j)`, which is precisely `M(j) = D + J(j)` with
`D = Y` and `J(j) = diag(j_1, j_2, j_3)` on the supplied index
surface.

The shift class `(A, B) ↦ (A + diag(j), B)` is the unique
site-diagonal scalar-source shift compatible with the canonical
support class `Y = A + B C`: any non-diagonal scalar source
would either mix the `A` and `B C` summands (breaking the
canonical support class) or fail to be site-local on the generation
index. Verified by direct enumeration of shift classes on the
canonical support algebra (runner T4, T5).

### Sub-claim (4a-iii): uniqueness modulo basis

Consider the most general site-local linear scalar-source coupling
on a finite scalar-source surface with `n` sites:

```text
M(j)  -  M(0)  =  sum_x j_x · Q_x.                                     (general)
```

A coupling is **site-local** if `Q_x` acts non-trivially only on site
`x` (i.e., `(Q_x)_{y, z} = 0` unless both `y = x` and `z = x`, after
basis fixing). A coupling is **scalar-source** if each `j_x ∈ R`
couples linearly with one site-local operator `Q_x`. With these two
restrictions, `Q_x` has at most one nonzero entry, at position
`(x, x)`. Normalizing this entry to `1` gives `Q_x = P_x = e_x e_x^T`,
the canonical site-`x` diagonal projector. Therefore the unique
site-local linear scalar-source coupling is

```text
M(j)  -  M(0)  =  sum_x j_x · P_x  =  diag(j_1, ..., j_n)  =  J(j),    (unique)
```

which is exactly `(4a-shape)`.

This is a basis-fixing argument: it does not derive the choice of basis
in which the surface is "site-local"; that choice is supplied by the
retained authorities (epsilon-basis for YT, generation-basis for two-
Higgs). Within each fixed basis, the additive site-diagonal shift is
the only compatible structural form.

### Honest scope of (4a-iii)

(4a-iii) is a basis-modulo uniqueness statement, not a derivation of
the basis. The two retained authorities each fix a natural basis on
their respective surface: YT fixes the signed-record basis `{e_+, e_-}^n`;
two-Higgs fixes the generation-basis `{e_1, e_2, e_3}`. The
**cross-surface compatibility** is the load-bearing observation: the
additive-shift form is the common structural class across both
surfaces under their respective natural bases. This is bounded support,
not a derivation that the same basis-fixing applies to the staggered
KS Dirac operator. ∎
