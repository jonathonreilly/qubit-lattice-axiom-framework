## 2. Essential self-adjointness of each generator (the boost is the crux)

### 2.1 The compact and abelian generators (`H`, `P^i`, `J^i`)

- **`H = E(p)` and `P^i = p^i`** are multiplication operators by the real,
  measurable, finite-a.e. functions `E(p)` and `p^i`. A multiplication operator by a
  real measurable function is self-adjoint on its natural maximal domain; since `E(p)`
  and `p^i` are of polynomial growth, the Schwartz space is an **operator core**, so
  the operators are **essentially self-adjoint** on `D` (Reed-Simon I, the
  multiplication-operator example following Thm VIII.3). `H` is moreover **bounded
  below** (`H >= m > 0`), the positive-energy spectrum condition.
- **`J^i = -i eps^{ijk} p^j d/dp^k + Sigma^i`** is the generator of the rotation
  group `SO(3)` (more precisely `SU(2)`) acting on `H` by the manifestly unitary
  representation `(R . psi)(p) = D^{1/2}(R) psi(R^{-1} p)`. `SO(3)` is **compact**;
  a strongly-continuous unitary representation of a compact (indeed any) Lie group
  has every Lie-algebra generator essentially self-adjoint on the Garding/smooth
  domain, and `D` is contained in it. (Equivalently: the rotation generators have a
  dense set of analytic vectors -- the finite-band spherical-harmonic times Hermite
  states -- by Nelson.) The rotation sector is therefore standard and not the
  difficulty.

The non-trivial generator is the unbounded, **non-compact** boost `K^i`.

### 2.2 The boost `K^i`: exact reduction to `-i d/dzeta` (load-bearing step)

Work in one boost direction (say `i = 1`; the three boosts are unitarily equivalent
by rotation, and Section 4 treats them jointly). For fixed transverse momentum
`p_perp=(p^2,p^3)`, set `M_perp = sqrt(m^2 + |p_perp|^2)` and foliate the mass
shell by the **rapidity** coordinate `zeta` along the boost direction:

```text
    p^1 = M_perp sinh(zeta),    E = M_perp cosh(zeta),    zeta in R,
```

The transverse momenta are spectator parameters, untouched by the boost.  The
special case `p_perp=0` gives `M_perp=m`, which is the one-dimensional reduction
used in the runner.
Two exact facts:

1. **The invariant measure is flat in rapidity.** With
   `p^1 = M_perp sinh(zeta)`,
   `dp^1 = M_perp cosh(zeta) dzeta = E dzeta`, so the one-dimensional Lorentz-invariant
   factor

```text
       dp^1 / (2E) = dzeta / 2
```

   is **constant** in `zeta`. The change of variables `p^1 <-> zeta` is therefore (up
   to the constant `1/2`, absorbed into a unitary rescaling) a **unitary** map
   `L^2(dp^1/2E) -> L^2(dzeta)`.

2. **The orbital boost is `-i d/dzeta`.** Since `d/dzeta = (dp^1/dzeta) d/dp^1 = E
   d/dp^1`, the orbital part of the boost is, **exactly**,

```text
       K_orb^1 = -i E(p) d/dp^1 = -i d/dzeta.
```

   No symmetrisation ordering ambiguity survives: in rapidity coordinates, where the
   measure is flat, `-i d/dzeta` is already the symmetric (formally self-adjoint)
   first-order operator. (Equivalently, in momentum coordinates the symmetric
   ordering `-(i/2)(E d/dp + d/dp E)` differs from `-iE d/dp` by the bounded
   multiplication `-(i/2)(dE/dp) = -(i/2) p/E`, which is exactly cancelled by the
   half of the measure Jacobian; the rapidity coordinate makes this manifest. Runner
   `N1` verifies the operator identity `d/dzeta = E d/dp` and the Hermiticity of the
   rapidity-spectral `-i d/dzeta` to machine precision, two independent ways.)

Now `-i d/dzeta` on the Schwartz core `S(R_zeta)` is **the** textbook essentially
self-adjoint operator: it is the momentum operator on the line, with deficiency
indices `(0,0)`, unique self-adjoint extension generating the translation group
(Reed-Simon I, Ex. 1 after Thm VIII.1; Thm VIII.3). Hence `K_orb^1` is essentially
self-adjoint on `D`.

**Including the spin Wigner carrier.** The little-group cocycle is `SU(2)`-valued,
so its infinitesimal fibre generator is anti-Hermitian; the self-adjoint boost
generator contains the corresponding **Hermitian** bounded multiplication term
obtained by multiplying that infinitesimal generator by `i`.  Denote this Hermitian
bounded term by `W^1(p)`.  It is a smooth `C^4 x C^4`-valued multiplication operator
and is uniformly bounded on `H_m^+` because it is a smooth function of
`p/E in (-1,1)`, from the companion note's little-group cocycle check. A **bounded** symmetric perturbation
of an essentially self-adjoint operator is essentially self-adjoint on the same core,
with the same closure domain (Kato-Rellich theorem with relative bound `0`;
Reed-Simon II, Thm X.12). Therefore `K^1 = K_orb^1 + W^1` is **essentially
self-adjoint on `D`**. The same holds for `K^2, K^3` by rotational equivalence.
Runner `N1` confirms the full spin-tensored representative
`K = K_orb (x) I_4 + w(p) (x) (i Sigma^{01})`, with bounded real `w(p)`, is
Hermitian on the core.

This is the crux: the unbounded non-compact generator is reduced -- by an exact,
explicitly verified coordinate identity -- to the canonical essentially-self-adjoint
example, plus a bounded perturbation handled by Kato-Rellich. No black-box appeal to
"the Wigner representation is unitary" is needed for this step.

## 3. A common dense set of analytic vectors (Nelson bound, exhibited)

Essential self-adjointness of each generator already follows from Section 2; to
**integrate the algebra to the group** (Section 4) we use Nelson's analytic-vector
theorem, which requires a dense set of vectors analytic for the Nelson Laplacian.
We exhibit such a set explicitly.

**Gaussian/Hermite analytic vectors.** On the rapidity line take the Gaussian family
`psi_a(zeta) = exp(-a zeta^2 / 2)`, `a > 0` (and Gaussians in `|p|` on the mass
shell, tensored with any fixed spinor); these are dense in `H`. For the boost
`K = -i d/dzeta`,

```text
    (d/dzeta)^n psi_a = g_n(zeta) psi_a,   g_0 = 1,  g_{n+1} = g_n' - a zeta g_n,
```

so `g_n` is (up to scaling) the probabilists' Hermite polynomial of degree `n`, and

```text
    || K^n psi_a ||^2 = integral g_n(zeta)^2 e^{-a zeta^2} dzeta
                      ~  a^n n! || psi_a ||^2     (leading Hermite term),
    => || K^n psi_a || ~ a^{n/2} sqrt(n!).
```

This satisfies the **Nelson bound** `||K^n psi|| <= C R^n n!` with, e.g., `C = R =
1`: since `a^{n/2} sqrt(n!) <= n!` for all `n >= 0` when `a <= 1`, and the ratio
`||K^n psi||/(R^n n!) ~ a^{n/2}/sqrt(n!) -> 0`. The associated Nelson series
`sum_n ||K^n psi|| t^n / n!` has **infinite** radius of convergence (the `n`-th root
of `||K^n psi||/n!` tends to `0`), so `psi_a` is an **analytic vector** -- indeed an
**entire** vector -- for the boost. The same Gaussians are analytic for `H = E(p)`
and `P = p` (multiplication by functions of at most linear growth on a Gaussian:
`||P^n psi|| ~ (const)^n sqrt((2n-1)!!)`, again `<= C R^n n!`) and for the compact
`J^i` (bounded growth on each angular-momentum band). They are therefore **common
analytic vectors for all ten generators**.

**Runner `N2`** computes `||K^n psi_a||` for `a = 0.5`, `n = 0..12`, **exactly**
(integer/rational Hermite recursion integrated against closed-form Gaussian moments,
no grid/aliasing error), and exhibits the growth

```text
    ||K^n psi||/||psi|| = 1, 0.50, 0.43, 0.48, 0.64, 0.96, 1.59, 2.87, 5.56, ...
```

-- the `~ a^{n/2} sqrt(n!)` law -- with the Nelson ratio `||K^n psi||/(R^n n!)`
(`C=R=1`) monotonically **decreasing** from `1` to `~3 x 10^{-7}` at `n = 12`,
confirming the bound holds for all `n` and the vector is analytic. An independent
low-order spectral (Fourier-derivative) evaluation reproduces the exact norms to
`< 5%` for `n <= 5` (the orders where the finite grid is accurate), cross-checking the
closed form.

## 4. Integration to the unitary Poincare group representation (Nelson)

The companion note verified that the ten generators, as symmetric operators on the
common core `D`, satisfy the Poincare Lie brackets `iso(3,1)` exactly (including the
load-bearing `[K^i,K^j] = -i eps^{ijk} J^k`). We now have, additionally:

- each generator is essentially self-adjoint on `D` (Section 2);
- `D` is a common invariant core (Section 1);
- `D` contains a dense set of common analytic vectors with the Nelson bound
  (Section 3).

**Nelson's analytic-vector theorem** (Nelson 1959, Thm 5; see also Reed-Simon II,
Thm X.39, and Simon) states: if `{T_1, ..., T_d}` are symmetric operators on a common
invariant dense domain `D`, representing a Lie algebra `g` of a simply-connected Lie
group `G`, and the **Nelson Laplacian** `Delta = sum_i T_i^2` is essentially
self-adjoint on `D` (equivalently `D` contains a dense set of analytic vectors for
`Delta`), then there is a **unique strongly-continuous unitary representation** `U`
of `G` on `H` whose generators are the closures `bar{T_i}`. The Gaussian/Hermite
vectors of Section 3 are analytic for `Delta = H^2 + sum (P^i)^2 + sum (J^i)^2 + sum
(K^i)^2` (a finite sum of operators each of which sends these vectors to vectors with
the `<= C R^n n!` iterated-norm bound, so `Delta` does too), hence `Delta` is
essentially self-adjoint on `D` by Nelson's analytic-vector criterion for a single
operator.

Applying the theorem with `G = ` the universal cover of the Poincare group
`R^{1,3} \rtimes SL(2,C)` (simply connected; `SL(2,C)` is the double cover of the
proper orthochronous Lorentz group, the correct group for a spin-1/2 representation)
yields the **strongly-continuous unitary representation** of (P-iii) -- the boost
one-parameter subgroups `exp(-i zeta K^i)`, the rotations `exp(-i theta J^i)`, and the
translations `exp(i a^mu P_mu)` are all unitary and compose according to the group law.
On the massive positive-energy sector this is precisely the **Wigner spin-1/2
irreducible representation** induced from the `SU(2)` little group, which is the
independent classical statement of the result (Wigner 1939; consistency, not a second
proof).

**Runner `N5`** realises the boost flow `exp(-i zeta K_orb)` exactly as the
rapidity-translation group on `L^2(R, dzeta)` and verifies it is a strongly-continuous
one-parameter **unitary** group (norm preservation to `1e-9`, group law `U(s)U(t) =
U(s+t)`, `U(0) = I`, strong continuity `||U(s)psi - psi|| -> 0`), and that the
generator recovered as `(U(s) - I)/(-i s)` reproduces `-i d/dzeta` on smooth vectors
-- Stone's theorem made concrete. This is the boost flow that preserves `H_m^+` and
the invariant measure (the companion note's mass-shell and measure checks), now upgraded to a **unitary
group**, not merely a measure-preserving flow.

## 5. Non-triviality controls (the checks are real discriminators)

A self-adjointness lemma is only meaningful if the checks distinguish the genuine
generators from operators that are **not** essentially self-adjoint. Runner `N4`
supplies two such controls:

- **Half-line momentum (deficiency `(1,0)`).** The same first-order operator
  `-i d/dx` on the **half-line** `[0, infinity)` (one boundary, no flux
  cancellation) is symmetric on `C_c^infty(0, infinity)` but **not** essentially
  self-adjoint: its closure has deficiency indices `(n_+, n_-) = (1, 0)` because
  `K^* psi = +i psi` has the `L^2` solution `e^{-x}` while `K^* psi = -i psi` has
  only the non-`L^2` solution `e^{+x}`. The runner verifies `||e^{-x}||_{L^2(0,inf)}`
  is finite (one deficiency solution) while `||e^{+x}||_{L^2(0,inf)} = infinity`
  (none on the other side), so the deficiency indices are **unequal** -- the
  operator is **not** essentially self-adjoint. This is the textbook contrast
  (Reed-Simon II, X.1): the **full-line** boost `-i d/dzeta` of Section 2 has
  deficiency `(0,0)`; the half-line operator does **not**. The boost's essential
  self-adjointness genuinely uses that the rapidity runs over the whole line `R`
  (the mass shell is the full hyperbola, with **no** boundary), exactly the
  geometric input.
- **Non-Hermitian perturbation (complex spectrum).** Adding a genuinely
  non-Hermitian term `+ 0.3 i I` to the truncated symmetric boost produces a
  **non-symmetric** operator with **complex** spectrum and a **non-unitary** Cayley
  transform -- it **fails** every leg of the essential-self-adjointness battery
  (`N3`), confirming those legs are not vacuously true.

Runner `N3` then verifies the genuine boost's signature on growing truncations
`N = 32, 64, 128`: deficiency indices `(0, 0)`, **real** spectrum, **unitary** Cayley
transform `(K - iI)(K + iI)^{-1}`, and resolvents `(K +- iI)^{-1}` of norm `<= 1`
with dense (full-rank) range -- the von Neumann / Cayley criterion for essential
self-adjointness. The genuine boost passes; both controls fail. The discriminator is
real.
