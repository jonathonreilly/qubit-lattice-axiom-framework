## 1. Claim scope

Let `R^d` be a `d`-dimensional real vector space (`d ≥ 0`) with the
graded exterior algebra `Λ^*(R^d) = ⊕_{p=0}^d Λ^p(R^d)`, where
`dim_R Λ^p(R^d) = binomial(d, p)` for `0 ≤ p ≤ d` and `Λ^p(R^d) = 0`
for `p > d`. The wedge product `Λ^p × Λ^q → Λ^{p+q}` raises form
degree additively. Then:

- **(W1) Degree of wedge-square.** For any 2-form
  `ω ∈ Λ^2(R^d)`, the wedge-square `ω ∧ ω` is a homogeneous element
  of degree `2 + 2 = 4` in the exterior algebra. So
  `ω ∧ ω ∈ Λ^4(R^d)` when `d ≥ 4`, and `ω ∧ ω = 0` when `d < 4`.

- **(W2) Top-form criterion.** The top-degree form space is
  `Λ^d(R^d)`, of dimension `binomial(d, d) = 1` (one-dimensional, the
  volume form). A homogeneous form `α ∈ Λ^p(R^d)` is a **top-degree
  form** iff `p = d`.

- **(W3) Wedge-square is top-form iff `d = 4`.** By (W1) and (W2),
  `ω ∧ ω` is a top-degree form iff `4 = d`, i.e., iff `d = 4`. At
  `d < 4`, `ω ∧ ω` vanishes (the form algebra truncates). At
  `d > 4`, `ω ∧ ω` is a 4-form of degree strictly less than `d`,
  hence not a top form.

- **(W4) Existence of a nonzero wedge-square at `d = 4`.** At `d = 4`
  with the standard basis 2-forms `e^μ ∧ e^ν` (`μ < ν`), there
  exists `ω ∈ Λ^2(R^4)` with `ω ∧ ω ≠ 0`. Explicit example:
  `ω = e^1 ∧ e^2 + e^3 ∧ e^4`; then

  ```text
  ω ∧ ω  =  2 · e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ e^3 ∧ e^4  ≠  0,
  ```

  a nonzero multiple of the top form on `R^4`.

- **(W5) Non-degeneracy of wedge-square map at `d = 4`.** The wedge-
  square map

  ```text
  σ : Λ^2(R^4)  →  Λ^4(R^4),    σ(ω) := ω ∧ ω
  ```

  is a homogeneous polynomial map of degree 2 from the 6-dimensional
  space `Λ^2(R^4)` to the 1-dimensional space `Λ^4(R^4)`. Its image
  spans `Λ^4(R^4)` (i.e., `σ` is surjective onto the top form
  space) because (W4) exhibits a nonzero image. The map is not
  identically zero; equivalently, the polynomial
  `σ(ω) = ω_{12} ω_{34} - ω_{13} ω_{24} + ω_{14} ω_{23}` (in
  components `ω_{μν}` for `μ < ν`) is a non-zero quadratic
  Plücker-type polynomial on `Λ^2(R^4)`.

- **(W6) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {2, 3}`.** For `d ∈ {2, 3}`, `Λ^p` is
  empty for `p > d`, so `ω ∧ ω` lies in `Λ^4(R^d) = 0`. Hence the
  wedge-square map `σ_d : Λ^2(R^d) → Λ^d(R^d)` is the zero map for
  every `ω`, and (W4)-(W5) fail.

- **(W7) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {5, 6, 7, ...}`.** For `d ≥ 5`, the
  wedge-square `ω ∧ ω ∈ Λ^4(R^d)` is a 4-form, not a top form
  (`dim Λ^d(R^d) = 1` lives at degree `d > 4`). The integral
  `∫_M ω ∧ ω` over a top-dimensional manifold `M ⊂ R^d` requires
  multiplying `ω ∧ ω` by additional `(d - 4)` 1-forms (and a
  determinant prescription) to reach the top form, so the bare
  wedge-square is **not** a top-form integrand.

Facts (W1)-(W7) are pure graded-algebra statements on

## 5. Proof

The argument is direct graded-algebra degree counting.

### 5.1 (W1) Degree of wedge-square

The wedge product `Λ^p × Λ^q → Λ^{p+q}` is bilinear with
`(α ∧ β) ∈ Λ^{p+q}` whenever `α ∈ Λ^p, β ∈ Λ^q`. For a 2-form
`ω ∈ Λ^2(R^d)`, applying this with `p = q = 2` gives
`ω ∧ ω ∈ Λ^4(R^d)`. By convention, `Λ^4(R^d) = 0` when `4 > d`
(no `4`-form basis elements exist on an `R^d` with `d < 4`).

### 5.2 (W2) Top-form criterion

The top-degree form space is `Λ^d(R^d)`, of dimension
`binomial(d, d) = 1`, spanned by the volume form
`e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ ⋯ ∧ e^d`. A homogeneous form
`α ∈ Λ^p(R^d)` (`α ≠ 0`) is a top-degree form iff `p = d`.

### 5.3 (W3) Wedge-square is top-form iff `d = 4`

Combining (W1) and (W2): `ω ∧ ω ∈ Λ^4(R^d)` is a top-degree form iff
`4 = d`. At `d < 4`, the wedge-square vanishes identically. At
`d > 4`, the wedge-square lies in a proper subspace of `Λ^*(R^d)` of
degree `4 < d`. Only at `d = 4` is the wedge-square a top-form
candidate.

### 5.4 (W4) Existence of nonzero wedge-square at `d = 4`

At `d = 4`, take `ω := e^1 ∧ e^2 + e^3 ∧ e^4 ∈ Λ^2(R^4)`. Then

```text
ω ∧ ω  =  (e^1 ∧ e^2) ∧ (e^1 ∧ e^2)
        + (e^1 ∧ e^2) ∧ (e^3 ∧ e^4)
        + (e^3 ∧ e^4) ∧ (e^1 ∧ e^2)
        + (e^3 ∧ e^4) ∧ (e^3 ∧ e^4).
```

The first and last terms vanish since `e^μ ∧ e^μ = 0`. The middle
two terms each contribute `e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ e^3 ∧ e^4` (the wedge
product is graded-commutative; rearranging gives the volume form
each time). Hence

```text
ω ∧ ω  =  2 · e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ e^3 ∧ e^4  ≠  0.
```

### 5.5 (W5) Non-degeneracy of wedge-square map at `d = 4`

In components, write `ω = ∑_{μ < ν} ω_{μν} e^μ ∧ e^ν` with
6 independent components `ω_{12}, ω_{13}, ω_{14}, ω_{23}, ω_{24},
ω_{34}`. The wedge-square is then

```text
ω ∧ ω  =  2 (ω_{12} ω_{34} - ω_{13} ω_{24} + ω_{14} ω_{23})
         · e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ e^3 ∧ e^4.                                  (P)
```

The coefficient `ω_{12} ω_{34} - ω_{13} ω_{24} + ω_{14} ω_{23}` is
the **Plücker coordinate** of the 2-form on `R^4` — a homogeneous
quadratic polynomial in the 6 component variables. It is not
identically zero (take `ω_{12} = ω_{34} = 1` and the other components
zero; the polynomial evaluates to 1).

Hence the wedge-square map `σ : Λ^2(R^4) → Λ^4(R^4)` is a surjective
homogeneous polynomial map of degree 2 from a 6-dim space to a 1-dim
space (the volume-form line).

### 5.6 (W6) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {2, 3}`

For `d = 2`: `Λ^4(R^2) = 0` (since `4 > 2`). Any 2-form
`ω ∈ Λ^2(R^2)` is a scalar multiple of `e^1 ∧ e^2` (since
`dim Λ^2(R^2) = 1`). The wedge-square `ω ∧ ω = c² (e^1 ∧ e^2) ∧
(e^1 ∧ e^2) = 0` because `(e^1 ∧ e^2) ∧ (e^1 ∧ e^2)` has a repeated
`e^1` factor (or repeated `e^2`).

For `d = 3`: `Λ^4(R^3) = 0` (since `4 > 3`). For any
`ω = ∑_{μ<ν, μ,ν ∈ {1,2,3}} ω_{μν} e^μ ∧ e^ν`, the wedge-square
expansion produces only `e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ e^3` (or similar 3-form-of-3
expressions) with each repeated `e^μ` factor giving zero. Explicit
computation: `(e^1 ∧ e^2) ∧ (e^1 ∧ e^3) = 0` (repeated `e^1`),
`(e^1 ∧ e^2) ∧ (e^2 ∧ e^3) = 0` (repeated `e^2`), etc. Every
non-vanishing wedge-square term would need a 4-th distinct generator,
which does not exist in `R^3`. Hence `ω ∧ ω = 0` for every
`ω ∈ Λ^2(R^3)`.

### 5.7 (W7) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {5, 6, 7, ...}`

For `d ≥ 5`, `Λ^4(R^d)` has dimension `binomial(d, 4) > 0`, so the
wedge-square `ω ∧ ω ∈ Λ^4(R^d)` may be nonzero. But `Λ^4(R^d) ≠
Λ^d(R^d)` since `4 < d`; the wedge-square is a 4-form in a higher-
dimensional manifold, hence not a top form on `R^d`.

To compute a top-form integral `∫_M ω ∧ ω` over a `d`-dimensional
domain `M ⊂ R^d`, one needs to wedge with an additional `(d - 4)`
1-forms to reach a top form. The bare wedge-square `ω ∧ ω` does not
integrate to a top-form value on `M` without this additional input.

### 5.8 Combining

From (W1)-(W7): the wedge-square `ω ∧ ω` is a top-degree form on
`R^d` iff `d = 4`. At `d < 4`, the wedge-square vanishes by the
truncation of the exterior algebra. At `d > 4`, the wedge-square is
a 4-form of degree strictly less than `d`. Only at `d = 4` is `ω ∧ ω`
both a nonzero element of `Λ^*(R^d)` and a top-degree form. QED.
