# Synthesis: gauge fields and matter registers

**Question.** Which gauge fields and charged matter multiplets, if any, are selected by the site and link registers?

**Answer from the kept results.** None is selected by the registers alone. What they permit is conditional on supplied models.

*Gauge fields.* Definite stationary links give classical Coulomb colour, a flat vacuum and one-body matter; estimates against measurement then exclude them, so links must carry odds (#9924). A one-mode site-valued ice flux makes Margolus fermions neutral dipoles (#9890). Link registers built from one-mode fermion sites carry an exact U(1) Gauss law and, with three colour rishons per end, an exact SU(3) Gauss law, on a supplied role assignment, with supplied label-blind hops and rishon sites carrying no records (#9926; logged #10026 finds the generic covariant cluster rule it tests abelian). On nested face registers, a supplied plaquette law has complete gauge-invariant loops; its classical energy tends to Maxwell and Yang-Mills at O(B_f^2) and its harmonic photon has speed 1, but in a single-loop diagnostic the bare coefficients miss the light cone at small registers, and the dressed coefficients are not established (#9968, using #9928; the site-bond layout is replaced). At one qubit per site, rung 2 carries exact Z2 structure, a U(1) ice diagnostic and a costly fermionic twist, with supplied energies (#10021). Block covariance and register size select no gauge group (#9882); the two-layer cube tick, on two modes per site, has exact internal algebra so(4), with no corner su(4) (#10010).

*Matter.* A rung-1 cell with a supplied internal action and supplied masses shows the 1:1:3:3 multiplicity pattern of one generation, without chirality (#9894). Five modes per site, which bends the Qubit axiom, is the smallest anomaly-free, mass-protected full Spin(2N) Fock content under three stated conditions, with finite-group loopholes open (#10014). But invariant free bands on five-mode sites carry the vector 10 at their cones (#10023), and on a fixed finite set of five-mode sites with site-bilinear Spin(10) every parity-odd excitation is vector class, never the 16 (#10027).

*Failed.* Definite links; the one-mode ice photon with Margolus fermions as charges; a covariant one-body su(3); a corner su(4) on the two-layer tick; a composite 16 on fixed five-mode sites with site-bilinear Spin(10).

*Open.* Register sizes and roles are supplied. Gauss laws are not derived from the tick (logged #10024: the free tick has no local constants of motion away from a decoupled point). Confinement, running and the continuum limit of the interacting register are not computed. A spinor needs a site-count change, deconfined spinons outside the finite-site theorem, or a Spin(10) not generated by site bilinears; the first two are tested under supplied models in logged #10038 and #10045, without chirality or a shown deconfined phase. Chirality and families are not obtained.

**Statement text from the note (verbatim, headings demoted):**

##### One-body covariance on a block

- Plain corner permutations split the 8 modes into multiplicity-free summands of dimension 1, 1, 3 and 3 (A1 + A2 + T1 + T2). The commutant has dimension 4 and is abelian (max [X, Y] = 4e-16), so it is U(1)^4.
- Under the spin-3/2 twist each rotation sends a corner mode to a unit phase times its image, and the coin W0 is covariant (error 6e-15).
- Twisted, |chi| is 8 at the identity, 2 on the 8 body-diagonal turns and 0 elsewhere, so 8 = 2 x (spin 3/2). The commutant is M_2(C): dimension 4, non-commutative (max [X, Y] = 0.71) with a one-dimensional centre. This is a forced copy U(2).
- There is one covariant operator per corner distance 0, 1, 2, 3. W0 lies in the copy algebra with eigenvalues +1 and -1, four of each.
- Covariant site-diagonal block operators are scalars only, so a per-site phase register can compensate only the U(1) block phase.
- The commutant of the rotations and the coin is span{1, W0}: the coin breaks the copy U(2) to U(1) x U(1).
- The symmetric-base su(3) used in the repository's Cl(3) colour embedding is not rotation covariant, plain or twisted (max ||[V_R, T^a]|| = 2.00). No su(3) fits in u(1)^4 or u(2), so no covariant one-body SU(3) acts on a block.

##### The copy algebra on the tick

Lift the three copy generators to all even blocks of the two-partition tick on the 4^3 torus (64 modes). Exactly one commutes with the massless tick, the body-diagonal one (singular values 9.238, 8.222, 0.0), because it anticommutes with both half-ticks. A mass a = 0.3 breaks it by 2.26. The copy SU(2) is not a symmetry of the tick.

##### Register size selects nothing

For link registers in G_L x G_R with the link operator in (N, N-bar), (N,1) + (1,N) carries covariant link operators in 2N states (dimension 2 for N = 2, 1 for N = 3), while (2,2) and (3,1) + (1,3-bar) carry none. Counting SU products that fit in m qubits gives 0, 1, 4, 11, 22, 43 for m = 1 to 6, of which 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 fill exactly. SU(3) x SU(2) never fits exactly, since 2 x 6 is not a power of 2, and no SU(3) factor ever fills a qubit register.

##### A supplied Fock register

Take Lambda(C^p + C^q) and read the even-parity sector as left-handed Weyl states. For (3, 2) the even sector is (3,2) + (3b,1) + (3b,1) + (1,2) + (1,1) + (1,1). Y = N2/2 - N3/3 is vacuum-neutral and free of the gravitational, Y^3, SU(3)^2 Y, SU(2)^2 Y and SU(3)^3 anomalies, with Q : u^c : d^c : L : e^c = 1 : -4 : 2 : -3 : 6. Without vacuum neutrality the anomaly-free U(1)s form span{Y, B - L} with B - L = 1 - 2 N3/3, and the empty register is neutral only for Y. A unique vacuum-neutral anomaly-free U(1) exists for every split with p + q in {4, 5, 6} and for none with p + q <= 3, so the condition fixes the U(1) once a split is given but does not choose 3 + 2. A block offers no covariant 3 + 2 split: twisted invariant mode subspaces have dimension 0, 4 or 8, and the plain block has only 1, 1, 3, 3 summands.

##### Many-body sectors

The twisted block rotations on n-particle Fock sectors carry multiplicities up to 10, so U(m) commutants with m >= 3 occur. They act on n-particle states, not as one-body transformations of the modes, so they are not minimal-coupling gauge groups.

##### Supplied and boundary

Supplied: the block, its 24 rotations, the spin-3/2 twist from the coin, one mode per corner, the link-register definition, the Fock register and its split, the even sector read as left-handed (a chirality choice), and vacuum neutrality (a normal-ordering choice). A register of more than one qubit per site would change the qubit axiom. No gauge group, 3 + 2 split, chirality or non-abelian carrier is established.

**Statement text from the note (verbatim, headings demoted):**

Claim type: bounded_theorem.
Primary runner: `scripts/a_site_qubit_ice_photon_makes_margolus_fermions_neutral_dipoles_2026_10_21.py`.

The [candidate-law register](CANDIDATE_LAWS.md) has zero premise weight. This note asks whether the sites' own qubits can carry an emergent photon through a charge-ice construction. The [minimal axioms](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-06-29.md) fix one qubit per site; the energy function used below is supplied. The result is negative on the decisive question. The qubits can carry an emergent photon in a supplied model, but then the Margolus fermions are not its charges. The route fails in its one-qubit form.

##### Statement

1. Forced flux variable. On one fermion mode the only parity-even operators are 1 and n. A site flux must therefore be E_s = n_s - 1/2, oriented from the site's even cube centre to its odd one. The sites are the bonds of the body-centred lattice of cube centres. The divergences are q_e = N_e - 4 and q_o = 4 - N_o, and the ice rule says every cube holds exactly 4 fermions.
2. The fermion is neutral. The emergent charges sum to zero for every configuration. Adding a fermion puts +1 on its even cube and -1 on its odd cube, so a Margolus fermion is a dipole of length sqrt 3 with net emergent charge 0.
3. The rules do not give the ice rule. A half-tick keeps its own partition's counts but changes the other partition's, so the full tick keeps no cube count. The tick has no flat band, so no finite region at any rung carries a conserved local count. The half-filled sea has count variance near that of independent sites. Any change inside one cube at fixed count creates at least two defects in the opposite partition, so no cube-local update moves within the ice states except by phases.
4. Supplied ice model. In H = J sum_cubes (N - 4)^2 + t sum_cubes c^dag W c, second-order perturbation gives ring exchange on 24 body-centred four-cycles per cell. On the 12 planar cycles, the fermion-exchange sign combines with the pi flux of the edge hop so that the two paths add, giving |K| = 2t^2/3J. On the 12 skew cycles, which have one pairing, |K| = t^2/3J. The planar terms alone are unfrustrated. With the skew terms, half of the three-cycle closed surfaces carry flux pi, so the full ring model is frustrated.
5. Photon in the supplied model. In a Gaussian (non-compact) reading, the planar terms give two gauge zeros and one massless transverse pair with omega^2 = 2 U_eff K k^2 in site units, isotropic. In the frustrated full model, the expansion about the best classical minimum keeps a gapless pair at this level. The classical ice ensemble shows an exact pinch point.
6. Charges. Count defects carry (q, n_F) = (+1, +1/2), (-1, -1/2), (-1, +1/2) and (+1, -1/2) for even-5, even-3, odd-5 and odd-3 cubes. Every local change of fermion number is emergent-neutral. Defects hop at first order, with amplitude t/sqrt 3, and are automatically minimally coupled.

##### What is supplied and the boundary

Supplied: the edge coin and the partitions, the count penalty J sum (N - 4)^2 and t/J << 1 (neither is in the rules), second-order perturbation theory, the Gaussian reading of the compact ring model, and the branch of the sea. Coulomb phases are established in the literature for spin-1/2 quantum spin ice and for cubic quantum dimers. Those results are cited, not rederived, and none is on this lattice.

Not established: an emergent photon whose charges are the Margolus fermions, and a Coulomb phase on this lattice. The statement that a gauge field needs Z_N with N >= 5 or a rotor is narrower than it reads: a spin-1/2 link suffices in the supplied model, but only if the qubits serve as flux rather than as matter. A count penalty added to the tick would remove the Dirac band of the edge coin.

Claim type: bounded_theorem.
Primary runner: `scripts/definite_stationary_links_give_classical_coulomb_colour_flat_vacuum_one_body_matter_and_undecaying_levels_2026_10_22.py`.

The [candidate-law register](CANDIDATE_LAWS.md) has zero premise weight. ##### Statement

With links definite, stationary and sourced by the actual configuration:

1. Colour is classical Coulomb. In the occupation basis every colour charge is a weight of one Cartan subalgebra (the three weights sum to zero and the two diagonal generators commute). With spatial links trivial and the time component in that Cartan, every commutator in the Yang-Mills equations vanishes, so the abelian Coulomb field is an exact stationary point; a quark-antiquark pair has parallel charges +-w for any colour orientation. The lattice Gauss law then holds exactly, the links are pure gauge in space and every plaquette is 1. The static pair energy is -g^2 |w|^2/(4 pi R) = -alpha_s/(3R) at large R and is bounded above by the self-energy at every separation, so the string tension is zero. A definite link configuration is a fixed product of unitaries around any loop, so |W(C)| = 1 for every loop and there is no area law at any coupling.
2. The vacuum links are flat. The response law measures every departure from the vacuum at the same configuration, so the vacuum is no source; definite vacuum links are then flat, F = 0, the topological charge is zero in every history, the topological susceptibility is zero and there is no gluon condensate.
3. Matter stays one-body. With c-number links each history's fermion update is a one-body unitary, so a Slater determinant stays a Slater determinant (its one-body density matrix stays idempotent), and a definite link sourced by the actual configuration gives, in every history, a product of one-body phases: two charges cannot be entangled through it.
4. Stationary levels never decay. If the odds are stationary, the fewest-change transport of the actuality law is to stay, so the actual configuration of an atom in a stationary excited level never moves, its current is zero, the definite link it sources stays static and the odds stay the eigenstate.
5. Any definite or classically mixed field gives g2(0) = <I^2>/<I>^2 >= 1 by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, so there is no antibunching.

##### B. The centre theorem (exact, any invariant H)

- B3/A4/F1. exp(2 pi i M_01) = prod_s exp(2 pi i (n_{s,0} - 1/2)) = (-1)^n_sites, a c-number (checked for
  n = 1, 2, 4). The centre generator is z = exp(i pi sum Cartans) = (-1)^F (-i)^n_sites.
- Consequences. Conjugation by -1 in Spin(10) is the identity on the whole operator algebra, but (-1)^F negates
  every odd operator. Therefore:
  - every local operator, and every excitation relative to any vacuum, transforms as a true SO(10)
    representation;
  - a fermion-parity-odd excitation has relative centre charge -1, which is the vector class (10, 120, 126, 320,
    ...);
  - a spinor (centre charge +-i) is impossible.
- This holds for every Spin(10)-invariant Hamiltonian of any coupling strength, range or order, interacting or not,
  on any fixed set of sites. It extends #10023 A2 from single-particle operators to all many-body excitations.
- Physical reading: the GUT structure Spin x_Z2 Spin(10) identifies the internal -1 with (-1)^F. A fixed-site lattice
  whose Spin(10) is generated by site bilinears realises Spin x SO(10) on its excitations. The 16 needs -1 in Spin(10)
