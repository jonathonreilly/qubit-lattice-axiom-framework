## Geometry Derivation

The finite tensor-word packet identifies the one-word object as the compressed
weight-box unit:

```text
tensor_word := diag_c · (N_f + N_fbar) · diag_c · (N_f + N_fbar)^T · diag_c.
```

The one-word tensor-transfer note gives the same packet as
`T = D M D M^T D`, with `M = N_f + N_fbar`. This is the finite word used on
the width-one chain.

For a second transverse unit in the same layer, the temporal-gauge authority is
decisive only after the geometric reading is fixed. It says each one-link
factor

```text
K_(x,mu)(U', U) = w_beta(U' U^(-1))
```

depends only on the same spatial link at adjacent slices. Its proof also says
distinct mixed plaquettes touch distinct spatial links and the mixed-kernel
contribution factorizes as a product over spatial links. Under that reading,
a second same-layer transverse unit is an independent rail and the rail-rail
factor is `1`.

The same authority also records the matrix-coefficient contraction

```text
integral_G K_(x,mu)(U', U) lambda_(i,j)(U) dU
  = (c_lambda(beta) / d_lambda) lambda_(i,j)(U').
```

Together with the SU(3) character-convolution note's

```text
integral chi_(p,q)(V W^(-1)) chi_(p',q')(W) dW
   = delta_((p,q),(p',q')) chi_(p',q')(V) / d_(p,q),
```

this supplies the shared-link matrix-element diagnostic
`delta_(lambda,mu) / d_lambda`. The character-level diagnostic drops the
`1 / d_lambda` factor and keeps only the same-label character delta. Both
shared-link diagnostics are implemented below, but both fail the pinned-rail
gate, so the measured width-lift branch is the linkwise-factorized one.

## Reviewer Addition (2026-06-12): the Pinned Gate Forces Factorization — the Reduction Map Is the Next Object

The pinned-rail gate used here (rail 2 projected to the trivial slice must
reproduce the width-one chain) is itself a modeling choice, and the
diagnostics show it is structurally too strong for coupled geometries: under
either shared-link branch, trivial-pinning annihilates the shared-link sum
down to a single channel (pinned object: 1 nonzero entry against 295;
max_diff `0.307`) — the same trivial-slice collapse mechanism proved in the
slice eigen-identity lemma. The only branch that can pass such a gate is the
factorized one, and a factorized ladder provably cannot move the pair-support
readout (its zero lift is expected, not informative about the geometry).

What the width axis actually needs, before any coupled width-two value can be
promoted: the derived width-reduction map — what "removing a rail" equals in
the linkwise-factorized geometry. The natural candidate, parallel to the
deep-rim treatment of the depth axis, is contraction of the removed rail
against its own Perron/vacuum weight rather than against the trivial slice;
whether that (or another form) is the licensed reduction must be derived from
the cited factorization rows, not imposed. Until then the shared-link
branches stand as constructed diagnostics, and the width-lift question is
open — not answered in the negative by this row.
