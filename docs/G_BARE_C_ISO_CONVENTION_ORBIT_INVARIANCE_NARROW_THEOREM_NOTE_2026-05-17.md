## 1. Theorem (bounded, C-iso convention-orbit invariance for g_bare)

Let `g_bare > 0` denote the bare gauge scale appearing in the
W-substitution closure on the canonical Cl(3) connection
`A_op = Σ_a A^a T_a` with retained `Tr(T_a T_b) = δ_ab / 2`. Let
`a_τ, a_s > 0` denote the temporal and spatial lattice spacings with
anisotropy `ξ := a_s / a_τ > 0`. Define the canonical leading-order
Trotter dictionary on the anisotropic Wilson plaquette surface (T-AT,
cited):

```text
β_σ(g_bare, ξ) := 2 N_c / (g_bare² · ξ)                          (LO-σ)
β_τ(g_bare, ξ) := 2 N_c · ξ / g_bare²                             (LO-τ)
```

**Hypotheses.**

(W-sub) The retained parent W-substitution closure carried by
`G_BARE_FORCED_VIA_WARD_SUBSTITUTION_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-17.md`:

```text
g_bare² · (1/(2 N_c)) = F_Htt^(0)(g_bare)²  =  1/6   (at N_c = 3)   (1)
```

with no a_τ, a_s, or ξ symbols anywhere in the load-bearing chain.

(LO-T-AT) The canonical leading-order Trotter dictionary
`(β_σ, β_τ)` as defined above, parameterizing the anisotropic Wilson
plaquette surface in terms of `(g_bare, ξ)` (carried by the cited
T-AT dictionary).

**Conclusion (T1) (ξ-invariance of g_bare² substitution).**
Substitution of (W1), (W2), (AN), (NC) into the W-substitution chain
at any ξ ∈ (0, ∞) yields exactly `g_bare² = 1`. In particular the
result of the closure is identical at `ξ = 1` (isotropic), `ξ = 4`,
`ξ = 16`, and any other anisotropy.

**Conclusion (T2) (geometric-mean invariant).** The product
`β_σ · β_τ` is ξ-independent:

```text
β_σ(g_bare, ξ) · β_τ(g_bare, ξ)  =  (2 N_c / g_bare²)²                (2)
sqrt(β_σ · β_τ)                  =  2 N_c / g_bare²  =:  β_iso(g_bare) (3)
```

so the geometric mean of the anisotropic doublet `(β_σ, β_τ)` recovers
the isotropic Wilson coefficient `β_iso = 2 N_c / g_bare²` for every ξ.
At `g_bare = 1`, `N_c = 3`, this gives `sqrt(β_σ · β_τ) = 6`
identically.

**Conclusion (T3) (ratio-form orbit parameterization).** The
ξ-degree-of-freedom is fully carried by the ratio:

```text
β_τ(g_bare, ξ) / β_σ(g_bare, ξ)  =  ξ²                                (4)
```

so the C-iso orbit is parameterized exclusively by `ξ²` in the
β-doublet, with the bare coupling appearing only in the geometric mean
(3). Conversely, given any pair `(β_σ, β_τ) > 0` satisfying the
dictionary, one recovers

```text
g_bare²  =  2 N_c / sqrt(β_σ · β_τ),    ξ  =  sqrt(β_τ / β_σ)         (5)
```

so the map `(g_bare, ξ) ↔ (β_σ, β_τ)` is a bijection of `R+² → R+²`,
and the g_bare coordinate is the geometric-mean direction while ξ is
the ratio direction. No mixing.

**Conclusion (T4) (counterfactual at ξ ≠ 1).** Specializing (T1) at
`ξ ∈ {1/4, 1, 4, 16}`, the substitution closure still produces
`g_bare² = 1` for all four anisotropies, demonstrating the invariance
is parametric in ξ, not coincidental at ξ = 1.

## 2. Proof

**`(T1)`.** The W-substitution chain consists of four identities (W1),
(W2), (AN), (NC) plus the algebra. Each is reproduced verbatim from
the parent narrow theorem. Inspect each in turn:

- (W1) `F_Htt^(0)(g_bare) = 1/sqrt(6)`: no a_τ, a_s, ξ symbols; the
  retained Ward Rep-B-independence identity is a function of
  `g_bare` alone on the `Q_L = (2,3)` block.
- (W2) `F_Htt^(0)(g_bare)² = g_bare² / (2 N_c)`: no a_τ, a_s, ξ
  symbols; the same-1PI coefficient identity is purely a function of
  `g_bare` and `N_c` on the same block.
- (AN) `F² = c0 ∧ F² = g²/(2N) ⇒ g² = 2 N c0`: pure symbolic
  polynomial algebra over symbols `(F, g, N, c0)`, no lattice spacings.
- (NC) `N_c = 3`: integer rank from the graph-first SU(3) integration
  authority, no lattice spacings.

The substitution `(F, g, N, c0) = (F_Htt^(0), g_bare, N_c, 1/6)` at
`N_c = 3` produces `g_bare² = 2 · 3 · 1/6 = 1`. This substitution
involves no lattice-spacing symbols and is therefore invariant under
any change of `(a_τ, a_s)` including the orbit motion
`a_τ → a_τ/ξ, a_s → a_s` for arbitrary ξ > 0. ∎(T1)

**`(T2)`.** Direct computation:

```text
β_σ · β_τ  =  (2 N_c / (g_bare² ξ)) · (2 N_c ξ / g_bare²)
           =  (2 N_c)² / g_bare⁴
           =  (2 N_c / g_bare²)²                                      (2′)
```

so `sqrt(β_σ · β_τ) = 2 N_c / g_bare² = β_iso(g_bare)`. ∎(T2)

**`(T3)`.** Direct computation:

```text
β_τ / β_σ  =  (2 N_c ξ / g_bare²) / (2 N_c / (g_bare² ξ))
           =  ξ²                                                      (4′)
```

Inversion: given `(β_σ, β_τ) > 0`, compute
`β_σ · β_τ = (2 N_c / g_bare²)²` so `g_bare² = 2 N_c / sqrt(β_σ β_τ)`,
and `β_τ / β_σ = ξ²` so `ξ = sqrt(β_τ / β_σ)`. The map is bijective on
`R+² → R+²` because both `(x, y) ↦ (xy, y/x)` and its inverse
`(p, r) ↦ (sqrt(p/r), sqrt(p·r))` are smooth and well-defined for
positive arguments. ∎(T3)

**`(T4)`.** Apply `(T1)` at ξ ∈ {1/4, 1, 4, 16}. In each case the
substitution algebra is independent of ξ, so the conclusion
`g_bare² = 1` is recovered identically. The corresponding `(β_σ, β_τ)`
doublets at `g_bare = 1, N_c = 3` are:

| ξ | β_σ = 6/ξ | β_τ = 6·ξ | sqrt(β_σ β_τ) | β_τ/β_σ |
|---|-----------|-----------|----------------|----------|
| 1/4 | 24 | 3/2 | 6 | 1/16 |
| 1   | 6  | 6   | 6 | 1    |
| 4   | 3/2 | 24 | 6 | 16   |
| 16  | 3/8 | 96 | 6 | 256  |

The geometric mean `sqrt(β_σ β_τ) = 6` is identically the isotropic
β = 6. The ratio `β_τ/β_σ = ξ²` carries the full anisotropy DOF. ∎(T4)
