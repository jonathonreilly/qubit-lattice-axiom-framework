## 1. Claim scope

Let `R^d` be a `d`-dimensional real vector space (`d ≥ 0`) endowed
with an inner product (metric) `g` of signature `sgn(g) ∈ {+1, -1}`
(Riemannian or Lorentzian). The graded exterior algebra is
`Λ^*(R^d) = ⊕_{p=0}^d Λ^p(R^d)`, with `dim_R Λ^p(R^d) = binom(d, p)`.
The Hodge star operator `*` is the unique linear map

```text
*  :  Λ^k(R^d)  →  Λ^{d-k}(R^d)                                     (S)
```

determined by the metric and the orientation via the identity
`α ∧ *β = g(α, β) vol` for all `α, β ∈ Λ^k(R^d)`, where `vol`
is the unit volume form. Then:

- **(H1) Source-target dimensions.** `*` maps `Λ^k(R^d)` (of dim
  `binom(d, k)`) to `Λ^{d-k}(R^d)` (of dim `binom(d, d-k) =
  binom(d, k)`). The map is a linear isomorphism between two vector
  spaces of equal dimension `binom(d, k)`.

- **(H2) Endomorphism condition.** `*` is an endomorphism of
  `Λ^k(R^d)` (i.e., `Λ^k(R^d) = Λ^{d-k}(R^d)`) iff `k = d - k`, i.e.,
  iff `d = 2k`. The space `Λ^k(R^d)` to which `*` returns is called
  the **middle-form space**.

- **(H3) Specialization to `k = 2`.** Taking `k = 2`, condition (H2)
  becomes `d = 2 · 2 = 4`. Hence `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ^2(R^d)`
  iff `d = 4`.

- **(H4) Square of Hodge star on a `k`-form.** The standard identity
  for the square of the Hodge star applied to a `k`-form on an
  oriented `d`-dimensional inner-product space of signature `sgn(g)`
  is

  ```text
  *^2 |_{Λ^k(R^d)}  =  (-1)^{k (d - k)} · sgn(g) · id_{Λ^k(R^d)}.    (Q)
  ```

  Specialized to `k = 2`: `*² = (-1)^{2(d - 2)} · sgn(g) · id =
  sgn(g) · id` (the factor `(-1)^{2(d-2)}` always equals `+1`).

- **(H5) Combined endomorphism + eigenvalue at `d = 4`.** Under
  (H3), at `d = 4`, `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ^2(R^4)`. By (H4),
  `*² = sgn(g) · id` on `Λ²(R^4)`:
  - **Riemannian case (`sgn(g) = +1`):** `*² = +id`, eigenvalues
    `±1`. The decomposition
    `Λ²(R^4) = Λ²_+(R^4) ⊕ Λ²_-(R^4)` into self-dual (`*ω = +ω`)
    and anti-self-dual (`*ω = -ω`) subspaces is well-defined over
    `R`. Each subspace has dimension 3 (since `dim Λ²(R^4) = 6` and
    `*` has 3 eigenvectors at each eigenvalue ±1; see explicit basis
    below).
  - **Lorentzian case (`sgn(g) = -1`):** `*² = -id`, eigenvalues
    `±i`. The decomposition exists over `C`:
    `Λ²(R^4)_C = Λ²_+(R^4)_C ⊕ Λ²_-(R^4)_C` with eigenvalues `+i, -i`.

- **(H6) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {2, 3}` (`k = 2`).** For `d = 2`,
  `*` maps `Λ²(R²)` (of dim 1) to `Λ^{2-2}(R²) = Λ⁰(R²)` (of dim 1).
  This is NOT an endomorphism of `Λ²(R²)`; it maps to the scalar
  space `Λ⁰(R²) = R`. The self-dual / anti-self-dual eigenspace
  decomposition of `Λ²(R²)` under `*` is not defined.

  For `d = 3`, `*` maps `Λ²(R³)` (of dim 3) to `Λ^{3-2}(R³) = Λ¹(R³)`
  (of dim 3). This is the classical 3-dimensional Hodge duality
  identifying 2-forms with 1-forms (vectors), not an endomorphism of
  `Λ²(R³)`. The decomposition (H) does not apply.

- **(H7) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {5, 6, 7, 8, ...}` (`k = 2`).** For
  `d = 5`, `*` maps `Λ²(R⁵)` (of dim 10) to `Λ³(R⁵)` (of dim 10).
  These are isomorphic by (H1) but distinct spaces (different exterior
  degree). The decomposition (H) does not apply.

  For `d = 6`, `*` maps `Λ²(R⁶)` (of dim 15) to `Λ⁴(R⁶)` (of dim 15).
  Again distinct spaces. The middle-form space at `d = 6` is
  `Λ³(R⁶)` (of dim 20), where `*` IS an endomorphism — but this is
  the `k = 3` middle, not the `k = 2` middle.

  For `d ≥ 5`, the `k = 2` self-dual / anti-self-dual decomposition
  fails because `*` does not return to `Λ²(R^d)`.

- **(H8) Explicit eigenbasis at `d = 4` Riemannian.** At `d = 4`
  with metric `δ_{μν}` and orientation `e^1 ∧ e^2 ∧ e^3 ∧ e^4`, the
  six basis 2-forms are
  `{e^1 ∧ e^2, e^1 ∧ e^3, e^1 ∧ e^4, e^2 ∧ e^3, e^2 ∧ e^4, e^3 ∧ e^4}`.
  The Hodge star acts by

  ```text
  * (e^1 ∧ e^2)  =  e^3 ∧ e^4,    * (e^3 ∧ e^4)  =  e^1 ∧ e^2,
  * (e^1 ∧ e^3)  =  -e^2 ∧ e^4,   * (e^2 ∧ e^4)  =  -e^1 ∧ e^3,
  * (e^1 ∧ e^4)  =  e^2 ∧ e^3,    * (e^2 ∧ e^3)  =  e^1 ∧ e^4.
  ```

  Three self-dual (`+1` eigenvalue) basis 2-forms:

  ```text
  η_1^+  =  (1/√2) (e^1 ∧ e^2 + e^3 ∧ e^4),
  η_2^+  =  (1/√2) (e^1 ∧ e^3 - e^2 ∧ e^4),
  η_3^+  =  (1/√2) (e^1 ∧ e^4 + e^2 ∧ e^3).
  ```

  Three anti-self-dual (`-1` eigenvalue) basis 2-forms:

  ```text
  η_1^-  =  (1/√2) (e^1 ∧ e^2 - e^3 ∧ e^4),
  η_2^-  =  (1/√2) (e^1 ∧ e^3 + e^2 ∧ e^4),
  η_3^-  =  (1/√2) (e^1 ∧ e^4 - e^2 ∧ e^3).
  ```

  Together these span `Λ²(R^4)`, giving the orthogonal direct-sum
  decomposition (H). Each subspace is 3-dimensional, matching the
  identification `Λ²_± ≅ R³` and the structure underlying
  `so(4) ≅ su(2)_+ ⊕ su(2)_-`.

- **(H9) Uniqueness over `d ∈ {0, 1, ..., 8}`.** Combining (H3)
  and (H6)-(H7): among `d ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}`, the Hodge
  star `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ²(R^d)` (i.e., the middle-2-form
  self-dual / anti-self-dual decomposition (H) is well-defined)
  exactly at `d = 4`. The forward direction at `d = 4` is (H8). The
  converse uses (H3): `* : Λ² → Λ^{d-2}` is an endomorphism of
  `Λ²` only when `d - 2 = 2`, i.e., `d = 4`.

## 5. Proof

The argument is direct linear-algebra dimension counting on the
exterior algebra `Λ^*(R^d)` and explicit construction of the eigenspace
decomposition at `d = 4`.

### 5.1 (H1)/(H2) Hodge star domain and endomorphism condition

By definition, `* : Λ^k(R^d) → Λ^{d-k}(R^d)`. The source and target
have equal dimension `dim Λ^k(R^d) = dim Λ^{d-k}(R^d) = binom(d, k)`
(by the symmetry of binomial coefficients `binom(d, k) = binom(d, d-k)`).
However, `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ^k(R^d)` (i.e., source = target
as vector subspaces of `Λ^*(R^d)`) if and only if `k = d - k`, i.e.,
`d = 2k`.

### 5.2 (H3) Specialization to `k = 2`

Substituting `k = 2`: `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ²(R^d)` iff
`d = 4`. This is the unique integer dimension where the Hodge star
maps 2-forms back to 2-forms.

### 5.3 (H4) Square identity `*² = (-1)^{k(d-k)} sgn(g) · id`

The standard identity for the Hodge star squared is

```text
*^2 α  =  (-1)^{k(d-k)} · sgn(g) · α       for α ∈ Λ^k(R^d),
```

derived from the defining relation `α ∧ *β = g(α, β) vol` and the
graded-commutativity of the wedge product. For `k = 2`,
`(-1)^{k(d-k)} = (-1)^{2(d-2)} = +1` (since the exponent is even for
every integer `d`). Hence

```text
*² |_{Λ²(R^d)}  =  sgn(g) · id_{Λ²(R^d)}                            (Q')
```

whenever `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ²(R^d)`, i.e., (by H3) whenever
`d = 4`.

### 5.4 (H5) Eigenspace decomposition at `d = 4`

By (Q'), at `d = 4` the operator `*` on `Λ²(R^4)` satisfies
`*² = sgn(g) · id`. The eigenvalue analysis:

- **Riemannian (`sgn(g) = +1`):** `*² = +id`. The minimal polynomial
  of `*` divides `x² - 1 = (x - 1)(x + 1)`, so `*` is diagonalizable
  over `R` with eigenvalues `±1`. The eigenspaces
  `Λ²_±(R^4) = ker(* ∓ id)` give the decomposition
  `Λ²(R^4) = Λ²_+ ⊕ Λ²_-`. By the explicit basis (H8) below, each
  eigenspace is 3-dimensional.

- **Lorentzian (`sgn(g) = -1`):** `*² = -id`. The minimal polynomial
  divides `x² + 1 = (x - i)(x + i)`, so `*` is diagonalizable over
  `C` with eigenvalues `±i`. The complexified eigenspaces give
  `Λ²(R^4)_C = Λ²_+ ⊕ Λ²_-` with eigenvalues `+i, -i`. The Riemannian
  real decomposition has no Lorentzian real analog (since `±i ∉ R`).

### 5.5 (H8) Explicit Riemannian eigenbasis at `d = 4`

At `d = 4` Riemannian with `g = δ`, the action of `*` on the canonical
basis 2-forms is determined by `e^μ ∧ * (e^μ ∧ e^ν) = δ_{ρρ} δ_{σσ}
\cdot vol`. Explicit computation gives

```text
* (e^μ ∧ e^ν)  =  (1/2!) ε_{μνρσ} e^ρ ∧ e^σ                          (E)
```

where `ε_{μνρσ}` is the Levi-Civita symbol with `ε_{1234} = +1`. The
six basis 2-forms `{e^μ ∧ e^ν}_{μ<ν}` split into three "spatial-time"
pairs `{e^1∧e^2, e^3∧e^4}`, `{e^1∧e^3, e^2∧e^4}`, `{e^1∧e^4, e^2∧e^3}`
each closed under `*`. The (anti)-symmetric combinations

```text
η^±_1 = (e^1∧e^2 ± e^3∧e^4) / √2,
η^±_2 = (e^1∧e^3 ∓ e^2∧e^4) / √2,
η^±_3 = (e^1∧e^4 ± e^2∧e^3) / √2
```

are eigenvectors of `*` with eigenvalues `±1` respectively. Each
triple `{η^+_i}` and `{η^-_i}` is an orthonormal basis of a
3-dimensional subspace, and the two subspaces are mutually orthogonal
under the metric-induced inner product on `Λ²(R^4)`. Hence

```text
Λ²(R^4)  =  span_R(η^+_1, η^+_2, η^+_3)  ⊕  span_R(η^-_1, η^-_2, η^-_3)
        =:  Λ²_+(R^4)  ⊕  Λ²_-(R^4).
```

### 5.6 (H6) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {2, 3}`

For `d = 2`: `* : Λ²(R²) → Λ⁰(R²)`. Source space dim = 1
(`binom(2, 2) = 1`), target space dim = 1 (`binom(2, 0) = 1`). These
are distinct subspaces of `Λ^*(R²)`: `Λ²` is the 2-form line spanned
by `e^1 ∧ e^2`, `Λ⁰` is the scalar line `R`. The Hodge star takes
`e^1 ∧ e^2 ↦ 1` (the scalar). It is NOT an endomorphism of `Λ²(R²)`.

For `d = 3`: `* : Λ²(R³) → Λ¹(R³)`. Both have dimension 3, but
`Λ² ≠ Λ¹` as subspaces (2-forms versus 1-forms). The Hodge star
realizes the classical identification of 2-forms with vectors in 3D
(`* (dx ∧ dy) = dz`, etc.), but is NOT an endomorphism of `Λ²(R³)`.

In both cases, the self-dual / anti-self-dual eigenspace decomposition
(H) of `Λ²(R^d)` under `*` is undefined, because `*` does not act
**on** `Λ²(R^d)`.

### 5.7 (H7) Counterfactual at `d ∈ {5, 6, 7, ...}`

For `d = 5`: `* : Λ²(R⁵) → Λ³(R⁵)`. Both dim 10. These are distinct
subspaces (2-forms vs 3-forms). Not an endomorphism.

For `d = 6`: `* : Λ²(R⁶) → Λ⁴(R⁶)`. Both dim 15. Distinct subspaces.
Not an endomorphism. (Note: at `d = 6`, the middle-form space is
`Λ³(R⁶)` of dim 20, where `*` IS an endomorphism — but `k = 3`, not
`k = 2`.)

For general `d ≥ 5`: `Λ^{d-2}(R^d)` has dimension `binom(d, d-2) =
binom(d, 2)` equal to `dim Λ²(R^d)`, but as a subspace of
`Λ^*(R^d)` it is `Λ^{d-2}` of degree `d - 2 ≠ 2`. The Hodge star is a
linear isomorphism between `Λ²(R^d)` and `Λ^{d-2}(R^d)` (not an
endomorphism). The decomposition (H) does not apply.

### 5.8 (H9) Uniqueness

Combining (H3) and (H6)-(H7): among `d ∈ {0, 1, ..., 8}`, the Hodge
star `*` is an endomorphism of `Λ²(R^d)` exactly at `d = 4`. The
forward direction at `d = 4` is constructed explicitly in (H8). The
converse uses (H3): `* : Λ² → Λ^{d-2}` is an endomorphism only when
`d - 2 = 2`, equivalently `d = 4`. QED.
