# KCPT D2 commutant extension: translation localization of the double-cover class (L=4 and L=6) (bounded theorem)

**Type:** bounded_theorem

registry id: `kcpt_d2_commutant_extension_translation_localization`

Date: 2026-07-25.

The Unit-26 central extension class `1 → {±I} → Comm(D2) → T ⋊ B₃ → 1`, restricted
along subgroups of `T ⋊ B₃`, is trivial on the entire point-group side (B₃, O, A₄, D₃
all split via an explicit `−I`-free section) and non-trivial on every
translation-containing subgroup tested (T, T_even, T_even⋊A₄, each certified by
`−I ∈ K`). The translation restriction has the unique nontrivial B₃-invariant alternating
bimultiplicative commutator form `β(s,t) = (−1)^{(Σs)(Σt) − s·t}` together with a
surface-dependent 2-torsion square class `q((L/2)v) = (−1)^{(L/2)² Σ_{i<j} v_i v_j}`.
Consequently `Comm ≅ T̃ ⋊ S` internally with `S ≅ B₃`, and the extension's failure to split is
already witnessed by the translation double cover `T̃ = π⁻¹(T)`.

## 1. Objects and setup

  20736 at L=6). Elements are pairs `(p, s)` with `p` a permutation of the N sites and `s`
  a `{±1}` sign field; the identity is `+I = (id, +1)` and the central element is
  `−I = (id, −1)`.
- **Unit-26 extension (restated).** The projection `π(p, s) = p` sends `Comm` onto the
  quotient group `Q = T ⋊ B₃` (`|Q| = 48N`), with kernel exactly `{+I, −I}` (gate G2). The
  kernel is central, so
  `1 → {±I} → Comm → T ⋊ B₃ → 1` is a central extension; Unit 26 established it is a
  NON-SPLIT double cover (`−I ∈ [Comm, Comm]`). Here `T = Z_L³` acts by lattice
  translations and `B₃` is the order-48 group of signed coordinate permutations (the
  hyperoctahedral group), realized on the lattice by the 0-fixing (linear) permutations.
- **Canonical-lift convention.** For each downstairs permutation `p ∈ π(Comm)` write
  `lift(p)` for the first-seen element of `Comm` with permutation part `p` (deterministic
  in the enumerator's insertion order). Both lifts of `p` are `lift(p)` and `−I · lift(p)`.
  The translation lift `t̃_μ = lift(translation by e_μ)`; the section generators
  `s_cyc, s_swap, s_flip = lift` of the three B₃ generator matrices
  `A_cyc = [[0,0,1],[1,0,0],[0,1,0]]`, `A_swap = [[0,1,0],[1,0,0],[0,0,1]]`,
  `A_flip = [[-1,0,0],[0,1,0],[0,0,1]]`.

**Splitting criterion (lemma, with proof).** Let `1 → {±I} → E → Q → 1` be a central
extension with kernel `{±I}`. Define `K(E) = ⟨ g², [g,h] : g, h ∈ E ⟩`, the
subgroup generated by all squares and commutators. If the extension splits then `E ≅ Q × {±I}`
as groups (a splitting homomorphic section `S` together with the central kernel gives
`E = S × {±I}`); in the direct
product every square `(q,ε)² = (q², +I)` and every commutator `[(q,ε),(q',ε')] = ([q,q'], +I)`
lands in the `+I` coset, so `−I ∉ K(E)`. Contrapositively, `−I ∈ K(E)` certifies that the
extension is NON-SPLIT. Conversely an explicit subgroup `S ⊂ E` with `π|S` bijective onto `Q`
and `−I ∉ S` is a section and certifies that it SPLITS. Because `E/E²` is elementary abelian
(every element squares into `E²`), commutators already lie in `E²`, so
`K(E) = ⟨ g² : g ∈ E ⟩ = E²`; the runner computes `K` this way and additionally exhibits the
named commutator witnesses, which agree.

**T2 (the translation commutator form β; gates G10–G16).** Because `{±I}` is central, the
commutator of two translation lifts depends only on their images, defining a form
`β(s, t) := [t̃(s), t̃(t)] ∈ {±I}` for `s, t ∈ T`. Measured over all `N²` pairs (4096 at L=4,
46656 at L=6):

- `β` is well-defined: every one of the `N²` translation-lift commutators lies in `{±I}` (G10).
- Closed form `β(s, t) = (−1)^{(Σs)(Σt) − s·t}` on all pairs (G11), with unit matrix
  `[[1,−1,−1],[−1,1,−1],[−1,−1,1]]` (G12).
- `β` is bimultiplicative — the closed-form exponent verified on all `N²` pairs (G11) is
  Z-bilinear in `(s, t)`, and first-slot additivity is re-checked directly on unit
  increments (G13) — and alternating: `β(s,s) = +I` for all `s`,
  `β(s,t)β(t,s) = +I` for all pairs (G14).
- `β` is B₃-invariant: `β(As, At) = β(s, t)` for the three B₃ generator matrices and all pairs
  (G15).

**Uniqueness (G16).** `β` factors through `T/2T ≅ F₂³` (all 64 mod-2 classes are internally
consistent; a genuine mod-2 factoring check). Among ALL 512 bimultiplicative `{±1}`-forms on
`F₂³` — the 3×3 matrices `M` over `F₂` via `(−1)^{uᵀMv}` — exactly 8 are alternating
(symmetric, zero diagonal), exactly 2 of those are invariant under the induced point-group
action (which factors through S₃ mod 2), and the unique nontrivial invariant one is
`M_ij = 1 − δ_ij`. Pulled back to the lattice it equals the measured `β` on all `N²` pairs.

form) disagree somewhere (an explicit wrong-value rejector count). Algebraic remark, bounded
to the computed surfaces: the closed form descends to `Z_L³` because L is even at both L
(`(−1)^L = 1`), so `β` is a well-defined form on the finite translation group at L ∈ {4, 6}.

**T3 (unit-lift orders and the square class; gates G17, G18, G21).** The unit-translation lifts
have order exactly L with `(t̃_μ)^L = +I` at both surfaces (G17). On the 2-torsion subgroup
generated by the `(L/2)e_μ`, the square class `q((L/2)v) := t̃((L/2)v)²` obeys

    q((L/2)v) = (−1)^{(L/2)² Σ_{i<j} v_i v_j}          (v ∈ {0,1}³),

matching measurement on all 8 classes (G18). Four-line derivation from T2 and G17:
(i) for 2-torsion `s`, `t̃(s)²` is central and lift-independent, so `q(s) ∈ {±I}` is
well-defined; (ii) `q` is a quadratic refinement of `β`:
`q((L/2)v) = Π_μ q((L/2)e_μ)^{v_μ} · Π_{i<j} β((L/2)e_i, (L/2)e_j)^{v_i v_j}`; (iii)
`q((L/2)e_μ) = (t̃_μ^{L/2})² = t̃_μ^L = +I` by G17; (iv) by bimultiplicativity
`β((L/2)e_i, (L/2)e_j) = β(e_i, e_j)^{(L/2)²} = (−1)^{(L/2)²}` for `i ≠ j`, giving the boxed
formula. The prefactor `(L/2)²` is even at L=4 (= 4) so `q ≡ +I` (q_minus_count 0), and odd at
L=6 (= 9) so `q = −I` on exactly `{(3,3,0), (3,0,3), (0,3,3), (3,3,3)}` (q_minus_count 4). The
same `β` governs both surfaces while `q` differs — an exact structural difference between the
two computed surfaces, governed entirely by the parity of `(L/2)²` (cross-surface gate G21).

**T5 (derived-cover identification; gate G8).** The order-`12N` obstruction subgroup on the

## 4. The localization picture

The Unit-26 double cover has an untwisted point-group restriction and an obstructed translation
restriction. Over the point-group factor the extension is honestly a direct product:
an explicit `−I`-free section `S ≅ B₃` exists, and its sub-sections realize O, A₄, and D₃ as
genuine `−I`-free subgroups, so restricting the class to any of these point groups gives the
trivial class. Restricting to any of the tested translation-containing subgroups (T, T_even,
T_even⋊A₄) makes `−I` reappear as a square/commutator: the commutator of two unit-translation
lifts along different axes is `−I`
(the off-diagonal `−1` of the `β` matrix). The alternating form `β` and 2-torsion square class
`q` record two exact invariants of that obstructed translation restriction. The two data pieces
separate cleanly: `β` is surface-independent (same unit matrix, same closed form at L=4 and
L=6), while `q` toggles with the parity of `(L/2)²` (trivial at L=4, nontrivial at L=6). The
internal decomposition `Comm = T̃ ⋊ S` packages the localization: `S` is a flat section, and
the global failure to split is already visible in `T̃ = π⁻¹(T)`. No completeness claim about
mixed translation–point-group extension data is made.
