## 2. Uniform one-particle closeness by a kernel argument

Put G(t)=F*exp(-itD)F and

    c_G=integral_0^infinity ||G(t)||dt,
    b_lambda=|lambda| ||M|| c_G,
    d_lambda=2 b_lambda/(1-b_lambda), b_lambda<1.         (C2)

The zero-integral premise gives finite c_G. For completeness, splitting D
into its massive positive and negative bands gives scalar oscillatory integrals
with Schwartz amplitudes vanishing to order at least two at p=0. The exact
coordinate p=x sqrt(2m+x^2)/v turns E into m+x^2 and the amplitude into
x^2 b(x), b Schwartz. Integration by parts gives t^-1 times an integral of
exp(+/-itx^2)[b(x)+x b'(x)]. Splitting that last integral at |x|=|t|^-1/2
and integrating by parts outside gives O(|t|^-1/2). Hence G(t)=O(t^-3/2).
On bounded times G is continuous and bounded. Also G(-t)=G(t)*, so

    integral_R ||G(t)||dt=2c_G.                          (C3)

For finite T define the synthesis map

    L_T z=integral_0^T exp(itD)F z(t)dt,
    L_T*:xi -> [t -> F*exp(-itD)xi].

The integral is a Bochner integral for z in L2 on this finite interval.
Its squared norm is the double integral with kernel G(t-s). Extending z
by zero and applying the scalar convolution inequality to its pointwise
norm gives

    ||L_T||=||L_T*|| <= sqrt(2c_G).                       (C4)

This proves a full-space analysis/synthesis estimate directly from the
kernel, without importing a spectral-density constant. The bounded-time
integrals and then density justify the calculation for every L2 input.

For an arbitrary xi in K, set F_t=exp(itD)F, x(t)=F_t*Z(t)xi and
x0(t)=F_t*xi. The exact equation for Z gives the causal identity

    x(t)=x0(t)-i lambda integral_0^t G(t-s)M u(s)x(s)ds.   (C5)

The causal operator has L2 norm at most ||M||c_G. The Neumann inverse
therefore gives

    ||x||_L2 <= sqrt(2c_G)/(1-b_lambda) ||xi||.

Substituting into Z(T)xi-xi=-i lambda L_T(Mu x), and applying (C4), proves

    sup_(T>=0, |u|<=1) ||Z(T)-I|| <= d_lambda.            (C6)

The use of (C4) is on each ordinary vector xi followed by a supremum.
No Hilbert--Schmidt norm of the identity is used. All constants are uniform
in T. If c_G=0, continuity at zero implies F*F=0, so the dynamics is free
and (C6) holds with d_lambda=0. For fixed nonzero channels d_lambda=O(|lambda|).

## 3. Correlation words and the CAR state limit

For any r>=1 and any real h_1,...,h_r in K, write
A=gamma(h_1)...gamma(h_r). A telescoping product and (C1),(C6) yield

    |Tr(rho_Y(T) A)-<Omega,A Omega>|
       <= r d_lambda product_j ||h_j||.                 (C7)

Indeed each transformed vector Z*h_j has norm ||h_j|| and differs from
h_j by at most d_lambda||h_j||. Each replacement in the product therefore
has the displayed field norm. Odd words happen to have zero expectation by
parity, but no parity cancellation is needed for this bound.

For the full state, S*=Z*exp(iTD). Replace the transformed vectors by
exp(iTD)h_j. Their norms are unchanged, and the vacuum is invariant under
the simultaneous free field automorphism. Consequently (C7) holds for rho_W
with the same constant. In particular it holds uniformly over every family
of probe vectors with bounded norms and every bounded degree r, including
vectors that depend on lambda, T, position or free query time.

Let CAR(K_R) denote the norm closure of finite complex linear combinations
of these real-field words and the identity, in this Fock representation.
For any fixed A in that C* algebra,

    sup_(T>=0, |u|<=1) |Tr(rho(T)A)-<Omega,A Omega>| ->0
                     as lambda ->0.                    (C8)

To prove it, choose a fixed polynomial P with ||A-P||<eta. The two state
errors cost at most 2eta, and (C7) makes the polynomial error tend to zero.
First take lambda to zero, then eta to zero. This is convergence of each
fixed bounded observable, with no quantitative polynomial degree asserted
for a general A. It does not replace that quantifier by a supremum over the
unit ball of the entire C* algebra.

## 4. Finite-mode occupation, reduced states and resource bounds

For rho_Y the exact positive one-particle occupation is

    N(T)=PZ(T)P_-Z(T)*P.

Since P P_-=0, (C6) implies 0<=N(T)<=d_lambda^2 I_h. For rho_W it is
the free unitary conjugate of this operator, so the same inequality holds.
Thus, for every finite-rank orthogonal projection Q on h of rank d,

    Tr[rho(T) dGamma(Q)] = Tr_h[Q N(T)] <= d d_lambda^2. (C9)

Q may depend on lambda and T. The finite rank makes dGamma(Q) bounded by d,
so this identity has no unbounded expectation issue. Fixing an orthonormal
basis of Qh gives a direct finite sum of exact occupation identities.

Factor Fock(h) into the occupation spaces of Qh and its orthogonal complement
with a fixed fermionic ordering. Let rho_Q be the reduced density operator
on Fock(Qh) and Omega_Q its vacuum. The operator inequality

    I-|Omega_Q><Omega_Q| <= dGamma(I_Q)

gives an excitation probability at most d d_lambda^2. A direct purification
argument then gives

    ||rho_Q-|Omega_Q><Omega_Q|||_1
        <= 2 min(1,sqrt(d) d_lambda).                    (C10)

To see the argument without a fidelity theorem, project the normalized
full vector onto Omega_Q tensor its complement and normalize that projection.
Its squared overlap with the original vector is the vacuum probability p.
The two pure density operators differ in trace norm by 2sqrt(1-p), as a
two-dimensional diagonalization shows. Partial trace is trace-norm contractive
by duality against operators A_Q tensor I with ||A_Q||<=1. The projected
vector has reduced state |Omega_Q><Omega_Q|. If p=0, use the trivial bound2.

In particular, if a finite-mode detector has excitation probability at
least eta>0, then necessarily

    rank Q >= eta/d_lambda^2.                            (C11)

Here d_lambda>0; if d_lambda=0, every finite-mode excitation probability is zero.
For small nonzero lambda and fixed channels this is an order lambda^-2
necessary rank resource. It is not a sufficient detector construction.
If range Q lies in the energy band E<=L, then 0<=Q E Q<=LQ and

    Tr[rho dGamma(Q E Q)] <= L d d_lambda^2.              (C12)

Thus collecting at least e0 of this bounded-band mean energy requires
d>=e0/(L d_lambda^2). No finite rank is inferred from a finite energy band:
the latter has infinite dimension on the supplied infinite line.

## 5. Global energy, vacuum weight and compact Fock observations

Now specialize u(t)=cos(omega t), fixed omega>0, and T=tau/lambda^2 with
fixed tau>=0. The independently reviewed V2 theorem supplies

    mu_lambda,T -> Law(omega N_tau),
    N_tau ~ Poisson(tau nu_omega), nu_omega=(pi/2)r(omega),
    E_lambda(T) -> tau omega nu_omega.                   (C13)

Here r is the exterior pair spectral density defined in that source. The
law convergence includes W1. For omega>2m the source gives explicit fixed
compact derivative-bump controls with nu_omega>0. At 0<omega<=2m the rate
is zero under the zero-integral premise. No general compact-control result
or fixed-nonzero-lambda late-time theorem is used.

The free gap gives ker H0=span{Omega} and H0>=m(I-P_Omega). Thus for any
energy cutoff a0 with 0<a0<m, 1_[0,a0](H0)=P_Omega. In the positive-rate
case omega>2m>a0, the limiting probability law has no mass at a0. Applying
weak convergence to (-infinity,a0] proves

    p_lambda:=<Omega,rho(T)Omega> -> exp(-tau nu_omega).  (C14)

The zero-rate case gives p_lambda->1 by the same argument (choose a0 away
from any positive jump) or directly from 1-p_lambda<=E_lambda/m. At tau=0
the state is exactly the vacuum. Therefore the exact pure-state distance
has the limit

    ||rho(T)-P_Omega||_1
       -> 2sqrt(1-exp(-tau nu_omega)).                   (C15)

The full and interaction states have identical p_lambda and the same
distance to the invariant vacuum. Equation (C15) is a computed distance
for this specified family of pure states, not a universal obstruction for
other protocols, preparations, finite-volume limits or physical models.

There is also a precise limit for each compact operator K on Fock(h):

    Tr[rho(T) K] -> exp(-tau nu_omega)<Omega,K Omega>.    (C16)

Proof: vectors made from finitely many fixed creation operators on Omega
are dense. For a unit vector xi in a finite-dimensional collection of such
nonvacuum vectors, take Q to contain all its one-particle slots. The positive
operator |xi><xi| is bounded above by the event of at least one excitation
in Qh. Consequently <xi,rho xi><=rank(Q)d_lambda^2. Positivity bounds every
mixed matrix element with such a vector by the square root of this number
times the other diagonal bound. All nonvacuum matrix elements thus tend to
zero, and the vacuum matrix element tends to (C14). Density and ||rho||<=1
extend this to arbitrary fixed Fock vectors. It gives convergence on finite
rank operators; operator-norm approximation and ||rho||_1=1 extend it to K.

For tau nu_omega>0, the functional in (C16) has trace less than one when
represented by exp(-tau nu_omega)P_Omega. This is compatible with the
normalized CAR state limit (C8): in infinite dimension, fixed compact Fock
tests and the norm-closed CAR algebra are different test collections. The
identity belongs to the latter and is not compact. No identification of
the two limiting topologies or trace-preserving compactness is assumed.

The conclusions (C8),(C13),(C15),(C16) are simultaneous. They describe fixed
bounded correlation observations, the measured total energy law, pure-state
distance and compact-operator observations separately. They do not establish
a spatial escape velocity, a local energy-current profile, or uniform
convergence over every bounded operator in a spatial local algebra.
