# Lattice handedness: a two-band finite-range free walker is left–right balanced whether time flows or ticks (and every finite-range free tick has zero three-dimensional winding); a chiral free tick needs tails

**Date:** 2026-09-27
**Type:** bounded_theorem
**Status:** exact results, one theorem resting on standard algebraic K-theory,
and finite numerical checks; unaudited; independent checks recorded below.

## In one paragraph

A free two-component walker that hops a bounded distance on a
translation-invariant lattice always has a mirror twin, whether it hops
continuously or in ticks. For more components, the winding that counts net
handedness vanishes. That the twins then pair up gap by gap rests on a cited
relation. For the
ticking walker the twin hides halfway to the zone edge, where a first count
misses it. A free walker without a twin must reach beyond any fixed distance.
Its amplitude can die off exponentially, by about a factor of five per site
in the numerical example here, but it never stops. It must also tick: no
continuous flow of time, generated by any Hamiltonian, produces it.

The real world is left–right asymmetric. Within the class treated here
(free, translation-invariant, finite-component walkers), that asymmetry has
to come from somewhere else:
- interactions that make the mirror twins heavy;
- a genuinely ticking rule with tails;
- structure outside this class, such as boundaries, extra dimensions,
  disorder, or motion that depends on a gauge background.

## Premises and declared objects

- **Axioms** (`docs/MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-06-29.md`). The walker's dynamics is
  not axiom content. The memo leaves "update laws" open, and every walker here
  is a supplied comparator.
- **Strictly local.** A translation-invariant one-body operator that moves an
  amplitude a bounded number of sites. Its Bloch matrix has entries that are
  Laurent polynomials in `z_j = e^{ik_j}`.
- **Chirality of a node.**
  - Flowing time: at a zero `k_0` of the traceless part `d(k)` of
    `H = d_0 + d.sigma`, chirality is `sign det (dd/dk)(k_0)`.
  - Ticking time: write `U = a - i b.sigma` (two bands, `det U = 1`). At a
    node `U(k_0) = +-1`, `U ~ +-(1 - i (+-J) dk.sigma)` with
    `J = (db/dk)(k_0)`. The local Weyl form around quasi-energy `0` (for `+`)
    or `pi` (for `-`) has chirality `sign det(+-J)`.
- **Winding number.** `W3[U] = -(1/24 pi^2) int_{T^3} tr(U^-1 dU)^3`,
  normalised so that `U ~ 1 - i k.sigma` counts `+1`. For `U` in `SU(2)` it
  equals the degree of `U : T^3 -> S^3`.

## T1 — flowing time: the partners are forced (Nielsen–Ninomiya, in-framework proof)

*Statement.* Let `d : T^3 -> R^3` be continuous, `C^1` near its zeros, with
nondegenerate zeros.
Then the chiralities of its zeros sum to zero.

*Proof.* View `d` as a map `T^3 -> R^3 ∪ {∞} = S^3`. It is bounded, so it
misses `∞`. A map that is not onto has degree 0. The degree also equals the
sum of the local degrees at the preimages of the regular value `0`, and these
are the chiralities. ∎

The campaign's walker `d = (sin k_1, sin k_2, sin k_3)` has exactly the eight
corner zeros, with chirality `(-1)^{#pi}`: four right, four left (exact). Five
random range-one two-band Hamiltonians, with all their zeros located
numerically, give total chirality 0 in each case (0, 4, 6, 8 and 6 zeros).

## T2 — ticking time: strict locality alone forces the partners

*Statement.* Let `U(k)` be unitary, `N x N` (any `N`), with entries in
`R = C[z_1^±1, z_2^±1, z_3^±1]`. Then `W3[U] = 0`. For `N = 2` with
`det U` constant, the nodes at quasi-energy `0` have total chirality `0`,
and so do the nodes at quasi-energy `pi`.

With a winding determinant `det U = c z^m`, the degeneracies need not sit at
`U = ±1`, and an odd winding can obstruct the reduction to `SU(2)`. That
case is not covered by the node statement. `W3 = 0` still holds.

*Proof.*
1. **Units.** `U^{-1} = U^dagger` also has entries in `R`, so `det U` is a
   unit of `R`: `det U = c z^m` with `|c| = 1`. Put
   `D = diag(c^{-1} z^{-m}, 1, ..., 1)`. Then `U' = U D` has determinant 1.
2. **Additivity.** On a closed 3-manifold `W3[fg] = W3[f] + W3[g]`, because
   the cross terms of `tr((fg)^{-1} d(fg))^3` are exact.
   - `D` depends on the single phase `m.k`, so its 3-form vanishes pointwise.
   - Hence `W3[U] = W3[U']`.
3. **K-theory (external mathematical tool).** `R` is a regular Noetherian
   ring. By the Bass–Heller–Swan decomposition and `K_0` of Laurent
   polynomial rings over a field, `K_1(R) = C^* ⊕ Z^3`. Both summands are
   detected by the determinant, so `SK_1(R) = 0`. Hence `U' ⊕ 1_M` is a
   product of elementary matrices `e_ij(r)`, `r` in `R`, for some `M`.
   (Suslin's theorem, `SL_n(R) = E_n(R)` for `n >= 3`, gives the same.)
4. **Homotopy.** Each `e_ij(r)` is joined to the identity by `e_ij(t r)`,
   `t` in `[0,1]`. So `U' ⊕ 1_M` is homotopic to the identity through maps
   `T^3 -> SL(C)`. The integral `W3` is a homotopy invariant for maps into
   `GL`, and `W3[U' ⊕ 1_M] = W3[U']`. Hence `W3[U] = 0`.
5. **Nodes, two bands, `det U` constant (take `det U = 1`).** `U` is a map
   `T^3 -> SU(2) = S^3`.
   - The nodes at quasi-energy `0` are the preimages of `+1`. Their local
     degrees are their chiralities, so their sum is the degree, `W3 = 0`.
   - Left multiplication by `-1` preserves orientation. The same argument
     applies to the preimages of `-1`, the nodes at `pi`. ∎

For more bands, the known relation between the chirality in a quasi-energy
gap and `W3` (Bessho and Sato, 2021; reference only) extends the node
statement. T2's `W3 = 0` holds for every `N`.

*Numerical consistency.* The runner computes `W3 = 0` to `1e-9` for:
- five random products of conditional shifts along random lattice vectors,
  with random coins;
- three random brickwork one-body circuits on a `2x2x2` cell, 16 modes,
  random `U(4)` gates.

## T3 — the ordered tick has sixteen nodes

*Statement.* `U_-(k) = S_x(k_x) S_y(k_y) S_z(k_z)`, with
`S_j(q) = cos q - i sin q sigma_j`, has exactly these nodes:

| where | `U` | quasi-energy | chirality | count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| corners, even number of `pi` | `+1` | `0` | right | 4 |
| corners, odd number of `pi` | `-1` | `pi` | right | 4 |
| `(pi/2)(+-1,+-1,+-1)`, product of signs `-1` | `+1` | `0` | left | 4 |
| `(pi/2)(+-1,+-1,+-1)`, product of signs `+1` | `-1` | `pi` | left | 4 |

*Proof of completeness.* `U_- = ±1` if and only if the rotation
`R_x(2k_x) R_y(2k_y) R_z(2k_z)` is the identity in `SO(3)`. Consider
`R_x(a) R_y(b) R_z(c) = I`.
- At `b = ±pi/2` there is no solution: `R_x(a) R_y(±pi/2)` sends `e_z` to
  `±e_x`, while `R_z(-c)` fixes `e_z`.
- Otherwise the x-y-z angles of the identity are unique up to the one
  alternative `(pi, pi, pi)`.

So `(2k_x, 2k_y, 2k_z)` is `(0,0,0)` or `(pi,pi,pi)` modulo `2 pi`: the
eight corners or the eight half-points. ∎ (This proof is from the
independent check.)

*Evidence.*
- The values `U = +-1` and the sign of `det(+-J)` at all sixteen points are
  exact (sympy).
- A root search from `12^3` starting points also finds exactly sixteen
  distinct nodes.
- `W3[U_-] = 0` numerically, as T2 requires.

The half-points are nodes because a half-turn about `x` followed by a
half-turn about `y` is a half-turn about `z`. In `SU(2)` that reads
`(-i sigma_x)(i sigma_y) = i sigma_z = e^{i(pi/2) sigma_z}`.

`U_-` is unchanged under `k -> k + G` for `G` in
`{(pi,pi,0), (pi,0,pi), (0,pi,pi)}`: the macro-step moves along body
diagonals, and `Z^3` splits into four BCC cosets. So the published BCC Weyl
walk, restricted to one coset, carries one right- and one left-handed node at
quasi-energy `0`, and the same at `pi`.

## T4 — a partner-free tick needs tails

*Statement.* Define

`U_g(k) = c_x c_y c_z - i g(k) s(k).sigma`,  `g(k) = sqrt((1 - c_x^2 c_y^2 c_z^2) / |s(k)|^2)`,

with `c_j = cos k_j`, `s_j = sin k_j`, and `g = 1` at the corners. Then:
- `U_g` is unitary.
- It is covariant under the 24 proper cubic rotations, acting on the coin by
  spin one-half.
- Its only nodes are the eight corners, all right-handed: `g > 0`, so
  `b = g s` vanishes only where `s` does.
- `W3[U_g] = 4`.
- Its real-space amplitude along an axis falls as `r^-6`: `1.0e-4`,
  `1.6e-6`, `2.7e-8`, `4.5e-10` at `r = 9, 17, 33, 65`, local exponents
  `-6.5, -6.2, -6.0`.

By T2 no strictly local tick has `W3 != 0`, so tails are unavoidable. Here
they come from `g`. Near a corner,
`g = 1 - (1/2)(sum_{i<j} q_i^2 q_j^2)/|q|^2 + ...`, whose leading correction
is homogeneous of degree two but not a polynomial. So `g` has no Taylor
expansion there.

*Exponential tails suffice (existence).*
1. Trigonometric polynomials are uniformly dense in the continuous functions
   on `T^3`. So `q_g = (a, g s)` has a trigonometric approximant within
   `1/4` in every component.
2. Averaging the approximant over the 24 proper rotations preserves the
   bound and makes it covariant.
3. The averaged `q` satisfies `|q - q_g| < 1/2`, hence `|q| > 1/2`, and
   `q/|q|` is unitary, real-analytic (so its Fourier coefficients decay
   exponentially), covariant, and homotopic to `U_g`: `W3 = 4`. ∎

*A numerical example (sampled, not certified).* Truncate the Fourier series of `(a, g s)` to
`|n_j| <= 2`, and call the result `q_tr`:
- On the runner's grid, `|q_tr| >= 0.9546` and `|q_tr - q_g| <= 0.0883`.
- The independent referee's off-grid optimisation gives `0.9546161892` and
  `0.0889311195`.
- These are sampled bounds, not certified ones. The existence argument above
  does not depend on them.
- On those sampled bounds, `V = q_tr/|q_tr|` would be unitary, real-analytic,
  covariant (the frequency box is invariant under signed permutations,
  checked to `1e-15`) and in `U_g`'s class. The runner computes
  `W3 = 4.00000` numerically. None of this is certified; the existence
  argument above is the proof.
- Its amplitude falls exponentially: `3.0e-4`, `2.9e-7`, `1.9e-10`,
  `5.9e-13` at `r = 5, 9, 13, 17`. That is about 1.5 e-folds per site.

So the obstruction of T2 is exactly finite range (Laurent polynomiality),
nothing more. The first exponentially local example came from the
independent check.

*No Hamiltonian flow produces it.* Let
`U = T exp(-i int_0^1 H(t) dt)` with `H(t, k)` continuous in `k` (local, or
with summable tails). Then `U_s = T exp(-i int_0^s H dt)` is a homotopy from
`1` to `U` through unitaries, so `W3[U] = 0`. A reflection-positive, hypercubic Euclidean action (the surface on which the
approved `kinetic_isotropy_primitive` protects one light cone) yields a
positive transfer matrix `T = e^{-aH}` with Hermitian `H`. The physical
evolution `e^{-iHt}` is then a Hamiltonian flow and is balanced. The converse
does not hold: a positive matrix alone does not construct a local
reflection-positive action. So on that surface free fermions are balanced
too.

## T5 — the magnetic picture

Put a field along `z` (Landau gauge; flux `2 pi/12` per plaquette on a
`12 x 12` torus). Each Weyl node contributes lowest-Landau-level branches
that move along or against `B`, according to its handedness.
- The ordered tick's states within `0.35` of quasi-energy 0:
  - at `k_z = 0` and at `k_z = pi`, 24 states, all moving along `B`;
  - at `k_z = pi/2`, 24 states, all moving against it. These are the
    left-handed partners of T3.
- The flowing walker at `k_z = 0` has 48 states in the same window: 24
  moving each way, once slopes are resolved within degenerate Landau levels.

The determinant of the slab's Floquet operator does not depend on `k_z`, so
the net spectral flow through any quasi-energy is zero. The partners carry the
return flow.

## What this does and does not say

What it says:
- The fork probe's "vector-like spectrum forced" extends from the Hamiltonian
  walker to two-band, constant-determinant, finite-range one-body ticks,
  whether time flows or ticks. This is proved at the level of nodes.
- For any number of bands, what is proved is `W3 = 0`. That the nodes in
  each gap then balance rests on the cited Floquet relation; it is not
  proved here.
- The hope that ticking time would let the lattice tell left from right
  (cycle 7's N7 steelman; this note's first census) is closed for free
  walkers.

Three routes to the Standard Model's chirality remain:
1. **Interactions that give the mirror partners mass** without breaking the
   gauge symmetries (symmetric mass generation). A known necessary
   condition is freedom from all anomalies of the symmetries to be
   preserved, including discrete ones.
   - With only the Standard Model gauge group, one generation's 15 Weyl
     fermions already cancel the gauge anomalies.
   - The count 16 (right-handed neutrino included) is required when gauged
     `B - L`, `Spin(10)`, or the corresponding discrete symmetry is also to
     be preserved.
   - The mirror-gapping proposals in the literature use that 16. That the
     mirror sector then gaps symmetrically in 3+1 dimensions is a conjecture
     of that literature, not an established result. This is literature
   (Eichten–Preskill; Wang–Wen; Tong and collaborators), reference only, not
   re-proved here. On this route the load-bearing framework question is the
   matter count: one qubit per site gives the naive walker 8 Weyl species
   (4 + 4). The registered default "do not enlarge the Qubit domain by
   inference" makes the count an owner-level question. The eight species
   come from the walker comparator; the Qubit axiom's `M_2(C)` does not by
   itself identify one site with eight Weyl fermions.
2. **A genuinely ticking update that is not of finite range.** Exponential
   tails suffice (T4). Such an update cannot be the Floquet operator of any
   continuous Hamiltonian flow, nor a positive transfer matrix. So it is in
   tension with the reflection-positive hypercubic surface that protects one
   light cone (the one-light-cone note of the same date). Overlap-type
   lattice fermions are the known Euclidean way to reconcile exponential
   locality, lattice chiral symmetry and the hypercubic regulator, through
   the Ginsparg–Wilson relation rather than through a chiral free spectrum.
   The axioms fix only the admissibility rule's range, not the dynamics', so
   this route is not excluded by the text.
3. **An extra dimension** (domain-wall fermions). The Lattice axiom supplies
   `Z^3` only.

Routes outside the proved class, not treated here:
- disorder or boundaries, including anomalous boundary modes (the
  domain-wall route);
- non-unitary record dynamics;
- motion that depends on the gauge background;
- an infinite internal dimension per site.

What it does not say:
- anything about interacting dynamics, gauge links, or which route nature
  uses;
- anything about many-body quantum cellular automata beyond one-body
  (quasi-free) walks, where exotic nontrivial automata exist in three
  dimensions.
