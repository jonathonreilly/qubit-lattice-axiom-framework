## Claim

Let `H_Λ = ⊗_{x ∈ Λ} ℂ²` be the finite-region qubit-lattice Hilbert
space (`Λ ⊂ Z³` finite), using the one-qubit operator algebra over the
`Z^3` spatial substrate of
[`MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md`](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md). Let
`A_Λ = ⊗_x M_2(ℂ)` and `P ∈ A_Λ` an orthogonal projection (`P² = P`,
`P = P†`).

**Premise (LSP — Lüders Sequential-Product instrument selection).**
The framework's measurement instrument for projective measurement of
`P` is the Lüders Kraus operator `K_P := P`. Equivalently, the
"square-root sequential product" `√P · X · √P` (Gudder–Lüders form)
reduces to `P X P` for idempotent `P`.

**Theorem (narrow, conditional on LSP).** For any effect
`E ∈ A_Λ` (i.e., `0 ≤ E ≤ 𝟙_Λ`) and any orthogonal projection `P`:

```text
M_{P, E}  :=  K_P† · E · K_P  =  P · E · P                              (1)
```

Equivalently, the post-measurement state update under the Lüders
instrument is

```text
σ ↦ σ|_P  =  (P σ P) / Tr(P σ P)                                        (2)
```

(assuming `Tr(P σ P) > 0`, i.e., outcome `P` has nonzero probability).

The conclusion (1) follows by direct algebra:
`K_P† E K_P = P† E P = P E P` (using `P† = P`). The conclusion (2)
follows from Bayes consistency on the Lüders instrument
(`σ|_P ∝ K_P σ K_P†`).

## What's load-bearing

**Conditional on LSP**, (1) and (2) are pure operator-algebra
identities that the runner verifies on multiple instrument families.

**Without LSP**, (1) is FALSE in general. The runner exhibits a
concrete counterexample (T5): the alternative Kraus
`K_P^twist := H · P` (where `H` is the Hadamard unitary) also
satisfies `K_P^twist† K_P^twist = P` (i.e., is a valid Kraus operator
for measuring `P`), but the resulting sequential composition

```text
M_{P, σ_x}^{twist}  =  K_P^twist† σ_x K_P^twist  ≠  P σ_x P
```

This is the literature observation from arXiv:0905.0596 /
arXiv:math/0211033: alternative sequential products exist on the
effect algebra, and the framework's choice of LSP carries load-bearing
content not derivable from the qubit-lattice operator algebra alone.

## Setup

By the one-qubit operator algebra on the `Z^3` spatial substrate, each
finite region's algebra is `A_Λ ≅ M_{2^|Λ|}(ℂ)`. An orthogonal
projection `P ∈ A_Λ` satisfies `P² = P`, `P = P†`. The projection
lattice is the standard `P(A_Λ)` (cf. the Gleason qubit-lattice narrow
theorem).

A **measurement instrument** for outcome `P` is a Kraus operator `K_P`
satisfying `K_P† K_P = P` (single-outcome Kraus normalization on the
effect `E = P`). The instrument's action on a state is
`σ ↦ K_P σ K_P†` (subnormalized) or `K_P σ K_P† / Tr(K_P σ K_P†)`
(post-selected).

**Existence of `K_P`** (in particular `K_P = P` itself) is supplied by
the retained-pending-chain
[`KRAUS_CHOI_REPRESENTATION_ON_QUBIT_LATTICE_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-20.md`](KRAUS_CHOI_REPRESENTATION_ON_QUBIT_LATTICE_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-20.md):
for the CPTP map `Φ(σ) = P σ P + (𝟙 - P) σ (𝟙 - P)` (Lüders
dephasing), the Kraus family is exactly `{P, 𝟙 - P}`. So `K_P = P` is
the Lüders selection for this canonical instrument.

**Non-uniqueness:** there are other instruments with the same POVM
`{P, 𝟙 - P}`. For any unitary `U_r` per outcome, `{U_1 P, U_2 (𝟙 - P)}`
is also a valid Kraus family. The Lüders selection picks `U_r = 𝟙`
("minimal disturbance"). This is the instrument-selection premise LSP.

## Sequential composition

Given two consecutive measurements:
- First: projective measurement of `P` with Lüders Kraus `K_P`
- Second: arbitrary effect `E ∈ A_Λ`

The joint probability of "outcome `P` then effect `E`" on input state
`σ` is, by standard instrument composition:

```text
p(P then E | σ)  =  Tr( K_P σ K_P† · E )
                =  Tr( σ · K_P† E K_P )
                =  Tr( σ · M_{P, E} )                                   (3)
```

where `M_{P, E} := K_P† E K_P` is the **joint effect operator** for
"P then E". This is the standard sequential composition for any Kraus
operator (not specific to Lüders). The framework-side content lives in
the LSP selection `K_P = P`, which then gives `M_{P, E} = P† E P =
P E P`.
