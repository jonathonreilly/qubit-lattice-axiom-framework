## 4. A constructive Weyl carrier, with the commutant included

multiplicity conclusion applies. Its statement and real-case proof were

following direct proof also specifies how the actual commuting V acts,
without importing an unchecked measurable-cocycle trivialization.

For a Schwartz kernel \(L(x,y)\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), define
\[
 \widetilde L(t,a)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-itx}L(x,x-a)\,dx,
 \qquad

              \widetilde L(t,a)Z(t)U(a)\,dt\,da.       \tag{M15}
\]
The linear coordinate change and one-variable Fourier transform preserve
Schwartz space, so the coefficient is integrable. The integral is defined
strongly on each vector, with
\(\|A(L)\|\le(2\pi)^{-1}\|\widetilde L\|_1\).
No operator-norm continuity of the unitary groups is needed.

Write
\((L_1L_2)(x,y)=\int L_1(x,z)L_2(z,y)\,dz\) and
\(L^*(x,y)=\overline{L(y,x)}\). Weyl multiplication in M14 gives
\[
 A(L_1)A(L_2)=A(L_1L_2),\qquad A(L)^*=A(L^*).          \tag{M16}
\]
For clarity, the coefficient of \(Z(q)U(c)\) in the product, after
factoring the outer \(1/(2\pi)\), is
\[
 \frac1{2\pi}\int\widetilde L_1(t,a)
       \widetilde L_2(q-t,c-a)e^{-ia(q-t)}\,dt\,da.     \tag{M17}
\]
It is also the Fourier transform of
\(\int L_1(x,x-a)L_2(x-a,x-c)\,da\).
This proves the product with the correct phase and normalization.
The adjoint follows from
\((Z(t)U(a))^*=e^{-iat}Z(-t)U(-a)\).
All uses of Fubini are dominated by products of the integrable
coefficients; kernel operations themselves remain Schwartz.

For Schwartz f,g, let \(L_{f,g}(x,y)=f(x)\overline{g(y)}\) and
\(E_{f,g}=A(L_{f,g})\). With inner products antilinear in the first
argument, M16 reads
\[
 E_{f,g}E_{u,v}=\langle g,u\rangle E_{f,v},\qquad
 E_{f,g}^*=E_{g,f}.                                   \tag{M18}
\]
We next check nondegeneracy; it cannot be assumed from these relations.

\varphi_\epsilon(a)\) gives
\[

                      Z(t)U(a)\,dt\,da\ \longrightarrow I
 \quad\hbox{strongly}.                               \tag{M19}
\]
To prove this, scale t,a by epsilon, use joint strong continuity at zero
on each fixed vector, and dominate by twice its norm times the fixed
integrable product \(\varphi(t)\varphi(a)\).
