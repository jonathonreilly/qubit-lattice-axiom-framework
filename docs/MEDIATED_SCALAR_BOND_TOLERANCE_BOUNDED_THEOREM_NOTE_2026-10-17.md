## Conditional O(m epsilon) scaling and its limits

Fix the nominal microscopic suppliers J_m,q,gamma_l, the calibrated processor and g,h. Along the discrete family let

    lambda_m=Lambda r^(2m-2), r=q/J_m<1,
    rho_m=sqrt(a lambda_m)

once this lies in the certified band. Suppose the actual error boxes satisfy m[-log(1-epsilon)]<=c for a fixed finite c, and all gaps and inverse margins in(2) have uniform positive slack. In particular H0,1/(1-theta_F),1/k_i,1/(1-eta_i) and the full internal recovery constants are uniformly bounded. These are substantive hypotheses, not conclusions from exact zero-energy products.

The checked numerator (`outputs/mediated_precision_2026_10_17/proofs/mediated_nonuniform_numerator_check__REVIEW.md`) separates two different sensitivities. The exact nonuniform hopping product has logarithmic error at most(2n_l+1)[-log(1-epsilon)], so its relative error is O(m epsilon) on this domain. In contrast, the unequal endpoint slope differences and their cubic remainders are O(epsilon) uniformly in length at fixed gap. Ordered selected port feedback retains both small cross factors when a closed perturbation returns to an open channel. Consequently the selected numerator has the form

    D(delta)<=C{lambda m epsilon+epsilon|delta|+epsilon|delta|³
         +lambda m epsilon delta²
         +lambda epsilon(a0+b0|delta|)²},                     (8)

where C depends on the fixed suppliers and uniform margins, not on m or epsilon. Here C is an analytical ceiling, unrelated to the actual scalar phase C(w). The finite exact D, not(8), is used in(2)--(3).

With R²=delta²+lambda², the constant hopping term uses integral lambda/R²<=pi. The slope term uses

    integral |delta|/R²=log(1+rho²/lambda²)=O(m).

Its coefficient is O(epsilon), so this logarithm produces O(m epsilon), not O(m² epsilon). The full raw L2 estimate carries sqrt(lambda); its exact rational moments likewise give sqrt(lambda)||D/R²||_2=O(m epsilon). Higher powers use the finite band where required. Neither multiple-pole stability nor a divergent full-line moment is assumed.

The source/complement cross blocks carry sqrt(lambda), but direct complementary terms remain bounded. Full reconstruction gives local integral O(m epsilon) and forcing L2 norm O(m epsilon). The changed mediator recovery is O(epsilon) multiplying a nominal source L1 norm O(m). Terms with an extra logarithm and sqrt(lambda) are bounded because sup_m m sqrt(lambda_m)<infinity. These are the checked mechanisms behind

    B_ret=O(m epsilon).

Using anchored Lipschitz witness bounds in(5), the preparation-term increment is O(epsilon); its nominal slope is bounded below by gamma1²/J_m²>0. The hopping residual increment is O(m epsilon)|t10|/rho=O(m epsilon sqrt(lambda)). Hence max(0,o_box-o_nominal)=O(m epsilon), and(6) increases the nominal COMPLETE output certificate by at most O(m epsilon), uniformly over its certified time domain.

This is not a bound tending to zero on the difference of the two full unitaries on all outside eigenvectors. Their possible phases and spectral types are paid by the actual witness allowance. Nor is it uniform for fixed nonzero epsilon and unbounded m, for closing margins, or for varying suppliers/g without further estimates. No coefficient multiplying O(m epsilon), admissible numerical epsilon, earlier onset, receiver size or practical precision advantage has been evaluated.
