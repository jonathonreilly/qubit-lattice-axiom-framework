There are two distinct conclusions that must not be conflated:

1. For parity-homogeneous observables, the **graded commutator** has the clean
   distance-tail bound. If either observable is even, this is also an ordinary
   commutator bound.
2. For arbitrary observables, the **ordinary commutator** obeys a Duhamel norm
   majorant with its initial norm added. For an odd-odd pair, the tail is an
   inhomogeneous majorant after unitary transport; it is not the Taylor series
   of the ordinary commutator with its constant term deleted.

## Supplied hypotheses and conventions

Let `Λ ⊂ Z³` be finite and possibly disconnected, with induced
nearest-neighbor bond set `E(Λ)`. The algebra is the finite CAR algebra
`CAR(Λ)`, generated by `c_x,c_x†` with

> `{c_x,c_y†}=δ_xy`, `{c_x,c_y}=0`, and `{c_x†,c_y†}=0`.

For `S ⊆ Λ`, `CAR(S)` is the subalgebra generated on `S`. The parity
automorphism gives the even and odd homogeneous subspaces. A general
observable may contain both parity components.

The Hamiltonian is supplied in the form

> `H = Σ_{b∈E(Λ)} h_b`,

where each `h_b ∈ CAR(b)` is even and Hermitian. If `E(Λ)` is nonempty, set
`J=max_b ||h_b||`; if it is empty, set `J=0` and `H=0`. Evenness and
Hermiticity are independent and load-bearing: evenness gives disjoint CAR
commutation and preserves parity, while Hermiticity gives unitary norm
transport.

Let `A∈CAR(X)` and `B∈CAR(Y)` for explicitly chosen, nonempty, disjoint
supports `X,Y`. A zero operator may be assigned any declared nonempty support.
Distance `d=d(X,Y)` is measured in the induced graph and is `∞` when the
supports lie in different connected components. Define

> `n_X = #{b∈E(Λ): b∩X≠∅} ≤ 6|X|`.

Time evolution is the supplied finite-matrix Heisenberg convention
`τ_t(O)=e^{itH}Oe^{-itH}`. Finite-dimensional exponential calculus,
time-ordered unitary propagators for continuous Hermitian generators,
operator-norm submultiplicativity, and Riemann integration are declared
analytic context. Negative time is reduced to positive time by `H↦-H`, which
preserves the supplied class and `J`.

The finite Jordan-Wigner representation is used only as a faithful
computational device for exact runner exhibits. It is not a framework
identification and does not supply the theorem's intrinsic locality proof.

## Graded locality and the fixed-order Jordan-Wigner exhibit

**Graded locality lemma.** If `S∩T=∅`, `P∈CAR(S)` is homogeneous of parity
`p`, and `Q∈CAR(T)` is homogeneous of parity `q`, then

> `PQ=(-1)^(pq)QP`, equivalently `[P,Q]_gr=0`.

For monomials, every generator on `S` anticommutes with every generator on
`T`. Moving a word of length `q` through a word of length `p` makes `pq`
cross-support swaps and gives the displayed sign. The monomials span the
local CAR algebra, so bilinearity proves the homogeneous statement. In
particular, every even element commutes with every disjoint element, while a
disjoint odd-odd pair anticommutes and can have a nonzero ordinary
commutator.

The runner corroborates the proof in a four-site faithful representation. It
first checks the CAR relations. It then verifies that the two generated
two-site PBW families are nonzero, pairwise distinct, and rank `16`; only
after those completeness checks does it test all `16×16=256` cross-family
pairs. Every pair obeys `(-1)^(pq)`, while exactly `192` reject the wrong
`(-1)^(p+q)` sign. A built-in duplicated-generator control drops the rank to
`8` even though a bare sign test remains green, so the exhaustiveness claim is
mutation-sensitive.

**Fixed-order Jordan-Wigner exhibit.** In the order `0<1<2<3`, the even hop
`c_1†c_3+c_3†c_1` contains the intermediate Jordan-Wigner string and fails to
commute with the bare qubit operator `X_2`. It is therefore not supported only
on endpoint qubit factors `{1,3}` in that fixed encoding. Intrinsically, the
same CAR element commutes with `c_2`, `c_2†`, and `n_2`. This proves only that
the fixed Jordan-Wigner representation does not uniformly map the allowed
CAR bond class into the preceding note's two-qubit-factor bond class. It does
not rule out all possible encodings or auxiliary constructions.

## Duhamel recursion and walk majorant

For `O∈CAR(Z)`, even disjoint locality gives the exact boundary reduction

> `[H,O]=Σ_{b:b∩Z≠∅}[h_b,O]`.

When `O=h_b`, the self term also vanishes and is removed before recursion.
Thus the first step has at most `n_X` choices, and every later bond has at most
ten adjacent nonself continuations on the cubic lattice.

Let `F_O(t)` be either the ordinary commutator `[τ_t(O),B]`, or, when `O` and
`B` are homogeneous, the graded commutator. Applying the ordinary or graded
Jacobi identity after the boundary reduction splits its derivative into

- a homogeneous term generated by the Hermitian sum of the transported
  touching bonds; and
- source terms containing `[τ_t(h_b),B]`.

The homogeneous time-dependent equation is unitary conjugation, so it
transports `||F_O(0)||` without growth. Variation of constants and
`||[P,Q]||≤2||P||||Q||` therefore give, for `t≥0`,

> `||F_O(t)|| ≤ ||F_O(0)|| + 2||O|| Σ_{b:b∩Z≠∅}`
> `∫_0^t ||[τ_s(h_b),B]|| ds`.

Every recursive source `h_b` is even. Its initial commutator with `B`
vanishes until its bond touches `Y`, irrespective of the parity of `B`.
Deleting the self bond makes the recursive sequences nonbacktracking only in
the immediate-self sense and gives at most
`n_X·10^(k-1)` length-`k` bond walks. A walk from `X` to `Y` requires
`k≥d`. Iterated integration contributes `|t|^k/k!`, while the norm factors
contribute `(2J)^k`. Hence the inhomogeneous contribution is bounded by

> `2||A||||B|| (n_X/10) Σ_{k≥d}(20J|t|)^k/k!`.

This argument is self-contained at the algebraic level. The preceding
all-time note supplies a parallel tensor-product specialization and useful
placement context, but is not treated here as retained authority.

## The bounded conclusions

**Graded-commutator theorem.** If `A` and `B` are parity-homogeneous, then
their disjoint initial graded commutator vanishes, and for all real `t`,

> `||[τ_t(A),B]_gr|| ≤ 2||A||||B|| (n_X/10)`
> `· Σ_{k≥d}(20J|t|)^k/k!`.

If either `A` or `B` is even, the graded commutator is the ordinary
commutator, so the same clean estimate holds ordinarily.

**Arbitrary-observable ordinary Duhamel theorem.** Without a homogeneity or
even-observable restriction, for all real `t`,

> `||[τ_t(A),B]|| ≤ ||[A,B]|| + 2||A||||B|| (n_X/10)`
> `· Σ_{k≥d}(20J|t|)^k/k!`.

For odd-odd observables the added term is indispensable. More strongly, the
actual ordinary Taylor coefficients below distance need not vanish: the
runner's four-site distance-three hopping chain gives squared norms
`(4,4,8,20)` for orders `0,1,2,3`. By contrast, the graded odd-odd
coefficients and the even-source ordinary coefficients vanish below distance.
The sum in the displayed ordinary theorem therefore bounds the transported
inhomogeneous source; it is **not** the Taylor series of
`[τ_t(A),B]-[A,B]`, and it does not make a distance-decaying ordinary
odd-odd commutator claim.

For finite `d` and `x=20J|t|`, the note-carried elementary tail arguments give

> `Σ_{k≥d}x^k/k! ≤ (x^d/d!)e^x`

and, for every `μ>0`,

> `Σ_{k≥d}x^k/k! ≤ exp(-μd+xe^μ)`.

These follow respectively from `d!/k!≤1/(k-d)!` and the termwise identity
`x^k/k!=e^{-μk}(xe^μ)^k/k!` with `e^{-μk}≤e^{-μd}`. The constants `20J`
and the `μ=1` readout `20eJ` are loose mathematical scales, not a sharp or
physical velocity.

If `d=∞`, the walk tail is zero by convention. The even Hamiltonian factors
between disconnected components, so the graded cross-component commutator is
zero and the ordinary commutator's initial statistical norm is transported
without a dynamical cross-component source. If `E(Λ)=∅`, `H=0` and the
ordinary inequality is equality at its initial term.

**Uniformity qualification.** For each finite `Λ`, the displayed constants do
not contain `|Λ|`. This is only per-volume independence for the supplied
instance. A uniform family theorem requires

> `J_* = sup_{Λ,b} ||h_b^(Λ)|| < ∞`

and uniform control of `||A_Λ||`, `||B_Λ||`, and `n_{X_Λ}` (or the support
sizes that bound it). Individual finiteness of every `J_Λ` does not imply this
family statement.
