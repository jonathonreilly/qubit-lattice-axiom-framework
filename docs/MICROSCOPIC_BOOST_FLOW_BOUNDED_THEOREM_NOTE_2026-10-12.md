## 2. Precise hypotheses and conclusion

Let \(\mathcal A\) be the quasilocal spin tensor algebra over
\(\mathbb Z^d\), \(d\ge1\), with finite-dimensional full matrix factors
(in particular qubits), the \(\ell^1\) metric, and local algebra
\(\mathcal A_{\rm loc}\). Set \(B_L=\{x:|x|_1\le L\}\).
Use the positive geometric convention
\(\tau_j\mathcal A(X)=\mathcal A(X+j)\). Supply
\[
h_x=\tau_xh_0=h_x^*,\qquad
\operatorname{supp}h_x\subset x+B_R,\qquad \|h_x\|\le h<\infty,
\quad R\in\mathbb N_0.                                      \tag{B1}
\]
The sums defining \(\delta_h=i\sum_x[h_x,\cdot]\) and
\(\delta_b=i\sum_xx_b[h_x,\cdot]\) are finite on each local observable,
and map local observables to local observables. The construction of an
individual boost actually needs only the stated support/norm bounds;
translation covariance is retained to match the frozen current setting.

Let \(E_L\) be the normalized-trace conditional expectation onto
\(\mathcal A(B_L)\). For real \(\nu\ge0\), define
\[
 p_\nu(A)=\|A\|+
 \sup_{r\ge0}(1+r)^\nu\|(1-E_r)A\|,
 \qquad
 \mathcal A_{\rm al}=\bigcap_{\nu\ge0}\{A:p_\nu(A)<\infty\}.
                                                               \tag{B2}
\]
Using only integer \(\nu\) gives the same rapid almost-local algebra and
Fréchet topology. This is localization about a fixed origin, not a
uniform seminorm over all translates. Any two fixed origins give
equivalent polynomial seminorms.

Supply a normalized positive state \(\omega\) on \(\mathcal A\). The
boost state identity, when used, is the explicit condition
\[
 \omega(\delta_b A)=0\qquad(A\in\mathcal A_{\rm loc}).             \tag{B3}
\]
For the joint energy/current construction additionally require
\[
 \omega(\delta_h A)=0\qquad(A\in\mathcal A_{\rm loc}).             \tag{B4}
\]
Identities are required for all complex local observables, or equivalently
for all self-adjoint local observables by complex linearity. The conditions
are on the same state. No initial self-adjoint H, B or P is a hypothesis.

**Conditional theorem.** Under B1:

1. The local weighted derivation generates a unique point-norm continuous
   all-time automorphism group \(\lambda_s\). Every \(p_\nu\) is preserved
   as a finite seminorm, with a uniform bound for \(s\) in each compact
   interval. This is the TWW literature conclusion with checked hypotheses.
2. For \(q>\nu+d+1\), the dyadic extension satisfies
   \(p_\nu(\delta_bA)\le C_{\nu,q}p_q(A)\); explicit constants and
   approximation rates are in B14–B17. In particular a loss of \(d+2\)
   suffices. Every rapid AL observable lies in the closed flow generator's
   domain, and \(\lambda_s\) is a \(C^1\) group on the rapid AL algebra
   with generator that extension.
3. Under B3 the supplied state itself is invariant. Its canonical GNS
   unitaries \(e^{isB_b}\) construct a self-adjoint \(B_b\), fix the cyclic
   vector, implement \(\lambda_s\) on the full represented algebra, and obey
   \(B_b\pi(A)\Omega=-i\pi(\delta_bA)\Omega\) for local and rapid AL A.
4. Any actual self-adjoint operator having this same action on all local
   cyclic vectors equals \(B_b\). The proof uses local graph approximations
   and a \(C^1\) Schrödinger curve, not an analytic-vector estimate.
5. Under B3/B4 the canonical H and the current P are also constructed.
   The same dense local cyclic domain \(\mathcal D\) is invariant under
   H, B and P, and \(i(HB_b-B_bH)=P_b\) holds on every vector in that domain.

These conclusions apply to each coordinate b for which B3 is supplied.
They do not assert commutation of distinct boost or momentum flows.

## 3. Checked finite-spin application of TWW

For a local C supported in \(B_L(x)\), contractivity gives the safe bound
\[
 \|C\|_{\nu,x}\le[1+2(1+L)^\nu]\|C\|
 \le3(1+L)^\nu\|C\|.                                         \tag{B5}
\]
Likewise with \(G(r)=e^{-4r}\),
\(\|C\|_{G,x}\le(1+2e^{4L})\|C\|\). Thus the zero-chain
\(\Phi_x=x_bh_x\) obeys
\[
 \|\Phi_x\|_{G,x}
 \le h(1+2e^{4R})|x_b|
 \le C_\Phi(1+|x|_1),\qquad C_\Phi=h(1+2e^{4R}).                \tag{B6}
\]
It is self-adjoint, autonomous, and its local commutator sums are finite.
The lattice volume bound \(|B_r(x)|\le3^d(1+r)^d\) holds. For h=0
the flow and generator are zero and no division by \(C_\Phi\) is needed.

Here is an explicit check of the LR input, including a normalization that
does not depend on the growing cutoff. Let
\[
 p=d+2,\quad f_0(r)=(1+r)^{-p},\quad S_p=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^d}f_0(|z|),
 \quad C=2^{p+1}S_p,\quad K(u,v)=C^{-1}e^{-|u-v|}f_0(|u-v|).
                                                               \tag{B7}
\]
The sum \(S_p\) is finite. Splitting the convolution sum according to
\(|u-z|\ge|u-v|/2\) or \(|z-v|\ge|u-v|/2\), and using the triangle
inequality for the exponential, shows
\(\sum_zK(u,z)K(z,v)\le K(u,v)\). Also K is symmetric and has a
uniformly finite row sum, at most \(S_p/C\). These are the exact kernel
hypotheses of NSY Theorem 3.1.

For completeness this also checks the uniform exponentially localized
zero-chain class in TWW's LR assumption, beyond the finite-range cutoffs
actually used here. If \(\sup_{t,x}\|\Psi_x(t)\|_{G,x}=M\), telescope
\(\Psi_x\) into \(E_{B_0(x)}\Psi_x\) and the differences
\(E_{B_n(x)}\Psi_x-E_{B_{n-1}(x)}\Psi_x\), \(n\ge1\).
Assign them to the balls \(B_n(x)\). Their norms are bounded by M at
n=0 and by \((1+e^4)Me^{-4n}\) for n≥1. If u,v lie in the assigned
ball, \(|u-v|\le2n\), and at most \(3^d(1+n)^d\) centers contribute.
Consequently the associated interaction \(\mathcal I\) satisfies
\[
 \sum_{X\ni u,v}\|\mathcal I(X,t)\|
 \le MD\,e^{-|u-v|}(1+|u-v|)^{-p},
 \quad
 D=1+(1+e^4)2^p3^d\sum_{n\ge1}(1+n)^{p+d}e^{-2n}<\infty.       \tag{B8}
\]
To check the estimate, multiply the contribution for radius n by
\(e^{|u-v|}(1+|u-v|)^p\), then use
\(e^{|u-v|}\le e^{2n}\) and
\((1+|u-v|)^p\le2^p(1+n)^p\). Absolute summability also justifies
the equality of the local derivation with the telescoped interaction's
derivation. Time continuity and the bounds are inherited term by term.

Its NSY interaction norm is therefore at most \(CDM\). The commutator
bound in that theorem and the trivial bound inside the cone give
\[
 \|[\alpha_{s,t}^{\mathcal I}(A),B]\|
 \le2|X|\|A\|\|B\|
 \exp\{-\bigl(\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)-2CDM|t-s|\bigr)_+\},
                                                               \tag{B9}
\]
for A in the X algebra and B in the disjoint Y algebra. Indeed the
kernel sum in NSY is at most \(|X|(S_p/C)e^{-\operatorname{dist}(X,Y)}\),
and \(2S_p/C\le2\). Intersecting supports are covered by the trivial
bound. The finite-volume estimates are uniform and pass to the bounded
interaction's norm dynamics; only strictly local finite-volume cutoffs
are needed in the TWW proof used here. Thus one can take TWW's decay
functions \(G(r)=e^{-4r}\), \(F(r)=e^{-r}\) and LR speed constant
\(v_{\rm LR}=2CD\). Both decay exponents \(\nu_F,\nu_G\) are infinite.

The spin adaptation is explicit: for a local tensor C, \(E_M C\) takes
normalized partial trace outside M and tensors back with the identity.
It extends contractively to the quasilocal algebra, is unital and positive,
has the bimodule property, and obeys \(E_ME_N=E_{M\cap N}\).
Disjoint local tensor algebras commute. These are precisely the expectation
and even-locality properties used in the TWW estimates. The NSY
commutator proof likewise consists of finite-volume unitary differential
equations, Jacobi and disjoint-support commutation; its even restriction
is unnecessary for spins. Neither proof needs fermionic anticommutation
or an additional spin representation hypothesis.

TWW uses the cutoff
\(\Phi_x^k=1_{\{|x|\le k/2\}}E_{B_{k/2}(x)}\Phi_x\), supported in
\(B_k\). Its weighted norm is bounded by \(C_\Phi(1+k/2)\), with
constants in B9 independent of k. If \(k/2\ge R\), the expectation
on every retained term is exactly the identity. If A is supported in
\(B_L\) and \(k/2\ge L+R\), its truncated local generator is exactly
\(\delta_bA\). This is the required local generator convergence.

TWW Theorems 7–8 now give the point-norm limit of these finite-volume
flows and, for every \(\nu>0\), constants \(a_\nu,c_\nu<\infty\)
with
\[
 p_\nu(\lambda_s A)
 \le a_\nu e^{c_\nu C_\Phi|s|}p_\nu(A).                       \tag{B10}
\]
For \(\nu=0\), use \(p_0(A)\le3\|A\|\) and isometry. In the
autonomous case uniqueness (or the autonomous finite-volume group laws)
gives an actual one-parameter group. Its closed C*-algebra generator L
agrees with \(\delta_b\) on local observables. The paper's convention
\(\partial_t\alpha_{s,t}A=\alpha_{s,t}i\mathcal L_\Phi A\)
matches the positive clock \(\lambda_s\) here.

Two literal source issues are kept distinct from the valid application.
After Theorem 8, the displayed local-observable corollary uses
\(\|A\|_\nu\le(1+k)^\nu\|A\|\); with the defined norm-plus-tail
norm this requires an enlarged constant such as B5. We use B5 throughout.
In the appendix generator-convergence lemma the first term writes
\(E_{B_k(x)}\), \(G(k)\), where the definition requires
\(E_{B_{k/2}(x)}\), \(G(k/2)\). Keeping the correct radius fixes that
estimate; for B1 the eventual exact stabilization above bypasses the
tail estimate altogether. TWW's definition of a general decay function
does not require monotonicity, so the present argument selects the
monotone exponentials explicitly. These observations are author source
checks, not an independent review of every possible TWW generalization.

## 4. Rapid AL approximation and explicit derivation loss

The spaces with finite \(p_\nu\) are complete: a Cauchy sequence is
C*-norm Cauchy, each tail map is norm continuous, and taking the
supremum preserves its Cauchy bound. Their intersection is therefore
Fréchet complete. It is a *-algebra. For example, compare AB with
\((E_rA)(E_rB)\), then apply the contraction E_r to the difference;
this bounds its tail by
\(2(\|A\|\|(1-E_r)B\|+\|B\|\|(1-E_r)A\|)\).

For \(q>\nu\) the commuting nested expectations give the useful exact
approximation estimate
\[
 p_\nu(A-E_LA)\le2(1+L)^{\nu-q}p_q(A),\qquad
 p_q(E_LA)\le p_q(A).                                        \tag{B11}
\]
For r≤L, \((1-E_r)(1-E_L)A=(1-E_L)A\); for r≥L it equals
\((1-E_r)A\). Bounding these two regions proves B11, including the
norm term. Thus locals are dense in the rapid AL topology.

More generally let \(\delta_w C=i\sum_xw(x)[g_x,C]\), where
\(\operatorname{supp}g_x\subset x+B_\rho\), \(\|g_x\|\le g\),
and \(|w(x)|\le(1+|x|)^\kappa\), with \(\kappa=0\) or 1.
For local C supported in \(B_L\),
\[
 \operatorname{supp}\delta_wC\subset B_{L+2\rho},\qquad
 \|\delta_wC\|\le K(1+L)^{d+\kappa}\|C\|,
 \quad K=2g3^d(1+\rho)^{d+\kappa}.                            \tag{B12}
\]
Only centers in \(B_{L+\rho}\) contribute. Count them by the volume
bound and use \(1+L+\rho\le(1+\rho)(1+L)\). Combining with B5,
\[
 p_\nu(\delta_wC)
 \le3K(1+2\rho)^\nu(1+L)^{\nu+d+\kappa}\|C\|.                \tag{B13}
\]
For the boost take \(g_x=h_x,\rho=R,\kappa=1,w(x)=x_b\).
For energy and the uniform current take \(\kappa=0\).

Define \(A_0=E_1A\), and, for m≥1,
\(A_m=E_{2^m}A-E_{2^{m-1}}A\). Then
\(A=\sum_{m\ge0}A_m\) in every rapid AL seminorm, and
\[
 \operatorname{supp}A_m\subset B_{2^m},\qquad
 \|A_m\|\le2^{q+1}(1+2^m)^{-q}p_q(A)\quad(m\ge1).
                                                               \tag{B14}
\]
Write \(u=\nu+d+\kappa\). B13–B14 give, for every q>u,
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 p_\nu(\delta_wA)\le C_{\nu,q}^{(\rho,g,\kappa)}p_q(A),\quad
 C_{\nu,q}^{(\rho,g,\kappa)}
 =3K(1+2\rho)^\nu
   \left[2^u+\frac{2^{q+1}}{2^{q-u}-1}\right].\quad}            \tag{B15}
\]
Here the extension is the absolutely convergent sum
\(\delta_wA:=\sum_{m\ge0}\delta_wA_m\) in every \(p_\nu\).
The first term is bounded by \(2^u\|A\|\), and the other terms
by the geometric series \(\sum_{m\ge1}2^{-m(q-u)}\), which proves
the displayed constant. It is not an optimized constant or endpoint claim.
An integer seminorm loss d+2 works for the boost; d+1 works for either
uniform derivation.

The tail of the same sum gives, for N≥0,
\[
 p_\nu\bigl(\delta_wA-\delta_w(E_{2^N}A)\bigr)
 \le
 \frac{3K(1+2\rho)^\nu2^{q+1}}{2^{q-u}-1}
 2^{-N(q-u)}p_q(A).                                          \tag{B16}
\]
Together with B11, this is local graph approximation in norm and in
every rapid AL seminorm. The extension agrees with the local formula:
for local A the dyadic decomposition eventually stabilizes and its finite
derivative sum telescopes. Uniqueness follows from B11 and the continuity
estimate B15. More precisely, any local sequence converging to A in a
seminorm p_q with q>ν+d+κ has derivatives converging in p_ν to this
extension. Arbitrary norm approximation alone is not asserted to suffice.

There is also a direct check on the infinite weighted sum. Split distant
centers into dyadic annuli. When \(2^{m-2}>\rho\), every term centered
at \(2^{m-1}<|x|\le2^m\) commutes with \(E_{2^{m-2}}A\).
The sum of commutator norms on that annulus is bounded by a constant
times \(2^{m(d+\kappa-q)}p_q(A)\). It is summable for q>d+κ.
The remaining centers are finite. Hence
\[
 \delta_wA=i\sum_xw(x)[g_x,A]                                 \tag{B17}
\]
is also an absolutely norm-convergent series on the rapid AL algebra.
To identify it with B15, approximate by E_LA; the uniform bound
\(p_q(E_LA)\le p_q(A)\) permits dominated convergence of the
commutator series. This provides a second justification of its meaning,
not an independent review of the proof.

## 5. Closed-generator identification and differentiability

The C*-generator L of \(\lambda_s\) is closed. Its local values are
\(LA=\delta_bA\). B11 and B16 therefore imply
\[
 \mathcal A_{\rm al}\subset\operatorname{Dom}L,
 \qquad LA=\delta_bA\quad(A\in\mathcal A_{\rm al}).             \tag{B18}
\]
No essential-self-adjointness, analytic-vector bound or generator core
assertion is needed for this step.

For a strongly continuous group of bounded operators, its generator
commutes with the group on the generator domain. Directly, apply
\(\lambda_s\) to the defining difference quotient. Since B10 preserves
the rapid AL algebra, B18 gives
\[
 \delta_b\lambda_sA=\lambda_s\delta_bA,
 \qquad \frac{d}{ds}\lambda_sA=\lambda_s\delta_bA
       =\delta_b\lambda_sA                                  \tag{B19}
\]
initially in C*-norm.

These orbits are continuous in every p_ν. One way to see the necessary
upgrade, without assuming it from a boundedness estimate, is the
interpolation inequality, for q>ν>0,
\[
 p_\nu(C)\le\|C\|+
 (2\|C\|)^{1-\nu/q}p_q(C)^{\nu/q}.                           \tag{B20}
\]
For each r the tail has both bounds \(2\|C\|\) and
\(p_q(C)(1+r)^{-q}\); take the geometric interpolation of the two
weighted bounds and then the supremum. B10 uniformly bounds p_q of
\(\lambda_sA-\lambda_{s_0}A\) for s in a compact interval, and
point-norm continuity makes B20 tend to zero. For ν=0 use p_0≤3 norm.

Apply this to \(\delta_bA\in\mathcal A_{\rm al}\). The norm identity
\(\lambda_tA-A=\int_0^t\lambda_s\delta_bA\,ds\) now holds as a
Fréchet integral too: continuous Riemann sums converge in every seminorm,
and their C*-norm limit is the displayed element. It follows that
\[
 p_\nu\left(\frac{\lambda_tA-A}{t}-\delta_bA\right)
 \le\sup_{|s|\le|t|}p_\nu(\lambda_s\delta_bA-\delta_bA)
 \longrightarrow0.                                         \tag{B21}
\]
Translation in group time gives C1 differentiability everywhere.
Iteration also yields smooth rapid AL orbits, but no quantitative
analytic-vector assertion follows from that observation.

## 6. From the local state identity to an actual self-adjoint boost

Assume B3. By B11/B16 and boundedness of \(\omega\),
\(\omega(\delta_bA)=0\) extends from locals to all rapid AL A.
For such A the actual differentiable orbit gives
\[
 \frac{d}{ds}\omega(\lambda_sA)
 =\omega(\delta_b\lambda_sA)=0.                               \tag{B22}
\]
Thus the supplied state is invariant on the rapid AL algebra, hence on
the local algebra, and by norm density on all \(\mathcal A\).
This proves invariance of the specified state; it is not a rule selecting
a physical state from the framework.

In its GNS representation \((\pi,\mathcal H,\Omega)\), define
\[
 W_s\pi(A)\Omega=\pi(\lambda_sA)\Omega.                        \tag{B23}
\]
Invariance applied to A* A proves that this preserves Hilbert norm,
including null vectors, so it is well-defined on the cyclic quotient.
The inverse time and density give a unitary group. Point-norm continuity
of \(\lambda_s\) gives strong continuity, and W_sΩ=Ω. Stone's theorem
then supplies a self-adjoint \(B_b\) with W_s=e^{isB_b}.
Differentiating B23 for rapid AL A proves
\[
 \pi(A)\Omega\in\operatorname{Dom}B_b,\qquad
 iB_b\pi(A)\Omega=\pi(\delta_bA)\Omega.                        \tag{B24}
\]
In particular \(B_b\Omega=0\). Since the local derivation is local,
\[
 \mathcal D:=\pi(\mathcal A_{\rm loc})\Omega\text{ is dense},
 \qquad B_b\mathcal D\subset\mathcal D.                        \tag{B25}
\]
Every finite power of B is therefore defined on D; it is not claimed
that these vectors have a positive analytic radius.

To pass from vacuum columns to full implementation, for quasilocal A,C,
\[
 W_s\pi(A)W_s^*\pi(C)\Omega
 =W_s\pi(A\lambda_{-s}C)\Omega
 =\pi(\lambda_sA)\pi(C)\Omega.
\]
Density gives
\[
 \boxed{e^{isB_b}\pi(A)e^{-isB_b}=\pi(\lambda_sA)
 \quad(A\in\mathcal A,\ s\in\mathbb R).}                     \tag{B26}
\]
Vacuum separation, purity and faithfulness are unnecessary for this
represented equality. No inference from XΩ=0 to X=0 is used.

## 7. Identification of a previously supplied operator
