## 2. The envelope preserves the causal-square gain

The exact corrected covariance and energy identities are

    Y*gamma(k)Y=gamma(Z*k),
    N(T)=PZ(T)P_- Z(T)*P,
    <W Omega,H0 W Omega>=||E^(1/2)PZ(T)P_-||_HS^2.       (M5)

The CAR covariance follows from the finite-time differential identity for
bounded real c_A and continuation through its jumps; it does not require a
constant amplitude. The weighted identity follows from finite-rank energy
cutoffs and monotone convergence as in corrected859. We will supply the
needed finite weighted norm rather than infer an energy trace from norm
convergence.

On L2([0,T];C^2) and its Hilbert--Schmidt amplification define

    G(t)=F*exp(-itD)F,
    (L_A x)(t)=-i integral_0^t G(t-s)c_A(s)M x(s)ds,
    X(t)=F_t*Z(t), X0(t)=F_t*, X=X0+lambda L_A X.        (M6)

The rows have finite HS norm ||X(t)||_HS=||F||_HS, by unitarity; hence
all finite-time Fubini and L2 amplification operations are legitimate.
The identity on K is never assigned an HS norm.

The checked threshold expansion in general470 gives, for q0(t)=(1+t)^-1/2,

    G(t)=q0(t)sum_(alpha=+-m) exp(i alpha t)C_alpha+R(t),
    ||R(t)||<=C(1+t)^-3/2.                              (M7)

It holds also with any fixed energy power and band in the Gram kernel.
Its proof is the smooth odd momentum substitution E=m+y^2 followed by the
damped Gaussian Fourier formula with a Schwartz amplitude and controlled
second-Fourier-moment remainder. No envelope enters this input.

Expanding the causal operator twice, keeping every matrix order, yields

    (L_A^2 x)(t)=-(1/2)sum_(sigma=+-1) integral_0^t
       H_sigma,A(t,s) exp(i sigma omega s)c_A(s)M x(s)ds,
    H_sigma,A(t,s)=integral_0^r G(r-z)A(s+z)
                              exp(i sigma omega z)M G(z)dz,
    r=t-s.                                              (M8)

For a leading-leading term the scalar integrand is, up to a phase in r,

    b_r(z)A(s+z)exp(i delta z),
    b_r(z)=q0(r-z)q0(z), delta=beta+sigma omega-alpha.

All such delta satisfy |delta|>=omega-2m. Crucially,

    sup_[0,r] b_r=q0(r), Var(b_r)<=2q0(r).

The BV product bound therefore gives

    Var(b_r A(s+.))<=(2A_star+V_star)q0(r).

Together with the two endpoint contributions, Stieltjes integration by
parts against exp(i delta z) proves

    |integral_0^r b_r(z)A(s+z)exp(i delta z)dz|
       <=(4A_star+V_star)q0(r)/|delta|.                 (M9)

This includes jumps of A as derivative-measure atoms. No pointwise derivative,
slow-variation approximation or positivity of A is required. Terms involving
R in (M7) are bounded absolutely by A_star times the convolution of q0 and
(1+t)^-3/2; splitting at r/2 bounds that convolution by C q0(r). Thus
||H_sigma,A(t,s)||<=C(A_star+V_star)q0(t-s).
Young's inequality applied to this nonconvolution kernel majorant gives

    ||L_A||<=C A_star sqrt(1+T),
    ||L_A^2||<=C A_star(A_star+V_star)sqrt(1+T).         (M10)

The second bound concerns the operator square, not the square of the first
norm. With the constants absorbed as C0,C2, the exact polynomial inverse is

    (I-lambda L_A)^-1=(I+lambda L_A)(I-lambda^2 L_A^2)^-1,
    ||(I-lambda L_A)^-1||
       <=[1+h C0 sqrt(1+T)]/[1-h^2 C2 sqrt(1+T)].       (M11)

All factors are polynomials or norm-convergent series in the same L_A, so

exterior pair vector has wavefunction Delta/sqrt(2). As checked in470,

For b(e)=r(e), e r(e), or r(e)(exp(is e)-1) for fixed real s, let

    C_b(t)=integral b(e)exp(i e t)de.

    integral b(e)|I_A,T(e)|^2 de
       =(pi/2)[b(omega)+b(-omega)] integral_0^T A(t)^2dt
          +O_b,A_star,V_star(log(2+T)).                 (M18)

Here b can be complex; no positivity or realness of C_b is used. Fubini
is valid at finite T because b is integrable and A bounded. Write the
cosine product as [cos(omega(t-s))+cos(omega(t+s))]/2.

For the difference-time term, extend A by zero off [0,T] and set

    J_A(delta)=integral_R A(s+delta)A(s)ds,
    J_A(0)=integral_0^T A(s)^2ds.

    integral_R |f(s+delta)-f(s)|ds<=|delta| |mu|(R).

Also J_A vanishes when |delta|>=T and |J_A(delta)-J_A(0)|<=2A_star^2 T.

    integral_R cos(omega delta)C_b(delta)d delta
                                    =pi[b(omega)+b(-omega)].

## 7. Limits, correlation consequences and calibrated cells

For J_lambda,A(T)->J the limiting characteristic function is continuous
at zero and equals that of omega times a Poisson variable of parameter
nu_omega J. Thus the energy laws converge weakly. Independently, (M3)
proves convergence of their nonnegative means. The identity
E[(X-R)_+]=E[X]-E[min(X,R)] supplies uniform integrability; truncating
Lipschitz test functions then proves W1 convergence.

The uniform W1 assertion over the permitted A,T follows by contradiction.
J_lambda,A(T) lies in [0,A_star^2 tau_star]. From any putative bad sequence
choose a subsequence along which J converges. The uniform characteristic
and mean estimates just proved imply W1 convergence along it to the Poisson
law at J. The comparison Poisson laws at the original J_lambda also
converge in W1: coupling their counts by an independent Poisson increment
