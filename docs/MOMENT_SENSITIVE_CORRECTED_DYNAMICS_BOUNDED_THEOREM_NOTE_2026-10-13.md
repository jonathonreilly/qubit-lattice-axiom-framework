## 2. Shifted moments and a global integer graph estimate

Fix an energy reference mu>0, independent of c, and define

\[
 S_c=1+\frac{G_c+K}{\mu}\ge1,
 \qquad \operatorname{Tr}\rho_c S_c^s\le M_s,
 \qquad s>0.                                               \tag{M7}
\]

The moment is the positive spectral moment, obtained by bounded spectral
cutoffs. Equivalently S_c^(s/2) rho_c^(1/2) is a Hilbert--Schmidt column,
with squared norm equal to that moment. Finite moment is not a claim that
rho_c's range lies in D(G_c), D(G_c^s), or D(H_c^s). In particular first
mean control does not put arbitrary ensemble vectors in D(G_c).

For clarity, the Hilbert--Schmidt statement follows directly from the
spectral convention. With a complete orthonormal basis (e_j), monotone
convergence gives sum_j ||S_c^(s/2) rho_c^(1/2)e_j||^2=M_s(actual), with
each column in the spectral domain. These square-summable columns define
a bounded Hilbert--Schmidt extension. Approximate an arbitrary vector by
finite basis sums and use closedness of S_c^(s/2) to identify this extension
with S_c^(s/2)rho_c^(1/2) on the whole Hilbert space. No cyclic trace of
an unbounded product is used.

Stationarity is essential: U_c(t) commutes with every bounded function of
S_c. Apply this first to bounded moment cutoffs and then use monotone
convergence. Thus the same actual moment bound holds for rho_c(t) for
all positive or negative times.

The next graph estimate strengthens the window bound in I §4. Suppress c.
If bounded commutators through order n-1 exist, I's domain induction gives

\[
 D(H^n)=D(G^n)=D(S^n).                                    \tag{M8}
\]

The last equality is ordinary scalar spectral calculus for a fixed affine
shift of G. Set a_0=max(K,mu), C_0=1, and recursively

\[
 C_n=a_0 C_{n-1}+
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}\kappa_j C_{n-1-j}.
                                                               \tag{M9}
\]

Then the actual uniform global estimate is

\[
 \boxed{\ \|H^nS^{-n}\|\le C_n.\ }                       \tag{M10}
\]

Here is the operator-domain proof. S^-n maps into D(S^n)=D(H^n).
On this range the already typed commutator identity in I (12) gives

\[
 H^n\psi=H^{n-1}G\psi-
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{j}
       \operatorname{ad}_H^j(V)H^{n-1-j}\psi.
\]

Multiplying on the right by S^-n, its first term is
(H^(n-1)S^(-(n-1))) G S^-1, because G and S strongly commute.
Its j-th remaining term factors as the bounded commutator times
(H^(n-1-j)S^(-(n-1-j))) S^(-(j+1)). All factors have the stated domains;
the latter inverse power has norm at most one. Finally
G S^-1=mu-(K+mu)S^-1 has norm at most max(K,mu), since the spectrum of
S lies in [1,infinity). Induction proves (M9)--(M10) as bounded operator
identities/extensions on the whole actual Hilbert space.

This is not an inference from the growth of ||H^n 1_(S<=R)|| alone.
That window norm does not by itself justify a global weighted operator
bound when H and S do not commute. The commutator identity proves the
needed bound directly. Nor will a fractional H/S domain equivalence be
assumed below.

From the free joint multiplication calculus and (M1), for every integer n

\[

 \|H_0^r P S^{-r}\|\le C_r.                              \tag{M11}

Indeed ||Q psi||<=Lambda_c^-n ||H_c^n psi|| on D(H_c^n).

## 3. Bounded right-weight interpolation, proved on its actual spaces

Let S>=1 be positive selfadjoint on a Hilbert space, and T a bounded
operator into any other Hilbert space. Suppose ||T||<=A and
||T S^-n||<=B for an integer n>=1. For 0<=theta<=1,

\[
 \boxed{\ \|T S^{-n\theta}\|\le A^{1-\theta}B^\theta.\ }
                                                               \tag{M12}
\]

A proof is included so that no fractional-domain assertion is imported.
Assume A,B>0, and first restrict the right vector by the spectral cutoff
E_L=1_[1,L](S). For unit vectors u,v in the output/input spaces, respectively,
f(z)=<u,T S^(-nz) E_L v> is analytic in the strip 0<Re z<1 and continuous
on its closure. The cutoff makes its logarithmic generator bounded.
On Re z=0 its modulus is at most A, because S^(-iny) is unitary; on
Re z=1 it is at most B. These bounds do not depend on L.

Divide by A^(1-z)B^z and multiply by exp(epsilon z^2), epsilon>0.
On the vertical boundaries the modulus is at most exp(epsilon). The
undamped normalized function is bounded throughout the strip, so the
horizontal boundaries of a rectangle at imaginary heights +/-Y vanish
as Y tends to infinity. The ordinary maximum principle on these rectangles
therefore bounds the interior by exp(epsilon). Let epsilon decrease to
zero, evaluate at theta, and then let E_L tend strongly to the identity.
Take the supremum over u,v. This proves (M12). If B=0, T vanishes on the
dense range of S^-n, hence is zero; A=0 is immediate. The endpoints are
the assumptions. Thus no limiting zero denominator is used.

Only bounded T and inverse powers of the positive interacting S occur.
The argument does not claim that fractional powers of noncommuting H and
G can be ordered, or that a fractional free domain is automatic.

u(p_c x)->1. The main term of T_c (M27) therefore tends to

\[

uniformly on both supports. The two-particle phase approaches pi at

### 7.2 Linear saturation for s>=2r

 \operatorname{Im}\langle\chi,e^{-iT_c\mathcal R_c}\chi\rangle
