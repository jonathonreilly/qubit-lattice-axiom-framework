## 2. Finite-product overlap lemma

Here is a separate general lemma about even implementers on fermionic Fock
space. Let V_1,...,V_q implement real one-particle unitaries Z_1,...,Z_q
in the convention V_j*gamma(k)V_j=gamma(Z_j*k). Assume, along a parameter,

 max_j ||Z_j-I||<=delta->0,
 max_j ||PZ_jP_-||_HS<=K<infinity, q fixed.             (T3)

Let L_j be the skew graph of V_j|0> in the fixed scalar positive-band frame,
and g_j=<0|V_j|0>. Thus, with the conjugations of corrected859,

 L_j=beta_j Gamma alpha_j^-1 C,
 V_j|0>=g_j F_(L_j),  F_L=exp(pair_creation(L))|0>,
 <0|F_L>=1, ||F_L||^2=det(I+L*L)^(1/2).               (T4)

Here alpha_j=PZ_jP and beta_j=PZ_jP_-. The exact normalized overlap and
skew graph construction are the previously proved Gaussian identities;
their small-operator and HS hypotheses follow from T3. In particular
||L_j||=O(delta), ||L_j||_HS<=C K, and g_j is nonzero for small delta.

For a fixed number of products, one-particle multiplication keeps the full
norm O(delta) and the off-diagonal HS norm bounded. The positive-to-negative
block of Z_U Z_V is alpha_U beta_V+beta_U d_V, where d_V=P_-Z_VP_-.
Consequently it differs from beta_U+beta_V in HS norm by O(delta).
Also L_U-beta_U J=O_HS(delta), J=Gamma C. Unitarity gives

 beta_(U^-1)=-alpha_U^-1 beta_U d_U*,
 L_(U^-1)=-L_U+O_HS(delta).                            (T5)

For the first identity, use the upper-right block of Z_U Z_U*=I; it is
alpha_U beta_(U^-1)+beta_U d_U*=0. The HS error follows with bounded
HS(beta_U), not with an HS norm assigned to the identity. Therefore

 L_(V_1...V_j)=sum_(i<=j)L_i+O_HS(delta),
 L_((V_1...V_j)^-1)=-sum_(i<=j)L_i+O_HS(delta).         (T6)

The convention indeed gives Z_(UV)=Z_U Z_V: transform a field first by U
and then by V in (UV)*gamma(k)(UV). No reversed product is substituted.

For Q=V_1...V_(j-1), the exact vacuum recursion is

 <0|Q V_j|0>=g_Q g_j
  exp[(1/2)Tr log(I+L_(Q^-1)* L_j)].                  (T7)

To see the scalar phase, Q*|0>=conj(g_Q)F_(L_(Q^-1)); taking its bra
gives g_Q, not conj(g_Q). The mixed Gaussian overlap proves T7 with the
trace-log branch continuous from zero. Its series converges in trace norm
because the product's operator norm is O(delta^2) and trace norm is bounded.
Terms beyond the first trace are O(delta^2). Replacing the inverse graph
using T6 costs O(delta) by the HS pairing inequality. Finally

 |g_j|=exp[-(1/4)||L_j||_HS^2+O(delta^2)].

Writing zeta_j=g_j/|g_j|, induction in T7 proves the finite-product lemma

 <0|V_1...V_q|0>=(product_j zeta_j)
  exp[-(1/4)sum_j||L_j||_HS^2
             -(1/2)sum_(i<j)Tr(L_i* L_j)+O(delta)].   (T8)

The complex error is bounded in modulus by C_(q,K)delta. There is no choice
of an uncontrolled scalar logarithm or supposition that the Fock unitaries

 <0|product_(j=1..N) U_h(t_j,s_j)|0>

move all R's to the right. The remaining2N implementers are, in order,

 R(S_(j-1))Y_j*R(-S_(j-1)),

The final R(S_N) fixes the vacuum. Each remaining one-particle transformation

## 5. Auxiliary bosonic representation and chronological specialization

On symmetric Fock space over H_b let D(v)=exp(a*(v)-a(v)) be the Weyl
displacement, with vacuum expectation exp(-||v||^2/2). The sign convention
gives

 <Omega_b|D(v_1)...D(v_q)|Omega_b>
    =exp[-(1/2)sum||v_a||^2-sum_(a<b)<v_a,v_b>].       (T16)

For unnormalized exponential vectors e(w), the identities
<e(w),e(z)>=exp(<w,z>) and
D(v)e(w)=exp(-||v||^2/2-<v,w>)e(w+v)
give T16 by iteration. The second identity preserves inner products on the
dense exponential-vector span and has inverse D(-v), so it defines the
unitary used here without an unbounded BCH interchange. Orthogonal direct
sums factor these exponential vectors and yield the tensor factorization
used below. It is not an additional physics premise.
Let K=dGamma(e) for the bounded positive one-particle multiplier e,
and b_tau(u,e)=1_(u<=tau). Define

 A_b(tau)=D(-b_tau)K D(b_tau),
 U_b(tau,s)=D(-b_tau) exp(isK)D(b_tau).                (T17)

The product rule exp(isK)D(v)exp(-isK)=D(exp(ise)v)
and its vacuum invariance show that every vacuum word in T17 has exactly
the right side of T15. Thus T15 identifies the full vacuum distribution
of the bounded energy unitaries with an explicitly constructed quantum
limit. It does not identify fermionic Fock states in trace norm with bosonic
Fock states. There is no assumed change in particle statistics of the model.

For times ordered increasingly t_1<=...<=t_N, fix an interval of u values
entering first at t_k. The nonzero displacement list in T14 is a suffix.
Adjacent +exp(iS_j e) and -exp(iS_j e) terms cancel as inverse Weyl factors.
Its contribution to the exponent is

 integral_(t_(k-1),t_k] [exp(ie sum_(j>=k)s_j)-1]dM.

Consequently the chronological characteristic product equals in the limit

 exp{sum_k integral_(t_(k-1),t_k]
                     [exp(ie sum_(j>=k)s_j)-1]dM}.    (T18)

This is the joint characteristic function of a positive compound-Poisson
process X_tau with independent increments and jump intensity M. A concrete
construction is N~Poisson(M(total)) followed, when that total is positive,

## 6. Fixed finite-bin sequential measurements

For each increasing observation time choose a fixed finite partition of the
energy line into intervals, including the two unbounded end intervals if
needed. Assume every finite partition boundary is not an eigenvalue of K in
T17. This is a sufficient regularity condition; it is not claimed necessary.
K is selfadjoint on a separable Fock space, so it has at most countably many
eigenvalues. There are therefore ample allowed boundaries. For a constant
carrier omega, avoiding the numbers n omega, n=0,1,..., suffices.

At finite h the protocol is explicitly the finite-outcome projective energy
measurement for that partition, repeated with intervening exact evolution.
For selected bins B_1,...,B_n its probability is

 p_h(B_1,...,B_n)
   =||1_(B_n)(A_h(tau_n)) ... 1_(B_1)(A_h(tau_1))|0>||^2. (T19)

Multiplication on the left by the final full propagator turns T19 into the
usual chain of Schrödinger-picture bin projections and propagators. Thus it
describes a specified finite-bin protocol. It is not an unqualified sharp
continuous-spectrum dephasing channel. Different finite partitions define
different finite-h instruments; the theorem is for each fixed such protocol.

Here is the required spectral-projection passage. From T15, every fixed
finite linear combination of energy-unitary words applied to |0> has
convergent Gram entries, equal to those in the representation T17. Its
spectral characteristic function for any one A_h(tau) is another such word.
It converges to the characteristic function for A_b(tau), continuous at zero.
The positive finite spectral measures therefore converge weakly (normalize
their masses when nonzero; a vanishing limiting mass is immediate).

For a regular bin B and such a vector, approximate1_B in the limiting
spectral L2 norm by a smooth compactly supported f. Boundary mass is zero
in every auxiliary vector, because A_b(tau) is unitarily equivalent to K
and the chosen boundaries are not eigenvalues. Tightness handles the
unbounded bins. The bounded function |1_B-f|^2 is continuous except at those
boundaries, so its integrals also converge under the exact spectral measures.
It follows that (1_B(A_h)-f(A_h)) on this word vector has uniformly small
limsup norm after choosing f. Smooth f has an integrable Fourier transform;
functional calculus expresses f(A_h) as its integral of energy unitaries.
Dominated convergence gives the corresponding mixed inner-product limits.
Finite Fourier sums approximate the limiting vector in norm; their exact-h
limsup errors agree by these same inner-product limits. No uniform-in-h
operator-norm continuity of exp(isA_h) is required.

Inductively apply this approximation after each projection in T19. Errors
already made do not grow under the next projection, whose norm is at most1.
At each step use a fixed finite word combination approximating the previous
vector. After finitely many steps the norm in T19 converges to the analogous
auxiliary norm. This proves the projection passage; it is not inferred just
from marginal energy laws or from an unweighted trace comparison.

To compute that auxiliary norm, split H_b into the mutually orthogonal slow
time intervals between observations, with any later interval left in vacuum.
Bosonic Fock space is their tensor product, K=sum_k K_k, and b_tau is the
sum of the corresponding interval vectors b_k. On the kth tensor factor put

 Q_k=D_k(-b_k)K_kD_k(b_k).

The vacuum spectral law of Q_k is compound Poisson with jump measure
M restricted to its interval. The Q_k on distinct factors commute and
their vacuum laws are independent. At observation j,

 A_b(tau_j)=sum_(k<=j)Q_k+sum_(k>j)K_k.               (T20)

Every future factor is still vacuum before its first observation interval.
The future K_k terms therefore act as zero on the vector then being
projected. By spectral calculus, the jth bin projection is exactly the
projection of sum_(k<=j)Q_k on the past tensor factors, leaving future
vacua untouched. Induction shows that the entire projected vector in T19
equals the product of the commuting bin projections of these partial sums.
Therefore

 p_h(B_1,...,B_n)->Prob(X_(tau_j) in B_j for all j).    (T21)

This is a finite-dimensional measurement-statistics theorem for every fixed
regular finite-bin protocol. It establishes the classical comparison law
for that protocol while retaining the quantum ordered-word limit T15.
No path-space convergence, dense observation limit, continuous monitoring,
universal instrument independence or finite-h joint classical PVM is asserted.

## 7. A control that detects operator ordering

Take a constant carrier omega, positive production mu=M(total)>0, and one

 U_h(tau,s) U_h(0,t) U_h(tau,-s) U_h(0,-t).            (T22)

The four nonzero displacement vectors from T14 are

 -b, exp(is omega)b, -exp(i(s+t)omega)b, exp(it omega)b.

Their sum is (exp(is omega)-1)(1-exp(it omega))b. Hence the modulus of
the limiting vacuum expectation is

 exp[-mu |exp(is omega)-1|^2 |exp(it omega)-1|^2/2].    (T23)

For s=t=pi/omega all four vectors are -b, so the expectation itself is
exp(-8mu), strictly below1. Commuting classical energy variables would give

classical process. It does not contradict the sequential-bin theorem: its
unitary ordering is a different operation. Positive mu is explicit, and the
zero-production class has no such witness. No broad no-go result is claimed.

## 3. Bilinear secular estimate with endpoints and BV jumps

For real compactly supported time amplitudes A,B on [0,T_*], extended by zero
to R, suppose their sup norms and total variations are bounded. They may have
different cutoff times. Write I_A(e)=integral A(t)cos(omega t)exp(iet)dt and
similarly for B. For b(e)=r(e)exp(ise) with fixed real s,

    integral b(e) overline(I_A(e)) I_B(e) de
       =(pi/2)[b(omega)+b(-omega)] integral A(t)B(t)dt
           +O(log(2+T_*)).                               (14)

The constant depends on the fixed amplitude and variation bounds and s.
The source threshold statement r(2m+x)=x rho(x), with the stated derivative
and rapid-decay bounds, gives |C_b(delta)|<=C_s(1+|delta|)^-2, where
C_b(delta)=integral b(e)exp(ie delta)de. This also follows by adding the two
source cases r and r(exp(ise)-1). No finite absolute first time moment is
assumed. In general it is false at this threshold.

To prove (14), Fubini gives the time kernel C_b(u-t). The difference-time
part of cos(omega t)cos(omega u) has coefficient 1/2. For delta=u-t set

    J_AB(delta)=integral A(t)B(t+delta)dt.

The BV translation estimate for the zero extension gives

    |J_AB(delta)-J_AB(0)|
       <=||A||_infinity Var(B)|delta|,
    |J_AB(delta)-J_AB(0)|<=C T_*.

Thus the difference is bounded by C min(|delta|,T_*), including when the
supports no longer intersect. Integration against |C_b(delta)| costs at
most C log(2+T_*). Fourier inversion is valid because b is continuous and
C_b is integrable, and yields integral cos(omega delta)C_b(delta)d delta
=pi[b(omega)+b(-omega)]. This proves the main term with its factor pi/2.

For the sum-time part the amplitude A(t)B(t+delta), on its support, has
uniformly bounded sup norm and total variation. The BV product inequality
and zero extensions include every jump and endpoint. Integration against
exp(i2omega t) has modulus at most its total variation divided by 2omega;
an interval formulation gives the same estimate with the two endpoint
terms included. Multiplying by exp(iomega delta) does not change the bound.
The remaining integral of |C_b(delta)| is finite. This part is O(1).
No symmetry, positivity or realness of C_b was used. The two different
cutoffs therefore cause no unaccounted endpoint phase. This proves (14).

Apply (14) with A(t)=a(h_lambda^2 t)1_[0,T_i](t) and
B(t)=a(h_lambda^2 t)1_[0,T_j](t). Their zero-extension variations are at
most Var(a)+2||a||_infinity, uniformly in lambda,i,j. From (11)--(12),

    (K_i^B,R_s K_j^B)_p
       =lambda^2 integral r(e)exp(ise) overline(I_i(e))I_j(e)de.

The diagonal time overlap is h_lambda^-2 J_min(i,j), b(-omega)=0, and
(13) follows. In particular distinct interval kernels K_j^B-K_(j-1)^B
are asymptotically orthogonal in pair norm and have squared norms mu_j.
The imaginary cross overlaps also tend to zero; real polarization of a
single norm would not have established that fact.

## 4. Pair operators, Gaussian number moments and a controlled Weyl limit

This section supplies an implementer argument, rather than inferring one
from the state covariance. For a skew HS kernel K put, initially at finite
rank in a conjugation basis,

    Q*(K)=(1/2)sum_ab K_ab a_a* a_b*,
    Q(K)=Q*(K)*,
    G(K)=Q*(K)-Q(K),    S(K)=exp(G(K)).                    (15)

Finite-rank truncations can be removed in HS norm by the bounds below.
The exterior-product estimate on the n-particle sector gives

    ||Q*(K)psi_n||<=sqrt((n+1)(n+2)/2)||K||_p||psi_n||,
    ||Q(K)psi_n||<=sqrt(n(n-1)/2)||K||_p||psi_n||,
    ||G(K)psi||<=C||K||_HS||(N_F+2)psi||.                (16)

For the last line combine the creation and annihilation bounds separately
across orthogonal output sectors, then use the triangle inequality. The
factor at n=0 agrees with ||Q*(K)Omega_F||=||K||_p.

A compact skew kernel has a unitary-congruence decomposition into two-mode
blocks kappa_r [[0,1],[-1,0]], kappa_r>=0. It follows by pairing vectors in
nonzero eigenspaces of K K* using the skew conjugate-linear map K C; the
partners are orthogonal: on the kappa^2 eigenspace the antiunitary
K C/kappa squares to -I. The nonzero multiplicities are even. A finite
block squeeze acts on its vacuum as

    cos(kappa_r)|00>+sin(kappa_r)|11>.

The off-diagonal one-particle block of S(K) has singular values
|sin(kappa_r)|, each twice, and its diagonal block has cos(kappa_r).
Consequently its off-diagonal HS norm is at most ||K||_HS and its full
one-particle distance from I is at most C||K|| when ||K|| is small.
The vacuum products converge for HS kernels because sum kappa_r^2 is
finite. Together with the operator-norm convergence of their one-particle
canonical maps, the CAR intertwining identity gives strong convergence
on the dense finite-creator orbit of vacuum. The same applies to inverses,
so the limit is a unitary implementer on Fock space. This also constructs
S(K) before any infinite-dimensional generator differentiation.

More generally, every even pure implementable quasifree vector with
one-particle off-diagonal block beta has, after a number-preserving mode
change, pair occupation probabilities p_r in independent two-mode blocks.
For states with small off-diagonal operator norm this also follows directly
from the source skew graph: p_r=ell_r^2/(1+ell_r^2). Therefore for t>=0,

    <exp(t N_F)>
       =product_r [1+p_r(exp(2t)-1)]
       <=exp[(exp(2t)-1)||beta||_HS^2/2].                (17)

The product is justified by finite sections and sum p_r<infinity. Thus all
number moments are bounded if ||beta||_HS is bounded. Every vector in
the squeeze and gauge comparison is a finite product of the given
implementers, their inverses,
number-preserving rotations, or squeezes S(uK), |u| bounded, acting on
vacuum. The off-diagonal HS norm of a product is at most the sum of its
factors' such norms: this follows from the block multiplication law and
contractivity of the diagonal blocks of a one-particle unitary. The same
assertion holds for inverses.
These sums are uniformly bounded. Their off-diagonal operator norms are
also O_word(eta_lambda), so the graph argument for (17) is available.

For complete domain bookkeeping, start all subsequent calculations with
finite-rank kernels. Duhamel's formula and (16), applied along the Gaussian
paths just described, show that changing a kernel by D changes its squeeze
on those vectors by at most C||D||_HS. Uniform bounds (17) hold along the
interpolations between kernels. This proves the Cauchy property under HS
truncation, convergence on the needed vectors, and passage of the integral
identities to the infinite-dimensional limit. The resulting S(K) is the
canonical implementer with the two-mode action above. No global bounded
norm is assigned to Q*(K), Q(K), or N_F. Only their stated number-graph
bounds are used.

The exact finite-rank CAR calculation is

    [Q(L),Q*(M)]=(L,M)_p I-dGamma(M L*),
    [G(L),G(M)]=-2i Im(L,M)_p I
                            +dGamma(M L*-L M*).          (18)

For example on one two-mode block L=l J_2, M=m J_2 the first identity
is bar(l)m(1-N_1-N_2). This checks both the exterior scalar and the sign
of the number remainder. Generally the four single-contraction terms in
the CAR expansion sum to -dGamma(M L*); the two double contractions give
(1/2)Tr L*M. The creator-creator and annihilator-annihilator commutators
vanish, giving the second identity.

Suppose ||L||,||M||<=epsilon and their HS norms are bounded. Set
c=-2i Im(L,M)_p and R=M L*-L M*. Then ||R||<=2epsilon^2 and

    S(tL)G(M)S(-tL)
       =G(M)+tc I+integral_0^t S(uL)dGamma(R)S(-uL)du.   (19)

In a finite-rank proof this is differentiation of a conjugation. On each
Gaussian path the integral remainder has norm <=C epsilon^2, since
||dGamma(R)psi||<=||R||||N_F psi|| and (17) applies. Compare the derivative
of S(tL)S(tM) with that of exp(t^2 c/2)S(t(L+M)). Duhamel, (19), and
unitarity of the comparison propagator give, for each permitted Gaussian
input psi with bounded number moments,

    ||[S(L)S(M)-exp(-i Im(L,M)_p)S(L+M)]psi||
                                                    <=C epsilon^2. (20)

The preceding finite-rank approximation justifies (19)--(20) without an
unbounded-generator BCH assertion. The constants depend on the finite HS
and number-moment resources; they are uniform in the rank.

Iterate (20) a fixed finite number of times. For kernels K_1,...,K_l with
bounded HS norms and operator norms O(epsilon),

    <Omega_F,S(K_1)...S(K_l)Omega_F>
       =exp[-i sum_(p<q) Im(K_p,K_q)_p
                         -(1/2)||sum_q K_q||_p^2]+O_l(epsilon^2). (21)

Indeed the remaining vacuum amplitude is product_r cos(kappa_r), for the
block values of sum K_q. For small epsilon these cosines are positive, and
sum log cos(kappa_r)=-(1/2)sum kappa_r^2
+O(||sum K_q||^2 ||sum K_q||_HS^2). This proves (21) and fixes its phase.
Equation (21) is the vacuum Weyl formula with the pair inner product. It is
not an operator-norm convergence of CAR to CCR on the full Fock space.

## 5. Removing implementer phases and the small gauge rotation

The exact Y_j is not determined by its vacuum graph alone. We now control

    K_j=L_j f(L_j*L_j),
    f(x)=arctan(sqrt(x))/sqrt(x),  f(0)=1.                (22)

In each skew block (22) replaces ell_r by arctan(ell_r). It remains skew,

    Y_j=exp(i phi_j)S(K_j)Gamma_F(U_j),

The second statement follows by composing the one-particle maps of S(K_j)*

For a unitary U on h, tensor-power telescoping proves

    ||[Gamma_F(U)-I]psi||<=||U-I||||N_F psi||.             (25)

Use (24)--(25) on the two exterior gauge factors in each observation

The Duhamel estimate (16) and (23), along the same Gaussian paths, then

## 6. Arbitrary word limit and its explicit formula

Free second quantization gives exactly

    exp(isH0)S(K)exp(-isH0)=S(R_s K).                     (26)

This is first the action on creators at finite rank and then the HS limit;
no derivative of H0 on an unspecified domain is used. For a word indexed
by j_q,s_q, define s_<q=sum_(p<q)s_p. After applying section 5, moving
all free rotations to the right, and using exp(isH0)Omega_F=Omega_F, the
vacuum expectation is a product of 2l squeezes with ordered kernels

    -R_(s_<q) K_(j_q)^B,
     R_(s_<q+s_q) K_(j_q)^B,    q=1,...,l.               (27)

Every pair inner product in (27) is controlled by (13), because
(R_s K_i^B,R_t K_j^B)_p=(K_i^B,R_(t-s)K_j^B)_p. The limiting Gram matrix
is exactly the Gram matrix of the ordered bosonic vectors

    -exp(i omega s_<q)c_(j_q),
     exp(i omega(s_<q+s_q))c_(j_q).                      (28)

Indeed <c_i,c_j>=nu J_min(i,j). Apply (21) to (27), use (13), and compare
with the exact bosonic Weyl relation

    D_B(c)D_B(d)=exp(-i Im<c,d>)D_B(c+d),
    <Omega_B,D_B(c)Omega_B>=exp(-||c||^2/2).

This proves (4). The gauge and kernel replacement error is O(eta_lambda),
the CAR commutator/vacuum-log error is O(eta_lambda^2), and the finitely many
Gram errors are O_word(h_lambda^2 log(2+h_lambda^-2)). The latter two are
smaller than the first for sufficiently small h_lambda. This proves the
stated fixed-word rate. It invokes no mathematical runtime.

For an explicit expression retaining every order-dependent phase, put

    theta_q=omega s_q, Theta_<q=sum_(p<q)theta_p,
    d_q=exp(i Theta_<q)(exp(i theta_q)-1)c_(j_q).

Then the right side of (4) equals

    exp[ i sum_q ||c_(j_q)||^2 sin(theta_q)
          -i sum_(p<q) Im<d_p,d_q>
          -(1/2)||sum_q d_q||^2 ].                       (29)

For a one-letter word it reduces to
exp(||c_j||^2(exp(i theta)-1)), verifying the one-time normalization.
For repeated adjacent identical observation times, the product reduces
exactly to the sum of their spectral parameters, as it must. All Gram
limits use lambda^2, so either sign of the real coupling has the same
answer. The real signed BV envelope enters through its squared integral;
no envelope sign has been replaced inside the microscopic evolution.

## 7. Chronological products and a noncommutative order check

Factor the bosonic Fock space into its k mode factors. In mode r the
observable B_j(s) is the unshifted rotation exp(iomega s N_r) if j<r,
and is the same displaced rotation D_B(sqrt(mu_r)e_r)*
exp(iomega s N_r)D_B(sqrt(mu_r)e_r) if j>=r.

In the chronological product B_1(s_1)...B_k(s_k), the unshifted rotations
are all on the left in each mode, where they disappear against the vacuum
bra. The remaining displaced rotations combine with their common
conjugating displacement. Their vacuum expectation is
exp(mu_r(exp(iomega sum_(j>=r)s_j)-1)). Multiplying the modes proves (5).
In reverse chronological order the unshifted rotations instead disappear
on the right against vacuum, proving the same formula. This mode argument
uses the additional finite-word theorem; the marginal Poisson law alone
would not justify it.

Other orders can be detected already with three distinct times. Let
mu_2>0, choose s_1=s_2=pi/omega and s_3=-pi/omega, and compare

    chronological: A_1(s_1) A_2(s_2) A_3(s_3),
    permuted:       A_2(s_2) A_1(s_1) A_3(s_3).           (30)

The limits are respectively

    exp(-2mu_1-2mu_3),
    exp(-2mu_1-8mu_2-2mu_3).                             (31)

Here is a separate direct check of the extra factor, including its
coefficient. In mode 2 write alpha=sqrt(mu_2), R(phi)=exp(i phi N_2),
and C(theta)=D_B(alpha)*R(theta)D_B(alpha). The permuted mode word is
C(pi)R(pi)C(-pi). Its vacuum expectation is the characteristic function
of N_2 in C(-pi)Omega_B. The latter vector is a coherent vector with
amplitude (exp(-i pi)-1)alpha=-2alpha, up to an irrelevant phase. Its
number law has mean 4mu_2, so its R(pi) expectation is exp(-8mu_2).
The chronological mode word is R(pi)C(pi)C(-pi), with expectation 1.
Modes 1 and 3 supply exp(-2mu_1) and exp(-2mu_3) in both orders.
This gives (31) without using the phase formula (29). The condition
mu_2>0 is realizable: the allowed corrected859 source supplies nonzero
compact witnesses with r(omega)>0, and one may take a=1 on that interval.

Thus there is generally an order dependence of order one in the limit.
No claim of convergence to a commuting energy process on all vacuum
words is valid. The limiting auxiliary observables contain the total
number operator, including modes not yet displaced at a given time;
those modes matter when later and earlier observables are interleaved.

## 8. Finite-bin spectral passage and the actual measurement protocol

Let E_j^lambda(B)=Y_j*1_B(H0)Y_j. Commutation of the free rotations with
the energy projectors and telescoping of full propagators turn (6) into

    p_lambda(z_1,...,z_k)
       =||E_k^lambda(B_k)...E_1^lambda(B_1)Omega_F||^2.  (32)

This is a palindromic expectation of projectors after squaring the norm.
It is not an ordered unitary characteristic function by definition.
The corresponding limiting projectors are

    E_j^B(B)=D_B(c_j)*1_B(omega N_B)D_B(c_j).             (33)

We justify their use, since weak marginal convergence would not suffice
for the product in (32). For each allowed bin choose a smooth function
0<=f_j<=1 on [0,infinity), equal to its indicator outside small strips
around the finitely many positive bin boundaries. Choose the strips to
contain no omega n. The first bin's extension is identically 1 near 0;
no transition is needed there. Each f_j on [0,infinity) can be represented
by a C_c^infinity function restricted to this half-line or by 1 plus such
a function, using a smooth extension below 0. There is also a nonnegative
C_c^infinity function g_j supported in those boundary strips, with

    |1_(B_j)(x)-f_j(x)|^2<=g_j(x),  x>=0,
    g_j(omega n)=0 for every n.                          (34)

Every C_c^infinity function has an integrable Fourier transform. Fourier
inversion, (4), and dominated convergence therefore give convergence of
vacuum expectations of any fixed product of these smooth spectral
functions. Constants are handled directly. Fubini is justified by the
product of the finite Fourier L1 norms and the unitary bound 1.

Write F_j^lambda=f_j(H_j^lambda). Product telescoping, retaining exact
projectors on the left and smooth factors on the right, bounds the norm
of the difference between the vectors in (32) and
F_k^lambda...F_1^lambda Omega_F by the sum over j of

    ||[E_j^lambda(B_j)-F_j^lambda]
                                 F_(j-1)^lambda...F_1^lambda Omega_F||.

Its squared summand is bounded by

    <Omega_F,F_1^lambda...F_(j-1)^lambda
          g_j(H_j^lambda) F_(j-1)^lambda...F_1^lambda Omega_F>. (35)

All f_j are real and contractions. The bound follows from spectral
calculus and (34), so no commutation of observations at different times
has been used. The smooth-word limit of (35) is zero: g_j(H_j^B)=0 as an
operator because H_j^B=D_B(c_j)*omega N_B D_B(c_j) has precisely the
lattice spectrum. Thus the vector error tends to zero. In the limiting
bosonic system f_j(H_j^B)=E_j^B(B_j) exactly. Applying the smooth-word
limit also to the squared norm proves

    p_lambda -> ||E_k^B(B_k)...E_1^B(B_1)Omega_B||^2.     (36)

There is no high-energy tail assumption hidden here: the unbounded final
bin is represented by 1 plus a compactly supported smooth function, and
all boundary errors have compact support. No rate for (36) is asserted.

It remains to evaluate the actual sequential protocol in (36), rather
than replace it by (5). Transform (36) back to the bosonic Schrödinger
picture. Its successive interaction propagators are

    D_B(c_j)D_B(c_(j-1))*=D_B(sqrt(mu_j)e_j),             (37)

because the prefix Gram products are real. At step j the newly displaced
mode has been vacuum and is orthogonal to all previously displaced modes.
The displacement leaves all earlier modes unchanged. Earlier projectors
onto bins of total number preserve the future modes' vacuum. In the
occupation basis, the new coherent mode has diagonal weights
exp(-mu_j)mu_j^n/n!, independently of the previous occupation indices.
A projector onto a bin of total number keeps exactly those basis vectors
whose cumulative occupation lies in the bin. Induction on j gives the
sum of products in (7). Within-bin coherences are allowed throughout;
they cannot change these diagonal weights because no later displacement
acts on the previous modes. This explicitly accounts for measurement
back-action and proves the finite-bin theorem.

## 9. Sharp qualifications and limitations of the inference

The positive-boundary condition cannot simply be dropped. H0 in the
continuum Fock representation has no positive eigenvalues: on every
n>=1 sector it multiplies by sum_(r=1)^n E(p_r), and every fixed positive
level has Lebesgue measure zero. For fixed other momenta, the remaining
one-variable equation has at most two solutions (or none), so Fubini
proves the assertion, also at thresholds. Only the n=0 sector gives the
vacuum eigenvalue 0. Hence 1_{omega}(H0)=0. If nu J_j>0 the limiting
Poisson law instead has mass exp(-nu J_j)nu J_j at omega. Thus arbitrary
Borel-bin convergence is false. In particular the actual probabilities
for [0,omega) and [0,omega] agree, whereas their proposed Poisson limits
differ. At least one of those endpoint conventions cannot obey the naive
boundary-free rule. This argument does not determine the separate limit
for an interval whose endpoint is exactly omega.

The independence in (7) concerns finitely many increments of the limiting
specified measurement protocol. It does not mean simultaneous values
exist for the noncommuting finite-lambda observables, or that all quantum
orderings become classical. Equation (31) preserves a contrary test.
There is no continuous-path tightness, point-process construction for the
microscopic dynamics, increasing observation count, vanishing bin width,
measurement-rate limit or exact-energy measurement instrument claim.

The state is the free vacuum, the number of observations is fixed, the
spatial slots are fixed real compact smooth functions, and the real BV
envelope and its finite horizon are fixed. The carrier is constant and
strictly above 2m. Constants are not uniform at the threshold, in a
massless limit, for diverging variation, varying spatial slots or word
length, finite-volume recurrence, or an interacting spectrum. No chirp,
new initial-state result, feedback protocol or adaptive observation-time
choice is imported. The results are continuum supplied-model theorems.
No calibrated-cell multi-time transfer has been included; its hypothesis
and instrument comparison would need separate accounting.

The moment control used to remove gauges is particle-number control on
specified Gaussian paths, not a general energy bound for all states after
projective collapse. The collapse argument instead uses the bounded
smooth-word approximation (34)--(36). All observation functions and
projectors are bounded. Their definitions require only selfadjointness
and unitary conjugation of H0. The unbounded pair-generator calculations
are justified by finite rank, number-graph estimates and Gaussian moment
bounds, not unbounded trace cycling or an informal BCH series.
