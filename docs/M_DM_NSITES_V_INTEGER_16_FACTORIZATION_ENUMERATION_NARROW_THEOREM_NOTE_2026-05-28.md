### 1.2 What it means to be a "candidate factorization" here

A **candidate factorization** of `N_sites = 16` is a pair `(a, b)` of
positive integers (or rationals) with `a · b = 16` such that some
distinct framework primitive identifies the factor `a` with a counting
structure on one framework substrate, and the factor `b` with a
counting structure on a different framework substrate (or the same
substrate viewed through a different algebraic lens). The
factorization is therefore distinguished by **which two primitives** it
identifies the two factors with — not by the arithmetic identity
alone, which is trivially `16 = a · (16/a)` for any positive divisor
`a`.

The arithmetic identity `a · b = 16` is exact integer arithmetic. The
**physical or algebraic identification** of each factor with a
specific framework primitive is what carries the (un)derived
mechanism content, and is what each cited authority covers (or
forecloses).
