## 2. A BV graph-domain lemma with explicit variation dependence

This abstract lemma holds for every positive integer r; (N2) chooses its
value for the density comparison. In particular r=2 controls fourth moments.
Fix a positive reference energy unit and express H and all energies in that
unit in this lemma; the occurrences of 1 can equivalently be replaced by that
unit after rescaling. Let H>=0 be selfadjoint and V(t)=V(t)* bounded. Suppose

    ||V(t)|| <= K,
    ||ad_H^j V(t)|| <= M_j,  1<=j<=r-1,                       (N8)

with the literal norm-orbit/domain meaning just stated. Assume V(t) and all
these bounded commutators are operator-norm BV. For the finite family this
follows from the BV scalar controls, with

    A_ell=||u_ell||_infinity, nu_ell=Var_[0,T](u_ell),
    K=sum_ell A_ell M_{ell,0}, M_j=sum_ell A_ell M_{ell,j},
    Omega=sum_ell nu_ell sum_{j=0}^{r-1} M_{ell,j}.               (N9)

Omega bounds the sum of the variations of the commutators of orders 0
through r-1. A smaller actual variation budget can be used. It counts
changes, including any specified endpoint jumps; it does not count the
length or number of constant holds. Bounded amplitudes without a variation
bound are not being substituted for this resource.

Define A_t=H+V(t)+K+1, so A_t>=1, and put

    b_0=2K+1, b_j=M_j (1<=j<=r-1),
    D_0=a_0=1,
    D_n=D_{n-1}+sum_{j=0}^{n-1} binom(n-1,j)b_j D_{n-1-j},
    a_n=a_{n-1}[1+sum_{j=0}^{n-1} binom(n-1,j)b_j],
    d_n=sum_{k=0}^n binom(n,k)D_k.                             (N10)

All constants are finite, nonnegative and independent of elapsed time for
fixed suprema. Domain bootstrap gives D(A_t^n)=D(H^n), n<=r. To check this
without assuming a power identity, norm C^{r-1} free regularity implies that
V preserves D(H^{r-1}) and each lower domain. Induct on n: on D(H^{n-1}),
A_t psi belongs to D(H^{n-1}) exactly when H psi does, since
(V(t)+K+1)psi already belongs there. This is precisely D(H^n).
The same induction in the opposite direction proves equality rather than
only inclusion. Bounded selfadjoint perturbation supplies selfadjointness
on D(H) at the first step.

The following three bounded closures follow:

    ||H^n A_t^{-n}|| <= D_n,
    ||A_t^n(H+1)^{-n}|| <= a_n,
    ||(H+1)^n A_t^{-n}|| <= d_n.                              (N11)

Here A_t^{-n} maps into the common n-th domain. For the first inequality
write H^n=H^{n-1}A_t-H^{n-1}(V+K+1), and commute H through the second
factor by the binomial Leibniz identity. A term with j commutators has
H^{n-1-j} A_t^{-n}; factoring A_t^{-(n-1-j)} leaves a contraction because
A_t>=1. This gives exactly D_n. For the second, apply the previous bound
for A_t^{n-1} to H psi+(V+K+1)psi and commute (H+1)^{n-1} through the
bounded second factor. Every remaining power is at most (H+1)^n in norm.
This yields a_n. Expand (H+1)^n and use the first inequality to obtain d_n.
All identities are on D(H^n) before taking bounded closures.

A crude iteration of equivalent graph norms would give an exponential in
variation. A triangular estimate retains a polynomial bound instead. Define
a strictly lower triangular (r+1) by (r+1) matrix C, indexed 0,...,r, by

    C_{n,l}=sum_{k=n-1-l}^{n-1}
             a_k binom(k,n-1-l) d_{k-(n-1-l)}, 0<=l<n,
    C_{n,l}=0 otherwise.                                      (N12)

Let 1_vec denote the vector with all entries one and define

    P_r(x)=[exp(x C) 1_vec]_r
          =sum_{k=0}^r x^k/k! [C^k 1_vec]_r,
    G_r=D_r a_r P_r(Omega).                                   (N13)

This is an explicit polynomial of degree at most r with nonnegative
coefficients, and G_r>=1. No infinite matrix or exponential growth in x is
hidden: C^{r+1}=0.

To verify the jump estimate let A'=A+D for a change D=V'-V and put
m_j(D)=||ad_H^j D||, omega(D)=sum_{j=0}^{r-1}m_j(D). On D(H^n),

    A'^n-A^n=sum_{k=0}^{n-1} A'^k D A^{n-1-k}.                  (N14)

Use (N11) for A', commute (H+1)^k through D, and then use (N11) for A.
The k,j term has norm at most

    a_k binom(k,j) m_j(D) d_{k-j} ||A^{n-1-j}psi||.             (N15)

For example the last step factors (H+1)^{k-j} A^{-(k-j)}; its other
factor is A^{n-1-j}psi. The domains in (N14) are legitimate because D
preserves each domain up to order n-1. Thus, if z_n=||A^n psi|| and
z'_n=||A'^n psi||,

    z' <= [I+omega(D) C] z                                   (N16)

entrywise. For a stationary hold generated by H+V, every z_n is exactly
preserved, since the group commutes with A. It is A, not H, whose graph
norm is conserved on that hold.

For a step control multiply (N16) over its jumps. All dominating matrices
are polynomials in the same nonnegative C. Their coefficient of C^k is
an elementary symmetric sum in the nonnegative jump budgets, bounded by
Omega^k/k!. Thus their product is entrywise at most exp(Omega C).
Since A_s>=1, all initial z_n are at most z_r, and (N11) proves

    ||H^r U(t,s)psi|| <= G_r ||(H+1)^r psi||,
    U(t,s) D(H^r)=D(H^r), 0<=s,t<=T.                          (N17)

The budgets may be taken on the whole interval, giving a uniform bound for
all subintervals. Applying the same argument to inverse stationary holds
and the reversed sequence of jumps proves the reverse domain inclusion.
Negative hold durations do not alter the graph-norm conservation argument.

For a BV path choose step approximants by ordered time sampling. For the
finite family they have the same amplitude bounds and no greater scalar
variations (or arbitrarily small enlargement at endpoints). In the abstract
case use the Banach norm given by the sum of the bounded commutator norms
of orders 0 through r-1: its variation is bounded by the sum of their
individual variations. Sampling has no greater variation in that norm.
In either case the potentials converge in L1 operator norm. Bounded interaction-picture
Dyson evolution exists, and its Duhamel estimate gives

    ||U_n(t,s)-U(t,s)|| <= integral_s^t ||V_n(x)-V(x)|| dx       (N18)

with the absolute orientation for reversed endpoints, uniformly on the
interval. Strong vector integrals suffice; norm continuity of an unbounded
free group is never assumed. V is bounded, strongly measurable, and the
interaction-picture coefficients are strongly measurable. These are enough
for the vector Dyson construction and its bounded norm estimates.

For psi in D(H^r), the uniform graph estimate and (N18) imply that U psi
belongs to D(H^r): a weakly convergent subsequence of H^r U_n psi has limit
H^r U psi by testing against D(H^r) and selfadjointness. Lower semicontinuity
gives (N17). Equivalently use bounded spectral cutoffs of H^{2r}.
Run the same argument for U_n* to obtain the domain equality. Uniform graph
boundedness alone is not claimed to give convergence in the graph norm;
that stronger statement is not needed here.

For any Hilbert-Schmidt column R with (H+1)^r R Hilbert-Schmidt, the same
proof, first on finite columns and then by closure, gives

    ||H^r U(t,s) R||_HS <= G_r ||(H+1)^r R||_HS.               (N19)

At jumps apply (N14) and the triangle inequality in Hilbert-Schmidt norm;
on holds each norm ||A^n R||_HS is conserved. At the BV limit, bounded
spectral cutoffs and strong/HS column convergence prove lower
semicontinuity. For R=rho^{1/2}, the input is the genuine free 2r-th moment.
It is not a requirement that all state vectors lie in D(H^{2r}); the column
condition is D(H^r) in mean square. Coherence and infinite rank cause no
change in these arguments.

For reference, a separate first-mean estimate needs no commutator condition.
For step controls conservation of the shifted interacting quadratic form
on each hold and the bounded change of potential at jumps give

    Tr rho_t H <= Tr rho_s H+2K+Var(V;[s,t]).                  (N20)

At finite free mean, bounded perturbation gives equal form domains for
H+1 and H+V+K+1. Spectral truncation of initial columns justifies their
expectations; jumps change the potential mean by at most ||D||. Pass to
BV controls by (N18) and bounded spectral cutoffs. Thus (N20) holds for
normal states initially only in the free form domain. It does not assert
higher free moments from a first mean.

This completes an actual BV graph-domain result before any density limit.
The same proof applies separately with (H,V)=(H_c,V_c) and (F_c,W_c).
It supplies actual free-domain propagation, not conservation of interacting
powers along a nonstationary path. With bounded suprema, (N13) gives

    G_r <= C_r(1+sum_ell nu_ell)^r,                           (N21)

where C_r depends on the fixed family, order and amplitude caps. The bound
is sufficient; no optimality of this variation power or its constants is
claimed. A scalar identity control can have arbitrarily large variation

## 3. Three propagators, and their separate comparison mechanisms

Denote by U_c(t,s) the actual propagator for H_c+V_c(t) on Pi_c, by
U_L(t,s) the propagator for H_{L,c}+W_c(t) on q_c, and by U_F(t,s) the
full-low propagator for F_c+W_c(t). The last preserves q_c. On that band
all three free generator domains have already been specified. These are
three different evolutions; compression of the generator is not assumed
to equal compression of U_c.

First block-diagonalize only the bounded interaction:

    V_bd(t)=P V_c(t) P+Q V_c(t) Q.

The generator H_c+V_bd(t) reduces P and Q. Its P evolution is exactly U_L.
The off-diagonal selfadjoint matrix V_c-V_bd has norm ||Q V_c P||, since
its square has diagonal blocks B*B and BB*. Bounded time-dependent Duhamel
therefore gives, on the actual physical space,

    ||U_c(t,0)-U_bd(t,0)||
       <= integral_0^t ||Q V_c(x) P|| dx <= B_c(T).             (N22)

No domain regularity of the initial state is needed. This bound is on
vectors, before any high trace is discarded. Its error is a sufficient
linear high-tail resource. No denominator Lambda_c and no invariant
interacting window are being smuggled into it.

For the kinetic comparison let R be any Hilbert-Schmidt low column supported
in q_c, with (F_c+1)^r R Hilbert-Schmidt. First use step controls. On a
constant interval, bounded perturbations preserve the common first domain;
(N17) also preserves its r-th power domain. The usual differentiated
product on D(N^r), then its strong vector integral, gives the difference
of U_L and U_F with source (H_{L,c}-F_c) U_F(x,0)R. Sum over the finitely
many intervals. By (N3), the joint free spectral order H_0^r<=F_c^r and
(N19),

    ||[U_L(t,0)-U_F(t,0)]R||_HS
      <= L_J c^a integral_0^t ||H_0^r U_F(x,0)R||_HS dx
      <= T L_J c^a G_r ||(F_c+1)^r R||_HS.                    (N23)

For the unbounded product rule one can first take finite particle/momentum
columns in D(N^r); the free remainders then act on the genuine common
domain. Graph continuity of a stationary selfadjoint group gives the
strong differentiation on each hold. The bound extends by the closed
column norms. Finally approximate both controls by the same step controls.
Their bounded interaction differences yield operator-norm convergence of
the two propagators separately, and G_r is uniform. This proves (N23) for
BV controls. It does not require an unproved graph-norm limit in (N18), nor
an unproved strong differentiation of a general BV path.

The same G_r from (N9)–(N13) is a valid upper bound for both actual and
corrected graph propagation, because (N5) bounds each required commutator.
The actual graph theorem and (N23) are different uses of those premises.
Neither one is being inferred from the other or from CAR convergence.

For later use, if F and L are Hilbert-Schmidt columns, then

    ||FF*-LL*||_1 <= (||F||_HS+||L||_HS)||F-L||_HS.             (N24)

This follows from (F-L)F*+L(F*-L*) and the Hilbert-Schmidt product inequality.
All density estimates below use (N24), partial-trace contraction and bounded

## 4. Full placed density theorem, including normal mixed states

Let rho_c be any normal initial density on the actual physical range, with

    E_p=Tr rho_c H_c^p < infinity, p>0,
    lambda_c=Tr_H rho_c,
    sigma_c(t)=U_F(t,0) lambda_c U_F(t,0)*,
    alpha_c(T)=sup_{0<=t<=T}
       ||U_c(t,0)rho_c U_c(t,0)*
            -sigma_c(t) tensor |Omega_H><Omega_H|||_1.         (N25)

The actual density in this formula is placed in F_L tensor F_H through the
supplied isometry; notation suppresses that isometry only. lambda_c is
supported in q_c and is a density. It need not tend to a continuum state.
Thus alpha compares matched actual/effective states, not a separately
selected fixed limiting state. All nonnegative moments mean monotone
spectral-cutoff expectations. Coherent, nonfaithful and infinite-rank
normal states are allowed; no parity assumption on rho_c is needed for
the estimates. Even initial states remain even under the stipulated controls.

For 0<R<Lambda_c put E_R=1_[0,R](H_c) and rho_R=E_R rho_c E_R. This is
an unnormalized corner. Since every occupied high mode costs at least
Lambda_c, E_R<=P exactly. The free initial cutoff is static; it is not
assumed invariant under U_c. Define lambda_R=Tr_H rho_R. The elementary
Hilbert-Schmidt factorization with rho_c^{1/2} gives

    ||rho_c-rho_R||_1 <= 2 sqrt(Tr rho_c(1-E_R))
                      <= 2 sqrt(E_p) R^{-p/2}.                (N26)

Apply each of the two channels in (N25) to this replacement. Both channels
contract trace norm, so their combined replacement cost is at most
4 sqrt(E_p) R^{-p/2}. This is why normalizing the corner is unnecessary.
The cross terms for this energy cutoff are not generally killed by low
partial trace; no incorrect positive-corner claim is made here.

On rho_R, (N22) contributes at most 2 B_c(T) by (N24). Its block-diagonal
output is U_L lambda_R U_L* tensor high vacuum. Taking R-column
lambda_R^{1/2} in (N23) contributes at most
2T L_J c^a G_r ||(F_c+1)^r lambda_R^{1/2}||_HS. Hence

    alpha_c(T) <= min{2,
       4 sqrt(E_p) R^{-p/2}+2 B_c(T)
       +2T L_J c^a G_r ||(F_c+1)^r lambda_R^{1/2}||_HS}.        (N27)

The final minimum is valid because the two full states have unit trace;
intermediate corners need not. The initial corner lies in the low free
window H_{L,c}<=R, and F_c is the smaller commuting multiplier there.
Consequently the final column is at most (1+R)^r. This already yields a
BV extension using any positive moment cap, with the same polynomial-in-R
graph order used in the stationary parent.

There is a stronger moment-sensitive bound. If 0<p<=2r, the initial joint
spectral orders, positivity and bounded cutoffs give

    Tr lambda_R F_c^{2r}
       <= Tr rho_R H_c^{2r} <= E_p R^{2r-p},
    ||(F_c+1)^r lambda_R^{1/2}||_HS
       <= 2^{r-1}[1+sqrt(E_p) R^{r-p/2}].                     (N28)

To justify the second line, use (1+x)^r<=2^{r-1}(1+x^r) in the single
spectral variable, followed by the Hilbert-Schmidt triangle inequality.
No commutation of lambda_R with F_c is required. The first line uses the
initial low free spectral cutoff and joint multiplication; it is not a
propagation claim about the interacting state. Thus for 0<p<=2r,

    alpha_c(T) <= min{2,
       4 sqrt(E_p) R^{-p/2}+2 B_c(T)
       +2^r T L_J c^a G_r[1+sqrt(E_p) R^{r-p/2}]}.              (N29)

This is an explicit finite-parameter estimate. For a useful choice define

    theta_c=T c^a G_r,
    eta_c=(2/Lambda_c)^r+theta_c,
    R=eta_c^{-1/r} <= Lambda_c/2.                             (N30)

Then (N29) gives

    alpha_c(T) <= min{2,
       2 B_c(T)+2^r L_J eta_c
       +(4+2^r L_J) sqrt(E_p) eta_c^{p/(2r)}}.                 (N31)

This version is convenient for 0<p<2r; it remains a valid bound at p=2r.
It includes the high-discard floor as well as the elapsed time. If eta_c
is not small it is still a bound after the cap by two, not a convergence
assertion. Fixed reference units are understood in this cutoff choice.

For higher initial moments a separate high-vacuum corner is more efficient.
Let rho_P=P rho_c P, lambda_P=Tr_H rho_P, and

    q=Tr rho_c Q <= min{1,E_p/Lambda_c^p},
    B_rho=||(1+H_c)^r rho_c^{1/2}||_HS.                        (N32)

The initial actual replacement costs at most 2 sqrt(q). In this particular
corner the cross high-vacuum/nonvacuum terms have zero high partial trace,
so lambda_c-lambda_P=Tr_H(Q rho_c Q)>=0 and its trace norm is exactly q.
The combined initial cost is therefore 2 sqrt(q)+q, rather than using a
second generic gentle bound. Joint free orders and compression imply

    ||(1+F_c)^r lambda_P^{1/2}||_HS <= B_rho.                  (N33)

For example square the columns, take their nonnegative traces, and note
that (1+F_c)^{2r} on high vacuum is at most the commuting (1+H_c)^{2r};
then pass through bounded cutoffs. This does not square a noncommuting
operator comparison.

When p>=2r, the scalar spectral probability distribution of H_c gives
B_rho<infinity and the explicit Lp/Minkowski bound

    B_rho <= (1+E_p^{1/p})^r.

Applying (N22) and (N23) directly to lambda_P yields

    alpha_c(T) <= min{2,
       2 sqrt(q)+q+2 B_c(T)+2 L_J theta_c B_rho}.              (N34)

The column assumptions in (N23) have now been established from the stated
initial moment; they were not assumed from a trace estimate. At p=2r this
is a useful sharper alternative to (N31). At arbitrary p>0, (N27)–(N31)
prove the density limit even when the untruncated initial state has no
finite 2r-th moment. The graph lemma is applied only to the corner in that
case. It would be false to claim (N19) for the untruncated state on the
basis of the first-moment density theorem.

## 5. Resource limits and the constant-control check

Keep the polynomial family, J and the coefficient suprema uniformly fixed,
and suppose the initial E_p is uniformly bounded. The precise sufficient
conditions for the displayed comparison are

    Lambda_c -> infinity, theta_c=T_c c^a G_r ->0,
    B_c(T_c)->0.                                             (N35)

For the fixed H^a family and bounded amplitudes, (N7) makes the last
condition automatic from the second: B_c(T_c)<=T_c o(c^a), G_r>=1.
Growing variations remain visible through G_r. From (N21) one sufficient
simpler resource is

    T_c c^{2J+2}(1+sum_ell Var(u_ell;[0,T_c]))^{2J+3} ->0.     (N36)

This is a sufficient joint resource, not a necessary criterion or a claim
that variation by itself causes growth. For growing coefficient suprema
one must recompute K, all M_j, G_r and B_c(T); no fixed-resource little-o
conclusion is imported silently.

Under the fixed amplitude and moment caps, for 0<p<2r formula (N31) is

    alpha_c(T_c)=O(eta_c^{p/(2r)})+2 B_c(T_c)
       when eta_c->0,
    eta_c=O(c^r)+T_c c^a G_r.                                (N37)

For p>=2r, (N34) instead gives

    alpha_c(T_c)=O(c^{p/2}+T_c c^a G_r)+2 B_c(T_c).             (N38)

All constants in these two O statements depend only on the named fixed
resources. At fixed positive time with bounded variations, these are
respectively the rates c^{a p/(2r)} and c^a. At bounded variations and
T_c>=1 the qualitative growing-time condition is T_c c^a->0. The c^r
term in eta correctly retains a small initial high occupation when T=0.
The formula is also valid for shrinking times; a shorthand (1+T)c^a
would be less precise there.

For a first free mean, p=1, the moment-sensitive exponent at nonshrinking
time is 1/(4J+6) in T c^{2J+2}. A second free moment, p=2, gives
1/(2J+3); a fourth free moment, p=4, gives 2/(2J+3). These exponents
refer to the moment of the initial free Hamiltonian and the trace norm in
(N25). They are not new assertions of energy convergence from that trace
norm. The stronger initial moments can be provided by a separately checked
preparation; bounded interaction strength alone does not provide them.

For constant controls all scalar variations vanish. Then P_r(0)=1,
G_r=D_r a_r is independent of T, and the sufficient horizon is exactly
the stationary parent's T=o(c^{-(2J+2)}). A stationary first interacting
cap Tr rho(G+K)<=E also supplies Tr rho H<=E by its bounded form order,
so it belongs to the p=1 class here. The parent's exponent 1/(4J+7) was
explicitly non-optimal: it bounded every truncated vector by R^r.
Retaining the mixed-state moment in (N28) gives 1/(4J+6). This does not
contradict its theorem or rely on a new interacting spectral cutoff.

Our high-block term 2 B_c(T) is generally worse than the stationary
endpoint/high-gap and T beta_c^2/Delta terms. At constant controls one
can use the minimum of the applicable stationary and present bounds.
For the fixed regular family beta_c=o(c^a), the loss does not change the
stated polynomial kinetic horizon. No claim that the sharper stationary
high-gap formula survives arbitrary BV switching is required for this
extension. Deriving such an improvement would need another argument.

To see directly why the avoided shortcut is invalid, consider even the
bounded two-dimensional example H=diag(0,2), with a jump from V=0 to
V=k sigma_x, k!=0. The state initially in the old low eigenline does not
lie in the new low eigenline immediately after the jump; the propagator
is continuous whereas the instantaneous spectral projection changes.
This abstract example is not offered as an exact local-CAR realization.
It suffices to refute the proposed general implication of instantaneous
window invariance. Our actual-model comparison instead uses the fixed
free P and a free initial cutoff, with no such hypothesis.

## 6. Other full-low generators and energy readouts

Suppose a separately supplied full-low bounded-operator selfadjoint
potential Z_c(t) is strongly measurable and has an integrable comparison

    d_c(T)=integral_0^T ||Z_c(t)-W_c(t)|| dt < infinity.        (N39)

Its operator norm is then integrable on the finite interval, since W_c is
uniformly bounded. The vector Dyson propagator exists. For this propagator
of F_c+Z_c(t), the bounded Duhamel formula on the full
low space and (N24) add at most 2 d_c(T) to any bound for alpha_c(T).
No commutator estimate for Z is needed for this trace comparison itself.
Z need not preserve q_c for this full-space statement, although the
exact compressed target W does. Its own graph-domain propagation and
free-moment bounds require its own domain/commutator hypotheses; closeness
in bounded operator norm alone does not transfer those conclusions.
This is a conditional substitution rule, not an explicit potential
expansion, a rate for one, or a verification of any excluded potential draft.

If a separate initial fourth-free-moment bound and the required first
commutator resources are supplied, the r=2 graph lemma here, or the sharper
own fourth-moment formulas, give uniform fourth moments when the amplitude
and variation budgets are uniform. Those actual/effective moment bounds
can be combined with the present alpha and the previously proved weighted
trace/energy-law estimates. The density comparison alone cannot replace
these moment and native-energy placement premises. Likewise the full-low
F_c is a corrected free generator, not H_c+V_c(t), and no time-dependent
interacting energy is called conserved. Formula (N20) and the graph lemma
state precisely what replaces that erroneous conservation argument.

## 7. Actual even-Fock controls on time and moment exponents

These examples use zero controls, u_ell=0, which belong to the stated BV
class for any fixed allowed family. The actual and corrected evolutions
are then the supplied free multipliers. The examples are actual even
normal states on their physical Fock bands. They do not claim preparation
by one fixed near-ground ramp or pulse. Such state-selection constraints
would be additional restrictions on the class in (N25).

The corrected parent gives, for z=c^2 p^2 and u=v^2 p^2/E(p)^2,

    e_{L,c}(p)=E(p) Phi(z,u),
    Phi(z,u)=sum_{n>=0} d_n(u) z^n,
    d_0=1, d_n(u)>0 for u>0,
    sum_{n>=1}d_n(u)=sqrt(1+u)-1<=1/2.                       (N40)

The positive coefficients are continuous polynomials in u; in particular
d_{J+1}(1)>0. Removing the first omitted term leaves a tail bounded by
(1/2)z^{J+2} uniformly for 0<=u,z<=1. Thus on any fixed compact momentum
set away from zero,

    c^{-a}[e_{L,c}(p)-e_c^[J](p)] ->
       g_J(p)=E(p)d_{J+1}(v^2p^2/E(p)^2) p^a>0               (N41)

uniformly. This argument comes from the literal supplied multiplier; a
symbolic Taylor check or a numerical sample is unnecessary.

First take two normalized positive-energy one-particle packets with disjoint
compact momentum supports away from zero, and let chi be their normalized
antisymmetric pair. It has two particles and is orthogonal to the vacuum.
For all sufficiently small c it belongs to q_c. The normalized even vector
psi=(Omega+chi)/sqrt(2) is then an actual allowed initial state under the
inverse physical embedding. Its moments of every fixed free order are
uniformly bounded. Choose the packet supports sufficiently narrow and a
fixed tau>0 such that tau[g_J(p_1)+g_J(p_2)] stays inside an interval away
from 2 pi Z on the support. At T_c=tau c^{-a}, (N41) gives a nontrivial
limiting pair phase relative to the vacuum. More explicitly the overlap
of the two evolved pure states is

    (1+z_c)/2,
    z_c=<chi, exp(-i T_c dGamma(e_{L,c}-e_c^[J])) chi>.         (N42)

Choose the phase interval around pi, so Re z_c<=0 eventually. The pure
state trace distance 2 sqrt(1-|(1+z_c)/2|^2) then stays bounded away from
zero. This disproves a general extension to T of order c^{-a}, even with
zero variation and uniformly bounded moments of all orders. It says
nothing against specially chosen stationary states or time-dependent
controls with cancellations. The theorem's strict small kinetic resource
has a genuine free-phase obstruction in the unrestricted state class.

There is also a state-family test of the moment exponent at fixed T=1.
Put

    L_c=c^{-a/r}, so c L_c=c^{1/r}->0.                       (N43)

Choose two disjoint compact intervals of positive scaled momenta x_1,x_2,
centered at positive q_1,q_2. Under p=L_c x, the first omitted term and
(N40) imply uniformly on those intervals

    e_{L,c}(L_c x)-e_c^[J](L_c x)
        -> v d_{J+1}(1) x^r.                               (N44)

Indeed E(L_c x)/L_c->v x, u(L_c x)->1 and c^a L_c^r=1. The higher tail
is at most a constant times L_c(c L_c)^{a+2}
=[c^a L_c^r] O((c L_c)^2)->0. All these momenta remain strictly inside
the actual band. Choose q_1,q_2 and then sufficiently small disjoint
intervals so that v d_{J+1}(1)(x_1^r+x_2^r) lies in
[3pi/4,5pi/4]. This is possible by a positive common rescaling of any two
distinct positive centers. Let chi_c be the normalized antisymmetric pair
of rescaled packets in these intervals. Then

    z_c=<chi_c, exp(-i dGamma(e_{L,c}-e_c^[J])) chi_c>,
    Re z_c<=0 eventually,
    H_c on the pair support lies between b L_c and B L_c      (N45)

for positive constants b,B. The last assertion uses the exact low branch
and c L_c->0, not an unbounded continuum substitution at the fold.

For any fixed p>0 let w_c=L_c^{-p} and, eventually w_c<=1/2, define

    psi_c=sqrt(1-w_c) Omega+sqrt(w_c) chi_c,
    rho_c=|psi_c><psi_c|.                                    (N46)

These are even coherent states with Tr rho_c H_c^p<=B^p uniformly.
Their free/corrected overlap at time one is 1-w_c+w_c z_c. By (N45),

    1-|1-w_c+w_c z_c|^2 >=2w_c(1-w_c)>=w_c.

The trace distance is consequently at least 2 sqrt(w_c). The opposite
bound follows, for example, by comparing both states to the common vacuum
and adding their pure-state distances, giving at most 4 sqrt(w_c). Hence

    alpha_c(1)=Theta(L_c^{-p/2})
              =Theta(c^{a p/(2r)}).                         (N47)

For 0<p<=2r this matches the moment-sensitive exponent in the theorem on
the class with only a uniform p-th initial free moment. In particular the
first-mean exponent cannot be improved uniformly over that state class
just by assuming smoother fixed potentials or smaller BV variations:
this control already sets all potentials to zero. The constants and the
variation dependence in (N31) are not claimed sharp by this example.

For p>2r, a fixed compact pair packet and a fixed nonzero vacuum/pair
coherence have all moments bounded, but at time one give an error of
order c^a. To check the lower bound, conjugate both output states by the
same corrected free unitary. The bounded norm-one imaginary vacuum/pair
coherence observable has an expectation difference proportional to
Im <chi,exp(-i dGamma(e_{L,c}-e_c^[J]))chi>. By (N41), this divided by
c^a tends to minus the strictly positive pair expectation of g_J.
The upper bound follows from the uniform O(c^a) remainder on that compact
support. Thus the linear c^a saturation in (N38) is also tested in actual
even free states. These are controls of uniform state-class exponents;
no lower bound for every prepared state or every fixed protocol follows.
