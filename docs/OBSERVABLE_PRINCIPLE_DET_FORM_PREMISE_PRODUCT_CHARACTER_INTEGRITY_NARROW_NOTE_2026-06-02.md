## 1. The question, stated precisely

The FORM theorem needs a scalar readout `chi : GL(n, C) -> C*` satisfying the
operator-**product** character law `(M)`. Three logically distinct
propositions are in play:

- **(Fac)** *the inserted operator is a product.* The source-deformed Dirac
  operator factorizes `D + J = D . (1 + D^{-1} J)`, so source insertion is the
  operator product `M -> M . S` with `S = 1 + D^{-1} J`. (FORM note eq. `F`,
  §1.1.)
- **(Mul)** *the weight `Z` is multiplicative over independent patches.*
  Stacking an independent source patch on a disjoint region **multiplies** the
  Berezin weight `Z = det(D+J)`, because the Grassmann Gaussian over
  independent patches factorizes. (FORM note §1.1, §7(b).)
- **(M)** *the readout is a product character.* `chi(A . S) = chi(A) . chi(S)`.

The FORM note offers `(Fac)` and `(Mul)` as the motivation for `(M)`. The
integrity question is the implication arrow: **does `(Fac) ∧ (Mul) ⟹ (M)`?**
If yes, the FORM premise is derived and the EXPONENT step is the sole
admission (the #2456 picture would be airtight). If no, `(M)` is a separate
admitted input.

## 2. Load-bearing step (Class A) — the two motivations do not entail `(M)`

The runner reproves the following at exact `sympy` precision on generic
symbolic `2 × 2` and `3 × 3` matrices, **18 PASS / 0 FAIL**. Each fact is
reproven from primitives; the `GL(n)` character classification (Fulton–Harris;
Goodman–Wallach) and the Cauchy classifier enter as comparators only.

### 2.1 `(Fac)` is a true identity, but it constrains the *argument*, not the *readout*

`D + J = D . (1 + D^{-1} J)` is verified exactly for invertible `D` (runner
T1, including a fully generic invertible `2 × 2` `D` via the explicit
adjugate inverse). So `(Fac)` is not in dispute: source insertion really is an
operator product.

But `(Fac)` is a statement **in** `GL(n)` — it says the *operator that is
inserted* is a product. It places **no** constraint on the functional `chi`.
An arbitrary scalar readout can be evaluated on the product operator `D . S`;
in particular `tr(D . S)` is a perfectly well-defined readout (runner T2). The
product structure of the *argument* does **not** make the *readout*
multiplicative: `tr(D . S) ≠ tr(D) . tr(S)` for generic `D, S` (runner T2,
explicit nonzero residual). So `(Fac)` does not imply `(M)`. (`(Fac)` is also
side-symmetric, `D + J = (1 + J D^{-1}) . D`; runner T5 — the product
structure is purely argument-side and carries no preference for `det` over
`tr`.)

### 2.2 `(Mul)` is the direct-sum / tensor axis — a property of `Z`, not of `chi`

"Independent patches multiply `Z`" is the factorization
`Z[J_A ⊕ J_B] = Z[J_A] . Z[J_B]` for the **block-diagonal** (direct-sum)
deformation, equivalently the tensor factorization of the Grassmann Gaussian.
The runner verifies `det(A ⊕ B) = det(A) . det(B)` (runner T3). Two things
follow:

1. **`(Mul)` lives on the `⊕` axis, not the `·` axis.** On the `⊕` axis the
   trace is **additive**: `tr(A ⊕ B) = tr(A) + tr(B)` (runner T3). This is
   exactly the axis on which the prior P1-bridge campaign foreclosed and on
   which `tr` is **not** excluded — the direct-sum axis structurally cannot do
   the det-vs-tr work. The runner records that `tr` **separates** the two
   axes: it passes `⊕`-additivity but fails `·`-multiplicativity (runner T3).
   So "the weight `Z` multiplies over `⊕` patches" is **not** the same
   statement as "the readout is a `·` product character."
2. **`(Mul)` is a property of `Z = det` alone, hence `chi`-independent.** The
   factorization `Z[J_A ⊕ J_B] = Z[J_A] . Z[J_B]` is true of the operator
   weight `Z = det(D+J)` regardless of which scalar readout `chi` one later
   applies. A proposition that holds independently of `chi` can never
   *constrain* `chi`. (Runner T4 encodes this as the model-theoretic gap
   below.)

### 2.3 The gap — a non-character readout respects both motivations yet breaks `(M)`

The decisive reproof. Let

- `P_arg` := "the inserted operator is a product `D . S`" (from `(Fac)`);
- `P_Zmul` := "`Z` is multiplicative over independent `⊕` patches" (from
  `(Mul)`);
- `P_M` := "the readout satisfies `chi(A . S) = chi(A) . chi(S)`" (`(M)`).

The model `chi = tr` satisfies `P_arg` (any operator can be a product; `tr`
reads it fine) and `P_Zmul` (because `P_Zmul` is the factorization of
`Z = det`, true independently of the choice `chi = tr`), but **falsifies**
`P_M` (`tr(A . S) ≠ tr(A) . tr(S)`; runner T4). Therefore

> ```text
> (P_arg ∧ P_Zmul)  does NOT imply  P_M.
> ```

The witness is `tr` — a continuous scalar readout that is *additive* over
independent patches (the natural additive image of `(Mul)`) and *reads
products without trouble* (`(Fac)`), yet is **not** a product character. So
`(M)` is an **extra requirement** imposed on the class of readouts, not a
consequence of the Berezin source-insertion structure. (Runner T4.)

### 2.4 Non-circularity guard

The conclusion "`(M)` is not derived" must not secretly assume the FORM
*conclusion* (det). It does not. Every step above uses only generic symbolic
matrices, `det` **only** as the Berezin weight `Z` (an operator-side fact, the
content of `P_Zmul`), and `tr` as the gap-witness. If the argument had
smuggled in `chi = det`, the witness could not have been `tr` — the very
competitor FORM excludes. The runner re-verifies that the gap-witness is `tr`

T5). The conclusion is therefore reached from something **prior** to choosing
`det`/`tr`, exactly as the discipline requires.

## 3. What this means — `(M)` is a co-admission, distinct from P1

### 3.1 `(M)` and P1 are logically independent (two atoms, not one)

`(M)` constrains the **operator-product (`·`)** composition law; P1 (the
exponent-fixing additivity step) constrains the **direct-sum (`⊕`)**
composition law. The runner shows these are orthogonal across `{tr, det}`

| Readout | `·`-character `(M)` | `⊕`-additivity (the P1-type condition) |
|---|---|---|
| `tr` | **fails** | **passes** |
| `det` (family `|det|^p`) | **passes** | passes (multiplicative); additive member `log` fixed only by P1 |

Knowing `(M)` (it is `det`) tells you nothing about which exponent P1 picks;
knowing P1 (pick the `⊕`-additive member) tells you nothing about det-vs-tr
(`tr` is `⊕`-additive too). The two admissions are on different composition
laws and `tr` separates them. Hence they are **two distinct admitted atoms**.
