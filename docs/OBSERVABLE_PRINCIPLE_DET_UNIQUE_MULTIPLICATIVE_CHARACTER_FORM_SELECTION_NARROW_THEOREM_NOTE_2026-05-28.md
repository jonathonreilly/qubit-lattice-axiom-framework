## 1. The two axes, side by side

There are two distinct ways a source can "compose" in this framework,
and they impose two algebraically distinct requirements on a scalar
readout `F`. Confusing them is the entire source of the prior campaign's
circularity, so they are separated explicitly here.

| Axis | Operation | Requirement on `F` | Does `tr` satisfy it? | Does `det` satisfy it? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct-sum (additive) axis** — *prior campaign* | `A (+) B` (block-diagonal, independent subsystems) | `F(A (+) B) = F(A) + F(B)` | **YES** (`tr(A (+) B) = tr A + tr B`) | only `log\|det\|` does, after Cauchy |
| **Composition (multiplicative) axis** — *this note* | `A . S` (operator product / source insertion) | `chi(A . S) = chi(A) . chi(S)` | **NO** (`tr(A . S) != tr A . tr S`) | **YES** (`det(A . S) = det A . det S`) |

The first row is where the family `{|det|^p : p in R}` lives: every
member is direct-sum-multiplicative (`|det(A (+) B)|^p = |det A|^p .
|det B|^p`) and continuous and CPT-even, and only the `p -> 0`
representative (`log`) is direct-sum-*additive*. Selecting that
representative is the P1 admission; `tr` is **not** excluded on this
axis (it is additive). This is the foreclosed door.

The second row is the new axis. The requirement `(M)` is a property of
the functional `chi` **as a map on `GL(n)`**, before any assumption that
`chi` is a function of `det`. On this axis `tr` is **excluded** — it is
not multiplicative under composition — and the character classification

is the det-vs-tr work the first axis cannot do.

## 2. Load-bearing step (Class A) — `det` is the algebraic/rational multiplicative-character generator

### 2.1 The character property and the abelianization theorem

A **multiplicative character** of `GL(n, C)` is an
algebraic/rational group homomorphism `chi: GL(n, C) -> C*` satisfying
`(M)`. The classification is classical:

> **Theorem (characters of `GL(n)`).** The commutator subgroup of
> `GL(n, C)` is `SL(n, C)`; equivalently, the abelianization
> `GL(n, C) / [GL(n, C), GL(n, C)] = GL(n, C) / SL(n, C)` is isomorphic
> to `C*` via `det`. Consequently every homomorphism
> `chi: GL(n, C) -> C*` to an abelian group factors through `det`,
> i.e. `chi = phi . det`. Inside the algebraic/rational character
> class, the allowed `phi: C* -> C*` are the integer powers
> `z -> z^k`, so `chi = det^k`.

This is standard representation theory. References:

- W. Fulton & J. Harris, *Representation Theory: A First Course*,
  Springer GTM 129 (1991), §IV (the abelianization
  `GL_n / SL_n = GL_1` and characters of the general linear group);
- R. Goodman & N. R. Wallach, *Symmetry, Representations, and
  Invariants*, Springer GTM 255 (2009), §1.5 and §5.5 (the determinant
  as the generator of one-dimensional representations of `GL(n)`);
- the elementary fact that `[GL(n, C), GL(n, C)] = SL(n, C)` for
  `n >= 1` over a field with enough elements (every matrix of
  determinant 1 is a product of commutators / elementary
  transvections), so any homomorphism to an abelian group kills
  `SL(n, C)` and factors through `det`.

The load-bearing consequence for this note is the **selection**: among
algebraic/rational scalar characters on `GL(n, C)`, only `det^k`
survives. The natural spectral-polynomial competitors tested here are
**not** characters and are excluded by `(M)`. The runner verifies those
exclusions one functional at a time at exact symbolic precision (§5).

### 2.2 The excluded functionals (verified symbolically)

For generic symbolic `A, S` (entries independent indeterminates), the
runner confirms:

- **`tr` fails.** `tr(A . S) - tr(A) . tr(S) != 0` and
  `tr(A . S) - (tr(A) + tr(S)) != 0` for `n = 2, 3`. The trace is
  neither multiplicative nor "multiplicative-as-additive" under
  composition. (A concrete numeric witness `A = [[2,1],[1,2]]`,
  `S = [[3,0],[1,4]]` gives `tr(A.S) = 15`, `tr(A) tr(S) = 28`,
  `tr(A) + tr(S) = 11` — all three distinct.)
- **Power traces `tr(M^s)` fail.** `tr((A . S)^s) - tr(A^s) tr(S^s)
  != 0` for `s = 2, 3` and `n = 2, 3`.
- **Elementary symmetric polynomials `e_1, ..., e_{n-1}` fail.**
  For `n = 2`: `e_1 = tr` fails, `e_2 = det` passes. For `n = 3`:
  `e_1, e_2` fail, `e_3 = det` passes. (The runner computes `e_k`
  basis-free from the characteristic polynomial and cross-checks
  `e_n = det`.)
- **Non-character mixtures fail.** `f(M) = tr(M) + det(M)` is not a
  character: `f(A . S) != f(A) f(S)`.

The positive side:

- **`det` passes.** `det(A . S) = det(A) det(S)` for `n = 2, 3`
  (generic symbolic), and for the three-fold composition
  `det(A . S . T) = det(A) det(S) det(T)`.
- **`det^k` passes** for `k = 2, 3`: `det(A . S)^k = det(A)^k det(S)^k`.

So the character axis does the **det-vs-tr** work directly: it excludes
`tr`, `tr(M^s)`, and `e_{1..n-1}` by the character classification, and
leaves `det^k` as the unique survivor in the algebraic/rational
character class.

## 3. Exponent fixing (Class A) — the additive form of the character is `c . log|det|`

The character classification leaves the integer power `k` (equivalently,
the family `{|det|^p}` if one relaxes to real powers / moduli) free. The
*exponent* — not the det-vs-tr choice — is fixed by the **additivity
over independent sectors** requirement, exactly as in the parent note,
and **only** there:

Let `r := |det| > 0`. Additivity over a block-diagonal (independent
two-sector) configuration combined with the multiplicativity of `|det|`
on direct sums gives the functional equation

```text
W(r_1 r_2) = W(r_1) + W(r_2),     r_1, r_2 > 0.                         (C)
```

For continuous `W: (R_+, x) -> (R, +)`, the Cauchy classifier gives the
unique solution `W = c . log r` (the runner solves the differentiated
form `r W'(r) = c` to `W = const + c . log r`, and verifies
`c . log(r_1 r_2) = c . log r_1 + c . log r_2` exactly). The character
power is absorbed into the scale: `log(|det|^k) = k . log|det|`, so a
different `k` is just a different `c`.

**Crucial scope point.** This Cauchy/log step is the *same* step the
prior campaign identified as Pattern L. It is included here **only** to
fix the exponent *within the already-selected `det` family*. It does
**not** perform the det-vs-tr selection — that was done in §2 by the
character property, on a different axis. The honesty test the prior
no-gos demand is: "does this route smuggle the det-vs-tr selection
through the additive-Cauchy step?" The answer here is **no**: the
det-vs-tr selection is complete *before* §3, by the character
classification alone, which never references direct sums. The runner's
Part 7 encodes this as a guard (below).

## 4. Pattern-L guard — the det-vs-tr selection does not use the direct-sum axis

The single most important defense of this note against the

- `tr(A (+) B) = tr(A) + tr(B)` — **`tr` PASSES direct-sum additivity.**
  Therefore the direct-sum axis cannot exclude `tr`; it is **not** the
  axis used for det-vs-tr here.
- `det(A (+) B) = det(A) . det(B)` — det's direct-sum multiplicativity
  is the *old-campaign* axis, where the whole family `{|det|^p}` is
  admissible and the exponent is unfixed. The new content is **not**

- On the **composition** axis, `tr(A . S) != tr(A) . tr(S)` while
  `det(A . S) = det(A) . det(S)`. This is the axis that separates `det`
  from `tr`, and it is logically independent of direct-sum additivity.

The combination — `tr` *fails* the composition-character property yet
*passes* direct-sum additivity — is precisely what shows the two axes
carry different content. The det-vs-tr selection rides on the
composition-character axis only.
