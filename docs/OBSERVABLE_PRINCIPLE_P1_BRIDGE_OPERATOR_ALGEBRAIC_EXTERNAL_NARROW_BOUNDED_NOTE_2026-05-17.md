## 1. Claim scope

This note records four standard operator-algebraic results plus an
explicit counterexample family, then exhibits the structural obstruction
to closing P1 positively via operator-algebraic forcing.

**(O1) Hilbert tensor product factorization.** Given independent
subsystems on a finite block `Λ = Λ_A ⊔ Λ_B` with no hopping bonds
across the partition, the substrate gives an isomorphism
`H_Λ ≃ H_{Λ_A} ⊗ H_{Λ_B}` on the per-site `Cl(3)` Fock module
tensored across sites. This is standard substrate substructure.

**(O2) Grassmann algebra factorization.** For `D = D_A ⊕ D_B` and
`J = J_A ⊕ J_B`, the Grassmann partition factorizes:
`Z[J_A ⊕ J_B] = det(D_A + J_A) · det(D_B + J_B) = Z_A[J_A] · Z_B[J_B]`.
This is elementary block-diagonal determinant factorization.

**(O3) Trace-state factorization on a von Neumann tensor product.** For
a type II_1 factor written as a von Neumann tensor product `M = M_1
⊗̄ M_2`, the unique-up-to-scale normalized trace `τ_M` factorizes:
`τ_M(A_1 ⊗ A_2) = τ_{M_1}(A_1) · τ_{M_2}(A_2)`. Uniqueness of the trace
up to scale on the hyperfinite II_1 factor is Connes 1976 / Connes-
Stormer 1973.

**(O4) Cluster decomposition (conditional).** For a gapped Hamiltonian
on `Cl(3) ⊗ Z^3`, vacuum-expectation connected correlators of local
operators at large spatial separation factorize. This is the standard
content of Lieb-Robinson + mass gap (Hastings-Koma 2006); the on-frame
context is the `retained_bounded`
`AXIOM_FIRST_CLUSTER_DECOMPOSITION_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-04-29.md` row.
That bounded row is context for correlator factorization, not a positive
retained primitive deriving scalar-generator additivity.

**(C5) Counterexample family — non-additive scalar functionals
compatible with multiplicative `Z`.** For any real `p ≠ 0`, the
candidate scalar functional

```text
F_p[J]  :=  r(J)^p     where r(J) := |det(D + J)| / |det D|,             (C5)
```

is a continuous CPT-even scalar functional on `|Z|` (real-valued,
defined on the same open neighborhood `U_0` of `J = 0` as the cited
admissibility class) and satisfies the **multiplicative factorization**

```text
F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B]  =  r_A(J_A)^p · r_B(J_B)^p  =  F_p[J_A] · F_p[J_B],      (M5)
```

inherited directly from `r(J_A ⊕ J_B) = r_A(J_A) · r_B(J_B)` (which
follows from (O2) plus absolute value). The functional `F_p` is
**not additive** in the block-additive sense: there exist explicit
witness pairs `(r_A, r_B)` with `r_A ≠ 1`, `r_B ≠ 1`, `p ≠ 0` for
which `F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B] ≠ F_p[J_A] + F_p[J_B]`. (See (P3) below for the
existential statement, the explicit coincidence locus where the
multiplicative-vs-additive defect happens to vanish, and the
rational witness pairs used by the runner.)

(C5) is the explicit class of candidate scalar functionals that are
compatible with operator-algebraic factorization (O1)-(O4) but lie
**outside** the additive admissibility class described in
`OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_REAL_D_BLOCK_UNIQUENESS_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10.md`.

## 2. Structural finding — P1 is **not** retired by operator-algebraic forcing

**(F1) Multiplicative factorization does not imply additive
classification.** Operator-algebraic primitives (O1)-(O4) yield
**multiplicative** factorization on `Z[J]` and on the trace state
`τ`. They do not by themselves yield **additive** factorization on a
scalar generator `W[J]`. The step from `Z[J_A ⊕ J_B] = Z_A · Z_B` to
`W[J_A ⊕ J_B] = W_A + W_B` requires choosing `W = c log|Z|` from the
wider class of continuous scalar functionals on `r(J)`. The choice
`W = c log|Z|` is the block-additive admissibility criterion of
the narrow uniqueness theorem cited above. The (C5)
counterexample family `F_p = r^p` for `p != 0`, together with the
separate logarithmic representative `log r`, is the explicit
witness that operator-algebraic factorization is **compatible** with
non-additive scalar functionals: alternative `p ≠ 0` candidates all
factor multiplicatively but not additively.

**(F2) Trace-state uniqueness does not produce additivity.** Connes-
Stormer 1973 / Connes 1976 establish that the trace state on the
hyperfinite II_1 factor is unique up to scale (within the class of
**normal faithful trace states**). They do not establish that the
"physical scalar bosonic generator" must lie in the additive class.
The identification "physical scalar generator = `-log τ`" (or
equivalently "free energy is the canonical extensive observable") is
the conventional definition of statistical mechanical scalar observable
generators; it is the **same** identification choice as P1 of the
parent `OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_FROM_AXIOM_NOTE.md`,
expressed in operator-algebraic vocabulary.

**(F3) Cluster decomposition (when retained) gives correlator
factorization, not generator additivity.** The cluster decomposition
theorem (Weinberg QFT §4.4; Haag *Local Quantum Physics* §V.4) states
that connected vacuum correlators of products of local field operators
factorize at large spatial separation. This is a property of expectation
values of products, not of scalar generators. Going from "correlators
factorize" to "the scalar generator is additive" still requires
defining the scalar generator via `log Z` — which packages additivity
into the definition by way of the `log`. Furthermore, on the live
ledger, `axiom_first_cluster_decomposition_theorem_note_2026-04-29` is
`retained_bounded`, not a positive closure primitive for P1. The
operator-algebraic attempt therefore cannot consume cluster decomposition as
a retained positive theorem deriving scalar-generator additivity even if the
additivity-from-factorization implication held.

**(F4) Reeh-Schlieder cyclicity is about density, not about generator
class.** The Reeh-Schlieder theorem (Reeh-Schlieder 1961, Streater-
Wightman 1964) states that the vacuum is cyclic-separating for the
local algebra of any open region. This provides the Tomita-Takesaki
modular structure on `A(O)` with respect to the vacuum, but it does
not constrain which scalar functionals of `|Z|` are "physical".
Furthermore,
`axiom_first_reeh_schlieder_theorem_note_2026-05-01` is `unaudited` on
the live ledger.

**(F5) Cross-discipline classification theorems do not derive
additivity.** Every cross-discipline additive functional accessible
from operator algebras (Connes index, Atiyah-Singer index, Euler
characteristic, K-theory direct-sum-of-modules, log Tr, von Neumann
entropy, modular entropy) is additive **by definition**: additivity is
baked into the formula via either a logarithm, an alternating sum of
dimensions, or a direct-sum-of-modules construction. None of them
**derive** additivity from a more primitive multiplicative or
tensor-product structure. The classification theorems on these
additive functionals (e.g., Chern character as ring map `K → H*`,
Connes-Stormer trace uniqueness) classify members of an
**already-additive** class.

**Conclusion of §2.** The operator-algebraic attempt reproduces — with
operator-algebraic vocabulary substituted for the Shannon/Khinchin
attempt's information-theoretic vocabulary — the same structural
admission as the Shannon/Khinchin attempt (the
`observable_principle_p1_bridge_shannon_khinchin_external_narrow_bounded_note_2026-05-17`
parallel attempt; context-only reference, not a load-bearing dependency
of this note).
Operator-algebraic primitives do not retire P1; they recast it. The
sharpened obstruction is that the explicit counterexample family
(C5) `F_p = r^p` exhibits non-additive continuous CPT-even scalar
functionals on `|Z|` that satisfy multiplicative factorization but
fail block-additivity.

## 5. What this note states (positive content at sharpened bounded
support level)

**(P1) Multiplicative factorization of `r(J)` on independent
subsystems.** For `D = D_A ⊕ D_B` and `J = J_A ⊕ J_B` with no
hopping bonds across the `A | B` partition,

```text
r(J_A ⊕ J_B)  =  r_A(J_A) · r_B(J_B),                                    (P1)
```

with `r(J) := |det(D + J)| / |det D|`. This is elementary linear
algebra (block-diagonal determinant factorization plus the absolute
value commuting with the product).

**(P2) Multiplicative factorization of `F_p`.** For any real `p` and
the candidate functional `F_p[J] := r(J)^p`, multiplicative
factorization holds:

```text
F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B]  =  F_p[J_A] · F_p[J_B].                                  (P2)
```

This is immediate from (P1) and the algebraic identity
`(ab)^p = a^p · b^p` for `a, b > 0`.

**(P3) Failure of additivity for `F_p` with `p ≠ 0` (existential
counterexample).** For any `p ≠ 0`, there exist witness pairs
`r_A, r_B > 0` for which

```text
F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B]  =  r_A^p · r_B^p   ≠   r_A^p + r_B^p  =  F_p[J_A] + F_p[J_B].   (P3)
```

That is, the multiplicative-vs-additive defect
`F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B] − (F_p[J_A] + F_p[J_B]) = r_A^p · r_B^p − r_A^p − r_B^p`
is nonzero on an open dense subset of `(r_A, r_B) ∈ R_+ × R_+`; the
trivial coincidence locus where the defect vanishes is the codimension-1
algebraic curve `r_A^p · r_B^p = r_A^p + r_B^p` (parametrized for
`p ≠ 0` by `(r_A^p, r_B^p) = (s, s/(s−1))` for `s > 1`, equivalent to
`(2, 2)` at the symmetric point and tracing a hyperbola in the
`r^p`-plane). Exhibiting any witness pair off this curve — for example
`(r_A, r_B) = (2, 3)` with `p = 1`, giving `6 ≠ 5` — suffices for the
existential conclusion that `F_p` is not globally additive. The runner
verifies (P3) at exact `Fraction` / `sympy` precision on a set of
explicit rational witness pairs `(p, r_A, r_B)`, none of which lie on
the coincidence curve. The universal "every pair off the trivial
single-point fixings `r_A = 1` or `r_B = 1`" version of (P3) is **not**
claimed here — the coincidence curve is exactly the additional locus
the auditor's verdict flagged, and the existential framing is what is
load-bearing for non-additivity.

**(P4) Additivity for the unique log-class representative `F_0 := log r`.**
The functional

```text
F_0[J]  :=  log r(J)  =  log|det(D + J)|  −  log|det D|                  (F_0)
```

is the **unique** (up to overall scale `c`) continuous additive
representative on the multiplicative class `r ↦ r_A · r_B`, by
Cauchy's multiplicative-to-additive functional equation theorem
(Cauchy 1821, Erdős 1946). This identifies `F_0 = W` of the cited
narrow uniqueness theorem.

**(P5) The classification step is Cauchy; the selection step is additivity.**
Cauchy's theorem classifies the additive class within continuous
functionals on `R_+`. The additivity admissibility criterion of class
(X2) in
`OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_REAL_D_BLOCK_UNIQUENESS_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10.md`
selects the additive class. The (C5) counterexamples lie **outside**
that additive class by construction. Operator-algebraic primitives (O1)-(O4)
constrain the class of compatible functionals to those that factor
multiplicatively in `r`; they do not constrain to the additive class
within that set. The Cauchy classifier is a converse-direction
theorem on the already-additive subclass.

**(b) Cluster decomposition theorem.** Cluster decomposition gives

`axiom_first_cluster_decomposition_theorem_note_2026-04-29` is
`retained_bounded`; it supplies bounded correlator-factorization context, not

**(c) Operator-algebraic free energy / type II_1 trace state.**
Connes-Stormer 1973 trace uniqueness on the hyperfinite II_1 factor

`M_1 ⊗̄ M_2` factorizes multiplicatively. The identification
