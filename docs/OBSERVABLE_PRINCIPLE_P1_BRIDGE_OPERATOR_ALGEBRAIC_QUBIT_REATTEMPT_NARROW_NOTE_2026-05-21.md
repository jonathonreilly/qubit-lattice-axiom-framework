### Exercise 2 — Elon first-principles

Strip to first principles:

**Why might the qubit-per-site structure matter?** Hypothesis: in a
general C* algebra, "independent subsystems" need not tensor-factor —
they only need to commute. In a UHF tensor product
`⊗_{x ∈ Λ} M_2(ℂ)`, "independent" forces a strong structural
property: independent sub-registers `A, B ⊂ Λ` (disjoint) give
commuting subalgebras `A_A := ⊗_{x ∈ A} M_2(ℂ)` and
`A_B := ⊗_{x ∈ B} M_2(ℂ)` with `A_A · A_B ≅ ⊗_{x ∈ A ∪ B} M_2(ℂ)
= A_{A ∪ B}`. This is the UHF tensor-factorization property: the
"independent" notion forces a literal tensor product rather than only
a commuting pair.

**Does this rigidity force additivity on the scalar generator?** No.
The rigidity is on the **operator algebra side** — it forces a tensor
product factorization. The scalar generator is a real-valued functional
on the partition function `Z[J] = det(D + J)`. Once the operator side
factorizes (`det(D_A ⊕ D_B + J_A ⊕ J_B) = det(D_A+J_A) · det(D_B+J_B)`),
the scalar side **multiplicatively** factorizes:
`r(J) = r_A(J_A) · r_B(J_B)`. From there, **selecting** the additive
representative `W = log r` from the multiplicatively-closed class of
continuous functionals on `r > 0` requires the Cauchy classifier on the
**additive** subclass — i.e., requires the P1 selection premise as
input.

**The structural distinction the qubit constraint does NOT change:**
multiplicative factorization on the algebra side is **necessary** for
the scalar generator to be a function of `r = |Z|` and be
multiplicatively closed under independent subsystems; it is **not
sufficient** to force the additive log representative over the
non-additive `r^p` representatives. The Cauchy classifier is a converse
on the additive subclass, not a derivation of additivity from
multiplicative closure.

**First-principles bottom line:** the qubit reframe is a constraint on
the per-site operator algebra. P1 is a selection criterion on
scalar real-valued functionals of the partition function. These live in
different algebraic layers; tightening the first does not tighten the
second.

3. **B. Blackadar**, *Operator Algebras: Theory of C*-Algebras and von

   C*-algebras and their tensor-product factorization on disjoint
   subsystems. The tracial state on `M_2(ℂ)^⊗(A ∪ B)` factorizes as
   `τ_{A ∪ B}(a_A ⊗ a_B) = τ_A(a_A) · τ_B(a_B)` for disjoint `A, B`;

The Glimm-Powers-Blackadar trio gives the qubit-reframe's UHF

### Exercise 4 — Math search (Tao-style)

**Posed question.** Let `A_Λ := M_2(ℂ)^⊗Λ` be the finite-region UHF
algebra on a finite site set `Λ ⊂ Z^3`. Let `τ_Λ := 2^{-|Λ|} Tr`
be the unique normalized tracial state. Let `D ∈ A_Λ` be a
self-adjoint operator (lattice Dirac block) and `J ∈ A_Λ` a source
perturbation. Is `log|det(D + J)|` additive under
`D = D_A ⊕ D_B`, `J = J_A ⊕ J_B` with `D_α, J_α ∈ A_α` for disjoint
sub-registers `α ∈ {A, B}` and `Λ = A ⊔ B`?

**Math answer.** Yes, **trivially**, by elementary linear algebra:
`det(D + J) = det(D_A + J_A) · det(D_B + J_B)` (block-diagonal
determinant identity), so `|det(D + J)| = |det(D_A + J_A)| · |det(D_B
+ J_B)|`, hence `log|det(D + J)| = log|det(D_A + J_A)| + log|det(D_B
+ J_B)|`. The qubit-reframe constraint provides this automatically
via UHF tensor structure, but the conclusion is identical to the
prior C* setting and does **not** depend on the UHF refinement.

**The non-trivial question** — and the actual P1 blocker — is the
classification of which real-valued scalar functionals of
`r = |det(D + J)|` are "physical scalar bosonic observable generators":
the additive subclass `c log r + const`, or some non-additive class
`r^p`? The math here is **converse**: for any continuous functional
`f : ℝ_+ → ℝ` satisfying `f(r_A r_B) = f(r_A) + f(r_B)`, Cauchy's
classifier gives `f(r) = c log r + const` uniquely. **Additivity is
the hypothesis**; the log form is the output. The math does **not**
derive additivity from multiplicative closure plus regularity; it
characterizes the additive class within the multiplicatively-closed
class.

**Conclusion of math search.** The qubit-reframe constraint is a
sharpening on the **algebra side** that yields multiplicative
factorization automatically. The P1 blocker is on the
**scalar-functional-classification side**, where the qubit refinement
adds no new constraint. The math answer to "does this re-attempt
close P1?" is **no**, with the structurally rigorous reason being
that the load-bearing identification (W = additive functional) is at a
layer the qubit constraint doesn't touch.

`r(J) := |det(D + J)| / |det D| ∈ ℝ_+` for `J` in the local
neighborhood where `det(D + J) ≠ 0`.

For disjoint sub-registers `A, B ⊂ Λ` with `Λ = A ⊔ B`, the UHF
tensor factorization gives:

```text
A_Λ  ≅  A_A ⊗ A_B,    where A_α := ⊗_{x ∈ α} M_2(ℂ),     α ∈ {A, B}.    (UHF-fact)
```

For block-diagonal `D = D_A ⊕ D_B`, `J = J_A ⊕ J_B` with `D_α, J_α
∈ A_α`, the determinant factorizes:

```text
det(D + J)  =  det(D_A + J_A)  ·  det(D_B + J_B),                       (det-fact)
```

and therefore

```text
r(J_A ⊕ J_B)  =  r_A(J_A)  ·  r_B(J_B).                                (r-mult)
```

This is **multiplicative** factorization, automatic from elementary
linear algebra and the UHF tensor structure. The (P1) selection
criterion seeks an **additive** scalar generator
`W[J] = W_A[J_A] + W_B[J_B]` on the same independent-subsystem
