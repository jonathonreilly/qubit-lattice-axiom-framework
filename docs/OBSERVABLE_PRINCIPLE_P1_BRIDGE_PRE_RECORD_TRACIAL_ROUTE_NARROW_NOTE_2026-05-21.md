## 0. Honest framing up front

This note records an attempt to close the P1 admitted premise of
`OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_FROM_AXIOM_NOTE.md` via the **pre-record tracial
route**: identify the framework's scalar observable generator `W` with a
tracial moment of the source-deformed object on the qubit-register
algebra `A = ⊗_{x ∈ Z^3} M_2(ℂ)`, then exploit the tracial factorization
`τ(A_A ⊗ A_B) = τ(A_A) · τ(A_B)` on tensor-decomposed regions to force
generator additivity.

The route **fails to close P1 positively** for two independent
structural reasons:

1. **The tracial state factorizes multiplicatively, not additively.**
   For `ρ = ρ_A ⊗ ρ_B` on disjoint regions `Λ_A ⊔ Λ_B`,
   `Tr(ρ) = Tr(ρ_A) · Tr(ρ_B)`. To go from this multiplicative
   factorization to **additive** generator factorization
   `W[J_A ⊕ J_B] = W[J_A] + W[J_B]` requires selecting the
   logarithmic representative `W = c log Tr(ρ)` from the wider class
   `{(Tr(ρ))^p : p ∈ R}`. The selection step is the same admitted
   classification choice as P1 (Cauchy classifier among continuous
   group homomorphisms `(R_+, *) -> (R, +)`); the tracial-state
   framing relabels P1 in operator-algebraic vocabulary but does not
   derive it. The counterexample family
   `F_p[J] = r(J)^p` (`p ≠ 0`,
   `r(J) = |det(D+J)| / |det D|`) is multiplicatively compatible with
   any tensor-factorizing tracial state for every real `p`.

2. **The qubit-register tracial state and the Grassmann partition
   function `Z[J] = det(D+J)` live on different operator algebras.**
   The qubit-register algebra `A = ⊗_x M_2(ℂ)` is the bosonic / spin
   operator algebra; the staggered-Dirac partition function arises
   from the finite Grassmann integral over fermionic generators on a
   different (open-gate) lattice fermion realization. Identifying
   `|det(D+J)|` with a tracial moment of an operator on the qubit
   register would require an explicit Jordan-Wigner-like bridge from
   Grassmann generators to qubit operators on this `Z^3` lattice; the
   staggered-Dirac realization gate is open per
   [`MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md`](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md)
   ("Staggered-Dirac realization derivation target (formerly A3) —
   open gate"). Without that bridge, the tracial property of the
   qubit register imposes no constraint at all on functionals of `Z`.

### Exercise 2 — Elon Musk first-principles

Strip the question to first principles. What is "scalar additivity on
independent subsystems" really asking?

The physical content of P1: when two physical systems A and B are
**physically uncoupled** (no shared degrees of freedom, no interaction
across the partition), a physical extensive scalar observable
generator `W` of the joint system should equal the sum
`W_A + W_B` of the individual generators. That is the operational
meaning of "extensive scalar observable": doubling the system size
doubles `W`.

Block-diagonality of `D` physically corresponds to "no fermion hopping
across the partition" (zero-coupling Hamiltonian / kinetic operator
between the regions). Under that condition, the joint Grassmann
integral factorizes into independent regional Grassmann integrals;
this gives multiplicative `Z[J_A ⊕ J_B] = Z_A · Z_B`. The
multiplicative factorization is elementary: it is the determinant of a
block-diagonal matrix, full stop.

The deep structural question is: **why must the physical scalar
observable generator be in the additive class, rather than the
multiplicative class?** Both `log|Z|` (additive) and `|Z|^p` (`p ≠ 0`,
multiplicative-only) are continuous CPT-even scalar functionals on
`|Z|` that respect block-diagonal `D`. The selection of additive over
multiplicative is what P1 admits.

"Independent" is being used here in BOTH the C*-algebra sense
(commuting subalgebras `A_A` and `A_B` acting on disjoint tensor
factors) AND the probabilistic sense (factorizable joint state
`ρ = ρ_A ⊗ ρ_B` on the qubit register). For the Grassmann partition
function it is also used in a third sense (block-diagonal `D`, no
hopping across the partition). All three senses give MULTIPLICATIVE
factorization on the respective object. None of them directly gives
ADDITIVE factorization on a derived scalar generator without an
additional class-selection step.

The deep reason additive vs. multiplicative cannot be settled by
factorization alone: factorization is a multiplicative-monoid
homomorphism property; additivity is an abelian-group homomorphism
property; the two are related only via `log: (R_+, *) -> (R, +)`,
which is precisely the Cauchy classifier. Selecting `log` from
`{(.)^p}` is the same as selecting additive from multiplicative. Any
route that produces multiplicative factorization (operator-algebraic,
tracial, free Grassmann, Hilbert tensor product, ... ) reaches the
same fork; the fork is P1.

### Exercise 3 — Literature search

Standard operator-algebra and quantum-information references on
tracial states and tensor-product factorization:

1. **Murray–von Neumann (1936–1943).** "On rings of operators."
   *Ann. of Math.* 37 (1936), 116–229; 41 (1940), 208–248; 44
   (1943), 716–808. Establishes the classification of factors and
   the existence/uniqueness of the trace on type II_1 factors. The
   trace factorizes on von Neumann tensor products of II_1 factors:
   `τ_{M_1 ⊗̄ M_2}(A_1 ⊗ A_2) = τ_{M_1}(A_1) · τ_{M_2}(A_2)`.
   **Multiplicative** factorization is the explicit content. No
   additive generator selection.

2. **Connes (1976).** "Classification of injective factors."
   *Ann. of Math.* 104, 73–115. Establishes uniqueness of the
   hyperfinite II_1 factor and the unique normalized trace on it.
   Same multiplicative factorization on tensor products. Same
   absence of additive scalar generator selection.

3. **Tomita (1967) / Takesaki (1970).** "Modular Hilbert algebras
   and a commutation theorem"; "Tomita's theory of modular Hilbert
   algebras." Standard reference for tracial state factorization on
   tensor products of finite-dim matrix algebras. For `A = M_n(ℂ)`,
   `B = M_m(ℂ)`, the trace `τ_{A ⊗ B} = τ_A ⊗ τ_B` is forced for
   simple finite-dim factors. Multiplicative factorization on
   `Tr(ρ_A ⊗ ρ_B) = Tr(ρ_A) Tr(ρ_B)` is standard.

4. **Powers (1967).** "Representations of uniformly hyperfinite
   algebras and their associated von Neumann rings." *Ann. of Math.*
   86, 138–171. Classification of UHF C*-algebras and the unique
   tracial state on UHF type `2^∞`. The framework's qubit-register
   tracial state is the canonical example of this construction.
   The result gives uniqueness of the tracial state but does
   **not** identify the additive scalar generator class.

5. **von Neumann (1932).** "Mathematische Grundlagen der
   Quantenmechanik." First introduction of the operator-algebraic
   formulation of quantum mechanics. Already records the
   multiplicative factorization of tensor-product traces and the
   identification of the entropy functional `S(ρ) = -Tr(ρ log ρ)`
   as the additive class representative. Note that von Neumann's
   own additive entropy choice was a **selection** matching
   classical Boltzmann/Gibbs intuition, not derived from tracial
   factorization.

6. **Petz (2008).** "Quantum Information Theory and Quantum
   Statistics." Springer. Modern textbook treatment of tracial
   states, tensor-product factorization, and the additivity
   property of von Neumann entropy. Explicitly notes that the
   "additivity of entropy on independent subsystems" is the
   defining property used to select `S = -Tr(ρ log ρ)` from the
   wider class of trace functionals; it is not derived from
   tracial-state factorization alone.

The collective finding: **standard operator-algebra literature on
tracial states gives multiplicative tensor factorization
`τ(A ⊗ B) = τ(A) · τ(B)`; the move to an additive scalar generator

### Exercise 4 — Math search (Tao-style bare problem)

**Bare problem.** Given the partition function `Z[J] = det(D + J)` for
a finite-dim self-adjoint operator `D` with source `J` on a
finite-dim Hilbert space `H`, and a decomposition
`H = H_A ⊕ H_B` with `D` respecting the decomposition
(`D = D_A ⊕ D_B`, `J = J_A ⊕ J_B`), is it always true that
`log|Z[J]| = log|Z_A[J_A]| + log|Z_B[J_B]|`?

**Answer: yes**, by elementary block-diagonal determinant identity:

```text
det(D_A ⊕ D_B + J_A ⊕ J_B)
  = det((D_A + J_A) ⊕ (D_B + J_B))
  = det(D_A + J_A) · det(D_B + J_B)                                    (*)
```

(equation `(*)` is `det(M ⊕ N) = det(M) · det(N)` for any
finite-dim block-diagonal pair; standard linear algebra, proven by
expanding the determinant along the block diagonal). Taking the
absolute value and then `log`:

```text
log|Z[J]| = log|det(D_A + J_A) · det(D_B + J_B)|
         = log|det(D_A + J_A)| + log|det(D_B + J_B)|
         = log|Z_A[J_A]| + log|Z_B[J_B]|.                              (**)
```

**The math closes by elementary linear algebra.** No tracial state, no
Connes-Stormer uniqueness, no operator-algebraic Tomita machinery is
needed.

**The remaining question** is whether `log|Z|` is the framework's
"physical scalar bosonic observable generator". That identification
is the same admitted classification choice as P1: of the
multiplicative-factorization class `{|Z|^p : p ∈ R}`, only `p ≈ 0`
(the logarithmic limit) gives an additive generator. The selection of
`log|Z|` is what P1 admits; the elementary linear-algebra identity
`(*)` is what makes `log|Z|` additive **given that selection**.

The tracial-state framing of the qubit register adds nothing to this
analysis. The tracial state factorizes multiplicatively on tensor
products (which is a separate statement on a separate algebra); it
does not exclude `F_p = r^p` for `p ≠ 0` as a candidate scalar
generator on `|Z|`. The `F_p` counterexample family is
multiplicatively-factorizing on `|Z|` for every real `p`; only the
`p → 0` (logarithmic) limit is additive.

**Math search conclusion.** The bare math question closes; the
remaining open content is the physical-selection question (additive
vs. multiplicative scalar generator class), and that is P1.

## 4. What this closes / what remains admitted

### What this closes

This note records the following sharpened bounded content:

1. **Elementary determinant factorization is the actual load-bearing
   step.** Multiplicative factorization on `|Z|` under block-diagonal
   `D` follows from `det(M ⊕ N) = det(M) · det(N)`, elementary linear
   algebra. The tracial-state framing of the qubit register adds no
   independent constraint not already present in this elementary
   identity.

2. **Tracial state factorization is multiplicative, not additive.**
   The qubit-register tracial state factorizes
   `τ(A_A ⊗ A_B) = τ(A_A) · τ(A_B)` on tensor-decomposed regions; this
   is multiplicative. To produce an **additive** scalar generator,
   the logarithmic representative `W = c log τ(M)` must be selected
   from the multiplicative class `{(τ(M))^p}`, and that selection is
   the same admitted classification choice as P1.

3. **The qubit-register algebra and the Grassmann partition function
   live on different algebras without a ratified bridge.** The
   staggered-Dirac realization gate
   (`MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md` §"open gates") would need to close
   before any direct identification of `|det(D + J)|` with a qubit-
   register tracial moment can be made load-bearing. Until that gate
   closes, the tracial-state framing of P1 is, at best, a parallel
   construction on a different algebra.

4. **The pre-record tracial route reaches the same `F_p` obstruction
   as the operator-algebraic external Route A.** The counterexample
   family `F_p[J] = r(J)^p` for `p ∈ R \ {0}` is multiplicatively
   factorizing on `|Z|` and on any tracial moment of the qubit

   Selecting `p ≈ 0` from the family is the same Cauchy-classifier
