## 1. The Doplicher-Roberts reconstruction theorem (verbatim)

### 1.1 Statement (DR 1989, 1990)

From S. Doplicher, J. E. Roberts, *A new duality theory for compact
groups*, Inventiones Math. 98 (1989), 157-218, Theorem 6.1; and
S. Doplicher, J. E. Roberts, *Why there is a field algebra with a
compact gauge group describing the superselection structure in particle
physics*, Commun. Math. Phys. 131 (1990), 51-107:

> **Theorem (Doplicher-Roberts reconstruction).** Let `C` be a strict
> symmetric tensor C*-category with simple tensor unit (i.e.,
> `End(1) = C`), conjugates (every object has a dual), direct sums,
> subobjects (idempotents split), and with all objects having
> finite-dimensional spaces of morphisms. Then there exists a compact
> group `G`, unique up to isomorphism, and an equivalence of strict
> symmetric tensor C*-categories `C ≅ Rep_f(G)` where `Rep_f(G)` is the
> category of finite-dim continuous unitary representations of `G`. The
> compact group `G` is recovered as the group of (unitary) tensor
> natural automorphisms of the fiber functor `F: C → Hilb_f` evaluating
> the dimension functor.

The DR theorem axiomatizes Tannaka-Krein duality: any STC*-category
satisfying the listed premises is equivalent to `Rep_f(G)` for a
**unique** compact group `G`. The Tannaka-Krein duality statement
(Tannaka 1939, Krein 1949, Saavedra Rivano 1972, Deligne 1990) is the
classical case for already-given group `G`; DR is the converse:
reconstruction of `G` from the abstract category.

### 1.2 The DR premises (explicit enumeration)

For the DR reconstruction theorem to apply to a category `C`, the
following premises must hold:

- **(DR-P1) Conjugates.** Every object `X` has a dual `X̄` with
  unit / counit (co/evaluation) maps satisfying the snake / triangle
  identities of a (left-)rigid structure.
- **(DR-P2) Direct sums.** Binary direct sums `X ⊕ Y` exist in `C`.
- **(DR-P3) Subobjects.** Every idempotent `e: X → X` with `e² = e`
  splits, i.e., `X` is the direct sum of two summands corresponding to
  `e` and `1-e` (Cauchy completeness).
- **(DR-P4) Simple tensor unit.** `End(1) = C` (the endomorphism algebra
  of the tensor unit is the complex numbers).
- **(DR-P5) Symmetric braiding.** `C` is equipped with a symmetric
  braiding `c_{X,Y}: X ⊗ Y → Y ⊗ X` satisfying `c_{Y,X} ∘ c_{X,Y} = id`.
  This is the "Bose statistics only" symmetric case; the asymmetric
  (super-symmetric / fermionic) case requires the DR supersymmetric
  extension which reconstructs a Z_2-graded compact (super-)group
  `G x Z_2^F` rather than a plain compact `G`.
- **(DR-P6) C*-structure.** Morphism spaces are Banach spaces over `C`
  with `*`-involution, and the C*-norm is compatible with tensor
  composition.
- **(DR-P7) Finite-dimensional Hom spaces.** Each `Hom(X, Y)` is a
  finite-dim complex vector space.

The reconstructed group `G` is then the **compact group of unitary
tensor natural automorphisms of the canonical fiber functor**, by
classical Tannaka-Krein.

For Z_2-graded (fermionic) target categories, the DR
super-reconstruction (cf. Doplicher-Roberts 1989 §4; Mueger 2000;
Sutherland 1999) reconstructs a **super-compact group** `G_{super} =
G x Z_2^F`, where `Z_2^F` is the parity automorphism and `G` is the
"bosonic" gauge group acting on the even-graded subcategory. The
parity Z_2 component is the fermion-parity superselection.

## 2. The framework's staggered superselection structure

### 2.1 What "superselection sectors" the framework has

The framework substrate (per-site Cl(3) Fock module + Z^3 sites +
Wilson plaquette + compact SU(3)) carries the following candidate
superselection structure recorded in the on-frame retained / unaudited
notes:

- **(S1) Fermion-parity superselection (`Z_2^F`).** Recorded in
  `FERMION_PARITY_Z2_GRADING_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-02.md`
  (current live ledger status: `unaudited`). The fermion-parity
  operator `F = (-1)^{Q_total}` gives `H = H_even ⊕ H_odd` and selects
  Z_2-even local operators as physical observables. This is a
  Bose-Fermi (Z_2-graded) selection rule on the local algebra; in DR
  language it is the parity grading of the field algebra into its even
  (bosonic-observable) and odd (fermion-creation) parts.
- **(S2) SU(3) color (compact Lie) gauge symmetry.** The framework's
  Wilson plaquette action enforces a local compact SU(3) gauge
  symmetry. By gauge-fixing on the local algebra one obtains the
  SU(3)-invariant subalgebra; sectors are labeled by SU(3)
  representations (3, 3̄, 8, 6, ...). This is a compact-Lie gauge
  structure of the standard Yang-Mills-on-lattice type, but is **not**
  itself the framework's selection rule for "physical scalar bosonic
  observable generator"; it is an internal symmetry of the field
  algebra.
- **(S3) U(1) fermion-number / charge superselection.** The framework's
  Noether structure (`AXIOM_FIRST_LATTICE_NOETHER_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-04-29`)
  produces a conserved `Q_total` integer charge; the corresponding U(1)
  charge superselection (DHR 1971) is the integer grading by total
  fermion number `Q_total`. Locally this acts as `e^{i α Q_total}` on
  the field algebra and selects the `Q_total`-invariant subalgebra
  (charge-neutral operators) as the physical observable algebra in
  vacuum sectors.

The framework's superselection structure is therefore the standard
Bose-Fermi + U(1) charge + SU(3) gauge selection of a staggered
lattice fermion field theory:

```text
        G_full  =  Z_2^F  x  U(1)_Q  x  SU(3)_color                     (S0)
```

The corresponding observable algebra is the `G_full`-invariant
subalgebra of the field algebra.

### 2.2 DR premises check on the framework

We check the DR premises (DR-P1)-(DR-P7) on the framework's
superselection category `C_F` (the category of finite-dim continuous
unitary representations of `G_full` realized on local Hilbert
subspaces):

- **(DR-P1) Conjugates.** Every irreducible Rep(`G_full`) has a dual
  (complex conjugate representation); finite-dim unitary
  representations of a compact group always have duals. **Satisfied.**
- **(DR-P2) Direct sums.** Standard for unitary representation
  categories. **Satisfied.**
- **(DR-P3) Subobjects.** Every idempotent in `Rep(G_full)` splits
  (Cauchy completion). **Satisfied.**
- **(DR-P4) Simple tensor unit.** The trivial representation has
  `End(1_triv) = C` exactly. **Satisfied.**
- **(DR-P5) Symmetric braiding.** Here is the substantive issue.
  `Rep(G_full)` for `G_full` containing `Z_2^F` is **not** purely
  symmetric: the `Z_2^F` parity grading makes the natural braiding on
  the fermionic / odd-graded objects **anti-symmetric**
  (the Koszul rule `v ⊗ w = -w ⊗ v` for odd v, w). The category
  `Rep(G_full)` is a **symmetric tensor super-category**, not a
  symmetric tensor category. The DR-symmetric reconstruction theorem
  applies after restriction to the even-graded subcategory
  `Rep(G_full)^{even}` ⊆ `Rep(G_full)`. This is the framework's
  **bosonic observable subcategory**.

  In the super-symmetric extension of DR (cf. Doplicher-Roberts 1989
  §4; Mueger 2000), the full Z_2-graded super-category is reconstructed
  to a super-compact group `G_{super} = G_full`, but the DR
  reconstruction theorem stated in §1.1 applies only after passing to
  even-graded objects. **Partially satisfied** (full super-category is
  super-symmetric; symmetric even subcategory satisfies DR-P5).
- **(DR-P6) C*-structure.** Standard for finite-dim unitary reps of
  compact groups. **Satisfied.**
- **(DR-P7) Finite-dimensional Hom spaces.** Standard for finite-dim
  reps. **Satisfied.**

**Verdict on premises.** DR applies to the even-graded subcategory
`Rep(G_full)^{even}` ⊆ `Rep(G_full)`. The reconstructed compact group
is `G = G_full^{even}` (the connected component of identity, in
practical terms; or more precisely the subgroup acting trivially on
the parity grading, which is `U(1)_Q x SU(3)_color`).

In the supersymmetric DR extension, the reconstructed super-compact
group is `G_full = Z_2^F x U(1)_Q x SU(3)_color`. In either case the
reconstruction acts on the **operator-algebraic side**.

## 3. Structural finding — DR Route C does NOT close P1

### 3.1 G-equivariant scalar functionals — the `F_p` family (F1-Route-C)

**(F1-Route-C) G_DR acts trivially on the trace `r(J) = |Z[J]|`,
hence every continuous function of `r` is G-equivariant.** The trace
state `τ` on the framework's type II_1-style factor is **G-invariant**
(it is the unique trace up to scale, and trace uniqueness under
G-action gives G-invariance). The scalar
`r(J) := |det(D+J)| / |det D|` is built from G-invariant trace data
(determinants of `Cl(3)`-tensored block matrices, which are products
of G-Casimir eigenvalues invariant under G-conjugation). Hence
`r: U_0 → R_+` is G-invariant. Therefore every continuous functional
of `r`,

```text
        F_p[J]  :=  r(J)^p,        p ∈ R,                                (F_p)
```

is also G-invariant (G-equivariant under the trivial character), for
every real `p`. The DR-reconstructed group action does **not**
distinguish among the `F_p` family.

### 3.2 The Cauchy classifier still has to choose `p = 0` (F2-Route-C)

**(F2-Route-C) G-equivariance does not select the additive
representative `p = 0` (`log r`) from the multiplicative family.**
Each `F_p` for `p != 0` is:

- continuous on `U_0` (composition of continuous functions);
- CPT-even (depends only on `|Z|`);
- G_DR-invariant (function of G-invariant `r`);
- multiplicatively factorizing on independent subsystems:
  `F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B] = r(J_A ⊕ J_B)^p = (r_A · r_B)^p = F_p[J_A] · F_p[J_B]`;
- **not** additive for `p != 0`:
  `F_p[J_A ⊕ J_B] != F_p[J_A] + F_p[J_B]` generically.

Only the `p = 0` limit (which is `log r`) is additive on independent
subsystems. Selecting `log r` over `r^p` for `p != 0` is the Cauchy
multiplicative-to-additive functional equation classifier (Cauchy 1821,
Erdős 1946), which IS P1 in different vocabulary. DR Route C therefore
reproduces Pattern L (D5) of the
[Route D consolidated no-go](OBSERVABLE_PRINCIPLE_P1_BRIDGE_ROUTE_D_SHARPENED_NO_GO_NOTE_2026-05-17.md).

### 3.3 G acts on operators, not on scalar functionals (F3-Route-C)

**(F3-Route-C) DR reconstruction is operator-algebraic; P1 is on
scalar functionals.** DR reconstructs the compact group `G` acting on
the **field algebra** `F` and the **observable algebra**
`A = F^{G}` (the G-invariants). The reconstruction gives a tensor-
category equivalence `C_F ≅ Rep_f(G_DR)`; an object in `Rep_f(G_DR)`
is a finite-dim Hilbert space with a G-action. The reconstruction
does NOT produce a canonical scalar functional `W: A → R` from the
reconstruction data alone. The candidate scalar functionals
`W = -log τ`, `W = log Z`, `W = -β F` each invoke `log` separately;
the `log` is the Cauchy classifier (= P1). The reconstruction-of-G
step is independent of and prior to the choice of scalar functional;
the latter is where P1 enters.

### 3.4 The four candidate scalar functionals on G-invariants (F4-Route-C)

**(F4-Route-C) The four standard scalar-functional-from-trace
constructions all invoke `log`.** The standard ways to extract a
scalar from the G-invariant trace data are:

- **(α) Free energy.** `W = -β F = log Z`. The `log` packages the
  multiplicative-to-additive conversion; without `log`, `Z` is
  multiplicative not additive.
- **(β) Negative log trace.** `W = -log τ(A)` for `A ∈ A_+`. Same `log`.
- **(γ) Entropy.** `S(ρ) = -tr(ρ log ρ)`. Same `log` applied to the
  state.
- **(δ) Connes-Stormer index.** `H ↦ - log τ(e_H)` on type II_1 (for
  finite index `e_H`). Same `log`.

None of (α)-(δ) is forced by DR reconstruction alone. Each is a
*choice* of scalar functional on G-invariant operator data. The
Cauchy classifier selects `log` as the unique continuous map taking
multiplicative tensor products to additive scalars; this is the
classification step P1, in operator-algebraic vocabulary. DR Route C
does not retire that selection.

### 3.5 Even the super-extension does not close P1 (F5-Route-C)

**(F5-Route-C) The DR super-reconstruction extension does not change
the conclusion.** Even using the full Z_2-graded reconstruction
(Mueger 2000; Sutherland 1999), the reconstructed super-compact group
`G_super = G_full = Z_2^F x U(1)_Q x SU(3)_color` acts on the full
graded field algebra. Restricting to the **bosonic observable
subalgebra** `A_bos = F^{G_super}` (the G_super-invariants AND
even-graded operators) gives the physical observable algebra. The
scalar-functional-from-bosonic-observable step is the same `log τ` /
`log Z` choice as in the symmetric case. The Z_2^F grading does not
provide an independent additivity classifier; it only forces
restriction to Bose statistics on observables, which is already a
runner-local consequence of `FERMION_PARITY_Z2_GRADING_THEOREM_NOTE`'s
admitted-context content.

### 3.6 Sub-question (a)/(b)/(c) status — final answer

The task posed three sub-question outcome candidates. Based on
(F1)-(F5)-Route-C:

- **(a) DR reconstructs G and constrains admissible scalars to
  additive class — closes P1.** **NO.** DR reconstructs `G_DR =
  U(1)_Q x SU(3)_color` (or super-extension `G_super = G_full`)
  acting on operators, but every `F_p[J] = r(J)^p` is G-equivariant
  (function of G-invariant trace), so G-equivariance does not select
  the additive class.
- **(b) DR reconstructs G but admits `F_p` — sharpens no_go.** **YES,
  this is the answer.** The reconstruction is non-trivial (G is the
  framework's gauge-content group), and every member of the `F_p`
  counterexample family is G-equivariant; the Cauchy classifier
  remains required to pass from multiplicative to additive.
- **(c) DR fails premises — orthogonal route.** **PARTIALLY.** DR's
  symmetric premise (DR-P5) requires restriction to the even-graded
  subcategory (`Rep(G_full)^{even}`), so the strict DR-symmetric
  theorem applies only after the framework's Z_2^F-superselection
  passes through. The super-symmetric extension applies to the full
  super-category. So DR (in one of its forms) does apply to the
  framework; the conclusion is then **(b)**.

The honest landing is therefore the **combined (b) + (c-partial)**:
DR (super-)reconstruction works, recovers the framework's expected
gauge group, but does not close P1 on the scalar-functional side.
