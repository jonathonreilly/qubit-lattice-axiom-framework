### Exercise 2 — Elon Musk first-principles

Strip to first principles. There are two algebraically distinct ways a source
"composes" in this framework, and confusing them is the entire source of the
prior circularity:

- **Direct-sum (⊕) axis** — `A ⊕ B`, block-diagonal independent subsystems.
  The requirement on a scalar `F` is `F(A ⊕ B) = F(A) + F(B)`. Here `tr`
  *passes* (`tr(A ⊕ B) = tr A + tr B`), so this axis cannot do det-vs-tr; and
  the whole family `{|det|^p}` is `⊕`-multiplicative, so selecting the additive
  representative is the Cauchy step = P1. This is the foreclosed axis.
- **Operator-product (·) axis** — `A · S`, source insertion
  `D + J = D · (1 + D^{-1} J)`. The requirement is the character law
  `chi(A · S) = chi(A) · chi(S)`. Here `tr` *fails* and `det` is the unique
  algebraic/rational survivor (`GL(n)` abelianization). This axis does the
  **form** work the ⊕ axis cannot.

So the form question (det-vs-tr) is genuinely a theorem on the · axis. But now
the deep question: **does the · axis also fix the exponent?**

No. `|det|^c` satisfies the · character law for **every** real `c`
(`(t_1 t_2)^c = t_1^c t_2^c`; runner T3). The product axis collapses the
function space to the same one-parameter scale freedom `c` (= the `p` of
`F_p`) that the ⊕ axis leaves open. Tightening to **algebraic/rational**
characters narrows `c ∈ R` to `det^k`, `k ∈ Z`, but (a) still leaves `k`
free, and (b) is incompatible with the real-valued `|det|^c` form actually
used for a real generator `W` (the modulus is not algebraic). Either way the
exponent is unfixed by the character axis.

What fixes the exponent? Exactly the demand that the real generator `W` be
**additive over independent (block-diagonal) sectors** — and that demand, in
every form it can take, is P1. The two-stage synthesis tried to escape via the
Gibbs-potential selector `∇W = ⟨O⟩` ("the generator is the potential of the
expectation field"). First-principles check: that selector says
`∂W/∂j_a = ⟨O_a⟩` is a **block-local field** (the expectation of the local
operator `O_a` depends only on the block containing `a`, on block-diagonal
`D`). But "`∂W/∂j_a` is independent of the other block" is condition **(Pot)**,
which §3.1 proves is *identical* to additivity **(Add)** and locality **(Loc)**
on smooth `W` with `W[0] = 0`. So the Gibbs-potential escape is P1 relocated to
differential form — exactly the skeptical reading of the two-stage synthesis,
now proven.

The one genuinely-different selector is the **normalized** gradient
`(1/p) Z^{-p} ∂(Z^p)/∂j` — the Born/probability normalization, `Tr ρ = 1`.
But that selector returns `∂(log Z)/∂j` for **every** `p` (runner T6): it is
exponent-blind and selects nothing. This is why no normalization-based escape
exists.

**First-principles bottom line:** the form is a theorem (· axis); the exponent
is the admission; every exponent-fixing selector is P1; the one exponent-blind
selector is too weak. The atom is the exponent-fixing additivity demand and it
is irreducible against the enumerated selector families.

## 3. Load-bearing step — the irreducibility Lemma (Class A)

### 3.1 Lemma ((Add) ⇔ (Loc) ⇔ (Pot) on smooth `W` with `W[0] = 0`)

**Statement.** Let `U ⊂ R^|A| × R^|B|` be a convex open neighbourhood of the
origin and `W : U → R` twice continuously differentiable with `W(0, 0) = 0`.
The following are equivalent:

- **(Add)** `W(J_A, J_B) = W(J_A, 0) + W(0, J_B)` for all `(J_A, J_B) ∈ U`;
- **(Loc)** `∂²W/∂j_a ∂j_b (J_A, J_B) = 0` for all `a ∈ A, b ∈ B`,
  `(J_A, J_B) ∈ U`;
- **(Pot)** for every `a ∈ A`, `∂W/∂j_a` is independent of `J_B`, and for
  every `b ∈ B`, `∂W/∂j_b` is independent of `J_A`.

**Proof.**

*(Add) ⟹ (Loc).* Differentiate (Add) in `j_a` (`a ∈ A`): the `W(0, J_B)` term
has no `j_a` dependence, so `∂W/∂j_a(J_A, J_B) = ∂W/∂j_a(J_A, 0)` is
independent of `J_B`; differentiating again in `j_b` gives `∂²W/∂j_a ∂j_b = 0`.

*(Loc) ⟺ (Pot).* `∂²W/∂j_a ∂j_b = 0` for all `b ∈ B` says exactly that
`∂W/∂j_a` has zero gradient in the `J_B` directions on the convex `U`, i.e.
`∂W/∂j_a` is independent of `J_B`; symmetrically for `∂W/∂j_b`. This is (Pot)
verbatim.

*(Pot) ⟹ (Add).* By (Pot), `∂W/∂j_a(J_A, J_B) = ∂W/∂j_a(J_A, 0)`. Put
`G(J_A, J_B) := W(J_A, J_B) − W(0, J_B)`; then `G(0, J_B) = 0` and
`∂G/∂j_a = ∂W/∂j_a(J_A, 0)` is independent of `J_B`. Integrating along the
`J_A`-path at fixed `J_B`,
`G(J_A, J_B) = ∫_0^{J_A} ∂W/∂j_a(J_A', 0) dJ_A'`, which is independent of
`J_B`; setting `J_B = 0` and using `W(0, 0) = 0` gives `G(J_A, J_B) =
W(J_A, 0)`. Substituting back, `W(J_A, J_B) = W(J_A, 0) + W(0, J_B)`, i.e.
(Add). ∎

**Reproof (runner T5).** On a generic smooth polynomial `W` (degree 3 in each
block, `W(0,0) = 0`), the runner computes the coefficient set each of (Add),
(Loc), (Pot) forces to vanish and verifies the three sets are **identical**
(`{c11, c12, c13, c21, c22, c31}`). The equivalence is therefore reproven from
primitives, not asserted by name.

The normalized (Born) selector replaces `∂W/∂j` by `(1/p) Z^{-p} ∂(Z^p)/∂j`

This is the decisive closure of the last open escape: the only selector that
