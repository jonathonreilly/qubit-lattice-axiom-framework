## Statement

For an Axiom 1 qubit site `x`, let `A_x ≅ M_2(C)` and choose Pauli generators
`σ_i` satisfying

```text
    {σ_i, σ_j} = 2 δ_ij I,    [σ_i, σ_j] = 2i ε_ijk σ_k.                    (1)
```

Define

```text
    S_i := σ_i / 2,        i ∈ {1, 2, 3}.                                   (2)
```

Then:

**(C1) su(2) Lie algebra.** `[S_i, S_j] = i ε_ijk S_k`.

**(C2) Casimir.** `S^2 := S_1^2 + S_2^2 + S_3^2 = (3/4) I`.

**(C3) Spin label.** Under the standard `su(2)` Casimir convention,
`j(j+1) = 3/4`, hence `j = 1/2`.

**(C4) S_z spectrum.** `S_3` has eigenvalues `{ -1/2, +1/2 }`.

**(C5) Irreducibility and uniqueness.** The action is irreducible, with
scalar commutant, and is the unique two-dimensional complex `j = 1/2`
`su(2)` module up to unitary equivalence.

## Proof

### Step 1 - Axiom 1 Supplies the Qubit Algebra

By [`MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md`](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md), Axiom 1
says that each lattice site carries the qubit algebra
`A_x ≅ M_2(C) = End(C^2)`, generated by Pauli matrices. The
Pauli-irrep theorem supplies the fixed-sign algebraic uniqueness of the
Pauli realization of the complexified Cl(3) relations. The proof below is
therefore a computation inside the Axiom 1 local algebra, not an appeal to the
older physical-Hilbert uniqueness row.

### Step 2 - su(2) Lie Algebra (C1)

Direct computation in the Pauli basis gives `[σ_i, σ_j] = 2i ε_ijk σ_k`.
With `S_i = σ_i / 2`,

```text
    [S_i, S_j] = (1/4)[σ_i, σ_j]
               = (i/2) ε_ijk σ_k
               = i ε_ijk S_k.                                             (3)
```

### Step 3 - Casimir and Spin Label (C2, C3)

Since `σ_i^2 = I`,

```text
    S^2 = Σ_i S_i^2 = Σ_i σ_i^2 / 4 = (3/4) I.                              (4)
```

For an irreducible finite-dimensional complex `su(2)` module, the
Casimir is `j(j+1) I`. Thus `j(j+1) = 3/4`. The nonnegative solution is
`j = 1/2`.

### Step 4 - S_z Spectrum (C4)

In the standard Pauli basis,

```text
    S_3 = σ_3 / 2 = diag(1/2, -1/2),
```

so the eigenvalues are exactly `{ -1/2, +1/2 }`.

### Step 5 - Irreducibility and Uniqueness (C5)

Let `A ∈ M_2(C)` commute with all three `S_i`. Since the `S_i` generate
`M_2(C)` as an algebra, `A` commutes with every element of `M_2(C)`, so
`A = λ I`. Equivalently, the commutant has complex dimension one. By
Schur's lemma, the action is irreducible. The finite-dimensional complex
`su(2)` irreducible with dimension `2` is unique up to equivalence, and
its label is `j = (2 - 1)/2 = 1/2`. ∎

## Corollaries

C1. **Local Pauli action is spin one-half.** Any use of the canonical
Pauli generators as local `su(2)` spin generators gives the `j = 1/2`
module on a single Axiom 1 qubit.

C2. **No larger irreducible spin action on one qubit under this action.**
The two-dimensional local module cannot itself be the irreducible
`j = 1`, `j = 3/2`, or higher `su(2)` representation; those have
dimension at least three or four. Higher-spin sectors, if present, must
come from larger tensor blocks, composites, or a different local action.

C3. **Spin-statistics remains out of scope.** This note supplies a local
Pauli `j = 1/2` representation-theoretic fact only. It does not derive
fermionic exchange statistics or identify the full physical rotation
generator on multi-site states.
