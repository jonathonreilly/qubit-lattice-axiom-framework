## Headline

The symmetry of the finite cutting system extends from the 48 cutting
symmetries to a group of order 384 that fixes all eight readings pointwise.
That group preserves exactly one partition of the 192 pieces into the supports
of eight pairwise disjoint cuttings — eight blocks of 24, each meeting each of
the four piece-orbits in 6. The induced action on the eight blocks realizes a
hidden three-bit affine geometry: the image has order 192 and splits as the 8
translations together with the full 24-element stabilizer of a distinguished
plane, and the original 48 cutting symmetries induce exactly the subgroup
whose linear part additionally fixes a distinguished direction lying off that
plane. The two automorphisms constructed in the previous cycle release that
pinned direction, enlarging the linear part from 6 to 24.

## The group E

Writing E for the group generated by the 48 cutting symmetries together with
the two extra automorphisms, the closure orders are 48 for the cutting
symmetries alone, 96 when the first extra automorphism is adjoined, and 384
when the second is adjoined — and the first extra automorphism already lies in
that last group, so E has order 384. E is transitive on all 192 pieces. The
stabilizer of a piece has order 2; its nonidentity element is an involution
with 16 fixed pieces, giving permutation rank 104 = 16 + 88. All 384 elements
fix all 8 readings elementwise.

| group | order | element-order census |

| the derived subgroup | 96 | `1:1 2:19 3:32 4:12 6:32` |
| E | 384 | `1:1 2:75 3:32 4:132 6:96 8:48` |

The centre of E has order 2. Its nonidentity element z is fixed-point-free —
0 fixed pieces — lies in the group of order 96 but not in the 48, and acts on
the four piece-orbits by swapping the first with the second and the third with
the fourth. The derived subgroup has order 96 but is not the group of order 96
generated by the first extra automorphism, and it does not contain the 48;
neither the 48 nor that order-96 subgroup is normal in E. All 384 squares lie
in the derived subgroup, so the abelianization has order 4 and is the Klein

## The eight blocks

Exactly one E-orbit of cuttings has size 8. Those 8 cuttings each have support
of size 24, are pairwise disjoint, and their union is all 192 pieces. They
therefore give an E-invariant partition of the pieces into 8 blocks of 24, and
each block meets each of the four piece-orbits in exactly 6. The same 8
cuttings already form a single orbit under the 48 cutting symmetries alone, so
the partition is not new to the extended group — what is new is that E
preserves it while acting on the blocks far more richly.

The kernel of the block action is exactly the centre, of order 2, containing
z. The image B of E therefore has order 192, and the image B48 of the 48
cutting symmetries has order 48, so the 48 act faithfully on the blocks.

## The affine three-bit structure

B has 25 fixed-point-free involutions. A complete search over commuting
triples of them finds exactly 4 subgroups of B that are elementary abelian of
order 8 and regular on the eight blocks, and exactly 1 of those 4 is normal in
B. That unique normal one, T, is the canonical translation group; no labelling
choice enters its construction.

Taking the block containing the first piece as origin, the map sending a
translation to the image of the origin is a bijection from T onto the eight
blocks. Fixing 3 generators of T labels the blocks by three-bit strings, and
for each element of B its linear part is the composite of that element with
the translation carrying its image of the origin back to the origin. Every
linear part fixes the origin, and every linear part is additive on all 64
pairs of labels: all of B is affine. The set L of linear parts has 24
elements, is closed under composition, and meets T only in the identity, so
the factorization is a genuine split with 8 times 24 equal to 192. The
element-order census of L is `1:1 2:9 3:8 4:6`.

L has 2 orbits on the 7 nonzero labels, of sizes 3 and 4, and no nonzero label
is fixed by all of L. The 3-orbit together with the zero label is closed under
bitwise addition, so it is a plane P; since any L-invariant plane is a union
of L-orbits together with the zero label, and the orbit sizes are 3 and 4,
P is the unique L-invariant plane — confirmed directly, 1 of the 7 planes.
L acts faithfully on its 4-orbit, with image of size 24, so L is the symmetric
group on four objects. This is a computational certificate, not a literature
import.

A complete search over all 512 three-by-three matrices over the two-element
field finds 168 invertible ones, of which exactly 24 map P onto P; converting
each element of L to a matrix by its images of the 3 basis labels, L equals
that set of 24. So L is the full stabilizer of the plane P inside the general
linear group of the three-bit space.

All 8 translations already lie in B48. The set L48 of linear parts of B48 has
6 elements, lies inside L, and is nonabelian, with element-order census
`1:1 2:3 3:2` — a nonabelian group of order 6. L48 has 3 orbits on the 7
nonzero labels, of sizes 1, 3 and 3, so exactly 1 nonzero label is fixed by
all of L48; call it the distinguished direction. It does not lie in P, so the
three-bit space splits as P together with the line through it. That direction
lies in L's 4-orbit, and its stabilizer in L has order 6 and equals L48. A
complete search over the 168 invertible matrices finds exactly 6 that preserve
P and fix that direction, and they are exactly L48: L48 is the joint
stabilizer of the plane and the direction, and the 48 cutting symmetries are
precisely the part of B whose linear part keeps both.

This is the sense in which the extension is geometric rather than incidental.
The 48 cutting symmetries were already affine on the eight blocks, but with a
direction pinned. Adjoining the two extra automorphisms keeps the plane and
releases the direction, taking the linear part from 6 to 24 and the block
image from 48 to 192.
