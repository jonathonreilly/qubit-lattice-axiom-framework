## Answer

**Yes, conditional on an orientation-preserving subgroup criterion, at the
group-theory level and uniquely.** The orientation-preserving
(`det = +1`) subgroup of `S_3` is exactly `A_3 = C_3 = {id, (012), (021)}`; the
three transpositions are the `det = −1` orientation-reversing elements (verified
on both the `C^3` permutation representation and the 2-dimensional `E` irrep).

Therefore, if the admissible generation-sector symmetry is required to preserve
the determinant/orientation sign in this representation, that criterion
forbids exactly the transpositions and selects exactly `C_3`. This selected
`C_3` is
simultaneously:

- a **proper** subgroup, hence it **lifts** the generation mass degeneracy (a
  generic `C_3`-invariant circulant mass matrix has three distinct eigenvalues);
- the framework's **distinguished cyclic structure `C_3[111]`**.

So the orientation-preserving criterion does not merely *permit* nondegeneracy
(as any proper subgroup would); it *selects* the specific `C_3`.

## Theorem (orientation-positivity selects C_3)

Let `S_3` act on `V = C^3` by permutation of `(X_1, X_2, X_3)`.

**(1) Orientation signs.** For `g ∈ S_3`, `det ρ(g) = sgn(g)`: the identity and
the two 3-cycles have `det = +1`; the three transpositions have `det = −1`. The
same signs hold on the 2-dimensional `E` irrep (`det ρ_E(g) = sgn(g)`).

**(2) Positivity-selected subgroup.** The set `{g : det ρ(g) = +1}` is exactly
`A_3 = C_3 = {id, (012), (021)}`, the unique index-2 subgroup of `S_3`, and it is
closed under composition.

**(3) Degeneracy lift.** `C_3` is a proper subgroup; a generic `C_3`-invariant
(circulant) Hermitian operator on `V` has three distinct eigenvalues. Hence the
positivity-selected symmetry both removes orientation-reversal and lifts the
forced `S_3` mass degeneracy.

**Conclusion.** Among all symmetry-breakings that lift the generation
degeneracy, an admitted orientation-preserving subgroup criterion selects the
unique orientation-preserving one, `C_3 = A_3`, coinciding with the framework's
`C_3[111]`. ∎
