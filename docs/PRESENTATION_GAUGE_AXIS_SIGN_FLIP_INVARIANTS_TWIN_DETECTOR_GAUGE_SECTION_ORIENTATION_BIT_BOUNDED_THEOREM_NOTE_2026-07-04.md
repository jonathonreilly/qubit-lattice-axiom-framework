## Theorem statements and proofs

### Theorem 1 (the flip group and the cubic factorization)

The axis-sign flips form `Z2^3`; splitting by determinant gives the Klein
four-group of `pi`-rotations (even, proper) and four odd improper flips
including `-I`; and `O_h = G+ . Z2^3` with `|G+ ∩ Z2^3| = 4`.

*Proof.* Diagonal `+-1` matrices are closed under multiplication, each its
own inverse, abelian — `Z2^3` (A1). Determinant is the product of signs, so
even flips (an even number of minus signs) are proper and odd flips
improper; the even flips are exactly the diagonal elements of `G+` (A2).
Counting: `|G+| = 24`, `|Z2^3| = 8`, `|G+ ∩ Z2^3| = 4`, so the product has
`24 * 8 / 4 = 48 = |O_h|` elements (A3). An improper element is thus an odd
flip composed with a proper rotation.

### Theorem 2 (flip-invariants are all-even-exponent functions)

A content polynomial is `Z2^3`-invariant iff every exponent is even.

*Proof.* Invariance under `x_i -> -x_i` for each `i` separately forces even
degree in each variable; conversely all-even monomials are manifestly
invariant. Scanned over `0 <= a,b,c <= 5` with agreement to the parity
predicate, and cross-checked by the flip-projection reproducing the all-even
truncation (B1-B2). The odd (`det`) character is absent from every
polynomial of degree `<= 8` (the `det`-isotype projector has rank zero
there) and first appears at degree nine (B3).

### Theorem 3 (flip-definable sets are inversion-symmetric)

Every set defined by flip-invariant functions is inversion-invariant; no
flip-invariant function separates a generic orbit from its inversion twin.

*Proof.* `-I in Z2^3`, so flip-invariant `f` obeys `f(v) = f(-v)` (C1); the
value-multiset of a flip-invariant function coincides on a generic orbit and
its twin, checked across several independent flip-invariant functions and
base points (C2). A chiral set — one distinguishing an orbit from its twin —
therefore has no flip-invariant defining function.

### Theorem 4 (the twin datum is a gauge section, one bit, locked to Psi9)

The unique twin-separating channel `Psi9` is flip-odd; separating the twins
requires a section of the flip group; the section is one `Z2` bit, locked to
`sign(Psi9)`, the bootstrap-twin selector.

*Proof.* `Psi9` separates the generic orbit from its twin and flips under a
single-axis flip (C3). The flip group modulo its even subgroup is `Z2`
(det); a section chooses signs (D1). Flipping the section flips
`sign(Psi9)`; the section `det`-bit times `sign(Psi9)` is invariant under an
odd flip and unmoved by an even flip (D2, with the even-flip negative
control), so the two are one bit. A single axis flip is a complex-antilinear
automorphism sending `omega -> -omega` (D3), the conjugation face already
seen for improper elements.
