## What is conditional

Admissibility says there is one fixed nearest-neighbor rule, covariant under
lattice translations and proper cubic rotations, and that for each site the
probability distribution over the possibilities is determined by the
nearest-neighbor conditions. The reading note in
[minimal axioms](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-06-29.md) says that this distribution
does not supply the formation site, probability, or rate, and the dynamics
section says Admissibility does not supply transition weights.

This note does **not** identify that distribution with a density operator on
`C^2 ⊗ C^2`. It answers a narrower question. Suppose a pair law is represented
by such a density, and suppose covariance means that a proper cubic rotation
`R` acts by the simultaneous Bloch rotation `R̂ = diag(1,R)` on the Pauli
coefficient matrix `C`. Then the invariant families are the spaces computed
below, and the singlet is an endpoint of the isotropic positive segment, not
its only point.

## Definitions

Let `G` be the set of `3×3` determinant-`+1` signed-permutation matrices. It
is generated by the quarter turns

```text
R_z = [[ 0, -1, 0],
       [ 1,  0, 0],
       [ 0,  0, 1]],
R_x = [[ 1,  0,  0],
       [ 0,  0, -1],
       [ 0,  1,  0]].
```

A coefficient matrix `C = (c_ab)`, with `a,b in {0,1,2,3}`, stands for
`(1/4) Σ c_ab σ_a ⊗ σ_b`, where `σ_0 = I`. The covariance constraint is
`R̂ C R̂^T = C`. Trace one is `c_00 = 1`. Hermiticity makes every `c_ab` real. The constraint matrices have rational
entries; their rational nullspace bases also span the real invariant spaces.
The runner uses rational fixtures, without restricting the theorem to them.

The directed stabilizer of the lattice axis `e_z` is
`{R in G : R e_z = e_z}`. The undirected stabilizer is
`{R in G : R e_z = ± e_z}`.

## Theorem 1 — three invariant spaces

**Conclusion.** `G` has order `24`. The `G`-invariant coefficient matrices
form a `2`-dimensional space, spanned by `c_00` and `c_11+c_22+c_33`. The
directed stabilizer has order `4` and fixes a `6`-dimensional space, with
basis

```text
c_00, c_03, c_30, c_33, c_11+c_22, c_21-c_12.
```

The undirected stabilizer has order `8`, is generated by `R_z` and the half
turn `R_x^2`, and fixes a `3`-dimensional space, spanned by

```text
c_00, c_11+c_22, c_33.
```

The only `G`-fixed vector in `Q^3` is `0`.

**Proof.** Both generators lie in `G`, and the group they generate is all of
`G`, by comparing the closure with the `3! 2^3 / 2 = 24` determinant-`+1`
signed permutations. The fixed-vector claim is the same orbit argument as in
the one-site control: every nonzero vector's orbit spans `Q^3`, so a proper
invariant subspace cannot exist. The runner recomputes that fixed space as a
`3`-variable nullspace and gets dimension `0`.

For a pair, each group element contributes the `16` linear equations
`(R̂ C R̂^T - C)_ij = 0`. Exact row reduction over `Q` produces the stated
nullspaces. The listed matrices are invariant because each reduces to zero
residual on every group element, and the computed nullspace has the same
dimension and sits inside their span. A single off-diagonal probe, `c_12`,
has nonzero residual under `R_z`, so the full-group result is not the set of
all coefficient matrices. The undirected nullspace still contains a nonzero
`c_33` component. The directed nullspace contains the twist `c_21-c_12`, and
every undirected null vector has that twist coordinate equal to zero. Those
two probes fail if the bases are swapped or truncated.

## Theorem 2 — the isotropic line

**Conclusion.** For `u = v = w`, the operator
`ρ_w = (I + w σ·σ)/4` is positive semidefinite exactly for
`w ∈ [-1, 1/3]`. Its CHSH maximum has square `8 w^2`. Inside the positive
segment that maximum exceeds `2` exactly for `w ∈ [-1, -1/√2)` and equals
`2√2` exactly at `w = -1`.

**Proof.** On the computational basis, `XX+YY` has eigenvalues `0,0,+2,-2`
on `|00⟩`, `|11⟩`, the symmetric combination of `|01⟩,|10⟩`, and the
antisymmetric combination. `ZZ` has eigenvalues `+1,+1,-1,-1` on those same
vectors. Therefore `w(XX+YY+ZZ)` has eigenvalues `w,w,w,-3w`. Adding the
identity and dividing by `4` gives positive semidefiniteness exactly when
`1+w ≥ 0` and `1-3w ≥ 0`, that is `w ∈ [-1, 1/3]`. The endpoint `w=1` fails
because the singlet eigenvalue is `1-3 = -2`. The endpoint `w=1/3` has
singlet eigenvalue `0`. The endpoint `w=-1` has triplet eigenvalue `0`.

For correlations, `⟨(a·σ)⊗(b·σ)⟩ = w a·b`. For unit settings `b,b'`, the
vectors `c=b+b'` and `d=b-b'` are orthogonal and satisfy
`|c|^2+|d|^2 = 4`. The CHSH combination is at most `|w|(|c|+|d|)`, and
`(|c|+|d|)^2 ≤ 2(|c|^2+|d|^2) = 8`, so the maximum square is `8 w^2`.
Equality holds when `|c|^2=|d|^2=2`. Thus the square exceeds the classical
value `4` exactly when `w^2 > 1/2`. Intersecting with `w ∈ [-1, 1/3]` leaves
only the negative side `w ∈ [-1, -1/√2)`, because `(1/3)^2 = 1/9 < 1/2`.
The square equals `8` on that segment only at `w=-1`.

## Theorem 3 — two undirected Tsirelson corners

**Conclusion.** On `c_11=c_22=u` and `c_33=v`, the eigenvalues of
`u(XX+YY)+v ZZ` are `v`, `v`, `2u-v` and `-2u-v`. The trace-one operator is
positive semidefinite exactly when all four shifted by `+1` are nonnegative.
The points `(u,v)=(-1,-1)` and `(1,-1)` meet that condition with a zero
eigenvalue. At both points an equatorial pair of orthogonal test vectors
with square length `2` gives CHSH square `8`. No other positive `(u,v)` does.

**Proof.** The eigenvalue list is the same computation as Theorem 2, with
`u` and `v` independent. Nonempty range requires `|u| ≤ 1`, because
`v ≤ 1-2|u|` and `v ≥ -1`. Every positive point therefore has `u^2 ≤ 1` and
`v^2 ≤ 1`.

Let `T` be the diagonal correlation matrix with entries `u,u,v`, and let
`Q = |Tc|^2+|Td|^2` for an admissible pair `c,d`. Then
`Q = 4u^2 + (v^2-u^2) S` with `0 ≤ S ≤ 4`, and
`(|Tc|+|Td|)^2 ≤ 2Q`, with equality only when `|Tc|=|Td|`. If the CHSH
square is `8`, then `Q ≥ 4`. But `Q/4` is a convex combination of `u^2` and
`v^2`, hence `Q ≤ 4`, so `Q=4` and both squared coefficients that carry
positive weight equal `1`. The case `v^2 > u^2` cannot put weight on two
equal squares, and the remaining endpoint `S=4` forces one of `c,d` to vanish,
so `|Tc| ≠ |Td|`. The case `v^2 ≤ u^2` forces `|u|=1`, then positivity forces
`v=-1`. Both surviving points have `T = ±I` on the `xy` plane, and
`|c|^2=|d|^2=2` in that plane gives `|Tc|^2=|Td|^2=2`, hence CHSH square `8`.

## What this does not do

- It does not derive a formation site, rate, or clock.
- It does not show that the admissibility distribution is a two-qubit density.
- It does not select `w`, `u`, or `v`.
- It does not derive the dynamics clause. That clause remains a way to point
  at the endpoint `w=-1` inside a family that covariance leaves free.
- It does not compute the CHSH region of the directed `6`-dimensional family.
- It does not claim that every positive undirected state with CHSH above `2`
  has been listed. Theorem 3 locates only the value `2√2`.
