## What it means, and what it does not

1. **It restricts a law's dependence, not the state.** A *law* may not depend
   on a choice the supplied structure leaves open; a *state* (a configuration
   of records) may still carry such a choice. So it does **not** impose mirror
   symmetry, and it leaves state-level (spontaneous) orientation free.

2. **"Admitted" names the only route that can apply.** A choice the supplied
   structure does not fix is, by definition, underivable and unbridgeable: a
   derivation or bridge that fixed the choice would mean the structure fixes
   it, contradicting "not fixed." So of the four routes in the base sentence,
   derivation and bridge cannot apply to an unfixed choice; the only way a law
   may come to depend on it is explicit admission or approved primitive
   registration. The clause names that route as "admitted"; it introduces no
   new mechanism.

3. **It makes explicit the reach the Qualification already asserts.** It is a
   clarification ("In particular"), not new axiom content: the base sentence
   already governs "further physical structure ... before use as a premise";
   the clause states that a law's *dependence* on an unfixed choice is such a
   use.
