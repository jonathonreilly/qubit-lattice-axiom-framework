# Route-2 Endpoint Triple: The Centre-Minus-Shell Readout Is A Global Cubic-Spline Artefact On A One-Site Spike

**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Status authority:** independent audit lane only. This source note does not set,
claim, or predict an audit outcome; later status is generated by the audit pipeline
after independent review.
**Type:** bounded_theorem
**Claim type:** bounded_theorem
**Primary runner:** `frontier_quark_route2_endpoint_triple_center_minus_shell_readout_cubic_spline_artefact_check_2026_09_30.py`


## Quantifier boundary (2026-10-04)

The all-site identity holds for the stated seven-site stencil inside a
Dirichlet box containing all six neighbours. Equality of centre and shell
readouts follows **only when every interpolation stencil avoids the origin**,
the anchor ignores it, and the required denominators are nonzero. Locality
alone does not force this: the six-point control touches the origin and leaks
the spike. The four-point counterexample refutes interpolation-independent
interpretation of the size-15 rationals; it does not select a physical
interpolation. The finite sampled ladder fails a plateau test. Any statement
below that it “does not converge” means that finite diagnostic only; an
asymptotic nonconvergence theorem is not supplied.

## 1. Plain statement

The Route-2 quark lane treats the readout triple
`(beta_T/alpha_T, alpha_T/alpha_E, beta_E/alpha_E) = (-1, -2, 21/4)` as a target
to be derived. The rationals came from a nearest-rational scan of the live
size-15 values of the gravity-metric readout
([QUARK_E_CHANNEL_ENDPOINT_QUOTIENT_LAW_NOTE_2026-04-19.md](QUARK_E_CHANNEL_ENDPOINT_QUOTIENT_LAW_NOTE_2026-04-19.md),
[QUARK_ENDPOINT_RATIO_CHAIN_LAW_NOTE_2026-04-19.md](QUARK_ENDPOINT_RATIO_CHAIN_LAW_NOTE_2026-04-19.md)),
which those notes already record as a numerical match, not a derivation. The
live values are in
[QUARK_ROUTE2_EXACT_READOUT_MAP_NOTE_2026-04-19.md](QUARK_ROUTE2_EXACT_READOUT_MAP_NOTE_2026-04-19.md) and
[QUARK_ROUTE2_HONEST_GRAVITY_METRIC_RHOE_CHARACTERIZATION_NOTE_2026-07-02.md](QUARK_ROUTE2_HONEST_GRAVITY_METRIC_RHOE_CHARACTERIZATION_NOTE_2026-07-02.md).
What is new here is that the values that were scanned are an interpolation
artefact.

The centre source and the shell source produce the same lattice potential at
every site except the origin, where they differ by exactly `1/6`. The readout
therefore has nothing to compare, except through the interpolation it uses to
turn lattice values into a smooth field. The repo interpolates with a global
cubic spline. The spline turns the one-site difference into an oscillating
tail that reaches the probe points, and the whole centre-versus-shell entry of
the triple is that tail. With a local interpolation the centre and shell
readouts are equal, `q_T = q_E = 1`, `beta_T/alpha_T = beta_E/alpha_E = 0`,
`rho_E = 0`, at every box size including 15.

So the two centre-minus-shell entries `(-1, 21/4)` are a property of the
interpolation, not of the lattice field. The size-15 agreement with
`(5/6, ., 15/8)` is one point of a ladder that does not converge.

## 2. Scope

This note records an exact identity, its immediate consequence for any local
readout, and a replay of the existing runner with the interpolation swapped. It
does not derive a readout law, does not choose an interpolation, does not say
which tensor functional is physical, and does not touch any audit row. The
middle entry `alpha_T/alpha_E` is a shell-only quantity and is not covered by
the identity (section 6). Numerical replays are same-family checks.

## 3. Exact identity

Let `H` be the unit-weight nearest-neighbour negative Laplacian on the
`(N-2)^3` interior of the Dirichlet box and `G = H^(-1)`. With `e0` the unit
source at the centre `c` and `s_unit = (1/6) sum_arms e_arm`, the centre stencil
gives `H e0 = 6 e0 - sum_arms e_arm`, so `s_unit = e0 - H e0 / 6` and

```text
phi(s_unit) = G s_unit = G e0 - e0 / 6        at every site,
phi(e0) - phi(s_unit) = delta_origin / 6.
```

The support-site version is in
[TENSOR_SUPPORT_CENTER_EXCESS_LAW_NOTE.md](TENSOR_SUPPORT_CENTER_EXCESS_LAW_NOTE.md);
what is added here is that the same relation holds at all sites, and, because
`phi` is linear in `q`, for every finite-difference point of the readout:
`phi(E0 + t d) - phi(S_UNIT + t d) = delta_origin / 6` for every direction `d`
and every `t`. The reduced-shell anchor uses only sites beyond radius 4.0, so it
is identical for the two sources.

Independent DST solver (no repo linear algebra), runner PART 1:

## 4. Consequence for any local readout

The readout reaches the potential only through `tcomp.interpolated_phi` (the
metric is `adm_metric(phi_grid, ...)`, the anchor uses the raw grid at radius
> 4.0). If the interpolation stencil at every evaluation point avoids the
origin site, the two sources give the same inputs (to floating-point rounding,
about 1e-17) at every finite-difference point, so `gamma_X(center) =
gamma_X(shell)` for `X = E, T`: `q_T = q_E = 1` and `rho_E = 0`, up to
floating-point noise amplified by the finite differences (at most 1e-6 below).

Replay of the unmodified runner functional (`base_eta_floor`, max-abs

Lagrange interpolant in place of the spline (runner PART 4):

orders of magnitude below `21/4`.

## 5. What the spline does

`interpolated_phi` calls `scipy.ndimage.map_coordinates(phi_grid, ..., order=3,
mode="nearest")`, a global cubic B-spline with the recursive prefilter of pole
`z = sqrt(3) - 2 = -0.2679`. The interpolant of a lattice delta is the cardinal
kernel with B-spline coefficients `sqrt(3) z^|k|` (runner PART 3 matches scipy to
3e-17): it spans the whole box, alternates in sign, and shrinks by `0.2679` per
lattice step.

At the first probe `(4.25, 0, 0)` for N = 15, the spline of the spike `delta_origin / 6`
has value `+3.95e-4` and second derivative `-6.10e-3`, while the spline of the
shell field itself has second derivative `+4.49e-3`. The ringing curvature is
1.36 times the field's own curvature and opposite in sign, and the readout is an
Einstein-tensor (second-derivative) functional. The unmodified spline replay at
the same size gives `q_T = 0.833328`, `alpha_T/alpha_E = -2.0054`,
`rho_E = 5.2575`; at N = 13 and 17 it gives `rho_E = -6.23` and `-41.02`
(runner PART 2; the N = 11 to 21 ladder is in the honest-characterization note).
The size-15 triple is one point on a ladder that does not converge.

## 6. What this changes, and what it does not

Changed:

- The entries `beta_T/alpha_T = -1` and `beta_E/alpha_E = 21/4` are not
  properties of the lattice gravity-metric field; with a local readout both are
  zero. The size-15 match `q_T = 5/6` to `5e-6` is a spline coincidence.
- The ladder's centre-minus-shell entries (`q_T`, `q_E`, `rho_E` at N = 13 to 21) are the
  spline's response to the spike. The honest-characterization note locates the
  ladder instability in the extremal floor functional itself; that account is
  incomplete for these entries.

Not changed:

- The exact carrier/readout algebra and the non-uniqueness statement of the
  readout-map note: `rho_E = 0` and `rho_E = 21/4` are both admissible maps on
  the restricted class; this note adds that the field itself gives `rho_E = 0`
  under a local readout.
- Conditional notes that take `{5/6, -2, -8/9}` or `rho_E = 21/4` as a supplied
  premise remain valid as conditionals. The motivation that the live replay
  lands near those rationals is withdrawn.
- The size-15 numbers in the existing caches are correct outputs of the spline
  pipeline.

Not covered: `alpha_T/alpha_E` compares two shell readouts and is not fixed by
the identity. In this replay it is not stable: at N = 13, 15, 17 it is
`+5.38, -2.01, +3.92` (spline), `+6.79, -0.60, +6.36` (4-point), `+5.02, -7.22,
-0.59` (6-point); this note does not diagnose why. The replay does not test
other tensor functionals or other interpolants, and does not say what the
physical answer for the gravity metric is.
