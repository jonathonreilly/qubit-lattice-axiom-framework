## Statement

Let the per-site algebra be the complex qubit `A = M_2(C)`, equivalently
the real Clifford algebra `Cl(3,0)` (axiom A1; the identification is the
retained backbone
[CL3_COMPLEXIFICATION_SPLIT_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10.md](CL3_COMPLEXIFICATION_SPLIT_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10.md)
and
[CL3_FAITHFUL_IRREP_DIM_TWO_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10.md](CL3_FAITHFUL_IRREP_DIM_TWO_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10.md)).
Call a self-adjoint `v in A` with `v^2 = I` and `Tr v = 0` a **unit
direction**, and call a set of unit directions **orthogonal** if its
members pairwise anticommute (`{v_a, v_b} = 0` for `a != b`). Then:

1. (**P1 — generation.**) The Pauli matrices `s_1, s_2, s_3` are three
   pairwise-orthogonal unit directions satisfying the `Cl(3,0)` relations
   `{s_a, s_b} = 2 delta_{ab} I`; they generate `A`.

2. (**P2 — maximal frame is exactly 3.**) The real vector space of
   traceless self-adjoint elements of `M_2(C)` is 3-dimensional, and
   orthogonal unit directions are trace-orthogonal
   (`{v_a, v_b} = 0 => Tr(v_a v_b) = 0`). Hence at most 3 orthogonal unit
   directions can coexist, and the only element of `M_2(C)` anticommuting
   with all of `s_1, s_2, s_3` is `0`. There is **no independent fourth
   direction**: `d_s <= 3`.

3. (**P3 — the complex unit is the volume element.**) The pseudoscalar
   `omega = s_1 s_2 s_3` equals `i * I`, is central, and squares to `-I`.
   The complex structure that distinguishes the qubit `M_2(C)` from a real
   two-state system `M_2(R) = Cl(2,0)` is precisely the **volume element
   of the 3-direction frame**: a *complex* qubit requires all three
   directions to build its own `i`. (This is exactly the framework's
   recorded reading "`omega = i`, recovering the `i` of quantum mechanics
   geometrically"; see
   [MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md](MINIMAL_AXIOMS_2026-05-20.md).)

4. (**P4 — dimension uniqueness.**) `dim_R Cl(d,0) = 2^d`, while
   `dim_R M_2(C) = 8`. Since `2^d = 8` only at `d = 3`, the isomorphism
   `Cl(d,0) ≅ M_2(C)` holds for `d = 3` and **no other** `d`. The real
   two-state algebra `M_2(R) = Cl(2,0)` (`d = 2`) and the quaternionic
   `M_2(H) = Cl(4,0)` (`d = 4`, `dim_R = 16`) are the nearest neighbours,
   both excluded.

5. (**P5 — rotations.**) The even subalgebra `Cl^+(3,0) = span{I, s_1 s_2,
   s_2 s_3, s_3 s_1} ≅ H ≅ su(2)` is the Lie algebra of `Spin(3) = SU(2)`,
   the rotation group of exactly the 3-direction frame; the qubit is its
   spin-1/2 module (retained
   [PER_SITE_SU2_SPIN_HALF_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-02.md](PER_SITE_SU2_SPIN_HALF_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-02.md)).
   This `su(2) = bivectors` fact is the same lever the prior
   native-SU(2)-tightness route uses with the *empirical* weak SU(2) as
   input; here it is a corollary, not the forcing mechanism.

6. (**T — spatial-dimension forcing, conditional on (GR).**) Adopt the
   geometric-realization premise

   ```text
   (GR)  spatial directions are the orthogonal unit directions
         (the vector grade) of the local algebra A.
   ```

   Then, using the full qubit algebra (A1), all three unit directions
   `s_1, s_2, s_3` are realized (they span the traceless observables, so
   none can be omitted without losing part of `A`), and by (P2) there is
   no fourth. Therefore the spatial-dimension count is

   ```text
   d_s = 3,   forced and unique.                                   (T)
   ```

   Consequently the lattice **dimension** in A2 is not an independent
   axiom; it is determined by A1 together with the single premise (GR).

## Why this note exists

Axiom A1 names the per-site algebra as the qubit `M_2(C) ≅ Cl(3,0)`, and
axiom A2 **separately** stipulates the spatial lattice `Z^3`. The integer
`3` therefore appears **twice** and is currently treated as two
independent commitments (the algebra's `Cl(3)` and the lattice's `Z^3`).
This note proves that the two are not independent: the spatial dimension
is forced to equal the vector-grade dimension of the per-site algebra,
which is `3` because A1 is the *complex* qubit. It converts A2's dimension
stipulation into a theorem on A1, leaving only the principled
identification (GR) — the framework's own "space emerges from the qubit"
stance made precise — in place of a free integer. The framework already
carries two other dimension-forcing routes (next section); this note adds
the one that uses **only A1**, with no empirical or selector input.

## Proof

Pure finite-rank algebra on `M_2(C)`. Each step is reproduced at exact
precision by the runner.

### Step 1. (P1) The Pauli frame generates `Cl(3,0)`.

Direct computation gives `{s_a, s_b} = 2 delta_{ab} I`, each `s_a` is
self-adjoint, traceless, and `s_a^2 = I`. The unital algebra generated by
`{s_1, s_2, s_3}` is 8-real-dimensional with basis
`{I, s_1, s_2, s_3, s_1 s_2, s_2 s_3, s_3 s_1, s_1 s_2 s_3}`, i.e. all of
`M_2(C) = Cl(3,0)`.

### Step 2. (P2) No fourth orthogonal direction; `d_s <= 3`.

If `{v_a, v_b} = 0` then `v_a v_b = -v_b v_a`, so
`Tr(v_a v_b) = Tr(-v_b v_a) = -Tr(v_a v_b)`, hence `Tr(v_a v_b) = 0`:
orthogonal unit directions are trace-orthogonal. They lie in the real
vector space of traceless self-adjoint `2x2` matrices, which is spanned by
`{s_1, s_2, s_3}` and is therefore 3-dimensional. A trace-orthogonal set
in a 3-dimensional space has at most 3 members. Concretely, writing a
general `M in M_2(C)` and solving `{M, s_a} = 0` for `a = 1, 2, 3`
simultaneously gives `M = 0` (the linear map `M -> ({M,s_1},{M,s_2},
{M,s_3})` has trivial kernel, rank 4). So no nonzero element anticommutes
with the whole Pauli frame: there is no independent fourth direction.

### Step 3. (P3) The qubit's `i` is the volume element.

`s_1 s_2 = i s_3`, so `omega = s_1 s_2 s_3 = i s_3^2 = i I`. Thus
`omega^2 = -I` and `omega` commutes with every `s_a` (central). The map
`Cl(3,0) -> M_2(C)` sends the top-grade volume element to the scalar `i`.
A two-direction subalgebra `span_R{I, s_1, s_2, s_1 s_2}` is closed and
isomorphic to `M_2(R) = Cl(2,0)` (a *real* two-state algebra): its element
`s_1 s_2` squares to `-I` but is **not** central, so it does not provide
the ambient complex unit. The complex structure of `M_2(C)` is supplied
only by the third generator through `omega = i I`. A complex qubit
therefore needs all three directions.

### Step 4. (P4) Dimension uniqueness.

`dim_R Cl(d,0) = 2^d` (the `2^d` basis monomials in `d` anticommuting
generators). With `dim_R M_2(C) = 8`, the equation `2^d = 8` has the
unique solution `d = 3`. The standard real Clifford classification
(Lawson & Michelsohn, *Spin Geometry*, ch. I) places `Cl(2,0) = M_2(R)`,
`Cl(3,0) = M_2(C)`, `Cl(4,0) = M_2(H)`; only `d = 3` yields `M_2(C)`, and
the runner confirms `2^d = 8 <=> d = 3` over `d = 1, ..., 6`.

### Step 5. (P5) Rotations of the frame.

`e_{12} = s_1 s_2`, `e_{23} = s_2 s_3`, `e_{31} = s_3 s_1` each square to
`-I` and multiply as the quaternion units (`e_{12} e_{23} = -e_{31}`,
cyclically), so `Cl^+(3,0) ≅ H ≅ su(2)`. These are the infinitesimal
rotations of the 3-direction frame: `Spin(3) = SU(2)` acts on the qubit as
the spin-1/2 representation.

### Step 6. (T) Spatial-dimension forcing under (GR).

By (GR), the spatial directions are the orthogonal unit directions of `A`.
Using the full algebra `A` (A1), all three of `s_1, s_2, s_3` are realized
— omitting any one fails to span the traceless observables, hence fails to
recover `A` (Step 1, Step 3) — and by (P2) no fourth exists. Therefore the
number of spatial directions is exactly 3: `d_s = 3`, forced and unique.
The dimension in A2 is thus a consequence of A1 and (GR). QED.
