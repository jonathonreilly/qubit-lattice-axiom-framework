## Claim

Let `Λ = {x₁, …, xₙ} ⊂ Z³`, with `n ≥ 1`, be a finite set of distinct sites. A
finite-dimensional joint presentation consists of unital `*`-embeddings
`ιₖ : M₂(ℂ) → B(H)` whose images commute elementwise for distinct sites. The
joint carrier presents every site algebra faithfully and unitally, and the
elementwise commutation expresses site independence. Then:

1. The algebra generated by the images is isomorphic to
   `M₂(ℂ)^{⊗n} ≅ M_{2ⁿ}(ℂ)`.
2. Every faithful finite-dimensional representation decomposes as
   `H ≅ (⊗_{x ∈ Λ} ℂ²_x) ⊗ ℂᵐ` for an integer multiplicity `m ≥ 1`.
3. Under the named minimality selection `m = 1`, the minimal faithful joint
   carrier is `H_Λ = ⊗_{x ∈ Λ} ℂ²_x`, unique up to unitary equivalence, and the
   generated algebra acts as all of `B(H_Λ)`.
4. `dim H_Λ = 2ⁿ`. Thus `n ≥ 2` gives `dim H_Λ ≥ 4 ≥ 3`, so every multi-site
   finite region meets the dimension hypothesis of the standard Gleason
   theorem. For `n = 1`, `dim H_Λ = 2 < 3`, so the projection-valued Gleason
   route does not apply; the separate Busch/POVM companion treats that case.
5. For `n ≥ 2`, the real span of the orthogonal projections on `H_Λ` is the
   full real vector space of Hermitian operators, of dimension `d²` for
   `d = 2ⁿ`. Consequently, if a finitely additive probability measure already
   has trace form `m(P) = Tr(σP)`, its values on projections determine `σ`
   uniquely.

## Derivation

Write `Aₖ = M₂(ℂ)` for the algebra at `xₖ`. Pairwise elementwise commutation of
the images makes

`Φ(a₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ aₙ) = ι₁(a₁) ⋯ ιₙ(aₙ)`

a well-defined unital `*`-homomorphism from `A₁ ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ Aₙ` onto the algebra
generated by the images. The source is the simple matrix algebra
`M₂(ℂ)^{⊗n} ≅ M_{2ⁿ}(ℂ)`. Because `Φ` is unital, it is nonzero; simplicity
therefore makes its kernel zero. This proves the generated-algebra
identification rather than assuming it from a dimension target.

Concretely, tensor words in `{I, σₓ, σᵧ, σ_z}` give `4ⁿ` Pauli strings. Their
Hilbert–Schmidt products satisfy
`Tr(Sᵢ†Sⱼ) = 2ⁿ δᵢⱼ`, so they are linearly independent. Since
`4ⁿ = dim_ℂ M_{2ⁿ}(ℂ)`, the canonical tensor action fills the entire matrix
algebra. The runner verifies these identities exactly for `n = 2` and `n = 3`
and separately rejects same-factor generators that violate the commutation
premise.

The finite type-I factor representation theorem now applies to the faithful
representation of `M_{2ⁿ}(ℂ)` on `H`. Matrix units split `H` into `2ⁿ`
equivalent subspaces, giving `H ≅ ℂ^{2ⁿ} ⊗ ℂᵐ` and the action
`M_{2ⁿ}(ℂ) ⊗ I_m` for some integer `m ≥ 1`. Identifying
`ℂ^{2ⁿ} ≅ ⊗_{x ∈ Λ} ℂ²_x` gives the asserted multiplicity decomposition. Site
reorderings act by tensor-factor permutation unitaries, so the carrier is
independent, up to unitary equivalence, of the displayed enumeration of `Λ`.
The named selection below chooses `m = 1`; then the commutant is scalar and the
generated algebra is all of `B(H_Λ)`.

Finally, in dimension `d`, the rank-one projections onto `e_r`, `e_r + e_s`,
and `e_r + i e_s` for `r < s` recover respectively the diagonal, real
off-diagonal, and imaginary off-diagonal Hermitian directions. Their real span
therefore has dimension `d²`. The trace pairing on Hermitian matrices is
nondegenerate, so equality of `Tr(σP)` on all projections forces equality of
the Hermitian matrices `σ`. This is a conditional uniqueness fact about a
given trace form, not a derivation of probability weights.
