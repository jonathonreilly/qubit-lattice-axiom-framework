## 2. Complete joint-simulation theorem

Define
\[
 Z=Z_1Z_2,\qquad
 Q=X_1X_2Y_aY_b,\qquad
 H_{\mathrm{eff}}=K-\lambda^2Q,\qquad
 c=2\lambda+\lambda^2 .
 \tag{5}
\]
The four-body \(Q\) appears in the comparison generator; it is not a physically installed four-body block. On the full sixteen-dimensional gadget Hilbert space, for every \(t\in\mathbb R\),
\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|e^{-itH}-e^{-ict}e^{-itH_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right\|
 =\frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}
        \left|\sin[(1+\lambda^2)t]\right|
 \le\frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}<2\lambda .}
 \tag{6}
\]
This is an exact error formula and a uniform-in-time approximation, not equality of the two undressed propagators. It includes their one specified common phase. The scalar \(2I\) in K remains inside \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}\); c is additional and counted once.

Tensoring any passive identity preserves (6). For every pure joint input it bounds the complete vector error. For any mixed joint input, including arbitrary old/ancilla/reference correlations, the output trace-norm difference is at most twice that error, capped at two. The same upper bound applies to the unhalved diamond distance between the joint unitary channels. All ancillary outputs are retained.

For \(0<\epsilon\le1\), choosing
\[
 0<\lambda\le\min\{\epsilon/2,\lambda_{\mathrm{cap}}\}
 \tag{7}
\]
gives complete vector error \(<\epsilon\) for all real times. The coefficient and cap conditions are explicit; (7) is a sufficient family, not an optimized cost or fabrication-precision claim.

## 3. Exact sectors and proof of the all-time formula

Let
\[
 T_1=Y_1Y_a,\qquad T_2=Y_2Y_b,\qquad
 V=\lambda(T_1+T_2),\qquad R=ZQ .
 \tag{8}
\]
\(T_1,T_2\) are commuting selfadjoint involutions. Each anticommutes with Z and with Q, while \([Z,Q]=0\). The Pauli signs give
\[
 ZT_1T_2=(Z_1Y_1)(Z_2Y_2)Y_aY_b=-Q,
 \quad R=-T_1T_2,\quad
 V^2=2\lambda^2(I-R).
 \tag{9}
\]
Therefore R commutes with Z and V. It is a nonidentity Pauli string, so its two eigenspaces have dimension eight. Q itself is not conserved by the actual V and cannot replace R as the sector label.

The physical Hamiltonian is
\[
 H=(2+c)I+A,\qquad A=(1-\lambda^2)Z+V.
 \tag{10}
\]
Since \(\{Z,V\}=0\),
\[
 A^2=(1+\lambda^4)I-2\lambda^2R=(Z-\lambda^2Q)^2 .
 \tag{11}
\]
Equal squares alone would not establish equal spectral signs or propagators. The R sectors resolve those obligations directly. With \(P_\pm=(I\pm R)/2\), the identity \(T_2=-rT_1\) on \(R=r\) yields:

| Sector | Actual nonscalar A | Comparison \(Z-\lambda^2Q\) |
|---|---|---|
| \(R=+1\) | \((1-\lambda^2)Z\), since \(V=0\) | \((1-\lambda^2)Z\) |
| \(R=-1\) | \((1-\lambda^2)Z+2\lambda X'\) | \((1+\lambda^2)Z\) |

Here \(X'=V/(2\lambda)=T_1\) on \(P_-\), so \(X'^2=Z^2=I\) and \(\{Z,X'\}=0\). Put \(\Omega=1+\lambda^2\). On \(P_-\) the actual axis has exactly this frequency:
\[
 \left[(1-\lambda^2)Z+2\lambda X'\right]^2=\Omega^2I .
 \tag{12}
\]
The two cosine terms in the propagators cancel, leaving
\[
 e^{-itA}-e^{-it\Omega Z}
 =-i\sin(\Omega t)
       \left[-\frac{2\lambda^2}{\Omega}Z
                     +\frac{2\lambda}{\Omega}X'\right].
 \tag{13}
\]
For real a,b, \((aZ+bX')^2=(a^2+b^2)I\). Thus the norm of (13) is
\[
 \frac{2\lambda}{\sqrt{1+\lambda^2}}|\sin(\Omega t)|.
 \tag{14}
\]
The other sector contributes zero and \(P_-\) is nonempty, so (14) is the exact full norm. Restoring the common physical scalar proves (6). No \(\lambda^4t\) remainder, adiabatic assumption or long-time expansion is being suppressed.

## 4. Mediated timescale, counterterm and product-input output

\[

\]

The supplied counterterm is part of the exact statement. Without \(h_c\), the nonscalar generator is \(Z+V\); its exactly rotated frequencies are described by
\[

\]
The small-\(\lambda\) expansion identifies \(Z+\lambda^2Z-\lambda^2Q+O(\lambda^4)\). The extra \(\lambda^2Z\) is not a common scalar on arbitrary old inputs, and it accumulates an order-one angle at time proportional to \(\lambda^{-2}\). The complete \(Y_aY_b\), including its pair-creation and pair-annihilation action, is essential. For the real product ancillary seed,
\[

For \(\xi=|00\rangle_{12}\) and \(\varphi=\theta\), the target has two nonzero old/ancilla Schmidt coefficients \(3/5,4/5\). Local old aging and the common phase do not change them. The real state \((|00\rangle-|11\rangle)/\sqrt2\) is one such eigenstate and is entangled. Product eigenstates require local Y-eigenstate coherences, not real computational-basis product preparation. Equation (19) is an additional input condition, not a preparation result of this device.

## 5. Unequal couplings and finite-time counterterm mistuning

Retain the same four sites, K and Q. For \(0<u,v<1\), put
\[
 a_0=\sqrt{(1-u^2)(1-v^2)},\qquad
 \kappa=uv,\qquad c_{uv}=u+v+1-a_0,
 \tag{U1}
\]
and install
\[
 K,\qquad (1-a_0)(I-Z),\qquad
 u(I+Y_1Y_a),\qquad v(I+Y_2Y_b).
 \tag{U2}
\]
There are still four positive real nearest-neighbor blocks on the same three edges. Since \(0<a_0<1\), the counterterm is positive. The old-site loads are respectively
\[
 3+2(1-a_0)+2u,\qquad 3+2(1-a_0)+2v,
 \tag{U3}
\]
and the ancillary loads are \(2u,2v\). In particular
\[
 3+2(1-a_0)+2\max(u,v)\le\Lambda
 \tag{U4}
\]
is sufficient. The function \(a_0\) uses the actual u,v; it is not automatic when all three coefficients are independently unknown.

Writing \(V_{uv}=uT_1+vT_2\), \(A_{uv}=a_0Z+V_{uv}\), the actual Hamiltonian is \(H_{uv}=(2+c_{uv})I+A_{uv}\). The same conserved \(R=-T_1T_2\) gives
\[
 V_{uv}^2=(u^2+v^2)I-2uvR,\qquad
 A_{uv}^2=(1+\kappa^2)I-2\kappa R=(Z-\kappa Q)^2.
 \tag{U5}
\]
The calibrated identity is \(a_0^2+u^2+v^2=1+u^2v^2\).

On \(R=r\), define
\[
 \Omega_r=1-r\kappa>0,\qquad
 \nu_r=|u-rv|,\qquad
 a_0^2+\nu_r^2=\Omega_r^2.
 \tag{U6}
\]
If \(\nu_r>0\), \(X'_r=V_{uv}/\nu_r\) is a real involution on that sector and anticommutes with Z. The actual nonscalar generator is \(a_0Z+\nu_rX'_r\), whereas the comparison is \(\Omega_rZ\). If \(\nu_r=0\), positivity of \(a_0,\Omega_r\) makes them equal. This checks spectral signs as well as squares.

Their cosine terms cancel. Squaring the sine-coefficient difference gives \([2-2a_0/\Omega_r]I\), so the exact complete error for every real time is
\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|e^{-itH_{uv}}-e^{-ic_{uv}t}e^{-it(K-\kappa Q)}\right\|
 =\max_{r=\pm1}d_r|\sin(\Omega_rt)|,\qquad
 d_r=\sqrt{2-\frac{2a_0}{\Omega_r}} .}
 \tag{U7}
\]
Both sectors are nonempty, and the zero-\(\nu_r\) case has \(d_r=0\). Since \(\Omega_-\ge\Omega_+\), \(d_-\ge d_+\); thus \(d_-\) bounds the error uniformly without requiring simultaneous sine maxima. For \(u=v=\lambda\), \(a_0=1-\lambda^2\), \(d_+=0\), and (U7) reduces exactly to (6).

For \(0<u,v\le1/2\), \(a_0\ge3/4\), and
\[
 d_r^2=\frac{2\nu_r^2}{\Omega_r(\Omega_r+a_0)}
 \le\frac{\nu_r^2}{a_0^2},\qquad
 d_-\le\frac{u+v}{a_0}\le\frac43(u+v).
 \tag{U8}
\]
At \(T=\varphi/(uv)\), the comparator is
\[
 e^{-ic_{uv}T}e^{-iTK}e^{i\varphi Q}.
 \tag{U9}
\]
The sum of the small couplings controls this sufficient error, while their product sets the mediated time. If one coupling is much smaller than the other, T can be arbitrarily large under a fixed upper bound on their sum.

Now replace the counterterm by
\[
 (1-a_0+\delta)(I-Z),\qquad
 \delta\in\mathbb R,\quad 1-a_0+\delta\ge0 .
 \tag{U10}
\]
Then \(H_{uv,\delta}=(2+c_{uv}+\delta)I+A_{uv}-\delta Z\). After retaining the actual scalar, Duhamel on \(-\delta Z\) gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\left\|e^{-itH_{uv,\delta}}
       -e^{-i(c_{uv}+\delta)t}e^{-it(K-\kappa Q)}\right\|\\
 &\hspace{1cm}\le
       \max_r d_r|\sin(\Omega_rt)|+|\delta|\,|t|.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{U11}
\]
No exact frequency matching is asserted at nonzero mistuning. If the comparator instead insists on nominal scalar \(c_{uv}\), another \(|\delta|\,|t|\) is a safe phase-error charge. Unknown \(\delta\) does not establish a known nominal-phase guarantee.

For example, for \(T_{\max}>0\) and \(0<\epsilon\le1\),
\[
 u,v\le\frac12,\qquad u+v\le\frac{3\epsilon}{8},
 \qquad |\delta|\le\frac{\epsilon}{2T_{\max}}
 \tag{U12}
\]
give vector error at most \(\epsilon\) on \(|t|\le T_{\max}\), provided positivity and the actual cap also hold. At \(T_{\max}=\varphi/(uv)\) with \(\varphi>0\), the condition is \(|\delta|\le\epsilon uv/(2\varphi)\). The old loads must now use \(2(1-a_0+\delta)\), not their nominal counterterm value. This is a sufficient finite-time tolerance, not a necessary precision or fabrication result.

The effective operator remains the same complete Q. Thus (18), including the product-seed limitation, holds with time \(\varphi/(uv)\). Unequal couplings do not provide common ancillary outputs or eigenstate preparation.

## 6. Exact dressing and bounded-background interfaces

The same algebra supplies an explicit proof transformation. Let
\[
 \alpha=\arctan\lambda,\qquad
 G=\frac{ZV}{2\lambda},\qquad
 D=P_++\cos\alpha\,P_- -\sin\alpha\,G .
 \tag{20}
\]
The full-space identities are
\[
 G^\dagger=-G,\qquad G^2=-P_-,\qquad GP_+=0 .
 \tag{21}
\]
All entries of G are real, so D is real orthogonal. On \(P_-\), \(G=ZX'\), and direct conjugation gives
\[
 DZD^\dagger=\cos(2\alpha)Z+\sin(2\alpha)X'.
 \tag{22}
\]
Using the two R sectors and the half-angle formulas,
\[
 H=cI+D H_{\mathrm{eff}}D^\dagger,\qquad
 d:=\|D-I\|=2\sin(\alpha/2)\le\alpha\le\lambda .
 \tag{23}
\]
D is a proof transformation; it is not an additional installed gate or a new physical supplier.

Extend the gadget by any declared finite system \(\mathcal S\), taking D and the base Hamiltonians tensored with \(I_{\mathcal S}\). Let B be any bounded selfadjoint operator on this complete joint space; it may couple the added system to the gadget and need not commute with any sector label. With \(B'=D^\dagger BD\),
\[
 H+B=cI+D(H_{\mathrm{eff}}+B')D^\dagger,\qquad
 \|B'-B\|\le2d\|B\| .
 \tag{24}
\]
The inequality follows by expanding
\[
 B'-B=D^\dagger B(D-I)+(D^\dagger-I)B .
 \tag{25}
\]
Duhamel bounds the change from \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}+B'\) to \(H_{\mathrm{eff}}+B\) by \(2d|t|\|B\|\). The two outer D factors cost at most another \(2d\). Hence, for every real t,
\[
 \boxed{
 \left\|e^{-it(H+B)}
       -e^{-ict}e^{-it(H_{\mathrm{eff}}+B)}\right\|
 \le2d(1+|t|\|B\|)
 \le2\lambda(1+|t|\|B\|).}
 \tag{26}
\]
The bound may be capped at two. It is valid on the entire physical space and every passive extension, including arbitrary initial correlations. For mixed outputs or the joint unitary channels, twice the capped operator bound is an upper bound, itself capped at two.

The target retains B exactly, including its scalar and old-energy contributions. There is no assumption that \(e^{-it(H_{\mathrm{eff}}+B)}\) factors into separate evolutions. In particular, the background-free factorization (15) is not asserted for noncommuting B. No R-sector invariance is needed after the background is added.

This background estimate is less sharp than (6) at \(B=0\), and it is not an exact norm formula. If
\[
 B=\lambda^2B_0,\qquad \|B_0\|\le C_B,\qquad
 |t|\le A/\lambda^2,\qquad A\ge0,
 \tag{27}
\]
with a stated uniform bound \(C_B\), then (26) gives
\[
 \text{complete operator/vector error}
 \le2\lambda(1+A C_B).
 \tag{28}
\]
Thus \(0<\lambda<1\) and
\[
 \lambda\le\frac{\epsilon}{2(1+A C_B)}
 \tag{29}
\]
are sufficient for vector tolerance \(\epsilon\) on this horizon, together with the actual hardware cap. \(B_0\) may depend on \(\lambda\) only if the stated bound \(C_B\) is uniform over the family.

An extensive sum of locally bounded terms is not automatically bounded by a volume-independent \(C_B\). For a fixed nonzero \(\|B\|\) at times proportional to \(\lambda^{-2}\), the uncapped bound in (26) can instead grow as \(\lambda^{-1}\); it then does not certify small error. This is a limitation of that estimate, not a lower bound on actual dynamics.

## 7. Charging physical background blocks

Equation (26) is a mathematical selfadjoint-background theorem. An arbitrary B need not be positive, real, local or two-body, so that theorem alone does not preserve the hardware class of (1).

To retain a real positive nearest-neighbor two-body device, separately supply an inventory. Using \(\kappa=uv\), with \(u=v=\lambda\) in the equal case, write
\[
 B=\kappa\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}b_\ell ,
 \tag{30}
\]
where each \(b_\ell\) is a positive real block on a listed allowed support of at most two ordinary qubits. The physical system then has four base blocks plus m additional blocks, and includes any new qubits and edges required by that inventory. They are not hidden in the original four-qubit count.

Writing
\[
 C_{\mathrm{sum}}=\sum_\ell\|b_\ell\|,\qquad
 L_B(x)=\sum_{\ell:\,x\in\operatorname{supp}b_\ell}\|b_\ell\|,
 \tag{31}
\]
one may use \(C_B=C_{\mathrm{sum}}\). The full-support incident loads to check are
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c}
 \text{site}&\text{incident norm sum}\\ \hline
 1&3+2(1-a_0)+2u+\kappa L_B(1)\\
 2&3+2(1-a_0)+2v+\kappa L_B(2)\\
 a&2u+\kappa L_B(a)\\
 b&2v+\kappa L_B(b)\\
 \text{new site }x&\kappa L_B(x).
 \end{array}
 \tag{32}
\]
Every row must be at most the selected cap. For equal couplings, \(a_0=1-\lambda^2\) and \(\kappa=\lambda^2\), so these reduce to the base loads (2) plus the addendum's \(\lambda^2L_B\) charge. For a fixed finite extra inventory with bounded \(C_{\mathrm{sum}}\) and \(L_B(x)\), sufficiently small equal couplings can meet both (29) and any \(\Lambda>3\). The unequal case must satisfy its corresponding error bound and all displayed actual loads. A growing inventory requires its actual norm and load bounds; small coefficients alone do not waive them.

The conjugated \(B'\) in (24) is only an analytical operator. No implementation of \(B'\) or D is used to avoid paying for B. Likewise, several gadgets sharing old qubits cannot be assembled by adding separate error lines or counting K and its counterterm afresh for each gadget. Their combined cross terms, coefficients, target and physical loads need a separate argument.

## 8. Meaning and limits of the milestone

The result in §§1–7 is a static four-qubit realization of one effective \(X_1X_2\)-conditioned ancillary-pair interaction, with exact all-time complete-error formulas and bounded background and mistuning interfaces. The calibrated positive counterterm exploits the fixed literal old gap. Equal physical couplings give the simplest formula; unequal couplings remain covered with their explicit nonlinear calibration. Absence of a secular remainder in the exactly calibrated base cases follows from the sector algebra.

No ancillary preparation is required to simulate the joint effective Hamiltonian on all inputs. That statement does not provide a common ancillary output for an old-only gate. Equation (18) is the actual complete comparison target from the real product seed, and (19) explicitly identifies the additional eigenstate condition that would change the target.

The four-qubit theorem does not realize arbitrary clock matrices, supply a clock-selected common output, prove a W-operation dwell interval, or derive physical preparation, capture, release, reset, reuse or permanent halt. It supplies no native derivation of the blocks, missing couplings, coefficients or time units. One K block is retained, while the added counterterm changes the standalone old-only Hamiltonian; the original source/contact apparatus is not present. Any further operation must use the actual combined Hamiltonian and retain all outputs and phases.
