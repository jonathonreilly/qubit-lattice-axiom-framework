## Admitted-context inputs

- **Complex conjugation of an SU(3) representation.** For any unitary
  representation `R : su(3) → End(V)`, the complex-conjugate
  representation `R̄ : su(3) → End(V̄)` is defined by
  `R̄(X) := -R(X)^*` for `X ∈ su(3)` (where `*` denotes componentwise
  complex conjugation of the matrix). The minus sign is the standard
  convention that preserves the Lie-bracket: `R̄([X, Y]) = [R̄(X), R̄(Y)]`.
  Standard finite-dim Lie-algebra fact.
- **Cubic anomaly index `A(R)`.** For a unitary representation `R` of
  `su(N)` with generators `T^a_R := R(t^a)` (where `{t^a}` is the
  abstract Lie-algebra basis and `T^a := λ^a/2` is the fundamental
  Gell-Mann realization), the cubic anomaly index is defined by the
  symmetric trace
  `Tr_R[T^a_R · {T^b_R, T^c_R}] = (1/2) · A(R) · d^{abc}`,
  where `d^{abc}` is the symmetric structure tensor of the fundamental
  cited from `su3_dabc_symmetric_theorem_note_2026-05-02` (and the
  `d^{abc}` definition cited there is `d^{abc} := 2 · Tr[{T^a, T^b} T^c]`
  on the fundamental, giving `d^{118} = 1/sqrt(3)`). The normalization
  `A(3) = 1` for `R` = fundamental of SU(3) is the standard convention
  (Peskin-Schroeder eq. 19.34; Slansky 1981 Table 22; Cvitanović). This
  is a definition, not a derivation.
- **Linearity and trace cyclicity.** Linearity of trace and the cyclic
  invariance `Tr[ABC] = Tr[CAB] = Tr[BCA]` for finite-dimensional
  matrices. Standard.

All three are pure mathematical / algebraic facts; no admitted physics
conventions or empirical inputs.

## Proof

### Step 1 — Conjugate-rep Hermiticity (M1)

The complex conjugate of a Hermitian matrix is Hermitian:
`(M^*)^† = (M^†)^* = M^*` for Hermitian `M = M^†`. Hence `(T^a_R)^*` is
Hermitian, and so is `-(T^a_R)^*`. By definition `(C)`,
`T^a_{R̄} = -(T^a_R)^*` is Hermitian on the carrier of `R̄`.

To check that `T^a_{R̄}` satisfies the SU(3) bracket `(F)`: compute

```text
    [T^a_{R̄}, T^b_{R̄}]  =  [-(T^a_R)^*, -(T^b_R)^*]
                       =  (T^a_R)^* (T^b_R)^* - (T^b_R)^* (T^a_R)^*
                       =  (T^a_R T^b_R - T^b_R T^a_R)^*
                       =  ([T^a_R, T^b_R])^*
                       =  (i f^{abc} T^c_R)^*
                       =  -i f^{abc} (T^c_R)^*               (f real ⇒ f^* = f)
                       =  -i f^{abc} (-T^c_{R̄})              (by (C))
                       =  i f^{abc} T^c_{R̄}.
```

Hence `[T^a_{R̄}, T^b_{R̄}] = i f^{abc} T^c_{R̄}`: the bracket `(F)` is
preserved on `R̄`. The corresponding statement for the anticommutator
`(D)` follows from the same complex-conjugate reasoning, with the
even-rank product preserving rank under the conjugate-rep sign flip.
The decisive odd-rank statement for the cubic anomaly trace is the
content of Step 2.

### Step 2 — Sign-flip identity (M2)

Insert `(C)` directly into `(A)` for `R̄`. The product
`T^a_{R̄} · T^b_{R̄} · T^c_{R̄} = -(T^a_R)^* (T^b_R)^* (T^c_R)^*`
carries one overall minus sign (odd number of generator factors).
The anticommutator `{T^b_{R̄}, T^c_{R̄}}` is then componentwise the
conjugate of `{T^b_R, T^c_R}` times `(-1)^2 = +1`, so

```text
    Tr_{R̄}[T^a_{R̄} · {T^b_{R̄}, T^c_{R̄}}]
      =  Tr_{R̄}[-(T^a_R)^* · ({T^b_R, T^c_R})^*]
      =  -(Tr_R[T^a_R · {T^b_R, T^c_R}])^*       (trace commutes with *)
      =  -((1/2) · A(R) · d^{abc})^*             (by (A) for R)
      =  -(1/2) · A(R) · d^{abc}.                (A(R), d^{abc} real)
```

So `(1/2) · A(R̄) · d^{abc} = -(1/2) · A(R) · d^{abc}` for every
triple `(a, b, c)`. The cited `d^{abc}` is not identically zero on
SU(3) (SU3_DABC_SYMMETRIC reference D4: e.g. `d^{118} = 1/√3 ≠ 0`).
Dividing by `(1/2) · d^{118} ≠ 0` gives `(★) A(R̄) = -A(R)`.

The **parity origin** of `(★)` is transparent: the cubic anomaly
trace involves an odd number (three) of factors `T^*_R = -T_{R̄}`,
so the overall sign flip is `(-1)^3 = -1`. ∎

### Step 3 — Specialization at `R = 3` (M3)

The fundamental of SU(3) (the cited Gell-Mann carrier) has
`A(3) = +1` by the standard normalization. Applying `(★)`:

```text
    A(3̄)   =   -A(3)   =   -1.
```

This is an exact rational identity at integer level. ∎

### Step 4 — Two-fermion contribution (M4)

For two left-handed Weyl fermions, each in `R̄ = 3̄`, the SU(3)^3
cubic anomaly trace sums by linearity:

```text
    Σ_{i=1}^{2} A(R̄_i) · d^{abc}   =   2 · A(3̄) · d^{abc}
                                      =   2 · (-1) · d^{abc}
                                      =   -2 · d^{abc}.
```

Hence `2 · A(3̄) = -2` as a rational identity. ∎
