operator algebra itself say about the unitary versus antiunitary realization of
axis permutations?

## Answer

On the fixed complex Pauli/qubit representation of the one-qubit operator
algebra `M_2(C) ~= Cl(3,0)`, the spatial volume form
`ω = e_1 e_2 e_3` equals `i·I` — the orientation of the three spatial axes *is*
the qubit's complex unit. An axis permutation `σ` conjugates `ω` by `sgn(σ)`.
Unitary (`C`-linear) symmetries preserve `i·I`, so they realize only the
`sgn = +1` permutations, `A_3 = C_3`; the orientation-reversing transpositions
(`sgn = −1`) send `i → −i` and cannot be realized as complex-linear unitaries
on this fixed generator triple.

Thus `C_3` is the unitary axis-permutation subgroup, and odd permutations are
orientation-reversing/antiunitary-type operations in the fixed complex
representation. Turning this algebraic split into the physical `S_3 → C_3`
selector still requires the separate physics premise that the relevant
generation-sector symmetry must be unitary rather than antiunitary. This note
does not supply that premise.

## Theorem

Let `e_1, e_2, e_3` be the `Cl(3,0)` generators on the qubit (`e_i^2 = I`,
mutually anticommuting), realized as the Pauli matrices on `C^2`.

**(1) Volume form is the complex unit.** `ω := e_1 e_2 e_3 = i·I`, with
`ω^2 = −I`, and `ω` is central.

**(2) Permutations conjugate `ω` by sign.** For `σ ∈ S_3`,
`e_{σ(1)} e_{σ(2)} e_{σ(3)} = sgn(σ)·ω`. The three transpositions give `−ω`; the
identity and two 3-cycles give `+ω`.

**(3) C_3 is unitary; transpositions are not complex-linear unitary on the fixed
generator triple.** There is an explicit
unitary `U` (the `120°` rotation about `(1,1,1)/√3`) with `U e_i U† = e_{i+1}`,
and `U ω U† = ω` (it preserves `i·I`). Conversely, a unitary `V` with
`V e_i V† = e_{τ(i)}` for a transposition `τ` would give
`V ω V† = sgn(τ) ω = −ω`, while `V (i·I) V† = i·I`; hence `i·I = −i·I`, a
contradiction. So no unitary realizes a transposition; its realization is
necessarily conjugate-linear/orientation-reversing at this fixed complex
structure.

**Conclusion.** The one-qubit operator algebra gives an exact
unitary/antiunitary split: even axis permutations preserve `ω = i·I` and include
the explicit unitary 3-cycle, while odd axis permutations reverse `ω` and cannot
be implemented by complex-linear unitaries on the fixed generator triple. ∎

## What this does NOT claim

- Does **not** fix *which* orientation (the overall sign of `i`) — that remains a
  labeling convention. What is algebraically invariant is that
  transpositions reverse the complex volume form and `C_3` preserves it
  unitarily.
- Does **not** derive the generation mass values, ordering, or labeling.
- Does **not** derive the chiral matter content or lift the `S_3` degeneracy by
  itself.
- Does **not** claim antiunitary transformations are never physical symmetries.
  The extra step "generation-sector physical symmetries must be unitary" remains
