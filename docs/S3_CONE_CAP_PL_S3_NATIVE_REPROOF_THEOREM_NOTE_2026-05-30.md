## Setup and definitions

All objects reuse the single-source-of-truth primitives in
[`scripts/frontier_s3_boundary_link_theorem.py`](./../scripts/frontier_s3_boundary_link_theorem.py)
(`cubical_ball`, `classify_vertices`, `analyze_2complex`).

**Cubical ball.** `B_R` is the union of all unit cubes whose 8 corners lie
within Euclidean distance `R` of the origin (`cubical_ball(R)`).

**Kuhn / Freudenthal triangulation.** Each unit cube of `B_R` is split into 6
simplices, one per axis-permutation `pi`: the monotone lattice path
`c -> c + e_{pi(0)} -> c + e_{pi(0)} + e_{pi(1)} -> c + (1,1,1)`. This
subdivision is **globally coherent**: on any shared square face it places the
same min-to-max diagonal (fixed by the global lattice order, identical from
either incident cube). So adjacent cubes triangulate face-coherently with no
diagonal-convention choice, and the **same** simplicial `boundary(B_R)` is the
object that is coned and glued. Call the triangulated ball `K(B_R)`.

**Cone-cap (simplicial join).** `boundary(B_R)` is the set of triangles in
exactly one tetrahedron of `K(B_R)`. With a single new apex vertex `a`, the cap
is the simplicial join `a * boundary(B_R)`: its tetrahedra are
`{a} u t` for every boundary triangle `t`.

**Glued complex.** `M_R = K(B_R) u (a * boundary(B_R))`, the union of the two
along their common `boundary(B_R)`.

**Recognizers.** `analyze_2complex` is the canonical combinatorial recognizer
(verdict `S^2`: closed, connected, `chi = 2`, orientable, every vertex link a
cycle; verdict `disk`: connected, `chi = 1`, one boundary circle, integer
`H_1 = 0` via Smith Normal Form, every vertex link a 1-sphere or 1-arc). For
the large whole-surface `S^2` checks the runner uses a fast closed-surface
`S^2` recognizer that tests the **same** `S^2` conditions
(closed + connected + `chi = 2` + orientable + every vertex link a cycle)
without computing the integer-`H_1` Smith Normal Form that the `S^2` verdict
never reads; it is cross-checked equal to `analyze_2complex`'s `S^2` verdict on
`boundary(B_R)` for `R = 2, 3, 4`. The per-vertex **disk** verdicts keep the
full `analyze_2complex` recognizer.

## The three native reproofs

### Fact (1): `B_R` is a PL 3-ball (star-shaped => collapsible; not Schoenflies)

**Star-shaped from the origin.** The present-cube set of `B_R` is a downset
under the per-axis "step one unit toward the origin" order: pulling any present
cube one lattice step toward 0 along any axis weakly decreases every corner's
absolute coordinate, hence (the membership predicate is a sum of per-axis
monotone-away-from-0 penalties) leaves the cube present. The runner verifies
this directly: **0 toward-origin-axis-step violations** for every checked `R`
(e.g. `R = 10`, 3,280 cubes, 0 violations). A union of cubes whose present set
is a toward-origin downset is star-shaped from the origin.

**Collapsible.** The load-bearing certificate is an **explicit elementary
free-face collapse**: the runner repeatedly removes a free face (a face
contained in exactly one strictly larger cell) together with that cell, and
verifies `K(B_R)` reduces to a **single vertex** (`R = 2, 3, 4`). A complex
that elementary-collapses to a point is collapsible. (The star-shapedness is
the geometric reason such a collapse exists; the runner exhibits the collapse
itself rather than relying on a discrete-Morse field.)

**PL 3-manifold-with-boundary.** The runner verifies, via the vertex-link
recognizer, that `K(B_R)` has no triangle in more than two tetrahedra, every
interior-vertex link is a combinatorial `S^2`, and every boundary-vertex link
is a 2-disk (`R = 2, 3, 4`); and `chi(K(B_R)) = 1`. So `K(B_R)` is a compact PL
3-manifold-with-boundary.

A collapsible compact PL 3-manifold-with-boundary is a PL 3-ball (the cited
comparator: J.H.C. Whitehead; cf. Rourke and Sanderson, *Introduction to
Piecewise-Linear Topology*). The argument is **forward**: it collapses `B_R`
directly from its interior star-shapedness and **never references
`boundary(B_R)`'s sphericity**, so it does **not consume the hard PL
Schoenflies** direction (which is what "`boundary(B_R)` is a PL 2-sphere
=> `B_R` is a PL 3-ball" would require, and which the all-R note flagged as
unavailable for non-convex `B_R`, `R >= 3`).

### Fact (2): `cone(boundary B_R)` is a PL 3-ball (simplicial join)

The cap is the simplicial join `a * boundary(B_R)`. The runner reproves the
join link identities on the explicit complex:

- **Apex link.** `lk(a, a * K) = K`: the apex link equals `boundary(B_R)`
  (exact set equality), a combinatorial `S^2`.
- **Base-vertex links.** `lk(v, a * K) = a * lk(v, K)`: every base-vertex link
  is the cone over its boundary circle `lk(v, boundary(B_R))`, hence a 2-disk;
  the runner verifies every base-vertex link is a `disk` and that the apex
  appears in every cone tetrahedron at `v` (the cone-on-link structure).
- **Boundary of the cone.** `boundary(a * K) = K`: the cone's degree-one faces
  are exactly `boundary(B_R)` -- the gluing 2-sphere is produced by
  construction.
- **Collapsible.** The runner exhibits two collapses of the cone to a single
  vertex: the **canonical** decreasing-dimension apex matching
  (each base face `s` pairs with `s u {a}`; collapsing in decreasing dimension
  of `s` lands deterministically on the **apex**), and a generic free-face
  collapse (lands on a single vertex). `chi(cone) = 1`.

A collapsible compact PL complex whose interior vertex links are 2-disks and
whose boundary is the `S^2` base is a PL 3-ball -- the cone on a PL 2-sphere is
a PL 3-ball (cited comparator: Newman; the Rourke and Sanderson cone lemma).
The cap is a ball **by construction** (it is literally a cone), so the hard
"an arbitrary embedded 2-sphere bounds a ball" direction is not used.

### Fact (3): `M_R` is a combinatorial 3-sphere (union of two PL 3-balls)

`M_R` is the union of two PL 3-balls -- `K(B_R)` (Fact 1) and the cone-cap
(Fact 2) -- glued along their common boundary PL 2-sphere `boundary(B_R)`. The
runner verifies the gluing is along the **entire** common boundary 2-sphere
(cap base `==` `boundary(B_R)` `==` apex link, exact set equality), and then
verifies the **combinatorial-3-sphere criterion** on the explicit glued
complex:

- **(C-pm) Pseudomanifold.** Every 2-face (triangle) of `M_R` is in exactly two
  tetrahedra (pure 3-pseudomanifold; closed, no branching). The interface is
  where the two balls' boundaries cancel: each boundary triangle has degree 1
  in `K(B_R)` and degree 1 in the cone, summing to 2.
- **(C-link) Every vertex link is a combinatorial 2-sphere.** The three vertex
  classes resolve as predicted: interior vertices of `B_R` -> octahedral `S^2`;
  boundary vertices of `B_R` -> their `B_R` 2-disk link glued to the cone disk
  over its boundary circle = `S^2` (disk + disk along a common circle = sphere,
  reproven, not asserted); the apex -> `boundary(B_R)` = `S^2`.
- **(C-sc) Strong connectivity.** The dual graph on tetrahedra (adjacent iff
  sharing a triangle) is connected.
- **(C-euler) `chi(M_R) = 0`.** The Euler characteristic of `S^3`. This is a
  **cross-check only**, not sufficient alone: a 3-torus also has `chi = 0`, so
  the manifold content is carried by (C-pm), (C-link), and (C-sc).

A finite pure strongly-connected 3-pseudomanifold with every vertex link a
combinatorial 2-sphere is a closed combinatorial 3-manifold; combined with the
explicit **by-construction** decomposition into two PL 3-balls glued along
their common boundary PL 2-sphere, it is a combinatorial 3-sphere. The `S^3`
conclusion comes from the **union of two PL 3-balls** reading -- each ball
carried here by its own executed collapse and link witness (Facts 1 and 2) --
**not** from a "closed 3-manifold => `S^3`" inference (which would only give a
manifold and would invite a Poincare-consumption objection). The cone
construction gives `S^3` directly.

Cited comparators (none consumed): Newman (1926, the union of two PL `n`-balls
along their common boundary PL `(n-1)`-sphere is a PL `n`-sphere; the cone on a
PL `(n-1)`-sphere is a PL `n`-ball); Alexander (1924); Moise (TOP = PL in
dimension 3); Perelman (PL Poincare). The hard PL Schoenflies / Newman
recognition direction ("an arbitrary embedded 2-sphere bounds a ball" / "an
arbitrary simply-connected closed 3-manifold is `S^3`") is structurally
avoided: both balls and the gluing sphere are produced by explicit join and
collapse data, and `S^3` is read off the explicit glued complex.
