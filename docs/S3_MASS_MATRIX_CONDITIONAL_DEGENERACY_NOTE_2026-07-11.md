## The Lemma

Let `V` be a supplied three-dimensional complex representation of `S_3`
isomorphic to `A_1 direct-sum E`. Every `S_3`-invariant Hermitian operator on
`V` has the form

```text
M = alpha I_3 + beta P_(A_1),
```

with real `alpha,beta` and the orthogonal projector `P_(A_1)` onto the
symmetric line. Its spectrum is therefore

```text
{alpha, alpha, alpha + beta}.
```

Thus this explicitly supplied representation permits at most two distinct
eigenvalues under exact `S_3` invariance. Under an axis-fixing `Z_2` subgroup,
the invariant Hermitian space has real dimension five.

## Proof

The irreducible summands `A_1` and `E` occur with multiplicity one. Schur's
lemma makes the commutant scalar on each summand, so an invariant Hermitian
operator is `lambda_1 P_(A_1) + lambda_E P_E`. Rewriting with
`P_E=I-P_(A_1)` gives the displayed form and spectrum. Restricting the
permutation representation to the subgroup generated by one transposition
gives `V|_{Z_2} ~= 2 A_+ direct-sum A_-`; the Hermitian commutant therefore
has dimension `2^2 + 1^2 = 5`.

## Scope Boundary

This theorem does not identify `V` with physical generations, a lattice taste
cube, or the repo's `hw=1` carrier. It does not prove that a physical mass
operator is `S_3` invariant, that three observed masses require a particular
breaking mechanism, or that no other carrier/representation can produce a
three-way split. Those are separate bridge questions.

The older `S3_MASS_MATRIX_NO_GO_NOTE.md` bundles the algebra with a
physical-carrier assertion and is separately targeted for fresh-context
