## 1. The two questions, separated

**Q-mechanism** (the structural one). Does the framework have the *kind* of
mechanism that makes a small scale natural — a marginal/log effect rather
than a tuned relevant operator?

**Q-value** (the quantitative one). Does a specific framework gauge channel
transmute the O(1) Planck coupling down `~17` orders so that its pole lands
at `Lambda_QCD ≈ 200 MeV` or `v ≈ 246 GeV`?

These have **different** answers and conflating them is the trap.

## 2. Q-mechanism: YES, structurally (and this is genuine, not wishful)

The SM hierarchy problem is that the Higgs mass-squared `m_H^2` is a
**relevant** operator (mass dimension 2): it is additively renormalized,
`δ m_H^2 ~ Lambda_UV^2`, so reproducing `m_H^2 ≪ M_Pl^2` requires tuning to
`~1` part in `(M_Pl/v)^2 ~ 10^{33}`.

The framework baseline A1 (`Z^3`) + A2 (`M_2(C)`) + A3 (record) carries
**no fundamental scalar mass term** — there is no `m_H^2` counterterm in
the construction to tune. The single dimensionful primitive `a^{-1} = M_Pl`
is a ruler with zero dimensionless content; every other scale is
`a^{-1} ×` (dimensionless). Any scale produced by `(DT)` is a marginal/log
effect: `Lambda/M_UV = exp(-2 pi /(b0 alpha))` is radiatively **stable**
(no additive quadratic sensitivity), exactly as `Lambda_QCD` is in the SM.

So the framework **structurally lacks the relevant tuned operator that *is*
the hierarchy problem.** This is a real structural feature, not wishful
language: the absence is a property of the axiom content, not an assumed
cancellation. (It is the same structural statement one makes for technicolor
/ compositeness: replacing an elementary scalar by a dynamically generated
condensate trades a relevant-operator tuning for a marginal log.)

## 3. Q-value: NO — the framework's O(1) coupling is the *wrong* O(1) value

This is the honest, load-bearing computation. With the framework's actual
O(1) coupling at `M_Pl`, `(DT)` gives (runner Section 2):

| channel | `b0` | `alpha(M_Pl)` | `-2pi/(b0 alpha)` | `Lambda` | `log10(Lambda/M_Pl)` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SU(3)_c full SM | 7 | `alpha_LM = 0.0907` | `-9.90` | `6.1×10^{14}` GeV | `-4.30` |
| SU(3)_c full SM | 7 | `alpha_bare = 0.0796` | `-11.28` | `1.5×10^{14}` GeV | `-4.90` |
| SU(3)_c pure gauge | 11 | `alpha_LM` | `-6.30` | `2.2×10^{16}` GeV | `-2.74` |
| SU(2)_L | 19/6 | `alpha_bare` | `-24.93` | `1.8×10^{8}` GeV | `-10.83` |

The framework's O(1) coupling transmutes only **~4 orders** (to
`~10^{14}`–`10^{16}` GeV), **not ~17**. The delivered transmutation factor
is `~10^{-4}`, short of the needed `~10^{-17}` by `~13` decades.

**Why** (runner Section 3): reaching `Lambda_QCD ≈ 210 MeV` with `b_3 = 7`
requires `1/alpha(M_Pl) ≈ 50.7`; reaching `v ≈ 246 GeV` requires
`1/alpha(M_Pl) ≈ 42.8`. The framework *has* `1/alpha ≈ 11`. The
discrepancy is a coupling that is `~4`–`5×` too **strong**. Independent
corroboration that `~1/50` is the physically correct UV value: running the
physical `alpha_s(M_Z) = 0.118` **up** to `M_Pl` with `b_3 = 7` gives
`1/alpha_s(M_Pl) ≈ 52.4`. Real QCD transmutes `17` orders precisely because
its Planck-scale coupling is *weak* (`1/alpha ≈ 52`); the framework's bare
lattice coupling (`1/alpha ≈ 11`) is far too strong for naive Planck-scale
transmutation to span the hierarchy.

## 4. Verdict

**FRAMEWORK-AVOIDS-HIERARCHY-PROBLEM-BUT-SCALE-NOT-PINNED.**

- The framework has the right **structure** for a natural hierarchy: an
  O(1) marginal coupling (`1/alpha ≈ 11`, genuinely not tuned to `10^{-17}`),
  log transmutation (radiatively stable), and **no relevant `m_H^2`
  operator to fine-tune**. It does **not** relocate the hierarchy into a
  tuned `10^{-17}` coupling. (Q-mechanism: YES.)
- But its specific O(1) value (`1/alpha ≈ 11`) is `~4`–`5×` too **strong**
  to transmute the `~17` orders to `Lambda_QCD` or `v` by naive
  Planck-scale gauge running; the delivered transmutation is `~10^{-4}`,
  not `~10^{-17}`. No gauge pole lands at `v`. The framework's actual
  `Lambda_QCD = 227 MeV` and `v = 246 GeV` are reached by **separate,
  still-open** chains (tadpole/CMT; `alpha_LM^{16}` bounded match), not
  pinned by a clean Planck-scale gauge pole. (Q-value: NO.)

The honest order-of-magnitude: **transmutation delivered `~10^{-4}`;
hierarchy needs `~10^{-17}`; gap `~13` decades, located entirely in the
strength of the framework's UV gauge coupling.**

## 5. Open gates (the paths this opens, not closes)

1. **UV-coupling magnitude (the real gap).** Why is the framework's bare
   `1/alpha ≈ 11` and not the `~50` that transmutation to `Lambda_QCD`
   needs? Whatever weakens the effective Planck-scale coupling (UV-IR /
   tadpole matching, threshold structure, or a not-yet-derived `M_GUT < M_Pl`
   anchor) is where the `~13` decades actually live. This couples to the
   open `alpha_LM` magnitude gate
   ([`HIERARCHY_ALPHA_LM_MAGNITUDE_DELTA0_OPEN_GATE_NOTE_2026-05-30.md`](HIERARCHY_ALPHA_LM_MAGNITUDE_DELTA0_OPEN_GATE_NOTE_2026-05-30.md))
   and to the Planck-scale matching `a/l_P` self-consistency left open by
   the scale-reference primitive.
2. **Effective marginal coupling for `v`.** Is there a framework marginal
   coupling (e.g. a top-Yukawa-driven Coleman-Weinberg quartic, given the
   RG-active top Yukawa over the dimensional-transmutation regime) whose
   transmutation constant `c` *is* coupling-independent group-theory data
   and whose pole lands at `v`? That would upgrade the structural win to a
   pinned value. The `alpha_LM^{16}` rewrite shows the current `c_eff` is
   not yet of that form.
3. **Strong-coupling transmutation.** The naive `(DT)` is the weak-coupling
   1-loop pole; at `1/alpha ≈ 11` the framework's UV coupling is not deep
   in the perturbative regime, so a non-perturbative scale-setting (lattice
   `Lambda` from the bare coupling directly, à la the asymptotic-scaling
   relation) may shift the delivered order of magnitude. This note's `(DT)`
   number is the perturbative benchmark, not the last word.
