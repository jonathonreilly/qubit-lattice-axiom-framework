# The scale ladder on one worked chain: what the corner fermion carries from the lattice to its long-wavelength theory

**Date:** 2026-10-18
**Type:** bounded_theorem
**Status:** bounded support, unaudited. Exact statements on the L = 4 torus for a supplied model, plus representation arguments; the program sections are interpretation, not claims. Nothing is adopted.

## Question

The framework's grid has one qubit per site at the finest resolution. Most results are stated much further out: neighbourhood blocks, Bloch bands, wavepackets many sites wide. Call each zoom level a **rung**. The question is whether thinking in rungs unifies the lanes.

A ladder is useful only if some data provably survives the climb, so that a statement proved at one rung can be used at another. This note tests that on the one chain where every rung is built: the corner fermion. The program sections then look at the ladder from five directions and record what probes on other lanes found.

## Result (supplied model, L = 4 torus)

1. **Lattice gauge data becomes long-wavelength momentum.**
   - Every twisted site rotation sends each Bloch space wholly onto one Bloch space, by k → Rk + πs_R. There is one shift vector s_R per rotation, and all eight shift classes occur.
   - Exactly two momenta are fixed by all 24 rotations: k* = (π/2)(1,−1,1) and −k*.
   - So the valley positions of the long-wavelength theory are read off from the lattice projective symmetry group. Without the shifts (all s_R = 0), the fixed momenta would instead be Γ and (π,π,π), the two points fixed by the linear action.
2. **The spin-½ class is carried at every strength.**
   - The twelve Bloch states at k* form a linear representation of the binary octahedral group 2O in which the central element acts as −1. It decomposes as E1/2 + E5/2 + 2 G3/2, and the same holds at −k*.
   - Hence every one-fermion Hamiltonian commuting with the gauge-dressed rotation operators (the projective symmetry group, not the bare site permutations) and the translations has only spin-½-class levels at ±k*, each of even multiplicity. This holds whatever its strength or range.
   - The runner checks six random symmetric Hamiltonians of strength 1, real and complex, of every range on the torus. Level multiplicities are 2 or 4, all spinorial.
   - Control: at Γ, which is not rotation-fixed, the same Hamiltonians have some nondegenerate levels.
3. **Isotropy emerges with quadratic suppression.**
   - O-invariance forces the linear Weyl term to be v σ·q, isotropic, and any rank-2 symmetric tensor to be a multiple of the identity.
   - The first anisotropy enters at relative order |q|². In the runner's Weyl model (its particular X; the speed v scales linearly with the strength of X) the angular spread of the cone speed is 2.60e−4, 6.49e−5 and 1.62e−5 at |q| = 0.04, 0.02 and 0.01: a ratio of 4.00 per halving.
   - A packet of width w has |q| ~ 1/w, so its anisotropy is of order (a/w)², with a the lattice spacing.
4. **The Dirac mass is natural in 't Hooft's sense.**
   - Keep the site rotations, the site inversion and the translations (allowing the staggered sign (−1)^(x+y+z)). Then every level of the folded Hamiltonian at k* has multiplicity divisible by 4.
   - Representation argument. The rotations decouple the E1/2, E5/2 and G3/2 sectors. In the projective symmetry group, inversion anticommutes with the w1-odd rotations: I R I⁻¹ R⁻¹ = A2(R). So inversion maps k* to −k* and the E1/2 sector onto the E5/2 sector, and their levels coincide. Each G3/2 level is already 4-dimensional.
   - So within this symmetry class no node quartet splits, at any strength or order. Splitting is necessary for a Dirac mass but not sufficient: see the control below. The runner illustrates this with three backgrounds that add random symmetric uniform and staggered terms of strength 0.3.
   - An A2 imaginary staggered term of strength ε reduces the site rotations to T and splits every quartet. The gap/ε is constant within the runner's 4e−3 tolerance for ε = 2e−3, 1e−3 and 5e−4; the checker measured changes of 2e−6 to 2e−9 between halvings. The sampled A2 term is not itself inversion-symmetric.
   - Control: with the rotations kept and inversion broken, every level stays even but some become 2-fold. Inversion partners split, while the doublets stay pinned Weyl points. This splitting is not a mass: the rotations alone keep k* gapless.
   - **Scope: one-fermion only.** A campaign block on filling constraints (B7, same-model-family worker, loop pack `B7_corner_LSM`) adds two things.
     - **Odd filling per site.** With translations and three mutually commuting C2s, the gauge-dressed lifts of two commuting C2s anticommute on the whole one-particle space. So every level at a momentum fixed by both (k* for O) is even, and odd filling per site is forced gapless for free fermions. The worker's result was independently checked; corrections are in `B7_check`.
     - **Interactions can escape.** In the doubled-cell class used here (site rotations and inversion about one site, plus the even translations), an entangled, symmetric pair state is a short-range-entangled insulator at one fermion per site. So the quartet protection is a free-fermion statement; symmetric interacting mass generation is not excluded by anomaly counting.
5. **What fixes a common speed (exact group theory).**
   - Only one symmetric rank-2 tensor on (t,x,y,z) is invariant under the 4D hyperoctahedral group of signed permutations of all four axes: the identity.
   - Under spatial O_h times time reversal there are two, δ_00 and δ_ij.
   - So for a leading kinetic term K_μν k_μ k_ν, putting time on the same footing as space is sufficient for one common speed across fields. With only spatial O_h and time reversal, each field's speed is a separate parameter unless something else fixes it, for example a flow to a Lorentz-invariant fixed point.
   - The checker extended the counts by exact group averages. General rank-2 tensors (the Dirac form γ_μ k_ν) give 1 against 2; quadratic forms in F_μν give 1 against 2; symmetric rank-4 tensors give 2 against 4. So the first hypercubic violation is at k⁴.

6. **Parameter-free step rules: speed from the grid alone.**
   - **The minimal Weyl automaton** (D'Ariano and Perinotti 2014) is a one-step-per-tick rule on the BCC lattice with no tunable coupling. Its eigenphases are ±arccos(c_x c_y c_z − s_x s_y s_z), with c_i = cos(k_i/√3) and s_i = sin(k_i/√3); this is one of the mirror pair.
     - **Speed.** 1/√3, a pure number from the geometry.
     - **Causal limit.** The fastest group speed is exactly 1/√2. The one-step causal limit is the cube |v_i| ≤ 1/√3, which the automaton saturates componentwise.
     - **Anisotropy and symmetry.** Anisotropy falls only linearly in |k|. The dispersion is invariant under 24 of the 48 signed permutations (T_d, proper part T). The term k_x k_y k_z, odd under the w1-odd rotations, is allowed.
     - **What removes the linear term** is full octahedral symmetry at the node; inversion alone does not.
   - **The corner reflection step** W = (H_link/√2)·(1 − 2P₂), where P₂ is the eigenvalue-2 space of the on-site corner hopping.
     - **It is exact.** H_link(k)² = 2 at every k, in infinite volume. Proof: each corner {a,b} at v has exactly two link neighbours, {−a,b} at v+a and {a,−b} at v+b. The only off-diagonal two-step endpoint is {−a,−b} at v+a+b. It is reached by two paths that bound a π-flux link plaquette, so they cancel.
     - **It is symmetric.** It commutes with the gauge-dressed rotations and the translations.
     - **Its only choices are discrete:** which on-site eigenspace to reflect.
     - **The node at k*** is 6-fold, G3/2 + E1/2, all spin-½ class.
       - The first-order matrix M(q) on the node satisfies M³ = (|q|²/3)M exactly (checked symbolically). So four bands leave at exactly ±|q|/√3, each 2-fold, with anisotropy falling as |q|².
       - Two bands are exactly flat, forced by dimension: the +1 spaces of the two reflections have dimensions 6 and 8 in 12.
       - This is the spin-1 ⊗ spin-½ six-fold type, not a Dirac electron.
   - **Lemma: no moving isolated spin-½ doublet from constant-spectrum factors (exact).**
     - **Statement.** Let a step (or a Hamiltonian) lie in the algebra generated by operators that commute with the gauge-dressed rotations and the translations and have k-independent spectra. Then every spin-½ level of it at k* that is exactly 2-fold has zero velocity.
     - **Proof.**
       - E1/2 and E5/2 each occur once at k*. So an isolated doublet D lies inside a whole eigenspace E of every generator at k*.
       - For a generator F = Σλ_i P_i(k) with constant λ_i, P_j ∂P_i P_j = 0 for all i, j. So the projection of ∂F onto E vanishes, and hence onto D.
       - The map G ↦ P_D ∂G P_D is a derivation on this algebra, because the generators act on D as scalars at k*. So it vanishes on every product and sum of generators.
     - **Numerical illustration.** Random symmetric on-site unitaries between link reflections give a largest doublet half-splitting/q of 2e−9. The checker found the same for many more mixed products: 24 isolated doublets, maximum 8.9e−9. The original corner Hamiltonian H0(t1,t2) also has exactly zero doublet velocity, which is why the Weyl model needed its supplied term X.
     - **What escapes it.**
       - **Multifold nodes**, like the six-fold node above, where the doublet mixes with G3/2.
       - **A symmetric factor with a k-dependent spectrum** that lies outside the algebra.
       - **Non-symmetric layers** whose product is symmetric.
       - **Giving up the full octahedral symmetry at the node**, which admits linear anisotropy.
   - **A parameter-free Weyl step: the diagonal shift.** Each corner {d,e} has a natural move along its own face diagonal d+e, one of the 12 FCC directions. The map S: {d,e} at v → {d,e} at v+d+e, with gauge signs fixed by symmetrising one hop, is a signed permutation. So it is exactly unitary in infinite volume and commutes with the gauge-dressed rotations and translations. Its spectrum depends on k, so it escapes the lemma.
     - **Weyl nodes at k*.** Steps S·U, S·R_L·U and R_L·S·U, with U an on-site Clifford-phase function of the on-site hopping and the chiral triangle operator Λ, give isolated spin-½ Weyl doublets at k*:
       - speed √2/3 = 0.4714045, with anisotropy falling as |q|²;
       - Berry flux ±1;
       - no flat bands.
     - **Example 1:** S·exp(iπ/2·Q_top), with Q_top the top eigenspace of Λ. The node sits at quasi-energy π/2, but other bands cross π/2 on surfaces elsewhere in the zone, so it is not isolated.
     - **Example 2:** S·R_L·U_H·U_Λ, with two Clifford-phase on-site factors (labels H23 and L110 in the scan). The node quasi-energy is ±π/2, depending on the phase conventions; the runner finds π/2. It is reached only at the eight points (π/2)(±1,±1,±1): a refined search in exploration, and a coarse grid in the runner (minimum distance to it elsewhere > 0.05). So this is a clean eight-node Weyl semimetal at that quasi-energy.
       - The two octahedrally fixed nodes ±k* are isotropic spin-½ Weyl nodes, speed √2/3, chirality ±1.
       - The other six are anisotropic Weyl nodes (speed spread about 0.3), chiralities three +1 and three −1. The net is zero.
       - These eight points are exactly the momenta at which commuting C2 lifts force even levels (campaign block B7, independently checked). The six extra nodes are the analogue of the 2³ doublers of naive lattice fermions.
       - **Structure of the doublers (numerical observation).** The six are k* + πe_i (chirality −1) and −k* + πe_i (chirality +1), for i = x, y, z. So they form three opposite-chirality pairs, one per lattice axis. Each is a uniaxial Weyl cone: speed 0.2467 along its own axis and 0.5837 transverse. The ratio is 1 − 1/√3 to 2e−11; this is unexplained. The C3 rotations permute the three.
       - **A second eight-node set (B17 check).** There is a second set at quasi-energy −π/2, with identical cones and opposite chiralities. It is forced: the spectrum of W(k) equals the spectrum of −W(k), and it is closed under complex conjugation. So the step has 16 Weyl nodes in all.
       - **This pattern is symmetry-forced (exact).** Under the affine rotation action, the eight points split into the orbits {k*} and {−k*}, with little group O, and two 3-point orbits k* + πe_i and −k* + πe_i. The little group of each point in the 3-point orbits is D4 about the axis e_i, which contains all three axis C2s (runner Part 1). So in every symmetric corner Hamiltonian or step:
         - levels there are even;
         - a band touching at k* is isotropic at linear order;
         - a touching at k* + πe_i is uniaxial about e_i.

         At odd filling all eight touch (B7, C20), so the forced low-energy content always has this 1 + 3 form.
       - So the step's Weyl content is one isotropic spin-½ pair at the octahedrally fixed momenta plus three axis-labelled uniaxial pairs. This mirrors the taste structure of staggered fermions. Any reading as species or generations is interpretation only and is not claimed.
     - **Corollary (doubling, exact).** Suppose a finite-range step W (so W(k) is continuous) commutes with the translations and with the gauge-dressed lifts of two commuting axis C2s. Suppose also that W has a quasi-energy gap: some E′ that no eigenphase reaches at any k. If W has a spin-½ Weyl doublet at k* at quasi-energy E (exactly 2-fold, with nonzero linear splitting), then E is also reached somewhere other than ±k*. A tilt cannot evade this: C2x and C2y act on q as diagonal sign matrices, which force the tilt to vanish, so one Weyl branch lies below E.
       - *Proof.* The two C2 lifts anticommute on the whole one-particle space (campaign block B7, independently checked). So at each of the eight points (π/2)(±1,±1,±1), which both C2s fix, every level is even.
       - Let n(k) count the eigenphases in the arc from E′ to E. It is locally constant wherever E is not an eigenphase.
       - If E were reached only at ±k*, n would take one value n₀ on the rest of the zone.
       - At the other six cube points, n₀ is even.
       - Next to k*, n₀ is the even count of levels at k* below E, plus the lower Weyl branch: odd. Contradiction.
       - The same counting at odd filling forces the touchings of Hamiltonians (campaign C20).
     - **What this explains.**
       - For Hamiltonians at odd filling the same counting gives C20's stronger pointwise obstruction: all eight points touch the chemical potential.
       - For steps it needs a genuine quasi-energy gap. For the eight-node step, the empty quasi-energy arcs shrink as the grid is refined: on a 28³ grid only fragments near the Weyl cones remain. So the hypothesis is not established there, and the corollary is not claimed for that step; its doublers are consistent with it.
       - A doubler-free spin-½ Weyl step must therefore be gapless somewhere in quasi-energy, or break the axis C2s (which T contains).
     - **Search result (not a no-go).** The family searched was S·U, S·R_L·U and R_L·S·U with Clifford-phase on-site U: about 12,000 steps, 3,738 with Weyl doublets at k*.
       - 4,476 node/step combinations have no level at the node quasi-energy at the other cube or high-symmetry points.
       - All of those have other bands crossing the node quasi-energy elsewhere: the best minimum distance on a 12³ grid away from ±k* is 0.007.
       - So in this family a clean node quasi-energy comes only with the six doublers, and the doublers sit where commuting C2 lifts force even levels.
       - Whether a parameter-free symmetric step can have only the two spin-½ nodes is open. The Clifford-phase pinning of levels to multiples of π/2 at the cube points is a plausible cause, not established.

### 1. Coarse-graining maps

Each rung transition is an encoding (an isometry from coarse to fine) followed by a projection onto a slow or low-energy subspace (a Bloch band, a Schrieffer–Wolff block).

- **The upside.** Every rung is explicit, so statements are maps, not slogans.
- **The cost.** The composite map is not unique. The question is not "is there a ladder" but "which data does every admissible ladder preserve". That question is the next direction.

### 2. Invariants that survive every rung

Anomaly matching ('t Hooft), Lieb–Schultz–Mattis-type constraints and projective symmetry classes are statements that some data cannot change under any symmetric map. Items 1 and 2 are instances.

- **The class.** The projective class of the lattice symmetry action reappears as the spin-½ class at the fixed momenta. It does so for every symmetric Hamiltonian, not just the one studied.
- **The shifts.** The gauge-data shifts s_R reappear as the valley positions.

This is the strongest unifying mechanism found. An obligation stated as an invariant can be discharged at whichever rung it is easiest to compute, then transported. The Maxwell probe below found the same pattern for the Gauss law: block sums of Gauss operators equal boundary flux exactly, so the electric 1-form symmetry survives coarse-graining.

### 3. Effective field theory and naturalness

Universality says a higher rung depends on a lower one only through its invariants and a few relevant couplings. Irrelevant operators are suppressed by powers of a/w.

- **Isotropy (item 3).** It costs nothing extra. O forbids every anisotropy below relative order (a/w)².
- **Small masses (item 4).** They are technically natural: m → 0 restores a symmetry, so the mass is proportional to the breaking.
- **Objection: per-field isotropy is not a common light cone.** Cubic symmetry makes each field's rank-2 kinetic tensor isotropic. It does not force two fields to share one speed. Loop corrections move speeds apart by amounts set by the couplings rather than suppressed by the cutoff: the fine-tuning problem of Collins et al. (2004).
  - The Weyl speed here, v ≈ 0.110 in lattice units, is set by the supplied term X and is unrelated to any gauge-field speed.
  - The Maxwell probe found the same gap in the gauge lane: a two-doublet coin has photon speeds 0.618 and 1.618, which is a consistent infrared theory.
  - A common light cone needs its own mechanism: a common dynamical origin, or a flow to a Lorentz-invariant infrared fixed point (Chadha and Nielsen 1983, where speeds converge only logarithmically).

  This is the clearest place where the ladder does not supply the answer by itself. Item 5 shows what would: time on the same footing as space (see the answer below).

### 4. Information-theoretic coarse-graining

A rung map is a quantum channel. Data processing then says that distinguishability can only fall going up.

- **What a higher rung can resolve.** Only what survives the channel: invariants, conserved charges and redundantly recorded data.
- **Records.** This is the natural home for the Record lane. A record is information copied redundantly enough to survive coarse-graining, the quantum Darwinism picture.
- **Conservation.** The channel view also shows what coarse-graining cannot give. A channel that discards fine data is generically dissipative, so energy conservation at a higher rung needs a Hamiltonian at that rung. The Maxwell probe reached the same conclusion for its conservation item.

### 5. Codes at every scale

Every rung is an encoding of the one below:

- block placement;
- the corner stabilizer code, whose checks are the fluxes;
- Bloch states spread over L³ sites;
- wavepackets spread over (w/a)³ sites.

A packet's logical content is spread over a "huge neighbourhood". Heuristically, a local operator at the finest rung acts on it with amplitude of order (a/w)^(3/2) per site touched. This is the code-theoretic reading of why large objects are robust to finest-scale structure. It has the same shape as the holographic and multiscale-entanglement codes (Vidal 2007; Swingle 2012; Almheiri, Dong and Harlow 2015).

## Answer to the question (interpretation)

The ladder does unify the lanes, but not by deriving everything from the finest rung. It gives three exact tools that every lane can use. It also collects what is left open onto a short list of shared primitives.

**What the campaign added (2026-10-18).** A fourth tool appeared, and three of the probe reframings became proved or decisive statements.

- **Fourth tool: the lattice fixes the species content at the top rung.** The gauge-dressed symmetry action forces even levels at the momenta fixed by commuting C2s, and odd filling then forces touchings at all of them. So the long-wavelength content is not free: in the corner model it is always one isotropic spin-½ pair plus three axis-labelled uniaxial pairs. This is a lattice LSM and doubling statement carried up the ladder: the top rung inherits a multiplicity structure, not just a class.
- **Formation.** Rung-0 formation with random priorities is provably at the trivial fixed point. Any long-range structure in Records must therefore come from the Hamiltonian rung or from the start, and spatial Record frequencies are then consistent.
- **Gravity.** Every gauge-invariant kernel gives a single cone, and lattice constraint closure selects DeWitt. The open gravity item is now precisely an underived seagull, which fixes the induced 1/G.

**The three tools.**

1. **Transport of invariants.** Data that no admissible coarse-graining can change can be computed at whichever rung is easiest, then used at any other:
   - projective classes;
   - affine momentum shifts;
   - Gauss laws and 1-form symmetries, whose block sums equal boundary flux exactly;
   - winding sectors;
   - Weyl monopole charges;
   - anomaly data.
2. **Uniqueness from symmetry plus redundancy, with power-law suppression.**
   - Lattice symmetry and a gauge redundancy leave one leading form in three lanes: Weyl/Dirac, Maxwell and linearised Einstein–Hilbert.
   - Corrections are of order (a/w)².
   - Masslessness and small masses are protected.
3. **Limits from treating coarse-graining as a channel.** This says what coarse-graining cannot supply:
   - conservation, which needs a Hamiltonian at that rung;
   - purity and preparation, which need a low-entropy start;
   - permanence;
   - Born weights from a finite apparatus set.

**Wrong-rung diagnoses.** Several open items turned out to be questions asked at the wrong rung:
- the 0.0756 stiffness;
- one-link 1/β coefficients;
- kinematic 2π/L gaps;
- iid-priority formation, which cannot carry long-range order;
- the six qubits per site that the corner code replaced.

**The answer the probes converge on.** Three lanes independently hit the same wall: a common light cone.
- Per-field isotropy is free.
- A common speed across fields is not, and neither is spin-2 isotropy, when each field's speed is a tunable coupling. In the corner Hamiltonian the speed scales with a supplied term; the Maxwell probe found a coin with two light cones.
- The grid's own speed limit does not help. A local qubit grid does give a hard limit (one site per step for a circuit; a Lieb–Robinson bound for a Hamiltonian). But a limit is a ceiling that fields can sit below at different speeds, not a common speed.

**A third route to a common cone: closure of the gravity constraint algebra (campaign block B14, independently checked).** This is the Teitelboim / Hojman–Kuchař–Teitelboim mechanism realised on the repo's lattice gravity model.

- In the 2026-09-24 curvature-member model, add a supplied scalar matter sector and require the total constraint algebra to close. At leading order, exactly on the lattice:
  - every matter species is forced onto one common cone, set by the gravity coefficients (c = K/(4α));
  - the curvature term is forced to the Lorentzian sign. The 09-24 runner's sign is opposite to its own action and corresponds to the Euclidean algebra.
- In the continuum at leading order, the relabelling bracket then forces the universal metric-stress coupling.
- On the lattice that bracket fails at O(a²), with an explicit obstruction monomial.
- **Supplied:** the matter Hamiltonian, its positivity and the scalar-lapse rule. Newton's constant is not fixed.
- **Consequence:** a common light cone does not require a parameter-free step rule or 4D hypercubic symmetry. It can come from requiring the coarse rung's gravity constraints to close with matter. Exact lattice closure beyond leading order is the open obligation.

**The closure route extends across spins and acts as a selection rule (campaign blocks B15–B17).**
- **Spin ½ (B15, independently checked).** A naive lattice Weyl/Dirac field closes against the same constraints only on the scalar's cone, v² = K/(4α). Hermitian Clifford generators square to +1, which gives v² > 0. So, given K, α > 0, the Lorentzian sign follows without a positivity premise on the matter. All eight naive doublers share the cone.
- **Exact lattice closure fails.** No linear relabelling of any range closes the fermion bracket. The obstruction is O(a²) at endpoint-mean timing and O(a) otherwise.
- **Spin 1 (B16, independently checked).**
  - A lattice Maxwell field closes on the same cone, independently of g.
  - Among translation-invariant timings this needs one of eight one-sided timings. Reflection-symmetric alternatives break unit translations and appear to fail elsewhere.
  - **Normalisation.** The relabelling normalisation that sets the cone is partly derived (B19, independently checked).
    - In the continuum at leading order, {G, G} and the single lapse rule force the scalar and Maxwell generators onto one structure constant. So those matter cones equal each other with no premise.
    - For spin ½ this is open. The Kosmann relabellings do not form a Lie algebra unless a triad or rotation constraint is added (companion lane note, Result 4).
    - Their value relative to the gravity coefficients additionally needs gravity's own {G, C} at order h, with a quadratic relabelling term. That bracket gives every matter cone = the graviton's TT cone, K₂/(4α). This is K/(4α) because block 62 carries one K on both curvature terms, reading block 101's quadratic Hamiltonian as the unit-lapse value of one lapse-smeared constraint. Further premises: one total constraint and relabelling, a local first-order lapse rule, and a one-derivative quadratic relabelling term.
    - On the lattice the relabellings do not close: the scalar at O(a²), Maxwell at O(a). There the normalisation is selected only in the continuum limit.
  - The one-sided timing forces an O(a) lattice obstruction.
- **Prior art.** In the continuum this is the known Teitelboim / Hojman–Kuchař–Teitelboim / Schwinger universality. What is new is its exact lattice realisation and the explicit obstructions.
- **Selection rule (B17, independently checked).**
  - Closure needs every low-energy species to carry the gravity sector's cone metric. That metric is isotropic when the gravity relabelling is cubic-covariant: {G, G} alone would also allow a constant anisotropic metric, but then all species would have to share it. Lapse rescalings, density improvements, anisotropic relabellings and coordinate changes cannot evade this.
  - The repo's 2026-09-25 walk passes. It is naive-type, and the invariance of Σ sin² k_j under k_j → k_j + π makes all eight species isotropic at one speed. So the closure route reproduces its α = K/4.
  - The parameter-free corner step fails. Its ±k* cones are isotropic, but the doublers at the same quasi-energy are uniaxial. They sit at D4 points that no species-exchanging symmetry relates to ±k*: k → k + π(1,1,1) exchanges only orbits of the same type.
  - This is conditional on the step's long-wavelength, band-projected theory coupling to a lapse as a scalar-weighted energy density. A global local Floquet Hamiltonian is not asserted, since the step is gapless away from ±π/2.
- **Consequence for the target below.** A parameter-free step can feed a closing gravity sector only if its doublers are isotropic at the ±k* speed. That takes a taste-type symmetry relating the 3-orbits to ±k*, or no doublers, which by the doubling corollary needs gaplessness or broken axis C2s.
- **The taste route, in the corner model (B18, independently checked).**
  - The diagonal shift S has no translation-covariant taste moving k* off ±k*. Its allowed affine momentum maps are exactly the PSG lifts times k → k + π(1,1,1).
  - Stabiliser orders, 24 at k* against 8 at k* + πe_i, mean any taste must enlarge the symmetry group.
  - Adding the link hopping's plane tastes leaves no spin-½ doublet at k*.
  - For the eight-node step, an exhaustive spectral test and the velocity-determinant invariant exclude every such map: v*³ = 0.1048 against v_ax v_tr² = 0.0841.
  - Words in S² do have exact tastes, which make their Weyl copies isotropic at √2/3 per S. But they carry uniaxial nodes at the same quasi-energy, at (π/4)-type points, or nodal lines.
  - So no step found so far passes the closure selection rule. Block B20 is searching further.

**The information-minimal target.** One local step rule on the base grid with no continuous parameters, from which every field is built. Then:

It is a target to derive or propose to the owner, not an adopted axiom.

The doubling corollary settles the open problem for gapped steps and odd-filling Hamiltonians: with the axis C2s, the doublers are forced, as in Nielsen–Ninomiya. The minimal clean form puts them at the other six cube points. The remaining escape routes are steps gapless in quasi-energy, which is the case for every Weyl step found, or giving up the axis C2s.
