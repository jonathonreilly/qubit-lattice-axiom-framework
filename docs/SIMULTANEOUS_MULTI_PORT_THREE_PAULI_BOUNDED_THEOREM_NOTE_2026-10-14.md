## 3. Global full-space gap, kernel and inverse restrictions

On the complete two-qubit space, -n_u-n_v<=S_uv<=n_u+n_v, including |11>. Since B_i is a Hermitian involution on a different factor from its cell auxiliaries,

    L_i >= (1-r)(n_(a_i)+n_(a_i'))
                +(1-h_d-r)(n_(b_i)+n_(b_i'))
         >= N_i/2.

This is an operator inequality, not a statement about simultaneous data eigenvectors. It may therefore be summed even when [L_i,L_j]!=0:

    L>=N_tot/2,     N_tot=sum_i N_i.                 (2)

Let J0 append Omega, P0=J0 J0*, Q0=I-P0. Every L_i annihilates the entire D tensor Omega, and N_tot>=Q0. It follows that

    ker L=ran J0,       L|_Q0>=I/2.                  (3)

The kernel has dimension dim(D), not one. There is one bare auxiliary vacuum for every original data vector. No restriction on the original state or any global Pauli-sign sector is introduced.

For an upper bound on one L_i, resolve B_i only locally. A path has vacuum0, one-excitation block

    D_b=[[1,-r],[-r,1+h_d b]],  b=+1 or-1,

and double-excitation energy2+h_d b. Its one-excitation norm is at most1+h_d+r=3/2, less than either double-excitation energy. The two paths commute within this cell at fixed B_i; their top energies sum to4. Thus0<=L_i<=4I, and L<=4M I. Every auxiliary fully occupied makes all exchanges zero and both detunings cancel, so L is exactly4M I on that data subspace. Hence

    ||L||=4M.                                       (4)

Each N_i commutes with **every** L_j: the auxiliary sets are disjoint and even data-dependent detunings preserve their auxiliary numbers. The stronger individual path numbers also commute with L. Consequently the full reduced inverse

    R=Q0 (L|_Q0)^-1 Q0,        ||R||<=2              (5)

preserves these occupation sectors. On the subspace where only cell i is excited and all other cell auxiliaries are vacuum, all L_j for j!=i vanish as operators, irrespective of their data commutators. Thus the restriction of R there is exactly the corresponding inverse of L_i. In particular on a single excited path it is D_(B_i)^-1 on that path and the original data. This justifies the local inverse used at second order.

On a sector with two different cells excited, the inverse is instead the actual inverse of L_i+L_j on that sector. In general it is neither a sum of one-cell inverses nor a simultaneous scalar-sign substitution. These mixed sectors will be retained in Y below. No global diagonalization of noncommuting B_i is assumed.

## 4. Exact second-order response and all cross contractions
