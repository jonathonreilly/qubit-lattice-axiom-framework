## 2. Actual protocol and sufficient resources

Fix m,v>0 and an energy reference mu>0. At spacing c use the actual placed
Fock carrier and its positive free Hamiltonian H_c from K, with maximal
free multiplication domains. Its vacuum Omega_c has H_c Omega_c=0. Put

    A_c=1+H_c/mu >=1.                                      (P1)

For a preparation interval [0,tau_c], choose a fixed finite list of formally
selfadjoint even ordinary-cell polynomials V_(ell,c), and real amplitudes
lambda_(ell,c)(t). The preparation generator is

    H_c+V_c^prep(t),
    V_c^prep(t)=sum_(ell=1)^L lambda_(ell,c)(t)V_(ell,c).     (P2)

The theorem also permits a varying list if its stated bounds are retained;
the simple uniform application fixes L, word degrees, coefficients and
spatial inputs independently of c. Real compact spatial inputs make each
even polynomial an actual finite-cell-hull even spin observable by the
supplied identification. Its total norm is bounded; it is not an extensive
sum over all cells. A fixed physical hull can contain a growing number of
cells without changing that fixed-polynomial resource.

For an integer n>=1, assume the temporal amplitudes are measurable and
integrable, and the bounded norm-flow commutators satisfy, almost everywhere,

    norm(ad_(H_c)^j(V_c^prep(t))) <= kappa_j,c(t),
    kappa_j,c(t)>=0,
    integral_0^tau_c kappa_j,c(t) dt < infinity,
                    0<=j<=n.                              (P3)

Assume the corresponding operator families are strongly measurable on their
stated graph spaces. The fixed finite CAR construction supplies this: norm
continuity of fields in their smearings and their norm-flow derivatives,
combined with measurable integrable scalar amplitudes, gives the needed
measurability and integrable envelopes. No time derivative of lambda is
required. Smooth temporal pulses, finite step pulses and piecewise continuous
pulses are examples. Mere bounded variation is not being used as a substitute
for a spatial commutator bound.

For explicit sufficient spatial constants, K(10)--(11) gives for a word
polynomial V_(ell,c)=sum_alpha z_alpha product_i gamma(J_c C_c f_(alpha i))

    M_(ell,j)=sum_alpha |z_alpha|
       sum_(j_1+...+j_d=j) j!/(j_1!...j_d!)
                            product_i norm(E^(j_i) f_(alpha i)),
    kappa_j,c(t)=sum_ell |lambda_(ell,c)(t)| M_(ell,j).       (P4)

Here E(p)=sqrt(m^2+v^2p^2); formal adjoint partners are included with the

Uniform preparation resources mean a uniform bound, as c decreases, on

## 3. Existence and integer graph propagation

Suppress c temporarily. In the interaction picture the bounded perturbation
is V_I(t)=exp(itH)V^prep(t)exp(-itH). Its norm has an L1 envelope kappa_0.
The strongly integrated Dyson series converges on the Hilbert space with
the usual factorial bound (integral kappa_0)^k/k!. Solving both time
directions and differentiating the bounded integral equations gives a
unitary propagator Y(t,s). Selfadjointness of V_I almost everywhere is what
gives unitarity. Define U_prep(t,0)=exp(-itH)Y(t,0). This is the actual
propagator, not a formal time-ordered expression with no domain control.

Bounded commutators through j give the actual graph identity

    A^j V_I(t) A^-j
      =V_I(t)+sum_(k=1)^j binom(j,k) mu^-k
                  ad_H^k(V_I(t)) A^-k.                    (P5)

The source norm-flow argument makes V_I(t) preserve D(A^j); every term
in (P5) is a bounded operator on the Hilbert space. Its norm is at most
kappa_0 plus sum_(k=1)^j binom(j,k)mu^-k kappa_k, an L1 function.
Thus one can also solve the Dyson equation on the Banach graph space
D(A^j), with norm norm(A^j psi). More explicitly, solve the bounded
equation for the conjugated operator in (P5), then multiply by A^-j.
Its image solves the original Hilbert-space integral equation. Uniqueness
identifies the two solutions. This proves graph preservation rather than
assuming it. In particular for j>=1 the original Schrödinger equation
holds strongly almost everywhere on its invariant first operator domain.

There is a better norm bound than the immediate graph-space exponential.
For psi in D(A^n), set x_j(t)=A^j Y(t,0)psi. These vectors are absolutely
continuous in the Hilbert space and, almost everywhere, obey

    i x_j'(t)=V_I(t)x_j(t)
      +sum_(k=1)^j binom(j,k)mu^-k
                      ad_H^k(V_I(t))x_(j-k)(t).           (P6)

Variation with the unitary Y(t,u) removes the leading selfadjoint V_I term.
Writing u_j(t)=norm(x_j(t)) gives the integral inequality

    u_j(t)<=u_j(0)+sum_(k=1)^j binom(j,k)
             integral_0^t mu^-k kappa_k(u)u_(j-k)(u)du.   (P7)

The j=0 norm is conserved. Thus the hierarchy is triangular; kappa_0 is
needed for existence but does not by itself increase a free graph moment.
No time derivative of a commutator or temporal pulse coefficient occurs.

Define nonnegative integrated budgets

    b_k,c(t)=mu^-k integral_0^t kappa_k,c(u)du,  k>=1,
    B_0,c(t)=1,
    B_j,c(t)=j! [z^j] exp(z+sum_(k=1)^j b_k,c(t)z^k/k!).  (P8)

Only a finite polynomial coefficient is extracted; no analytic-series
claim or computation is involved. For example

    B_1=1+b_1,
    B_2=(1+b_1)^2+b_2,
    B_3=(1+b_1)^3+3(1+b_1)b_2+b_3.

These scalar functions start at B_j(0)=1 and satisfy the triangular equality
corresponding to (P7), with B_0=1. This follows by differentiating the finite
coefficient formula almost everywhere, or by integrating its polynomial
derivatives. Induction in j compares (P7) with this equality.

For psi=A^-n x, all initial graph norms through n are at most norm(x).
The induction therefore proves the actual operator bound

    norm(A_c^n U_prep(t,0) A_c^-n)<=B_n,c(t).             (P9)

The free interaction-picture factors commute with A, so they do not change
the bound. It holds on the whole Hilbert space as a bounded extension;
U_prep maps D(A^n) into itself. It also directly gives the same bound on
Hilbert--Schmidt columns, without diagonalizing an evolving density.

The expression B_n is polynomial in the finitely many integrated spatial
commutator budgets. Bounding the entire conjugated perturbation and using
Gronwall would give a valid but coarser exponential. That route is preserved
as a nonoptimal bound, not falsely called a counterexample or a failed test.

## 4. Fractional moments without an extra initial moment

Here is the interpolation fact needed for arbitrary positive moment order.
Let A,B>=1 be positive selfadjoint operators, and T bounded, with

    norm(T)<=C_0,
    B^n T A^-n bounded with norm at most C_n.              (P10)

The endpoint includes its domain assertion: T maps D(A^n) into D(B^n).
For 0<=theta<=n,

    norm(B^theta T A^-theta)
                    <= C_0^(1-theta/n) C_n^(theta/n).   (P11)

To prove this without postulating a fractional domain equivalence, insert
bounded spectral projections E_B,M on the left and E_A,L on the right.
For input/output unit vectors take the scalar matrix element of
E_B,M B^(nz) T A^(-nz)E_A,L in the strip 0<=Re z<=1. The finite spectral
cutoffs make it analytic. At Re z=0 the bound is C_0 by unitary imaginary
powers. At Re z=1 factor it through the bounded endpoint in (P10); its
bound is C_n. Divide by C_0^(1-z)C_n^z and multiply by exp(epsilon z^2).
The rectangle maximum principle, followed by imaginary height to infinity
and epsilon to zero, gives (P11) for the cutoffs.

First let the output cutoff tend to one on an input spectral core.
Monotone convergence of the B^theta spectral norm proves membership in
D(B^theta). Then remove the input cutoff in the A^theta graph norm;
closedness of B^theta proves the domain assertion and bound for all
D(A^theta). Zero endpoint constants are handled by density of the inverse
power range. Thus no noncommuting power inequality has been used.

With B=A and T=U_prep, unitarity and (P9) give

    norm(A_c^theta U_prep A_c^-theta)
                         <=B_n,c^(theta/n), 0<=theta<=n. (P12)

Let rho_in,c be a normal initial density with the spectral moment

    Tr rho_in,c A_c^s<=M_in,s(c),   s>0,
    n=ceil(s/2),
    rho_prep,c=U_prep(tau_c,0)rho_in,c U_prep(tau_c,0)^*.  (P13)

The moment convention means A_c^(s/2)rho_in,c^(1/2) is a closed
Hilbert--Schmidt column. Its existence follows by spectral cutoffs,
square-summable basis columns and closedness, exactly as in I. In particular
it does not assign ensemble vectors an unstated D(H_c^s) domain.
Attach this column to (P12), and use unitary invariance of its right factor.
The result is

    Tr rho_prep,c A_c^s
                    <=B_n,c(tau_c)^(s/n) M_in,s(c).     (P14)

For the actual free vacuum M_in,s=1 for every s. An even protocol preserves
parity, so its prepared vacuum state is an actual even pure state. No
normalization, postselection, measurement update or imported finite-level
surrogate is present. For general normal inputs the same moment estimate
holds without a parity requirement on the density.

## 7. Actual finite-pulse control when uniform regularity is omitted

Here is a countercontrol to the stronger claim that finite smooth protocols
with a bounded integrated potential norm and bounded L2 smearings always
prepare uniform positive moments. Its inputs and temporal profiles vary
with c. It does not challenge the fixed-data commutator-budget theorem.

Work in the free instance and choose two normalized orthogonal actual
positive-high modes u_1,c,u_2,c with disjoint fixed compact high-coordinate
momentum supports, inside the physical band for small c. The high
polarization is the supplied constant P_0. Their normalized even pair chi_c
has high vacuum absent and

    norm(H_c chi_c)<=2 U_c,
    U_c=sqrt(m^2+4v^2/c^2)<=m+2v/c.                       (P26)

The bounded selfadjoint even pair operator

    R_c=a*(u_1,c)a*(u_2,c)+a(u_2,c)a(u_1,c)               (P27)

has norm one and exchanges Omega_c and chi_c. It is a finite quadratic
real-CAR polynomial. To see this in the supplied normalization, for each
positive mode u put f=(u+Gamma u)/sqrt(2), and choose the analogous real
g for iu. Then sqrt(2)P_* f=u and
a*(u)=[gamma(f)-i gamma(g)]/2, exactly as in Local L18/section 8.
All four real coefficients are in the actual high band.

Current C8--C9 is a real isometry onto the two physical coefficient bands.
Pull these four coefficients back to real lattice l2 vectors and truncate
each to finitely many cells. The truncated vectors approximate them in
l2 to any chosen accuracy. CAR norm continuity and finite product
telescoping give a formally selfadjoint finite-cell even polynomial R_c^fin
with norm(R_c^fin-R_c)<=epsilon, uniformly chosen epsilon>0.
Its finite word count and degree are fixed; the cell hull may vary with c.

These lattice coefficients are literal ordinary-cell images of smooth
compact functions, not a replacement coefficient map. Choose a real
smooth bump b supported inside (0,1), with integral b=1. For a finitely
supported lattice vector z=(z_j), set

    f_c(x)=c^(-1/2)sum_j z_j b((x-cj)/c).                 (P28)

Then (C_c f_c)_j=z_j, f_c is real smooth compact, and
norm(f_c)=norm(b)norm(z). Thus the four input L2 norms can be uniformly
bounded. Their derivatives generally scale as c^-k; no uniform H^k or
commutator budget is available. Each resulting even polynomial is an
actual finite-hull spin observable at its spacing.

Let theta_0=pi/2, choose a nonnegative smooth h supported in (0,1) with
integral h=1, and use a single pulse of duration tau_c=delta c with

    V_c^prep(t)=(theta_0/tau_c)h(t/tau_c) R_c^fin.         (P29)

Each pulse is temporally smooth and its integrated operator norm is at most
theta_0(1+epsilon), independent of c. Its first commutator action is
necessarily unbounded: norm([H_c,R_c^fin])>=Lambda_c(1-epsilon), by applying
