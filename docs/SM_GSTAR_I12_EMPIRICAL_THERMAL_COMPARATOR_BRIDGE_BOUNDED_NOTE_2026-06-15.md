## Claim

Given the declared comparator-premise packet:

- the declared empirical small-neutrino-mass band
  `m_nu in {0.05 eV, 0.1 eV, 0.6 eV}` as representative comparator values;
- the one-Higgs convention `<H> = 174 GeV`, so a Dirac Yukawa obeys
  `y_nu = m_nu / <H>`;
- the radiation-era comparator
  `Gamma_nuR ~ y_nu^2 T` and
  `H ~ 1.66 sqrt(g_*) T^2 / M_Pl` with `g_* = 427/4`;

the empirical small-neutrino-mass comparator implies
`y_nu ~ 3e-13` to `3e-12`, while thermalization requires

```text
y_thr(T) = sqrt(1.66 sqrt(g_*) T / M_Pl).
```

At the most lenient census temperature `T = 100 GeV`,
`y_thr ~ 1.2e-8`, so the `0.05 eV` and `0.1 eV` comparators are more than
four decades below thermalization threshold, while the deliberately generous
`0.6 eV` edge remains more than three decades below it. At `T = 1e9 GeV` and
`T = 1e12 GeV` the gap is larger. Conversely, forcing thermalization at
`100 GeV` implies
`m_nu = y_thr <H> > 1 keV`, far above the declared sub-eV comparator band.

Therefore the I12 `nu_R` thermal-exclusion branch is supported by this
comparator packet, conditioned on the declared empirical small `m_nu` and
on the standard order-of-magnitude thermalization estimate. This is
bounded support only, not a framework-native derivation of either input.
