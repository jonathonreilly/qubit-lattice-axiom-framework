- the one-Higgs convention `<H> = 174 GeV`, so a Dirac Yukawa obeys

`y_nu ~ 3e-13` to `3e-12`, while thermalization requires

`y_thr ~ 1.2e-8`, so the `0.05 eV` and `0.1 eV` comparators are more than
four decades below thermalization threshold, while the deliberately generous
`0.6 eV` edge remains more than three decades below it. At `T = 1e9 GeV` and
`T = 1e12 GeV` the gap is larger. Conversely, forcing thermalization at
`100 GeV` implies
`m_nu = y_thr <H> > 1 keV`, far above the admitted sub-eV comparator band.

Therefore the I12 `nu_R` thermal-exclusion branch is supported by this
comparator packet, conditioned on the admitted empirical small `m_nu` and

## 2026-06-18 prefactor-robustness repair

The source-side risk in this packet is not the exact arithmetic; it is whether
the textbook comparator constants are doing hidden load-bearing work. This
repair makes the margin executable.

Write the thermal comparison with an adversarial relative prefactor

```text
Gamma_nuR / H  =  E * y_nu^2 M_Pl / (1.66 sqrt(g_*) T),
E = c_Gamma / c_H.
```

Here `E > 1` is the hostile direction: it strengthens `nu_R` production and/or
weakens expansion. The runner verifies the whole grid

```text
m_nu in {0.05, 0.1, 0.6} eV,
T in {100 GeV, 1e9 GeV, 1e12 GeV},
E in {1e-4, 1e-2, 1, 1e2, 1e4}.
```

Even at the deliberately generous `m_nu = 0.6 eV` edge, the most favorable
thermalization point (`T = 100 GeV`, `E = 1e4`) gives

```text

```

Equivalently, a thermalizing Dirac Yukawa at `T = 100 GeV` still implies
`m_nu > 20 eV` under that same hostile `E = 1e4`, which remains more than
thirty times above the `0.6 eV` generous comparator edge. Thus the conclusion
does not rest on the exact `1.66`, exact rate prefactor, or an O(1)-O(100)
channel-multiplicity choice. The remaining load-bearing inputs are exactly the
ones already named: the empirical small-`m_nu` comparator and the parametric
scalings `Gamma ~ y_nu^2 T`, `H ~ T^2/M_Pl`.
