## 1. The `nu_R` equilibration channel

The right-handed neutrino `nu_R = (1,1)_0` is a **complete gauge singlet**: it
carries zero color, zero isospin, and zero hypercharge. It therefore has **no**
gauge interaction with the thermal bath. Its **only** renormalizable coupling
to the Standard-Model fields is the Dirac Yukawa

```text
L_Yuk  =  - y_nu  L_bar  tilde{H}  nu_R  +  h.c.,
```

coupling `nu_R` to the gauge-charged operator `H . L` (the lepton doublet `L_L`
and the Higgs doublet `H`, both of which **are** in thermal equilibrium via
their gauge interactions). Consequently `nu_R` can equilibrate with the bath
**only** through processes proportional to `y_nu^2` (inverse decays
`L H -> nu_R`, and `2 <-> 2` scatterings such as `L H <-> ...` mediated by the
Yukawa vertex). Its equilibration rate at high `T`, where masses are negligible,
is

```text
Gamma_nu_R  ~  y_nu^2  T                                          (order of magnitude).
```

This is the single physical fact that governs I12: **the only knob that can
bring `nu_R` into the light-relativistic census is the size of `y_nu`.**

## 3. The Yukawa needed for `nu_R` thermalization (equilibration threshold)

A gauge-singlet equilibrates when its interaction rate overtakes the Hubble
expansion. With the radiation-era Hubble rate

```text
H  ~  1.66  sqrt(g_*)  T^2 / M_Pl,        M_Pl = 1.22e19 GeV,
```

the equilibration criterion `Gamma_nu_R >~ H` becomes

```text
y_nu^2  T  >~  1.66  sqrt(g_*)  T^2 / M_Pl
=>  y_nu^2  >~  1.66  sqrt(g_*)  T / M_Pl
=>  y_thr   ~  sqrt( 1.66  sqrt(g_*)  T / M_Pl ).
```

The threshold **rises** with temperature, so the **easiest** epoch for `nu_R` to
thermalize (the smallest required `y_nu`) is the **lowest** census temperature:

```text
T = 100 GeV (EW scale, most lenient)        ->  y_thr ~ 1.2e-8,
T = 1e9 GeV (leptogenesis)                  ->  y_thr ~ 3.7e-5,
T = 1e12 GeV (leptogenesis)                 ->  y_thr ~ 1.2e-3.
```

(The `Gamma ~ y_nu^2 T` estimate is an order-of-magnitude equilibration
criterion; it is the same parametric form that underlies the well-known
right-handed-Dirac-neutrino decoupling bounds in the cosmology literature. It is
used here only as an order-of-magnitude comparator, not as a derivation input,
and the conclusion below holds with several orders of magnitude of margin, so it
is insensitive to O(1)-O(100) prefactor choices.)

## 4. Incompatibility: the implied Yukawa is far below threshold (the core argument)

The Yukawa implied by the empirical small neutrino mass (§2) and the Yukawa
needed for thermalization (§3) are mutually exclusive by **many orders of
magnitude**, even in the most favourable case for thermalization:

```text
implied (m_nu = 0.1 eV)         :  y_nu  ~  5.7e-13,
threshold at T = 100 GeV (best) :  y_thr ~  1.2e-8,
margin                          :  ~ 4.3 decades  (>= 4 orders),
threshold at T = 1e9 GeV        :  y_thr ~  3.7e-5,
margin                          :  ~ 7.8 decades  (>= 7 orders).
```

Equivalently, for the empirically-implied Yukawa,

```text
Gamma_nu_R / H  ~  2.4e-9   at T = 100 GeV,        (<< 1)
```

so `nu_R` falls out of (in fact never reaches) equilibrium at every census
epoch. The reverse statement is the steelman's test (§6): to make `nu_R`
thermalize at `T = 100 GeV` one needs `y_nu >= y_thr ~ 1.2e-8`, which by §2 forces
`m_nu = y_nu <H> >~ 2 keV` — vastly above the sub-eV empirical bound. Higher
census temperatures force an even larger `m_nu`. **A thermalized `nu_R` is
incompatible with the empirical small neutrino mass.**
