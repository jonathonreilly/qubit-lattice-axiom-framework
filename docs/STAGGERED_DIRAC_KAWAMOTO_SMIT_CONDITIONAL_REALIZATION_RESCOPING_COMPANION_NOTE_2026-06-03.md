## §0. Why this exists (audit-rejection vector)

An audit-readiness panel reviewing the parent KS forcing theorem flagged
that **Step 3 of the parent proof introduces the identification**

```
ω(x) = ε(x) · ω_global                                                      (S3)
```

with `ω_global = +i` (canonical positive chirality) and `ε(x) = (-1)^(x_1+x_2+x_3)`
**as "the natural identification" — but this is an ANSATZ that selects a
specific identification among possibilities consistent with the cited
primitives (F1 fermion-parity Z_2 grading, U2 per-site Cl(3) uniqueness,
BPG bipartite-graph parity), not a forced derivation.**

Specifically, the panel's objection is:

- F1 (Z_2 fermion-parity grading) is a per-site grading on the Fock
  space; it does NOT pin a SPATIAL identification of per-site chirality
  with sublattice parity. F1 says only that `(-1)^F` exists at each site.
- U2 (per-site Cl(3) uniqueness with central pseudoscalar ω) fixes that
  `ω` exists at each site with eigenvalues `±i`; it does NOT pick the
  sign assignment site-by-site.
- BPG (bipartite-graph parity) gives the geometric `ε(x) ∈ {+1, -1}` on
  Z³; it does NOT couple `ε(x)` to the algebraic Cl(3) chirality
  eigenvalue at that site.
- Composing F1 + U2 + BPG gives at most: at each site, both a Z_2
  fermion-parity grade AND a Cl(3) chirality eigenvalue exist. The
  IDENTIFICATION `ω(x) = ε(x) · ω_global` is one specific choice among
  several compatible with the cited primitives:
  - the constant assignment `ω(x) = ω_global` (no sublattice coupling)
  - the inverse assignment `ω(x) = -ε(x) · ω_global` (opposite sign)
  - more general site-dependent sign functions compatible with F1
  - the staggered assignment `ω(x) = ε(x) · ω_global` (the one used)

The parent says "natural identification ... is forced (up to global
sign) by sublattice structure + Cl(3) chirality + Z_2-grading
assignment" — but does not exhibit a uniqueness lemma showing that the
staggered assignment is the *only* compatible choice rather than the
*natural-looking* one. **This is the real audit-rejection vector.**

The panel identified two repair routes:

- **(a) heavier:** derive `(S3)` from F1 + U2 + BPG via a real uniqueness
  lemma. This is genuine new lemma work and may not succeed without
  additional inputs.
- **(b) lighter:** rescope the parent theorem so that `(S3)` is an
  explicit named conditional hypothesis rather than a derived step. The
  rescoped theorem still says "Kawamoto-Smit phases η_1=1,
  η_2=(-1)^{x_1}, η_3=(-1)^{x_1+x_2} are uniquely forced ... GIVEN the
  staggered-chirality assignment".

The user's chosen route is **(b)**. This companion implements it.

## §3. The conditional premise `H_staggered-chirality`

**`H_staggered-chirality` (Staggered-chirality assignment, conditional
premise).** The per-site Cl(3) central pseudoscalar `ω = γ_1 γ_2 γ_3` is
assigned a site-dependent sign in coordination with the bipartite Z_2
sublattice parity:

```
ω(x) := ε(x) · ω_global,     ω_global = +i · I_2  (canonical positive
                                                   chirality)            (H)

where ε(x) = (−1)^{x_1+x_2+x_3} is the bipartite-graph parity from
Step 1 of the parent.
```

Equivalently: sites with `x_1+x_2+x_3 ≡ 0 (mod 2)` carry per-site Cl(3)
chirality eigenvalue `+i`, sites with `x_1+x_2+x_3 ≡ 1 (mod 2)` carry
`-i`.

**This is an explicitly NAMED conditional hypothesis — NOT a derived
step.** The framework primitives A1, A2, F1, U2, BPG, and NR do not
uniquely force `(H)`. They permit `(H)`, as well as the constant
assignment `ω(x) = ω_global` (no sublattice coupling), the inverse
assignment, and more general site-dependent sign functions. `(H)` is the
*staggered* choice among the compatible identifications.

**Provenance of `H_staggered-chirality`:** In the historical literature,
the staggered-chirality assignment is the choice made by Kawamoto and
Smit (1981) when they construct the staggered-fermion action. It is
adopted in the framework as the choice that yields the Kawamoto-Smit
phases and connects to the Block 03 sub-structure (which is downstream
of this companion in the dependency graph). The choice is *natural* in
the sense that it is the unique compatible assignment that respects the
bipartite sublattice structure; but "natural" is not "derived". This
companion makes the conditional dependence explicit.

**Honest scope of `H_staggered-chirality`:**

- `(H)` is a per-site coordination rule, not an axiom about Hilbert
  space or local algebra. It does not introduce new operators or new
  states. It selects a sign function on Z³.
- `(H)` is consistent with F1 (a Z_2 fermion-parity grading does exist
  at each site under `(H)`), with U2 (the central pseudoscalar exists
  at each site), and with BPG (the bipartite parity is the unmodified
  graph-theoretic ε).
- The rescoped theorem does NOT claim `(H)` is the *unique* compatible
  choice. Under the rescoping, alternative compatible choices (constant
  assignment, etc.) yield *different* kinetic operators on the
  framework's Hilbert space — Kawamoto-Smit phases are specific to
  `(H)`.
