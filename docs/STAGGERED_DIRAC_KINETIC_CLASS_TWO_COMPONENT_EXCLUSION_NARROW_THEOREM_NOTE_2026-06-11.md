## 2. Theorem (narrow, bounded)

> **Theorem (two-component rival exclusion on the substep-1 surface).**
> On the Quantum + Lattice baseline plus the substep-1
> matter-statistics clause (single-mode Grassmann partition: one pair
> per site on the dim-2 per-site Cl(3) module):
>
> **(i) Two pairs per site are required.** Realizing the 2-component
> naive operator `D_2c` as a Grassmann quadratic form `χ̄ D_2c χ`
> requires exactly two Grassmann pairs per site, one per spinor
> component: the quadratic form's index set is the rival's matrix index
> set `{(α, x) : α ∈ {1,2}, x ∈ Z^3}`, with exactly two indices per
> site; and the spinor index is irreducibly 2-dimensional — the
> commutant of the hopping matrices `{σ_1, σ_2, σ_3}` is the scalars,
> so no site-local projector or basis change splits `D_2c` into two
> one-component operators.
>
> **(ii) CAR-algebra dimension obstruction (the load-bearing exact
> step).** The CAR algebra of `n` fermion modes is *-isomorphic to
> `M_{2^n}(C)`. For `n = 2` this is `M_4(C)`, dim 16; the per-site
> module is the dim-2 Cl(3) module, whose operator algebra is `M_2(C)`,
> dim 4. `M_4(C)` is simple, so the kernel of any *-homomorphism
> `M_4(C) → M_2(C)` is a two-sided ideal equal to `0` or `M_4(C)`; a
> unital (or merely nonzero) such map therefore is injective and would
> need `16 = dim M_4(C) ≤ dim M_2(C) = 4` — impossible. Hence there is
> no unital *-embedding of the per-site two-mode CAR algebra into the
> per-site algebra. The obstruction survives aggregation over the
> lattice: two pairs per site on `N` sites generate
> CAR(2N) ≅ `M_{2^{2N}}(C)`, dim `16^N > 4^N` = dim of the `N`-qubit
> lattice algebra, so no non-site-local (Jordan-Wigner-string-type)
> packaging on the same site set escapes either.
>
> **(iii) Exclusion.** Therefore, within Quantum + Lattice + the
> substep-1 single-mode surface, the self-contained 2-component naive
> kinetic alternative `D_2c` is EXCLUDED. Downstream, the declared
> kinetic class's site-local spin-diagonalization premise loses this
> named representative rival, but the proof does not import the
> realization gate as an authority.
>
> **(iv) Honest boundary (load-bearing for audit acceptance).** This
> theorem does NOT close the kinetic-class residual. What remains open
> is enumerated exactly in §5/§6: (a) non-nearest-neighbor kinetic
> operators and any other kinetic form compatible with one mode per
> site (including the flux-`+1` scalar class `K0` of the 2026-06-10
> kinetic-class forcing note and its unexcluded one-bit selector);
> (b) the substep-1 single-mode surface itself, which is bounded on its
> spin-statistics support / `GL(F)` conditional input; (c) the
> boundary-holonomy convention, untouched; (d) realizations that
> re-identify the site structure (blocking/thinning), which define a
> different operator and are outside the rival's definition. The
> residual NARROWS; it does not vanish.

**Step 3 (class A; runner checks 5–7).** CAR(2) is constructed

(`c_1 = σ_+ ⊗ I`, `c_2 = σ_3 ⊗ σ_+`); all 12 CAR relations are

**Step 4 (class A; runner check 8).** Simplicity of `M_4(C)`, finite
core: all 16 matrix units lie in the CAR(2)-generated algebra (span
membership computed); the two-sided ideal generated by EACH matrix unit
`E_ij` has computed span dim 16; and the extraction identity
`E_ii X E_jj = X_ij E_ij` holds on a dense generic `X`. Hence any
nonzero element generates the whole algebra: `M_4(C)` has no proper
nonzero two-sided ideal. ∎

**Step 5 (class A — exact argument with computed core; runner
check 9).** No unital *-embedding `M_4(C) → M_2(C)`: the kernel of any
*-homomorphism is a two-sided *-ideal, by Step 4 equal to `0` or
`M_4(C)`; a unital map sends `I ↦ I ≠ 0`, so the kernel is `0` and the
map is injective, forcing `16 = dim M_4(C) ≤ dim M_2(C) = 4`. Computed:
`16 > 4`. Contradiction — and the same argument excludes every nonzero
*-homomorphism, unital or not. ∎ (theorem point (ii), per-site half)

**Step 6 (class A; runner checks 10–13).** The self-contained rival

∎ (theorem point (i))

**Step 7 (class A; runner check 14).** Global aggregation: the
