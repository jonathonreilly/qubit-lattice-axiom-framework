## 2. Theorem (no-go within A_min)

> **Theorem (AC_φλ no-go within A_min).** Under the physical Cl(3) local
> algebra plus Z^3 spatial substrate baseline + the
> substep-1/2/3 upstream stack + the two 2026-05-17 substep-4 AC
> bridges + NQ + forbidden imports, statement `(D)` of §1.2 does NOT
> admit a proof. That is, no canonical species-identification bijection
> `π : {c_1, c_2, c_3} → L_3` is derivable from A_min.

### 2.1 Proof outline

The proof proceeds via a **structural invariant** that A_min preserves
but `(D)` would have to break.

**Invariant (orbit-equivariance under C_3).**
> Every property of `{c_1, c_2, c_3}` derivable from A_min is invariant
> under the cyclic permutation `C : c_α ↦ c_{(α mod 3) + 1}` (up to
> simultaneous relabeling of the identifier names α ∈ {1,2,3}).

By contrast, the existence of a canonical bijection
`π : {c_1, c_2, c_3} → L_3` distinguishes a **specific** element of
the C_3 orbit of bijections (there are exactly 3 C_3-equivariant
bijections, all related by global cyclic relabeling). A canonical π
would be a property of `{c_1, c_2, c_3}` that is NOT invariant under C_3
in the necessary sense — it would pick out one of the three orbit
elements as derivationally preferred over the others.

The invariant argument shows: such preference cannot be a theorem of
A_min unless an A_min-derivable C_3-breaking element exists. By the
10-probe A3 obstruction campaign (cited in §1.4), no such element
exists. Therefore `(D)` cannot be derived from A_min. □

### 2.2 Counter-models (witness)

A no-go is most cleanly proved by exhibiting two A_min-indistinguishable
"worlds" that disagree on the conclusion. We exhibit two such worlds.

**World A — identity labeling.** π_A is defined by `π_A(c_α) = ℓ_α`
for α = 1, 2, 3.

**World B — single-cycle relabeling.** π_B is defined by
`π_B(c_α) = ℓ_{(α mod 3) + 1}` for α = 1, 2, 3, i.e., π_B = π_A ∘ C
where C is the cyclic generator.

**Indistinguishability claim.** For every A_min-derivable proposition
`P(π)` about the species map π, `P(π_A) ⟺ P(π_B)`. Equivalently, no
A_min-derivable statement can distinguish π_A from π_B.

**Argument.** Let `P(π)` be A_min-derivable. By the orbit-equivariance
invariant (§2.1), `P(π)` is C_3-equivariant in the carrier labels.
Since π_B = π_A ∘ C with C acting only on the carrier basis (not on
the label set L_3), and since `P` cannot depend on the carrier labels
in a C_3-distinguishing way, `P(π_A) ⟺ P(π_B)`. □

**Concrete instances of indistinguishability.**
- Three-orbit-eigenvalue statement: "the three corner-basis states
  have equal expectation value `a = Tr(H)/3` under every
  C_3-equivariant Hermitian H" — holds under both π_A and π_B.
- Off-diagonal-vanishing statement: "every K commuting with the
  three lattice translations is diagonal in the corner basis"
  (the AC_λ bridge corollary) — holds under both π_A and π_B.
- NQ statement: "M_3(C) on hw=1 has no proper exact quotient" —
  holds under both π_A and π_B.

The runner exhibits all three propositions explicitly under both π_A
and π_B, verifying the indistinguishability claim on a concrete
finite carrier.

### 2.3 Exhaustiveness of closure paths

Suppose for contradiction that (D) admits a proof from A_min plus some
**single** additional premise X not in A_min. We claim X must reduce to
one of the three named premises in §3 (P1 / P2 / P3).

Argument: X must distinguish π_A from π_B (by the indistinguishability
of §2.2). The distinguishing content of X is therefore either:

(i) a **stipulated bijection** (X says "the answer is π_A" by fiat) —
    this is P1 (labeling-convention premise);
(ii) a **C_3-breaking dynamical element** that prefers one orbit element
    via a derivable dynamical asymmetry — this is P2 (C_3-breaking
    dynamics premise);
(iii) an **empirical fact** (e.g., observed lepton mass values) that
    fixes the bijection via observed-spectrum matching — this is P3
    (PDG-empirical premise).

There is no fourth distinguishing content for X under the framework's
forbidden-imports policy (no new framework primitive, no PDG, no C_3-breaking
primitive). Hence P1/P2/P3 enumerate ALL closure routes.
