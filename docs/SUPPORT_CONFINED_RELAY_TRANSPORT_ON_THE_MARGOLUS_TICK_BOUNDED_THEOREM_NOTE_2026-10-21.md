# Support-confined relay transport on the Margolus tick

Claim type: bounded_theorem.

The switching rule of the candidate-law register on the source branch (proportional shortfall switching among connected targets) does not keep Born statistics on one Margolus block: the worst inflow error over 300 sampled states is 0.317. That register is not the [candidate-law register](CANDIDATE_LAWS.md) on `main`, whose CL-1 to CL-3 aliases are other clauses; the rules used here are defined in this note. A transport that uses only nonzero elements of the half-tick always exists. For any target set S, P_S U = P_S U P_{N^-1(S)}, so with U unitary ||P_S U psi||^2 <= ||P_{N^-1(S)} psi||^2. That is the supply-demand (Hall) condition, and max-flow/min-cut then gives a plan. Its largest excess over all 254 sets is 5.6e-16. The earlier "minimal switching" of the straight-pairing picture (PR #9885, which is not on `main` or this branch; its scope is recorded only in the source map of `docs/RECORDS_BORN_CONSOLIDATED_BOUNDED_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-10-30.md`) also keeps Born, but only by sending 32 to 100 per cent (mean 68 per cent) of each half-tick's switched mass along zero elements of W.

The law tested here, a supplied candidate with zero premise weight: the configuration moves by the plan that (i) uses only nonzero elements, staying included, (ii) has row sums equal to the old odds and column sums equal to the new odds, (iii) changes the fewest mode occupations on average, and (iv) has the largest entropy among such plans.

Clause (iii) alone need not give a single plan. For the 60 sampled random states at each mu, the optimal face had dimension 10 at mu = 0, where no stays are allowed, and dimension 5 at mu = 0.3 (B1). This is a finite sample, not every state. The statement "clause (iii) alone never gives a single plan" is false. Counterexample: a basis state psi = e_i has source odds p_s = delta_si. Nonnegativity and the zero row sums for s != i force every other row of a feasible plan to vanish, and the target margins then force J(i, t) = q_t = |U(t, i)|^2. That is the only feasible plan, so it is the unique least-cost plan, with face dimension 0, at mu = 0 and at mu = 0.3, and it uses only nonzero elements of U. The runner checks this for all 8 basis states at both mu (B1b). An LP vertex depends on the labels (95 per cent of cases). Clause (iv) gives one plan, because entropy is strictly concave on the nonempty convex optimal face. It is equivariant under relabelling (to 1.7e-16) and under translation. Under perturbations of size 1e-6 of the sampled states, the largest finite-difference ratio |dJ|/|dpsi| was 0.72; this is a finite diagnostic, not a Lipschitz bound. Least squares is also unique and equivariant, but it couples unentangled relays (5.4e-3), while max entropy does not (1.9e-15). Counting the whole configuration as staying, rather than counting modes, also couples unentangled relays (0.077).

Results. Over 16 half-ticks the distribution stays the odds to 1.8e-17 for one relay and to 3.4e-16 in total variation for two entangled relays. With relay number free, relays begin and end in pairs, the total variation stays at 7.4e-11, and no block value is violated. Moving each relay by its own marginal fails, with total variation 0.343. CHSH reaches 2.83. Two readers of one system (F3): with ideal ballistic pointers (muP = 0) they never disagree, against 0.418 when each pointer moves by its own marginal. With massive pointers (muP = 0.4), whose odds spread, they do disagree, with frequency 0.2341 against the Born disagreement 0.2339; the joint transport reproduces the Born law, disagreement included, while own-marginal pointers give 0.4545. Correction (2026-11-14): the earlier wording "gives agreeing readers" and "two readers never disagree" holds only for the ideal ballistic pointers. A step acting only on A never moves B. For plane waves the mean drift equals the group velocity to 1e-10, for any block-confined transport.

Relays jump on 53 to 100 per cent of half-ticks and zigzag. In 5 of 8 (K along x) and 4 of 8 (K along xy) mu = 0.3 plane waves, the per-tick chain splits into two sublattice streams that never mix, for example at 1.507 and 0.513 site/tick against a group velocity of 0.839. Relays land on sites they have already burnt on 44.5 per cent of half-ticks at mu = 0.3 and 2.3 per cent at mu = 0. One transport per half-tick differs from two block-by-block transports of the same unitary: slicing moves 0.37 to 0.67 more modes.

Supplied: the configuration space, the cost (modes moved), the max-entropy tie-break, the half-tick as the transport step, the basis rotations, the pointer couplings, the coins and pulse, and the states.
