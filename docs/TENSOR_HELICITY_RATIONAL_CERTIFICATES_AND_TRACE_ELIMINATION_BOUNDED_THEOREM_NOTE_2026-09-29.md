Let n be a real unit direction, M(n) a real symmetric 3 by 3 tensor linear
in n, z=M n, and f=n^T M n. The squared Frobenius helicity weights are

    H1(M,n)=2(z^T z-f^2),
    TT(M,n)=tr(M^2)-2z^T z+f^2-(tr(M)-f)^2/2.

Angular brackets mean normalized integration over the unit sphere.
Expanding the 18 real coefficients of M, exact sphere integration gives

    <H1> >= <TT>/4.

This is an inequality for linear tensor moments, not for arbitrary
momentum-dependent tensors. The runner constructs the rational quadratic
form Q=<H1-TT/4> and an exact LDL^T factorization, checks its reconstruction,
all 18 nonnegative pivots and rank 10. Sphere monomials with any odd exponent
integrate to zero; even moments are the product of (power-1) double factorials
divided by (total power+1) double factorial. This supplies an algebraic
certificate for every real coefficient vector, rather than sampled directions.
The corresponding statement for complex coefficients follows by applying
the real inequality separately to the real and imaginary parts.

The scalar-compatible subspace

    |n|^2 tr M - n^T M n = 0

has polynomial constraint rank 10 and dimension 8. It is exactly spanned by

    M(n)=sym(n tensor xi)+sym([n]_cross A),

where xi is any vector and A is any symmetric trace-free matrix; sym includes
a factor 1/2. Independence and constraint satisfaction are exact coefficient
identities. On this subspace direct integration gives

    <H1>-<TT>/4 = |xi|^2/3.

The full vector constraint M(n)n=0 has rank 18, so such linear moments vanish.
The transverse-only constraint n cross M(n)n=0 has rank 15, with the
three-dimensional space M(n)=ell(n) I as its kernel. These are linear
polynomial classifications under the stated constraints, not classifications
of all finite-slot Hamiltonians or composite phases.

For completeness a second exact polynomial control uses symmetric trace-free
T, quadratic n dependence and the 24 proper cubic rotations. Its 90-dimensional
coefficient space has a Reynolds projection of rank 6. Requiring the derivative
of the form to vanish on all helicity-one tensors sym(n tensor (n cross u))
leaves a one-dimensional subspace, generated by

    F_EH(n,T)=|n|^2 tr(T^2)/2-|T n|^2.

The runner checks all polynomial coefficients, not a finite set of directions.
On unit-direction normalized helicity-two and helicity-zero tensors this form
has weights 1/2 and -1/6. Thus this particular helicity-one-annihilating cubic
quadratic form is not positive on the whole spin-two space. Positivity on a
constraint quotient is a different question; a negative off-shell trace does
not itself prove a physical ghost.

## Trace elimination: the missing hypothesis must be stated

Assume a finite positive Gram of local move amplitudes. In a small momentum
parameter epsilon, suppose the single trace column is a+epsilon c+O(epsilon^2),
with a nonzero constant vector in the space of move families, while all other
columns have the form epsilon B(n)+O(epsilon^2). Eliminating this gapped trace
column by its Schur complement yields

    V_eff=epsilon^2 B(n)^* P B(n)+O(epsilon^3),
    P=I-aa^*/(a^*a).

P is a constant orthogonal projector in family space. Projecting each first
moment family by P preserves linear dependence on n, so the angular quarter
inequality applies to the effective family too. If a=0 there is no gapped
trace elimination of this form and the unprojected quarter inequality applies.
The runner independently checks the leading Schur coefficient with arbitrary
symbolic first moments and trace correction c. The constant-projector argument
extends to any finite number of families by the same displayed matrix algebra.

This premise is essential: an arbitrary analytic positive matrix symbol is
not asserted to admit this finite local positive-Gram representation. An
unreduced helicity weight can disappear after eliminating a gapped sector;
the runner supplies a small exact example. Neither inequality alone determines
frequencies: inertia, physical constraints, positivity on the actual quotient,
and the state/readout bridge remain separate obligations. No universal
partner theorem or finite-site gravity no-go is claimed by this note.
