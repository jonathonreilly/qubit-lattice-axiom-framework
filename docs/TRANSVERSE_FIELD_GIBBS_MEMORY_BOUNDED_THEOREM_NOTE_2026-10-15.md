## 1. Result and hypotheses

Let V be a finite qubit set, with a finite ordinary nearest-neighbor edge set, and count each undirected edge once. Take real finite coefficients and the Hermitian Hamiltonian

\[
G=\sum_i h_i n_i+\sum_{e=\{i,j\}}(J_e n_i n_j+c_e I)
 +k_0I+\sum_i(g_iI-a_iX_i-b_iY_i),
\quad g_i=\sqrt{a_i^2+b_i^2},\quad n_i=(I-Z_i)/2.
\]

Assume

\[
c_e\ge\max(0,-J_e),\qquad k_0\ge0,\qquad
h_i-\sum_{e\ni i}\max(0,-J_e)\ge\gamma>0,
\]

and \(0<\beta_{\min}\le\beta<\infty\), \(0\le g_i\le g_{\max}<\infty\). Define the probability law

\[
p(x)=\langle x|e^{-\beta G}|x\rangle/\operatorname{Tr}(e^{-\beta G}).
\]

Then every complete one-bit conditional, for every site i and every computational record y on all other sites, satisfies

\[
\boxed{\quad p(x_i=1\mid x_{-i}=y)\le
q_*(\beta,\gamma,g_i)\le\bar q<\tfrac12,\quad}
\]

where

\[
q_*(\beta,\gamma,g)=\frac12\left[1-
\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\gamma^2+4g^2}}
\tanh\!\left(\frac\beta2\sqrt{\gamma^2+4g^2}\right)\right],
\qquad
\bar q=q_*(\beta_{\min},\gamma,g_{\max}).
\]

The bound is independent of graph size, signed-edge pattern, degree, the size of a particular conditioning probability and the positive scalar reserves. It is sharp within this class: a single isolated qubit with h=gamma, g=g_max and beta=beta_min attains it. A graph with zero-coupling neighboring sites gives the same example if an ambient connected NN graph is required.

The conclusion is a positive result for this exact onsite transverse-field form. It does not assert a similar bound for arbitrary noncommuting off-diagonal couplings. The proof below conditions on full imaginary-time paths, uses an elementary one-dimensional comparison, and only then conditions on the observed computational record. No global spatial ferromagnetism or independence assumption is used.

## 2. Positivity and removal of the onsite phases

The margin implies h_i>=gamma>0. Thus h_i n_i is positive. The edge block J_e n_i n_j+c_e I has eigenvalues c_e and c_e+J_e, both nonnegative. The onsite block g_i I-a_iX_i-b_iY_i has eigenvalues 0 and 2g_i. Together with k_0 I, these show G>=0 on the whole qubit space without requiring the summands to commute.

For g_i>0 choose phi_i with a_i=g_i cos(phi_i), b_i=g_i sin(phi_i), and let R_i=exp(-i phi_i Z_i/2). Then

\[
R_iX_iR_i^*=\cos\phi_i X_i+\sin\phi_i Y_i.
\]

For g_i=0 use phi_i=0. The product R is diagonal in the computational basis, so it leaves every computational diagonal entry unchanged. It commutes with all n_i and n_i n_j, and

\[
R^*GR=C I+D-T,\qquad
D=\sum_i h_i n_i+\sum_eJ_e n_i n_j,\quad
T=\sum_i g_iX_i,\quad
C=k_0+\sum_e c_e+\sum_i g_i.
\]

The scalar C cancels from normalized probabilities and conditional ratios. This is an algebraic simplification for this probability calculation, not permission to remove its physical action, work or channel cost. The onsite phases are therefore harmless for the stated diagonal question; this argument would not remove arbitrary multi-site phases or general off-diagonal interactions.

## 3. A finite positive path measure

For an integer m>=3 put Delta=beta/m and

\[
Q_m=(e^{-\Delta D}e^{\Delta T})^m.
\]

All factors act on a fixed finite-dimensional space. The product formula gives Q_m -> exp[-beta(D-T)] in operator norm as m tends to infinity. One way to see the needed convergence is to expand the two one-step products: e^(-Delta D)e^(Delta T)=I+Delta(T-D)+O(Delta^2), whereas e^(Delta(T-D)) has the same linear term. For the fixed finite operators, a telescoping product estimate bounds the difference of their mth powers by a constant times m Delta^2, with a finite exponential norm factor. No rate uniform in graph size is needed or claimed.

Since different X_i commute,

\[
\langle x|e^{\Delta T}|x'\rangle=\prod_i w_i(x_i,x_i'),
\quad
w_i(u,v)=\begin{cases}\cosh(\Delta g_i),&u=v,\\
\sinh(\Delta g_i),&u\ne v.\end{cases}
\]

Consequently Tr(Q_m) is the sum of nonnegative weights over periodic records x^0,...,x^(m-1), x^m=x^0:

\[
W_m(x^0,\ldots,x^{m-1})=
\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}e^{-\Delta D(x^t)}
\prod_i w_i(x_i^t,x_i^{t+1}).
\]

Its normalized endpoint law is p_m(x^0)=<x^0|Q_m|x^0>/Tr(Q_m). Although the nonsymmetric product Q_m need not be Hermitian at finite m, its displayed diagonal and trace are positive real quantities and the path measure is an ordinary probability measure. Constant paths give positive weight to every endpoint record, including when some g_i vanish. Operator-norm convergence then gives p_m(x)->p(x), with the scalar and diagonal gauge already removed.

This path measure is a mathematical representation. Constructing it for the proof does not supply a sampler, bath, imaginary-time evolution, state preparation or controller.

## 4. Condition on all other paths, not just their endpoints

Fix a site i, and condition on a complete choice of all paths x_j^t for j!=i having nonzero probability. Write u_t=x_i^t. Every factor independent of u cancels from this conditional law. The remaining weight is exactly

\[
\mu_{d,g_i}(u)\ \propto\
\exp\left[-\Delta\sum_{t=0}^{m-1}d_t u_t\right]
\prod_{t=0}^{m-1}w_i(u_t,u_{t+1}),\qquad u_m=u_0,
\]

with

\[
d_t=h_i+\sum_{e=\{i,j\}}J_e x_j^t
\ge h_i-\sum_{e\ni i}\max(0,-J_e)\ge\gamma.
\]

This is the point where all signed or frustrated spatial edges are handled. The lower bound holds for each other-path record, not merely on average or on typical paths. There is no need to compare different spatial configurations by an attractive global interaction. The conditional problem is a single ring in imaginary time with nonnegative temporal coupling.

When g_i=0 only constant u paths have nonzero weight. Their relative weight immediately gives

\[
\mu_{d,0}(u_0=1)=\frac1{1+\exp(\Delta\sum_t d_t)}
\le\frac1{1+e^{\beta\gamma}}.
\]

The remaining proof takes g_i>0. Both temporal weights are then strictly positive.

## 5. Elementary comparison for an inhomogeneous temporal field

For g>0 write z_t=1-2u_t and

\[
K_m=\tfrac12\log\coth(\Delta g)>0.
\]

Up to a common positive constant, w(u_t,u_(t+1))=exp[K_m z_t z_(t+1)]. The ring law is therefore

\[
\mu_{d,g}(u)\propto
\exp\left[-\Delta\sum_t d_t u_t+
K_m\sum_t(1-2u_t)(1-2u_{t+1})\right].
\]

We need the following elementary fact, established here: replacing each d_t>=gamma by gamma can only increase each bit's probability of being 1. For a single-coordinate conditional, the two adjacent edges give

\[
\mu_{d,g}(u_t=1\mid u_{-t})=
\frac1{1+\exp\{\Delta d_t-4K_m(u_{t-1}+u_{t+1}-1)\}}.
\]

This probability increases with each neighbor bit and decreases with d_t. Consider two mathematical single-site update chains, using the same uniformly chosen coordinate and the same uniform random number for that conditional update. The lower chain uses the fields d_t and the upper chain uses all fields gamma. If their bit strings are ordered coordinatewise, the displayed conditional probabilities preserve that order after a coupled update. Start both at the all-zero string.

For fixed finite m, all one-site conditional probabilities lie strictly between 0 and 1. Each chain is irreducible and aperiodic: any finite bit string can be reached by updates with positive probability, and a coordinate can remain unchanged with positive probability. Updating one coordinate from its exact conditional leaves its displayed Gibbs law invariant, as follows by summing over that coordinate with all others fixed. Thus the unique stationary laws are mu_(d,g) and mu_(gamma,g), and the finite-state chains converge to them. Taking the limit of the maintained order proves

\[
\mu_{d,g}(u_0=1)\le\mu_{\gamma,g}(u_0=1)=:q_m(\beta,\gamma,g).
\]

This proof uses no spatial FKG assumption, quantum correlation inequality, operator monotonicity of the exponential, or imported theorem about a frustrated quantum model. The update chains are only an elementary way to prove an ordering of two finite probability laws; they are not a proposed physical protocol. Taking m>=3 avoids the irrelevant one- and two-site ring conventions in the conditional formula.

## 6. Return to an arbitrary full computational record

Let A_y be the event x_j^0=y_j for every j!=i. It depends only on the other paths. Conditional expectation, first over a complete other-path realization and then over its distribution given A_y, gives

\[
p_m(x_i^0=1\mid A_y)
=\mathbb E_m[\mu_{d,g_i}(u_0=1)\mid A_y]
\le q_m(\beta,\gamma,g_i).
\]

The distribution inside this expectation can have arbitrary spatial correlations and can heavily favor unusual other paths. The inner bound is uniform over every allowed path, so neither rarity nor signed interactions change it.

For the constant-field ring, q_m is precisely the endpoint occupation for the one-qubit product

\[
(e^{-(\beta/m)\gamma n}e^{(\beta/m)g_iX})^m.
\]

It converges to the corresponding diagonal probability for exp[-beta(gamma n-g_iX)]. The g_i=0 expression already has that exact value for every m. The full finite-graph endpoint probabilities converge as in Section 3. Because exp(-beta G) is positive definite for finite beta and finite coefficients, every computational diagonal is strictly positive. Thus every fixed A_y has strictly positive probability, and taking the ratio limit is legitimate:

\[
p(x_i=1\mid x_{-i}=y)\le q_*(\beta,\gamma,g_i).
\]

There is no denominator estimate proportional to Pr(A_y) in the bound. We take the exact limit for each finite graph and each record, then obtain a graph-independent right side. We do not exchange an infinite-graph limit with conditioning, nor claim a graph-independent finite-m approximation error. This distinction retains arbitrarily rare full records within the finite theorem.

## 7. Explicit one-qubit evaluation and uniform parameters

Let r=sqrt((gamma/2)^2+g^2) and s=2r=sqrt(gamma^2+4g^2). Since

the exact exponential is

\left[\cosh(\beta r)I+

Indeed f(u)=tanh u-u sech^2u has f(0)=0 and f'(u)=2u sech^2u tanh u>0. Therefore beta>=beta_min and g_i<=g_max give q_*<=q_*(beta_min,g_max)=qbar. The subtracted term is strictly positive, so qbar<1/2. A usable positive margin is explicitly

## 8. Sharpness and parameter limits

One qubit with h=gamma, a=g_max, b=0, k_0=0 and no edges has positive Hamiltonian gamma n+g_max I-g_max X. At beta=beta_min the displayed qbar is attained. Thus a uniformly smaller bound cannot follow just from the stated three numerical certificates. This is an exact analytic example, not a numerical fixture.

Several limits test rather than extend the claim:

* As beta_min tends to zero at fixed gamma and g_max, qbar tends to 1/2. A positive temperature-independent bias margin is not supplied without beta_min>0.
* As g_max tends to infinity at fixed gamma and beta_min, qbar tends to 1/2. A finite upper transverse-field certificate matters.
* As gamma tends to zero, the same single-qubit example approaches 1/2. A uniform strict field margin matters.
* For fixed gamma>0 and g>0, the low-temperature one-qubit limit is (1-gamma/sqrt(gamma^2+4g^2))/2>0. Cooling alone does not force this source's computational population to zero, though its strict bias remains. For g=0 that limit is zero.

These are parameter-family limits within exact formulas, not a broad impossibility statement about other preparation methods. The previously studied two-qubit source with an X_1X_2 off-diagonal term is outside this onsite-field class.
