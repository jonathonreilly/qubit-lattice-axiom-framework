# A weak periodic-drive energy limit in the supplied free CAR model

For a nonempty explicit class of compact smooth quadratic controls, a drive
of size lambda acting for tau/lambda^2 has a finite, generally nonzero
deposited free energy. The full free-energy law converges to a Poisson law
with jumps of the drive frequency. The sufficient extra condition used in
the proof is that every component of both real smearings has zero integral.
The result for arbitrary compact smooth smearings is left open here; the
failure of the present uniform estimate in that larger class is not an
impossibility theorem.

## 1. Supplied model, conventions and exact statement

Fix m,v>0. Let K=L2(R;C^2), with inner product conjugate-linear in the
first slot, unitary Fourier transform with phase exp(-ipx), and real
conjugation Gamma hhat(p)=overline(hhat(-p)). The supplied signed generator,
positive energy and polarization are

    D(p)=-vp sigma_x-m sigma_y,
    E(p)=sqrt(m^2+v^2 p^2),
    P=(1+D/E)/2, P_-=1-P, Gamma P Gamma=P_-.

The positive one-particle space is h=P K, with Hamiltonian h0=E|h. On its
fermionic Fock space use H0=dGamma(h0), free vacuum Omega, and real fields

    gamma(f)=a*(sqrt(2)P f)+a(sqrt(2)P f),
    {gamma(f),gamma(g)}=2<f,g>I,
    omega(gamma(f)gamma(g))=2<f,Pg>.

Here a is conjugate-linear, a* is linear, and real smearings have
||gamma(f)||=||f||. The convention gives
exp(itH0)gamma(f)exp(-itH0)=gamma(exp(itD)f).

Let f,g be fixed real C_c^infinity(R;R^2), and put

    Q=(i/2)[gamma(f),gamma(g)],
    V=Q-<Omega,Q Omega>I,
    H_lambda(t)=H0+lambda cos(omega t)V.                    (1)

The word 'even' refers to fermion parity; no spatial reflection symmetry
of f or g is required. The perturbation is bounded and selfadjoint. Its
scalar centering affects a phase only. Write W_lambda(t) for the full Fock
propagator of H_lambda(t), and define

    Y_lambda(t)=U_lambda(t)=exp(itH0)W_lambda(t).

Thus U_lambda is the unitary interaction-picture Dyson solution. Below,
Y denotes this same interaction-picture propagator. Every finite time is
legitimate; the issue addressed below is uniformity as time grows.

Let mu_lambda,T be the spectral probability law of H0 in
U_lambda(T)Omega and let

    E_lambda(T)=||H0^(1/2)U_lambda(T)Omega||^2.

This is deposited free energy, equivalently energy measured after the
drive is switched off. Units set hbar=1. No value of m, v, omega or the
control normalization is selected by this construction.

Choose the scalar positive-band frame

    theta(p)=arctan(vp/m),
    e_+(p)=(exp(i theta(p)/2),-i exp(-i theta(p)/2))/sqrt(2),
    u(p)=sqrt(2)e_+(p)^* fhat(p),
    w(p)=sqrt(2)e_+(p)^* ghat(p),
    Delta(p,q)=u(p)w(q)-w(p)u(q).                         (2)

The vacuum pair spectral density, whose normalization is proved below, is

    r(s)=1/2 integral_R2 |Delta(p,q)|^2
                          delta(s-E(p)-E(q)) dp dq.       (3)

Equation (3) denotes the ordinary pushforward density, not an assumed
distributional rule. Section 3 gives a nonsingular formula. Extend r by
zero below 2m. It is continuous, nonnegative, and rapidly decaying at
infinity. Put kappa(omega)=(pi/2)r(omega) for omega>0.

**Theorem.** Suppose in addition that

    integral f_j(x)dx=integral g_j(x)dx=0, j=1,2.          (4)

For each fixed omega>0 and tau>=0, as real lambda tends to zero through
nonzero values and T=tau/lambda^2,

    E_lambda(T) -> tau omega kappa(omega),                 (5)

    mu_lambda,T -> Law(omega N_tau),
    N_tau ~ Poisson(tau kappa(omega)).                    (6)

The convergence in (6) is weak and also in Wasserstein distance of order
one. The mean error in (5) is at most C tau |lambda|+C_omega lambda^2
for sufficiently small |lambda|, with fixed input-dependent constants.
In particular the mean convergence is uniform on bounded tau intervals.
Characteristic functions have the same uniform-on-bounded-tau conclusion
for each fixed spectral test parameter. For negative omega replace it
by |omega|. For omega=0 the limiting law and mean are zero.

The rate is zero for 0<omega<=2m. For every fixed omega>2m there are
real compact smooth f,g satisfying (4) for which r(omega)>0. Degenerate
choices such as g proportional to f correctly have r identically zero.
No nonzero response is asserted for every nonzero quadratic control.

The conclusion is a global energy-law limit. It does not assert convergence
of driven vectors in Fock norm or realization of the control in nature.
All hypotheses, including the chosen free vacuum and the drive, are
supplied model data. The unrestricted compact-smooth class is separated
from (4) in Section 9.

## 2. Pair normalization and exact one-particle dynamics

Write u=sqrt(2)Pf and w=sqrt(2)Pg before choosing the frame (2). Direct
expansion of the two fields shows

    V Omega=i a*(u)a*(w)Omega=i(u wedge w),
    ||V Omega||^2=||u||^2||w||^2-|<u,w>|^2.                (7)

The exterior vector u wedge w has wavefunction
(u(p)w(q)-w(p)u(q))/sqrt(2), explaining the factor 1/2 in (3).
Its free energy is E(p)+E(q). In particular its weighted spectral
density is s r(s), with no spin degeneracy multiplier to add afterward.

The CAR commutator, before any perturbative expansion, gives

    i[Q,gamma(h)]
        =2[<f,h>gamma(g)-<g,h>gamma(f)].                  (8)

Define F:C^2->K by F(z1,z2)=z1 f+z2 g and

    k=2i [[0,1],[-1,0]],
    K_V=F k F*=2i(|f><g|-|g><f|).                        (9)

K_V is selfadjoint and Gamma K_V Gamma=-K_V. The right side of (8)
is gamma(i K_V h); thus neither the factor 2 nor the sign is arbitrary.
Let S_lambda solve the one-particle Schrödinger equation

    i S_lambda'(t)=[D+lambda cos(omega t)K_V]S_lambda(t),
    S_lambda(0)=I,
    Z_lambda(t)=exp(itD)S_lambda(t).                     (10)

These are unitaries preserving real conjugation. Differentiating the
bounded field equations, or their interaction-picture integral equations,
proves

    W_lambda(t)^* gamma(h) W_lambda(t)
                           =gamma(S_lambda(t)^*h),
    Y_lambda(t)^* gamma(h) Y_lambda(t)
                           =gamma(Z_lambda(t)^*h),
    i Z_lambda'=lambda cos(omega t)F_t k F_t* Z_lambda,
    F_t=exp(itD)F.                                      (11)

The coefficient equation for the full Heisenberg field acts on the right:
R'=R i[D+lambda cos(omega t)K_V], so R=S_lambda^*. In the interaction
picture the coefficient is Z_lambda^*=S_lambda^*exp(-itD). These are
distinct identities. The scalar centering in (1) disappears from both.

The interaction-picture covariance is Z_lambda P Z_lambda*. On h its
particle occupation operator therefore is

    N_T=P Z_lambda(T)P_- Z_lambda(T)^*P.

The full-picture covariance and occupation replace Z_lambda by S_lambda.
The two states have the same H0 spectral law and energy expectation,
because Y_lambda=exp(itH0)W_lambda.

Indeed for a positive mode xi, a*(xi)=[gamma(f_xi)-i gamma(g_xi)]/2,
where f_xi=(xi+Gamma xi)/sqrt(2) and
g_xi=i(xi-Gamma xi)/sqrt(2). Substitution into the covariance gives
<a*(xi)a(xi)>=<xi,(1-Z P Z*)xi>. Positivity and completeness of a
one-particle basis give the exact energy identity

    E_lambda(T)=||E^(1/2)P Z_lambda(T)P_-||_HS^2.         (12)

It initially holds with bounded one-particle energy cutoffs and finite
rank sums. Monotone convergence on Fock sectors and in the positive trace
then proves (12) as an extended nonnegative identity. The estimates below
make the right side finite, so no unbounded trace cycling is assumed.

The first Born block and the corresponding energy are

    B1(T)=-i lambda integral_0^T cos(omega t)P F_t k F_t*P_- dt,
    E_Born(T)=||E^(1/2)B1(T)||_HS^2
       =lambda^2 integral_(2m)^infinity s r(s)|I_T(s)|^2 ds,
    I_T(s)=integral_0^T cos(omega t)exp(ist)dt.            (13)

For the second equality, P K_V P_-=i(|u><Gamma w|-|w><Gamma u|).
The Hilbert--Schmidt kernel has squared modulus |Delta(p,q)|^2.
Symmetry under p<->q replaces its output energy E(p) by
[E(p)+E(q)]/2, reproducing exactly the many-body normalization (7).

## 3. Density, thresholds and a nonzero compact witness

The smooth change of variable

    y=sign(p)sqrt(E(p)-m),
    p(y)=y sqrt(2m+y^2)/v,
    p'(y)=2(m+y^2)/(v sqrt(2m+y^2))>0                  (14)

turns the pair energy into 2m+y^2+z^2. For x>=0, ordinary polar
coordinates therefore give

    r(2m+x)=1/4 integral_0^(2pi)
       |Delta(p(sqrt(x)cos(phi)),p(sqrt(x)sin(phi)))|^2
       p'(sqrt(x)cos(phi)) p'(sqrt(x)sin(phi)) dphi.       (15)

This proves existence and the coefficient in (3) by an ordinary
change of measure. The factor 1/4 is the exterior factor 1/2 times
the radial identity rho d rho=d(rho^2)/2.

A separate normalization check changes each momentum to its energy.
Writing k(epsilon)=sqrt(epsilon^2-m^2)/v and
j(epsilon)=epsilon/[v sqrt(epsilon^2-m^2)], one obtains, for s>2m,

    r(s)=1/2 sum_(sigma,tau in {+1,-1}) integral_m^(s-m)
         j(epsilon)j(s-epsilon)
         |Delta(sigma k(epsilon),tau k(s-epsilon))|^2 d epsilon.

The endpoint square-root singularities are integrable. Substitution
epsilon=m+(s-2m)sin(phi)^2 removes them for direct quadrature; it
gives the distinct spectral check used in Section 11.

For arbitrary compact smooth inputs the scalar functions u,w are
Schwartz. Antisymmetry gives Delta(0,0)=0 and hence r(2m+x)=O(x).
Under (4), u(p)=p u1+O(p^2), w(p)=p w1+O(p^2). The degree-two
product cancels, so Delta(p,q)=O(|p q|(|p|+|q|)) near zero and

    r(2m+x)=O(x^3) under (4).                            (16)

The remainders are uniform on the small circles in (15). Equation (15)
is smooth for x>0 and continuous at zero. At large x at least one
of its two momenta grows polynomially in sqrt(x); Schwartz decay of
both u and w, with the polynomial Jacobians in (14), proves decay
faster than any chosen inverse power. These facts justify all spectral
integrals below. The estimates (16) are upper orders; special controls
can have additional zeros.

For an explicit nonempty class take a real even nonnegative nonzero bump
b in C_c^infinity((-L,L)), and set

    f=(b',0), g=(0,b').                                  (17)

Every component integral is zero. With B(p)=bhat(p), which is real even,

    u(p)=i p B(p)exp(-i theta(p)/2),
    w(p)=-p B(p)exp(i theta(p)/2),
    Delta(p,q)=2 p q B(p)B(q)
                         sin([theta(q)-theta(p)]/2).     (18)

For omega>2m put k0=sqrt((omega/2)^2-m^2)/v. At p=k0,q=-k0,

    Delta(k0,-k0)=2v k0^3 B(k0)^2/E(k0).

Choose L so that k0 L<pi/2. Then B(k0)>0 by the positive cosine
integral. The last display is nonzero. The corresponding point of
the energy circle has a neighborhood of positive angular measure
on which the integrand in (15) is positive. Thus r(omega)>0, with
no fitted normalization or numerical lower-bound assumption.

## 4. The dispersive estimates supplied by zero integrals

This section is the extra ingredient beyond fixed-time perturbation theory.
Define the channel correlation matrix and its time-integral resource by

    G(t)=F* exp(-itD)F,
    C1=||k|| integral_0^infinity ||G(t)|| dt.              (19)

Under (4), C1 is finite. To prove this, split exp(-itD) into the
positive and negative projectors. Every matrix element becomes an
integral of exp(+/- itE(p)) times a Schwartz amplitude vanishing to
second order at p=0. Use (14): the amplitude including dp/dy is
y^2 b(y), with b Schwartz. Integration by parts gives

    integral exp(+/- it y^2)y^2 b(y)dy
       =-(1/(+/- 2it)) integral exp(+/- it y^2)
                                              [b(y)+y b'(y)]dy.

For a smooth integrable amplitude with integrable derivative the last
oscillatory integral is O(|t|^-1/2). One direct proof splits at
|y|=|t|^-1/2; the inner interval costs its length times the supremum,
and integration by parts with (2ity)^-1 d/dy on the two outer rays
costs at most C|t|^-1/2(||b||_infinity+||b'||_1).
The displayed amplitudes and their derivatives satisfy these bounds.
Consequently

    ||G(t)||<=C(1+|t|)^(-3/2).                           (20)

The same argument works with any fixed power of E inserted and with
P or P_- retained. There is no massless or uniform-in-m assertion.

We also need a time-synthesis bound, separately from (20). For q>=0,
define S_q,+ z=integral_R E^(q/2)P F_t z(t)dt. If
a(p)=(e_+(p)^*fhat(p),e_+(p)^*ghat(p)) and
k(epsilon)=sqrt(epsilon^2-m^2)/v, its spectral density matrix is

    M_q,+(epsilon)=epsilon^q/|E'(k(epsilon))|
                [a(k(epsilon))^*a(k(epsilon))
                         +a(-k(epsilon))^*a(-k(epsilon))].

It is bounded: a(p)=O(p) at zero, while |E'(p)| is of order |p|
there, and at infinity all needed weighted amplitudes decay. Fourier
Plancherel in time, with the nonunitary integral used above, proves

    ||S_q,+|| <= [2pi sup_(epsilon>m)||M_q,+(epsilon)||]^(1/2)
                  =:s_q,+ < infinity.                   (21)

The analogous negative-band synthesis map has a finite constant s_q,-.
Taking the orthogonal sum yields a finite unweighted full-space constant
s0 for S0 z=integral F_t z(t)dt. Their adjoints are the corresponding
time-analysis maps. Restriction to any [0,T], by zero extension, keeps
all these constants independent of T. For operator-valued z the same
bound holds in Hilbert--Schmidt norm, by applying the scalar estimate to
each basis column and summing. This is a proved spectral bound, not a
postulated smoothing theorem.

Finally the pair correlations

    C_j(t)=<u wedge w,H2^j exp(itH2)(u wedge w)>, j=0,1,
    H2=h0 tensor I+I tensor h0 on wedge^2 h,

satisfy |C_j(t)|<=C(1+|t|)^-3. For j=0 this follows by expanding
the exterior inner product into the difference of two products of
one-particle correlations. For j=1 apply h0 to either factor; all
four products still have two factors bounded by (20). Hence

    integral (1+|t|)|C_j(t)|dt < infinity.                (22)

This last estimate will control the secular coefficient, while (19)--(21)
control the actual evolution. They play different roles.

## 5. Uniform exact-to-Born comparison

Suppress lambda on Z and write c(t)=cos(omega t). For t in [0,T] put

    X(t)=F_t* Z(t)P_-, X0(t)=F_t*P_-.

The exact bounded integral equation in (11) gives

    X(t)=X0(t)-i lambda integral_0^t
                           c(s)G(t-s)k X(s)ds.          (23)

Young's inequality for this causal convolution, applied after zero
extension outside [0,T], bounds its L2 operator norm by C1. This
works on ordinary Hilbert-space columns and on Hilbert--Schmidt columns.
Let

    M0=||F*P_-||_HS,
    z=|lambda|C1<1.

Since ||X0||_(L2 HS)=M0 sqrt(T), the exact solution of (23) satisfies

    ||X-X0||_(L2 HS)<=z/(1-z) M0 sqrt(T).                 (24)

There is no assumption that the full Dyson series is small in
|lambda|T. Its causal equation has instead been inverted in a norm
whose small parameter is |lambda|C1, independent of T.

By (11), (21) and (24), for B(T)=P Z(T)P_- and every fixed q>=0,

    ||E^(q/2)B1(T)||_HS <= |lambda|A_q sqrt(T),
    ||E^(q/2)[B(T)-B1(T)]||_HS
              <=lambda^2 A_q C1 sqrt(T)/(1-z),
    A_q=s_q,+ ||k|| M0.                                 (25)

The second bound uses the synthesis map on c(t)k[X-X0](t). On
each finite time interval its energy-weighted Bochner integral is defined
because the two channel vectors belong to every fixed E graph domain;
the estimate and closedness identify it with the weighted block in (25).
In particular, (12) and ||x+y||^2-||x||^2 bounded by 2||x||||y||+||y||^2
give

    |E_lambda(T)-E_Born(T)|
       <= A_1^2 lambda^2 T[(1-z)^(-2)-1]
       <= 8 A_1^2 C1 |lambda|^3 T, z<=1/2.               (26)

Also E_lambda(T)<=A_1^2 lambda^2 T/(1-z)^2. These statements hold
for every T>=0 under the same smallness condition. The zero-vector or
zero-channel cases cause no division problem; if C1=0 the corresponding
remainder is zero. More generally (25) bounds Tr(h0^q N_T), not by
itself the q-th many-body energy moment.

A second consequence, needed for the full energy law, is

    ||Z(T)-I|| <= |lambda| s0^2 ||k||/(1-z), all T.       (27)

For this use X(t)=F_t* Z(t) on all of K. Its inhomogeneous term
F_t* is the bounded analysis map K->L2(R;C^2), of norm at most s0.
Invert (23) on L2 columns, then synthesize with S0 in (11). This
proves (27) without replacing a Hilbert--Schmidt norm by a dimension
dependent operator bound. In particular the diagonal block PZP is
invertible on h for sufficiently small |lambda|, uniformly in T.

## 6. The secular coefficient and the energy mean

For omega>0 and a spectral density b whose inverse Fourier integral
C_b(t)=integral b(s)exp(ist)ds has integrable (1+|t|)|C_b(t)|, the
identity cos(omega t)cos(omega s)=[cos(omega(t-s))+cos(omega(t+s))]/2
gives

    integral b(s)|I_T(s)|^2 ds
          =(pi/2)T[b(omega)+b(-omega)]+O_b,omega(1).     (28)

Here is an explicit justification of the uniform remainder. The first
cosine term equals
one half of integral_(-T)^T (T-|u|)cos(omega u)C_b(u)du.
Replacing T-|u| by T and extending to the real line costs at most
(1/2)integral |u||C_b(u)|du. Fourier inversion gives the linear
term displayed in (28). For the second cosine term, at each fixed
u=t-s, integration of cos(omega(2s+u)) over the allowed s interval
has absolute value at most 1/omega. Its total contribution is at most
||C_b||_1/(2omega). The same argument works for complex b.

Use b(s)=s r(s), supported in [2m,infinity). Its C_b is C_1 in (22),
so (13) and (28) imply

    E_Born(T)=lambda^2 T(pi/2)omega r(omega)+O(lambda^2).

Together with (26) this proves (5), including its stated error. The
calculation derives the pi/2 from the positive-frequency half of a
cosine and temporal Fourier normalization; it is not a coefficient
inserted to agree with a rate formula.

For omega=0, |I_T(s)|<=2/s on the positive pair spectrum, and hence
E_Born(T)<=4lambda^2 integral r(s)/s ds. Equation (25) at
T=tau/lambda^2 gives an energy-weighted remainder O(|lambda|), so
the exact energy is O(lambda^2) on bounded tau intervals. The same
O(lambda^2) conclusion holds for a fixed 0<omega<2m. It also holds
at omega=2m under (4), because (16) makes
integral s r(s)/(s-2m)^2 ds finite. None of these zero-rate cases is
a no-go for multiphoton absorption on a different time scale.

## 7. Full energy law: the small-pair limit

This section distinguishes a genuine finite-energy probability law from
merely a nonzero mean carried by an uncontrolled rare high-energy tail.
It supplies the operator facts used, rather than assuming a kinetic
or Markov approximation.

Identify h with scalar L2(R,dp) using (2), and let C be scalar complex
conjugation there. Then h0 is real in this representation. The map
J=Gamma C:h->P_-K is linear, unitary and energy intertwining.
Put A=PZP, B=PZP_-. By (25)--(27),

    ||B||_HS<=C |lambda|sqrt(T),
    ||A-I||+||B||<=C|lambda|.

The transformed annihilator of the interaction-picture state Y(T)Omega
is a(A xi)+a*(B Gamma xi). Invert A and define the linear pair operator

    L=B Gamma A^-1 C on h.                              (29)

Its matrix is antisymmetric, L^T=-L: this follows by imposing the
vanishing anticommutator of two transformed annihilators and changing
variables from xi to A xi. It is Hilbert--Schmidt, and

    ||L||<=C|lambda|,
