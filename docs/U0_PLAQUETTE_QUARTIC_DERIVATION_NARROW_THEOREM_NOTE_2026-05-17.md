## Statement

Let `G` be any finite-dimensional matrix Lie group with faithful
fundamental representation of complex dimension `N ∈ Z_{≥1}`, let
`U_p ∈ G` denote the ordered product of the four gauge-link variables
`U_p := U_1 U_2 U_3 U_4` around an elementary plaquette on a cubic
lattice, let `<P> := ⟨(1/N) Re Tr U_p⟩` denote its mean-field
expectation in the link variables (an abstract positive real), and let
`u_0 ∈ R^+` denote a tadpole-improvement constant. Assume the two named
inputs:

**(P1) Four-link plaquette structure.** The elementary plaquette is the
ordered product of exactly four link variables:

```text
U_p  =  U_1 U_2 U_3 U_4,                                                    (P1)
```

i.e. the elementary closed loop on a square face of a cubic lattice has
exactly four edges. This is a geometric statement on cubic-lattice
incidence (each elementary square face is bordered by four edges) and
holds in any lattice gauge theory whose elementary loop is a unit
square.

**(P2) Tree-level mean-field unit-normalization principle** (named
external admission, Lepage-Mackenzie 1993 / standard mean-field
improvement): the tadpole-improvement constant `u_0 ∈ R^+` is defined
to be the unique positive real such that, under the link rescaling

```text
U_μ  ↦  U_μ / u_0     for all gauge links μ,                                (P2a)
```

the **dressed** plaquette expectation equals unity:

```text
⟨(1/N) Re Tr ( (U_p / u_0^L) ) ⟩  =  1,                                     (P2b)
```

where `L` is the loop length (number of link factors in the loop). This
is the defining principle of mean-field tadpole improvement: each link
is dressed by a factor of `1/u_0` so that the resulting smeared mean
operator has unit expectation at tree-level. Treated here as a named
external-mathematics import, equivalent to the standard tadpole
construction in any introductory lattice-QCD text. Not a framework
derivation.

Then the tadpole-improvement constant takes the closed-form expression

```text
u_0  =  <P>^{1/4},                                                          (Q1)
```

with `<P> := ⟨(1/N) Re Tr U_p⟩`, and the **exponent 1/4** is the unique
exponent forced by combining the loop length `L = 4` of `(P1)` with the
unit-mean principle `(P2)`.

## Derivable corollaries

```text
(Q-C1) Loop-length scaling. For any closed loop W of length L (number
       of links), the analogous tadpole constant from the same principle
       is u_0^(W) = ⟨(1/N) Re Tr W⟩^{1/L}. The 1/4 exponent for u_0 is
       the L = 4 instance.

(Q-C2) Counterfactual exponent (triangle plaquette). On a triangular
       lattice (L = 3), the same principle gives u_0 = <P_△>^{1/3}.
       The 1/4 is specific to cubic (square-face) lattices.

(Q-C3) Hexagonal counterfactual (L = 6). On a honeycomb lattice the
       same principle gives u_0 = <P_⬡>^{1/6}.

(Q-C4) Boundary at free action. When U_p ≡ I (trivial gauge field),
       Re Tr U_p = N, so <P> = 1 and (Q1) gives u_0 = 1, matching the
       u_0 = 1 boundary of (T4) in the vertex-power narrow theorem.

(Q-C5) Composition into vertex-power identity. Substituting (Q1) into
       (T1) of ALPHA_S_TADPOLE_IMPROVEMENT_VERTEX_POWER_NARROW_THEOREM_NOTE_2026-05-10
       gives alpha_s(v) = alpha_bare / u_0^2 = alpha_bare / <P>^{1/2},
       reproducing (T6) of that companion note as a forward chain.

(Q-C6) Inverse algebra. Conversely, given (Q1), the dressed plaquette
       expectation is identically 1: <P_dressed> = <P>/u_0^4 = 1.
       (Q1) is therefore exactly the unit-mean fixed point of the link
       rescaling map.

(Q-C7) Monotone calibration. The map P ↦ P^{1/4} is strictly increasing
       on R^+, so larger mean plaquettes give larger u_0. In particular,
       0 < <P> < 1 gives 0 < u_0 < 1, consistent with the standard
       lattice-QCD regime (`u_0 ≈ 0.88` at `beta = 6` is the canonical
       numerical instance — not consumed here).
```

All seven corollaries follow algebraically from `(Q1)` plus elementary
properties of the fourth-root function.

## Proof

Pure algebra in two steps.

**Step 1 (loop length L = 4 from (P1)).** From `(P1)`, the elementary
plaquette is `U_p = U_1 U_2 U_3 U_4`, the ordered product of four link
variables. Hence the loop length (number of link factors in the
plaquette word) is

```text
L  =  4.                                                                    (S1)
```

This is forced by the cubic-lattice geometric incidence: an elementary
square face of a cubic lattice is bordered by exactly four edges. (On a
triangular lattice the elementary loop would have `L = 3`, on a
honeycomb lattice `L = 6`, etc.)

**Step 2 (substitute L = 4 into (P2b) and solve).** Apply the unit-mean
principle `(P2b)` at `L = 4`. The dressed plaquette is

```text
U_p_dressed  =  U_1/u_0 · U_2/u_0 · U_3/u_0 · U_4/u_0
              =  U_p / u_0^4,                                                (S2)
```

since each of the four link factors picks up one `1/u_0` from the
rescaling `(P2a)` and the scalars commute with the matrix product.
Taking the trace and the mean:

```text
⟨(1/N) Re Tr ( U_p / u_0^4 ) ⟩  =  ⟨(1/N) Re Tr U_p ⟩ / u_0^4
                                 =  <P> / u_0^4.                              (S3)
```

The principle `(P2b)` demands this equals 1:

```text
<P> / u_0^4  =  1,                                                            (S4)
```

equivalently

```text
u_0^4  =  <P>.                                                                (S5)
```

Taking the unique positive fourth root (`u_0 > 0` by definition):

```text
u_0  =  <P>^{1/4}.                                                            (Q1)
```

This is `(Q1)`. The exponent `1/4` is forced by the loop length `L = 4`
of `(S1)` combined with the unit-mean principle `(P2b)`. ∎

## Pinning the exponent: why 1/4, not 1/L for any other L

The exponent `1/4` is **not** chosen by convention; it is uniquely
determined by:

1. Cubic-lattice geometry (`(P1)`): elementary loops are square faces
   bordered by exactly four edges. (On other lattices the loop length
   would differ and the exponent would differ accordingly — see
   `(Q-C2)`, `(Q-C3)`.)
2. The unit-mean principle `(P2b)`: the mean-field dressing must make
   the smeared plaquette expectation equal to one. (Any other target
   value would give a different exponent — but the unit-mean target is
   the unique fixed point that makes `u_0 = 1` the no-improvement
   boundary, matching `(T4)` of the vertex-power note and the free-action
   limit `(Q-C4)`.)

Within the named external admission `(P2)` and the framework's cubic
lattice `(P1)`, the exponent `1/4` is the **only** positive real for
which `(S4)` holds identically. There is no slack in the algebra.
