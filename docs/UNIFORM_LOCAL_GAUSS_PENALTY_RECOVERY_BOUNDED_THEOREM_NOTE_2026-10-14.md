## 1. Abstract finite time-dependent interaction estimate

Let the physical carrier be a finite tensor product, with a passive reference of arbitrary dimension. An interaction H(s)=sum_X h_X(s) has a termwise Hermitian, piecewise continuously differentiable decomposition, with every h_X periodic of period p>0; each fixed support X is nonempty, has at most k>=1 sites, and

    g = sup_i sum_(X contains i) sup_s ||h_X(s)|| < infinity.

Multiple labelled interactions on the same X are allowed; sums below count their labels. Suppose g>0. Define a_X=p^{-1} integral_0^p h_X(s) ds, A=sum_X a_X, r_X=h_X−a_X and F_X(t)=integral_0^t r_X(s) ds. The integral has period p and F_X(0)=0. Its norm is at most2p sup_s||h_X(s)||. Consequently F=sum_X F_X has local interaction norm at most f=2pg and support size at most k. Its derivative is H−A. This is a local norm; ||F|| may grow with carrier size.

For interactions Phi,Psi with support sizes at most k_Phi,k_Psi and local norms g_Phi,g_Psi, assign the commutator of each overlapping pair to its union. Disjoint pairs commute. Counting a site i through either member of the pair gives

    g_[Phi,Psi] <= 2(k_Phi+k_Psi) g_Phi g_Psi.

Indeed pairs with i in X cost at most2 sum_(X contains i)||Phi_X|| sum_(Y intersects X)||Psi_Y|| <=2 k_Phi g_Phi g_Psi; pairs with i in Y have the analogous bound2 k_Psi g_Phi g_Psi. Double counting an intersection only weakens the bound. By induction, ad_F^n(H), and identically ad_F^n(A), have support size at most(n+1)k and local norm at most

    (2kf)^n (n+1)! g.                                             (1)

Let U solve i U'=H(t)U with U(0)=I, and set W(t)=exp(iF(t))U(t). Finite-dimensional differentiation of the exponential gives W(0)=I and generator

    H_W = exp(i ad_F) H − integral_0^1 exp(iu ad_F)(H−A) du
        = A + K,
    K = sum_(n>=1) i^n/n! [n/(n+1) ad_F^n(H)
                           +1/(n+1) ad_F^n(A)].                  (2)

The sign follows directly from i(d exp(iF)/dt)exp(−iF)=−integral_0^1 exp(iuF)F'exp(−iuF)du. Set q=2kf<1. Equation(1) makes the series(2) absolutely convergent in local interaction norm, and

    g_K <= g sum_(n>=1)(n+1)q^n
         = g[(1−q)^−2−1].                                       (3)

For each finite carrier this also gives ordinary operator convergence since total interaction norm is at most(number of sites) times local norm. The effective K terms can have growing support; only their uniformly summable per-site norm is used next. No fixed arity for K is claimed.

## 2. Local stability bound with passive references

For the static A with support size at most k and local norm at most g, put

    M_ij = sum_(X contains i,j) ||a_X||.

Its nonnegative row sums are at most kg. For an observable O supported on S and the passive reference, and B_X supported on physical X, the finite interaction expansion yields

    ||[B_X, exp(iuA) O exp(−iuA)]||
      <= 2||B_X|| ||O|| sum_(i in S,j in X) (exp(2uM))_ij, u>=0. (4)

Here is the required expansion argument rather than an appeal to an unnamed theorem. Define C_B(Y,u) as the supremum of the commutator norm over unit-norm observables on Y and the passive reference. Commutator differentiation and removal of the homogeneous commutator term by a unitary conjugation give

    C_B(Y,u) <= C_B(Y,0)
              +2 sum_(Z intersects Y)||a_Z|| integral_0^u C_B(Z,v) dv.

The initial bound is2||B_X|| times the indicator that Y intersects X, hence at most2||B_X|| |Y intersect X|. Iterating gives chains of overlapping interaction sets. For each overlap select a site in the intersection; summing all such selections overcounts each valid chain and gives powers of M. The zeroth term is the diagonal identity contribution. The exponential series converges for each finite carrier. Passive-reference factors commute with every a_Z and B_X, so every step uses physical support only and the same operator norm; no reference-dimension factor enters. This proves(4). Equivalently one may reverse the time-evolution commutator using unitary invariance, so the bound covers the displayed ordering.

For any Hermitian time-dependent perturbation K with a convergent local interaction decomposition of norm at most g_K, sum(4) over its terms and use sum_(X contains j)||K_X||<=g_K. This gives

    sum_X ||[K_X(s), exp(iuA)O exp(−iuA)]||
       <= 2|S| ||O|| g_K exp(2kgu).                              (5)

Duhamel interpolation between the dynamics of A+K and A, with the outer propagator unitary, therefore bounds the local Heisenberg difference by

    2|S| ||O|| g_K integral_0^t exp(2kgu) du.

The final frame transformation in U=exp(−iF)W costs at most||[F,O]||<=2|S| f ||O||. There is no initial transformation because F(0)=0. Combining this with(3), for ||O||<=1,

    ||U(t)^* O U(t)−exp(itA)O exp(−itA)||
       <= 2|S| f + (|S|/k)[(1−q)^−2−1](exp(2kgt)−1).            (6)

The original derivation incorrectly inferred(7) by substituting the bound6q into(6): that substitution gives2|S|f[6 exp(2kgt)−5]. To recover the stated smaller constant, use the following additional periodic-primitive argument. For r=t modulo p in[0,p], zero mean gives F_X(r)=integral_0^r(h_X−a_X)ds=−integral_r^p(h_X−a_X)ds. Thus ||F_X(r)||<=2 min(r,p−r) sup_s||h_X(s)||<=p sup_s||h_X(s)||. The actual local norm of F is therefore at most f0=pg=f/2. Adding these two charges gives

    2|S| f [3 exp(2kgt)−2].                                    (7)

At t=0 the exact difference is zero; the positive displayed bound merely pays a uniform final-frame estimate. Trace duality makes the same bound, capped at2, valid for the reduced S-plus-reference output difference for every initial joint state. It is not the trace distance of the whole physical carrier unless S is the whole carrier, in which case the explicit |S| is extensive. The g=0 case is stationary and requires no displayed division.

## 3. Commuting integer penalty application

## 1. A finite graph lemma with signed cycles

Let G=(V,E) be a finite connected simple graph, with |V|>=2. Fix total orders of vertices and edges and put one physical qubit on each edge. For an oriented edge u-v with underlying edge e, define exactly

    B_v = product_(f incident v) Z_f,
    A_uv = epsilon_uv X_e
           product_(f<e, f incident u) Z_f
           product_(f<e, f incident v) Z_f,
    epsilon_uv=+1 for u<v, and -1 for u>v.

The prefix contains no e. Since the graph is simple, different endpoint prefixes share no other edge. Thus each A is a Hermitian involution, A_vu=-A_uv, and it anticommutes with exactly B_u and B_v. Distinct A operators anticommute if their edges share a vertex and commute otherwise: at a shared vertex exactly the smaller edge's X meets a Z in the larger edge's prefix. These conclusions follow from one-qubit XZ=-ZX, without a representation theorem.

For any oriented simple cycle c=(v_0,...,v_(r-1),v_0), let

    S(c)=i^r A_(v_0 v_1)...A_(v_(r-1) v_0).

An edge outside the cycle shares zero, one or two of its endpoints with cycle vertices; it therefore anticommutes with zero, two or four cycle A factors respectively. A cycle edge meets exactly its two cycle neighbors. Hence S(c) commutes with every A_e; the same even incidence shows commutation with all B_v. This includes an edge joining two cycle vertices and the triangle case. All S(c) therefore commute with each other. Hermitian conjugation reverses r factors. The number of anticommuting pairs among the cycle edges is r (three for a triangle and r for r>=4), so reversal contributes (-1)^r. Together with conjugating i^r this gives S(c)*=S(c); the same count gives S(c)^2=I.

The X mask of S(c) is exactly the edge-incidence vector of c: every cycle edge contributes its own unique X, and all other letters in the A prefixes are Z. Products of S have X mask equal to the mod-two sum of their cycle incidence vectors.

The key signed claim is that **any product of cycle S's with zero X mask equals +I**, not -I or a hidden pure-Z word.
