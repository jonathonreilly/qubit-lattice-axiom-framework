## Summary

Step 2 of the universal-GR tensor closure: does the W-native induced graviton show **approximate
spin-2 isotropy** in the finite staggered-fermion packet? The cubic `Z³` lattice splits spin-2 into
two irreps; for graviton momentum along a cubic axis the two transverse-traceless polarizations
`E_g = (h_yy−h_zz)` and `T_2g = h_yz` sit in **different** little-group irreps (`C_4v`: `B_1`, `B_2`),
so the cubic group does **not** force their stiffnesses equal — only full SO(3) does. The diagnostic
is the Zener anisotropy `A = 2 C_44/(C_11−C_12)` of the graviton stiffness tensor (`A=1` iff
isotropic; `C_11=C_yyyy`, `C_12=C_yyzz`, `C_44=C_yzyz`).

The decisive finding:

> Spin-2 isotropy support depends **entirely on which lattice fermion realizes the framework's matter
> packet.** On the **naive** Dirac fermion (`2^d` doublers), the graviton is strongly anisotropic (`A ≈ 2.1`), an
> **O(1) leading-order** violation that does **not** vanish as `k→0`. On the framework's **proper
> staggered Kähler-Dirac** fermion (1-component, `η`-phases, `2^{d/2}` tastes, matching the retained
> matter-sector SO(4) packet), the graviton is **approximately
> isotropic** (`A ≈ 0.97`) — a **~40× improvement** — with only a small residual consistent with the
> framework's known small cubic lattice-gravity anisotropy (the `ℓ=4` `K4` cubic harmonic).

So `E_g` and `T_2g` agree to a few percent on the staggered fermion. The naive-Dirac O(1)
anisotropy is a wrong-fermion artifact, not evidence against the staggered W-native packet.
