### 4.3 One-shot W versus reset/corpus/frequency

Fix binary one-shot weight `q=1/3`. Compare a length-four IID corpus with a
frozen-memory corpus that samples one bit and repeats it four times. Every
trial position has marginal `q`, and both empirical means have expectation
`q`. Their variances are

```text
IID:     q(1-q)/4
frozen:  q(1-q).
```

The one-shot Born/trace law does not imply a frequency theorem. The reset or
joint-process condition is law data; concentration is its theorem; the actual
corpus is history data.

### 4.4 Measure versus actual member

One law with `q=1/3` admits actual member `0` and actual member `1`. Conversely,
actual member `0` has positive weight under both `q=1/3` and `q=1/2`. Hence a
branching measure does not select its member, and one member does not identify
the measure.

An explicitly sampled law can bundle the semantics of member production.
The sampled value still belongs to `H`. A deterministic delta/unique-history
law collapses the distinction.

### 4.5 Transition table versus equivalence category

Take two fixed states with distinct scalar content values. With the content
dictionary held fixed, only identity is an automorphism. If the entire state
and content dictionary are transported together, the label swap is an exact
isomorphism. The transition table is identical in both readings.

Thus the exact law can derive a stabilizer only after the compared structure
and transport rule are named. Foundation sort preservation is definitional;
law-relative physical equivalence is a theorem/definition over the complete
protocol category.

### 4.6 Event support versus clock rates

Two competing clock labels with hazards `(1,1)` and `(1,2)` have the same
event support. Their probability that `A` occurs next is `1/2` and `1/3`.
Multiplying `(1,2)` by three leaves `1/3` unchanged. Therefore:

- a common overall scaling can be conventional; but
- a dimensionless relative rate is predictive law content.

Commit order/count does not derive that ratio.

### 4.7 Isolated behavior versus collision/routing

Three append laws agree exactly when no front arrives or only `L` or `R`
arrives. When both arrive, one writes `L`, one writes `R`, and one cancels.
Every isolated-front theorem is shared; the first collision separates them.

### 4.8 Vacuum/one-species agreement versus matter coupling

Two response laws use couplings `(g_A,g_B)=(1,1)` and `(1,2)`. They agree in
vacuum and on species `A`; species `B` in the same source separates them.
Locality, covariance, and a common carrier do not choose universal coupling.
A deeper symmetry may derive it, but until then the coupling is in `G/O`.

### 4.9 Equal lapse versus tensor response


`gamma=0` and `gamma=1` have the same clock/lapse component and different
spatial response; a null-response proxy proportional to `(1+gamma)Phi`

gravity theory. It proves only that clock slowing does not logically specify
