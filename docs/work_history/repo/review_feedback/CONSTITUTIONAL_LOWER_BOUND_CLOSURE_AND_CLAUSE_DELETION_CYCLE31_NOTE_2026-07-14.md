## 2. Three Different Kinds Of Completeness

Confusing these three notions generated most of the apparent disagreement.

### 2.1 Semantic completeness of an instantiated structure

An instantiated model is semantically complete when every symbol and type has
an interpretation: lattice, onsite algebra, records, `A`, complete law fields,
boundary data, and—where stochastic—one normalized record-history measure. A
versioned model artifact can do this. Nothing in model semantics requires the
artifact to be derivable from the schema.

This is the strongest case for the phrase “the law is model data.” It is
honest, useful, and ordinary scientific practice. It does not prove that the
public theory selects the artifact.

### 2.2 Predictive completeness of a theory

Let `Prot` be the legal record protocols named by the theory. A theory is
predictively complete at record level exactly when, for every `p in Prot`, all
its admitted models assign the same record transcript law to `p`, or when the
theory explicitly conditions the claim on a named model parameter.

Thus a family can be semantically well formed while predictively open. If
models `M_1` and `M_2` share the same foundation and the same extensional
availability map but assign probabilities `1/2` and `2/3` to the next readable
record, the theory containing both does not entail either number.

### 2.3 Empirical model selection

Experiment can select, estimate, or disfavor members of a model family. Once
one member is selected, that empirical model can predict new records. This is
empirical completeness relative to a data set and model class, not a theorem
that the four axioms entailed the selected table or coefficient.

A TOE may intentionally include measured constants or an empirically selected
law artifact. It must label them as empirical model data. It cannot
simultaneously count them as derived from the axioms. The constitutional
question is therefore not whether model data is allowed; it is whether the
claimed endpoint is a framework family, an empirical model, or a
parameter-free predictive theory.

## 3. What “One Fixed Rule” Supplies

The existing sentence supplies four things:

- one rule symbol/slot per intended model;
- one interpretation of that symbol inside the model;
- constancy rather than history-by-history rule switching; and
- local/covariant typing constraints on that interpretation.

It does not supply:

- one common extension across all models of the theory;
- the numerical successor, branch, rate, instrument, or history weights;
- a proof that every admissible `A` is record-predictively equivalent; or
- the complete object `L*`, because the current memo types the named symbol as
  availability and explicitly walls it off from dynamics.

Therefore a TOE can treat the exact law as model data **under the existing
sentence** in either of two honest ways:

1. keep the document as a schema and state all results uniformly or
   conditionally; or
2. publish an instantiated model artifact containing extensional `A` and all
   remaining `L*` fields.

It cannot infer a unique unconditional record prediction merely from the words
“one fixed.” The singular grammar is intra-model functionality, not
inter-model extensional identity.
