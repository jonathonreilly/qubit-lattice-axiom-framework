## Result Up Front

The search found serious positive uniqueness theorems. They are much stronger
than slogans such as “local,” “minimal,” “causal,” or “information-theoretic”:

- D'Ariano, Erba, and Perinotti completely classify isotropic quantum walks in
  dimensions one through three at coin dimension two. In three dimensions the
  result is a pair of Weyl walks.
- Schumacher and Werner characterize reversible QCA structurally, and
  Arrighi, Nesme, and Werner prove that a causal unitary is locally
  implementable.
- Shore and Johnson uniquely characterize maximum-entropy or minimum-cross-
  entropy inference once the sample space, prior, and expectation constraints
  are supplied.
- Anomaly cancellation can fix charge ratios after a gauge group, matter
  representations, multiplicities, and interaction assumptions are supplied.
- Lovelock classifies the allowed local metric field-equation tensor in four
  dimensions under its covariance, derivative-order, and divergence
  assumptions.
- Causal-set covariance and causality classify a broad family of sequential
  growth laws, rather than one member.
- Operational and categorical reconstructions can select the quantum
  representation of states and transformations from substantial operational
  premises.

No theorem in the audited primary-source set selects a unique exact
microscopic law, or proves one exact physical-equivalence class, on the present
`Z^3 + M_2(C) + permanent-record` foundation while also supplying the complete
record-forming, actuality, statistics, and boundary interfaces. That sentence
is a scoped literature result, not a universal impossibility theorem.

The nearest positive result is the two-component isotropic-walk
classification. It is important because it shows that sparse principles can
almost select a law. It is also an exact diagnostic of what remains: its
domain is a one-particle coherent walk on a particular Cayley graph, its exact
three-dimensional answer is two Weyl walks rather than one, and it contains no
record instrument, actual-history rule, matter collision law, or boundary
selection.

The finite companion runner independently demonstrates why the other
mechanisms do not silently do more than their theorems say:

1. two distinct microscopic Markov laws have the same exact coarse law;
2. two prefix machines reverse which of two candidate rules is simplest;
3. maximum entropy changes when the physical atomization or prior changes;
4. different actions can have the same exact stationary path;
5. anomaly equations leave two one-generation hypercharge branches until
   interaction content distinguishes them, and retain an overall scale;
6. two onsite `M_2(C)` unitaries satisfy locality, translation covariance,
   proper-cubic covariance, and reversibility but have different readouts;
7. two different rewrite rules are each causally invariant and have the same
   event-order shape but produce different terminal records; and
8. one deterministic law has distinct histories from distinct initial data.

The constitutional consequence remains conservative. Literature does not
license adding “minimal,” “maximum entropy,” “least action,” “causal
invariance,” “anomaly free,” “universal,” or “all rules” as a substitute for
the missing exact referent. A future theorem could make the axiom update zero.
If science instead supplies the law, the minimal eventual move remains one
stable Admissibility identification of that exact object or proved physical-
equivalence class. No live edit is justified by this audit.

## What Would Count As Success

The target is not uniqueness of a notation, circuit factorization, continuum
equation, universality class, charge normalization, inference distribution, or
causal schedule. A successful theorem for this framework must return one of:

1. one exact microscopic predictive law on the generated physical domain; or
2. one exactly defined equivalence class, together with a proof that every
   representative gives the same probability for every finite readable record
   protocol in scope.

It must also say which inputs belong to universal law and which belong to a
physical boundary or realized history. A unique law need not select the
universe's contingent initial state. Conversely, selecting a boundary does not
identify the law.

The distinctions used below are:

- **classification:** restricts or enumerates a class of mathematical laws;
- **representation:** shows how a supplied dynamics or operational theory can
  be written;
- **low-energy universality:** fixes behavior after coarse-graining or in a
  limit while allowing different microscopic laws;
- **parameter fixing:** fixes some coefficients after a model class is
  supplied;
- **rule identity:** identifies the exact microscopic law or proved physical
  class;
- **actual history:** identifies one realized member when alternatives remain;
- **boundary selection:** selects contingent seed, state, domain, or asymptotic
  data.

## 1. Renormalization, Fixed Points, And Universality

Wilson and Kogut's renormalization-group framework explains why very different
microscopic systems can share fixed-point behavior and critical exponents. A
fixed point can classify relevant and irrelevant perturbations, and a critical
surface can reduce the number of low-energy parameters. That is real
selection at the level of an infrared universality class.

It is not inversion of the coarse-graining map. The companion runner gives an
exact four-state example: two different stochastic matrices are strongly
lumpable to the same two-state transition matrix. The coarse process cannot
recover which microscopic transition occurred. RG can therefore close a
`CONTINUUM` interface after a microscopic class and coarse map are supplied;
it does not by itself fill `ATOMIC_LAW`, `ACTUALITY`, or the physical boundary.

This route remains live in a more ambitious form. A UV fixed point plus a
finite-dimensional critical surface and measured relevant parameters might
define a predictive continuum theory. It would still need a theorem tying one
microscopic record law, or one exact record-equivalence class, to that fixed-
point data.

## 2. QCA, Locality, Unitarity, Homogeneity, And Isotropy

This is the strongest positive route found.

Schumacher and Werner show that translation-invariant finite-propagation QCA
have a local structural form and that their inverses remain local. Arrighi,
Nesme, and Werner show that unitary causality implies a local circuit
realization. Gross, Nesme, Vogts, and Werner give a complete one-dimensional
index for connected deformation and gluing classes. These theorems answer
important representation and topological-classification questions. They do
not choose which causal unitary is Nature's law.

D'Ariano, Erba, and Perinotti go farther. At cell dimension two they give a
complete classification of isotropic walks in dimensions one, two, and three;
in three dimensions there are two Weyl walks. The broader QCA program obtains
Weyl, Dirac, and Maxwell free-field forms from linearity, unitarity, locality,
homogeneity, isotropy, and minimal dimension in the relativistic small-wave-
vector regime.

### Extra premises that do the narrowing

The positive theorem must be cited with its actual inputs. The relevant stack
includes a linear one-particle walk, unitarity, homogeneity, finite locality,
an isotropy group and its action on generators and coin, cell dimension two,
and the Cayley-graph/group setting. The free-field continuum work adds the
small-wave-vector regime and the chosen composition used for Dirac or Maxwell
fields. None of those are supplied merely by the current statement that every
site has an `M_2(C)` possibility domain and one fixed nearest-neighbor
admissibility rule.

The pair might still collapse to one physical class if reflection or another
operational transformation is proved to identify the two walks for every
readable record protocol. That is a live positive route. It requires the exact
record decoder and physical equivalence relation; calling the walks “the same
up to chirality” is not the proof.

The runner's simpler onsite control is intentionally weaker but exact. The
identity and a quarter-turn phase gate, installed at every site, are both
range-zero, unitary, translation-covariant, and proper-cubic-covariant. They
give different `X` readouts from the same `|+>` input. Thus the generic
structural QCA predicates alone are not a selector. The D'Ariano theorem works
because its additional nontriviality, carrier, graph, and isotropy premises do
real work.

## 3. Anomaly Cancellation, Gauge Structure, And Matter

Anomaly cancellation is one of the best examples of genuine parameter
selection after a model class is supplied. Nowakowski and Pilaftsis exhibit a
minimal neutral-fermion extension in which anomaly cancellation fixes the
fermion hypercharges. Weinberg's soft-particle S-matrix analysis likewise
derives charge conservation and universal gravitational coupling from Lorentz
invariance, pole structure, and supplied massless spin-one and spin-two
particles.

The converse boundary is equally primary-source-supported. Batra, Dobrescu,
and Spivak construct anomaly-free chiral completions for arbitrary charge
assignments under an additional `U(1)`. Costa, Dobrescu, and Fox parameterize
the general integer solutions of the `U(1)` anomaly equations. Anomaly freedom
is therefore a consistency filter, not a stand-alone selector of gauge group,
representations, family number, scalar sector, couplings, or microscopic
dynamics.

The exact finite ablation fixes one Standard-Model-like generation of
left-handed Weyl representations and normalizes the quark-doublet charge to
one. The mixed nonabelian, gravitational, and cubic anomaly equations leave
two solutions:

```text
(q, u^c, d^c, l, e^c) = (1, -4, 2, -3, 6),
(q, u^c, d^c, l, e^c) = (1,  2,-4, -3, 6).
```

A Higgs charge and oriented Yukawa coupling distinguish which singlet is the
up-type field. The common charge scale remains conventional until a coupling
normalization is supplied. This is successful conditional parameter fixing,
not microscopic rule or history selection.

## 4. Action, Extremality, Relativity, And Gravity

“The actual history extremizes an action” becomes predictive only after the
action, its domain, boundary class, and quantization or sampling rule are
specified. The runner gives two exact actions with the same unique stationary
middle point but different off-shell values and curvatures. It also gives a
third action with a different stationary point. Extremality is therefore a
law schema, not an action selector.

There are nevertheless strong conditional form theorems:

- Lovelock shows that in four dimensions the symmetric divergence-free
  rank-two concomitants of a metric and its first two derivatives reduce to
  the metric and Einstein tensors. Their coefficients, sources, matter
  coupling, and boundary data remain.
- Hojman, Kuchar, and Teitelboim recover geometrodynamics after imposing the
  hypersurface-deformation/path-independence structure and canonical
  assumptions.
- Weinberg derives soft spin-one and spin-two consistency consequences after
  Lorentz-invariant S-matrix and particle-content premises are supplied.

These are excellent `GRAVITY` and continuum-interface targets for a completed
microscopic law. They do not identify the `Z^3 + M_2(C)` record law from which
the metric, stress tensor, S-matrix, or hypersurface algebra would have to be
derived.

## 5. Algorithmic Simplicity And Universal Priors

Solomonoff assigns prior weight using program lengths for a universal Turing
machine. Kolmogorov complexity makes description length precise up to the
choice of description machine. This is a powerful theory of inference and
prediction over computable hypotheses.

It is not an ontic selector in the form needed here:

- the universal prior is a mixture over rules, not one actual rule;
- the reference universal machine changes finite description lengths by a
  machine-dependent constant;
- program equivalence and physical observational equivalence are different
  quotients; and
- a prior does not select one actual program, initial state, or history.

The exact two-code ablation is deliberately minimal. Each of two prefix-free
description languages can be embedded in a universal machine. One gives rule
`A` a one-bit code and `B` a two-bit code; the other reverses the assignments.
The “simplest” rule and the `2^{-length}` prior ranking reverse. Invariance up
to an additive constant protects asymptotic theory; it does not supply a
machine-independent finite identity for the universe's rule.

An algorithmic principle could still become physics if the framework derives
a privileged physical description language, an exact prior or minimization
functional, and a theorem mapping the selected program to all record
protocols. Those are extra premises to expose, not reasons to dismiss the
route.

## 6. Maximum Entropy And Information Principles

Jaynes derives statistical-mechanical distributions by maximizing entropy
subject to stated information. Shore and Johnson axiomatize consistent
updating and uniquely select maximum entropy or minimum cross entropy when
new information is supplied as expectation constraints.

Their 1983 correction is load-bearing for the discrete statement: preserving
a prior that already satisfies the new information must be included explicitly
in the consistency package. That correction strengthens, rather than weakens,
the placement conclusion here—the uniqueness theorem is conditional on a
precise inference domain and precise update axioms.

That theorem can close a `STATISTICS` field after the physical sample space,
prior, and constraints have been identified. It does not select those inputs,
the transition dynamics, or one actual sample. The finite control shows both
dependencies exactly:

- uniform MaxEnt gives event `A` weight `1/2` on atoms `{A,B}` but `1/3`
  after the alternative is physically refined to `{A,B1,B2}`; and
- with no new constraint, minimum cross entropy returns the supplied prior,
  so two different priors give two different posteriors.

Operational quantum reconstructions are analogous positive results at a
different layer. Chiribella, D'Ariano, and Perinotti reconstruct finite
quantum theory from operational principles including purification. De la
Torre, Masanes, Short, and Mueller prove that theories with local qubits and a
continuous reversible interaction have the quantum global structure. These
theorems can fix the representation of state and transformation spaces. They
do not choose the Hamiltonian, QCA, formation instrument, boundary, or actual
history inside quantum theory.

## 7. Constructor And Categorical Reconstructions

Constructor theory organizes physics by possible and impossible
transformations and derives substantial information-theoretic structure.
Categorical reconstructions characterize finite quantum theory from dagger,
discarding, purification, and related compositional principles. Tull's result,
for example, still permits complex or real quantum theory depending on the
premises.

These routes are valuable languages for `DOMAIN`, `STATE`, `CONTEXT`, and
composition. They classify what a theory can look like. The possible/
impossible transformation table is itself determined by the laws; it is not a
selector of that table. A categorical equivalence also does not choose one
microscopic dynamics or one realized history.

## 8. Causal Growth And Wolfram-Style Mechanisms

Rideout and Sorkin derive a broad family of classical sequential-growth laws
from causality and discrete general covariance. Varadarajan and Rideout extend
the result to include zero transition probabilities and still obtain a
general family. This is a particularly relevant positive precedent: sparse
causal principles can classify the form of stochastic growth, but residual
couplings remain law data.

Gorard proves within the Wolfram-model setting that causal invariance is
equivalent to a discrete version of general covariance, with update-order
changes acting as discrete gauge transformations. Under additional fixed-
dimensional continuum assumptions, the work derives discrete Einstein-like
constraints. The official project material likewise defines causal
invariance as a property of certain supplied rules for which update orders
lead to the same causal result.

The finite rewrite ablation gives two different rules. Each has two independent
events, is exactly order independent, and has the same two-node antichain
causal graph. One writes terminal records `BB`; the other writes `CC`.
Causal invariance removes schedule presentation within a supplied rule. It
does not choose the rule or identify different readable terminal records.

### Does Wolfram contribute more than schedule quotient?

Conceptually, yes. The ruliad proposal is an all-rule ontology, not merely an
update-order lemma. It proposes one limiting object containing all rules, all
initial conditions, and all paths; observer-dependent sampling, rulial
location, and equivalence classes are then meant to explain why an embedded
observer experiences effective laws. The program also proposes continuum and
observer-coarse-graining selection mechanisms.

For the present canonical-law closure, however, the rigorously reusable result
is narrower: causal invariance supplies a schedule-gauge test and, with
additional continuum premises, a relativity/gravity acceptance test for a
supplied rewrite rule. The official ruliad account explicitly treats
uniqueness as a claim whose construction involves subtleties about limits,
sampling, rule equivalence, and observer parsing. Because the ruliad contains
all rules and all initial conditions, it does not by itself return one exact
record law, one physical observer quotient, one weight measure, one boundary,
or one actual record history for this framework.

So the concise answer is:

> Wolfram contributes a broader all-rule/observer-equivalence research
> hypothesis and useful continuum criteria in addition to schedule quotient.
> What is currently theorem-grade and directly usable here is still an
> acceptance/equivalence test for a supplied rule, not the unique identity of
> the record-forming law.

That is more than “nothing,” and less than the missing selector.

## 9. Exact Finite Ablations

The ablations are separation witnesses, not models of the universe.

### A. Coarse-graining is many-to-one

Four microstates are grouped into two macrostates. Two distinct exact
row-stochastic matrices satisfy the strong lumpability condition and induce
the same macro matrix

```text
Q = [[3/4, 1/4],
     [1/3, 2/3]].
```

They differ on finite micro-transition questions. This is the exact finite
analogue of low-energy universality failing to invert to microscopic identity.

### B. Simplicity ranking depends on the description machine

The prefix-free code sets `{0,10}` and `{10,0}` have the same Kraft sum. They
reverse the one-bit and two-bit descriptions of candidate rules `A` and `B`,
and therefore reverse both shortest-description ranking and `2^{-length}`
weight.

### C. MaxEnt depends on atomization and prior

Uniform entropy on two atoms and three atoms gives different probability to
the same named coarse event. Relative entropy with no new constraint returns
whichever prior was supplied. The inference functional is unique conditional
on its physical inputs; it does not choose them.

### D. Extremality does not choose the action

With fixed endpoints and one interior path value `x`, both

```text
S_0(x) = (x-1)^2,
S_1(x) = (x-1)^2 + (x-1)^4
```

have the same unique stationary minimum `x=1`, despite different off-shell
actions. The action `S_2(x)=(x-3/2)^2` has a different minimum. “Extremize”
without the exact functional selects neither.

### E. Anomaly constraints narrow but do not fully identify

The fixed one-generation anomaly system factorizes to

```text
-18 (u-2)(u+4) = 0
```

after the linear constraints and charge-scale convention are imposed. The two
branches exchange the singlet assignments. Yukawa/Higgs content selects their
physical names; absolute normalization remains outside the anomaly equations.

### F. Local unitary structure leaves rule freedom

`I` and `diag(1,i)` are two exact onsite qubit unitaries. Their homogeneous
lattice products satisfy range-zero locality, translations, proper cubic
rotations, and reversibility. On `|+>` they give different `X` expectations,
respectively `1` and `0`.

### G. Causal invariance is not rule selection

The independent replacement systems `A->B` and `A->C`, begun from two `A`
tokens, are confluent and schedule independent. They have isomorphic event
causal graphs but different readable terminal labels.

### H. Law identity is not boundary identity

The same deterministic bit-flip law gives complementary finite histories from
initial bits zero and one. Choosing the exact rule does not choose its
contingent seed.

## 11. Constitutional Placement Consequence

This audit does not support a new selector axiom such as:

```text
The law is the simplest law.
The law maximizes entropy.
The law is causally invariant.
The law is the unique isotropic QCA.
The universe follows all rules.
```

Each sentence either omits its exact mathematical domain and functional, or
overstates the cited theorem. The strongest QCA theorem gives a pair on a
different semantic domain. MaxEnt selects an inference distribution after
inputs. Causal invariance tests update-order equivalence after a rule. The
ruliad moves specificity into observer sampling and equivalence.

The current three-way constitutional gate remains:

1. **Derivation:** prove one complete exact law or one complete record-
   equivalence class from the current foundation; then make no axiom edit.
2. **Identification:** scientifically select a complete exact law and identify
   it by one stable Admissibility reference; formation, probability, time,
   matter, resource, and gravity claims become downstream theorems.
3. **Open:** if neither occurs, keep the law-selection problem explicit rather
   than filling Record with mechanism prose.

The literature audit advances route 1 by identifying the most credible
theorem templates. It does not close it.
