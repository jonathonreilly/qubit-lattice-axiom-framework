## Result

The complete deterministic table is **not globally confluent on its
unrestricted extensional record-configuration domain**.

Fix two adjacent open sites `A` and `B`. Every other lattice site is already a
permanent record. `A` therefore sees five recorded neighbours and omits only
the direction toward `B`; `B` sees five recorded neighbours and omits only the
direction toward `A`.

The Cycle-178 law contains:

```text

```

The exhaustive pair result is:

```text

A remains enabled, B disables A                1,152

both remain enabled with the same outputs            0
```

Thus 99.998894 percent of the tested adjacent row pairs are mutual-disable
critical pairs. None has the local diamond in which both original writes
remain available after either order.

One exact witness is:

```text

  -x L5, -y S8, -z R2, +z L7, +y FP  -> F

B at (1,0,0):
  -y S8, -z L7, +z R2, +y FP, +x L5  -> F
```

The ten supporting record sites are disjoint. Initially both exact arity-five
rows fire. If `A` writes `F` first, `B` acquires the sixth neighbour
`-x:F`; that arity-six signature has no law row. If `B` writes first, the
corresponding `+x:F` extension at `A` also has no row.

Because every site other than `A` and `B` is already recorded, there is no
third open site that can later alter either signature. The two sequential
maximal completions are therefore:

```text
background + record F at A
background + record F at B.
```

They are distinct permanent record configurations. All 24 proper-cubic images
preserve the same critical pair.

The exact conclusion is:

The campaign's schedule-confluence results remain valid for their exact

The probe is explicitly not a reachable-state counterexample from the official

## Why this matters for bare metal

There are at least three different dynamics compatible with the same displayed
availability table:

1. **asynchronous one-at-a-time:** whichever of `A` or `B` occurs first blocks
   the other;
2. **synchronous pre-state update:** both initially enabled sites write in one
   global step, producing a third terminal configuration; and
3. **weighted or selected history:** a sampler, priority, boundary, or global
   history law chooses or weights the alternatives.

The nearest-neighbour table does not choose among them. The choice changes
readable permanent facts.

This is the exact reason “a record forms when its signature is available” is
not a complete occurrence law. Availability says which events are candidates.
It does not say whether candidates fire serially, in batches, by priority, by
sampling, or as branches of a global history object.
