B1  block 48's hitting probability and its shell constant
     For the directed walk with step frequencies f, the probability of hitting the
     origin from x is the multinomial h(x,f) = (n!/prod x_k!) prod f_k^{x_k}, n = |x|_1.
     Integrating it over the simplex of step frequencies:
ok        shell n = 2: the integral is the SAME at every site listed, and equals 2/((2+1)(2+2)) = 1/6
ok        shell n = 3: the integral is the SAME at every site listed, and equals 2/((3+1)(3+2)) = 1/10
ok        shell n = 4: the integral is the SAME at every site listed, and equals 2/((4+1)(4+2)) = 1/15
     The shell-constancy is block 48's substantive claim and it holds exactly.  On the
     VALUE, a note on convention: with the uniform PROBABILITY measure on the simplex
     (density 2 on a triangle of area 1/2) the constant is 2/((n+1)(n+2)); with plain
     Lebesgue measure it is 1/((n+1)(n+2)), which is the form the unit quotes.  Both
     are correct; the statement needs the measure named, and this script uses the
     probability measure throughout.

B2  (a) the shadow of one slowing site
     A record that has passed the slowing site carries kappa v and therefore HOPS at
     kappa times its old rate.  The number flux through every site is unchanged, so to
     carry the same flux at a lower speed the slowed population must be denser by
     exactly 1/kappa.  Writing h = h(x,s) for the fraction of the flux at x that came
     through the origin:
ok          density   = rho*(h - kappa*(h - 1))/kappa = rho (1 + h(1/kappa - 1)) - the shadow is DENSER
ok          number flux = rho*v = rho v - UNCHANGED, as it must be
       momentum density = the SAME expression, because in this model the velocity is
         proportional to the content: sum(density x velocity) and sum(density x content)
         are literally the same sum.  That is not a second check, it is one fact read
         twice, and it is why the extra records exactly make up for their smaller
         contents.
ok          momentum CURRENT = rho*v**2*(h*kappa - h + 1) = rho v^2 (1 - h(1-kappa))
     - and THAT is the shadow: the number current and the momentum density are both
     untouched, while the momentum current is short by exactly rho v^2 h (1 - kappa).
     A slowing body casts a shadow in momentum flux and in nothing else, which is why
     it can pull without growing.

B3  (b) two slowing sites
     A second slowing site takes (1 - kappa_2) of the momentum current reaching it.
     The isotropic part of that current is the same from every direction and cancels;
     what survives is the deficit B2 computed, which is (1 - kappa_1) h times the
     current, so the net push on site 2 is
       F_2 = h*m2*rho*(1 - kappa_1)*(1 - kappa_2)   (times block 48's geometric factor; m2 = <m^2>)
ok        This is SYMMETRIC under exchanging the two bodies (checked by a simultaneous swap),
       and block 48's geometric factor
       depends only on the separation, so ACTION AND REACTION ARE EQUAL AND OPPOSITE.
     (Contrast the halo of a producing lump, where the response depends on one body's
      production and the other's size and the pair law is not symmetric.)
     Neither body grows: a slowing site keeps no record, by the definition of the
     clause, so the capture rate is identically zero and block 49's tie between
     attraction and growth is evaded - the momentum is taken from the gas, not the
     records.
     The magnitude carries the weight <m^2>, not <m>: the momentum current is
     quadratic in the content, so a gas of slow records pulls much more weakly than
     its number density suggests.

B4  (c) the price, and the inequality
     Each passage multiplies m by kappa, so along a path a record meets n_b sigma per
     unit length and its speed decays at the rate n_b sigma <m> (1 - kappa):
       run-down time      tau = 1/(m1*n_b*sigma*u)   with u = 1 - kappa   (LINEAR in u)
       pull between a pair F  = m2*rho*u**2/r**2   (QUADRATIC in u)
ok          approach time      t = sqrt(r M / F) = sqrt(M)*r**(3/2)/(sqrt(m2)*sqrt(rho)*u)
     The attraction is usable only if the pair closes before the gas runs down, t < tau:
ok          (t/tau)^2 = M*m1**2*n_b**2*r**3*sigma**2/(m2*rho)
ok        and KAPPA HAS CANCELLED: the condition does not depend on the slowing strength at
       all.  The pull is quadratic in 1 - kappa and the run-down linear, and the
       approach time goes as the inverse first power, so the two cancel exactly.
     The inequality is therefore
       M r^3 n_b^2 sigma^2 <m>^2  <  rho <m^2>,
     i.e. a gas can sustain attraction out to
       r^3 < rho <m^2> / (M n_b^2 sigma^2 <m>^2),
     which is set by the DENSITY of slowing sites and the gas's <m^2>/<m>^2, and not
     by how hard any one of them slows.  Making the slowers gentler buys exactly as
     much life for the gas as it costs in pull.

SUMMARY: PARTIAL on attraction without growth: a slowing site leaves the number current and the momentum DENSITY exactly unchanged - the slowed population is denser by 1/kappa and carries kappa times the content, and the two compensate - while the momentum CURRENT is short by exactly rho v^2 h (1 - kappa), so a slowing body casts a shadow in momentum flux and in nothing else, which is how it pulls without capturing anything; the push on a second slowing site is (1 - kappa_1)(1 - kappa_2) rho <m^2> h times block 48's geometric factor, symmetric under exchange so action and reaction are equal and opposite, and weighted by <m^2> rather than <m>; and for the price, the run-down of the gas is LINEAR in 1 - kappa while the pull is QUADRATIC, but the approach time goes as the inverse first power, so kappa cancels exactly from the usability condition, leaving M r^3 n_b^2 sigma^2 <m>^2 < rho <m^2> - a bound set by the density of slowing sites and the gas's <m^2>/<m>^2, not by how hard any one site slows.
