S1  the current rule's fourth-rank moment
     Axis hops only, one way, rate |s_k|/sqrt3, so sum_d a d_k d_l = delta_kl |s_k|/sqrt3
     and T_ijkl = <s_i s_j |s_k|> delta_kl / sqrt3.  With the unit's sphere moments:
       T_1111 = <s_1^2|s_1|>/sqrt3 = sqrt(3)/12
       T_1122 = <s_1^2|s_2|>/sqrt3 = sqrt(3)/24
       T_1212 = 0                      (delta_kl kills k=1, l=2)
     An isotropic fourth-rank tensor symmetric in (ij) and (kl) is
       T = A delta_ij delta_kl + B(delta_ik delta_jl + delta_il delta_jk),
     so T_1212 = B = 0, forcing T_1111 = A = T_1122.  But
ok          T_1111 - T_1122 = sqrt(3)/24 != 0: NOT isotropic, as block 51 says

S2  the reduction
     Write M_kl(s) = sum_d a(s,d) d_k d_l.  If M(s) = m(s) I with m invariant under the
     cubic group, then T_ijkl = <s_i s_j m(s)> delta_kl, and cubic symmetry alone gives
     <s_1^2 m> = <s_2^2 m> = <s_3^2 m> and <s_1 s_2 m> = 0, so <s_i s_j m> = (<m>/3)
     delta_ij and T = (<m>/3) delta_ij delta_kl - isotropic (the B = 0 branch).
     So it is ENOUGH to make the hop second moment a multiple of the identity at each
     content.  That is the condition the rest of this script works with.
     First, the unit's sphere moments, recomputed here rather than taken on trust:
ok          <|s_k|>       = 1/2
ok          <s_i^2 |s_i|> = 1/4
ok          <s_i^2 |s_k|> = 1/8  (i /= k)
     - all three agree with block 51, so S1's arithmetic rests on checked numbers.
     Now the reduction itself, on a non-constant cubic invariant m = s_1^4+s_2^4+s_3^4:
ok          <s_1 s_2 m> = 0
ok          <s_1^2 m> = <s_3^2 m> = 1/5
     so <s_i s_j m> = (<m>/3) delta_ij for a cubic-invariant m, as claimed, and the
     fourth-rank moment is isotropic whenever M(s) = m(s) I.

S3  forward hops along the axes only: impossible
     Take s = e_1.  The only axis neighbour with s.d > 0 is d = (1,0,0), so
       M = a (1,0,0)(1,0,0)^T = diag(a, 0, 0),
ok        which is a multiple of the identity only if a = 0, i.e. the record never moves.
     So NO forward-only rule on the axis neighbours alone can be isotropic.  This is
     the sense in which the unit's 'the cubic term comes from hops along lattice axes
     only' is right - but S4 shows it is the FORWARD-ONLY part that is doing the work.

S4  axes only, if a record may also step backwards
     Take a(s, +-e_k) = lambda (1 +- kappa s_k), non-negative for every unit s when
     kappa <= 1 since |s_k| <= 1.  Then
ok          mean displacement = 2 lambda kappa s   (proportional to s, as required)
ok          second moment      = 2 lambda I        (a multiple of I, INDEPENDENT of s)
     The total rate is 6 lambda, also independent of s.  By S2 the fourth-rank moment
     is isotropic, with no diagonal neighbours used at all.
     So the anisotropy of the current clause is NOT caused by restricting hops to the
     axes.  It is caused by the rate |s_k| being ONE-WAY: the second moment then
     inherits the content's direction.  A two-way linear rate on the same six
     neighbours removes it exactly.

S5  forward-only on the full 26-neighbour set: exact witnesses
     If backward steps are refused (a(s,d) = 0 whenever s.d <= 0), diagonals become
     necessary by S3.  They are also sufficient.  Exact rational rates, symmetrised
     over the stabiliser of s in the cubic group, normalised to m = 1:
ok        s = (1, 0, 0): 4 neighbours, mean = (1, 0, 0) parallel to s, M = 1 I, all rates >= 0, every hop forward
ok        s = (1, 1, 0): 6 neighbours, mean = (1, 1, 0) parallel to s, M = 1 I, all rates >= 0, every hop forward
ok        s = (1, 1, 1): 6 neighbours, mean = (3/5, 3/5, 3/5) parallel to s, M = 1 I, all rates >= 0, every hop forward
     The s = (1,0,0) witness is the clean one: rate 1/4 on each of the four body
     diagonals (1,+-1,+-1).  Their mean is (1,0,0) and their second moment is exactly
     I, because each has d_k^2 = 1 in every coordinate and the signs cancel pairwise.
     Generic directions work too (checked separately by exact rational programming at
     (3,2,1) and (5,1,1); those rates are not symmetric and are not reproduced here).

S6  (b) what stays and what moves
     total hop rate at s = (1, 0, 0): 1
     total hop rate at s = (1, 1, 0): 2
     total hop rate at s = (1, 1, 1): 6/5
ok        the total rate is NOT the same at every content for these witnesses, so 'one event
       per record' does not come for free: a rule with a content-dependent total rate
       changes the event clock, and the uniform product measure's stationarity has to
       be re-argued rather than inherited from block 44.  By contrast S4's rule has
       total rate 6 lambda at every content, so it keeps a single event clock.
     Capture: block 48's law |s|_1 counts records stepping onto a site, which for axis
     hops is sum_k |s_k|.  Under S4 the analogous count is sum over the six axis
     neighbours of lambda(1 -+ kappa s_k) = 6 lambda MINUS the flux 2 lambda kappa s.n,
     so capture becomes affine in s rather than |s|_1 - a different law.  NOT verified
     here against a simulator.
     Collisionless shadow: NOT computed.  The shadow is a statement about straight-line
     transport behind a body, and S4's rule makes every record diffuse as well as
     stream, so the shadow should soften; I have not measured it.

S7  (c) the cost
     Three different prices, one per construction:
       S4 (axes, two-way): records step BACKWARDS against their content at positive
         rate.  Cheapest in geometry (no new neighbours, six as before, one event
         clock) and most expensive in reading: a record's content no longer determines
         the direction it moves, only the direction it moves ON AVERAGE.
       S5 (forward-only, 26 neighbours): the owner's reading is preserved - a record
         only ever steps somewhere it is heading - at the cost of diagonal neighbours,
         so the lattice's adjacency is no longer the nearest-neighbour graph the other
         clauses use, and the total rate varies with content.
       Keeping the present clause: the wind stays anisotropic, which is block 51's
         finding and the reason the unit exists.
     Nothing here chooses between them; that is the owner's call.

SUMMARY: PARTIAL on the isotropic streaming clause: non-negative rates with mean displacement proportional to s and an isotropic fourth-rank moment DO exist, and the anisotropy of the present clause is not caused by restricting hops to the lattice axes. The fourth-rank moment is isotropic as soon as the hop second moment M(s) = sum_d a(s,d) d d^T is a multiple of the identity with a cubic-invariant coefficient, since cubic symmetry then gives T = (<m>/3) delta_ij delta_kl. Forward hops on the axes alone cannot do it (at s = e_1 the only forward axis neighbour gives M = diag(a,0,0)), but the two-way axis rule a(s,+-e_k) = lambda(1 +- kappa s_k), non-negative for kappa <= 1, has mean displacement exactly 2 lambda kappa s and second moment exactly 2 lambda I independent of s, using no diagonal neighbours at all; while if backward steps are refused, exact rational forward-only witnesses on the 26 neighbours exist, the cleanest being rate 1/4 on each of the four body diagonals (1,+-1,+-1) at s = (1,0,0), whose mean is (1,0,0) and whose second moment is exactly I. The two constructions differ in cost: the two-way axis rule keeps one event clock (total rate 6 lambda at every content) but lets records step backwards, whereas the forward-only rule preserves the owner's reading but needs diagonal neighbours and has a content-dependent total rate
