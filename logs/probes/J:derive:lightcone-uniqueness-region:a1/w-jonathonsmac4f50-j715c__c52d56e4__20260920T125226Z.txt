C1  the universal partner equation
ok   M'(z) = -(z-a)q(z): M(z) is the mass above z of the signed density (z-a)q,
ok       and M(1) = 0, so M(z) = int_z^1 (t-a)q(t)dt
ok   M(-1) = 0 forces a = coth k - 1/k, the mean of z: the signed density (z-a)q has
     zero total mass for that a and no other, which is what makes the pairing exist.
ok   M(z) = Mmax - c*omega(k(z-a)) with c = e^{ka}/(2k sinh k), omega(x) = e^x(x-1)+1
     so the two solutions of M(z_-) = M(z_+) are the two solutions of
     omega(zeta_-) = omega(zeta_+) in the single variable zeta = k(z-a): the pairing
     is k-free in that variable.

C2  the antitone pairing is an admissible plan
ok   omega'(x) = x e^x: omega decreases on x<0 and increases on x>0, so each level of M
     has exactly two solutions z_- < A < z_+, and pairing them transports the
     deficit {z < A} onto the excess {z > A}: the mass below z_- equals the mass
     above z_+ by construction (both are M evaluated at the same level).
ok   at k=7/10 a level of M is met on both branches (zeta_+ ~ 0.351878, zeta_- ~ -0.460745)

     with equality at f = w.s.  The perpendicular influence is exactly A(k)/k.
