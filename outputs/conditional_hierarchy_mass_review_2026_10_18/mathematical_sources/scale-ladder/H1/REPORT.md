**Plain summary**
- **(a) The 16 is not carried up the ladder.**
  - Adding a Wilson term changes the zero count from 16 to 1. That term respects every symmetry the axioms allow, including the B3a four-direction form.
  - What an anomaly or index would carry is the signed count, and that is 0.
  - The determinant degree is just the number of sites in the block: 16 on a 2^4 block, 256 on 4^4.
  - Even a carried integer would not give α^16, because the base α is not carried either: it runs, and its three scheme versions differ step by step by u0 (a factor u0^16 ≈ 1/8 at the 16th power).
  - So B4 cannot be closed by transport. It stays main's one-rung attachment gap.
- **(b) The ladder's own content has nothing to transmute.** U(1), Weyl fermions and linearised gravity have no asymptotically free coupling.
  - If SU(N) with fundamental flavours at g_bare² = 1 is supplied, 15 one-loop decades need b0 ≤ 2.29.
  - For every N from 2 to 100 that forces b1 < 0, so the two-loop flow ends at an infrared fixed point and makes no scale.
  - SU(3) content with b1 > 0 reaches at most 6 decades.
- **(c) Compounding over zoom levels makes no scale.** The product of (a/w)^p over rungs telescopes to (a0/aN)^p, and the rung count changes when rungs are regrouped.
  - For a relevant coupling, ξ/a ≤ C·(1/δ)^(1/y).
  - With inputs δ ≥ 1e-2 and the ladder's mass exponents y ≥ 1, that is at most about 100·C.
- **"Relevant couplings are never fixed" (PR line 367).** It can be proved in this form: the four tools can force a relevant coupling to zero, never to a nonzero value. The tools are invariant transport, uniqueness, channel limits and constraint closure. Closure drops on-site masses exactly.
  - That is the HIER-NO-GO for the tool class.
  - It is not the no-go for (c). (c) is about what fixed rung-0 data do, and the bound above decides that.

**(a) Is 16 a cross-rung invariant?** Admissible deformations here are those covariant under translations and proper cubic rotations (axioms note `:37-41,57-61`), plus the B3a hypercubic form.

1. **Species count (proved and checked).**
   - The Wilson term is invariant under all 384 signed permutations (`r1.log:12`).
   - The zero count is 16 at r = 0 and 1 for every r > 0 (`r1.log:9-10`), with doubler mass 2r going to 0 continuously (`:11`). So it is not locally constant on an admissible family.
   - Its only protection is the doubler shift k → k+πe_μ, which Wilson breaks (`:13`). That shift is not generated by axiom symmetries (`:14`), so the protection is supplied.
   - Exact factor-2 blocking folds all 16 corners onto the coarse Γ point. So the number of zeros is not blocking-invariant; only the free-fermion multiplicity is.
2. **Signed index and exact-symmetry anomaly (proved; checked in a 2D U(1) toy).**
   - Corner S has chirality (−1)^|S|, so the signed sum is (1−1)^d = 0 (`r1.log:15-21`).
   - In 2D, the naive operator has 4|Q| zero modes and zero spectral flow. Wilson flow by branch is +Q, −2Q, +Q, total 0 (`:26-31`).
3. **Singlet-axial anomaly coefficient (reading).** It equals 2^d, but that rotation is not a lattice symmetry. The coefficient follows the IR content, which item 1 changes from 16 to 1.
4. **Mod-16 anomaly (comparator, from memory).** 16 Weyl species give ν ≡ 0 under Spin-Z4, so they are anomaly-free and gappable (García-Etxebarria–Montero; Tong). The repo's Spin(10) atomic-gap note (`SPIN10_..._2026-09-14.md:54-56`) is a witness. Here 16 is the order of the anomaly group, so it protects nothing.

**(c) Rung compounding**

- **Hyperbolic bound:** proved under four hypotheses, and checked on 200 random maps (worst run/bound 0.968, `r4.log:1`).

  - H2: a cone condition with exponent y.
