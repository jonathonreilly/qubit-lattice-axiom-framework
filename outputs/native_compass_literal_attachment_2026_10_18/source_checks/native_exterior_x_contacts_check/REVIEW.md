## 2. Four-site invariant sectors and exact error

There are four physical qubits L,1,2,R and four positive blocks:
\[
 H_4=K+J(3I-X_LX_1-X_1X_2-X_2X_R),\qquad J>0.
 \tag{R1}
\]
The three axis blocks each have eigenvalues 0 and \(2J\); K has eigenvalues 1 and 3. The old incident load is \(3+4J\), the outer-site load \(2J\). This diagnostic sets \(J_y=J_z=0\) explicitly and does not pretend to be the positive-transverse model.

Since \(X_L,X_R\) commute with \(H_4\), their eigenvalues \(a,b=\pm1\) label exact invariant blocks. A supplied \(|a\rangle_X|b\rangle_X\) remains that environment vector. On the old XX eigenspace \(p=\pm1\), use \(Z=Z_1Z_2\), \(X=X_1\); both preserve this two-dimensional space and anticommute there, and \(X_2=pX\). Therefore
\[
 H_{ab,p}=(2+3J-Jp)I+Z-d_pX,\qquad
 d_p=J(a+bp),\qquad \Omega_p=\sqrt{1+d_p^2}.
 \tag{R2}
\]
For \(p=ab\), \(d_p=2aJ\); for \(p=-ab\), \(d_p=0\). Exactly one old sector is detuned, irrespective of endpoint signs.

For \(\cos\theta=3/5,\sin\theta=4/5\),
\[
 W=e^{i\theta}e^{i\theta X_1X_2}
   =\frac{9+12i}{25}I+\frac{-16+12i}{25}X_1X_2 .
 \tag{R3}
\]
Comparing against \(e^{-i(3Jt+\theta)}e^{-itK}W\), the target adjoint times actual evolution in sector p is precisely
\[
 F_p=e^{ip\delta}e^{itZ}e^{-it(Z-d_pX)},\qquad
 \delta=Jt-\theta .
 \tag{R4}
\]
The \(2I\) part of K and the \(3JI\) from the positive bonds have thus been included. They have not been replaced by sector-dependent phases.

Writing \(A_p=Z-d_pX\), \(A_p^2=\Omega_p^2I\) gives the exact SU(2) half-trace
\[
 c_p=\cos t\cos(\Omega_pt)+\frac{\sin t\sin(\Omega_pt)}{\Omega_p}.
 \tag{R5}
\]
It is real and lies in \([-1,1]\), since it is half the trace of \(e^{itZ}e^{-itA_p}\). If \(\phi_p=\arccos c_p\), that unitary has eigenvalues \(e^{\pm i\phi_p}\). Consequently the complete induced norm error is exactly
\[
 E_{ab}(J,t)=\max_{p=\pm1,\,\sigma=\pm1}
       2\left|\sin\frac{p\delta+\sigma\phi_p}{2}\right|.
 \tag{R6}
\]
No preferred old state is used. Passive extension preserves the operator norm. The unaffected block has \(\phi_p=0\). Changing a,b changes the affected p or the sign of d; (R5) is insensitive to the latter, while changing p conjugates/permutates the phases in the maximum. Thus the final error is independent of the endpoint-sign choice.

The unhalved channel distance is at most \(\min(2,2E_{ab})\). The note correctly does not identify this upper bound with the exact channel distance or optimize away a common vector phase.

## 3. Nominal-time nonzero error, small-J limit and the limits of the diagnostic

At \(\tau=\theta/J\), \(\delta=0\). The unaffected old sector agrees exactly and the affected one gives
\[
 E_{ab}(J,\tau)^2
 =2\left[1-\cos\tau\cos(\Omega\tau)
          -\frac{\sin\tau\sin(\Omega\tau)}{\Omega}\right],
 \quad \Omega=\sqrt{1+4J^2}.
 \tag{R7}
\]
To make this zero, \(e^{-i\tau(Z-dX)}\) would have to equal \(e^{-i\tau Z}\). Its nonzero d forces \(\sin(\Omega\tau)=0\) by the X coefficient. The Z coefficient then forces \(\sin\tau=0\), and the scalar coefficients require the two cosines to have the same sign. Thus
\[
 \tau=m\pi,\qquad \Omega\tau=n\pi,\qquad m,n>0
\]
with equal parity. But these equations would imply
\[
 n^2-m^2=\frac{4\theta^2}{\pi^2}\in(0,1),
 \tag{R8}
\]
an impossibility for integers. This proves strictly nonzero nominal-time error for every finite \(J>0\). A different common scalar cannot restore nominal equality on the entire old space, because the unaffected sector already fixes that scalar to 1.

This is not a uniform positive floor. With \(\Delta=(\Omega-1)\tau\),
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 c&=\cos\Delta+(1/\Omega-1)\sin\tau\sin(\Omega\tau),\\
 E^2&\le\Delta^2+2(1-1/\Omega)
       \le4(\theta^2+1)J^2 .
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{R9}
\]
Here \(\Omega-1=4J^2/(\Omega+1)\le2J^2\), so \(\Delta\le2\theta J\), and \(1-1/\Omega\le2J^2\). Hence the error tends to zero as \(J\to0\) while \(\tau\) grows. In the other limit, \(\tau\to0,\Omega\tau\to2\theta\), giving \(E\to2\sin\theta=8/5\); this limit may violate a fixed incident cap.

At shifted times, the unaffected sector requires \(\delta\in2\pi\mathbb Z\) for exact equality with the specified phase. The affected block additionally requires its integer recurrence conditions. The nominal identity (R8) must not be transplanted to arbitrary shifted times: it used \(\tau=\theta/J\). The source does not do that or exclude all possible later gates. Neither a large Duhamel upper bound nor this exact nominal-time calculation proves a general obstruction to embedding or approximate operation.

## 4. A uniform detuning bound that needs no endpoint preparation

For any real d, let \(\Omega_d=\sqrt{1+d^2}\), \(N_d=(Z-dX)/\Omega_d\). Direct Pauli squaring gives
\[
 \|N_d-Z\|^2=2-2/\Omega_d
 =\frac{2d^2}{\Omega_d(\Omega_d+1)}\le d^2 .
 \tag{R10}
\]
The equal-frequency exponential difference has norm
\[
 \|e^{-it(Z-dX)}-e^{-it\Omega_dZ}\|
 =|\sin(\Omega_dt)|\,\|N_d-Z\|\le|d|.
 \]
The remaining frequency difference is at most
\[
 \|e^{-it\Omega_dZ}-e^{-itZ}\|
 =2|\sin[(\Omega_d-1)t/2]|
 \le(\Omega_d-1)t\le d^2t/2
 \tag{R11}
\]
for \(t\ge0\). Thus the stated \(|d|+d^2t/2\) bound holds for every d, not merely an asymptotic perturbation expansion.

Applying this to \(d=0,\pm2J\) and then charging the commuting XX angle error gives the full four-site operator estimate
\[
 \left\|e^{-itH_4}
    -e^{-i(3Jt+\theta)}[e^{-itK}W]\otimes I_{LR}\right\|
 \le2J+2J^2t+J|t-\theta/J|.
 \tag{R12}
\]
The exact \(a,b,p\) block decomposition covers the entire sixteen-dimensional space, and the right side is uniform over all signs. Therefore (R12) holds on arbitrary superpositions of endpoint signs and arbitrary old/endpoint correlations. It is not limited to the product X eigenstates initially used to derive (R2).

## 5. All 117 compass edges in the 54-site box

The dimensions \(6,3,3\) give
\[
 |\Omega|=54,\quad N_x=5\cdot3\cdot3=45,\quad
 N_y=6\cdot2\cdot3=36,\quad N_z=6\cdot3\cdot2=36.
 \tag{R13}
\]
There are 117 compass edges and, with K once on the old edge, 118 positive blocks. The old sites and L,R are interior. The maximum graph degree is six, ordinary interior incident norm sum is \(4(J+s)\), and the old sites add three. Thus \(3+4(J+s)\le\Lambda\) is sufficient, using actual full-support norms.

Let \(S=\{L,1,2,R\}\). Each of its four sites has two y and two z neighbors, and every such neighbor lies outside S. Hence exactly eight y and eight z edges touch S. Within each direction, four are old–E edges and four are E–E edges at L/R. There is no double counting of an edge between two members of S, since S is a straight x segment.

The actual isolated environment \(E=\Omega\setminus\{1,2\}\) has 52 qubits and \(42+32+32=106\) compass blocks: removing old-incident edges removes three x, four y and four z edges. The reference environment further omits the eight transverse E–E edges at L/R, leaving \(42+28+28=98\) blocks. In particular “98” is a comparison count, not the number of actual environment blocks.

All retained E–E terms commute with \(X_L,X_R\): the terms touching those sites are x bonds only; the other terms act away from them. This is true even though different retained environment terms need not commute with each other. Thus \(H_{E,\mathrm{ref}}\) commutes with \(H_4\), since it is disjoint from the old sites and commutes with the two endpoint controls.

The exact actual split is
\[
 H_{\mathrm{actual}}
 =H_4+H_{E,\mathrm{ref}}+8sI+V,\qquad
 V=-J_y\!\sum_{8\ {\rm y\ edges}}\!YY
   -J_z\!\sum_{8\ {\rm z\ edges}}\!ZZ .
 \tag{R14}
\]
All sixteen physical positive blocks are installed. Their identity contributions are \(8sI\), and \(\|V\|\le8s\) by full-space triangle inequality. The identity coefficient of the reference side is
\[
 (2+3J)+(42J+28s)+8s=2+45J+36s,
 \tag{R15}
\]
matching K and the full actual compass inventory exactly.

Factor the commuting reference evolutions, apply (R12) before the environment unitary, and then apply full-space Duhamel to V. This gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \|e^{-itH_{\mathrm{actual}}}-T_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)\|
 &\le 2J+2J^2t+J|t-\theta/J|+8st,\\
 T_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)
 &=e^{-i[(3J+8s)t+\theta]}
        [e^{-itK}W]\otimes e^{-itH_{E,\mathrm{ref}}}.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{R16}
\]
No eigenstate preparation is needed. The reference environment is explicitly a comparison Hamiltonian. Calling its output the actual isolated-environment evolution would be incorrect; the note expressly avoids that identification.

## 6. Actual isolated-environment target and phase transfer

Among the omitted transverse edges, precisely four y and four z edges are E–E. Define their signed Pauli sum \(B_E\) as in the addendum. Then
\[
 H_{E,\mathrm{all}}=H_{E,\mathrm{ref}}+4sI_E-B_E,
 \qquad \|B_E\|\le4s .
 \tag{R17}
\]
The first Hamiltonian contains all 106 actual E-only positive blocks. It generally fails to commute with \(X_L,X_R\); the addendum neither assumes nor needs that commutation.

Duhamel compares \(e^{-it(H_{E,\mathrm{ref}}+4sI)}\) with \(e^{-itH_{E,\mathrm{all}}}\) at norm cost at most \(4st\). The exact identity
\[
 e^{-i[(3J+8s)t+\theta]}e^{-itH_{E,\mathrm{ref}}}
 =e^{-i[(3J+4s)t+\theta]}
       e^{-it(H_{E,\mathrm{ref}}+4sI)}
 \tag{R18}
\]
fixes the scalar sign and prevents double counting. Thus
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 T_{\mathrm{all}}(t)
 &=e^{-i[(3J+4s)t+\theta]}
        [e^{-itK}W]\otimes e^{-itH_{E,\mathrm{all}}},\\
 \|e^{-itH_{\mathrm{actual}}}-T_{\mathrm{all}}(t)\|
 &\le2J+2J^2t+J|t-\theta/J|+12st .
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{R19}
\]
Checking the identity coefficients again gives \(2+(3J+4s)+(42J+32s)=2+45J+36s\), as required.

\(H_{E,\mathrm{all}}\) is the actual isolated E Hamiltonian, meaning the sum of all installed E-only terms. It is not an exact generator of the reduced E evolution while old/E contacts remain active. Their effects, and the detuned exterior x fields, remain in the error budget. The addendum states this distinction correctly.

## 7. Full window, cap, all outputs and correlated inputs

For either target, let \(0<\epsilon\le1\),
\[
 J\le\frac{\epsilon}{128(\theta+1)},\qquad
 \tau=\frac{\theta}{J},\quad w=\frac{\epsilon}{32J},\qquad
 t\in[\tau-w,\tau+w],\qquad
 3+4J(1+r)\le\Lambda,\quad r=s/J.
 \tag{R20}
\]
The lower endpoint is positive because \(\theta>\pi/4>\epsilon/32\). On the entire interval, \(Jt\le\theta+\epsilon/32<\theta+1\). The first, second and angle terms obey
\[
 2J<\epsilon/64,\qquad
 2J^2t<\epsilon/64,\qquad
 J|t-\tau|\le\epsilon/32 .
 \tag{R21}
\]
For the original reference target, \(r\le\epsilon/[256(\theta+1)]\) gives \(8st<\epsilon/32\). For the actual isolated-environment target, \(r\le\epsilon/[384(\theta+1)]\) gives \(12st<\epsilon/32\). Thus the corresponding total in each case is strictly below \(3\epsilon/32<\epsilon/8\), with unhalved complete channel distance below \(3\epsilon/16<\epsilon/4\). The tighter second ratio has not been silently attributed to the unchanged original note.

The domain is nonempty for every \(\Lambda>3\): take positive r below its ceiling and \(0<J\le\min\{(\Lambda-3)/8,\epsilon/[128(\theta+1)]\}\). Since r is less than one, this implies the cap. At fixed \(\Lambda>3\) and saturated accuracy choice for sufficiently small \(\epsilon\),
\[
 J=\frac{\epsilon}{128(\theta+1)},\qquad
 \tau=\frac{128\theta(\theta+1)}{\epsilon},\qquad
 w=4(\theta+1),\qquad w/\tau=\frac{\epsilon}{32\theta}.
 \tag{R22}
\]
This verifies the stated sufficient time scaling and constant absolute half-window. Smaller chosen J lengthens both. The unit ZZ coefficient in given K fixes the normalization; shrinking J is a coefficient-ratio restriction relative to that splitting, not a relabeling of time. At fixed positive r and J the policy does not certify arbitrary accuracy, and it is not a generic independent-coefficient perturbation theorem or optimality statement.

Equations (R16) and (R19) are operator estimates on the full old/E Hilbert space. They remain unchanged after tensoring any passive identity. For every pure joint input they give the displayed phase-pinned vector bound; for every joint density operator they give a trace-norm bound at most twice the operator error. The latter also bounds the unhalved diamond distance between the joint unitary channels. All 52 E outputs are retained.

For initially correlated states, the target is the same initial state acted on by the displayed old and environment unitaries. It is generally correlated. Tracing E as an optional consequence gives the same trace-norm bound from the ideal old/passive marginal, because partial trace is invariant under the environment unitary. This does not define a universal exact reduced old channel for arbitrary correlated preparation assignments.

For \(\rho_{OF}\otimes\sigma_E\), where \(\sigma_E\) is fixed independently of the old/passive input, the comparison factors with the common \(e^{-itH_E}\sigma_Ee^{itH_E}\). The environment may be mixed and internally entangled. For unknown or varying \(\sigma_E\), the bound is uniform, but the complete comparator contains its actual evolved state. No common product output is manufactured from arbitrary initial correlations.

## 8. Additional scope check: any larger finite open box

ROOT separately asked whether the proof requires isolating exactly the 54-site box. It does not. Let \(\Omega'\) be any rectangular finite open lattice box containing that box, with the same directional coefficients, all its compass edges installed, and given K once. S and all its immediate neighbors already lie in the original box, so exactly the same sixteen transverse edges touch S in \(\Omega'\). The three four-site x bonds are unchanged.

Let its side lengths be \(n_x,n_y,n_z\), and define
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q&=n_xn_yn_z,\\
 M_x&=(n_x-1)n_yn_z,\quad
 M_y=n_x(n_y-1)n_z,\quad
 M_z=n_xn_y(n_z-1),\\
 M&=M_x+M_y+M_z .
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{R23}
\]
The actual system has Q qubits and \(M+1\) positive blocks on M distinct edges. Removing all old-incident edges removes three x, four y and four z edges, so \(H_{E,\mathrm{all}}\) has \(M-11\) blocks on \(Q-2\) environment qubits. Removing the additional four y/four z edges at L/R gives \(M-19\) reference-environment blocks. For the displayed box these specialize to \(54,118,106,98\), respectively.

Every extra environment term commutes with \(X_L,X_R\): after those same local transverse omissions, only x terms can touch L/R. Therefore the factorization used in (R16) remains exact. The V and \(B_E\) sums still have sixteen and eight terms, respectively, with the same norm bounds \(8s\) and \(4s\). No bound on the extensive norm of \(H_E\) is used. The local incident cap is unchanged because each lattice site has at most two neighbors per axis, and the old sites remain interior.

For an independent scalar check, the reference and actual environment identity coefficients are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_{E,\mathrm{ref}}&=(M_x-3)J+(M_y-8)J_y+(M_z-8)J_z,\\
 C_{E,\mathrm{all}}&=(M_x-3)J+(M_y-4)J_y+(M_z-4)J_z.
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{R24}
\]
Adding K and the respective \(3J+8s\) or \(3J+4s\) prefactor recovers the actual \(2+M_xJ+M_yJ_y+M_zJ_z\) in both cases. Thus (R16)–(R22), including the actual isolated-environment target, hold uniformly in the size of this finite-volume family.

This establishes that the accuracy estimate does not demand a physical cut exactly at the original 54-site boundary. It still specifies a finite system with all its internal bonds and its actual finite environment output. Q and M must be charged for the larger device; it is false to retain the counts 54 and 118 for those volumes. An unknown finite volume can satisfy the uniform bound, but its comparator still uses that volume's actual \(H_{E,\mathrm{all}}\).

The derivation constructs no infinite-volume global Hamiltonian, global phase, complete state norm, unitary or dynamics limit. In particular its volume-dependent identity scalars must not be treated as a finite infinite-system scalar. A separate local-observable or algebraic limiting theorem would be another task. No such theorem is needed for this finite-volume-uniform conclusion.
