# Uniform dual-flux route: residual check

Disposition: promising route, but DO NOT promote the existing ten-layer pole or its curvature to polynomial grids from generic stability alone. The encoding is locally plausible at each fixed order; an excitation-band theorem and a microscopic low-energy transfer still require explicit work. There is a potentially easier finite-order window 1<p<2 described below. This is not a no-go theorem.

## Dual encoding and constraints

In the fixed positive row/column plane-character sector, the plaquette syndrome labels a unique normalized orbit state. P_p becomes a dual Z_p. A physical adjacent-column X pair toggles its four syndrome corners and becomes the corresponding product of four dual X operators with unit orbit amplitude. These are encoding qubits, not new physical degrees of freedom.

The physical encoded space is not the full dual tensor product. It satisfies even flux parity on each dual row and each dual column. In addition, positive physical longitudinal-plane Z parity selects even enclosed column area. For a four-corner rectangle this excludes odd a*b, notably the (1,1) rectangle. This extra constraint must be retained. On an even torus it can be written as an additional dual Z parity with weight x*y modulo two (after fixing coordinates). Row/column constraints alone do not impose it.

For a bounded-support native plane-invariant diagonal monomial, expressing it through plaquette Z products may fill its bounding rectangle; the resulting support remains bounded at each fixed perturbative order. Off-diagonal column flips act by local syndrome differences. Positive kernel characters avoid orbit phases. This supports a local dual representation of each fixed-order effective coefficient, but does not prove a uniformly summable all-order interaction. A nonlocal inverse map on arbitrary operators must not be mistaken for locality of the invariant operator algebra.

One can extend a finite-range encoded Hamiltonian to the unconstrained dual tensor product, where the all-plus Z vacuum is a product state, provided every term commutes with the constraint parities and the extension is stated. A unique extended-system ground continued from that vacuum remains in the physical sector. This is useful for ground stability. It does not make unphysical one/two-flux excitations permissible in the physical excitation spectrum.

## What primary theorems actually provide

Yarotsky's [Quasi-particles in weak perturbations of non-interacting quantum lattice systems](https://arxiv.org/html/math-ph/0411042), Theorems 1–4, assumes nondegenerate gapped on-site vacua and small bounded finite-range interactions. Its ground/spectral estimates are uniform in volume. Its analytic one-particle dispersion theorem additionally assumes an isolated nondegenerate on-site excitation whose energy is not a sum of other positive on-site energies. Those latter assumptions do not describe this four-flux cluster. The constrained sector is not itself the on-site tensor-product system in that theorem. Extending to dual qubits helps the ground theorem but does not repair this excited-band mismatch.

[Zheng et al., Linked Cluster Series Expansions for Two-Particle Bound States](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0010354) supplies multiparticle linked-cluster expansion methodology; its abstract explicitly distinguishes orthogonal block reduction and bound/continuum spectra. It is not, merely by citation, a uniform convergence/error theorem for this constrained native four-flux problem.

## Ground stability is not the desired pole theorem

After dividing by lambda^4 the dual background has an order-one single-flux field, diagonal perturbations starting at lambda^2 and transverse four-corner flips at lambda^(L-4). This is a strong-field structure for ground control. But the unperturbed physical four-flux eigenspace contains rectangles of all separations. Its degeneracy is not a single translated on-site excitation. The needed hierarchy resolves flux count, thin versus nonthin states at relative order lambda^2, and shortest-thin binding at relative order lambda^4. The orientation pair has an even smaller splitting at physical order lambda^(2L-6).

A generic spectral stability interval around the four-flux energy has width O(lambda^2) in rescaled units. That is too coarse to isolate a lambda^4 binding depth within the four-flux cluster, and much too coarse for the dispersion or orientation splitting. One must construct a connected effective excitation operator, with ground dressing/subtraction, and prove bounds relative to each successive gap. Disconnected vacuum corrections must cancel before counting excitation norms; an extensive global energy correction cannot be substituted for a finite-excitation bound.

A plausible target is a two-orientation bound-cluster subspace with exponentially summable translation kernels. Prove the full four-flux complement gap, control couplings to higher allowed flux counts, and obtain weighted kernel estimates. This would permit a momentum-fibre Schur analysis. Merely grouping overlapping shortest rectangles into fictitious independent sites does not satisfy a product-ground-state theorem: the clusters overlap and are constrained.

## Derivatives and polynomial momentum resolution

For R=lambda^(-p), p>1, the smallest nonzero momentum is O(lambda^p), which does resolve q<<lambda. The expected energy difference at that momentum is order Jlambda^(2L-8+2p). A uniform o(lambda^8) pole-energy error does not resolve its sign. Either prove second-moment/exponential bounds on the effective hopping kernels (or a suitable complex-momentum analytic strip), or bound finite-volume energy differences to o(Jlambda^(2L-8+2p)). Error-controlled energies alone at a much coarser scale do not establish curvature.

The two orientations should be treated jointly before diagonalization. At small nonzero momentum the C4-even/odd labels are not separate fixed-momentum sectors, and their gap is only order lambda^(2L-6). The q<<lambda condition makes the anisotropic diagonal difference smaller than this gap, but an error estimate must also resolve that small gap. A fixed complex-momentum strip for the individual symmetric eigenvalue cannot simply be assumed when its nearest partner approaches it as lambda tends to zero. A matrix-valued expansion followed by a quantitatively controlled small-q diagonalization is the natural route.

## Microscopic elimination is a separate obstacle

The original unperturbed Hamiltonian has an extensive aligned-column degeneracy. It is not a product system with a nondegenerate on-site ground, even if each whole column is regarded as one site. Therefore the cited nondegenerate strong-field theorem cannot be applied directly to the original weak transverse perturbation to produce a uniform exact N0 elimination.

The existing local finite-order normal form is useful and remains uniform in local interaction norm at fixed L and fixed order. Its global remainder is O(JR^2 lambda^(n+1)). For any prescribed finite polynomial growth exponent p, increasing the fixed order makes that error as small a power of lambda as needed. This does not prove convergence of the infinite SW series or an exact volume-uniform block diagonalization. A dual strong-field theorem for a truncated N0 Hamiltonian must still be connected to the microscopic low-energy spectrum using these remainder and wall-sector estimates.

For 1<p<2, the leading N0 bandwidth O(Jlambda^4 R^2) tends to zero, so the whole N0 band can plausibly remain separated from wall sectors. The wall-relative bound, with the vacuum scalar subtracted, can establish that separation without using ||lambda V||. This is a promising restricted polynomial-volume route: take a sufficiently high fixed-order local normal form, prove its dual low-energy excitation estimates, and then use the small global remainder. It avoids demanding a convergent microscopic SW transformation.

For p>=2, the leading full N0 bandwidth no longer tends to zero; its high-energy states can overlap wall-sector energies. An exact spectral isolation of the entire N0 block cannot be justified by the earlier order-one band-gap argument. A low-energy window or excitation-relative method is then required. This does not imply that the low-energy pole disappears.

The original-readout source also needs a local/connected dressing estimate through its first nonzero order lambda^L. The raw norm of C grows like R. Ground stability alone neither supplies the pole's nonzero residue nor preserves its leading coefficient after projection.

## Concrete remaining obligations

1. Specify the invariant local dual algebra and all physical parity constraints, with uniform interaction-norm bounds for every retained microscopic coefficient.
2. Establish uniform ground dressing/subtraction for the dual effective Hamiltonian, and isolate the physical four-flux excitation sector with connected norm bounds.
3. Perform the thin/shortest-thin reductions with errors small relative to lambda^6 and lambda^8, and track the orientation matrix through order lambda^(2L-6).
4. Prove spatially weighted bounds sufficient for the leading momentum dependence, or resolve actual grid energy differences at the much smaller scale above.
5. Transfer the low-energy cluster and centered source back to the microscopic model using a sufficiently high fixed-order remainder and an actual wall-sector separation estimate. Preserve the selected-ground-sector premise unless uniform sector selection is separately proved.

Until these are supplied, the logarithmic-box zero-momentum theorem and coefficient-level positive curvature remain the established claims. The dual construction suggests a viable new proof strategy; it is not itself that proof.

## Addendum: finite-order p close to one may suffice

The proposed alternative is credible and substantially narrower than a convergent all-orders theorem. For L=10 and p=1.05, the desired grid energy difference is lambda^(12+2p)=lambda^14.1. A crude third-order flux-elimination bound using ||W10||=O(lambda^10 R^2) and flux gap lambda^4 is lambda^22 R^6=lambda^15.7, which is sufficiently small. This comparison works for 1<p<5/4. Native staggered parity makes the N0 expansion even: the next off-diagonal correction is order twelve, not eleven. A crude mixed second-order volume term lambda^18 R^2 is lambda^15.9 at p=1.05 and is also small. Retaining a sufficiently high fixed native order can make its global remainder smaller than lambda^(12+2p); for a generic remainder lambda^(n+1)R^2 require n+1>12+4p.

However, second-order winding cancellation must be an operator statement on the excitation sector, not only a subtraction of vacuum energy. Far pair creation followed by annihilation produces vacuum-mediated rank-one hopping between distant four-flux configurations; complementary eight-flux intermediate paths must cancel that disconnected contribution as well. With a finite-range diagonal reference and disjoint supports, additivity of the diagonal energies supplies the matching denominators, suggesting exact cancellation away from the existing defect. An explicit canonical effective-operator calculation must retain both intermediate channels and their normalization terms. Near supports leave connected finite-degree matrix elements.

The same global norm estimate cannot be used inside the four-flux cluster: there the thin/nonthin and endpoint denominators are lambda^6 and lambda^8. Use the bounded-degree projected winding graph and connected corrections. Unknown order-twelve and higher local winding coefficients can generate order-fourteen endpoint shifts via these smaller denominators. Those shifts can be common and harmless to dispersion, but they are not smaller than lambda^14.1 as absolute errors. One must prove they are momentum independent at the relevant order, or bound their momentum differences by q^2 times an appropriate local coefficient norm. Likewise the two-orientation matrix must retain the leading order-fourteen mixing before extracting the lower branch on the grid.

Thus a finite-order construction may avoid a general infinite cluster theorem: exact disconnected cancellation at second winding order, bounded-degree reduced operators, local displacement-weighted estimates for every retained contribution, and a high-order microscopic remainder could be enough. The existing argument is not yet complete until these operator cancellations and internal-cluster estimates are written. No obstruction to pursuing this restricted 1<p<5/4 route was found.

## Addendum: local Q-conserving normal form resolves the disconnected-cancellation obstacle

The stronger proposed construction is sound at its core. In the N0 encoded algebra set Q=sum_p B_p, B_p=(1-P_p)/2. The leading rescaled Hamiltonian is (L/64)Q plus scalar. Since the P_p commute and have integer-valued total defect number, conjugation by exp(itQ) charge-decomposes each local term; projection to charge zero and inversion of nonzero ad_Q eigenvalues use only this finite local charge spectrum. Conjugation enlarges physical support only by the plaquettes meeting it. Iterated commutators therefore remain connected and of bounded range at any chosen fixed order.

For each individually Q-preserving, plane-equivariant coefficient t, choose an enlarged neighborhood containing its support, incident plaquettes and the finite paths needed for its invariant diagonal representation. If that neighborhood has no bad plaquettes, t cannot create a defect there while preserving Q, nor remove one outside its support. Any off-diagonal action leaving the syndrome unchanged would be a flat-kernel row/column flip. There is no nontrivial such bounded-support flip once R exceeds the fixed support range. The diagonal action is independent of the local flat background by plane equivariance. Thus, after subtracting its scalar flat value,

    t=Pi_local t Pi_local,
    Pi_local <=sum_(p in enlarged neighborhood) B_p.

The assertion must be made for these grouped local invariant coefficients, not deduced from scalarity of an arbitrary summed decomposition. Summing bounded-overlap neighborhoods gives the quadratic-form bound C Q, with constants independent of R at each fixed order. In particular the Q=4 restriction has bounded norm independent of volume. This also follows directly in a valid local dual-qubit extension, where a number-preserving local term maps its local vacuum to a scalar multiple of itself.

This route cancels disconnected vacuum-mediated hopping through its connected commutator algebra, including off-diagonal disconnected pieces; an explicit second-order global Riesz cancellation is no longer necessary. It does not require an all-orders convergent expansion. Its global finite-order tail remains volume times a controllable higher power of lambda.

The next two reductions still need to be constructed. On Q=4, use the order-six thin/nonthin diagonal gap and then the order-eight endpoint gap. All retained Q4 coefficient norms are now uniform, so fixed-order reductions can be carried out with uniform constants. Preserve the two-orientation endpoint matrix until the final small-q diagonalization. For momentum control, record finite-range or exponentially weighted translation bounds on its coefficients; these should follow from bounded numbers of local moves at each retained order, but are an additional estimate to write, not a consequence of operator norm alone. Common endpoint energy shifts may be retained without computing them, provided their translation independence is proved. Then coefficient errors in q-differences can be bounded by q^2 times weighted norms rather than by their absolute energies.

The microscopic bridge for 1<p<2 also has a viable finite-order form. At fixed L>=10, original N-conserving terms through order three have only a scalar wall-free restriction. The local wall-relative argument bounds their nonconstant part by C lambda N. Retained order-four and higher terms, including complete-column flips at order L, have total norm O(lambda^4 R^2) after subtracting lower scalars. This tends to zero in that range of p and separates the whole low N0 block from wall sectors. A high enough fixed-order original SW remainder can be made smaller than the desired momentum-energy difference. Complete-column flips must be retained in the N0 block; they do not invalidate this estimate, but forbid claiming that its higher-order restriction is diagonal.

Disposition after this refinement: the local-Q lemma is supported and removes a major identified obstacle. A finite-order polynomial-grid proof now looks viable without generic quasiparticle stability or an infinite linked-cluster convergence theorem. Promotion still awaits the explicit two reduced-block constructions, weighted translation estimates, source transformation, and quantitative microscopic remainder choice. This is a constructive proof route, not yet a completed actual-dispersion theorem.
