## Classification of fourth-order returns

Each transverse operation flips two physical spins at one longitudinal height. A return to the aligned manifold either restores every spin or flips complete columns. At L4 a changed column requires all four operations to touch it, one at each height. At each height transverse flip parity is even; therefore another complete column must change. With only four operations there is no room for additional flips. The second changed column must be the same neighbor at every height, so this process is exactly pair-column tunneling on one transverse edge. No single-column, diagonal-neighbor, four-column, or phase-dependent different off-diagonal operator can appear at this order.

If every physical spin returns, the four transverse edges form an even-degree graph at every height. Repeated edges give scalar contributions. A connected four-distinct-edge return is an elementary square at one height: each of its degree-two columns forces its two incident operations to the same height. Transverse periods greater than4 exclude noncontractible four-edge loops. Mixed-height repeated-edge paths can contribute constants but their original aligned-spin Y factors cancel in pairs. This exhausts the nonconstant possibilities at order4.

PVP=0 and at second order no entire L4 column can flip. The only second-order returns repeat the same physical edge; their K2 and metric N2 are scalar in aligned-column coordinates. Thus the canonical folded term -{N2,K2}/2 contributes only a constant, leaving both plaquette and pair-tunneling coefficients unchanged.

For a Y-oriented pair-column tunnel, each -YY vertex has current-spin coefficient s_a*s_b. Distinct heights have untouched spins before their single operation, and the four factors multiply to (s_a*s_b)^4=1. The three negative resolvents therefore give the same negative hopping sign as the four -XX vertices. Both orientations yield -t Xbar_a Xbar_b at even L4, without an extra Zbar-dependent phase.
