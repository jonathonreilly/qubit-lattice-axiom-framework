## Why the long-row support is exhausted

In the relative even-parity E8 sector, finite configurations are two columns with one common interval. For initial interval length>=5, changing the column pair requires changing at least five sites on a column removed from that pair. Four transverse operations cannot do that. Therefore every fourth-order E8 output retains the same column pair.

For axial distance two, each longitudinal layer whose occupation changes requires at least two transverse edges. Four operations can therefore change at most two layers. A final common interval permits only one-end displacements by one or two layers, or one-layer moves at both ends, apart from the diagonal. No longer displacement or additional long-row target is possible at this order.

For simultaneous moves at separated ends, each two-operation process has disjoint longitudinal support and independent intermediate energy changes. The direct four-step contribution is the product-type disconnected term canceled by the canonical normalization/folded subtraction. Root's displayed direct -1/8 plus folded+1/8 is consistent with this general factorization; it is not a physical simultaneous-end hopping. The same cancellation applies to translations and double extensions/contractions once endpoint neighborhoods are disjoint.

For a one-layer endpoint move, two operations are required on that layer; the remaining two either form a same-layer detour or a return loop. Only loops on a neighboring layer can be connected through H0. For a two-layer move all four operations are exhausted on those layers. Thus these endpoint coefficients depend only on a bounded local wall motif, fully contained in the independent single-wall patch. The other endpoint cannot modify them for ell>=5 with an equally adequate exterior/remote arc. Diagonal connected returns likewise occupy at most two active longitudinal layers (or a same-layer plaquette); remote disconnected pieces cancel.
