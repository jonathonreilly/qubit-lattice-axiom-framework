# Focused check: checkerboard parity and missing odd coefficients

## Exact microscopic symmetry and canonical inheritance

Conjugation by Z on every transverse checkerboard site with x+y odd reverses each X-X or Y-Y transverse bond and preserves every longitudinal Z-Z bond. Hence U H(lambda) U=H(-lambda). This is exact on open transverse grids, infinite bipartite grids as a local-algebra automorphism, or periodic grids with both transverse periods even. It is not valid across an odd-period wrap bond. In infinite volume the relative sign of a finite excitation is well defined without treating an infinite product as a bounded Hilbert-space operator.

Since U preserves H0 and each H0 energy projector, an equivariant canonical SW construction inherits U K_k U=(-1)^k K_k. This follows recursively when the averaging/inverse-Liouvillian maps and normalization convention commute with U. It should be stated as a property of that specified canonical construction, rather than of arbitrary parameter-dependent basis choices.

Above the uniform reference, a two-column matching interval has relative U sign (-1)^[ell*(chi(r1)+chi(r2))]. Thus even ell always has U=+; odd ell has U=+ for same-checkerboard-color pairs and U=- for opposite-color pairs.

## Which slow states are positive?

The active projected slow graph previously used is entirely U=+:

* every ell=2 host configuration, regardless of transverse separation;
* every long axial-distance-two interval, since its columns have the same checkerboard color.

Consequently every odd canonical coefficient has zero matrix elements between any two states of this active graph. In particular its direct K3 block vanishes exactly.

However, the complete slow complement is not entirely U=+. Non-nearest, opposite-color pairs with odd ell>=3 have U=- and are frozen at K1. They are legitimate finite E8 configurations. Diagonal pairs are same-color and positive, but farther pairs include both signs. Therefore an unqualified assertion that all of Pslow has U=+ is false if Pslow denotes the full complement of the fast first-order channel.

At K2, the negative slow states have no endpoint motion: their transverse separation has odd Manhattan parity and is non-nearest, so no length-two path connects the columns. Since ell>=3 also prevents changing the column pair in two operations, their K2 block is exactly -4 times the identity in the infinite uniform-reference coefficient calculation. K2 cannot connect them to the positive graph by symmetry. Positive frozen states may also have the same -4 coefficient; their presence does not convert the negative block into the active [-8,0] host continuum.

## Consequence near the axial-arm bottom

For a targeted active mode near slow coefficient energy -6, the negative frozen K2 block at -4 is separated by two coefficient units. The active host continuum containing -4 does not by itself remove that separation: the relevant inverse for eliminating the negative block is its own resolvent, not the active-block spectrum. Thus there is no resonant K3 coupling to these frozen negative states at energy -6 at this coefficient order.

Nevertheless K3 can couple the positive graph to the negative block. In the slow-scaled Hamiltonian this is an off-diagonal order-lambda term. If its operator norm and the separation are uniformly controlled, its virtual second-order effect is order lambda^2 in slow units, i.e. order Jz lambda^4 physically. This is the same precision as the proposed resonance width, so symmetry removes a direct Jz lambda^3 positive-block term but does not eliminate the need to compute its induced Jz lambda^4 contribution. The isolated negative block is off shell here, so its leading eliminated operator is Hermitian on real energies; later resonance effects still require the coupled calculation.

This gap argument remains conditional on uniform bounds over the growing-interval sector. Uncontrolled higher terms or length-dependent potentials must not be presumed unable to close it. The exact second-order classification supplies a useful starting separation, not a full-native spectral bound.

## Remaining fast-channel issue

K3 may also connect positive slow states to negative-parity components of the fast block. That fast block contains both U signs, with K1 exchanging them, and its continuum remains ungapped. Such contributions are not covered by the frozen-negative gap argument. Interference between native K2 and K3 fast vertices can occur at physical order Jz lambda^4, depending on the continued fast resolvent and the actual matrix elements. No conclusion that all dangerous third-order effects vanish follows from the direct positive-block selection rule alone.

The next useful native calculation is therefore the K3 off-diagonal action from the positive active graph into (i) negative frozen states and (ii) negative fast states, together with the direct K4 positive block. This narrows the work substantially without assuming either state selection or an autonomous scalar.
