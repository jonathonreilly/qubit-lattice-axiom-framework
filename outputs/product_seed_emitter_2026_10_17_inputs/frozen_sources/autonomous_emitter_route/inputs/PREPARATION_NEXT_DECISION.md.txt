# Incoming-packet preparation: route decisions, not a theorem

This is root's unverified planning note. No preparation result, new control
permission, native identification or publication acceptance follows from it.
The next prerequisite is the concrete two-body interval bridge and its check.

The local source inventory PREPARATION_SOURCE_SEARCH.json pins five existing
notes. Root inspected title/keyword hits and the installed-bus and local
conserving preparation wrappers, not their complete underlying arguments.
No new mathematical dependency is accepted from that partial search. The
existing results already cover driven prefix-sine preparation, matching swaps
into retained memory, positive complementary controls and fixed-slew variants
with changed memory comparators. A generic repeat of those devices would not
constitute a new physical supplier.

The specific missing input here is a complex one-head clock packet with a
certified finite-support approximation, independent of arbitrary old/reference
input, while the actual K and source ladder remain installed. Its common
scalar and total old aging must include preparation time. Native vacuum,
phase reference, controlled bonds and external preparation timing are separate
permissions; calling the subsequent runtime autonomous does not derive them.

Two possible attacks need comparison before choosing one:

1. Prepare far from the collision region and bound old/source disturbance by
   actual finite propagation during preparation. Sequential local preparation
   time grows with support, so distance, packet width and speed must be solved
   together. A distant seed alone does not prove negligible total backaction.
2. Temporarily add explicit positive complementary local controls, retain all
   installed terms, prepare via nearest-neighbor one-head rotations and onsite
   phases, and price the complete release. Complementary controls and their
   negative nonscalar pieces are added physical permissions. Their scalar
   reserves and incident loads must be counted. Existing bus arguments already
   contain this mechanism; only a checked new packet/release application could
   be useful. A fast release can require growing slew; fixed slew needs an
   actual small-contact or compensated evolution estimate.

For a complex finite vector, a sequence of one-head rotations can in principle
be parameterized from tail weights, but branch phases, zero/small amplitudes,
finite precision, pulse reserves and off-one-head action must be derived and
checked. Merely citing finite controllability supplies neither a complete
preparation isometry nor its physical cost. A finite rational approximation
may avoid undecidable exact-zero choices; an error budget is still required.

The generalized packet proof already prices truncation and separates actual
preparation error eta_a. Its sufficient time can be extremely conservative.
A higher-smoothness packet/tail estimate might reduce that price, but would
need higher matching and closed-tail derivative bounds, not just a prettier
finite simulation. No such improvement is established here.
