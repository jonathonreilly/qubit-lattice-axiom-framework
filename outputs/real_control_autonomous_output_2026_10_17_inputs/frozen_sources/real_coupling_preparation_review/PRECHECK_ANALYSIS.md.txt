## 6. Work, extreme scales and surviving obligations

On a finite apparatus with actual unitary state rho(t), differentiation
of Tr[(HR+A(t))rho(t)] leaves Tr[A'(t)rho(t)] because the commutator
term vanishes. A(0)=A(5)=0 gives the stated endpoint work identity.
During the onset ramp A'=u'R is PSD, so its work is nonnegative and at
most Aiso; the descending ramp gives a nonpositive contribution of
magnitude at most Aiso. On the ideal isolated core, each separate pulse
has conserved generator expectation, so its rise and fall have zero
net work. This is not asserted of the actual leaked component.

The total variation of a nonnegative unimodal pulse w is 2 max w.
Full pair-plus-spectator generator norm is at most three (in fact three),
and the last onsite norm is one. Therefore

    |Wsetup|<=2Aiso+6w1,max+6w2,max+2w3,max
            =2Aiso+165pi/16<33.

The estimate is uniform in M and counts all full-space control norms.
It is a bound on modeled Hamiltonian work, not fabrication/heat/controller
reset cost. At kappa=10^-8, T=10^16, M=5*10^17 and 2M+6=10^18+6,
so release-to-onset and release-to-window-end become total times
5+10^16 and 5+2*10^16. These are Hamiltonian time units, not seconds.
No all-future finite-device or optimal-resource statement follows.

Countercases explicitly checked in this derivation are omission of the pin,
reversal of the second exchange, use of the old single-rail scalar,
