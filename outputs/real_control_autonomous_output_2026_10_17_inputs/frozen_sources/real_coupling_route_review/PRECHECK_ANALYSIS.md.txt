## 1. Exact realification and local implementation

Use the physical internal computational basis. Hplus=A+iD with A^T=A and
D^T=-D real. For Jf=[[0,-1],[1,0]], direct multiplication gives
Jf(1,-i)^T=i(1,-i)^T and Jf(1,i)^T=-i(1,i)^T. Therefore
HRtilde=A tensor I+D tensor Jf is real symmetric and its two complex-linear
restrictions are A+iD and A-iD. The one-head position space, doubled into
rails, is unitarily position tensor C^2. There is no doubling or conjugation
of the old/source input space and no anti-linear operation on arbitrary old
or passive-reference amplitudes.

The clock-emitter matrix element at L+2 is -kappa(c+i s). Its real part is
-kappa c on each rail. Its D entry is -kappa s, so the clock-row/emitter-column
2-by-2 rail block is -kappa c I-kappa s Jf:

    [[-kappa c, +kappa s],[-kappa s,-kappa c]].

This independently fixes both opposite cross signs in the route. At L the
block is kappa I. Symmetric reverse edges give all six real hoppings. Flipping
only one cross sign destroys this block and the claimed conjugate-sector
intertwining; the sign is substantive, not a gauge convention left unfixed.
The common internal operator B acts as B tensor I on the rail factor. It must
be included once. Duplicating B would change both branches and old aging.

For an arbitrary real hopping h, the full-qubit cell Q has diagonal entries
0, |h|, |h|, 2|h| in the 00,01,10,11 basis, with the single off-diagonal
exchange h between 01 and 10. Its spectrum is 0,0,2|h|,2|h|. This proves
positivity on the entire two-qubit Hilbert space, not just the head sector.
The incident absolute hopping sum is kappa d at each emitter, kappa at each
c_L, and kappa(c+s) at each c_(L+2), d=1+c+s. Adding the route's onsite and
vacancy terms cancels every attachment occupation coefficient, leaves E0 at
each emitter, and leaves exactly 2kappa d I. This is a full-space identity.
Since c,s>0 and d<3, E0>3*10^-8 and kappa<=10^-8 make the emitter onsite
coefficients positive. These center inequalities are particularly slack:
q0 lies between 4 and 5, cos k0=(q0^2-1)/(q0^2+1), and
E0=4J/(q0^2+1)>4*.03/26, which already exceeds 3*10^-8.
Also 0<phi=4 arctan(1/q0)<pi/2, so c,s>0 and c+s<=sqrt(2)<2.

Each rail's B-contact vacancy term contributes nu I. Both rails therefore
add 2nu I, not nu I. In the invariant one-head sector the physical operator
is exactly HRtilde+cR I, cR=2nu+2kappa(1+c+s). Relative to the old physical
branch scalar nu+2kappa this is the additional scalar nu+2kappa(c+s).
This scalar affects actual time-dependent output phase and seed mean energy;
it cannot be discarded as a harmless unrecorded constant.

The remaining cells are positive: K=2I+Z1Z2 has eigenvalues 1,3; the source
exchange is a cell of the preceding type; all positive occupation terms and
g n_source(I+X_old), lambda n_clock(I+X_source) are products of commuting
positive factors on their displayed one or two sites. Head number is
conserved by all cells. The one-head realification is not a many-head
realification theorem. Infinite bounded-degree clock hopping has bounded
restriction in each finite head-number sector; the finite source/contact
terms and scalar reserves are bounded. Their direct sum gives the stated
finite-excitation selfadjoint representation. No infinite collection of
vacancy constants is being summed.

The displayed cubic embedding can be checked without a finite graph test.
Each piecewise rail segment is an axis path, and the joining pairs are at
distance one. For the six emitter bonds: e0=(8,2,1) neighbors c12,0=(8,1,1),
c14,0=(9,2,1), c14,1=(8,3,1); e1=(9,3,1) neighbors c12,1=(9,3,0),
c14,1 and c14,0. A and B each neighbor their two rail contacts at z=+/-1.
The old/source square edges are all unit edges. The two infinite tails lie
on x>=10,y=2,z=1 and x=8,y>=4,z=1, respectively, and are disjoint from
each other and the finite core. The intermediate axis segments have distinct
coordinates as well. Incidental geometric neighbors must remain uninstalled,
as the route expressly assumes. This is a permitted selected-edge device,
not a theorem for a uniform all-neighbor interaction.

The load table follows from actual positive-cell norms. Old added loads are
2gA and 2gB, and the existing old edge contributes norm 3 separately. Relative
to the inherited source loads, A gains 2lambdaA and B gains 2lambdaB because
the boundary contacts are duplicated. At c0, the sum is 3J+2lambdaA+mu0;
at c1 it is 4J+2lambdaB+nu. At c12 and c14 the increments are 3kappa and
3kappa(c+s). Each emitter has 2kappa d+(E0-kappa d)=E0+kappa d. A finite
far end has one bond of norm 2J plus its compensating onsite J. These match
all displayed decimal sums and formulas. There is no all-site cap two.
The counts 2M+6 qubits, one-head dimension 16(2M+2), and full dimension
2^(2M+6) are correct. A matching block from each conjugate branch is reused;
the physical model is not certified by a purported new small matrix inverse.

At the supplied seed, B iota=iota K and only e0 is occupied. Its three
outgoing amplitudes have squared norm kappa^2(1+c^2+s^2)=2kappa^2 and
are in orthogonal head positions. This gives both compressed moment
identities (12), including cR, without commuting K through the interaction.

## 2. Seed sectors and the reverse-time packet

The literal e0 seed is (e xi_plus+e xi_minus)/sqrt(2). Orthogonality of the
flag eigenvectors fixes both weights at one half; the Hamiltonian commutes
with Jf and cannot change them. For a real rail vector the two projections
have equal norm by complex conjugation. Such a real superposition generally
is not a product state of the two emitter qubits. The successful-sector seed
(10-i01)/sqrt(2) is an entangled one-head state with an extra phase resource.

The required family premise is an exact isometric packet Gamma_0 with
||E-Gamma_0||<.003, exact intertwining at arbitrary positive and negative
times, f in H1_0(I), ||f||2=1, kappa^2||f'||2<.01, and
|R_j'|<1000/kappa^2. The full localized operator packet has bound
3/(sqrt(2pi)kappa^2 v|t|). Family equations (49)-(50) explicitly retain
two potentially aligned columns in each parity and orthogonal parity blocks;
the 3 already includes the necessary sqrt(2). No new per-column orthogonality
may be assigned to localized source/emitter coordinates.

Starting from the backwards intertwining identity,
exp(+itHplus)Gamma_0=Gamma_-t exp(+itDb), complex conjugation in a real
Bell/input and physical internal basis gives

    exp(-itHminus) conjugate(Gamma_0)
        = conjugate(Gamma_-t) exp(-itDb).

Because E is real, the norm distance from E to conjugate(Gamma_0) is exactly
the original .003 seed bound. Matrix conjugation here still defines a
complex-linear isometry on all input coefficients. It is not conjugation
of the state being processed. In particular arbitrary passive references
are covered by the same operator norm.

Writing out the two plane waves after this conjugation gives

    [conjugate(f)e^(+ikn)+conjugate(f R_j)e^(-ikn)]e^(-itE).

The first wave propagates to positive n and is common to all old channels.
The second propagates toward negative n and becomes a small unwanted
restriction on n>=0. This verifies the signs independently: the old reflected
amplitude appears on the incoming, discarded-in-the-bound wave, not on the
leading reverse outgoing wave. The latter carries the identity, not W*,
W or an input-conjugating map.

For the unwanted second term, |R_j|=1 and ||f||2=1 give derivative norm
less than (1000+.01)/kappa^2<1001/kappa^2. Its open vacuum columns are
orthogonal, so their operator bound is the maximum column error. For the
first term the outgoing negative-tail estimate uses .01/kappa^2. The
positive-real normalization of its retained half-line part costs
1-sqrt(1-q^2)<=q, not an unjustified whole-line normalization identity.
The endpoint trace remains zero after conjugation/multiplication. Its tail
bound at onset is .01/25000+2.5*10^-6=2.9*10^-6<1/2, so beta_minus exists.

The localized integration-by-parts proof applies at -t and under
conjugation because its absolute value/Hilbert norms and |t| denominator
are unchanged. It retains all seven complementary emitter coordinates,
all closed tails and both old-energy columns in each parity. The .003 seed
distance also retains any point-spectrum component. No completeness or
absence-of-bound-states assumption is newly introduced.

At T=10^6/kappa^2, v=1/40 gives (kappa^2 vT)^-1=1/25000. The complete
reverse error is bounded by

    .003+(1001+.01+1.5)/25000+5*10^-6
      =.0431054=215527/5000000<.044.

All variable terms decrease as t grows; the spread allowance remains
bounded by the stated constant. This proves equation (18) for every t>=T,
uniformly in the exact kappa family. It also explains why merely conjugating
the late forward result at the same sign of time would have given a wrong
answer: the backward identity is essential.

## 3. Complete-output separation

Let a=zeta xi_plus beta_plus tensor Omega and b=xi_minus beta_minus tensor
Omega. They are unit and orthogonal regardless of the two actual profiles.
The branch errors belong to the corresponding orthogonal flag subspaces,
so the actual complete output, after removing only e^(-it cR) and the literal
old e^(-itK), is within sqrt[(.004^2+.044^2)/2]=sqrt(61)/250 of

    Vx=(a tensor W x+b tensor x)/sqrt(2).

This root-sum-square uses flag orthogonality, not assumed orthogonality of
closed source columns. A less sharp triangle error would still be small,
but the displayed sharper expression is justified. The seed approximation
errors, including point spectrum, stay inside their flag sectors.

After applying W* on the old output, take the two real P=X1X2 parity
eigenvectors (01+/-10)/sqrt(2). They both have K energy 1, and W eigenvalues
omega_plus=(-7+24i)/25 and omega_minus=1. The corresponding ancillary states
are (a+conjugate(omega_plus)b)/sqrt(2) and (a+b)/sqrt(2). Their distance is
|omega_plus-1|/sqrt(2)=8/(5sqrt(2)). For every common eta (even without unit
normalization, or outside their span), the triangle inequality forces one
error to be at least half this distance. Consequently the requested operator
distance is at least 4/(5sqrt(2))-sqrt(61)/250.

The exact rational margins are sound: (4/(5sqrt(2)))^2=8/25 is greater
than (9/16)^2; sqrt(61)<8. Their difference exceeds
9/16-8/250=1061/2000=.5305>.53. A common phase, old-independent emitted or
returned waveform, or additional source/emitter support in eta cannot cure
this bound. Testing only one gate eigenvector would miss it, because that
one vector has its own usable ancillary state. Allowing a different eta for
each old input, tracing the flag, measuring/postselecting it, or supplying
the chiral seed changes the contract. The bound is an operator-isometry
statement and persists when tensoring an arbitrary passive reference. It
does not say every particular input has large reduced-old-state error.

## 4. Finite clocks and exact scope of the horizon

Unitary flag decomposition gives the same one-head spectral radius 19 as
the original family, centered at 2J+10.5 before the actual cR shift. Complex
conjugation preserves spectrum. The finite operator, including J at each
far end, is the true compression with the same scalar; its numerical range
remains in this interval. No extensive full-Hilbert norm is substituted.

From e0 there is a nonzero edge to c14 on each rail, since c,s>0. Any first
arrival at a site with index larger than 14 must then traverse consecutive
clock edges; every cross edge has index at most 14. Thus the first omitted
site cM is at distance 1+(M-14)=M-13. Internal source/old motion leaves the
head coordinate fixed and cannot shorten it. This proves the distance for
the full infinite geometry, not by extending a finite adjacency sample.
Low-degree polynomial actions agree for degrees below this distance.

The inherited Chebyshev bound can therefore be applied without an extra
factor for the two rails: it is already an operator bound on the whole seed
space. At M=ceil(50T), t<=2T, use sinh(1)<6/5 to get

    dM-19 t sinh(1)-9 >=(22/5)T-22>0.

Since e>8/3, e^-1<3/8 and (1-e^-1)^-1<8/5. The remaining tail is less than
(32/5)(3/8)^9=19683/20971520<.001. Triangle subtraction from the infinite

## 5. Attempted falsifiers, repairs and live alternatives

The principal attempted counterarguments above were: reverse the cross sign;
conjugate the forward instead of backward evolution; identify the reverse
output with W*; fit separate ancillary phases on the two old eigenvectors;
use the original scalar instead of the doubled vacancy scalar; count localized
columns as orthogonal; use one rail's finite distance without checking cross
shortcuts; and extend the finite horizon to all times. They respectively
change the matrix, get the propagation direction wrong, change the target
contract, or omit a required cost. None falsifies the stated narrow result.

There is also a concrete counterexample to an overbroad real-state barrier.
For two neighboring preparation qubits, the real positive exchange cell
g(n0+n1+a0^dagger a1+a1^dagger a0) evolves |10> at time pi/(4g) to
e^(-i pi/4)(|10>-i|01>)/sqrt(2). Real coefficients do not force evolved
states to remain real. This is not a completed repair: the current e0/e1
positions are not neighbors, the extra interaction breaks this route's
conserved Jf, and timed switching/handoff or an autonomous preparer must be
implemented with its full outputs and elapsed old age. It explains why the
route correctly leaves that preparation architecture open.

Equation (25) is exactly a free-lead form-factor identity for real scalar
weights. It prevents a one-sided zero in that substitution, not all
directionality with multiports or feedback. Equation (26) follows because
the inverse of a real symmetric matrix at real energy is real symmetric;
the restriction E outside the eliminated spectrum is essential. Open-channel
retarded elimination, a change of encoding/basis, time dependence or explicit
extra outputs is outside that same-basis closed-channel assertion.

For equation (27), define y_j=<real seed_j,Phi_j> with the conjugate-linear
first-slot inner product. Unique real-energy matching and one open channel
give Phi_j=R_j conjugate(Phi_j) and therefore y_j=R_j conjugate(y_j).
In the spectral expansion the outgoing coefficient is proportional to
R_j< Phi_j,seed_j>=R_j conjugate(y_j)=y_j (any flux factor is real positive).
If y_j=omega_j h for a common nonzero h and unit-modulus target omega_j,
then R_j=(omega_j/conjugate(omega_j))(h/conjugate(h)); dividing channels
gives R_j/R_l=(omega_j/omega_l)^2. This is a correct conditional necessary
phase relation. It does not certify common magnitudes, uniqueness, seed
normalization, closed tails, finite-time errors, or a new one-port emitter.
At zeros of h the division is unavailable, as the source correctly states.

Live alternatives include chiral-sector preparation with an accounted
handoff, a real one-port emitter satisfying the squared-reflection phase
condition plus magnitudes, real multiport transmission with all ports
retained, encoded real quantum walks with a decoder and complete-output
proof, a different multi-excitation mechanism, or replacing the existing
real boundary apparatus's supplied packet and three-body permission.
No search of these alternatives was performed here. No minimum number of
failed routes or formal no-go status is claimed. The standard realification
and free-chain moment argument remain credited prior work. Detailed external
literature correctness is not a new checked dependency of this mathematical
review.
