## 1. Determinant, root and rotation domain

In the ordered one-excitation basis (emitter occupied, endpoint occupied), summing the nominal three terms gives

    Q(-kappa0)+(E0-kappa0)n_e+(J-kappa0)n_end
       -> [[E0,-kappa0],[-kappa0,J]].

The endpoint's other ordinary J bond is not part of this combined local block. Keeping the block trace E0+J and setting its new off-diagonal entry to -kappa_a gives the proposed matrix

    [[E0+Delta,-kappa_a],[-kappa_a,J-Delta]].

Its determinant is E0 J+(J-E0)Delta-Delta²-kappa_a². Equality with E0 J-kappa0² is therefore equivalent to

    g Delta-Delta²=s, g=J-E0>0, s=kappa_a²-kappa0².

The root continuous at Delta=0 when s=0 is

    Delta=(g-sqrt(g²-4s))/2
          =2s/(g+sqrt(g²-4s)).

The rationalized formula is exact, including s=0. Its real domain requires s<=g²/4. The branch connected to the nominal configuration is fixed by the positive square root; the other root equals g at s=0 and exchanges the diagonal ordering rather than continuing the desired nominal block.

For the physical one-sided range 0<=kappa_a<=kappa0, s<=0, so the radicand is >=g² and

    Delta<=0, |Delta|=2|s|/(g+sqrt(g²+4|s|))<=|s|/g.

The proposal's `kappa_a<=kappa0` should be read with nonnegative coupling magnitude, as in its endpoint convention. Negative arbitrary kappa_a with only that one-sided inequality would not imply s<=0. Strict positive coupling is needed for any later emission theorem, but kappa_a=0 is harmless for this local algebra.

Equal trace and determinant fix both eigenvalues of a real symmetric two-by-two matrix. More constructively, the traceless coordinates (Delta-g/2,-kappa_a) obey

    (Delta-g/2)²+kappa_a²=g²/4+kappa0².

This is exactly the constant-radius orbit generated by real rotations of the one-excitation eigenvectors. The selected branch lies on the orbit continuously connected to the nominal matrix. A fixed spectrum cannot produce arbitrary unbounded hopping; the discriminant condition is the precise constraint.

## 3. Endpoint resolvent resummation: sign, order and norms

Make the proposal's P convention precise: let P map the background Hilbert/core space to the endpoint internal fiber, with PP*=I there. Then G=P R P*, and the common endpoint shift changes H_bg to H_bg+delta_b P*P. Under the (energy-H)^-1 convention the matching/resolvent matrix changes by MINUS delta_b P*P.

Whenever the inverses exist, the exact finite-rank identity is

    R_b=R+R P* delta_b (I-delta_b G)^-1 P R.

Compression to the endpoint gives

    G_b=G+delta_b G(I-delta_b G)^-1G
       =G(I-delta_b G)^-1,
    G_b-G=delta_b G²(I-delta_b G)^-1.

The plus sign in the difference is correct for a positive Hamiltonian onsite shift. Since delta_b is a common SCALAR, (I-delta_b G)^-1 commutes with G, justifying the displayed product order. This does not assume that arbitrary internal background matrices commute. For an internal matrix-valued shift, the formulas and allowed order would need to be stated anew; one cannot retain the scalar G² formula without checking commutation.

For the Euclidean operator norm, if ||G||<=36 and 36|delta_b|<1, the Neumann series implies

    ||(I-delta_b G)^-1||<=1/(1-36|delta_b|),
    ||G_b-G||<=1296|delta_b|/(1-36|delta_b|).

No normality, positivity of the retarded G, or off-real unitarity is used. The endpoint perturbation keeps its sign delta_b=-Delta but the norm estimate properly uses its absolute value.

One convention qualification should be explicit in publication: at real energy inside the open lead band, `(E-H_bg)^-1` is not a bounded whole-Hilbert-space resolvent. The identity is valid first where that resolvent exists (for example Im E>0), then for the retarded finite-coordinate boundary kernel. On the certified lower momentum disks it may instead be applied directly to the finite matching inverse and its analytic endpoint compression. That is exactly the G for which the proposed complex bound36 is supplied. The algebra does not assert a bounded infinite outgoing resolvent on the continuum or analytically continue it as such.
