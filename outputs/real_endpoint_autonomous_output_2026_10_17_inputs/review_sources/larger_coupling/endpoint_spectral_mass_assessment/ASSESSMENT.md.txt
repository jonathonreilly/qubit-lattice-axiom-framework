## 1. Exact quantity that must be bounded

Fix an old input column j and subtract its known scalar c_new+b_j+E0. Write x=E(k)-E0, and let d=v sigma=2.5*10^-8. The symmetric energy interval [-d,d] is contained in the original certified physical interval. Restricting to it for a lower mass bound does not widen any premise.

For the normalized delivered emitter coordinate u_j, exact block elimination gives

    Htilde=Hphys-c_new I,
    m_j(x+i0)=<e_j,(x+E0+b_j+i0-Htilde)^-1 e_j>
             =1/[x-kappa^2 s_j(x)],
    s_j=Gjj+kappa^2 Gjc Dcc^-1 Gcj.

Here Htilde is the full infinite self-adjoint operator, G is the background endpoint resolvent before adding the emitter, and Dcc retains all seven complementary emitter coordinates. More invariantly, s_j is the scalar resolvent compression of the self-adjoint complement obtained by deleting only e_j, including its remaining emitter couplings. Consequently Im s_j(z)<=0 for Im z>0. This Herglotz sign follows from the actual self-adjoint apparatus; it need not be inferred from an approximate formula.

The source proves smooth local density and no singular spectrum on the certified interval. Stone's sign and the energy Jacobian give exactly

    dmu_j/dx=-(1/pi) Im m_j(x+i0)
             =kappa^2[-Im s_j(x)]/[pi |x-kappa^2 s_j(x)|^2].

This is also |F_j(k)|^2/E'(k). It is the seed's spectral measure, not the trace measure of the whole core and not a normalized conditional distribution. Since mu_j(R)=1,

    ||P_outside_band e_j||^2
      <=1-integral_[-d,d] dmu_j.

All exterior point, singular-continuous and absolutely continuous mass is included on the left. No need to locate or classify that spectrum remains if the right side is small. This is the central advantage over trying to extend a scattering basis globally.

## 2. A sufficient finite-interval lemma

Put s_j(0)=a-ib and suppose on [-d,d]

    |a|<=A, 0<b<=B, b>=b_min>0,
    |s_j(x)-s_j(0)|<=L |x|,
    kappa^2 L(1+A/b_min)<=1/2,
    d>kappa^2 A.

Let gamma=kappa^2 b and c=kappa^2 a. The comparison m0=1/(x-c+i gamma) is an algebraic comparison function only; it is not assumed to describe the actual measure outside the band. Its density integrates to one on the real line by elementary integration.

Since |x|/|x-c+i gamma|<=1+A/b_min, the Lipschitz condition implies

    |x-kappa^2 s_j(x)| >= |x-c+i gamma|/2,
    |m_j-m0| <=2 kappa^2 L |x|/[(x-c)^2+gamma^2].

Splitting |x|<=|x-c|+|c| and integrating yields the conservative bound

    integral_[-d,d] |x|/[(x-c)^2+gamma^2] dx
      <=2 log(1+(d+kappa^2 A)/(kappa^2 b_min))
          +pi A/b_min =:H.

The two tails of the algebraic comparison density have total mass at most

    2 kappa^2 B/[pi(d-kappa^2 A)].

Therefore the actual missing spectral mass is bounded by

    q_j <= q_mass(kappa)
       :=2 kappa^2 B/[pi(d-kappa^2 A)]
           +(2 kappa^2 L/pi) H.                 (1)

The actual off-band amplitude is sqrt(q_mass), rather than kappa/d. The derivation uses no approximation at the resonance, where x is of order kappa^2, and keeps the real shift c. The logarithmic correction is the price of proving a density comparison in absolute value; cancellations could improve it, but are not assumed.

The same argument applies to the exact scalar reference resolvent. Its self-energy is s_ref=-exp(ik(x))/J, with energy derivative norm

    |s_ref'|=1/(J E')<1334.

Thus (1), with L_ref=1334, bounds 1-p0 directly on the same inner interval. This reference bound uses its actual global normalized seed measure; it does not normalize a fitted Lorentzian.

## 5. Alternative local-contour certificate and exact obstacles

A finite-core complex-energy route can replace the Lipschitz certificate. Choose an inner energy interval and integrate the exact scalar m_j(z) along a rectangle in the upper half-plane, closing onto its real boundary. The resolvent is analytic there. Cauchy's theorem identifies the real-axis integral with the top and both vertical sides; taking the negative imaginary part gives the seed's exact in-band spectral mass. An enclosure of this contour integral to absolute error below, for example, 10^-8 would directly prove a useful missing-mass bound. All three off-real pieces and endpoint limits must be retained. The infinite lead is represented by its exact retarded self-energy; a finite isolated-core spectrum is not a substitute.

To avoid subtracting nearly equal numbers numerically one should integrate m_j-m0 and use the exact arctangent integral of m0. Uniform denominator enclosures, branch choice, complex rounding/error enclosures and an integral remainder estimate would be the new checkable finite-core data. No execution is authorized or performed in this assessment. A future numerical certificate would need its own bounded resource plan and independent check.

Neither the real-axis epsilon_D relative denominator estimate nor epsilon_F profile-ratio estimate suffices alone for this purpose. The latter only gives in-band mass >=(1-epsilon_F)^2 p0, allowing an O(epsilon_F) missing mass and therefore an O(sqrt(epsilon_F)) amplitude, far too large. Even an O(epsilon_D) mass loss is too large. A modulus-only contour bound at Im z of order d can revert to the old kappa^2/d^2 loss unless the endpoint self-energy is quantitatively controlled. Positivity/Herglotz structure does not turn an arbitrary approximate profile into an exact normalized global Lorentzian.

There is no claim that such a useful endpoint regularity or contour certificate is impossible. The smallest next proof obligation is to derive a uniform energy bound |s_j'|<=10^6 on the inner band from the exact pre-emitter matching matrices, or replace it with a comparably strong validated scalar contour-mass enclosure. Independently checking that one obligation would make (1) applicable; the kappa-extension and full error/resource composition must then be checked before any new apparatus/runtime claim is published.
