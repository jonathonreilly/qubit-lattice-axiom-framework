## 1. Scalar resolvent identity and sign

Let e_j be the normalized emitter coordinate with internal B eigenvector u_j and bare energy b_j+E0. Since B u_j=b_j u_j, deleting only e_j from the full self-adjoint Hamiltonian leaves a self-adjoint complement whose coupling to e_j is -kappa times the endpoint coordinate u_j. In the convention (z-H)^-1, its compressed resolvent has nonpositive imaginary part in the upper half-plane. The exact one-coordinate Schur identity therefore is

    m_j(x+i0)=1/[x-kappa^2 s_j(x)],
    Im s_j(z)<=0 for Im z>0.

Eliminating the seven remaining emitter coordinates inside this complement yields precisely

    s_j=G_jj+kappa^2 G_jc D_cc^-1 G_cj.

The plus sign in s_j and minus sign in x-kappa^2 s_j agree with the canonical D_j formula. There is no adjoint inserted between retarded blocks and no claim that their product is pointwise positive; positivity of the measure follows from the actual self-adjoint complement. The seven complementary coordinates remain present.

Write s_j(x)=a(x)-i b(x). Stone gives

    -(1/pi)Im m_j(x+i0)
       =kappa^2 b(x)/[pi|x-kappa^2 s_j(x)|^2].

This is the seed's density in energy x. Its momentum density is |F_j(k)|^2, so division by E'(k)>0 is correct. There is no extra2pi, square root of velocity, core-trace factor or old-column multiplicity.

The inner energy interval [-d,d], d=v sigma, lies inside E(I)-E0 by monotonicity and E'>v along both sides of k0. Its use can only weaken a lower bound on the original band mass; it does not widen a certified interval.

## 2. Pointwise denominator comparison

Use the lemma's assumptions s_j(0)=a-ib, |a|<=A, b_min<=b<=B, b_min>0 and |s_j(x)-s_j(0)|<=L|x| on [-d,d]. Define c=kappa^2 a, gamma=kappa^2 b and D0=x-c+i gamma. Then

    |x|/|D0| <= |x-c|/|D0|+|c|/|D0|
               <=1+|c|/gamma <=1+A/b_min.

The smallness hypothesis kappa^2 L(1+A/b_min)<=1/2 gives

    |(x-kappa^2 s_j(x))-D0|<=|D0|/2,
    |x-kappa^2 s_j(x)|>=|D0|/2>0.

Subtracting the two scalar inverses, rather than comparing only their moduli, yields

    |m_j-m0| <=2kappa^2 L |x|/[(x-c)^2+gamma^2],

This remains valid through the resonance; there is no division by |x| or an assumption that the resonance is centered at zero. The nonzero real shift is retained explicitly.

## 3. Finite logarithmic integral and comparison tails

Put D=d+kappa^2 A. With u=x-c, the integration domain is contained in [-D,D]. Splitting |x|<=|u|+|c| gives

    integral_[-d,d] |x|/[(x-c)^2+gamma^2] dx
      <=log(1+(D/gamma)^2)+pi |c|/gamma
      <=2log(1+D/(kappa^2 b_min))+pi A/b_min =H.

The first term comes from the integral of |u|, whose antiderivative supplies one logarithm of1+(D/gamma)^2, not an extra factor two. The final bound uses 1+r^2<=(1+r)^2 and is conservative. The shift term is bounded using the full-line integral pi/gamma. The finite D must remain in the logarithm; no divergent full-line absolute first moment is introduced.

The normalized comparison density is gamma/[pi((x-c)^2+gamma^2)]. If d>kappa^2 A, each of its two exterior tails is bounded using 1/(u^2+gamma^2)<=1/u^2. Their sum is

    <=gamma/pi[1/(d-c)+1/(d+c)]
    <=2kappa^2 B/[pi(d-kappa^2 A)].

The actual density differs in absolute value from the comparison density by at most |m_j-m0|/pi. Integrating and combining the two estimates proves

    missing mass <=2kappa^2 B/[pi(d-kappa^2 A)]

Thus equation (1)'s factor2, pi and finite endpoints are correct.
