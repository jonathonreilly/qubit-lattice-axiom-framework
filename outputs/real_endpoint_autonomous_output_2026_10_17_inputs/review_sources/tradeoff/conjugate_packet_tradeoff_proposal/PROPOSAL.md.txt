## 1. Exact alternative comparison and its initial error

For each real old basis column j in a fixed parity, write omega=omega_p and

    g_j=omega f,
    a0,j=conjugate(g_j)=conjugate(omega) conjugate(f).

Use the same unit-incoming generalized column Phi_j, with actual reflection R_j and localized remainder C_j. The proposed intermediate spectral packet has incoming coefficient a0,j and outgoing coefficient R_j a0,j. Since integral|f|^2 dk=1, a0,j is normalized in the unchanged local multiplicity-one synthesis isometry. No new state preparation is prescribed: the packet is only a mathematical comparison to the actual delivered seed.

The canonical source gives, pointwise on I,

    |F_j/Fref-omega|<epsilon_F=.000752,
    f=Fref/sqrt(p0), 1-1.6e-9<=p0<=1,
    F_j=R_j conjugate(F_j), |R_j|=1.

For a real basis seed, its actual incoming spectral coefficient is conjugate(F_j). Therefore the in-band state comparison obeys

    ||conjugate(F_j)-a0,j||_L2=||F_j-g_j||_L2
      <=epsilon_F sqrt(p0)+(1-sqrt(p0))
      <.0007520016.

The unchanged outside-band amplitude is <=4e-5, including the allowed point spectrum. Adding it gives <.000792002 per column. The source's sqrt(2)<1.415 aggregation for the two potentially overlapping old-energy error vectors yields <.001121 for the preparation-to-alternative-packet operator. Parities are orthogonal. General complex old/reference inputs follow by linear extension of these real basis-column estimates.

## 2. Explicit outgoing profile defect

Let e_j=F_j/sqrt(p0)-g_j. Then |e_j(k)|<epsilon_F |f(k)|. Since p0 is positive real, the exact real-seed identity also gives

    F_j/sqrt(p0)=R_j conjugate(F_j/sqrt(p0)),
    R_j a0,j-g_j=e_j-R_j conjugate(e_j).

Hence pointwise and in L2,

    |R_j a0,j-g_j|<=2epsilon_F |f|,
    ||R_j a0,j-g_j||_L2<.001504.                    (1)

No division by 1-epsilon_F or removal of a column phase is needed. The outgoing full-line Fourier map is isometric, and restriction to physical m>=0 is a contraction. At every time the physical outgoing defect is therefore <.001504 per column. Its images lie in mutually orthogonal open internal output fibers u_j for the different old columns. Consequently (1) is also the complete operator ceiling; it does not acquire sqrt(2). The factors exp(-it b_j), omega_p and exp(-it c_new) retain the same actual elapsed old phase and scalar. This step pays a permanent waveform discrepancy of the *intermediate packet*, while leaving the requested final common target g_j unchanged.

## 3. Incoming coefficient and pole-free rectangle

Use k_L=k0-sigma, k_R=k0+sigma, h=sigma/2=5e-7. Write alpha_D=kappa^2/J, E(z)=2J(1-cos z), and

    Dbar(z)=E(z)-E0+alpha_D exp(-iz),
    a0,j(z)=conjugate(omega zeta0) A_f E'(z) exp(-i113z)/Dbar(z),
    A_f=kappa/[sqrt(2pi)J sqrt(p0)]<17 kappa.

This is a holomorphic continuation of the actual conjugate coefficient on the real interval, with all constants fixed. For z=x-iy, x in [k_L,k_R], 0<=y<=h,

    Im Dbar(x-iy)
      =-sin(x)[2J sinh(y)+alpha_D exp(-y)] <0.        (2)

**Sign detail:** exp(-i(x-iy))=exp(-y)exp(-ix). Thus the alpha_D term in (2) is exp(-y), not cosh(y). The proposed cosh form would correspond to retaining only the cosine part of that exponential and is not the exact imaginary part. Both expressions happen to be negative, but this proof uses the exact expression. The no-pole conclusion and constants below do not need a lower bound from the positive alpha_D term at y>0.

Because sin(x)>.4 and alpha_D>0, (2) is strictly negative even at y=0. Therefore Dbar has no zero anywhere on the closed rectangle, and contour deformation of the incoming integral is valid. The numerator is entire. The exact lower-strip Fourier/time factor has modulus

    |exp[-imz-itE(z)-i113z]|
      =exp[-(m+113)y-2Jt sin(x)sinh(y)]
      <=exp[-(m+113+vt)y],                         (3)

where E'(x)=2J sin(x)>v=.025 and sinh(y)>=y. The 113 propagation phase is kept, not stripped as a constant.

The upper real-axis integral is thus bounded by the lower horizontal integral plus the two vertical edge integrals. Those vertical terms are precisely the cost of the hard cutoff; neither endpoint is set to zero.

## 4. Explicit rectangle constants

For this tiny strip, cosh(y)-1<=y^2 and cosh(y)<=1+y^2. Since 2J<.061,

    |E'(x-iy)|=2J|sin(x-iy)|<=2J cosh(y)<.062.      (4)

At either real endpoint x=k_L or k_R, |E(x)-E0|>=v sigma. The exact real part is

    Re Dbar(x-iy)
      =E(x)-E0-2J cos(x)(cosh(y)-1)
         +alpha_D exp(-y)cos(x).

Thus, using exp(-y)<=1 and alpha_D<kappa^2/.03,

    |Re Dbar(x-iy)|
      >=v sigma-.061h^2-alpha_D
      >.024 sigma.                                (5)

Indeed v sigma=2.5e-8, .061h^2=1.525e-14 and alpha_D<3.334e-23; their subtraction exceeds 2.4e-8. Using the looser alpha_D cosh(h) bound suggested during development would also suffice, but is unnecessary.

Equations (4)-(5) imply the vertical-side coefficient bound

    A_f |E'(z)|/|Dbar(z)|
      <17*.062*kappa/(.024sigma)<44 kappa/sigma.

After (3), integrating y from zero to h and enlarging to [0,infinity), both vertical sides together contribute at site m>=0 at most

    [88 kappa/sigma]/(m+113+vt)
      <=[88 kappa/sigma]/(m+vt).

The same lattice sum and Fourier normalization as in the canonical proof give vertical incoming norm

    <=88 kappa/[sigma sqrt(pi vt)].                 (6)

On the horizontal bottom, (2) gives |Dbar(x-ih)|>=2J sin(x)sinh(h)>v h. Consequently the weaker convenient bound is

    A_f|E'(x-ih)|/|Dbar(x-ih)|<44 kappa/h.

Its length is 2sigma. Summing exp(-2mh) exactly over m>=0, retaining the Fourier normalization, gives bottom norm

    <= [88 kappa sigma/h] exp(-vth)
          /sqrt[2pi(1-exp(-2h))].

For 0<2h<=1, 1-exp(-2h)>=h, and sqrt(2pi)>2. Therefore

    bottom norm <44 kappa sigma/(h sqrt(h)) exp(-vth)
                 =88 kappa/sqrt(h) exp(-vth)
                 <1.3e-7 exp(-vth)<=1.3e-7.         (7)

The final numeric inequality can be checked without evaluating any enormous exponential: (88e-12)^2/(5e-7)=1.5488e-14<(1.3e-7)^2=1.69e-14. Dropping the nonnegative-time decay in (7) is conservative. At t>=10^30, sqrt(pi vt)>2e14, so (6) is <4.4e-19. Thus the total incoming operator norm is <2e-7. Open fibers are orthogonal across old columns, so again no sqrt(2) is required. This bound retains all finite cutoff effects and is valid uniformly for the stated all-future domain.

    ||p_t-beta_t||=1-||p_t||
