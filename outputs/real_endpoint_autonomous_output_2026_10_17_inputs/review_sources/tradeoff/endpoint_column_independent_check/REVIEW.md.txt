## 1. Scope of the pre-deletion block bound

The needed `G(s,end)` and `G(s,b)` are physical 8-by-8 internal blocks of the intermediate two-port operator before input termination, where s ranges over the 112 background core head positions. The canonical note's lines 417–425 do cover this full scope, not merely two external open-channel amplitudes.

Here is an independent reconstruction of that coverage. For one bare arm, the 16-dimensional contact matching inverse bounds core/core blocks by 166. The core/tail blocks follow from the recurrence as R E1 Q^(n-1), or their transpose, so their norms are at most 166 because ||Q||=1. A tail/tail block is A Q^|n-m|+Q^(n-1)Tg Q^(m-1), of norm at most 40+206=246. Thus every bare physical site-to-site block, including contact sites and arbitrarily distant sites, is below 250.

For the two-site filter, each map from one physical site to the two perturbation sites has norm at most sqrt(2)*250. The local perturbation norm is below 1/20 and its full 16-dimensional response inverse is below 2000. Its contribution to any site-to-site kernel therefore has norm below

    2*250^2*(1/20)*2000=12,500,000.

Adding the bare block gives 12,500,250<13,000,000. The other arm is unchanged. A physical coordinate after the real lead mixing is a linear combination of at most two orthonormal head coordinates. At each of the two block ends the coefficient absolute sum is at most sqrt(2), so a factor two suffices even without exploiting zeros in the arm direct sum. Every required mixed physical block is therefore below 26,000,000<30,000,000. This applies uniformly to both end=20 and b=21 on the input port and to every retained arm, input or output-zero site s. No source degree of freedom was dropped.

The filter response and bare inverse are inherited qualified bounds; this argument verifies their use and coordinate coverage rather than independently recalibrating them.

## 2. Endpoint column and reference plane

Use the canonical port coordinates m,n>=0, with end=ell=20 and b=ell+1=21. Its full internal kernel is

    G(m,n)=A Q^|m-n|+Q^m T_aa Q^n.

Set X=Q^ell T_aa Q^ell. Since A and Q commute, but T_aa need not commute with Q,

    G(b,end)=AQ+Q^(ell+1)T_aa Q^ell=Q(A+X),
    G(end,b)=(A+X)Q.

Both are exact physical Green blocks. No scattering-amplitude normalization, traveling-wave reference-plane shift, or commutation of T_aa through Q is used. The canonical bounds imply

    ||A+X|| <=40+40*(12e-6)+1e-28 <41,
    ||G(b,end)||<41.

This is the particular neighboring block that the generic bound had overestimated. Keeping only the scalar open component here would have been invalid; the identity and bound above retain all eight internal components.

At positive imaginary spectral part, removing b is the ordinary block resolvent deletion. Taking the already controlled retarded limit gives

    Gtr(s,end)=G(s,end)-G(s,b)Gbb^-1 G(b,end).

Removal of b disconnects its outer tail; that disconnected component does not change the retained-side kernel. The canonical uniform bound ||Gbb^-1||<22 guarantees this is well-defined. Consequently

    ||Gtr(s,end)|| <3e7(1+22*41)=2.709e10<3e10.

This confirms proposal (1). The endpoint is still the old input site ell at this stage; the deleted b has not yet been reintroduced as a different emitter block. Confusing those stages would invalidate this calculation, but the proposal keeps them separate.

## 3. Literal background core and endpoint compensation

The retained background has 45+45 arm sites, 21 input sites m=0,...,20, and output site m=0: 112 head positions. Each parity fiber is eight-dimensional, giving 896 coordinates. The completed core adds the eight-dimensional emitter fiber, giving 113*8=904 coordinates. The output sites m>=1 are eliminated by the same retarded free-tail self-energy in both problems. The source/old subsystem is an internal fiber shared across head positions; it does not add separate head sites or double this count.

Let E be the real coordinate inclusion at input end into this 896-dimensional background. Its columns are orthonormal, E*=E^T and ||E||=1. The background finite matching matrix is the compression of the terminated Hamiltonian with the output self-energy included. Its inverse R_bg is exactly the 896-coordinate restriction of Gtr: eliminating an unchanged free output tail and taking the finite core kernel are the same Schur operation. The uniform retarded kernel construction supplies invertibility here; no bounded inverse on the entire continuous-spectrum Hilbert space is claimed.

There is a potentially consequential compensation issue, and it checks correctly. Deleting the ordinary end-to-b bond while preserving the Dirichlet compression leaves end's diagonal 2J, implemented in the background by one remaining ordinary bond plus J n_end. When the emitter is installed, Q(-kappa) contributes +kappa on end and +kappa on the emitter. The added (J-kappa)n_end and (E0-kappa)n_e yield total diagonals 2J and E0 respectively. Thus the end diagonal of the completed core's background compression remains exactly the same 2J. Removing the emitter coordinate algebraically does not remove the +kappa diagonal contribution already in that compression.

There is no extra vacancy scalar in this installation. B acts once as the common internal operator and c_new is unchanged. The emitter coupling to background is precisely -kappa E in H and +kappa E in lambda-H. The old input location b may be reused geometrically for the emitter without identifying its original free-site diagonal or -J bond with the new E0 diagonal or -kappa bond.

## 4. Block norms and complex transpose

For any y=(y_1,...,y_112) with y_s in C^8, the generic bound ||(R_bg)_st||<2e16 gives

    ||(R_bg y)_s|| <=2e16 sum_t ||y_t||,
    ||R_bg|| <112*2e16=2.24e18.

For any v in C^8, the newly bounded endpoint column gives

    ||R_bg E v||^2=sum_s ||Gtr(s,end)v||^2
                   <112*(3e10)^2 ||v||^2,
    ||R_bg E||^2<1.008e23.

These are operator block estimates, not entrywise scalar estimates. No additional factor of eight belongs in either line. A factor eight will be needed later when taking a trace over an eight-dimensional diagonal block.

The real physical Hamiltonian is symmetric in the fixed real basis. Its retarded self-energy JQ is complex symmetric, because Q is a complex linear combination of fixed real symmetric orthogonal projectors. Thus the finite matching matrix and R_bg are complex symmetric; Hermiticity is neither true nor required. Since E is real,

    E^T R_bg=(R_bg E)^T.

For any rectangular complex V, V^T=conjugate(V*) and entrywise conjugation is an antiunitary isometry on the domain and codomain. Hence ||V^T||=||V*||=||V|| in the Euclidean operator norm. This does not say V^T=V* or make V^T V positive. The proposal uses the transpose only in the exact symmetric Schur identity and uses V*V separately to compute its norm, which is the correct distinction.

## 5. Complete emitter Schur update

In background/emitter order the actual matching matrix is

    M=[[R_bg^-1,kappa E],[kappa E^T,Dbare]],
    Dbare=(lambda-E0)I-B,
    De=Dbare-kappa^2 E^T R_bg E.

This De agrees with the canonical eight-dimensional emitter Schur complement because E^T R_bg E=Gtr(end,end). The source bounds its full inverse by 1e23, including the seven complementary internal directions.

Direct block elimination, keeping every internal direction, gives

    M^-1=[[R_bg+kappa^2 R_bg E De^-1 E^T R_bg,
                   -kappa R_bg E De^-1],
          [-kappa De^-1 E^T R_bg,De^-1]]
         =diag(R_bg,0)+V De^-1 V^T,
    V=[-kappa R_bg E; I8].

The signs can also be checked by multiplying M into its displayed emitter column. The update has at most rank eight; no rank-one emitter approximation is used.

Now V*V=I+kappa^2(R_bg E)*(R_bg E), a positive Hermitian matrix. Therefore

    ||V||^2=1+kappa^2||R_bg E||^2<1.1008,
    ||M^-1||<2.24e18+1.1008e23
             =1.1008224e23<1.2e23.

The margin to 1.2e23 is 0.0991776e23. This confirms proposal (4) and the primary inverse lemma on the actual orthonormal 904-coordinate core. It needs neither an averaged inverse nor a derivative of De^-1.

    integral|f|||x'||<1.2e23(.061*.002+.1*86)

    M^-1-(M^-1)*=-(i/E')xx*,

    ||x||^2<.122*8*(112*1e20+1e23)
