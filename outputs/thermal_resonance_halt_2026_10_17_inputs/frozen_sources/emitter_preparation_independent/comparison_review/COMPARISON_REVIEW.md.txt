## 2. Full-space cancellation and the interacting auxiliary Hamiltonian

Use root's notation \(G_i=g_i n_i(I+X_{\rm old,i})\),
\(b_i=2g_i\), and
\[
Q_{ej}=\kappa(n_e+n_j)+t_j a_j^\dagger a_e+
\overline{t_j}a_e^\dagger a_j,\qquad |t_j|=\kappa.
\]
On their complete two-qubit spaces, \(0\le G_i\le b_iI\) and
\(0\le Q_{ej}\le2\kappa I\). Hence the four pair complements in root
equation (3) are positive without an excitation-sector restriction.
Their full pair spectra are respectively \(\{0,b_i,b_i,b_i\}\) and
\(\{0,0,2\kappa,2\kappa\}\).

The exact cancellations are
\[
G_i+(b_iI-G_i)=b_iI,\qquad
Q_{ej}+(2\kappa I-Q_{ej})=2\kappa I,
\]
\[
\nu(I-n_1)+\nu n_1=\nu I,\qquad
\kappa(I-n_j)+\kappa n_j=\kappa I,
\]
\[
(E_0-2\kappa)n_e+
(E_0-2\kappa+\Delta)(I-n_e)
=(E_0-2\kappa)I+\Delta(I-n_e).
\]
Combining the two emitter-pair reserves and two attachment reserves gives
\(E_0+4\kappa\), not \(E_0+2\kappa\). Thus root's full-space identity is
correct:
\[
H_{\rm prep}=K+c_{\rm prep}I+H_{\rm aux},\quad
c_{\rm prep}=b_A+b_B+\nu+E_0+4\kappa,
\]
\[
H_{\rm aux}=C+H_c+V_c+\Delta D,\qquad
D=(I-n_e)+n_A+n_B+\sum_j n_{c_j}.
\tag{R1}
\]
Every remaining term in \(C,H_c,V_c\) is positive and annihilates the
blank clock/source configuration. Both endpoint diagonals in \(H_c\)
remain. The emitter has no remaining hopping in \(H_{\rm aux}\), and
\(\Delta D\) has the one-dimensional zero eigenspace spanned by \(\Omega\).
Therefore \(H_{\rm aux}\Omega=0\), \(H_{\rm aux}\ge\Delta D\) and
\(\ker H_{\rm aux}=\mathbb C\Omega\). These are statements on all thermal
sectors. They do not require \([H_{\rm aux},D]=0\), which generally fails.
The old \(K\) is untouched and decoupled for every auxiliary state.

All complements use existing nearest-neighbor supports, with no three-body

## 3. Gibbs tail and mean energy: the noncommuting steps

Let the \(2^N\) eigenvalues of \(H_{\rm aux}\) and \(D\), in increasing
order and with multiplicity, be \(\lambda_j,d_j\). The finite-dimensional
min-max formula gives \(\lambda_j\ge\Delta d_j\) from (R1): every Rayleigh
quotient of the first operator is at least that of the second.
The \(d_j\) list contains each \(k=0,\ldots,N\) with multiplicity
\(\binom Nk\). It is legitimate to apply a scalar decreasing function to
each matched pair of eigenvalues even when the two matrices do not commute.

The unique ground state is exactly \(\Omega\), so its Gibbs weight is
\(1/Z\). With \(q=e^{-\beta\Delta}\),
\[
1\le Z=\sum_j e^{-\beta\lambda_j}
\le\sum_{k=0}^N\binom Nk q^k=(1+q)^N,
\]
\[
d_{\rm thermal}=1-Z^{-1}
\le1-(1+q)^{-N}\le Nq.
\tag{R2}
\]
The final inequality is the tangent-line bound
\((1+q)^{-N}\ge1-Nq\). No operator-exponential monotonicity, product Gibbs
ansatz, free-fermion spectrum or one-head truncation enters (R2).
The exponential is diagonalized separately in the two eigenvalue lists.

For root equation (7), the additional condition \(\beta\Delta\ge1\)
is essential and is stated correctly. The function \(x e^{-\beta x}\)
is then nonincreasing for \(x\ge\Delta\). For each positive eigenvalue,
\[
\lambda_j e^{-\beta\lambda_j}
\le\Delta d_j e^{-\beta\Delta d_j}.
\]
Summing and using \(Z\ge1\) proves
\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\Gamma_\beta H_{\rm aux})
\le\Delta\sum_{k=1}^N k\binom Nk q^k
=\Delta Nq(1+q)^{N-1}.
\tag{R3}
\]
If \(Nq\le r<1\), this is at most \(\Delta r e^r\le\Delta r/(1-r)\).
The step is valid even though \(H_{\rm aux}\) includes interacting
conditional flips. If the hypothesis were dropped, it would fail: for one
qubit, \(\Delta=1,\beta=1/100,H_{\rm aux}=100n\) obeys
\(H_{\rm aux}\ge\Delta n\), but its mean \(100/(1+e)>25\) exceeds
\(\Delta e^{-\beta\Delta}<1\). This is a check of the stated condition,
not a counterexample to root's theorem.

For marginal half-trace delivery error \(\eta_{\rm delivery}\), the good
projector probability differs by at most that amount. Thus the actual bad
weight is at most \(\min(1,Nq+\eta_{\rm delivery})\).
The nominal mean-energy bound does not follow from that probability alone:
root correctly retains the full-space
\(2\|H_{\rm aux}\|\eta_{\rm delivery}\) energy correction or requires
an actual mean. That norm can grow with the device.

The proposed numerical tail inequality can be certified directly, without
the root checker. The series for \(e\) gives \(e>8/3\), and
\[
(3/8)^{10}=\frac{59049}{1073741824}<\frac6{10^5}.
\]
Since \(N\le5\,10^{31}+3<6\,10^{31}\), at \(\beta\Delta=100\),
\[
Nq<6\,10^{31}(6/10^5)^{10}
=\frac{6^{11}}{10^{19}}
<\frac1{22500000000}.
\tag{R4}
\]
The last comparison is the ordinary integer inequality
\(225\,6^{11}=81629337600<10^{11}\).
Consequently \(3\sqrt d<2\,10^{-5}\), as claimed. This is a hand-derived
exact bound; no new numerical program or sampled spectrum was used.

## 4. Arbitrary old/reference extensions and energy

The root's sharp pure-factor lemma is applied to the right target:
the exact unconditioned old/reference marginal at the declared delivery
or release-start time. If \(P=|\Omega\rangle\langle\Omega|\) and
\(d=1-\operatorname{Tr}(P\rho_{\rm aux})\), purification and the good/bad
split give
\[
\|\rho-P\rho P\|_1\le\sqrt{d(4-3d)}.
\]
Writing \(P\rho P=P\otimes\tau_{OR}\), the difference
\(\rho_{OR}-\tau_{OR}\) is positive of trace \(d\). Therefore
\[
\|\rho-P\otimes\rho_{OR}\|_1
\le d+\sqrt{d(4-3d)}=g(d).
\tag{R5}
\]
The rank-two matrix in root's proof has the displayed trace norm;
orthogonality is between the good and bad full vectors and does not
require the two retained-reference reduced states to be orthogonal.
The monotonicity in a known upper bound for \(d\) is valid:
\[
g'(d)=1+\frac{2-3d}{\sqrt{4d-3d^2}}\ge0,
\]
with direct endpoint values. For \(d\ge2/3\) the needed squared
inequality reduces to \(4(1-d)(3d-1)\ge0\).
Also \(g(d)\le3\sqrt d\).

An independent auxiliary/old-reference product input with the exact Gibbs
density has complete error \(2d_{\rm thermal}\), although the auxiliary
Gibbs density itself is interacting. Its ground block is an eigenblock and
has no coherence with its orthogonal complement. A marginal-only delivery
does not imply that joint product. A Schmidt purification of the Gibbs
density shows why the square-root correlated price cannot be replaced by
the linear product price.

Since (R1) is an additive old/auxiliary Hamiltonian, its mean is
\(\operatorname{Tr}(K\rho_O)+c_{\rm prep}+E_{\rm aux}\) even for correlated
inputs; no statistical-independence assumption is needed for that mean.
The root's work inequality follows from
\(\dot H=\sum_r u'_r A_r\le0\), or \(\dot H=a'A\le0\),
and the exact finite-space energy-balance identity.
Using \(H_{\rm run}\ge I\) and \(K\le3I\) gives its conservative lower
work bound \(-(2+c_{\rm prep}+E_{\rm aux})\).
No covariance, fluctuation, cooling-cost or controller-return conclusion
follows, and none is required in the source.

If the target is instead an old/reference state before a bath interacted,
the complete preceding bath/reference evolution must be included. An
auxiliary-only bath coupling suffices to preserve free old marginal
aging, but does not by itself fix the joint old/reference state when the
bath is part of that reference. Root's use of the actual delivered
unconditioned marginal avoids that unsupported backward extension.

## 6. Staged asynchronous release

This is the load-bearing new scheduling observation. All non-emitter
complements and fields obey
\[
A_r\mathcal E=a_r\mathcal E,\qquad
a_r=b_A,b_B\ \text{for the two old/source complements, and }0
\text{ for all other rest blocks}.
\tag{R8}
\]
In this section \(D\) is the early-stage duration, as in the addendum,
distinct from the defect-count operator denoted by \(D\) in (R1).
It follows from the actual local terms, not from positivity alone. Keep
both emitter complements exactly at coefficient one. On the full seed
range, their off-diagonal \(-\kappa(V_1+V_2)\) cancels the runtime
\(\kappa(V_1+V_2)\) for every time and every old input. Hence
\[
H_{\rm early}(s)\mathcal E
=\mathcal E[K+c_{\rm run}+2\kappa+b_Au_A(s)+b_Bu_B(s)].
\tag{R9}
\]
Every source and clock term either annihilates the seed or contributes
the explicitly shown scalar. Changing different cold fields at different
times cannot create an excitation on that range. Self-adjointness and
uniqueness of the finite-dimensional Schrödinger equation then give the
exact reducing-range propagator
\[
U_{\rm early}(D)\mathcal E
=\mathcal E e^{-iDK-i\Phi_{\rm early}},
\quad
\Phi_{\rm early}=(c_{\rm run}+2\kappa)D+
b_A\int_0^D u_A+b_B\int_0^D u_B.
\tag{R10}
\]
No slowly varying or adiabatic premise is hidden in this identity.
Monotonicity is used for the work sign, not for seed invariance.

The rest blocks can therefore be released sequentially. At most one
changing block in a nonoverlapping cubic window of duration \(\ell\)
contributes the derivative norm \(3\|A_r\|/(2\ell)\) at an incident site.
Taking every such bound below the supplied allowance gives the root's
sufficient construction. The total duration is the sum of those windows
and gaps and may grow as \(M\); its actual value remains in (R10) and
the old age. One cannot assert a length-independent total setup time.

The notation \(D\ge0\) at addendum line 38 should be restricted to \(D>0\)
for this nonempty rest list and finite derivative caps. A continuously
varying nonzero block cannot go from coefficient one to zero on a
zero-length interval. Conditional statements with \(D=0\) have no
admissible schedule here; all exhibited schedules use positive durations.

After every rest coefficient has reached zero, the late generator has
supported action
\[
H_{\rm late}(s)\mathcal E
=\mathcal E[K+c_{\rm run}+2\kappa a(s)]
 +(1-a(s))\kappa(V_1+V_2).
\tag{R11}
\]
The same independent inhomogeneous-evolution calculation gives
\[
\|U_{\rm late}U_{\rm early}\mathcal E-
\mathcal E e^{-i(D+\tau_e)K-i\Phi_{\rm staged}}\|
\le\kappa\tau_e/\sqrt2,
\]
\[
\Phi_{\rm staged}=\Phi_{\rm early}+c_{\rm run}\tau_e+\kappa\tau_e.
\tag{R12}
\]
There is no early-duration contribution to this nominal error. At the
emitter the two late block norms sum to \(4\kappa\), so the cubic slew
is at most \(6\kappa/\tau_e\), precisely as stated.
If the late envelopes differ, orthogonality of the two output destinations
gives the residual \(\kappa\sqrt{(1-a_1)^2+(1-a_2)^2}\), with scalar
\(c_{\rm run}+\kappa(a_1+a_2)\). Both separate areas must be retained.

The scalar accounting is also consistent under asynchronous cold fields.
Let \(a_{\rm run}\) denote the full Pauli identity coefficient of the
literal \(H_{\rm run}\):
\[
a_{\rm run}=2+
\frac{\Delta_A+\Delta_B+g_A+g_B+\lambda_A+\lambda_B+\mu_0+\nu+E_0}{2}
 +h_{AB}+u/4+JM+2\kappa.
\]
Writing \(\alpha_r\) for that coefficient of each rest block, the
*whole-Hamiltonian* identity contribution in the early stage integrates to
\[
(a_{\rm run}+2\kappa)D+\sum_{r\in{\rm rest}}\alpha_r\int_0^D u_r.
\tag{R13}
\]
The late identity contribution is \(a_{\rm run}\tau_e+\kappa\tau_e\).
Cold-projector areas can disappear from the seed scalar (R10) because
their nonidentity Pauli parts cancel their identity action on that seed.
They are not deleted from (R13), work, block loads or controller precision.
The old/source complement has \(\alpha_r=3g_i/2\), the emitter complement
has identity coefficient \(\kappa\), and every positive onsite projector
has half its coefficient. This reconstructs the complete scalar ledger.

Exact cancellation, isolation and known completion time are indispensable.
If a supported off-diagonal error is bounded by \(\eta(s)\), Duhamel
