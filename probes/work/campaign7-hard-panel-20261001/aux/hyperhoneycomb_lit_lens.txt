=== A. PAPERS ===

[1] M. Hermanns, K. O'Brien, S. Trebst, "Weyl spin liquids", PRL 114, 157202 (2015), arXiv:1411.7379. READ (5 pp main text; the PRL supplemental material was NOT read).
 - Model: hyperhoneycomb Kitaev, zero-flux sector (flux gap ~0.2 J_K), field along [111]. Third-order term kappa~ sigma sigma sigma becomes next-neighbour Majorana hopping, coefficient u~_jk = -eps_abg; kappa := kappa~/J_K.
 - kappa=0: nodal loop in the plane k_y=-k_x (their BZ labels).
 - Small kappa>0: loop gapped except TWO Weyl points at +-Q, pinned to zero energy by inversion + particle-hole. Charge = chirality sign(v1.(v2 x v3)). Chern numbers of planes jump by the node charge (their Fig. 4). Footnote: "system always remains globally neutral" (total Weyl charge 0). Surface: one Fermi arc between the projected nodes. Bulk C~T^3, arcs C~T; thermal Hall K ~ node separation.
 - Key result: at kappa = (1/2) sqrt(3/5) ~ 0.39 "each WP splits into three" (two of the same chirality, one opposite, chirality conserved locally), arcs 1 -> 3. So nodes 2 -> 6.
 - Large kappa: nodes on the high-symmetry line Gamma-Y recombine at kappa=infinity; nodes on the BZ face do not, instead merge into nodal lines at kappa=infinity.
 - Tilted field only shifts node positions (valid if field has nonzero components on all three axes). A footnote says the discussion refers to the gapless phase around the isotropic point.
 - Caveat they state: the effective model is rigorous only for kappa << 1 (below flux gap); larger kappa is the supplied quadratic model's evolution, not the spin model.
 - Fig. 5 carries a second marker "kappa = 1/2" whose meaning the text does not explain: NOT VERIFIED what happens there.
 - Results are numerical/graphical except the quoted kappa value; no derivation of it appears in the main text, no general-coupling formula.

[2] K. O'Brien, M. Hermanns, S. Trebst, "Classification of gapless Z2 spin liquids in three-dimensional Kitaev models", PRB 93, 085101 (2016), arXiv:1511.05569 (v2, Jul 2016). READ in part (36 pp: abstract, Table II, Sec. V intro/V-A, Sec. IV-C, IV-G on (10,3)b, parts of (8,3)b); not read in full. A PRB 94, 079908(E) (2016) erratum appears in search results; contents not examined.
 - Lattice (10,3)b = hyperhoneycomb: nodal line at kappa=0 -> Weyl nodes under TRS breaking (Table II). Zero-flux sector checked numerically (vison gap ~0.13 J at isotropic point). Phase diagram symmetric under J_x <-> J_y (only x- and y-bonds are lattice-related).
 - Text for (10,3)b: "Weyl points move along the k_z axis up until kappa=(1/2)sqrt(3/5), where four additional Weyl nodes appear"; axis nodes recombine at kappa=infinity; the other four become part of nodal lines at kappa=infinity (one velocity vanishes).
 - General argument that Weyl nodes MUST appear: treat k_z as a tuning parameter; slices cutting the nodal line are 2D gapless Kitaev (-> Chern insulator with TRS breaking), others trivial; the boundary between them is a Weyl point; pairs by periodicity.
 - Other lattices (not ours but relevant as precedent): (8,3)b: two opposite Weyl nodes annihilate at a high-symmetry point at exactly kappa = 3 sqrt3 - 5 ~ 0.196 (the only exact algebraic Weyl critical value I saw besides 1/2 sqrt(3/5)). (8,3)c: line nodes -> six Weyl nodes. (10,3)c (no inversion): 6 pairs, 12 Fermi pockets, nodes off zero energy.
 - For (10,3)b I found NO Weyl-node evolution versus (J_x,J_y,J_z) at kappa != 0; only the kappa=0 phase diagram (Fig. 26).

[3] S. Mandal, N. Surendran, "Exactly solvable Kitaev model in three dimensions", PRB 79, 024426 (2009), arXiv:0801.0229. READ (text).
 - Zero-flux gap closes iff J_z^2 = sqrt(J_x^2+J_y^2+2cos k1 J_xJ_y) * sqrt(J_x^2+J_y^2+2cos k2 J_xJ_y) with a phase condition fixing k3; solutions exist only for the triangle inequalities (J_z<=J_x+J_y etc.). Contour shrinks to the point (0,0) as J_z -> J_x+J_y=2 (their Fig. 3). No TRS breaking, no kappa.

[4] R. Schaffer, E. K.-H. Lee, Y.-M. Lu, Y. B. Kim, "Topological spinon semimetals and gapless boundary states in three dimensions", PRL 114, 116803 (2015), arXiv:1409.5125. READ (text).
 - Zero-flux nodal ring on hyperhoneycomb and harmonic lattices: for J_x=J_y, delta=J_z/J_x, 4cos(k1/2)cos(k2/2)=delta^2 e^{-i(k3-k1/2-k2/2)}; ring exists for delta<2 (= J_z<J_x+J_y); class BDI winding numbers, flat surface bands. kappa=0 only; no Weyl nodes.
 - NOTE: the task named "Schaffer, Lee and Thomale". I found no such paper (no Thomale co-author in anything verified); the Schaffer/Lee paper is with Lu and Kim.

[5] S. S. Jahromi, H. Yarloo, R. Orus, "Thermodynamics of 3D Kitaev quantum spin liquids via tensor networks", Phys. Rev. Research 3, 033205 (2021), arXiv:2011.11577. READ the relevant passage. Finite-T tensor-network study; restates kappa_c=(1/2)sqrt(3/5) citing [1], "from two to six" Weyl points, nodes at (0,0,+-k0) at small kappa, thermal-Hall plateau drops at kappa_c. It uses kappa/K=0.001 for the numerics. NOT independent evidence for the node structure.

=== B. COMPARISON WITH YOUR LIST ===

Agreement
 (i) Weyl count/charges: [1],[2] give 2 nodes at small kappa, 6 after the splitting, each +-1 chirality, local and global neutrality. Matches "six nodes, charges +-1, total 0".
 (ii) Isotropic flip value: your kappa_c^2 = J(J+2)/(4(4+2J-J^2)) at J=1 is 3/20, kappa_c = 0.387298..., identical to (1/2)sqrt(3/5) (checked: both 0.3872983346). Their split-into-three (two same, one opposite chirality) IS a charge flip of the on-axis node (+1 -> -1 +1 +1). This is a strong cross-check IF your kappa has their normalization (kappa~/J_K with unit +-1 coefficients); I could not verify their coefficient conventions beyond the stated u~_jk = -eps_abg.
 (iii) Triangle regime: [3],[4] give the gapless (nodal-line) region exactly as the triangle inequalities, symmetric under J_x<->J_y, as in [2].
 (iv) Qualitative picture: TRS-protected nodal line at kappa=0 (BDI, flat surface band) -> Weyl phase for every kappa>0 (class D, nodes at zero energy by inversion+PH), Fermi arcs 1 -> 3 at the split, large-kappa reappearance of nodal lines for the face nodes. All as in [1],[2],[4].
 (v) The dimensional-reduction proof that nodes must exist [2] is the same Chern-jump picture as your charge census.
