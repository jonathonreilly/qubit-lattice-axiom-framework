# A55 report: a Dirac mass for light's π-flux charges under exact turns

## 1. Verdict
- **No term that respects single coarse steps gives these charges a mass.** I tested every translation-invariant term: on-site terms and hops up to range² 6 (coarse units), with and without time-reversal symmetry. None gives the Kawamoto–Smit nodes a mass (CHECKED; COMPARATOR: the Susskind shift argument). So A52's uniform record background cannot supply a mass, however far its hops reach.
- **Records can supply the mass while the law stays fully covariant (EXACT construction, CHECKED).**
  - The least-structured arrangement I found is the **cyclic texture**, f_i = (−1)^{x_{i+1}}.
  - A covariant function of the records then gives exactly the staggered sign: h_v = Σ_d (f_v·d)[d·(f_v × f_{v+d})] = −12 ε(v).
  - The bands become ±√(4t²Σcos²k + m²), so both tastes get the same Dirac mass.
- **The price has two parts.**
  - Something must tell the two kinds of corner apart. The options are a supplied texture with period 2 coarse (4 fine), a painted two-colouring, or a sublattice chosen spontaneously by a covariant repulsion.
  - The Dirac reading also needs a filled lower band. Without it, a lone charge in empty space is non-relativistic in every sector.

## 2. Answers

### Q1. The standard staggered mass (EXACT; CHECKED)
**Rule.** For a turn x → c + R(x − c) that keeps the role pattern, ε(image) = ε(x)·(−1)^{Σ(c−Rc)}. For a translation by a, the factor is (−1)^{Σa}.

| Centre | Turns that keep the pattern | Keep ε | Flip ε |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corner | 24 | 24 | 0 |
| Cube centre | 24 | 12 (the even-permutation turns, T) | 12 (the C4s and face-diagonal C2s) |
| Link centre | 8 | 4 | 4 |
| Face centre | 8 | 4 | 4 |

- **Translations:** single coarse steps flip ε; steps with an even coordinate sum (the FCC lattice) keep it.
- **So** M_ε = mΣε_v n_v is invariant under exactly the group generated by corner turns, which is the corner turns plus the FCC translations. That is an index-2 subgroup of the role pattern's space group, and M_ε is odd on the other coset.
- **A52's law respects single coarse steps,** so adding M_ε lowers its symmetry.

**n_v is a covariant local operator.**
- Z₂ form: n_v = (1−P_v)/2, where P_v is the product of the six leg Z's. P_v maps to P_gv with 0/6144 mismatches.
- U(1) form: n_v is the divergence G_v; the outward leg field maps to the outward leg field (0/144 failures).
- The corner qubit is not needed.
- In U(1) form the mass is ΣμG = Σ_links(μ_tail − μ_head)E_l, a single-link field term.

**Kawamoto–Smit check.** {hopping, ε} = 0. On 8³ the spectrum is ±√(4Σcos²k + m²) to 3.3e-14, with gap 2|m|.

**Without a pattern?** Only if the turns are limited to those about corners. Under cube-centre turns and single coarse steps, M_ε needs a two-colouring of the corners, and the 2×2×2 role pattern does not supply one.
- COMPARATOR (Susskind): writing q = n − (1−ε)/2 turns Σεn into Σ|q|, but only under the constraint q_v ∈ {0, ε_v}. The pattern moves into the Gauss law rather than disappearing.

### Q2. Mass from records
**A single record cannot do it (EXACT).**
- The 8 diagonals form one orbit under the 24 turns, f and −f included.
- On the corner qubit, the only operators that commute with all 24 SU(2) lifts are multiples of 1 (residual 1.2e-16).
- So any one-record coefficient is a constant, which is a chemical potential, not a mass.

**Lemma S (EXACT).** If the background has a symmetry that swaps the two sublattices, then Σ_v ε_v μ_v = 0 for every covariant μ.

**Two-sublattice backgrounds all fail.**
- 64/64 have such a swap: any two diagonals are exchanged by a half-turn, which combined with one coarse step swaps the sublattices.
- The uniform, alternating ±f₀ and hedgehog f_i = (−1)^{x_i} backgrounds give m = 0 for all 7 functions tried (EXACT; c3).

**Period-2 backgrounds (coarse).**
- With F(000) fixed there are 2,097,152 of them; 99.3% have no swapping symmetry.
- The most symmetric survivors are exactly two mirror images, f_i = (−1)^{x_{i±1}}. Each keeps 12 symmetries (counted modulo shifts by two coarse steps) and acts transitively on each sublattice.
- Each uses all 8 diagonals once per 4×4×4 fine cell, and any covariant μ takes only two values on it.

**The function.**
- Every bilinear invariant I tried gives a staggered part of 0. These were f·f′, (f·d)(f′·d), d·(f×f′), f′·d and the range-√2 handedness.
- The simplest one that works is cubic: h_v above.
- On the cyclic texture, the neighbour along e_i flips one component of f, so every term equals −2f₁f₂f₃ = −2ε. That gives h = −12ε with mean 0; the mirror texture gives +12ε.
- In words: for each neighbour, take the handedness of the turn from my record to its record about the bond, signed by whether my record leans toward that neighbour.

**Checks.**
- Covariance: 0/364 failures, covering 7 functions × 52 elements (24 corner turns, 24 cube-centre turns, 4 translations) on a random background.
- π flux on 8³ with μ = 0.25h on the cyclic texture: the spectrum equals ±√(4Σcos²k + 9) to 5.2e-14, and the gap is 6.000 = 2|m|.
- Controls (uniform, alternating, hedgehog): gap 0.

**Encoded law.** The term Σλh_v[r](1−P_v)/2 is diagonal and covariant, and it commutes with A52's CZ map V. So the records stay invisible to the hops and the ring, and only the mass term depends on them.

### Q3. Other mass mechanisms
**(a) Farther hops (decision 22).** I worked at the free-fermion level and found the allowed terms by group projection, up to range² 6:

| Symmetry kept | Allowed terms (TR-even/TR-odd) | First-order mass at the node | Gap after random scan |
|---|---|---|---|
| translations only | 81 (41/40) | 4.8e-16 | ≤3e-13 |
| all turns + translations | 6 (6/0) | 6.7e-16 | 8e-14 |
| C3 + translations (uniform records) | 29 (15/14) | 4.1e-16 | 2e-13 |
| cyclic-texture stabiliser (control) | 58 | 1.7 | 1.23 (gapped) |

- The scan used 60 random allowed additions of norm 0.6t and 2.5t, then refined the best one.
- Allowed terms move or split the nodes, which lifts the tastes, but never gap them.
- Grades: CHECKED up to range² 6 and couplings of 2.5t; ARGUED beyond that; COMPARATOR: each of the 8 Dirac masses is odd under some single-step shift.

**(b) A flux between 0 and π.**
- A uniform intermediate flux is covariant only if it is tied to the records, φ_p = φ·sign(f·n_p), because turns that reverse a plaquette send φ to −φ.
- With the field along f₀:
  - φ = π/2 and π/3: a quadratic band touching at k = 0 at half filling (splitting grows as k², gap about 1e-16);
  - φ = 2π/3: half filling falls inside a band, so a metal.
- None of these is Dirac-like, and none has a mass (CHECKED at these fluxes).

**(c) The zero-flux sector.** One band, −2tΣcos k. It is massive (m* = 1/(2ta²), isotropic) but non-relativistic, with no antiparticle branch, and it has a Fermi surface at half filling. It is not Dirac.

**(d) Spontaneous mass (ARGUED, mean field).**
- A covariant nearest-neighbour repulsion V(1−P_v)(1−P_w)/4 at half filling gives the gap equation 1 = (Vz/2)⟨1/E_k⟩, so V_c = 0.73t.
- The law stays covariant; the ground state picks one sublattice, so there are two vacua.

**Empty vacuum (EXACT).** A lone charge sits at the bottom of its band.
- In π flux the bottom is E = −2√3t, with m* = √(12t²+m²)/(4t²).
- So a Dirac mass matters only when the Fermi level sits at the node.

### Q4. The price, in plain terms
1. **Something that tells the two kinds of corner apart.** Any one of these works:
   - a painted two-colouring, which breaks covariance on half of the space group;
   - a supplied record texture of period 2 coarse with no sublattice swap. The least-structured one is the cyclic texture, one of a mirror pair; the choice of mirror image sets the sign of m. Its mass coefficient depends on 7 records, the corner and its six coarse neighbours;
   - a spontaneous choice, from a covariant repulsion above about 0.73t (mean field).
2. **A filled lower band (a Dirac sea).** That vacuum choice is not made here.
3. **Doubling stays.** Both tastes get the same mass, and the net handedness is still zero.

A52's uniform background is not enough on its own.

## 4. Open edges
1. "Least structured" is shown only among period-2 backgrounds, measured by residual symmetry. Longer periods and non-periodic textures were not searched.
2. A translation-invariant gap at couplings above 2.5t, or at range² above 6, is argued against but not ruled out.
3. I did not run an encoded many-body check of the mass term (one cube, c3b-style). The result relies on A52's free-fermion identification, n_v = (1−P_v)/2.
4. How the cyclic texture forms (its seed, and copying across a two-step gap) needs an owner reading, as A52's background does.
5. The spontaneous route is Hartree mean field only. Light's Coulomb interaction and gauge fluctuations are left out.
6. The intermediate flux was tested only with the field along (111), at four values.
7. Whether the half-filled vacuum is the intended one is open.

## 5. Plain-language summary
In the π-flux sector the charges move like massless electrons, doubled. Giving them mass needs the grid's corners to come in two alternating kinds, like a chessboard. No rule that respects single steps produces a mass, however far it reaches. Stored diagonals can mark the chessboard: if they follow a particular twisting pattern that repeats every two corners, a simple rule depending on neighbouring records gives exactly the chessboard sign, and the charges become massive Dirac particles. A strong enough repulsion could also pick the chessboard spontaneously. Either way, the Dirac picture needs a filled sea.
