**1. A hard limit on motion (exact, for the premises listed).** Suppose a change is reversible, reaches only next-door sites in one tick, uses one qubit per site with sites combined the ordinary (not the fermionic) way, and treats every site and turn of the grid exactly alike on every tick, with each site's possibilities turning exactly as the grid turns (your Q3). Then nothing can move under it: no record it carries, no light, no matter wave. Something has to give.
- **What does the work (A42, 3 October, exact).** Next-door movement needs two different directions of the possibilities that every symmetry treats exactly alike. Your Q3 rules that out; so does a weaker gluing, and so does any law that treats every direction of the possibilities alike. With one fermion per site nothing moves either. Without gluing, or with a "sign twist" gluing, next-door rules can spread change, but only by favouring particular directions of the possibilities. The repo's archived July census (no claim authority) had the simplest kind of tick; the proofs here cover every reversible next-door tick.

**2. The most coherent way through: "ticks for records, smooth change for possibilities" (the record-tick shape).**
- **How it works.** Records step at most one site per tick by trading places with a neighbour with no record (which needs "records are permanent" to mean "never destroyed, may move", decision 7), and the shared possibilities change smoothly in between.
- **It uses two of the limit's ways out at once:** a smooth change that reaches past next door (faintly, if the change per tick is small), and record steps that cannot be undone. Together they keep records to one site per tick.
- **The price.** Unrecorded influence loses its exact speed limit. A leak always remains, and it stays exponentially faint only if the change per tick is small.
- **Swap or flow (A37, checked).** If nothing is made or destroyed, then whatever the rule, a stepping record sends exactly one spot's worth of possibilities back where it came from (exact). On a single line, for steps that move whole contents, the only tidy way is the swap; your "everything pushes right" needs an endless conveyor that changes things arbitrarily far away at once, or a step that wipes out one spot's worth. On the full grid the possibilities can flow around the record, picking a side at random. Over calm empty space the choice makes no difference; it changes how records move only if their stepping odds look at their neighbours (swap: they bounce back; line push: they keep going; flow: they turn), a memory in what was pushed, not momentum.

**3. Your tick, in that shape (checked).**
- **No more seams.** The possibilities never notice the ticks, so the earlier troubles (mirror seams, freezing while waiting) disappear.
- **Global or neighbourhood.** A tick written into the rule is the same everywhere if the rule treats every place exactly alike (or a checkerboard of two sub-grids half a tick apart: a supplied pattern, unchanged by a one-site shift only if time also shifts by half a tick, which records can faintly tell apart). Ticks can still differ by place without memory if the shared ticks are numbered in a repeating cycle and the records around each spot choose which numbers it uses (A36); the cycle's own count must then be kept somewhere (decision 18), and "one site per tick" (for records that step, decision 7) holds per shared instant. Otherwise set ticks that differ by place need a per-place memory, or fire only when new records form.
- **Influenced.** Ticks chosen by the records right next to a spot feel only those records, not gravity (A36). A tick rate averaged from neighbours' rates would reach further, but needs a stored rate at every place. How often records form can follow gravity's slowing through the chance per tick, but not through the ticks themselves if gravity is carried by the shared possibilities (ticks set that way would let faraway choices steer records, which is ruled out only if you adopt the no-signalling sentence that no faraway choice may change local record odds).
- **Visible?** With small chances per tick that scale with each spot's own interval, the choice is far too faint to see; it shows at full strength if records set how often a spot's tick comes and each tick keeps the same chance.
- **Light.** If records form only next to records, the tick never touches how light crosses empty space.
- **Minimum tick.** Your approved primitive already says one tick is one edge "in form". Nothing tonight derives a smallest tick; what follows is a longest one: if nothing may outrun one site per tick and light goes equally fast every way, the tick must be shorter than light's time to cross a grid step, divided by √3. If the record tick were the primitive's tick (main lists that tie as an open gate), light would outrun records on the diagonals. That is fine in the record-tick shape, where light is not held to one site per tick, but not if one site per tick is to limit everything.

**4. Empty space (exact, within the toys).** If records form only next to records, empty space far from matter is exactly quiet whatever it is made of, and distant light crosses it without ever being recorded there. The price:

## What we found

### 0. Ticks for records, smooth change for possibilities (A27, checked)

**Why it is needed.** The exact limit (section 3) shows that, on one qubit per site and with your Q3, a reversible change held to one site per tick, and exactly symmetric on every tick, moves nothing. The ways out each carry a cost:
- cycling partner pairs (sub-grid privilege);
- a longer but still strict reach (scrambling, in the simplest class);
- more room per site;
- sameness only on average;
- a change with no strict reach at all, only a faint tail;
- for records only, steps that cannot be undone (section 3).

The record-tick shape takes the last two: the faint tail for the possibilities, steps that cannot be undone for records. Its costs are below.

**What A27 found instead.** A27 separates the two kinds of motion.

**Records**
- In this option, they are classical facts that step on ticks by their own rule.
- The step: a record trades places with one neighbour with no record, with odds that are a straight average over what that neighbour holds, or with fixed odds (which ignore the neighbours; that goes against your instinct that moves are decided by the neighbourhood, as formation is (I3). Admissibility's own "varies with" is about which possibility a forming record locks, and a step that keeps its content locks nothing new).
- **Wording it needs.** A record that steps needs "records are permanent" to mean "never destroyed, but may move" rather than "stays on its site" (decision 7). On main, an unaudited July note leaves this open ("Migrating-record semantics — OPEN") and a September note records your reading that records move; nothing is adopted.
- What it satisfies:
  - It never moves more than one site per tick.
  - Its content is kept.
  - It is symmetric under all 24 turns.
  - It is not tied to the grid's directions (my check).
  - When two records want one spot, the spot "claims" one with the right relative odds, and they never share. The literal "both try, then pick by odds" rule leaks faraway choices; the claim rule does not.
- **Not yet checked:** a record that trades places keeps its content, but its new spot's own conditions might not allow that content (for example, a recorded neighbour there may set a different menu). With fixed odds the step ignores this entirely. The claim rule and one of the step weights also look two sites away, one step beyond the nearest neighbours. The record-tick shape also takes menus from records, which is the proposed menu text below, not your Q7 as decided.

**Possibilities**
- They change smoothly and identically everywhere between ticks, holding recorded spots fixed, so records act as fixed weights and walls.
- This change reaches beyond one site in every tick, though only faintly when the change per tick is small. So it takes the "longer reach" way out of the exact limit, in its gentlest form. In the simple toy it moves things without any supplied pattern.
- Below a simple bound on the change per tick, there is no time-doubled twin.

**What you give up**
- **No exact speed limit for unrecorded influence.** A leak beyond one site per tick always remains, and it stays exponentially faint only if the change per tick is small.
- **The change per tick shows up in how records behave** (argued). So "one universal tick or local ticks" now has real content, though at Planck ticks the effects are tiny and the tick becomes nearly undetectable in practice.
- **Steps that look at their neighbours nudge them slightly.** Steps that ignore the neighbours do not.
- **Only slow-particle ripples over the simplest calm background (A31).** Over a calm empty background where every spot points the same way, a change built from pairs of neighbours that treats every spot and turn alike must reduce to one simple form once any record holds the opposite content (exact). Rules with terms on three or more spots of a neighbourhood keep that background calm in more ways (the fourth review's check). Either way, single ripples behave like slow, heavy particles, never like light (exact for single ripples). Over that background, light-like ripples need a fixed pattern of plus and minus signs on the rule (section 10); a background whose spots share their possibilities is partly searched: in tidy backgrounds built from flips, the disturbances it protects stay in flat sheets (A33); other kinds are open.
- **A direction for the calm background.** The empty background stays calm only if every record's content lies along the background's own direction. The rule cannot take that direction from the unrecorded possibilities without leaking faraway choices. So it must either be fixed by the law, which would privilege a possibility, against Qubit's "No possibility is privileged", or passed on from earlier records, and who sets the first one is open.
- **Smaller costs:**
  - overlapping formation spots need an ordering rule;
  - left- and right-handed things still come in equal amounts.

**What it keeps**
- Your records-on-ticks instinct, and one site per tick, exactly for records.
- Campaign 7's change sentence, as written. This holds for the simple toy change; the light-like pieces built tonight (matter in A26, the shape field in A25) still use fixed patterns.
- The gravity field route, with smooth field dynamics and no time-doubled twin. It does not remove the field's fixed pattern of jobs in space (A25; see section 6).

### 1. One site per tick limits the change too (A3)
- If moving records' places always match the odds, the change can carry possibilities at most one site per tick.
- A change that is the same everywhere and runs smoothly through a whole tick leaks past that: about 28% with a full unit of change per tick, falling steeply (about the fourth power) as the change per tick shrinks.
- A smooth change working on one set of neighbour pairs at a time does not leak. So, if records follow the possibilities, what is ruled out is Campaign 7's change sentence as written. The record-tick shape drops that "if", and the sentence comes back. Smoothness is not ruled out, and your "it evolves continuously" can stay.
- But one set of pairs per tick is a supplied pattern. It cannot treat every site and turn alike on every tick (A20). Once records meet, it tells sub-grids apart (section 3).

### 2. A record whose place can be read every tick must not ride the shared possibilities (A7, A19, A27)
- **One way: re-forming.** The record forms afresh at each step, and the shared possibilities change at once to agree with it. That "change at once to agree" is your Q1 wording; the Record axiom itself says only that a record "locks exactly one admissible local possibility", and the repo's own notes say the lock alone does not fix how the other possibilities update. The record-tick shape's step is the other way: the record keeps its own content and trades places, never riding the possibilities.
- **Why (exact, in a small supplied two-record toy).** Otherwise, for some linked states, a faraway choice shows up in the record's path.
- **Consequence:** in these toys, readable moving records behave like dust. They wander, never interfere, and carry no momentum. (The repo's notes of 20 to 26 September use "records" for other supplied movers: classical records that move with detailed balance, a gas of records that travel along their content and conserve momentum, and coherent two-component walkers. Under this section a coherent walker is an unrecorded possibility until something records it, and the inertial gas ties what a record can lock to the grid's six directions. None is adopted.) Things that interfere (electrons, atoms, light) must be unrecorded possibilities that leave a fresh record only now and then.
- **Everyday motion (inertia).** It works only if each record pins the moving thing down loosely, over a stretch much wider than its own ripples.
  - Then the records line up straight and evenly spaced, and a thrown ball qualifies easily.
  - The loose pinning comes from records forming on something small that bumped the thing, as in the textbook account of cloud-chamber tracks (a comparison, not adopted).
  - Records formed straight from the thing's own neighbourhood make it forget its speed.
  - This toy moves things with a fixed round of partner pairs; see section 3.
- **Catch, avoidable: the "time-doubled" twin (A22).**
  - In the ticked toys, a twin that flickers at the tick rate exists exactly when each tick fully swaps a set of partners, which is also what lets light run at the grid's top speed.
  - In that case no interaction that respects the turning of possibilities can remove it. Slow collisions push up to about half (different particles) or nearly all (identical particles) of what they scatter into the twin.
  - With small changes per tick, starting from empty space, no collision of fewer than about 90–200 particles can make a twin (exact). Many-particle production falls off extremely fast (argued from a standard result and a small toy).
  - The price: light runs well below the grid's top speed. A18's package needs small changes anyway in one way of writing its coupling, but not in the other (section 6). The record-tick shape's smooth change has no such twin at all below a simple bound.

### 3. How can anything move at all? (A3, A10, A20, plus my check)
- **The exact limit (A20; exact for the premises listed).** Take any reversible step on one qubit per site, with sites combined in the ordinary quantum way (the repo has not chosen that over the fermionic way; its archive reaches the same conclusion only for number-preserving fermionic ticks), that treats every site and every turn of the grid exactly alike on every tick, with each site's possibilities turning exactly as the grid turns (your Q3). If a site's possibilities can reach only its six neighbours in one tick, the step changes nothing at all. That stops everything under that step, not just records: no record it carries, no light, no matter wave. (A record can still step by an irreversible rule of its own: below, and section 0.)
  - **What does the work.** The proof uses full turning of the possibilities (two of its steps need it). The repo's archived census of the simplest ticks (one-qubit Clifford ticks reaching next door; July, no claim authority) finds only the do-nothing tick when the possibilities turn with the grid, and next-door ticks that spread content when the turns leave the possibilities alone. New tonight: the proof for every reversible next-door tick, without assuming Clifford ticks or a conserved number. Section 15 (A42) settles the other ways of turning and the fermionic way of combining sites: next-door movement needs two directions of the possibilities that every symmetry treats exactly alike. (June notes on main already have this shape for one fermion mode per site: a strict one-step reversible tick does nothing or moves exactly one edge per tick, never hosting mass, and covariant 3D polynomial ticks are flat. The repo also has the same pattern for light on edges and faces: a one-step, norm-preserving tick there moves nothing, and its escapes are more layers, more room, or looser locality. All unaudited.)
- **Ways it can give:**
  - **A supplied pattern.** Cycling the neighbour pairs moves things (A10), but once two records meet, sites can be told apart by which of 8 interleaved sub-grids they sit on. That conflicts with "No site is privileged".
  - **A longer reach.** Fully symmetric rules reaching 4 sites per tick do move things, but what travels is a spreading web of links, not a record or a particle, and no calm empty background survives. This is exact for the simplest class of such rules; rules reaching 2 or 3 sites are untested for one qubit per site (with fields on edges and faces the repo has a tick reaching three sites that treats every turn alike and carries light; unaudited).
  - **More room per site, or sameness only on average.** More room per site changes the Qubit axiom's M₂(ℂ), so it is carried as a named supplied premise (see decision 13); the repo's edge-and-face light tick, three local layers reaching three sites, is an example. Sameness only on average must live with your approved realized-state primitive: a number that would change with the realized schedule is registered data, not a derived result. Both remain possible.
- **For a single record, a step that cannot be undone works** (A1, my check).
  - The record re-locks its own possibility onto one of the grid's six directions, then trades places with the neighbour that way. Its new content points the way it stepped.
  - It is exactly symmetric under all 24 turns. It goes straight 1/3 of the time, turns 2/3 of the time, never steps back, and never stays put. Over many ticks it wanders like dust.
  - **Costs:**
    - Its lockable possibilities are the grid's six directions, which ties forming records to the grid; your Q3 left formation unglued.
    - What a record locks changes at most steps.
    - As written it covers one record only. Two records can step into one site, up to 1 in 9 per tick. Letting a blocked record stay fixes that part, but a rule for two records wanting the same site with no record is not built.
  - **It moves records only.** Light and anything that interferes still need a reversible change, so one of the ways above must give for them. With records stepping by their own rule, section 1's one-site limit on the change no longer applies (argued).

### 4. An exact speed limit, and relativity at low speed, for ticked change (A5)
- Nothing gets further in one tick than the change reaches. That is an exact limit (one site per tick if the change is held to one site), where smooth change only gives an approximate one.
- In a one-dimensional toy, moving clocks slow by Einstein's formula with each kind of matter's own top speed in place of light's, so the amount is Einstein's only at low speed.
- The record-tick shape's smooth change gives up this exact limit for unrecorded influence, and these exact one-dimensional identities with it.
- In 3D this is not secured. Rules giving equal speeds in every direction either split speeds in direct proportion to energy, which gamma-ray burst timing rules out for light if the tick is anywhere near the Planck time, or need a pattern of plus and minus signs painted onto the rule (section 10).

### 5. The tick: shared in practice for ticked change; free and nearly invisible in the record-tick shape (A5, A14, A15, A17, A32)
- **In step:** neighbourhood ticks that keep in step cannot be told apart from one universal tick.
- **Out of step:** if each site keeps its own fixed tick without waiting, the pair between out-of-step neighbours never acts under the natural rule (a pair acts only if both ends pick it). Getting anything through needs a second gate within one step, which breaks the one-site-per-tick limit there, or an unrecorded random choice, which loses coherence. The seam is a mirror wall that records can reveal (ticked change only).
- **Waiting:** if sites wait for each other, no region can keep a different long-run rate, and a ring of sites all waiting on each other freezes everything.
- **Where variation can live:** in these toys, time running differently in different places cannot sit in how often each place's tick comes. It can sit in how much change and record-forming each shared tick brings (for the change, to leading order when the change per tick is small).
- **That amount must vary smoothly.** Set by the records right next to each spot, it jitters badly:
  - it washes out atom interference within a nanosecond;
  - at Planck ticks, it shakes an electron apart within seconds.
- **The averaging needed:** the jitter shrinks only with the width of the region averaged over, and averaging over time does not help (exact). The region needed is a billion or more sites across. A rule using only neighbouring records cannot build that average.
- **What is left open:** a smooth field carried by the shared possibilities, changing by a fixed rule (A17; argued, not built). For it to spread at all, its rule must escape section 3's limit.

**The tick redone in the record-tick shape (A32, checked).** In this option, records form and step on ticks; the possibilities change all the time.
- **The ticks are invisible to the change (exact).** Moving one record event in time changes everything recorded later only in proportion to the chance of a record on that tick and to how much the change does during the shift. If that chance shrinks with the tick's length, every kind of schedule gives the same world in the limit: global or local, regular or random.
- **No mirror walls.** Out-of-step neighbourhoods no longer bounce anything back. In the toy, 82% of a ripple crossed a seam whether the ticks were in step or not, where the ticked rule let nothing through. Nothing waits, so nothing freezes.
- **Global or neighbourhood (exact, construction by construction).**
  - A tick written into the law is the same everywhere if the law treats every place exactly alike (the four axioms do not choose a tick at all; this is what covariance forces once one is written in). A checkerboard of two sub-grids half a tick apart is a supplied pattern: a one-site shift leaves it unchanged only if time also shifts by half a tick (an unnoticeable overall shift), and records can faintly tell its sub-grids apart.
  - Ticks set differently by place need each place to remember its phase (decision 9), unless the shared ticks are numbered in a repeating cycle: then the records around each spot can choose which numbers the spot uses, with no memory at each place (A36, below), though the cycle's own count must then be kept somewhere (decision 18).
  - Random local times are just a thinned-out global tick.
  - Times set by a possibility are just a formation chance.
- **Records can run a little faster across out-of-step regions.** Chains of new records can then run along places whose ticks come one after another, so the strict one-site-per-tick limit softens. If that chance is small, it does so only in proportion to the chance per tick, unless each place remembers the pattern from its last tick.
- **Influenced by gravity.** How often records form may vary from place to place.
  - If clocks that count records are to agree with all other clocks near heavy bodies (your choice), the chance of forming a record per unit of local time must be the same everywhere. Otherwise clocks that count records and clocks carried by the possibilities disagree near heavy bodies, by the full gravitational amount.
  - The simplest way, needing no memory: one global tick, with each place's record chances slowed by gravity's time-stretch.
  - Ticks that themselves run slower in gravity would need each place to keep its own phase (at Earth's surface, taking one grid step per tick as light's speed, neighbouring grid points would drift a full cycle apart about once a year). They also need gravity's slowing to be a fixed, given background. If the slowing is carried by the shared possibilities, as on the gravity-field route, such ticks would let faraway choices steer records (A36; this is ruled out only if you adopt the no-signalling sentence that no faraway choice may change local record odds), and only the chances can follow it.
- **Light never sees the tick (exact, when records form only next to records).** In empty space the ticks act on nothing, so they add no energy-dependent speed and no twisting of light's polarisation. Clocks made of light-like matter slow with motion as Einstein says, with corrections around 10⁻³⁸ at laboratory speeds; in this shape that matter needs section 10's sign pattern.
- **A preference for the grid's own frame.** In a two-band toy, no positive local recording rule makes the count of records follow a moving body's own time, and at most half the count slows. This needs a rule that treats left and right alike; my check found that one that doesn't can do differently. It is unobservable at the recording rates already allowed.
- **Same tick (exact).** Records formed on one tick at different spots never leak faraway choices.
  - If each spot's rule looks only at itself, the order within a tick does not matter at all.
  - If it looks at neighbours, an order or a joint rule is needed, and the difference is tiny (second order in the chance).
- **Minimum distance and minimum tick.** No smallest tick follows from your instincts and the four axioms. The framework already has an approved primitive that grains the emergent tick like the grid step (one tick is one edge "in form"); whether records form on that tick is an open gate on main. What follows is an upper limit (exact, given the premises): for one site per tick to be the universal limit, the tick must be shorter than light's time to cross one grid step, and in 3D shorter than that divided by √3. A tick of exactly one step would sit right at that limit in one dimension and past it in 3D: if the record tick were the primitive's tick, light would outrun records on the diagonals. That is fine in the record-tick shape, where light is not held to one site per tick, but not if one site per tick is to limit everything.
  - A tick near light's crossing time is favoured only if every tick carries a real chance of a record and recording is to run near its fastest. Shorter ticks then slow recording beside records (they do not forbid it), and such chances already freeze slower motion beside records.
  - With small chances the tick is only limited from above.

**Ticks chosen by the surrounding records (A36, checked).**
- **What records can and cannot do.** Records carry no time of their own and do not change between record events, so by themselves they cannot mark a later moment. Ticks set by the unrecorded possibilities would let a faraway choice send a message (exact, given Campaign 7's no-signalling sentence, which you have not adopted).
- **With one shared clock underneath** (the shared ticks numbered in a repeating cycle), each spot can use the instants that its pattern of recorded neighbours selects (exact construction). The result:
  - it is set and differs by neighbourhood;
  - it is influenced by records;
  - it needs no per-place memory;
  - it treats every place and turn alike, though not every instant (the numbered cycle is a pattern in time).

  The costs:
  - your "one site per tick" then holds per instant of the shared clock. Per spot's own cycle, a chain of new records can advance as many sites as the cycle has instants in use (at full chances, three times faster along diagonals; at small chances only slightly faster). Keeping one site per spot's own cycle needs memory;
  - supplied: the cycle's numbering, the table of which patterns use which instants, the range of the rule (the records within one site), a rule for claims across instants, and an order for same-pattern neighbours.

  "Records form only next to records" is the simplest case: records switch the shared tick on or off. There are 10 neighbour patterns up to the grid's turns if only "recorded or not" counts. Counting each record's content there are 57, or 33 if the law cannot tell the two contents apart (a law that favours neither content, which is the repo's reading of "No possibility is privileged"). With contents counted, exactly one pair of patterns are mirror images of each other, so a rule could even time a pattern and its mirror image differently. The repo's September mirror note (unaudited) has the same counts.
- **Without a shared clock,** the only set moments are the moments new records form. Letting those trigger neighbours at once can record a whole region in an instant once the chance per record is high enough (about 0.3 in 3D).
- **Not gravity's slowing.** The influence comes only from records in contact, not from a heavy body farther away. Through a gas of wandering records it could at most reproduce the record-carried slowing of section 6, with its shortcomings (argued). A tick rate that each place averages from its neighbours' rates would reach further, as in unaudited repo notes, but needs a stored rate at every place.
  - With a fixed chance per tick it would make record-counting clocks run at rates set by how crowded their spot is, against your choice, if you make it, that all clocks slow together.
  - With the chance shrinking with the tick, the influence all but vanishes from what records show.
- **Far from all records,** if records form only next to records, nothing sets a tick and nothing forms; the smooth change is the only time empty space has.

### 6. Gravity-like clock slowing: real in toys; record-carried gravity falls short; a field route is mapped (A6, A8, A14, A18, A23, A25, A26)

**The basic effect**
- With a thin gas of wandering records, and clumps that keep swallowing them, nearby clocks slow with the right sign, falling off as one over distance.
- A perfectly swallowing clump stops time inside, and its effect grows with its radius (black-hole-like). Swallowing needs a "stopped" mark that record content alone cannot carry.
- Crowding clumps that don't swallow always leak. In the 3D toys, a clump lasted roughly in proportion to its size or a bit less (about 4 to 9 times longer for 8 times more records, depending on the toy), nothing like a black hole's lifetime growing as mass cubed.

**First mismatches**
- In this toy, the next-order term is half of Einstein's, so Mercury comes out wrong.
- Light bends half as much, unless passing between neighbours is slowed twice over.
- The slowing spreads by diffusion: about 50 micrometres in the age of the universe.
- Gravity's weakness has to be put in by hand.

**A dialled toy package (A18)**
- **What it is:** a shared tick, records averaged over a very large region setting the change per tick, and one exponential shape for the slowing.
- **What it gets right:** for light made of two-site ripples, at the level of rays, the right main light bending, radar delay and Mercury orbit.
- **Costs:**
  - both key numbers are dialled in, not derived;
  - four-site-loop light bends 1.5 times too much;
  - light runs at under 1% of the grid limit;
  - the carriers must not slow themselves;
  - nearest-neighbour versions jitter far too much;
  - if wandering records also hold light back (as they do in A14), the photon-mass limit forces them so sparse that the averaging region must be about the size of our galaxy, so the Sun's own field could not be resolved.

**Why record-carried gravity falls short (argued)**
- With diffusing carriers, a moving body's field survives only in a thin wake behind it, so a moving Earth could not hold the Moon.
- There are no gravitational waves, and no frame dragging (a spinning body twisting nearby paths).
- Records, taken as locked possibilities, carry no momentum, so a crowd of them can only spread out, not ripple. (Under a different supplied clause in the repo's unaudited notes, where a record's content is its direction of travel, records do carry momentum and the gas has sound.)
- Wanderers that fly straight dilute the clock rate as one over distance squared, not one over distance. (The repo's unaudited wind notes get an inverse-square pull from such a gas under a supplied closure, but no one-over-distance clock potential.)

**The field route (A23)**
- **The idea.** Gravity carried by a field of the shared possibilities: a smooth stretch that records never lock, passed neighbour to neighbour by the fixed rule, and pushed by energy and motion.
- **What any such field gets:**
  - a fixed rule in which the field sets the pace leaks no faraway choices;
  - moving bodies get proper fields, not wakes;
  - one-over-distance comes free;
  - noise is tiny;
  - gravity's weakness becomes "matter is very light on the grid" (a mass ratio of about 8×10⁻²⁰ that the approved primitives do not supply).
- **A one-number (scalar) field fails** (argued, against measurements). Tied to matter in the usual ways, it bends light either not at all or only half as much as seen. The full amount needs the grid's rest frame singled out, which tests for a preferred frame rule out. It also gives no frame dragging and the wrong kind of waves.
- **Two numbers per place already bend light fully.** A time-stretch plus one common space-stretch is already enough for the full static bending, as in the repo's unaudited "curvature member" notes. The full shape field is what waves, frame dragging and moving sources need.
- **A "shape" field (how each direction is squeezed and twisted) works at linear order.**
  - If the field's bookkeeping is kept exact (nothing lost at any step, and the field unchanged by relabelling places), the grid's own turns fix its form exactly as in Einstein's linear gravity at long wavelengths (checked, and confirmed by hand twice). The repo already holds unaudited notes that reach the same linearised form on a Regge-style complex and on a staggered tensor field, so this is a re-derivation in tonight's setting, by a new route (the grid's turns plus exact bookkeeping, with no edge-length assumption), not a new result. Not fixed: the ripples' speed compared with light (the repo's notes find the two speeds agree exactly when one kinetic coefficient takes a particular value), gravity's strength, and its sign (attraction goes with the healthy sign of its kinetic term, which is also supplied). At grid scale, direction-dependent corrections are free but tiny.
  - A stepping rule gives two wave polarisations at one common speed, equal to light's only if the two are set together, with no time-doubled twin. With matter tied in (A26), it gives full light bending, and everything whose weight sits at single places falls alike.
- **Conditions:**
  - **A fixed pattern of jobs (A25, checked).**

A 30 September note finds that finite-range fields on that carrier cannot meet the next constraint order within a stated class.

### 7. Empty space must essentially never form records, or every region freezes (A4, A6, A9, A12, A28)
- **Freezing time.** At any steady rate, empty space freezes within about one over that rate, in ticks.
- **The half-filled sea.** The repo's September notes use a half-filled staggered sea as a supplied vacuum; the repo itself says the vacuum is not chosen. In its massless form, a one-tick, straight-average rule that looks only at a site and its six neighbours on the sea's own grid forms false records at least 5 per million times as often as it records a real passing thing. That is the best case for the massless sea (a staggered mass lowers the floor, to about 1.4 per ten million at mass 0.5, but never to zero); an energy-based rule is about 1 in 80. At Planck ticks (massless-sea values):
  - a rule strong enough to record a particle within a second fills empty space within days;
  - a rule weak enough to keep space clear for the age of the universe would need some 70,000 years to record anything.
- **Many-tick rules.** Rules that build up over many ticks, like real detectors absorbing energy, can make false records exponentially rare, but never zero for a rule that ignores records. They need a small memory at every site, which the axioms do not provide. A memory made of matter is not ruled out.
- **A hint from the sky.** Light from distant galaxies arrives without forming records on the way.
  - Letting records form only next to existing records would explain this and keep empty space exactly quiet. The cost: a grid with no records would never get any.
  - A rule that simply ignores light would explain the first but not the second.
- **Records form only next to records (A28, checked).** Tested inside the record-tick shape.
  - **What it gives:**
    - empty space far from every record never forms one, whatever kind of emptiness it is (exact);
    - light crossing it is never recorded and no recording rule acts on it there; it changes only through its links to records formed elsewhere (your Q1), and on average not at all;
    - no faraway choice can steer it, because the rule looks only at the records next door, and those always agree with the spot they sit on;
    - records spread at most one site per tick, provided the rule looks at the records present at the start of the tick and there is one shared tick.
  - **The problem moves to the skin of matter.** Next to any record, the half-filled sea always has some chance of being recorded (exact for the simplest half-filled sea on an infinite grid; the fourth review's check confirms it for the repo's staggered kind; small periodic grids in the repo's notes do have exact zero-odds record patterns). So records creep into it, and each new one heats its surroundings, far beyond what is seen if the rule's energy scale is the Planck scale (argued). Next to matter, empty space would have to be the tidy, lined-up kind, or recording there would have to build up slowly over many ticks. My check reproduced the key numbers exactly.
  - **Other costs:**
    - a world with no records never gets any, so the world must start with some (your approved realized-state primitive leaves which records it starts with to the realized world; whether a law under which a grid with no records never gets any "privileges" that state is your call);
    - whether everything fills up and stops depends on one more choice, namely whether a spot fully surrounded by records may still form one;
    - in dense transparent matter such as glass or water, light escapes recording only if records are point-like rather than nucleon-sized, recording is weak, or the rule ignores light.
  - **One encouraging case, with a catch.** With a tidy emptiness and records whose content lies along its direction, recording stops once there is nothing left to record, while records keep moving forever: no freezing, no thinning. But this needs stepping odds that let records into calm space, so fully recorded regions then slowly dissolve (section 9). And if record contents are the opposite of the emptiness, every step that brings records together or apart changes the energy by a grid-scale amount.

### 8. Handedness, the chirality problem (A1, A2, A11)
- **Single free particles:** on a uniform grid, steps of strictly limited reach always pair each left-handed mover with a right-handed one. This is exact, resting on one standard algebra theorem reached two ways. Interactions and record edges were not tested in this campaign's toys; the repo's unaudited dynamics-clause and record-wall notes test some of them and find handedness only as paired crossings, or as a layer-only index set by the record pattern.

- **Circulation:** on a flat 2D toy, a round of trades circulates possibilities one way around every recorded region. In 3D with all 24 turns, a rule-level cycle of trades leaves nothing on flat faces or straight edges (a recorded pattern can still supply a direction, as in the repo's unaudited carved-network notes); slanted edges are open.

### 9. Your black-hole idea under the record-tick shape (A30, checked)
- **What "nothing happens" means there (exact, within the record-tick shape):**
  - no record forms or moves inside a full region (read literally, "records are permanent" already keeps every record in place; stepping records are your reading, decision 7);
  - the possibilities inside never change;
  - nothing from outside can ever reach in (for records and possibilities; gravity's field may still cross, depending on decision 21 and on more room per place, a named supplied premise; see decision 13);
  - if time is the piling-up of records, the region has used up all its time.
- **Sealing is possible as a choice.** If a record may step only into a neighbouring spot that already holds something like its own content, and the region's contents are the opposite of calm empty space, the region never loses a record (exact).
  - The price: a lone record in empty space can never move either, and records stop moving through calm space altogether, so section 7's "records keep moving forever" does not hold under it. It is also the stepping rule that fits the stricter reading of "admissible" (decision 15; the axiom text uses "admissible" for what a forming record locks).
  - With fixed odds, or any chance of stepping into calm space, the region slowly dissolves, in a time that grows with its size far more slowly than a black hole's lifetime would. If its contents are the opposite of calm space, each record that leaves also changes the energy by a grid-scale amount. Two other stepping rules seal flat or box-shaped regions (A30).
- **Grey, not black (exact, checked).** Its edge catches part of whatever hits it.
  - In a one-line toy, at the catching strength that is best for the fastest things, how much is caught depends only on the speed of what arrives: things near the grid's top speed are caught almost always, slow things mostly bounce. A strength tuned for slow, heavy things catches them much better (up to 99% in the fourth review's check) but other speeds less; no fixed strength catches the slowest things.
  - Catching harder makes it reflect more, not less (the edge starts acting like a mirror).
  - No fixed catching rule catches everything at every speed (proved in a one-line toy), and the records' own pull on what arrives can cap catching at two-thirds.
  - A deeper, graded catching layer helps for all but the slowest things (one-line toy); in a flat 2D toy a spongy edge caught about 10% more of the slower waves tested, with a third fewer records.
  - Anything records never lock (such as a never-recorded gravity field, or light under a rule that ignores light) is never caught at all.
- **Assumes the swap step.** The sealing and capture results in this section assume records step by swapping; for push or flow steps (section 11) they would need redoing (A37 errata).
- **A library, not a vault.** Whatever is caught becomes a permanent record on the skin, in plain view, layered in the order it arrived. A black hole in Einstein's gravity hides what fell in (a comparison, not adopted).
- **No glow.** A sealed region gives off nothing. No rule found here makes it glow with a temperature that depends on its size, as black holes are expected to (a comparison, not adopted).
- **Would Einstein's gravity call it a black hole?** If each recorded spot carries ordinary mass and that mass pulls (which needs more room per place, or gravity places that are never recorded), and if the grid spacing is the Planck length, any packed region bigger than about 10⁻²⁶ m (about 14 kg, at one nucleon per spot) would lie inside its own horizon (a comparison from memory, not adopted; the repo lists "spacing equals the Planck length" as an open question, and its older lattice-floor notes argue no horizon forms at all). But the gravity field route is only worked out for weak gravity, so it cannot yet describe a horizon.
- **Do clocks stop inside?** That depends on choices for you and on strong gravity, which is not worked out.
  - If each spot holds only its one qubit's possibilities (the axioms as written), there are two cases. If records may form at every place, a full region has nothing left to carry gravity, so everything stops; but then its contents do not pull as ordinary mass, gravity ripples bounce off it, and ordinary matter would wall off gravity ripples too, which detected ripples rule out if matter carries about one record per nucleon (A31). If gravity's places never hold records (decision 21), no region can be fully recorded, and gravity runs through it.
  - If each spot can also hold a never-recorded part for gravity ("more room per place": a named supplied premise; see decision 13), gravity's clocks keep running inside, only slower, as long as gravity there is weak. For any region big enough to be black-hole-like, gravity is strong, and the field route cannot yet say whether clocks stop (open).

### 10. Putting the record-tick shape together (A31, checked)

- **What holds up: records.** They sit still or trade places with a neighbour with no record (reading "permanent" as "never destroyed, may move", your 20 September reading; decision 7), never pile onto one spot, cannot be steered by faraway choices, and empty space far from records stays exactly quiet.

Among rules built from pairs of neighbours that treat every spot and turn alike, only the plain "line up with your neighbour" rule keeps it calm once any record holds the opposite content. - **Over that background, light-like matter with one internal state per place needs a painted-on sign pattern.** A fixed pattern of plus and minus signs on the neighbour links, the standard lattice trick, gives light-like ripples (in the repo's unaudited staggered notes this is the one unforced "flux −1" choice, an open gate outside the axioms).   - **Signs on the whole rule.** Records can tell neighbouring sub-grids apart once things interact.
  - **Signs on the hopping only.** This can be hidden from records only if a record's step carries the same signs, so the step itself must know the pattern. The rule then also favours one direction of the possibilities, and under your exact-turn reading (Q3) 16 of the 24 turns are no longer treated alike.

If it behaves differently on the background's different sub-grids (a different "parity" under the half-turn), the grid's symmetry no longer forbids the background's own 8-fold pattern from supplying the twist (exact symmetry statement, my check; not built, A33).
- **New: gravity's places never hold records, if gravity lives in the one qubit per place (exact consequence).** If the field lives in each place's one qubit and records never lock it, the places carrying the field can never be recorded. As built, every place carries part of the field, so this would forbid every record. It needs a one-qubit version of the field that leaves some places free (not built), or more room per place (a named supplied premise; see decision 13). Otherwise records wall the field off: with about one record per nucleon, gravity ripples in the Earth would die out within about 70 m (argued), yet ripples that crossed the Earth have been detected (a comparison, not adopted). Under this rule no region can be fully recorded, so section 9's "everything stops" picture cannot arise.

  - the field's bookkeeping can hold only on average, never exactly for every state, if records are to register anything;
  - the time-stretch that paces the field would have to be a moving part of the field, so each place holds more (argued);
  - record chances must also slow with the time-stretch, so record clocks run slow in gravity like everything else.

### 11. Swap or flow: your picture of records pushing the possibilities (A37, checked)
- **The one fixed fact (exact).** Each spot always holds exactly one spot's worth of possibilities. So if no possibility content is made or destroyed, when a record steps from one spot to the next, net exactly one spot's worth must end up back on the side it came from, whatever the rule. Pushing versus swapping only decides which content lands where. My check confirmed this for random rules of that kind: always exactly one qubit's worth. Rules that wipe out or freshly make a spot's worth escape this, at the costs below. (An archived July note, with no claim authority, already uses the standard measure of such net flow, the GNVW index, for a shift of qubits.)
- **On a single line (exact for steps that move whole contents).** If nothing is made or destroyed and nothing jumps more than one site, the only rule is the swap, apart from shifting the whole line, records and all, which changes nothing and is impossible once a second record is on the line.
  - Your "everything pushes right" as a push across every cut is that whole-line shift. As a push ahead of the record only, it is an endless conveyor: everything ahead shifts and a fresh spot appears behind. That changes things arbitrarily far away at the same moment, breaking every speed limit, and it needs an endless line with no records on it.
  - The other options are a push that sends one piece back several sites, or a push that wipes out one spot's worth each step.
- **On the full grid (exact, checked).** The possibilities can flow around the record: front to side, back along the side, and into the spot the record left.
  - It passes the same checks as the swap (one site per tick for the record, no faraway leaks, nothing made or destroyed), reaches at most two sites, and shares the swap's open questions (whether the moved content suits its new spot; energy near excitations).
  - In 3D no side is special, so unless the record's own content picks the side (which would tie records to the grid's directions), the rule must pick one at random. That noticeably blurs linked possibilities nearby: where a linked piece went is spread over the sides (A37). In 2D (a toy remark only; the axioms are 3D) an "always turn left" rule is allowed.
- **When it makes a difference.**
  - In calm empty space: no difference at all (exact).
  - If records' stepping odds ignore their neighbours, records move exactly the same way under every rule (exact): no drag and no momentum.
  - If the odds look at the neighbours, it matters. A record that steps onto something leaves it behind under swap, so it tends to bounce back. A push along the line shoves it ahead, so the record tends to keep going (or, with the opposite preference, gets hemmed in), but on a line such pushes need a long jump back, an endless conveyor or wiping something out. The tidy flow around the record on the full grid puts it to the side, so the record tends to turn instead (the review's check; with an "always turn left" rule in 2D it circles).
  - That is a memory left in what was pushed, not true momentum.
  - Later records can show where linked possibilities ended up, and on a line nothing can slip past a pushing record.
- **Clashes.** "One wins with its relative probability" still works for pushing records, but only if the law settles each tangle of overlapping pushes at once. Combining separately computed odds lets faraway choices leak. Tangles can chain across many records, so that settling rule can reach far and needs small chances (argued); with an endless conveyor every pair of records on a line clashes.
- **Not redone:** section 9's sealing and capture results assume the swap step; under a push or flow step they would need redoing (A37 errata).

### 15. Daytime follow-up (3 October): what the hard limit really needs, and two-part matter (A42, A43; each checked by me)
