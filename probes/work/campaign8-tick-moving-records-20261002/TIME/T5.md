## Short answers (for the owner)

1. **Does the repo force a clock, rate, tick or schedule from the four axioms?** No. In this era it proves the opposite, repeatedly: the axiom memo itself says Admissibility "does not ... define a time metric, or provide a record-production process", and the August to September notes show exact cases where the axioms leave the formation probability per step, the schedule (parallel versus sequential) and the time unit free. Every clock/rate note I read supplies its clock and says so (the scope-only notes say the same in their claim scopes). The "forced from smaller axiom sets" derivations are not in this theme; I found two pointers for the other reviewers (section 3, chain 7, and Top finding 1).
2. **Global versus local tick; ticks following gravity; stored phase; clock from records alone.** The repo has results on all four, but built on a different premise from the night's: a *supplied local rate field* (positive rates `w_x` at each place) or *supplied per-site memoryless clocks*. Details and verdicts in section 4.
3. **"Ticks are a basic unit and hence alike" (uniform).** Fits: the approved kinetic-isotropy primitive, the uniform-lapse closure, the "a global (non-local) clock is exact, a local one fails" result (with a caveat: that exact global clock is a configuration-wide time change, not a unit clock), and every "any clock gives one law" theorem. Assumes something else: the whole rate-field / "gravity clock" campaign (about 30 notes), the ruler/Regge notes, and the neighbour-count clocks. Under uniform ticks those notes collapse to their trivial case.

### Chain 1 - "Do the axioms force a clock?" (answer: no; exact witnesses say they do not)
1. **Axiom text** (T5-01). Lattice: Z^3, translations, proper cubic rotations. Qubit: M_2(C), no possibility privileged. ## 5. Top findings (plain language)

1. **The repo does not force a clock; it proves the axioms leave one free, and the axiom memo says so itself.** In this period every note supplies its own clock, rate or schedule and states that the axioms do not choose a time parameter, a formation rate or a record-production process. Exact examples (history copy of 08-11): a per-step chance of 1/3 and of 2/3 give the same content odds; forming two neighbouring records together or one after the other gives different correlations (0 versus 3/5); the time unit is arbitrary. Where note titles say "forces" or "the axiom text admits", their claim scopes are narrower (for example `NO_MASTER_CLOCK` gives only a first-order linearization). The derivation that came closest to forcing a tick is archived: July notes that selected "one edge per tick" for a supplied unitary tick, using the pre-2026-08-05 Admissibility wording that no longer holds.
2. **Records alone cannot make a clock, and the repo says it three times.** June: record histories give an order, not a time (any increasing tau fits). September: a clock that looks only at neighbouring records is constant between events; "time as record count" has no rate law. A working clock in the repo is always a stored register that is supplied (finite, it recurs, and exact permanent capture at a fixed finite time is excluded for closed unitary evolution). This agrees with the night.
3. **Almost all the repo's clock work assumes ticks that differ by place, and a uniform tick removes that assumption.** About 30 notes rest on a supplied local rate field; under "ticks are alike" they reduce to the trivial case (no clock gradient). Two kinds of "local rate" must be kept apart: a memoryless formation rate (equivalent, by standard thinning, to a uniform tick with a local chance per tick, which matches the night) and a rate that scales the amount of change per tick (exact only to leading order in discrete ticks). The night's claim that, if gravity is carried by the shared possibilities, only chances and not ticks can follow it, has no counterpart in the repo: there the rate field itself carries the gravity-like clock.
4. **"Alike" is not enough for extended structure; "coordinated" is.** The repo's results favour a shared clock or order (a global, non-local clock is exact where a neighbourhood clock fails, though that global clock is a configuration-wide time change and not a unit clock; a uniform lapse closes exactly; any clock on a causal predecessor graph gives one law) over independent identical clocks (which nucleate everywhere at density 1/7 and cannot build extended supports). But a global order on an infinite lattice has no first element, so it describes an order and not a process that starts from empty space, matching the night's "the world must start with some records".
5. **Tick size: only an upper bound exists, and the night's new lower bound clashes with it if one tick must do both jobs.** The repo has the upper bound in Courant form (the light-carrying leapfrog tick needs h < 1/sqrt(3) of an edge per light time) and an open gate for "record tick = one edge" that it calls a naming convention. It has nothing like "records never outrun light, so tau >= a/c". Together the two bounds say the light-carrying update and the record tick cannot be the same tick; the record-tick shape (records tick, possibilities change smoothly between) separates them.
