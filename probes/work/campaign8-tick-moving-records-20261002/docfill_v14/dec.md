## Decisions for you: the full list (0 to 28)

0. **The overall shape (A27).** Do you want "ticks for records, smooth change for possibilities"? Records step one site per tick by their own symmetric swap rule, and the shared possibilities change smoothly in between. The price is giving up an exact speed limit for unrecorded influence: it leaks past one site per tick, and the leak stays exponentially faint only if the change per tick is small. It brings its own choices: how much the change does per tick, how often records step and with which weighting, the claim rule (which looks two sites away), and an order for overlapping formation spots. Decisions 1, 2 and 8 then mostly resolve in its favour. Decision 12 does not yet: over the calm, all-pointing-one-way background, light-like ripples cannot be made without a painted-on pattern (A31; decision 17).
1. **Change within a tick.** May the change run smoothly within a tick on one set of neighbour pairs? That pattern of pairs is supplied structure: it cannot treat every site and turn alike on every tick (A20). Archived July results, with no claim authority, already found that uniform pair changes leak at every nonzero time or move nothing, and that rounds of partner pairs never look the same after every one-site shift.
2. **Reach of the change.** Choose between:
        - strictly one site per tick, in which case nothing moves if every tick treats every site and turn alike (A20);
        - a longer reach (4 sites works but scrambles; 2–3 untested);
        - a faint longer reach (the record-tick shape takes this one: the change reaches everywhere, faintly).
3. **What moves.** Records re-formed at every step (classical wanderers), or unrecorded possibilities that leave a fresh record only when one forms (interference survives)?
4. **Empty space.** Does it form records on its own? Keeping regions from freezing says no, which puts the half-filled-sea vacuum (the 3 September panel's preferred vacuum; in the landed notes only a supplied reference, never adopted) in doubt unless a many-tick rule with memory is allowed. One way to say no: records form only next to records (A28). That brings its own choices: the world starts with some records; the rule looks at the records present at the start of each tick; you decide what a fully surrounded spot does; and the emptiness next to matter is tidy, not half-filled. Under that rule every forming site has a recorded neighbour. This bears on your Q4 (equal odds at an uninfluenced site; your reading of "No possibility is privileged"): if "uninfluenced" means "no recorded neighbour", it would never apply to an actual formation; if it means "nothing tilts the odds", it still applies next to records (argued, A28).
5. **Time.** Does it count every formation (unbounded, stored in no record) or only new records (bounded)? (Unaudited repo notes already find that record order and count do not fix a time metric or rate.)
6. **Menu wording.** The proposed text above.
7. **Permanence.** Does "permanent" mean "never destroyed, may move next door (by re-forming, or by trading places as in the record-tick shape)"? That is Record-axiom wording, so it needs your exact text. Main already carries your 2026-09-20 reading that records move as a supplied clause, not axiom text, next to a site-tagged reading in which a migrating record is "an untested alternative". Under the six-direction step, what a record locks also changes at most steps.
8. **How records step.** By their own irreversible rule with a six-direction menu, so the content points the way the record stepped? That is symmetric under the 24 turns, but it ties lockable possibilities to the grid (your Q3 left formation unglued) and still needs a rule for two records wanting one site. Or by a supplied sub-grid pattern (A10)? Or by trading places with a neighbour with no record and keeping its content (the record-tick shape, A27)? That one is not tied to the grid; the open item is whether its content suits its new spot (decision 15).
9. **Memory.** Build-up-over-time formation, local ticks and smoother pacing all need a small memory at each site (A12, A15, A17). A memory besides the possibilities would be more room per place, which is carried only as a named supplied premise (see decision 13). Under the standing default, may that memory be held by records instead (neighbouring records, or matter), and for ticks, would a numbered shared cycle (A36) do?
10. **Pacing.** When neighbours' change per tick differs, does passing between them go at the product (full light bending), the average, or the slower of the two (half bending)? Are the carriers of the slowing slowed themselves (A14, A18)?
11. **Direct records.** May a lone moving thing form records straight from its own neighbourhood? If so, it loses all inertia (A19).
12. **How unrecorded things move.** Light and matter waves need a reversible change that moves something (section 3). Which gives: longer reach, a supplied pattern, or sameness only on average? (More room per site would also work; it is a named supplied premise, see decision 13.)
13. **A gravity field.** May the shared possibilities carry a never-locked "shape" field that the fixed rule passes along? May the gluing to the grid tilt from place to place as part of that field? Must a forming record leave energy unchanged (A23)? May the places carry a fixed 2×2×2 pattern of jobs for it, one of 8 layouts that never changes (A25)? If matter is tied to it (A26): is weight carried in links classed as weight rather than motion, and is the in-block shuffle allowed? (As built, the field needs more distinguishable settings per place than one qubit gives. More room per place touches the parked Qubit-domain entry in the deferred-decisions registry, whose standing default is one qubit per place, so it is carried only as a named supplied premise; under the default, the open route is a one-qubit version of the field, for example a collective ripple of many places.)
14. **When records form.** Only on caught, settled things, slowly compared with the tick (A24)? Under the gravity field this is needed for almost all records (others only extremely rarely), and it also gives straight tracks (A19).
15. **A moved record's new spot.** Under the record-tick shape's swap step, a record carries its content to a new spot whose own conditions might give that content zero odds. Should a step be allowed only where the content is on the new spot's menu (A29)?
16. **The calm background's direction.** Under the record-tick shape, empty space stays calm only if every record's content lies along its direction. Is that direction fixed by the law (which would privilege a possibility, against Qubit's "No possibility is privileged"), or passed on from a first record (A29)?
17. **Light and matter under the record-tick shape.** Over the calm, all-pointing-one-way background, no change that treats every spot and turn alike gives single ripples that move like light (exact). Light-like matter with one internal state per place needs a painted-on sign pattern (A31); in the repo's unaudited staggered notes this is the uniform "flux −1" choice, which the four axioms do not force. Either it goes on the whole rule: records can show it; under your exact-turn reading (Q3) it cannot keep the turns the field's pattern keeps, and tying the two together leaves 12 of 24 turns; and no simple way to give that matter a mass was found. Or it goes on the hopping only: one direction of the possibilities is favoured, against your Q3 for 16 of 24 turns, and records' steps must carry the signs. Under the repo's reading of the turns (each turn up to a local phase at every place) the pattern keeps all 24 turns. A tidy background that supplies the pattern itself was searched and not found (A33): in tidy backgrounds built from flips with one condition per place, a disturbance the background protects can at best move within flat sheets. An ordinary ripple that behaves differently on the sub-grids of an 8-fold patterned background remains possible in principle (decision 24). Matter whose internal parts turn with the grid can avoid a painted pattern: a supplied walk already in the repo (unaudited) gives neutral two-part matter eight exactly round crossings, four of each hand. Its two parts flip sign under a full turn, so they can never be a local disturbance of qubits, however many per place (exact, A43). With one qubit per place they appear only as half-pieces of a highly entangled background with a hidden field of its own (exact at the simplest level of approximation; no rule is known to produce that background); with fermionic room per place (a named supplied premise, see decision 13) the walk appears directly. Charged matter of that kind so far gives only near-misses (A41). So: if light-like matter uses a painted sign pattern, under which reading of the turns, and which version, if either? Is a fixed pattern acceptable at all, given that the record-tick shape was chosen to avoid one for records?
18. **What a tick means.** Is "records form at set ticks" a physical statement or bookkeeping? Ticks that are all alike need no count: the law just includes the gap between them. Ticks that come in a repeating pattern (a round of partner pairs, sub-grids taking turns, a numbered shared cycle) need the position in the pattern kept somewhere: in the law itself (a built-in clock), in a stored counter that is not a record (the axioms say "A state is a configuration of records"), or in an ever-growing front of fresh records (an archived July note shows a repeating round needs one of the last two when the law has no clock). The repo names a shared, uniform tick the uniform-clock clause: declared, never adopted (section 16). And is the record tick your primitive's "one tick is one edge in form" (an open gate on main), or shorter? If you keep set ticks, A32 lists the choices (in the record-tick shape, with small record chances per tick, any schedule shows in the records only in proportion to that chance):
        - does the chance per tick scale with the tick's length?
        - one global tick, or neighbourhood ticks that need each place to remember its phase?
        - which time-stretch paces a record's step between places of different gravity (through the chances, if the time-stretch is part of the shared possibilities)?
        - do same-tick neighbours see each other's new records?
        - does a forming site look at the records present at its own tick (then chains of new records can outrun one site per tick), or at the previous tick's (a memory)?
        - does recording follow each place's own time (needed for gravity if record-counting clocks are to agree; only partly possible for motion)?
        - if ticks are to differ by neighbourhood without memory, is numbering the shared ticks in a repeating cycle acceptable (A36)? The records around each spot would then choose which numbers that spot uses. Without it, set ticks are the same everywhere, need memory, or fire only when a neighbouring record forms (which can record a whole region at once).
        - does "one site per tick" refer to the shared clock's instants, or to each spot's own cycle (which needs memory)?
        - may new records trigger their neighbours at the same moment?
        - on the gravity-field route only the chance of forming records, not the ticks themselves, can follow gravity's slowing, given the no-signalling sentence that no faraway choice may change local record odds, which you have not adopted (A36, review). Is that acceptable for your "influenced" tick?

        This is exploration of your instinct, not a position.
19. **Sealing records in.** May a record step only into a neighbouring spot that already holds something like its own content (A30)? That lets a fully recorded region last forever, and it fits the stricter reading of "admissible" (decision 15). But a lone record in calm empty space could then never move, and records would not keep moving forever (section 7). The alternative, fixed odds (A31's preference), lets records move but makes every fully recorded region slowly dissolve.
20. **Recording light next to records.** May light-like possibilities be recorded next to existing records? A fully recorded region is dark to light only if so (A30), but in glass or water that recording must be very weak if records are nucleon-sized (A28).
21. **Gravity's places.** If gravity's field lives in one qubit per place and records never lock it, may the places carrying it be ones that never hold records (A31)? As built, every place carries part of the field, so this needs a one-qubit field that leaves some places free (not built), and it is a fixed pattern of record-free places, held in the state rather than the law. Under it, no region can ever be fully recorded. Otherwise records wall off gravity ripples, which detected ripples rule out if matter carries about one record per nucleon.
22. **Pairs only?** Should the change be built from pairs of neighbours only? With three-spot terms, the calm, aligned emptiness allows more rules, including a handed one, though none tried makes single ripples move like light (fourth review). (Campaign 7's sentence 2 allows terms on a site and its six neighbours together. The repo's unaudited pair-only classification leaves three couplings when possibilities turn with the grid, as in your Q3, and only the plain exchange if the change must also treat both sites' possibilities alike when they are turned together.) For the half-particle background, A49 found no rule within one place and its six neighbours, and none reaching across a square or cube, that makes it the calmest state (section 15). Your answer (4 October): yes, explore rules that reach beyond a place's six neighbours (an exploration scope; no clause text adopted). A51: no reach found makes the background the calmest state (section 15).
23. **A shared-possibility background?** A background whose spots share their possibilities, instead of all pointing one way, gives light-like ripples with the plain rule and no painted pattern (a comparison from magnets, not adopted). But next to matter it gets recorded and heats (A28). Is it worth exploring? A39: with the plain rule, if records can sense its energy it cannot be exactly quiet; section 13 lists the ways out. A43 (section 15): on one qubit per place, such an entangled background is also the only way to get the two-part walk's clean crossings, as half-pieces that bring a hidden field of their own. A44 (section 15): for the next-door rules the grid allows, ordered magnets beat that background; it would need richer rules that spoil magnetic order. A46 and A47 (section 15): ring rules, a next-nearest coupling and the light-like extra hop do not change that; ordered magnets still win or tie. A49 (section 15): working backwards, no simple rule inside one place's neighbourhood, or reaching across a square or cube, makes it the calmest state either. A51 (section 15): with farther-reaching rules it beats every rival tried under its own best rule, but on a 16-place grid the calmest state sits far below it. Would matter and light coming out of the same shared empty space, in the spirit of your Q1, fit your picture?
24. **An 8-fold patterned background for matter.** May matter's empty background carry an 8-fold pattern held in the state (1 of 8, like the field's jobs), so that its ripples can behave differently on the different sub-grids (A33)? The grid's symmetry then allows the background itself to supply the twist light-like matter needs, possibly with the same pattern as the gravity field (which would also need more room per place: a named supplied premise; see decision 13). Not built yet. Whether it gives light rather than heavy matter is open, and records might still show the pattern (argued). It would trade a pattern painted onto the law for a pattern held in the state. A related choice (A33): may the rule carry a uniform minus sign on one kind of term? A33's in-sheet twist needs one. A38: if each spot simply points one way, the only turn-respecting 8-fold pattern (under your Q3) has no half-turn holding a spot fixed, so this route needs spots that share their possibilities, or more room per place.
25. **Heavy or light from that route.** If an 8-fold patterned background gives ripples the twist but they come out heavy, would heavy matter from this route be welcome (it supplies the mass the painted version lacked), or is light the point (fifth review)?
26. **What matter is.** Should matter be disturbances the background protects, which would need a background with more conditions than places (blocked so far on one qubit per place), or ordinary ripples with sub-grid labels (fifth review)?
27. **How a stepping record moves the possibilities (A37).** By swapping (the simplest), by flowing around it with a random side (full grid only), or by a push that sends one piece back several sites? Should records' stepping odds look at their neighbours? That is the only case where the rules differ for how records move: swap makes them bounce back, a line push makes them keep going, and flowing around makes them turn. Which of these, if any, matches your "neighbourhoods can shift", or did you mean shifting a whole group of records together as a block (the 3 September "neighbourhood tick", already in an unaudited repo note), which is a different thing from flow?
28. **Exact turns, or turns up to a local relabeling?** Your Q3 has the possibilities turn exactly with the grid. The repo's light and matter notes allow each turn to come with a local relabeling at every place. Under exact turns: a painted sign pattern costs half the turns (section 10), a one-part charged hop through light's links is impossible (section 14), and light's charges can never be electron-like for the simple ways of dressing their hops (section 15). A48 (section 15) sorted out what the looser reading means. A fixed relabeling pattern is your exact turns written in other labels, so all three costs stay. The only genuinely looser option lets some places' possibilities stop turning with the grid, a step back from Q3 at those places. That removes the first two costs (for the sign pattern, sideways steps must also combine up to an overall sign) but not the third. So the choice is really: keep Q3 at every place, or let some places' possibilities stop turning? Your answer (4 October): keep exact turns everywhere, for now.
