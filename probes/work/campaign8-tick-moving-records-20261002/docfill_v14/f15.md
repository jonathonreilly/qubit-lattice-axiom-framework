## 15. Daytime follow-up (3 October): what the hard limit really needs, and two-part matter (A42, A43; each checked by me)
**What the hard limit really needs (A42, exact).** Take a reversible tick on one qubit per site that reaches only next-door sites and treats every site and turn exactly alike on every tick.
- **The deciding question.** Next-door movement needs two different directions of the possibilities that every symmetry treats exactly alike: one that the neighbours feel, and one that the tick turns into it. With no such pair, nothing moves (exact).
- **Under each way the turns can act on the possibilities:**
    - your full gluing (Q3): the tick does nothing at all (the hard limit);
    - the weaker "axis" gluing: a site may turn about one special diagonal direction of its possibilities depending on its neighbours, but nothing ever leaves its neighbourhood;
    - the "sign twist", or no gluing: next-door rules that spread change exist (my check confirmed one, reaching 7, 13, 43, 49, 79 and 85 sites over six ticks), but each favours particular directions of the possibilities, and what spreads is a scrambling web, not a particle.
- **A law that treats every direction of the possibilities alike** (a law-level reading of "No possibility is privileged", which sits in a parked registry entry, so it is a named supplied premise here): every next-door tick does nothing, under every way of gluing. A family of twelve turns is already enough.
- **One fermion per site** (the fermionic way of combining sites): nothing moves next door under any gluing, whether or not particles are conserved.
- **So** the limit rests on "No possibility is privileged" plus next-door reach plus exact sameness on every tick; your Q3 is one way to get it, not the only one. The ways out in section 3 are unchanged, and the record-tick shape's pair (a faint longer reach for the possibilities, steps that cannot be undone for records) stays the natural one.
- **Credit.** The repo's archived July census (no claim authority) has the simplest (Clifford) case. A42 agrees with it, corrects how one of its rows is read, and extends it to every reversible next-door tick and to one fermion mode per site.

**Two-part matter from one qubit per place (A43, exact unless marked).**
- The repo's two-part walk is real: eight perfectly round crossings, four of each hand, all at one speed, treating every turn alike (exact; my check).
- Its two parts flip sign under one full turn of the grid. Nothing made by acting locally on qubits can do that, however many qubits a place holds (exact; my check of the key step). So these crossings never appear as an ordinary local disturbance of qubits. On one qubit per place there are two ways in:
    - **Magnet-type ripples.** If neighbours prefer opposite settings, the background carries two kinds of ripple that move at one speed in every direction, protected by symmetry (checked; my check of the speed). They have no handedness, and this background replaces the calm, all-aligned one.
    - **Half-pieces of a highly entangled background.** The walk's exact crossings appear as fractions of an entangled empty space (exact at the simplest level of approximation; whether such a phase exists is open). They come with a hidden field of their own that might itself behave like light. No rule is known that produces this background, and it stays quiet only if records form only next to records.
- With fermionic room per place (a named supplied premise; see decision 13) the walk appears directly. Two qubits per place without it give three-part matter with a flat or heavy partner.
- **Decision for you:** see decision 23.

**A rule that makes that background the calmest state? (A44; key algebra checked by me.)**
- **A familiar state in disguise (exact).** Give every spot's possibilities a fixed half-turn that depends on its place, and the two-part background becomes the standard "π-flux" entangled state, which treats every direction of the possibilities alike. So asking for a rule that makes our background the calmest state is the same as asking for one that makes the π-flux state the calmest, with the rule transformed by the same half-turns (the plain neighbour coupling turns into a mix of plain and directional couplings).
- **Not with next-door rules (checked).** For the neighbour rules the grid allows (plain, directional and handed couplings) it is never the calmest state. It beats the best plain arrangement of spins only by a hair (at most 0.004 per bond, in a narrow range of rules); once the small quantum jiggles of the ordered arrangements are counted, ordered magnet-like states win everywhere by about 0.1 per bond (variational Monte Carlo up to 8×8×8, and exact results on 16 sites).
- **One candidate region, not evidence.** Richer rules that spoil the magnetic order (the half-turned version of a second-neighbour coupling) bring it level near one setting, exactly where the simple estimates break down; known results for that setting point to ordered states (a comparison).
- **Its hidden field is larger than expected.** At the simplest level of approximation it is of the kind with three internal directions (SU(2)), not the single-phase kind that acts like light, so a further step would be needed.
- **Ring-type rules do not rescue it (A46; key identity checked by me).** Four-spin ring terms on the grid's square faces never make it the calmest state; the sign that helps it helps the fully aligned state even more. Adding a star-local next-nearest coupling as well gives at best a tie with weakly ordered states within measurement noise, while an exact 16-site calculation finds a different state well below all of them. The extra hops that would turn its hidden field into the light-like single-phase kind keep the walk's crossings but cost energy rather than saving it.
- **So** with one qubit per place and next-door rules, the calmest states found are ordered magnets, whose ripples are bosonic and handless. The two-part matter-and-light route needs richer rules; no rule is known in physics either that makes the 3D π-flux state the calmest (a comparison).

**Known recipes for light and matter, mapped onto the grid (A45; key step checked by me).**
- **Light fits.** Put the light field on the in-between (edge) places of the 2×2×2 pattern, as the repo's light notes do. It moves by turning around small squares, a star-local rule that respects every turn, and its charge bookkeeping (Gauss's law) can hold exactly. If records form only where charge is, empty space never triggers records, which is one of section 13's escapes. Whether this gives real light on the cubic grid is not established; the repo's own estimates are upper bounds.
- **Its charges are never electron-like under your exact turns (exact, for the simple ways of dressing their hops).** A half-turn of the grid always makes the two opposite hops of a charge commute, so the charges behave as bosons.
- **Electron-like matter on one qubit per place** then needs either a pattern held in the state that removes those half-turns (as the repo's record-carved networks do) or the half-particles of the entangled background above.
- **Only the half-particle route could give matter and light together** with one qubit per place and no pattern: one extra covariant hop turns its hidden field into the light-like single-phase kind and keeps all eight crossings, at the cost of their speeds splitting. Its gap is energetic, and the decisive energy contest (A47; its spin-wave numbers recomputed by me) failed. The light-like change gains nothing: its best setting is indistinguishable from not adding it. Ordered magnet states win or tie at every rule strength tried, and the one near-tie becomes a loss on the larger grid (checked on 6×6×6 and 8×8×8 grids, plus exact 16-site results). So no known rule makes it the calmest state. It would still need multi-spin star rules or more room per place (parked).
- **The looser reading of turns does not rescue light's charges (A48; its counting checked by me).** "Turns up to a local relabeling" has two halves. With one fixed relabeling pattern it is your exact turns written in other labels: nothing physical changes, and all three costs in decision 28 stay (exact). The only genuinely looser option lets some places' possibilities stop turning with the grid. That allows a one-part charged hop through light's links and lets the painted sign pattern keep all 24 turns (if sideways steps may combine up to an overall sign). But light's charges stay bosons under every version, as long as the grid's three straight half-turns about each corner are kept (exact for the simple ways of dressing hops; checked). Keeping only one half-turn would allow electron-like charges, but the grid's turns include all three.
