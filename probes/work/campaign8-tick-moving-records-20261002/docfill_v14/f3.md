## 3. How can anything move at all? (A3, A10, A20, plus my check)
- **The exact limit (A20; exact for the premises listed).** Take any reversible step on one qubit per site, with sites combined in the ordinary quantum way (the repo has not chosen that over the fermionic way; its archive reaches the same conclusion only for number-preserving fermionic ticks), that treats every site and every turn of the grid exactly alike on every tick, with each site's possibilities turning exactly as the grid turns (your Q3). If a site's possibilities can reach only its six neighbours in one tick, the step changes nothing at all. That stops everything under that step, not just records: no record it carries, no light, no matter wave. (A record can still step by an irreversible rule of its own: below, and section 0.)
    - **What does the work.** The proof uses full turning of the possibilities (two of its steps need it). The repo's archived census of the simplest ticks (one-qubit Clifford ticks reaching next door; July, no claim authority) finds only the do-nothing tick when the possibilities turn with the grid, and next-door ticks that spread content when the turns leave the possibilities alone. New tonight: the proof for every reversible next-door tick, without assuming Clifford ticks or a conserved number. Section 15 (A42) settles the other ways of turning and the fermionic way of combining sites: next-door movement needs two directions of the possibilities that every symmetry treats exactly alike. (The repo also has the same pattern for light on edges and faces: a one-step, norm-preserving tick there moves nothing, and its escapes are more layers, more room, or looser locality; all unaudited.)
- **Ways it can give:**
    - **A supplied pattern.** Cycling the neighbour pairs moves things (A10), but once two records meet, sites can be told apart by which of 8 interleaved sub-grids they sit on. That conflicts with "No site is privileged".
    - **A longer reach.** Fully symmetric rules reaching 4 sites per tick do move things, but what travels is a spreading web of links, not a record or a particle, and no calm empty background survives. This is exact for the simplest class of such rules; rules reaching 2 or 3 sites are untested for one qubit per site (with fields on edges and faces the repo has a tick reaching three sites that treats every turn alike and carries light; unaudited).
    - **More room per site, or sameness only on average.** More room per site changes the Qubit axiom's M₂(ℂ), so it is carried as a named supplied premise (see decision 13); the repo's edge-and-face light tick, three local layers reaching three sites, is an example. Sameness only on average must live with your approved realized-state primitive: a number that would change with the realized schedule is registered data, not a derived result. Both remain possible.
- **For a single record, a step that cannot be undone works** (A1, my check).
    - The record re-locks its own possibility onto one of the grid's six directions, then trades places with the neighbour that way. Its new content points the way it stepped.
    - It is exactly symmetric under all 24 turns. It goes straight 1/3 of the time, turns 2/3 of the time, never steps back, and never stays put. Over many ticks it wanders like dust.
    - **Costs:**
        - Its lockable possibilities are the grid's six directions, which ties forming records to the grid; your Q3 left formation unglued.
        - What a record locks changes at most steps.
        - As written it covers one record only. Two records can step into one site, up to 1 in 9 per tick. Letting a blocked record stay fixes that part, but a rule for two records wanting the same site with no record is not built.
    - **It moves records only.** Light and anything that interferes still need a reversible change, so one of the ways above must give for them. With records stepping by their own rule, section 1's one-site limit on the change no longer applies (argued).
