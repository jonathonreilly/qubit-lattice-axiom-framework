## 5. The tick: shared in practice for ticked change; free and nearly invisible in the record-tick shape (A5, A14, A15, A17, A32)
- **In step:** neighbourhood ticks that keep in step cannot be told apart from one universal tick.
- **Out of step:** if each site keeps its own fixed tick without waiting, the pair between out-of-step neighbours never acts under the natural rule (a pair acts only if both ends pick it). Getting anything through needs a second gate within one step, which breaks the one-site-per-tick limit there, or an unrecorded random choice, which loses coherence. The seam is a mirror wall that records can reveal (ticked change only).
- **Waiting:** if sites wait for each other, no region can keep a different long-run rate, and a ring of sites all waiting on each other freezes everything.
- **Where variation can live:** in these toys, time running differently in different places cannot sit in how often each place's tick comes. It can sit in how much change and record-forming each shared tick brings (for the change, to leading order when the change per tick is small).
- **That amount must vary smoothly.** Set by the records right next to each spot, it jitters badly:
    - it washes out atom interference within a nanosecond;
    - at Planck ticks, it shakes an electron apart within seconds.
- **The averaging needed:** the jitter shrinks only with the width of the region averaged over, and averaging over time does not help (exact). The region needed is a billion or more sites across. A rule using only neighbouring records cannot build that average.
- **What is left open:** a smooth field carried by the shared possibilities, changing by a fixed rule (A17; argued, not built). For it to spread at all, its rule must escape section 3's limit.

**The tick redone in the record-tick shape (A32, checked).** In this option, records form and step on ticks; the possibilities change all the time.
- **The ticks are invisible to the change (exact).** Moving one record event in time changes everything recorded later only in proportion to the chance of a record on that tick and to how much the change does during the shift. If that chance shrinks with the tick's length, every kind of schedule gives the same world in the limit: global or local, regular or random.
- **No mirror walls.** Out-of-step neighbourhoods no longer bounce anything back. In the toy, 82% of a ripple crossed a seam whether the ticks were in step or not, where the ticked rule let nothing through. Nothing waits, so nothing freezes.
- **Global or neighbourhood (exact, construction by construction).**
    - A tick written into the law is the same everywhere if the law treats every place exactly alike (the four axioms do not choose a tick at all; this is what covariance forces once one is written in). A checkerboard of two sub-grids half a tick apart is a supplied pattern: a one-site shift leaves it unchanged only if time also shifts by half a tick (an unnoticeable overall shift), and records can faintly tell its sub-grids apart.
    - Ticks set differently by place need each place to remember its phase (decision 9), unless the shared ticks are numbered in a repeating cycle: then the records around each spot can choose which numbers the spot uses, with no memory at each place (A36, below), though the cycle's own count must then be kept somewhere (decision 18).
    - Random local times are just a thinned-out global tick.
    - Times set by a possibility are just a formation chance.
- **Records can run a little faster across out-of-step regions.** Chains of new records can then run along places whose ticks come one after another, so the strict one-site-per-tick limit softens. If that chance is small, it does so only in proportion to the chance per tick, unless each place remembers the pattern from its last tick.
- **Influenced by gravity.** How often records form may vary from place to place.
    - If clocks that count records are to agree with all other clocks near heavy bodies (your choice), the chance of forming a record per unit of local time must be the same everywhere. Otherwise clocks that count records and clocks carried by the possibilities disagree near heavy bodies, by the full gravitational amount.
    - The simplest way, needing no memory: one global tick, with each place's record chances slowed by gravity's time-stretch.
    - Ticks that themselves run slower in gravity would need each place to keep its own phase (at Earth's surface, taking one grid step per tick as light's speed, neighbouring grid points would drift a full cycle apart about once a year). They also need gravity's slowing to be a fixed, given background. If the slowing is carried by the shared possibilities, as on the gravity-field route, such ticks would let faraway choices steer records (A36; this is ruled out only if you adopt the no-signalling sentence that no faraway choice may change local record odds), and only the chances can follow it.
- **Light never sees the tick (exact, when records form only next to records).** In empty space the ticks act on nothing, so they add no energy-dependent speed and no twisting of light's polarisation. Clocks made of light-like matter slow with motion as Einstein says, with corrections around 10⁻³⁸ at laboratory speeds; in this shape that matter needs section 10's sign pattern.
- **A preference for the grid's own frame.** In a two-band toy, no positive local recording rule makes the count of records follow a moving body's own time, and at most half the count slows. This needs a rule that treats left and right alike; my check found that one that doesn't can do differently. It is unobservable at the recording rates already allowed.
- **Same tick (exact).** Records formed on one tick at different spots never leak faraway choices.
    - If each spot's rule looks only at itself, the order within a tick does not matter at all.
    - If it looks at neighbours, an order or a joint rule is needed, and the difference is tiny (second order in the chance).
- **Minimum distance and minimum tick.** No smallest tick follows from your instincts and the four axioms. The framework already has an approved primitive that grains the emergent tick like the grid step (one tick is one edge "in form"); whether records form on that tick is an open gate on main. What follows is an upper limit (exact, given the premises): for one site per tick to be the universal limit, the tick must be shorter than light's time to cross one grid step, and in 3D shorter than that divided by √3. A tick of exactly one step would sit right at that limit in one dimension and past it in 3D: if the record tick were the primitive's tick, light would outrun records on the diagonals. That is fine in the record-tick shape, where light is not held to one site per tick, but not if one site per tick is to limit everything.
    - A tick near light's crossing time is favoured only if every tick carries a real chance of a record and recording is to run near its fastest. Shorter ticks then slow recording beside records (they do not forbid it), and such chances already freeze slower motion beside records.
    - With small chances the tick is only limited from above.

**Ticks chosen by the surrounding records (A36, checked).**
- **What records can and cannot do.** Records carry no time of their own and do not change between record events, so by themselves they cannot mark a later moment. Ticks set by the unrecorded possibilities would let a faraway choice send a message (exact, given Campaign 7's no-signalling sentence, which you have not adopted).
- **With one shared clock underneath** (the shared ticks numbered in a repeating cycle), each spot can use the instants that its pattern of recorded neighbours selects (exact construction). The result:
    - it is set and differs by neighbourhood;
    - it is influenced by records;
    - it needs no per-place memory;
    - it treats every place and turn alike, though not every instant (the numbered cycle is a pattern in time).

    The costs:
    - your "one site per tick" then holds per instant of the shared clock. Per spot's own cycle, a chain of new records can advance as many sites as the cycle has instants in use (at full chances, three times faster along diagonals; at small chances only slightly faster). Keeping one site per spot's own cycle needs memory;
    - supplied: the cycle's numbering, the table of which patterns use which instants, the range of the rule (the records within one site), a rule for claims across instants, and an order for same-pattern neighbours.

    "Records form only next to records" is the simplest case: records switch the shared tick on or off. There are 10 neighbour patterns up to the grid's turns if only "recorded or not" counts. Counting each record's content there are 57, or 33 if the law cannot tell the two contents apart (a law that favours neither content, which is the repo's reading of "No possibility is privileged"). With contents counted, exactly one pair of patterns are mirror images of each other, so a rule could even time a pattern and its mirror image differently. The repo's September mirror note (unaudited) has the same counts.
- **Without a shared clock,** the only set moments are the moments new records form. Letting those trigger neighbours at once can record a whole region in an instant once the chance per record is high enough (about 0.3 in 3D).
- **Not gravity's slowing.** The influence comes only from records in contact, not from a heavy body farther away. Through a gas of wandering records it could at most reproduce the record-carried slowing of section 6, with its shortcomings (argued). A tick rate that each place averages from its neighbours' rates would reach further, as in unaudited repo notes, but needs a stored rate at every place.
    - With a fixed chance per tick it would make record-counting clocks run at rates set by how crowded their spot is, against your choice, if you make it, that all clocks slow together.
    - With the chance shrinking with the tick, the influence all but vanishes from what records show.
- **Far from all records,** if records form only next to records, nothing sets a tick and nothing forms; the smooth change is the only time empty space has.
