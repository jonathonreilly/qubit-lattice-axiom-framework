## 6. Gravity-like clock slowing: real in toys; record-carried gravity falls short; a field route is mapped (A6, A8, A14, A18, A23, A25, A26)

**The basic effect**
- With a thin gas of wandering records, and clumps that keep swallowing them, nearby clocks slow with the right sign, falling off as one over distance.
- A perfectly swallowing clump stops time inside, and its effect grows with its radius (black-hole-like). Swallowing needs a "stopped" mark that record content alone cannot carry.
- Crowding clumps that don't swallow always leak. In the 3D toys, a clump lasted roughly in proportion to its size or a bit less (about 4 to 9 times longer for 8 times more records, depending on the toy), nothing like a black hole's lifetime growing as mass cubed.

**First mismatches**
- In this toy, the next-order term is half of Einstein's, so Mercury comes out wrong.
- Light bends half as much, unless passing between neighbours is slowed twice over.
- The slowing spreads by diffusion: about 50 micrometres in the age of the universe.
- Gravity's weakness has to be put in by hand.

**A dialled toy package (A18)**
- **What it is:** a shared tick, records averaged over a very large region setting the change per tick, and one exponential shape for the slowing.
- **What it gets right:** for light made of two-site ripples, at the level of rays, the right main light bending, radar delay and Mercury orbit.
- **Costs:**
    - both key numbers are dialled in, not derived;
    - four-site-loop light bends 1.5 times too much;
    - light runs at under 1% of the grid limit;
    - the carriers must not slow themselves;
    - nearest-neighbour versions jitter far too much;
    - if wandering records also hold light back (as they do in A14), the photon-mass limit forces them so sparse that the averaging region must be about the size of our galaxy, so the Sun's own field could not be resolved.

**Why record-carried gravity falls short (argued)**
- With diffusing carriers, a moving body's field survives only in a thin wake behind it, so a moving Earth could not hold the Moon.
- There are no gravitational waves, and no frame dragging (a spinning body twisting nearby paths).
- Records, taken as locked possibilities, carry no momentum, so a crowd of them can only spread out, not ripple. (Under a different supplied clause in the repo's unaudited notes, where a record's content is its direction of travel, records do carry momentum and the gas has sound.)
- Wanderers that fly straight dilute the clock rate as one over distance squared, not one over distance. (The repo's unaudited wind notes get an inverse-square pull from such a gas under a supplied closure, but no one-over-distance clock potential.)

**The field route (A23)**
- **The idea.** Gravity carried by a field of the shared possibilities: a smooth stretch that records never lock, passed neighbour to neighbour by the fixed rule, and pushed by energy and motion.
- **What any such field gets:**
    - a fixed rule in which the field sets the pace leaks no faraway choices;
    - moving bodies get proper fields, not wakes;
    - one-over-distance comes free;
    - noise is tiny;
    - gravity's weakness becomes "matter is very light on the grid" (a mass ratio of about 8×10⁻²⁰ that the approved primitives do not supply).
- **A one-number (scalar) field fails** (argued, against measurements). Tied to matter in the usual ways, it bends light either not at all or only half as much as seen. The full amount needs the grid's rest frame singled out, which tests for a preferred frame rule out. It also gives no frame dragging and the wrong kind of waves.
- **Two numbers per place already bend light fully.** A time-stretch plus one common space-stretch is already enough for the full static bending, as in the repo's unaudited "curvature member" notes. The full shape field is what waves, frame dragging and moving sources need.
- **A "shape" field (how each direction is squeezed and twisted) works at linear order.**
    - If the field's bookkeeping is kept exact (nothing lost at any step, and the field unchanged by relabelling places), the grid's own turns fix its form exactly as in Einstein's linear gravity at long wavelengths (checked, and confirmed by hand twice). The repo already holds unaudited notes that reach the same linearised form on a Regge-style complex and on a staggered tensor field, so this is a re-derivation in tonight's setting, by a new route (the grid's turns plus exact bookkeeping, with no edge-length assumption), not a new result. Not fixed: the ripples' speed compared with light (the repo's notes find the two speeds agree exactly when one kinetic coefficient takes a particular value), gravity's strength, and its sign (attraction goes with the healthy sign of its kinetic term, which is also supplied). At grid scale, direction-dependent corrections are free but tiny.
    - A stepping rule gives two wave polarisations at one common speed, equal to light's only if the two are set together, with no time-doubled twin. With matter tied in (A26), it gives full light bending, and everything whose weight sits at single places falls alike.
- **Conditions:**
    - **A fixed pattern of jobs (A25, checked).**
        - Each layer of the step uses next-door moves only (the whole step reaches three places). Its rule treats every turn of the grid alike once the jobs are relabelled along with the turn, and it keeps its bookkeeping exact at every step. At linear order its ripples come out right: two kinds, all at one speed, with nothing extra.
        - The price: places take fixed jobs in a repeating 2×2×2 pattern (1 of 8 layouts; the same eight-fold pattern as in the repo's unaudited U(1) role-encoded gauge note), some holding the field's stretch parts, some its shear parts, some the in-between parts that pass change along. Nothing says which of the 8 possible layouts is the real one, and it never changes.
        - If every place does the same job, the field comes out as 8 copies of itself: eight kinds of ripple where there should be one. This is proved for the usual bookkeeping, and it holds whether change comes in ticks or smoothly, so the smooth change of the record-tick shape does not avoid it. Patching it by hand breaks the bookkeeping and lets ripples grow without limit. An unusual bookkeeping that avoids both costs is open. The repo's unaudited Regge-complex version is one candidate (all parts at the same places, exact bookkeeping, one ripple branch where sampled), at the price of only part of the grid's turns and an extra heavy variable per cell.
        - Each place must also hold more mutually distinguishable settings than one qubit's two. (The possibility domain itself has room for any finite list of labels, as the repo's U(1) role-encoded note observes; what it cannot give is that many settings in one place that records could tell apart.)
    - Forming a record must leave energy unchanged on average, or a phantom weight remains where the record formed (A23). (A different part of the same problem, the field's own energy when a spread-out source collapses to a point, is already in unaudited notes on main.) A24 found exactly when that holds: when the rival possibilities no longer overlap through any of the spot's links, because the change has already carried the difference away ("settled"). Something still freely spreading is never settled. A24 built one working example in a supplied toy, "catch first, record later":
        - A small thing is caught at one spot, and its spare energy flies off.
        - The record then forms at the catching spot. Once the spare energy has left it costs almost nothing; forming sooner costs more.
        - Recording a freely spreading thing at one exact spot jolts it by about half its whole energy range, which is enormous on the finest grid. Recording it loosely costs less, but never nothing.
        - So, under the gravity field, almost all records would have to form on caught, settled things, and slowly. Earth's heat flow allows other records only extremely rarely. This matches how real detectors work (absorb, then amplify; a comparison, not adopted).
        - The toy needs a trap and a separate channel for the spare energy. Whether the framework's own change can catch and release like that is open.
        - It is a decision for you; the axioms don't imply it.
    - Whether the field can be fitted into one qubit per place, for example as a collective ripple of many places, is open.
    - The Mercury term needs second order. In the repo's unaudited curvature-member notes (with a supplied ray model), the second-order bending matches Einstein's only when a body's clock charge equals its length charge (a body at rest falls short). A 30 September note finds that finite-range fields on that carrier cannot meet the next constraint order within a stated class.
- **Matter on the field (A26, checked).**
    - Suppose matter is tied to the shape field in the simplest fixed way: each place's own change slows with the local time-stretch, and each step to a neighbour also stretches with the local space-stretch.
    - Then, at the level of rays, to first order in the field and for a small change per tick, light and matter both follow the field's own geometry.
        - Light bends by the full amount. A18's separate choice of how two neighbours' slowings combine is no longer needed: the stretch of the steps supplies it.
        - Everything whose weight sits at single places falls alike, and moving things fall slightly faster in the proportion nature shows.
    - **Weight carried in links.** It falls twice as fast unless it is classed as weight rather than motion. The field's own bookkeeping requires that classing anyway (argued, at long wavelengths), but at grid level it is supplied.
    - **The diagonal ripple.** One of the two ripple types is felt only if each step's sense of direction is turned by a fixed three-move shuffle inside each 2×2 block. None of the simple next-door tweaks tested does it, and one of them makes the two copies of each particle feel opposite ripples. The shuffle is supplied, checked in 2D only, and whether it treats all 24 turns alike in 3D is unchecked. It is built from ticked moves, and it reaches diagonally across the block. A smooth change made only of next-door terms whose strength does not depend on the ripple's wavelength (the record-tick shape, and the only kind tested) cannot produce it. A coupling that depends on the ripple's wavelength, such as a curl, was not tested; the repo's unaudited note on the two gravity-wave polarisations finds one that works for a different measurement. So matter on the field has not yet been built in the record-tick shape.
    - **Precision.** Measured light bending needs a small change per tick, or one specific way of writing the coupling.
    - **My check.** I rebuilt the 2D toy's band structure independently: the slopes of all four bands follow the field's geometry exactly, and massive bands agree to about 0.1%.
- **The repo.** None of its gravity notes is audited (the ledger has no audit-ratified row at all), but there are many, and tonight's field-route results partly re-derive or sharpen them and partly add new toys and theorems. They include a Regge-style version of the linearised-Einstein result (with the tick on the same footing as a grid step); a September family on a "curvature member" (a time-stretch and a space-stretch per place plus a staggered tensor field) with two equal-speed ripples, the same constraint-closing kinetic ratio, light bending doubled, and a conserved-stress price for matter; and the 30 September note on the next constraint order (above). Your approved kinetic-isotropy primitive fixes only that a tick has the same footing as a grid step (for the Euclidean regulator). Using it to supply Einstein's weights for a gauge-invariant field goes beyond what it is registered to supply, although the unaudited Regge notes already assume it. The same weights also follow from keeping the field's constraints exact, with no primitive. Extending the primitive would be your call.
