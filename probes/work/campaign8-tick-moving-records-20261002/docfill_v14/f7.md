## 7. Empty space must essentially never form records, or every region freezes (A4, A6, A9, A12, A28)
- **Freezing time.** At any steady rate, empty space freezes within about one over that rate, in ticks.
- **The half-filled sea.** The repo's September notes use a half-filled staggered sea as a supplied vacuum; the repo itself says the vacuum is not chosen. In its massless form, a one-tick, straight-average rule that looks only at a site and its six neighbours on the sea's own grid forms false records at least 5 per million times as often as it records a real passing thing. That is the best case for the massless sea (a staggered mass lowers the floor, to about 1.4 per ten million at mass 0.5, but never to zero); an energy-based rule is about 1 in 80. At Planck ticks (massless-sea values):
    - a rule strong enough to record a particle within a second fills empty space within days;
    - a rule weak enough to keep space clear for the age of the universe would need some 70,000 years to record anything.
- **Many-tick rules.** Rules that build up over many ticks, like real detectors absorbing energy, can make false records exponentially rare, but never zero for a rule that ignores records. They need a small memory at every site, which the axioms do not provide. A memory made of matter is not ruled out.
- **A hint from the sky.** Light from distant galaxies arrives without forming records on the way.
    - Letting records form only next to existing records would explain this and keep empty space exactly quiet. The cost: a grid with no records would never get any.
    - A rule that simply ignores light would explain the first but not the second.
- **Records form only next to records (A28, checked).** Tested inside the record-tick shape.
    - **What it gives:**
        - empty space far from every record never forms one, whatever kind of emptiness it is (exact);
        - light crossing it is never recorded and no recording rule acts on it there; it changes only through its links to records formed elsewhere (your Q1), and on average not at all;
        - no faraway choice can steer it, because the rule looks only at the records next door, and those always agree with the spot they sit on;
        - records spread at most one site per tick, provided the rule looks at the records present at the start of the tick and there is one shared tick.
    - **The problem moves to the skin of matter.** Next to any record, the half-filled sea always has some chance of being recorded (exact for the simplest half-filled sea on an infinite grid; the fourth review's check confirms it for the repo's staggered kind; small periodic grids in the repo's notes do have exact zero-odds record patterns). So records creep into it, and each new one heats its surroundings, far beyond what is seen if the rule's energy scale is the Planck scale (argued). Next to matter, empty space would have to be the tidy, lined-up kind, or recording there would have to build up slowly over many ticks. My check reproduced the key numbers exactly.
    - **Other costs:**
        - a world with no records never gets any, so the world must start with some (your approved realized-state primitive leaves which records it starts with to the realized world; whether a law under which a grid with no records never gets any "privileges" that state is your call);
        - whether everything fills up and stops depends on one more choice, namely whether a spot fully surrounded by records may still form one;
        - in dense transparent matter such as glass or water, light escapes recording only if records are point-like rather than nucleon-sized, recording is weak, or the rule ignores light.
    - **One encouraging case, with a catch.** With a tidy emptiness and records whose content lies along its direction, recording stops once there is nothing left to record, while records keep moving forever: no freezing, no thinning. But this needs stepping odds that let records into calm space, so fully recorded regions then slowly dissolve (section 9). And if record contents are the opposite of the emptiness, every step that brings records together or apart changes the energy by a grid-scale amount.
