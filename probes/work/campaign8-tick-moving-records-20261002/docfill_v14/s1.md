## The night in one page

**What we did.** We worked out what your instincts would do, using exact derivations and small computer toys. The instincts:
- records form at set ticks;
- they move at most one site per tick;
- clashes are settled by relative odds;
- the possibilities flow;
- a full region may act like a black hole.

These are explorations of your instincts, not positions: nothing is adopted. Nine rounds of hostile review, by four separate agents, attacked the claims; their corrections, and those of a check against the repo, are folded in.

**1. A hard limit on motion (exact, for the premises listed).** Suppose a change is reversible, reaches only next-door sites in one tick, uses one qubit per site with sites combined the ordinary (not the fermionic) way, and treats every site and turn of the grid exactly alike on every tick, with each site's possibilities turning exactly as the grid turns (your Q3). Then nothing can move under it: no record it carries, no light, no matter wave. Something has to give.
- **What does the work (A42, 3 October, exact).** Next-door movement needs two different directions of the possibilities that every symmetry treats exactly alike. Your Q3 rules that out; so does a weaker gluing, and so does any law that treats every direction of the possibilities alike. With one fermion per site nothing moves either. Without gluing, or with a "sign twist" gluing, next-door rules can spread change, but only by favouring particular directions of the possibilities. The repo's archived July census (no claim authority) had the simplest kind of tick; the proofs here cover every reversible next-door tick.

**2. The most coherent way through: "ticks for records, smooth change for possibilities" (the record-tick shape).**
- **How it works.** Records step at most one site per tick by trading places with a neighbour with no record (which needs "records are permanent" to mean "never destroyed, may move", decision 7), and the shared possibilities change smoothly in between.
- **It uses two of the limit's ways out at once:** a smooth change that reaches past next door (faintly, if the change per tick is small), and record steps that cannot be undone. Together they keep records to one site per tick.
- **The price.** Unrecorded influence loses its exact speed limit. A leak always remains, and it stays exponentially faint only if the change per tick is small.
- **Swap or flow (A37, checked).** If nothing is made or destroyed, then whatever the rule, a stepping record sends exactly one spot's worth of possibilities back where it came from (exact). On a single line, for steps that move whole contents, the only tidy way is the swap; your "everything pushes right" needs an endless conveyor that changes things arbitrarily far away at once, or a step that wipes out one spot's worth. On the full grid the possibilities can flow around the record, picking a side at random. Over calm empty space the choice makes no difference; it changes how records move only if their stepping odds look at their neighbours (swap: they bounce back; line push: they keep going; flow: they turn), a memory in what was pushed, not momentum.

**3. Your tick, in that shape (checked).**
- **No more seams.** The possibilities never notice the ticks, so the earlier troubles (mirror seams, freezing while waiting) disappear.
- **Global or neighbourhood.** A tick written into the rule is the same everywhere if the rule treats every place exactly alike (or a checkerboard of two sub-grids half a tick apart: a supplied pattern, unchanged by a one-site shift only if time also shifts by half a tick, which records can faintly tell apart). Ticks can still differ by place without memory if the shared ticks are numbered in a repeating cycle and the records around each spot choose which numbers it uses (A36); the cycle's own count must then be kept somewhere (decision 18), and "one site per tick" (for records that step, decision 7) holds per shared instant. Otherwise set ticks that differ by place need a per-place memory, or fire only when new records form.
- **Influenced.** Ticks chosen by the records right next to a spot feel only those records, not gravity (A36). A tick rate averaged from neighbours' rates would reach further, but needs a stored rate at every place. How often records form can follow gravity's slowing through the chance per tick, but not through the ticks themselves if gravity is carried by the shared possibilities (ticks set that way would let faraway choices steer records, which is ruled out only if you adopt the no-signalling sentence that no faraway choice may change local record odds).
- **Visible?** With small chances per tick that scale with each spot's own interval, the choice is far too faint to see; it shows at full strength if records set how often a spot's tick comes and each tick keeps the same chance.
- **Light.** If records form only next to records, the tick never touches how light crosses empty space.
- **Minimum tick.** Your approved primitive already says one tick is one edge "in form". Nothing tonight derives a smallest tick; what follows is a longest one: if nothing may outrun one site per tick and light goes equally fast every way, the tick must be shorter than light's time to cross a grid step, divided by √3. If the record tick were the primitive's tick (main lists that tie as an open gate), light would outrun records on the diagonals. That is fine in the record-tick shape, where light is not held to one site per tick, but not if one site per tick is to limit everything.

**4. Empty space (exact, within the toys).** If records form only next to records, empty space far from matter is exactly quiet whatever it is made of, and distant light crosses it without ever being recorded there. The price:
- the world must start with some records (a grid with no records would never get any; whether that means the law "privileges" a state is your call);
- next to matter the emptiness would have to be the tidy, lined-up kind (a different vacuum from the half-filled sea, carrying only slow ripples), recording there would have to build up slowly over many ticks, or records there would have to be blind to light (A39); otherwise records creep in and, if the rule's energy scale is the Planck scale, heat it far beyond what is seen (argued).

**5. Your black-hole idea (checked).** In the record-tick shape, a fully recorded region is a place where no record can form or move again (read literally, "records are permanent" already keeps records in place; stepping is your reading, decision 7), and no record or possibility from outside reaches in (gravity may still). Under A40's candidate layout no region can ever be fully recorded, since light and gravity places never record. With one stepping choice it never wears away, but that choice also freezes lone records in empty space.
- **Grey, not black.** In a one-line toy, at the catching strength best for fast things, it catches those almost always and bounces slow ones.
- **No glow.** What it caught stays as records on its skin, in plain view, and a sealed region gives off nothing.
- **Clocks inside: open.** Whether gravity's clocks stop inside depends on your choices and on strong gravity, which is not worked out.

**6. What the shape does not yet give.**
- **Light and matter.**
    - Over the calm, all-pointing-one-way background, a rule that treats every spot and turn alike moves single ripples only like slow, heavy particles, never like light (exact for single ripples; for the simplest such rule an unaudited repo note already has this).
    - Light-like matter with one internal state per place then needs a pattern of plus and minus signs painted onto the rule (one unforced choice in the repo's unaudited notes). Matter whose internal parts turn with the grid can avoid it: a supplied repo walk with two such parts (unaudited) gives eight exactly round crossings, four of each hand. But those two parts flip sign under a full turn, so on qubits they can never be made locally, however many qubits a place holds (exact, A43): with one qubit per place they appear only as half-pieces of a highly entangled background, which brings a hidden field of its own. Charged matter so far gives only near-misses (A41, below).
    - In the version that keeps your gluing, that pattern cannot keep the turns that gravity's pattern keeps (proved). A painted pattern can be tied to gravity's only by giving up half the grid's turns. Both use your exact-turn reading (Q3); under the repo's reading (each turn up to a local relabelling) the pattern keeps all 24 turns.
    - **The pattern from elsewhere?** No tidy background built from flips, with one condition per place, supplies it: a disturbance such a background protects can at best slide within flat sheets (proved, A33). One route stays open, not built: an ordinary ripple that behaves differently on the sub-grids of an 8-fold patterned background (to move like light, its energy must match on all four kinds of sub-grid place). It cannot use a background whose spots each simply point one way: under your Q3 the only such background that respects every turn gives a third of a turn per face, never the half turn (exact, A38). Finely tuned richer rules reach only a partly light-like point, with heavy ripples beside it.
    - **Quiet empty space and light (A39).** If records can sense every part of the rule's energy, exactly quiet empty space is automatically the rule's calmest state, and its gentlest ripples are then slow, never light (exact, when the zero cost comes from a local change of empty space; ice-like empty space is not covered). Ways out: light invisible to records, slow energy-selective recording with a memory per spot, or empty space that is not the calmest state (where A38's point sits).
    - **One pattern for everything? (A40, A41).** The same 8-way pattern gravity needs (the repo's own unaudited light and gravity notes already use it) could sort places into kinds for light, gravity and matter, but only with matter sharing places with part of gravity, more room at every place, a change to the Record axiom's wording, records stepping two places at a time, a staggered background charge for light's bookkeeping at the corners (the repo's earlier finding for one-qubit links), and, under your Q3, charged matter with three internal parts and one sign of charge. A follow-up (A41), with light's links as fixed numbers, found only near-misses for such matter: round cones at one setting of a free angle, with zero-energy lines beside them, and negative energies unless each charge is given an energy cost.
- **Gravity.**
    - If you choose to keep its bookkeeping exact, a field of the shared possibilities has its form fixed by the grid's turns to Einstein's linear gravity at long wavelengths. Unaudited repo notes already reach this form in other settings, so tonight's is a re-derivation, not a new result. Its ripples' speed relative to light, its strength and its sign (attraction) are not fixed.
    - In a ticked toy with matter tied to it, light is bent by the full amount (given a supplied choice: the field's time-stretch also paces its own change), and everything whose weight sits at single places falls alike.
    - The costs:
        - a fixed 2×2×2 pattern of jobs (1 of 8 layouts, as in the repo's unaudited notes on light);
        - more distinguishable settings per place than one qubit's two, or a one-qubit version of the field (not built) whose places are never recorded;
        - almost all records formed gently, on caught, settled things (one working toy: "catch first, record later").
- **Handedness.** Neither ticks nor smooth change give single free particles on a uniform grid a preferred handedness (exact). Interactions and record edges were not tested tonight; the repo's unaudited notes test some and find handedness only in pairs, or in single layers set by records.
