# Empty-start spectrum screen

Declared 2026-09-21 after the kernel/Fourier controls and before cubic outputs.
Start every graph completely vacant. Periodic three-dimensional cubes have
side 8 or 16. Compare normalized raw (3,1,2) and (12,1,2), with (mobility,
epsilon) equal to (0,0.1), (1,0.1), (1,0.01). These twelve cases each have
128 trajectories, seeds 20284000 through 20284127. Add a side-8 W=1 control
at mobility=1, epsilon=0.1 with 512 trajectories from the same seed start.
Observe 21 evenly spaced tau=6 epsilon t values through four. All cases and
unfavorable outcomes are retained. This is an exploratory screen, not a test
of a declared phase boundary. At most two simulation processes run at once.

The spectrum probe preserves the calibrated event law at mobility one: 144
paths match the original kernel exactly. At mobility zero and one, six empty
four-cycle comparisons to full 2,401-state generators pass the predeclared
five-SE sanity gate (8,192 trajectories each; maximum discrepancy 3.56 SE).
Thirty-six arbitrary fields have every Fourier and real-space shell checked
against explicit sums. Parseval and direct global/lowest-mode identities
are checked on every measured snapshot. These controls are not independent
proofs of an infinite-volume physical interpretation.

Store the full shell spectra and spatial pair correlations for each trajectory.
Shells group integer vectors with equal squared Euclidean length; the physical
lattice wave number is 2 pi n/L, whereas real-space separation is n. They must
not be plotted as the same axis. The vector structure factor includes all
three content components. The density structure factor is uncentered: its
zero mode needs ensemble centering; use sample variance of N divided by volume
with the finite-sample correction if that connected zero mode is reported.

Compare at tau=2 and ensemble mean density 0.9, with 0.75 and 0.95 secondary.
Use the already stated whole-trajectory bootstrap and ensemble-curve inversion.
Show the available spectrum and spatial correlation curve, not just the most
favorable mode. Report finite-volume effects, nonzero modes and successful
motion. A finite-wave-number peak may indicate domains; it does not by itself
prove a stable pattern, massless field, ordering transition or force.

The exact empty-start startup coefficient provides a mathematical control,
not a claim that a short-time Taylor approximation applies at tau=2. In
particular, holding tau fixed while decreasing epsilon increases physical
time; the finite-time and rare-formation limits must remain separate.

The direction of the moving-versus-immobile mean-density difference at each
fixed tau is also recorded. The proved first nonzero short-time coefficient
is positive but does not decide its sign at these later observation times.
If signs differ, keep that outcome and investigate it before extrapolating.
