## Steps (line, `p = 0`)
1. **Chains and solutions (PROVED; CHECKED A1–A3).** In the gauge `ψ(n) = iⁿ f_n` each of the two chains (↑ even/↓ odd and the other) is the same real Jacobi matrix with zero diagonal and bonds `b_n = μ^{2n+1}/2`. Solutions `f_ε(n;z) = (iε/μ)ⁿ S_ε(zλ^{−n})`, `ε = ±1`, with `c_j = 2iε c_{j−1}/(λ^j − λ^{−j})` (entire in `z`). Both are square-summable at `+∞` for every `z` (the landed T1/the earlier attempt's series, re-derived in this gauge; remainder only at `X⁷` checked symbolically in `λ`).
2. **Slow end without importing Weyl (PROVED; CHECKED A4).** `d_±(J_min)` is the dimension of the space of square-summable pointwise solutions of `Jf = ±if` (`J_min*` is the pointwise operator). On `n ≤ N` the matrix is bounded (row sum ≤ `λ^{N+1/2}`-scale), so it is self-adjoint with trivial kernel of `J_N − z` for `Im z ≠ 0`. The solutions on `n ≤ N−1` that are in `ℓ²` form a space `V` of dimension ≤ 1 (the residual of the equation at `N` is an injective linear functional on `V`), and `(J_N − z)^{−1}δ_N ∈ V` is nonzero. So `dim V = 1` per chain; extended upward by the recurrence it is automatically `ℓ²` at `+∞` (step 1). Hence `(d_+, d_−) = (2, 2)` on the line without citing Weyl. *Scope: line only. In a 3D sector the same count needs all four Floquet-series solutions to be `ℓ²` at the fast end for every `z`, which I take from the landed 3D note, not re-proved.* This repeats a known fact by an elementary route: not new.
3. **Slab → half-line (PROVED).** Coupling `J_[a,b] → J_(−∞,b]` has norm `b_{a−1}`, so the resolvents differ by ≤ `b_{a−1}/|Im z|²`, uniformly in `b` (second resolvent identity). CHECKED D0 (`a = −9` vs `−12`, change < `4^{−9}`).
4. **The far-wall solution (PROVED; CHECKED B3, D5).** `θ_b = f_+ + κ_b f_−` with `κ_b = −f_+(b+1)/f_−(b+1) = η_b(1+O(zλ^{−b}))`, `η_b = (−1)^b`, locally uniformly in `z`. So `θ_b → θ^η = f_+ + η f_−` along each parity class, geometrically (rate `λ^{−1}` per step of `b`). In the zero-energy boundary coordinates `(c_+, c_−)` of `u_{ε,v}(n) = (ε/μ)ⁿ v` this is the plane `L_η = {c_+ = η c_−}`: Lagrangian for `G = diag(−iσ₁, iσ₁)` for both `η` (CHECKED B1, B2, B3), chain-separate (B6).
5. **Boundary term vanishes (PROVED; CHECKED D6, D7).** For `u ∝ θ^η` near `+∞`, `b_N(θ_N \bar θ_{N+1} − θ_{N+1}\bar θ_N)` is exactly `0` at `z = 0` for every `N` (the factor `(1+ηs)(1−ηs) = 0`, `s = (−1)^N`) and `O(λ^{−N})` at `z ≠ 0`. At `−∞` it vanishes because `b_n → 0`.
6. **Strong resolvent convergence on each parity class (PROVED modulo the cited textbook facts).**
   - Kernels `G_b(m,n) = φ_−(m∧n) θ_b(m∨n)/W_b → G^η = φ_−θ^η/W^η` pointwise; with `‖R_b‖ ≤ 1/|Im z|` this gives weak convergence on `ℓ²`.
   - `W^η ≠ 0`, because `φ_− ∝ θ^η` would give an `ℓ²` solution for `Im z ≠ 0` whose boundary terms at both ends vanish (step 5), forcing `Im z·‖u‖² = 0`.
   - Step 5 gives `‖R^η f‖² = Im⟨f, R^η f⟩/Im z`, the identity every true resolvent obeys, and `‖R_b f‖²` obeys it for each `b`. Weak convergence plus convergence of norms gives strong convergence.
   - `R^η` satisfies the resolvent identity (strong limit of products with a uniformly bounded sequence), and is injective: `R^η h = 0` makes `(A_n, B_n) = (Σ_{m≤n} φ_− h, Σ_{m>n} θ^η h)` solve a `2×2` system with determinant `−W^η/b_n ≠ 0`, so `h = 0`. So `R^η` is the resolvent of a self-adjoint `J^η`.
   - CHECKED D1–D2, D5: exact Gaussian-rational slab values `G_b(0,0)` at `z = i`, `a = −12`: same-parity squared gaps shrink by > 100× per step of 2; the limits differ (`|ΔG|² ≥ 0.30` at every `b = 6..13`); `G_{14}(0,0)[↑↑] = 0.889i` and `G_{13} = 0.500i`, matching the series Green functions with `η = +1, −1` to 1e-6.
7. **No limit, and no identity (PROVED; CHECKED A6, B4, B5).** `J_[a,b]` and `J_[a+1,b+1]` are related by the unit translation up to `λ^{±1}` (exact, A6: bonds scale by `λ`). Passing to the limit along `b` even, `T₁ J^{+} T₁^{−1} = λ^{∓1} J^{−}`. If `J^{+}` kept `U(t)T₁ = T₁U(λ t)`, then `J^{+} = J^{−}` (up to the same `λ`-scalings), contradicted by step 6's different Green functions. So neither parity limit is kept by the unit shift, and the family has no strong resolvent limit. Equivalently on the boundary space: `T₁ = diag(μ,−μ)` swaps `L_+ ↔ L_−`; a `T₁`-kept plane must split along the two `T₁` eigenspaces, and `L_η` meets each in `{0}`. Both do keep the shift by two (every realization does, landed T2/T4).
   - Reading: this is the sense in which slabs with a held wall fail to supply a fast-end condition compatible with the clock's scaling identity. It does not show the slab is "the wrong" physics, only that it is not in the identity-keeping family.

## Steps (3D sector, witness `λ = 4`, `p = (1/4, 1/3)`, `m = 5/12 < m* = 3/4`)
8. **Zero-energy planes (PROVED at zero energy; CHECKED C1–C7).** The four Floquet solutions `a, b, c, d` have `r = 3/2, −2/3, 2/3, −3/2`, spinors `v₊ (N=+m), v₊, v₋, v₋`. The Dirichlet condition at `b+1`, split into its `v₊` and `v₋` parts, gives the exact plane `P_b = span( b − (r_b/r_a)^{b+1} a , c − (r_c/r_d)^{b+1} d )`, coefficients of modulus `(4/9)^{b+1}` (= `((s−m)/(s+m))^{b+1}`). `P_b` is Lagrangian for every `b`; it tends to `span(b, c)` geometrically in both parities, and `span(b,c)` is one of the four unit-shift-kept pairs (isotropic: `G_bb = G_cc = G_bc = 0`).
9. **Resolvent level (CHECKED only; D3, D4).** Exact slab Green functions at `z = i`: successive gaps (both parities together) shrink with ratio `0.198 ≈ (4/9)² = 0.1975`, with no parity dependence. This is evidence that the slab resolvents converge to the resolvent of the realization `bc`. **Not proved**: steps 3–6 need the `z`-dependent solutions in 3D (landed claim, not re-derived here).
10. **Reading.** The Dirichlet condition kills the slowly decaying components (`|r| = 3/2` for `a, d`) and leaves the faster-decaying pair. On the line all four have `|r| = 1`: a tie, which is why parity decides. As `m → 0`, `(s−m)/(s+m) → 1`, so the rate is not uniform near `m = 0`, consistent with the landed obstruction that the label `bc` has direction-dependent limits there. If a fiberwise limit exists a.e. and fibers are uniformly bounded, dominated convergence over transverse momenta would give a realization on the infinite `Z³` (the `m = 0` sectors being a null set): **suggested, not checked**.
