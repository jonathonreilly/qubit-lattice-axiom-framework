## 2. Steps

1. **PROVED (restating landed T2(e)).** `8KQ = H + F`, `4K(P+Q) = Hr + 2T`, `4K(P-Q) = T - F` for content away from the walls. Source: block-110 note T2(e), proof by homogeneity, with the referee's wall hypothesis. Not new.
2. **PROVED, elementary, check.py A1.** Solving the first two for `P, Q`: `4KQ = (Hr+T+F)/2`, `4KP = (Hr+3T-F)/2`. Hence `P/Q = (Hr+3T-F)/(Hr+T+F)`, `P/Q - 1 = 2(T-F)/(Hr+T+F)`, and `1 + 2Q/(P+Q) = 2 + (F-T)/(Hr+2T)`. With `Hr + T + F > 0` and `Hr + 2T > 0`: `P = Q  <=>  T = F  <=>  bending ratio = 2`, and `T < F  <=>  P < Q  <=>  ratio > 2`. The closed forms for `P/Q` and the ratio in the three energies are not written in the landed note (it states only `P = Q iff F = H_hop`); they are a one-line corollary, so small.
3. **PROVED, check.py A2.** For the pinned body (`T = 0`) the forms give `P/Q = w0 = 1/(1+2s)` and ratio `(2+3s)/(1+s)`, which is block 60 T4(b),(d) (not new); here they come out of the energies alone, using `2 N0 = 1 + w0`.
4. **CHECKED (float64, residual 1e-13), check.py B1.** A damped fixed point for the combined problem (eigenvector -> `e, tau` -> the two field equations `Delta chi = -e/(8KN)`, `Delta N = (e+2tau)/(8K chi)` -> eigenvector) converges on a `7^3` box. At the solution: `Q` from the sites equals `Q` as the wall flux; `<H> = Hr + T`; `8KQ = <H> + F`; `4K(P-Q) = T-F`; `4K(P+Q) = Hr + 2T`; step 2's closed forms — all to `<1e-10`. Independently of my `e, tau` formulas, a finite-difference gradient of (lowest positive eigenvalue of `H(fields)` + `F`) in log-rate and log-length at the centre, a neighbour and two other sites is below `1e-9`: the solution is a stationary point of the stated functional. (A stationary point; I do not claim it is a minimum.)
5. **CHECKED, B2.** Compact branch (started from block 60's pinned body, continued in `K`): `m = 3`, `K = 0.1 ... 0.02` and `m = 6`, `K = 0.05 ... 0.01`, centre clock `w0` from 0.46 down to 0.12 (strong field, `mu` up to 75), weight on the central site `0.989 ... 0.9999`. At strong field the hop energy is a vanishing share of the field energy: the compact body is block 60's pinned body to within `O(1/m^2)`.
6. **CHECKED, B3.** The balance exists on the self-bound branch. Heavier bodies balance at weaker field. Beyond the balance the ratio falls below 2 (`P > Q`, `T > F`) until the bound state delocalises (the weakly bound end of the branch is not tracked here).

## 3. Where the route stops

- **(a), the lattice virial.** Not attempted to completion. The exact box identity for the state's dilation involves the two-step walk `H2` and the force density, and no combination with step 1 gives `T = F`. Step 6 shows why no such identity can exist for this content: **`T = F` is not an identity of self-bound bodies** — a one-parameter family of self-bound solutions has `T < F` (compact), another part has `T > F`, and `T = F` holds only on a codimension-one locus. This is a no-go for the claim "a lattice virial forces `T = F`" within the supplied content class (one staggered walker, hop 1, interior-only Hilbert space); it is not a no-go for other content (for example a many-walker or Fermi-sea body), and it rests on floating-point solutions (residual 1e-13), not exact arithmetic.
- **(b), the compact branch.** Done at the two ends: pinned (`P/Q = w0`, ratio `(2+3s)/(1+s)`, block 60) and first correction `c2(mu)/m^2` (numerical; the weak-field limit 3 is analytic). A closed form for `c2(mu)` at general `mu` is the first unproved piece. Also unproved: continuity of the compact branch into the balance region (I followed it by continuation; no bifurcation analysis), existence of other branches at the same `(m, K)`, and the extended (many-site) self-bound branch in a large box, which a2 treated only at leading order.
