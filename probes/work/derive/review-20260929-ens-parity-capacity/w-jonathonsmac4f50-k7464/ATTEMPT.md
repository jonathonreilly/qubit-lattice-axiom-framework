## Plain-language summary

The branch-only continuation says: if the charge offset on the ENS transmon is allowed to be anything, a cosine junction can split the two charge-parity lines of the 0-to-6 transition by at most 0.54 MHz, while the fitted Andreev-shape junction can split them by up to 3.46 MHz; the digitized Ramsey plot shows a doublet about 2.3 MHz apart, so only the Andreev shape "has the room".

I recomputed all of this with different code. The numbers are right (0.5445 and 3.4646 MHz; rounding-corner ranges 0.540-0.549 and 2.586-4.597 MHz match to 1e-6 MHz). What the source did not say, and what I show:

1. The maximum over offsets is just the endpoint value (offset 0 against 1/2). The offset dependence has an exact odd-harmonic form, so the quarter-offset zero is structural and the four-interval extremum searches are redundant (margin factor about 2e5).
2. The room is fragile. It is set by a 500 kHz second difference of the three cavity lines. A shift of one line by 14 kHz (fres2), 26 kHz (fres3) or 30 kHz (fres1) takes the Andreev room down to the 2.32 MHz doublet. The table values are printed to 10 kHz. The lowest printed-rounding corner (2.586 MHz) is already below the top of the plot-bin range (2.654 MHz).
3. The "physical-offset" coupling correction, which the source spends most of its argument on, changes the room by 1e-4 (337 Hz), which equals a 0.0035 kHz shift of one cavity line. It is exact and correct, and it is three to four orders below the uncertainties that matter.
4. The room is not an independent test: across corners and across both junction shapes, its logarithm is a monotone function of the predicted f06 line (Spearman -0.998 / -0.9995), which is the same level-6 misfit that already separates the two shapes.

No experimental success is accepted, and none is claimed.
