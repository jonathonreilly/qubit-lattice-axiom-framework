## Plain-language summary

The landed Wilson note computes a local number, `Delta_f`, from derivatives of the Euclidean electron propagator at zero momentum. The open question was whether that number is what a physical, gauge-independent, mass-calibrated electron "sees" at momenta p much larger than its mass M. For the electron half, at order e^2, the answer is yes, and now with the pieces separated:

* The physical on-shell shift (after subtracting the rest-mass shift) equals the landed local number, as the mass goes to zero, with a measured remainder of at most 0.06 m ln(1/m).
* It splits cleanly into a hard part (the landed massless lattice integral, reproduced to 14 digits) and a soft part that equals the landed "conversion constant" 1/(24 pi^2). The soft part does not depend on p/M, so that constant is physical, not a regulator artefact.
* The shift is the same in Feynman and Coulomb gauge for every bare cone speed tested. The photon-cloud residue is not gauge independent and diverges logarithmically in the infrared (standard infraparticle behaviour). The energy and its derivative are infrared finite. So "an isolated pole with finite residue" is not available, but the observable (energy, group velocity) is.
* The "floating trend" in p/M appears as soon as electron and photon cone speeds differ. It is exactly the known logarithmic running of the speed difference, with coefficient fixed analytically (exact integral, `-1/(3 pi^2)`) and matched by the lattice on-shell data to 0.1 to 0.25 percent. So the local number is the value at the cutoff, and the value at the energy p is that number times roughly `(p/Lambda)^gamma`, `gamma = e^2/(2 pi^2) = 0.0046`. This is computed, not a free cutoff knob: for `Lambda/p` between 10^3 and 10^19 it changes the mismatch by 3 to 18 percent and never changes its sign.
* It is not complete. The photon half (what the photon does on its own mass shell) is not computed. The sign of "electron faster or slower than photon" comes out opposite for the two frozen settings, so one of them has photon decay and the other electron radiation; the widths and the fixed-order domain beyond |p| of order sqrt(M) are open. No calibration/test envelope is frozen.
