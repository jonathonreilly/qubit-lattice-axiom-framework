## Plain-language summary

The member's field equations are known to second order in the fields only as far as block 144 reached (the quadratic action, which equals the second-order part of the comparator's action when `alpha = K/4`). The next order (cubic terms, which govern how a moving body's own field acts back on itself and on other bodies) was "not supplied". I asked: if we demand that the member's lapse and shift constraints keep being preserved by the evolution at the next order, are those cubic terms forced?

Within a precisely stated class, yes, up to two things. The class: local polynomial cubic terms in the lapse, shift and strain fields with exactly two derivatives, invariant under the 24 proper cubic rotations of the lattice (or, separately, under all rotations). The two things: (a) local rewritings of the fields (field redefinitions), which change the terms but not the physics; (b) an overall multiple of the cubic part relative to the quadratic part, which vacuum consistency leaves free. What is forced is a single direction: the cubic part of `N sqrt(gamma)(K_ij K^ij - K^2 + R)`. I found this by exact linear algebra on a finite space, not by a hand proof: the consistent cubic terms form a 31-dimensional space (19 with full rotation symmetry), of which 30 (18) are pure field redefinitions, and the comparator's cubic part is the one remaining direction.

Three further findings. (1) If only the shift (momentum) constraints are required to survive, and not the lapse constraint, there are more consistent cubic completions (23 against 19), with an explicit extra one: so uniqueness uses the time relabelling invariance at second order. (2) Static conformal data, which is all that block 60's static member sees, cannot force the cubic part: restricted to such data the comparator's cubic part is itself a field redefinition. Time-dependent data can. So the "moving content" terms are forced by consistency, not by the static member. (3) The result is classical in a Lorentz-covariant setting; what is checked here is that it also holds with only the lattice's cubic rotations, a 3+1 split and the repo's own quadratic action.

What is not shown: that "constraints preserved at second order" is exactly the local gauge identity used (assumed, A1); what fixes the overall multiple (suggested: the sources' unit transport); any lattice (finite-spacing) correction; any source coupling; the case without a shift field.
