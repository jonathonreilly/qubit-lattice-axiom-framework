### F1 conclusion: best criterion and hypothesis check

The strongest *explicit one-box uniqueness criterion* located is Dobrushin–Shlosman C_V: one finite volume satisfying the worst-case boundary-influence sum inequality implies uniqueness. It does not alone give exponential decay. The strongest *finite-volume-to-all-volume analyticity/correlation route* located is Dobrushin–Shlosman complete analyticity: the 1987 abstract says its alternatives are constructive from finitely many bounded-size volumes, but exact conditions remain unavailable. The strongest *compact-continuous-spin bridge* is Stroock–Zegarlinski Theorem 1.2, equating uniform LSI and Dobrushin–Shlosman mixing for finite-range lattice gases. The exact finite-box form and its application to a four-link Wilson plaquette remain unknown.

**Exact one-box condition recovered from the [ZbMATH Open review](https://zbmath.org/?q=ti%3A%22Constructive+Criterion+for+the+Uniqueness+of+Gibbs+Field%22) (Zbl 0569.46042).** It supplies the condition omitted from the publisher abstract: for a nearest-neighbor spin system with finite single-site set S, let k(V,t) be the maximum Kantorovich–Rubinstein distance between the two finite-volume conditional Gibbs laws when boundary conditions differ at one site t, using Hamming distance on configurations in V. The finite set V satisfies C_V when \(\sum_{t\in V}k(V,t)<|V|\). The review quotes the theorem: “Suppose there exists a volume V for which the condition C_V holds. Then there exists exactly one Gibbs state with interaction U.” The maximum is over **all** one-site boundary discrepancies. The review says noncompact spins and infinite-range interactions can also be incorporated, but it does not characterize the compact-metric/Lie-group version. It gives a concrete 3×4 box for the Dobrushin–Kolafa antiferromagnet application; the corresponding *Phase diagram of the two-dimensional Ising antiferromagnet (computer-assisted proof)* by Dobrushin, Kolafa and Shlosman is published, and its Springer abstract explicitly says the proof used a computer. That application is recorded only as GUIDE and is not used as proof evidence here.

### Strongest finite-box criterion found

The strongest published criterion with a direct SU(2) gauge-theory statement is Kanazawa’s Theorem 1 (§2.1, Eq. (4)): a finite-volume twisted-partition-function inequality bounds a Wilson loop; its text says the bound works for arbitrary one-plaquette actions. If the twisted-boundary ratio has exponential area decay, the consequence is a Wilson-loop area law. The Wilson one-plaquette action/reflection-positivity inputs fit the target, but the twist-sensitivity hypothesis is not verified at order-one coupling and the conclusion is not the requested all-observable exponential correlation decay. The closest published *general compact-spin* candidate is Stroock–Zegarlinski’s Theorem 1.2: for a “finite range lattice gas with a compact, continuous spin space,” uniform LSI is equivalent to their Dobrushin–Shlosman mixing condition (1.5). The theorem itself is **abstract-only here**; the condition, its finite-volume quantifiers, and allowed interaction order were not reached. The strongest *explicitly stated one-box uniqueness* test is Dobrushin–Shlosman’s (C_V) criterion, Σ_t k(V,t)<|V|. These complementary sources do not establish (HS-sup) or (DS-tw) for the target theory.
