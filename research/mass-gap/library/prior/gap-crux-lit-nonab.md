**Puskar Mondal, “A Geometric Approach to the Yang-Mills Mass Gap,” arXiv:2301.06996.** The abstract says it equips the orbit space of connections modulo gauge transformations with a Riemannian metric from the reduced classical kinetic action and says the metric has positive sectional curvature. It says a “regularized Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature (if positive)” is shown to produce a gap for 2+1 and 3+1 dimensional Yang–Mills, **assuming existence** of the quantized theories. It calls the gap calculation “at least a heuristic one.” For 3+1D it says a trace operation introduces a length scale, which “remains to be understood in a rigorous way.” The stated group scope is compact semisimple; the abstract does not name a lattice, finite/infinite volume, or a coupling interval. It supplies no theorem number or short theorem statement. Status: arXiv preprint; published status not established from the page read. URL read: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06996>.

- **Key input (abstract only):** positive regularized Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature on the infinite-dimensional orbit-space metric, plus an assumed quantized theory, is asserted to yield the gap. The abstract gives no proof location or exact curvature lower bound.
- **U(1) test:** the abstract restricts the heuristic to non-abelian theories with compact semisimple group. It does not show an exact lattice property false for U(1). The quotient-curvature motivation may be group-specific, but without the paper text and a derived lattice bound this method alone has no SU(2) gap implication. It would need a volume- and weak-coupling-uniform lower bound for the actual finite-lattice measure/Hamiltonian, with the regulator removal justified.
- **Z2 test:** not addressed in the abstract. No conclusion about permitting a finite-coupling transition can be made.
- **Small parameter / open point:** no small parameter is stated. The 3+1D scale obtained from the trace remains, in the abstract's words, “to be understood in a rigorous way.”
- **Verdict: GUIDE (weak).** It identifies orbit-space Bakry–Émery curvature as a candidate concept, but it does not provide a statement usable for (HS-sup), (ZF_B), (TM_B), (SC_B), or (AC'-lat). No exact O'Neill term or lattice Ricci estimate is confirmed from the permitted page.

### Follow-up paper and the O'Neill-type term

Semantic Scholar's citation data for 2301.06996 listed Puskar Mondal's later paper, **“Mass and infinite dimensional geometry,” arXiv:2308.09304** (2023). Its abstract and full HTML were read: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09304>, <https://arxiv.org/html/2308.09304>. It cites Singer, Babelon–Viallet and Orland for orbit-space geometry, then cites the 2301.06996 paper as the source proving its general “Theorem (gap)” ([31] in its references). Semantic Scholar's later-citation request for 2308.09304 returned HTTP 429; I did not retry it.

Theorem (gap), Section II, says: “Under the assumption of the existence of a quantum field theory” and a stated lower bound on the **regularized Bakry–Émery Ricci curvature**, the regularized Hamiltonian has `ΔE ≥ ℏ² Δ/2` (equation (9)). The theorem has no number. It is conditional on the quantum theory and the Ricci lower bound; the author says its proof is elsewhere and omits it. The result depends on regulator energy scale `χ`.

Section III, equation (24), gives the orbit-space sectional-curvature term
`K_{X,Y} = 3 <[X,Y], Δ̂_A^{-1}[X,Y]>`.
This is the commutator/O'Neill-type contribution requested in the task. It vanishes for an abelian Lie algebra, while the SU(2) Lie bracket need not vanish. The paper's U(1) section, equation (19), says the regularized bound yields only `ΔE ≥ 0`, “the well-known fact that there is no mass gap in U(1) gauge theory.” Thus the **candidate geometric curvature input distinguishes nonabelian from U(1)** at the formal configuration-space level.

- **Exact scope:** formal Hamiltonian geometry for quantum fields on `R^{1+3}`; no lattice, finite-volume, or Wilson-coupling theorem. The theorem assumes a quantized QFT already exists. The curvature and gap are regulator dependent.
- **Key input/location:** the conditional regularized Bakry–Émery lower bound in Theorem (gap), Section II; the nonabelian part enters through the bracket square in Section III, equation (24). The paper reports neither a uniform lower bound over all SU(2) configurations nor its persistence as `χ` is removed.
- **U(1) test:** the input genuinely differs formally: for U(1), `[X,Y]=0` and equation (19) gives only nonnegativity, no positive gap. This still does **not** establish a useful SU(2) lower bound; it only locates where an abelian argument could fail.
- **Z2 test:** not addressed; no statement about finite-coupling transitions.
- **Small parameter/open point:** no small coupling parameter is used. The regulator scale `χ` remains in the gap. Section III says the regularized Ricci curvature and its Yang–Mills contribution need further study; the full gap theorem is conditional and its proof is referred elsewhere.
- **Verdict: GUIDE.** It isolates a nonabelian commutator term that could in principle distinguish SU(2) from U(1), but does not give an exact lattice supplier for (HS-sup), (ZF_B), (TM_B), (SC_B), or (AC'-lat).

**Abhishek Goswami, “Mass gap in weakly coupled Abelian Higgs on a unit lattice,” arXiv:1811.00422.** Abstract and full HTML read: <https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00422>, <https://arxiv.org/html/1811.00422>. The arXiv version is a preprint; a journal publication was not established from the page. Theorem 2 (section 6) states for the Abelian Higgs theory on a unit lattice, with masses `mu` and `m_A` fixed and sufficiently large, that “the truncated two-point correlation of `dA`” at plaquettes `p1,p2` is bounded by `exp(-m d(p1,p2))` for some `m>0`. The paper's displayed result (1.28) gives the corresponding exponential bound for the field strength `F_{mu nu}`. The lattice region is finite with constants intended independent of its size; the theorem does not assert a continuum limit. It is a gauge-field observable in a U(1) model with Higgs matter, not pure U(1) Wilson theory.

- **Input:** section 6 differentiates the logarithm of the source partition function and bounds the connected correlation by summing connected polymers containing both plaquettes; the key inputs are the cluster estimate (5.18), the polymer-size lower bound (6.1), and the cluster-counting lemma cited there.
- **U(1) test:** the theorem is true in a *different* U(1) model with a Higgs field and fixed large mass parameters. The text says the Higgs mechanism gives the gauge boson mass; its small-`e0` convergent polymer expansion and large Higgs mass are essential. This cannot yield a gap for pure four-dimensional U(1) Wilson theory, which has a weak-coupling massless phase. Transferring to SU(2) would require an independently proved uniform Higgs-like block mass, absent here.
- **Z2 test:** not a theorem about Z2 gauge theory or a family of couplings crossing a transition. The small-coupling polymer-convergence hypothesis restricts its own domain; it neither rules out nor analyzes a transition outside that domain.
- **Small parameter/open point:** the expansion requires `e0` small (explicit powers `e0^...` in the cluster weights) and fixed, sufficiently large `mu,m_A`. The paper does not state an open problem in the conclusion. The theorem is a fixed-unit-lattice Higgs gap, not a weak-coupling pure-YM gap or continuum result.
- **Verdict: GUIDE** for the *shape* of a last-step extraction: an effective action with a convergent connected-polymer bound yields exponential decay for a gauge-invariant field-strength observable. It does not supply any of (HS-sup), (ZF_B), (TM_B), (SC_B), or (AC'-lat) for pure SU(2).

The paper itself compares its Theorem 2 to **Balaban, Imbrie, Jaffe and Brydges**, who prove exponential decay for integer-charge observables in the Abelian Higgs model. It also cites **Balaban, Feldman, Knörrer and Trubowitz** for power-series bounds, Balaban's determinant identity, and **Jonathan Dimock**'s “The Renormalization Group according to Balaban” parts I–II. ## T5. Solved model with the full infrared difficulty: Göpfert–Mack

**Markus Göpfert and Gerhard Mack, “Proof of confinement of static quarks in three-dimensional U(1) lattice gauge theory for all values of the coupling constant,”** *Communications in Mathematical Physics* 82 (1981), 545–606, DOI 10.1007/BF01961240. Springer abstract page read: <https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01961240>. The published abstract says the pure 3D U(1) Villain lattice gauge theory is exactly dual to a 3D integer ferromagnet (and to a Coulomb system), with nonzero string tension `alpha` for all coupling `g^2`. For small `a g^2`, it gives `alpha >= const · m_D beta^(-1)` and says the continuum limit at `a→0` with `m_D` fixed is a scalar free field of mass `m_D`; the physical string tension diverges in that limit. The abstract does not give a numbered theorem or state exponential decay of a local plaquette correlation as a mass-gap theorem. Status: published. Authors: Markus Göpfert and Gerhard Mack.

- **Key input:** exact duality to the integer ferromagnet/Coulomb gas makes monopole charges and screening available, as stated in the abstract. The precise Debye-screening step is not described there; full paper not read.
- **U(1) test:** this is itself U(1), but three-dimensional and Villain. It proves static-charge confinement for all coupling; it does not imply a four-dimensional U(1) local mass gap. The result therefore demonstrates that “abelian” alone does not identify the four-dimensional Coulomb phase. The model-specific dual monopole gas is the relevant extra structure.
- **Z2 test:** the abstract does not address Z2 or phase transitions. The exact duality is model-specific; no conclusion about allowing a finite-coupling transition follows from the abstract.
- **Small parameter/open point:** the nonzero string tension is claimed for all couplings; the displayed lower bound is for small `a g^2`. The abstract states no open problem. No transition-crossing or continuum nonzero mass-gap claim is given; the continuum scalar mass is held fixed by scaling, while the physical string tension diverges.
- **Verdict: GUIDE.** It identifies exact duality to monopole/Coulomb variables and Debye screening as the extra mechanism behind this solvable case. It gives no supplier for the five SU(2) crux statements.

**Sourav Chatterjee, “A short proof of confinement in three-dimensional lattice gauge theories with a central U(1),” arXiv:2602.00436.** Abstract and full HTML read: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00436>, <https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00436>. The abstract says “To appear in Adv. Nonlin. Studies, Special volume in honor of Len Gross”; publication itself was not verified from the journal page (the DOI endpoint returned an empty response). Theorem 3.1 states: for compact Lie `G ⊂ U(n)` containing every scalar matrix `zI`, for finite `Λ⊂Z³`, free boundary condition, every `β>0`, and rectangular Wilson loop with `R≤T`, `|⟨W_ℓ⟩| ≤ n exp{-C(1+nβ)^{-1} T log(R+1)}` with universal `C>0`. This is logarithmically growing quark-antiquark potential, not area law or a mass-gap theorem.

- **Input:** the central full U(1) lets the proof add auxiliary U(1) edge variables, factor the loop into a phase and bounded G-matrix term, condition on the latter, then use a two-dimensional weighted-XY/Mermin–Wagner estimate; this is the “Idea of the proof” following Theorem 3.1.
- **U(1) test:** applies to U(1) itself and U(N), so its input is true in an abelian theory; the conclusion is only 3D confinement with logarithmic potential, not a 4D local spectral gap. It cannot distinguish SU(2) from U(1) because it requires a *full central circle*, while SU(2)'s center is only `{±I}`.
- **Z2 test:** Z2 does not contain the required central circle, so the theorem is silent and permits a transition outside its assumptions.

**Vladimir Kazakov, “Bootstrap for Finite N Lattice Yang-Mills Theory,” arXiv:2404.16925.** Abstract and HTML read: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16925>, <https://arxiv.org/html/2404.16925>. Section 2 derives finite-N lattice Makeenko–Migdal equations and states that SU(2) loop equations close linearly on single-trace Wilson loops, using the SU(2) trace identity `tr U tr V = tr(UV)+tr(UV†)`. The paper studies SU(2) in dimensions 2, 3, 4. Its abstract says it bootstraps Wilson loops only up to length 24; the text explicitly uses advanced optimization software for those finite computations. The exact identity and formal loop equation are exact, but the numerical bootstrap output is computer-assisted and is not evidence for this task's proof route. The paper gives no theorem of positivity/monotonicity in beta or mass gap at `N=2`, `beta` of order one. Its plotted results use the paper's `lambda=2N^2/beta` convention and finite loop cutoff, not a theorem over all lattice sizes. Status: arXiv preprint; published status not established from the page. Author: Vladimir Kazakov.

- **Key input:** SU(2)'s two-trace identity collapses double-trace terms, making the finite-N loop equation linear and closed on single traces (section 2). This is false as stated for U(1) Wilson-loop variables only in the sense that the group/trace-algebra identity is different; the abstract makes no connection to a spectral gap. It is a nonabelian algebraic identity but supplies no lower bound uniform in volume.
- **U(1) test:** not a U(1) measure-gap input. U(1) has its own loop equations; this finite SU(2) trace closure is group-specific. It would need positivity or monotonicity of a Wilson-loop functional at `beta≈1`, proved in the exact infinite-volume model, to inform a gap route.
- **Z2 test:** not addressed. Finite loop truncation and numerical optimization do not decide whether the exact theory permits a transition.
- **Small parameter/open point:** the finite cutoff `L_max` is the computational truncation; it is not a small analytic parameter. The paper calls for further insights from the equations but states no analytic gap theorem.
- **Verdict: NO** for gap closure; GUIDE for an exact fixed-N algebraic form only. It supplies none of (HS-sup), (ZF_B), (TM_B), (SC_B), or (AC'-lat).
