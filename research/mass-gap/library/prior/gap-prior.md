## 5. Overlap and disagreement

The MDTN notes and SU(3) β=6 reduction both use strong-coupling area-law/confinement reasoning and cite Osterwalder–Seiler/Münster, but they concern different groups, dimensions/truncations and coupling targets; neither claims the Clay continuum gap. The crossover branch and two-block branch both carry the soft-kernel physical-gap source packet, and their `parts/physical-gap.md` says the same-law weak-to-strong crossover and physical-scale comparison remain open. The two-block response supplies a finite one-step conditional estimate, while the crossover critique explains why finite or dimensionless contraction does not imply a physical gap after scale growth; these statements are compatible. The neutral `ym/nt-gap-interface` note connects the soft-kernel gap to the hard-constraint route only under a common trajectory, blocking and observable family, and insists NT2/nonfreeness is separate from a gap.

The aligned-response branch has more extensive finite-model SU(2) response work than the two-block branch, including two exact hidden layers, marginal laws and small nonidentity terminal backgrounds. Its authors still state that general conditional classes, inherited transitions, depth/cutoff control and physical mass remain open. Thus it does not contradict the crossover branch: the estimates are at different laws and finite-depth scopes. The cluster-adjoint branch is a conditional finite-dimensional temporal implication; it explicitly disclaims the spatial gap input, so it is also compatible. The SU(3) β=6 reduction is the strongest sounding coupling claim, but its own missing premise is absence of a second-order bulk critical point; it does not conflict with the mass-gap target branches because it is not unconditional and stays at fixed lattice spacing. No two reviewed branches state incompatible proved physical mass-gap claims.
