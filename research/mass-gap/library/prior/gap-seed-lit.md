## S1. Is the doubling criterion already published?

**Andrei-Sebastian Ursachi, “The two-dimensional O(3) sigma model: a volume-uniform mass-gap certificate and the one inequality that remains,” Research Square rs-11440706/v1.** Preprint, posted 21 October 2026; the page says it has not been peer reviewed. [Research Square article page](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-11440706/v1), read 11 November 2026. The page serves the abstract and a PDF link; its embedded `fullText` field is empty. Because it is not an HTML full-text without a PDF, I did not open or fetch the PDF. The abstract's numbered theorem is not exposed. Its short exact claim is: “We give a finite criterion for the gap, built from two partition-function ratios on small tori.” It says that satisfying the criterion at one seed size gives a volume-uniform transfer-matrix gap, and that its criterion is an explicit free-energy doubling defect below 0.2239 at some finite size. It reports an exact dichotomy: either the defect remains above threshold at all sizes, or it collapses and the gap follows; it says the gapless case must stay above threshold. The proof input and lemma number are **not visible** on the HTML page. The abstract also reports certified computations at eight couplings, with a separate numerical test at β=1.4–1.6; those computations cannot be proof inputs here. U(1): no four-dimensional U(1) Wilson model is tested. The paper studies a two-dimensional O(3) spin model; its abstract also reports failure for O(2), but that is not the four-dimensional U(1) gauge theory test. Z₂: no Z₂ lattice-gauge test is reported. Open: the abstract says the all-coupling inequality is not proved. Verdict: **GUIDE** for the seed's form and for Theorem S's criterion architecture; **NO** as evidence for the 4D SU(2) seed or its proof. Comparison: this is the closest published-facing item, but it is a preprint, its body was not served as HTML, and the 2D O(3) threshold/model do not establish Theorem S or Lemma K for 4D Wilson SU(2).

**Marius Lemm and Evgeny Mozgunov, “Spectral gaps of frustration-free spin systems with boundary,”** arXiv:1801.08915v2; published in *Journal of Mathematical Physics* (2019), DOI [10.1063/1.5089773](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.5089773). [arXiv HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.08915), read §§1, 2.3 and the open-problem paragraph in §1. Published. Theorem 2.5 gives the finite-size criterion

`γ_m ≥ [min{γ_n^B, γ_(n−1)^E} − 2√6 n^(−3/2)] / (2^8 √(6n))`

for `m ≥ 8` and `4 ≤ n ≤ m/2`. The result concerns finite-size spectral gaps of frustration-free, nearest-neighbour one-dimensional quantum spin chains with an open boundary. The proof input is the frustration-free local-Hamiltonian decomposition and its bulk/edge gap estimates; see §2.3 and Theorem 2.5. It does not compare torus partition functions. U(1): the hypothesis is not a nonabelian group property; the theorem gives no U(1) Wilson transfer-operator estimate. Z₂: no Z₂ gauge test or model application is made. The authors say the periodic 2D threshold extension is left for future work, and that optimality of the `n^(−3/2)` threshold for boundary systems is unknown (§1). Verdict: **GUIDE** for the general finite-size-to-volume spectral-gap strategy; **NO** for the seed and for Theorem S as a direct input. The paper itself distinguishes its problem from fully translation-invariant 2D periodic systems.

**S1 conclusion.** A one-seed finite-size criterion for a transfer gap is reported in the Ursachi preprint, in the same broad shape as Theorem S, but its full proof and numbered statement were not accessible without the PDF. Published frustration-free spin-chain criteria are structurally adjacent and use local Hamiltonian gaps, not the Wilson torus ratio. Theorem S is therefore **partly** anticipated at the abstract level, not found as the exact 4D SU(2) statement. Lemma K's particular deficit monotonicity is **not established as published by the pages read**; the inaccessible Ursachi body prevents a stronger negative claim. No seed tool true for SU(2) and false for U(1) was found in S1.

## S2. Correlation inequalities and monotonicity

The prior digest already records De Angelis–de Falco’s bibliographic-only “Correlation inequalities for lattice gauge fields,” Ginibre’s general framework, and Kanazawa’s twist/reflection-positivity inequality (`library/prior/gap-crux-lit-2.md`, §F7/T7). I do not repeat those entries. Ginibre’s accessible abstract explicitly says no noncommutative example satisfying its hypotheses was then known; De Angelis–de Falco’s accessible page supplied no theorem or gauge group. Kanazawa gives a conditional Wilson-loop bound from twist sensitivity, not a correlation inequality that proves the seed. The new searches below add one Abelian Villain result and one Z₂ current result, but no SU(2)-specific inequality.

**Ilya Chevyrev and Christophe Garban, “Villain Action in Lattice Gauge Theory,”** arXiv:2404.09928v2; published *Journal of Statistical Physics* 192 (2025), article 38, DOI [10.1007/s10955-025-03420-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10955-025-03420-1). [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.09928), read abstract and §§1.1–1.2. The accessible HTML stops in the preliminaries before the numbered theorem and its proof; no PDF was fetched. The exact-numbered result is therefore **not reached**. The abstract states: “We prove that Villain interaction applied to lattice gauge theory can be obtained as the limit of both Wilson and Manton interactions.” The introduction says this works for compact connected structure groups, including non-Abelian examples such as SU(3); it then says the Abelian case extends the Ginibre correlation inequality to Villain lattice gauge interaction. The proof input, according to §1.1, is a “carpet graph” limit of larger Wilson/Manton graphs, analogous to cable-graph limits for O(N) spins. The authors say they do not know how to derive the Villain Ginibre inequality without this limit (Remark referenced in §1.1; exact remark number unavailable in the served text). U(1): the stated Ginibre inequality is **Abelian**, so it is not false for U(1); however this page does not establish it for the finite 4D Wilson action at a fixed size. Its non-Abelian carpet-graph limit does not assert a Ginibre inequality for SU(2). Z₂: the principal limit theorem assumes a compact connected group and so does not cover the disconnected finite group Z₂; the Ginibre consequence is stated for Abelian Villain interactions, without a Z₂ gauge result. Open: the authors identify the route without the carpet-graph limit as unclear; the served text does not state a proof of the SU(2) inequality. Verdict: **GUIDE** for the seed as a possible Abelian comparison method; **NO** for Theorem S as a seed input. It provides no established SU(2)-true/U(1)-false tool.

**Malin P. Forsström and Fredrik Viklund, “Current expansion and couplings for Ising lattice gauge theory,”** arXiv:2502.19942v2 (17 November 2025 preprint). [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19942), read §§1, 3–4 and 6. No journal publication record was present in the readable page. Proposition 6.3 states, for every finite box and `β ≥ 0`, both

`E[W_{γ₁}W_{γ₂}] ≥ E[W_{γ₁}]E[W_{γ₂}]` and `d E[W_γ]/dβ ≥ 0`.

These give Griffith’s second inequality for Wilson loops and monotonicity of their expectations in coupling in the Z₂ gauge model. The proof input is the positive plaquette-current expansion (Theorem 1.1) and its switching lemma (Lemma 1.2); the proof of Proposition 6.3 applies the switching lemma with `F=1`. The representation is a sum over plaquette currents/surfaces, rather than the edge-current paths of the Ising spin model. U(1): the paper does not assert this result for U(1) Wilson theory, and it uses no non-Abelian property. Thus it does not provide an input known to fail for U(1). Z₂: the inequality is proved for Z₂ at every `β ≥ 0`, so it is a positive control case, not an SU(2)-specific result. The authors prove exponential covariance decay at sufficiently small and sufficiently large `β` in Proposition 6.8. They say it would be interesting to use the representation at other couplings, “especially near the critical value where the phase transition occurs” (§6, before Proposition 6.5). Verdict: **GUIDE** for a positive-current route and for the value of a coupling-monotonicity tool; **NO** for direct use on the SU(2) seed or Theorem S. This does not establish monotonicity in the torus size or in the free-energy doubling defect.

**Not reached:** J. Fröhlich, B. Simon and T. Spencer, “Correlation inequalities and quark confinement in lattice gauge theories,” *Nuclear Physics B* 180(2) (1981), 294–300, DOI [10.1016/0550-3213(81)90421-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0550-3213(81)90421-1). The ScienceDirect page returned HTTP 403, so I stopped there. Its full statement and proof input are not used. The accessible bibliographic/search lead describes inequalities for Z₂ and U(N) Wilson loops and discusses possible application to SU(2), but this report does not treat that as a read source or quote it. Best open URL to try later: [arXiv search by exact title](https://arxiv.org/search/?query=%22Correlation+inequalities+and+quark+confinement+in+lattice+gauge+theories%22&searchtype=title).

## S3 — Representations with positive weights

The prior T6 digest already treats Cherrington's three-dimensional SU(2) spin-foam construction and related 6j/15j, loop and surface expansions. Those are not repeated here: the recorded results transform the partition function but do not give the positive, volume-monotone observable needed to bound `Phi(n)` in four dimensions. I did not reach full texts for the Anishetty et al. 3D Ponzano–Regge construction or later SU(2) representation papers; see the not-reached list at the end.

**Hendryk Pfeiffer and Robert Oeckl, “The dual of non-Abelian Lattice Gauge Theory,”** arXiv:hep-lat/0110034v1; published in *Nuclear Physics B Proceedings Supplements* 106 (2002), 1010–1012, DOI [10.1016/S0920-5632(01)01913-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0920-5632(01)01913-2). [arXiv HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/hep-lat/0110034), read the abstract, §§1–2 and §3. The unnumbered main result is an “exact strong–weak duality transformation” (Abstract) in any dimension `d ≥ 2` for a compact Lie gauge group, replacing link variables by irreducible representations and intertwiners. The construction uses character expansion of each plaquette Boltzmann factor followed by Haar integration at links; §§2.1–2.2 express the dual partition function as a sum over plaquette representations and invariant tensors. On signs, §3 states that standard-action dual Boltzmann coefficients are strictly positive, but the invariant contractions `C(i)` and `D(i)` are not guaranteed positive and their signs are assigned to the observable. Thus the complete spin-foam amplitudes are not termwise nonnegative. U(1): the introduction explicitly recalls the four-dimensional electric–magnetic duality of pure U(1) lattice gauge theory, and the general compact-Lie-group construction includes U(1); its key character/Haar mechanism is not SU(2)-specific and so supplies no input false for U(1). Z₂: it is a finite compact Lie group, but no Z₂-specific mass-gap or positive-amplitude consequence is stated. The paper states no open problem relevant to this seed; it provides an exact representation, not a bound on the size dependence of the periodic-torus partition functions, `Phi(n)`, or a spectral gap. Verdict: **GUIDE** for studying representations and their signed contractions; **NO** as a seed bound or Theorem S input. URL read: https://arxiv.org/html/hep-lat/0110034.

**S3 conclusion.** The read full-text representation is not a positive-weight probabilistic expansion: only the local character coefficients are strictly positive, and its invariant contractions can change sign. It applies to U(1) as well as non-Abelian compact Lie groups. The known 3D SU(2) 6j route is dimensionally mismatched and previously found not to establish a four-dimensional positivity or monotonicity theorem. No representation tool was found that bounds the SU(2) seed and fails for U(1).

## S4 — Monotone quantities along the scale for gauge theories

The second-pass digest already records Kanazawa's twisted/untwisted partition-function identities and coupling mismatch, plus the conditional Tomboulis–Yaffe twisted-vortex inequality. Those ratios concern center twists and Wilson-loop bounds; they are not a monotonicity theorem for periodic `Z(n,n,n,n)` versus `Z(2n,2n,2n,2n)`. No source in this pass established a lattice a-theorem or a step-scaling inequality that controls `Phi(n)`.

**Stefan Sint and Pol Vilaseca, “Lattice artefacts in the Schrödinger Functional coupling for strongly interacting theories,”** arXiv:1211.0411v1 (2012 conference preprint; no journal reference appears in the arXiv API record). [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1211.0411), read Abstract, §§1, 4.2 and 5. The report has no theorem number. Its abstract's exact result description is that it studies “the step scaling function for SU(2) and SU(3) gauge groups” with one-loop O(a)-improvement and a redefinition to reduce cutoff effects. The paper's §4.2 calls the SU(2) cutoff-effect improvement an open question; its conclusion says higher-order removal still needs checking. The input is one-loop perturbative analysis of a Schrödinger-functional renormalized coupling with Dirichlet temporal boundary conditions, not a comparison of periodic four-torus partition functions. U(1): no U(1) theorem/test is stated; this is not an SU(2)-true/U(1)-false inequality. Z₂: likewise not studied. What remains open in the paper: whether the proposed reduction of lattice artefacts works for SU(2), and control beyond one loop. Verdict: **NO** for the seed and Theorem S; **GUIDE** only as an example of finite-volume scale observables whose boundary/cutoff dependence must be separated from periodic-volume monotonicity. URL read: https://arxiv.org/html/1211.0411.

**S4 conclusion.** Step scaling is a renormalized-coupling comparison under scale change; the read paper offers a perturbative and numerical program, not a rigorous monotonicity statement for the partition-function doubling defect. Twisted free energies and 't Hooft-loop inequalities remain a distinct, conditional route already documented in the prior digest. No SU(2)-true/U(1)-false seed tool found.

## S6 — Number and density of states

**Kurt Langfeld, Biagio Lucini and Antonio Rago, “The density of states in gauge theories,”** arXiv:1204.3243v2; published *Physical Review Letters* 109 (2012), 111601. [arXiv HTML full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1204.3243), read Abstract, §§2 and 4. The paper has no numbered theorem; its main result is an algorithmic calculation of the action density of states for SU(2) and compact U(1) using a modified Wang–Landau algorithm, which the abstract says is “reliably calculated over a range of 120,000 orders of magnitude.” The input is the Wang–Landau sampling procedure for the action-density function, validated against thermodynamic quantities and applied up to `20^4` lattices. It is expressly numerical: it estimates action density of states, not a rigorous bound on the eigenvalue multiplicities of the transfer operator. U(1): the same method is applied to compact U(1), so the tool is not SU(2)-specific; it recovers the U(1) weak first-order transition. Z₂: not tested. The conclusion proposes thermodynamic potentials, vortex free energies and electric fluxes for later study of mass gap and confinement; those applications are not performed here. Verdict: **GUIDE** as a computationally motivated diagnostic (not admissible as proof input under this task); **NO** for a rigorous seed bound or Theorem S. URL read: https://arxiv.org/html/1204.3243.
